## Overview

*Bad* (1987) is [Michael Jackson](/people/michael-jackson/)'s follow-up to *Thriller* (1982), the best-selling album ever made. Produced with Quincy Jones, it debuted at No. 1 on the *Billboard* 200, produced five consecutive No. 1 singles on the *Billboard* Hot 100 — the first album ever to do so — won two Grammy Awards, and stands at 11× Platinum in the United States ([GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/michael-jackson/13202/); [Parade](https://parade.com/entertainment/michael-jackson-bad-39-years-five-no-1-singles); [That Grape Juice](http://thatgrapejuice.net/2022/08/michael-jackson-becomes-first-singer-history-own-multiple-diamond-albums-singles/)).

For clarity: this profile covers the 1987 studio album *Bad* — not the title song alone (the Recording Academy lists "Bad (Album)" and "Bad (Single)" as separate entries), and not any other work that happens to be called "Bad." It was Jackson's third studio collaboration with Jones, and its Bad World Tour ran from 1987 to 1989 — still counted among the highest-grossing concert tours of all time ([GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/michael-jackson/13202/)).

## A chart record nobody has beaten

*Bad* debuted at No. 1 on the *Billboard* 200 on September 26, 1987, and stayed six weeks, in stores since August 31 ([Parade](https://parade.com/entertainment/michael-jackson-bad-39-years-five-no-1-singles)). Its lead single, "I Just Can't Stop Loving You" — a duet with Siedah Garrett — topped the Hot 100 that month ([Parade](https://parade.com/entertainment/michael-jackson-bad-39-years-five-no-1-singles)). What followed was unprecedented: "Bad," "The Way You Make Me Feel," "Man in the Mirror" and "Dirty Diana" each reached No. 1, with "Dirty Diana" topping the chart on July 2, 1988 — nine months later ([Parade](https://parade.com/entertainment/michael-jackson-bad-39-years-five-no-1-singles)). The Recording Academy confirms that five consecutive No. 1 singles from one album "had never been achieved" before ([GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/michael-jackson/13202/)). By *Billboard*'s count, no album had managed it since the Hot 100 began in 1958 ([Parade](https://parade.com/entertainment/michael-jackson-bad-39-years-five-no-1-singles)). Only one album has matched the feat since: Katy Perry's *Teenage Dream*, which pulled five No. 1s in 2011 ([Parade](https://parade.com/entertainment/michael-jackson-bad-39-years-five-no-1-singles)). *Thriller* — the best-selling album ever made — sent seven singles into the top 10, but only two reached No. 1: "Billie Jean" and "Beat It" ([Parade](https://parade.com/entertainment/michael-jackson-bad-39-years-five-no-1-singles)).

## Two Grammys — and the Album of the Year that got away

At the 30th Grammy Awards in 1988, *Bad* was nominated for Album of the Year, Best Pop Vocal Performance–Male, Best R&B Vocal Performance–Male (for the "Bad" single), and Producer of the Year–Non Classical (Jackson with Quincy Jones) ([GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/michael-jackson/13202/)). Jackson won none of them: Album of the Year went to U2's *The Joshua Tree*; the pop, R&B vocal and producer awards went to Sting, Smokey Robinson and Narada Michael Walden ([GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/awards/30th-annual-grammy-awards/)). *Bad*'s sole 1988 Grammy — Best Engineered Recording–Non Classical — was credited to engineers Bruce Swedien and Humberto Gatica ([GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/awards/30th-annual-grammy-awards/)). The "Man in the Mirror" single earned a Record of the Year nomination in 1989 ([GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/michael-jackson/13202/)). Then, in 1990, the "Leave Me Alone" video won Best Music Video–Short Form at the 32nd Grammys ([GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/awards/32nd-annual-grammy-awards/)). The stat, source, and implication: five Hot 100 No. 1s — the first album ever to do so ([GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/michael-jackson/13202/)) — yet Album of the Year went to U2; Grammy voters honored the year's defining statement while the marketplace belonged to Jackson.

## The *Bad* era — and why the 2026 biopic ends there

*Bad* defined a cultural era, not just an album cycle. Britannica notes the album produced five chart-topping hits, including the title song and "Man in the Mirror," while "Leave Me Alone" answered the pile-up of tabloid rumors about Jackson's lifestyle accumulated since *Thriller* — rumors casting him as a Neverland Ranch eccentric recluse, citing his hyperbaric chamber, pet chimpanzee Bubbles and amusement park ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Michael-Jackson)). The Bad World Tour ran from 1987 to 1989 and remains one of the highest-grossing concert tours of all time ([GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/michael-jackson/13202/)). Twenty-five years on, Spike Lee's *Bad 25* documentary (2012) revealed that Jackson had written 60 demos for the record, 11 of which made it — with Sheryl Crow his background singer on the tour ([Associated Press](https://lasentinel.net/spike-lee-works-on-michael-jackson-documentary.html)). The album was re-released on September 18, 2012 with additional tracks, a DVD and bonus material ([Associated Press](https://lasentinel.net/spike-lee-works-on-michael-jackson-documentary.html)). That is why the 2026 biopic [*Michael*](/creative-works/michael-2026-film/), per Britannica, ends its narrative in the late 1980s — at the height of the *Bad* era ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Michael-Jackson)).

## Frequently asked questions

**How many No. 1 singles did *Bad* produce?**
Five consecutive No. 1 singles on the *Billboard* Hot 100: "I Just Can't Stop Loving You," "Bad," "The Way You Make Me Feel," "Man in the Mirror" and "Dirty Diana." It was the first album ever to do so, and only Katy Perry's *Teenage Dream* (2011) has matched it. *Thriller*, by contrast, produced just two No. 1s.

**How many Grammy Awards did *Bad* win?**
Two. At the 1988 Grammys it won Best Engineered Recording–Non Classical, credited to engineers Bruce Swedien and Humberto Gatica. At the 1990 Grammys, the "Leave Me Alone" video won Best Music Video–Short Form. Jackson was nominated for Album of the Year in 1988 but lost to U2's *The Joshua Tree*.

**How many copies of *Bad* were sold?**
The RIAA certifies *Bad* at 11× Platinum in the United States — over 11 million US units — updated in August 2022. Worldwide totals are widely claimed at around 35 million, but this profile could not verify that figure from a tier1 or tier2 source, so it is not asserted here.

**What is the "Bad era"?**
The 1987–1989 period around the album and the Bad World Tour, still one of the highest-grossing concert tours ever. Britannica notes that "Leave Me Alone" answered the era's tabloid rumors about Jackson's lifestyle. The 2026 biopic *Michael* ends its story here, at the height of the *Bad* era, in 1988.
