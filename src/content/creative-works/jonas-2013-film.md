## Overview

**Jonas** is a 2013 American religious thriller directed by Adam Rehmeier and
starring Gregg Gilmore as Jonas Nuckolls, a man who washes up on the California
coast and spends a week in Los Angeles preaching a message he believes comes
from God ([Bloody Disgusting](https://bloody-disgusting.com/news/20662/teaser-trailer-for-religious-thriller-jonas/)).
Conceived as the companion piece to Rehmeier's debut *The Bunny Game* — which
Britain's BBFC refused to classify — it was shot in six weeks, told in six
stand-alone verses, and released free online ([Warped Perspective](https://warped-perspective.com/2013/07/film-review-jonas-2013/);
[Vimeo](https://vimeo.com/user3875433/videos/sort:date/format:detail)).

## The shadow of a banned film

Rehmeier's debut, *The Bunny Game* (2011), was refused classification by the
British Board of Film Classification over its unbroken focus on sexual and
physical abuse — effectively banning it in the United Kingdom, as this site's
profile of that film documents ([The Bunny Game](/creative-works/the-bunny-game/)).
*Jonas* was conceived as its deliberate inverse. "In contrast to THE BUNNY
GAME, JONAS is shockingly square and squeaky-clean, almost old-fashioned,"
Rehmeier told Bloody Disgusting ([Bloody Disgusting](https://bloody-disgusting.com/news/20662/teaser-trailer-for-religious-thriller-jonas/)).
His own Vimeo channel describes *Jonas* explicitly as "the companion piece" for
the 2011 debut, following Jonas Nuckolls — played by Gregg Gilmore — door to
door through Los Angeles delivering a powerful message ([Vimeo](https://vimeo.com/user3875433/videos/sort:date/format:detail)).
The connection is textual as well as thematic: viewers of the earlier film have
already met Jonas, "very briefly, at the film's close." Even so, *Jonas* is a
thematic sequel rather than a direct one, and it can be watched with no prior
experience of its predecessor ([Warped Perspective](https://warped-perspective.com/2013/07/film-review-jonas-2013/)).

## Six verses, six weeks, real people

The entire production spanned only six weeks from initial concept to full
execution, which Rehmeier described as "strangely effortless"
([Bloody Disgusting](https://bloody-disgusting.com/news/20662/teaser-trailer-for-religious-thriller-jonas/)).
The film is told in six separate verses that can be watched as a sequential
feature or taken apart and viewed piece by piece, out of order — an experiment
with the nature of film itself ([Warped Perspective](https://warped-perspective.com/2013/07/film-review-jonas-2013/)).
It was planned but improvised, using real people and real responses, which
makes it "as much a documentary as it is a fictional film"
([Warped Perspective](https://warped-perspective.com/2013/07/film-review-jonas-2013/)).
Across a single week, Jonas moves through Los Angeles meeting a cross-section
of the city — believers, skeptics, the vulnerable — in conversations about
faith, love and humanity that belong as much to the people he meets as to him.
The stat, source and implication: six weeks, concept to completion
(Bloody Disgusting) — a production speed implying the film was built as a
guerrilla experiment in faith and form, not a conventionally developed feature.

## The free release

The most unusual documented fact about *Jonas* is its distribution: Rehmeier
and Gilmore decided the film should be seen for free, announcing it would be
"FREE to watch in upcoming weeks via www.jonasmovie.com"
([Warped Perspective](https://warped-perspective.com/2013/07/film-review-jonas-2013/)).
Reviewer Nia Edwards-Behi read the strategy as an extension of the film itself
— Jonas travels the city giving away Bibles, so a free film mirrors his
mission — and judged the finished work "a powerful, complex and provocative
film," crediting Rehmeier's composing, shooting and editing and Gilmore's
conceptualisation and performance as crucial to its working
([Warped Perspective](https://warped-perspective.com/2013/07/film-review-jonas-2013/)).
Plex lists a 9 October 2013 release date and a 1-hour-51-minute runtime
([Plex](https://watch.plex.tv/movie/jonas-2013-1)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is *Jonas* a sequel to *The Bunny Game*?**
Not directly. It is a thematic sequel and companion piece: the character Jonas appears very briefly at the close of *The Bunny Game*, but *Jonas* tells a self-contained story and can be watched with no knowledge of the earlier film. Rehmeier framed it as the deliberate inverse of his debut — "shockingly square and squeaky-clean" where the debut was extreme.

**Why was *Jonas* released for free?**
Rehmeier and star Gregg Gilmore decided the film should be seen for free, announcing free streaming via jonasmovie.com in 2013. The contemporary review read the strategy as mirroring the film itself: Jonas travels Los Angeles giving away Bibles, so a free film extends his mission.

**What does the "six verses" structure mean?**
The film is told in six separate verses that work as a sequential feature but can also be taken apart and watched piece by piece, out of order. Rehmeier's own Vimeo channel released at least the first verse ("Verse 1") as a stand-alone video.

**How was *Jonas* made in only six weeks?**
Rehmeier said the production ran six weeks from initial concept to full execution. It was planned but improvised, shot cinéma-vérité style with real people giving real responses across Los Angeles — a method closer to documentary than to conventional narrative filmmaking.
