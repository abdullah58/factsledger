## Overview

Kitabi Dede Gorgud — known in English as *The Book of Dede Korkut* — is the heroic epic of the Oghuz Turks, a collection of twelve tales in prose and verse gathered around the court of Bayindir Khan. Its warrior-nobles — Salur Kazan, Bamsi Beyrek, Basat and others — face captivity, rescue, marriage contests and monster-slaying, while the sage Dede Korkut names heroes and closes each tale with a moral blessing ([Columbia University, World Epics](https://edblogs.columbia.edu/worldepics/project/the-book-of-dede-korkut/); [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/culture-magazines/book-dede-korkut); [Penguin Books](https://www.penguin.co.uk/books/34953/the-book-of-dede-korkut-by-lewis-geoffrey/9780141199030)).

The epic is written in Oghuz Turkic in Arabic script and survives in 16th-century manuscript copies, though its tales circulated orally centuries earlier ([Visions of Azerbaijan](http://www.visions.az/en/news/33/60e1544a/); [Columbia University, World Epics](https://edblogs.columbia.edu/worldepics/project/the-book-of-dede-korkut/)). In Azerbaijani usage it is *Kitabi-Dədə Qorqud*; in Turkish and English usage it is *Dede Korkut Kitabı* / *The Book of Dede Korkut*. Azerbaijan treats it as a national literary monument, while its heritage is shared across the Turkic world, including Turkey, Turkmenistan, Kazakhstan and, to a lesser degree, Kyrgyzstan ([Presidential Library of Azerbaijan](https://unesco.preslib.az/en/page/yzm6CkPpGj); [UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/01399)).

## Contents and characters

The book has no single hero. Instead, each tale follows a different Oghuz noble household, giving the epic a collective hero: the Oghuz nobility as a whole, bound by loyalty, hospitality, courage and family honour ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/culture-magazines/book-dede-korkut)). Its recurring protagonists are Salur Kazan, the beylerbey whose household is repeatedly threatened; Bamsi Beyrek, the romantic hero held captive for sixteen years; Basat, who kills the one-eyed giant Tepegöz; Deli Dumrul, who confronts Azrael, the angel of death; and Kan Turali, who wins his bride by defeating a lion, a bull and a camel ([Columbia University, World Epics](https://edblogs.columbia.edu/worldepics/project/the-book-of-dede-korkut/); [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/culture-magazines/book-dede-korkut)). Women are active and outspoken figures: Banu Chichek tests Beyrek in archery, horse-racing and wrestling, Lady Burla rides to rescue her son, and Dumrul's wife offers her life for his ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/culture-magazines/book-dede-korkut)). The tales mix Islamic and older pre-Islamic motifs, and Dede Korkut's closing blessings frame each adventure as a lesson in conduct ([Columbia University, World Epics](https://edblogs.columbia.edu/worldepics/project/the-book-of-dede-korkut/); [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/culture-magazines/book-dede-korkut)).

## Manuscripts and textual history

The epic survives as a shifting network of tales rather than a single fixed text, compiled and recopied by unknown hands ([Columbia University, World Epics](https://edblogs.columbia.edu/worldepics/project/the-book-of-dede-korkut/)). The Dresden manuscript contains a prose prologue and twelve narrative tales and was likely copied in the 16th century from a 15th-century substratum, while the Vatican manuscript, found by Ettore Rossi in 1950, holds six tales and differs substantially in language, structure and interpolations ([Columbia University, World Epics](https://edblogs.columbia.edu/worldepics/project/the-book-of-dede-korkut/); [Presidential Library of Azerbaijan](https://unesco.preslib.az/en/page/yzm6CkPpGj)). The tales reached European readers when Heinrich Friedrich von Diez published a German translation of the Basat–Tepegöz tale in 1815, and the first printed edition appeared in Istanbul in 1916, edited by Kilisli Rifat ([Columbia University, World Epics](https://edblogs.columbia.edu/worldepics/project/the-book-of-dede-korkut/); [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/culture-magazines/book-dede-korkut)). A side-by-side edition of the Dresden and Vatican texts by Semih Tezcan and Hendrik Boeschoten appeared in 2001, and in 2018 a third, Gonbad manuscript surfaced in northern Iran, with two tales and declamations including a previously unknown episode about Kazan's naming ([Columbia University, World Epics](https://edblogs.columbia.edu/worldepics/project/the-book-of-dede-korkut/)).

## UNESCO recognition and modern influence

In 2018 [UNESCO](/organizations/unesco/) inscribed the heritage of Dede Qorqud / Korkyt Ata / Dede Korkut on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, on a joint nomination by Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan and Türkiye, under reference 01399 ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/01399)). The inscribed heritage rests on twelve heroic legends and thirteen traditional musical compositions transmitted through oral expression and performing arts, with Dede Qorqud appearing in each story as a sage of minstrels ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/01399)). Its stated values include heroism, dialogue, unity and respect for nature, and its music uses the Kobyz to imitate natural sounds ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/01399)). The inscription followed the 1,300th anniversary marked by Azerbaijan and UNESCO in 2000 ([Azernews](https://www.azernews.az/culture/87828.html)). The epic had already shaped modern nationalism: Ziya Gökalp urged readers to treat it as a source of Turkish language, culture and values, and later writers such as Yaşar Kemal and Latife Tekin drew on its themes ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/culture-magazines/book-dede-korkut)). On screen, Tofig Tagizada filmed a two-part version in Azerbaijan in 1975, later described as a pearl of Azerbaijani cinema ([Azernews](https://www.azernews.az/culture/87828.html)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Kitabi Dede Gorgud?**
Kitabi Dede Gorgud, or *The Book of Dede Korkut*, is the heroic epic of the Oghuz Turks: twelve tales in prose and verse about warrior-nobles such as Salur Kazan, Bamsi Beyrek and Basat. The sage Dede Korkut narrates, names heroes and closes each tale with a moral blessing.

**How many manuscripts of the epic survive?**
Three early manuscripts are known: the Dresden copy with a prologue and twelve tales, the Vatican copy with six tales, and the Gonbad copy found in northern Iran in 2018 with two tales and declamations. Older claims of only two manuscripts are superseded by the Gonbad find.

**What did UNESCO inscribe in 2018?**
UNESCO inscribed the heritage of Dede Qorqud / Korkyt Ata / Dede Korkut — twelve heroic legends plus thirteen musical compositions — on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. The nomination came jointly from Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan and Türkiye.

**Who was Dede Korkut?**
Dede Korkut is the epic's sage, narrator and name-giver: a seer, tribal elder and minstrel who advises the Oghuz, settles disputes and sings each tale. Tradition sometimes treats him as author, but scholarly sources describe the book as compiled by an anonymous scribe.

**Why do dates for the epic differ?**
Oral tales circulated from the 9th–10th centuries, while written compilation is placed in the 14th–15th centuries and surviving copies are 16th-century. Some Azerbaijani research proposes an 11th-century first writing, so this profile reports the full range rather than settling on a single date.

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not settle the epic's date: oral roots, written compilation and surviving copies are three different dates, and sources disagree on the written stage.
- It does not treat Dede Korkut as the verified author; it treats him as narrator and character, with authorship-by-tradition flagged as a disagreement.
- It does not repeat the claim that UNESCO declared the book a Masterpiece in 2000; the verified UNESCO act is the 2018 Representative List inscription.
- It does not claim the 2018 inscription covers Turkmenistan; UNESCO's official record names Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan and Türkiye.
- It does not present the Dresden manuscript as an original; sources describe it as a 16th-century copy of an earlier text.
- It does not cover the reported Bursa manuscript, which was not verified from a source read in full for this profile.
- It does not assert that the epic is a literal historical record; it is a literary monument that also preserves Oghuz custom, values and memory.
