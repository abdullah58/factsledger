**Mann Ki Baat** is the Prime Minister of India's monthly radio address, on air since 3 October 2014 — the festival of Vijayadashami — on All India Radio and carried visually by [Doordarshan](/organizations/doordarshan/)'s television channels ([ThePrint via ANI](https://theprint.in/india/pm-modis-mann-ki-baat-positively-influences-citizenship-behaviour-over-100-crore-have-listened-to-programme-at-least-once-survey/1537044/)). Hosted by [Narendra Modi](/people/narendra-modi/), it airs on the last Sunday of each month, is translated into dozens of Indian and foreign languages, and in its 117th episode (December 2024) named the animated series ["Krish, Trish and Baltiboy"](/creative-works/krish-trish-and-baltiboy/) on air, calling it a favourite among children ([Prime Minister's Office](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/pms-address-in-the-117th-episode-of-mann-ki-baat/)).

For clarity: *Mann Ki Baat* is a government broadcast produced within the Prime Minister's Office and aired through the public broadcaster — not a commercial media product or podcast brand. Its stated purpose, per then I&B Minister [Anurag Thakur](/people/anurag-thakur/) in a Rajya Sabha reply, is to "establish a dialogue with the citizens on issues of day-to-day governance" ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/why-air-doordarshans-revenue-from-pm-modis-mann-ki-baat-fell-by-90-in-the-last-3-yrs/699680/)).

## Origins and broadcast footprint

It has aired on the last Sunday of each month since April 2018 ([ThePrint via ANI](https://theprint.in/india/pm-modis-mann-ki-baat-positively-influences-citizenship-behaviour-over-100-crore-have-listened-to-programme-at-least-once-survey/1537044/); [NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/more-than-100-crore-people-listened-to-mann-ki-baat-at-least-once-study-3975334)). It runs across the entire All India Radio network, with a regional-language broadcast following the Hindi original, and is carried live on DD National, DD News, DD Bharati, DD India and DD Kisan ([IndianTelevision.com](https://old.indiantelevision.com/regulators/ib-ministry/no-policy-announcements-its-simply-mann-ki-baat-161206)). Minister of State Rajyavardhan Rathore told Parliament the programme carries no public policy announcements — listeners suggest topics and some recorded listener messages are woven into the broadcast ([IndianTelevision.com](https://old.indiantelevision.com/regulators/ib-ministry/no-policy-announcements-its-simply-mann-ki-baat-161206)). Its scale is unusual: beyond 34 Doordarshan channels, 91 private satellite TV channels have carried it; [Prasar Bharati](/organizations/prasar-bharati/) has re-broadcast it in 51 languages and dialects, and its CEO reported translation into 22 Indian languages, 29 dialects and 11 foreign languages, from French and Chinese to Baluchi and Swahili ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/why-air-doordarshans-revenue-from-pm-modis-mann-ki-baat-fell-by-90-in-the-last-3-yrs/699680/)). Doordarshan also broadcasts it in sign language ([ThePrint via ANI](https://theprint.in/india/pm-modis-mann-ki-baat-positively-influences-citizenship-behaviour-over-100-crore-have-listened-to-programme-at-least-once-survey/1537044/)).

## What the reach numbers say — and what they don't

An IIM-Rohtak listener-feedback study, released by Prasar Bharati CEO Gaurav Dwivedi ahead of the 100th episode, interviewed 10,003 people and reported nearly 96% awareness, more than 100 crore having heard it at least once, and 23 crore regular listeners or viewers ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/more-than-100-crore-people-listened-to-mann-ki-baat-at-least-once-study-3975334); [ThePrint via ANI](https://theprint.in/india/pm-modis-mann-ki-baat-positively-influences-citizenship-behaviour-over-100-crore-have-listened-to-programme-at-least-once-survey/1537044/)). The medium split is striking for a radio programme: 44.7% watched on television, 37.6% on mobile, 17.6% heard it over radio; 65% in Hindi, 18% in English ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/more-than-100-crore-people-listened-to-mann-ki-baat-at-least-once-study-3975334)). The 100th episode aired on 30 April 2023, marked by a government commemorative coin and an I&B ministry conclave on the programme's themes ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/more-than-100-crore-people-listened-to-mann-ki-baat-at-least-once-study-3975334)). The stat, source and implication: 23 crore regular listeners against over 100 crore ever-listeners (IIM-Rohtak), yet the independent Centre for the Study of Developing Societies found only about one in ten respondents had listened regularly in the previous year ([The New Indian Express](https://www.newindianexpress.com/opinions/2023/apr/28/mann-ki-baat-and-the-exercise-of-power-2570354.html)) — which implies the headline figures are best read as upper-bound awareness estimates, not measured monthly listenership.

## The listenership dispute

The disagreement between the two surveys is the most documented analytical fact about the programme's reach. CSDS's November 2022 "Media in India" report devoted a four-page section titled "PM's Mann Ki Baat: A Reality Check", finding that even in Hindi-speaking states popularity was low: in high-media-presence households, two-fifths had not heard it in the previous year and three in ten had heard it once or twice; 51% of BJP supporters and 68% of Congress supporters had never heard a single episode ([The New Indian Express](https://www.newindianexpress.com/opinions/2023/apr/28/mann-ki-baat-and-the-exercise-of-power-2570354.html)). Separate AIR figures obtained under the Right to Information Act and reported by NewsClick showed average all-India listenership falling from 30.82% in 2015 to 25.82% in 2016 to 22.67% in 2017 ([NewsClick](https://www.newsclick.in/modis-mann-ki-baat-was-flop-show-air-data-suggests-so)). Advertising revenue tells the same story of institutional investment, not commercial return: Rs 10.64 crore in 2017-18, only Rs 1.02 crore in 2020-21 — a roughly 90% fall — Rs 30.8 crore total since 2014 ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/why-air-doordarshans-revenue-from-pm-modis-mann-ki-baat-fell-by-90-in-the-last-3-yrs/699680/)).

## The 117th episode and "Krish, Trish and Baltiboy"

In the 117th episode (December 2024), the Prime Minister told listeners that "KTB means Krish, Trish and Baltiboy" — a favourite among children — whose three animated characters narrate stories of freedom-struggle men and women "who are not discussed much" ([Prime Minister's Office](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/pms-address-in-the-117th-episode-of-mann-ki-baat/)). He noted it airs in many Indian and foreign languages on Doordarshan and OTT platforms, and that its second season, "Bharat Hain Hum", launched at the [International Film Festival of India](/events/international-film-festival-of-india/) in Goa ([Prime Minister's Office](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/pms-address-in-the-117th-episode-of-mann-ki-baat/)). The Tribune independently confirmed the IFFI Goa launch, reporting that the [Ministry of Information and Broadcasting](/organizations/ministry-of-information-and-broadcasting/) launched the season there in 12 Indian and 7 international languages ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/entertainment/second-season-of-animated-series-krish-trish-and-baltiboy-bharat-hain-hum-launched-at-iffi/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Mann Ki Baat?**
It is the Prime Minister of India's monthly radio address, hosted by Narendra Modi since 3 October 2014, broadcast on All India Radio and carried visually on Doordarshan's television channels — a government programme, not a commercial media product.

**How often does it air and where can it be heard?**
On the last Sunday of each month, across the All India Radio network, Doordarshan National, DD News, DD Bharati, DD India and DD Kisan, 91 private satellite channels, the AIR mobile app, and a sign-language broadcast on Doordarshan.

**What did the 117th episode say about Krish, Trish and Baltiboy?**
The Prime Minister said the animated series — "KTB" — is a favourite among children, whose characters tell the stories of lesser-known freedom fighters; it airs in many Indian and foreign languages on Doordarshan and OTT platforms, with Season 2 launched at IFFI Goa.

**How many people actually listen to it?**
An IIM-Rohtak study found 23 crore regular listeners/viewers and over 100 crore ever-heard; an independent CSDS survey found only about one in ten had listened regularly in the previous year. The methods differ, so they cannot be merged into one figure.

**Does Mann Ki Baat make policy announcements?**
No. Minister of State for Information and Broadcasting Rajyavardhan Rathore told Parliament the broadcast carries no policy announcements; its stated role is outreach, not policymaking.

## What this profile does not claim

- It is not a commercial media product, podcast brand, or private-network show; it is a government radio broadcast produced within the Prime Minister's Office and aired through the public broadcaster.
- It does not reconcile the IIM-Rohtak and CSDS listenership findings into a single audience number; the two studies use incompatible methods, and the disagreement is reported, not resolved.
- It does not endorse the raw AIR RTI listenership percentages; they come from one outlet's report of an RTI response whose underlying document was not independently read.
- It does not assert a precise total episode count as of this writing; only the 100th (30 April 2023) and 117th (December 2024) episodes were fixed from citable sources in this pass.
- It does not repeat the "first visually enriched radio programme" description found in tertiary sources; no primary I&B statement to that effect was verified here.
- The PIB press release on the "Krish, Trish and Baltiboy" Season 2 launch was inaccessible (HTTP 403); nothing unique to that release is cited or claimed.
