**The Hunger Games: Catching Fire** is Francis Lawrence's 2013 dystopian
science-fiction action film, the second installment in *The Hunger Games* film
series. Starring Jennifer Lawrence as Katniss Everdeen, it adapts Suzanne
Collins' 2009 novel — the second book of the trilogy — and follows Katniss and
Peeta Mellark into the 75th Hunger Games, the Third Quarter Quell
([Scholastic](https://eu-shop.scholastic.co.uk/products/The-Hunger-Games-number-2-Catching-Fire-Suzanne-Collins-9781407132099);
[SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/528746/the-hunger-games-catching-fire-cost-50-million-more-than-the-original-plus-new-imax-poster/)).
It grossed $865 million worldwide, making it the franchise's biggest hit and
Lionsgate's highest-grossing film until 2026 ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/michael-jackson-movie-overtakes-oppenheimer-as-biggest-biopic-ever-with-1-billion-in-sight/)).

The film marked a changing of the guard behind the camera: Gary Ross, who
directed the first *Hunger Games*, turned the sequel down in April 2012, citing
the tight production schedule, and Francis Lawrence — of *I Am Legend* and
*Water for Elephants* — was officially named his successor on 3 May 2012
([Entertainment Tonight](http://etonline.com/movies/121489_Francis_Lawrence_to_Direct_Hunger_Games_Sequel_Catching_Fire/)).
Made for over $130 million against the original's $78 million, it opened in the
US on 22 November 2013 ([SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/528746/the-hunger-games-catching-fire-cost-50-million-more-than-the-original-plus-new-imax-poster/);
[Rotten Tomatoes](https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_hunger_games_catching_fire)).
With a 90/100 score on Rotten Tomatoes from 292 reviews, it is the best-reviewed
film of the franchise ([Rotten Tomatoes](https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_hunger_games_catching_fire)).

## The record run: Lionsgate's crown, 2013–2026

It opened to $158.1 million domestically — the biggest November opening ever and
the sixth-biggest opening on record, ahead of the *Twilight* November sequels
([Box Office Mojo](https://www.boxofficemojo.com/article/ed1266549764/)).
It also set the biggest three- and five-day Thanksgiving box-office totals ever
([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/bwwtv/article/THE-HUNGER-GAMES-CATCHING-FIRE-Tops-400-Million-at-Domestic-Box-Office-Now-2013s-1-FILM-20140110)).
By January 2014 it was the highest-grossing domestic film of 2013 — the first
2D film since 2008 to top the yearly chart — and the first franchise with its
first two installments each over $400 million domestic
([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/bwwtv/article/THE-HUNGER-GAMES-CATCHING-FIRE-Tops-400-Million-at-Domestic-Box-Office-Now-2013s-1-FILM-20140110)).
Worldwide it finished at $865 million — the franchise best and Lionsgate's
highest-grossing film of all time ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/michael-jackson-movie-overtakes-oppenheimer-as-biggest-biopic-ever-with-1-billion-in-sight/)).
It held the studio crown for 13 years, until *Michael* passed it at $977 million
on 28 June 2026 (Variety via MBW) on its way to $1.001 billion — Lionsgate's
first billion-dollar release ([Telegraph India](https://www.telegraphindia.com/entertainment/michael-becomes-lionsgates-highest-grossing-film-ever-inches-closer-to-900-million-mark/cid/2164502);
[Michael](/creative-works/michael-2026-film/)).
The stat, source and implication: $865 million on a $130M-plus budget (THR via
SlashFilm) — a better-than-6x return that made one sequel Lionsgate's most
valuable asset for over a decade.

## How it was made: a new director and a bigger arena

When Gary Ross bowed out in April 2012 — his written statement cited the tight
schedule, with Lionsgate targeting an August production start — the studio moved
fast, officially naming Francis Lawrence on 3 May ([Entertainment Tonight](http://etonline.com/movies/121489_Francis_Lawrence_to_Direct_Hunger_Games_Sequel_Catching_Fire/)).
The budget followed the ambition: over $130 million against the original's $78
million, with IMAX cameras added for the arena spectacle (THR via SlashFilm).
Jennifer Lawrence's salary jumped from $500,000 to $10 million, with her co-stars raised too ([SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/528746/the-hunger-games-catching-fire-cost-50-million-more-than-the-original-plus-new-imax-poster/)).
The screenplay came from Simon Beaufoy and Michael Arndt, and the new cast added
Philip Seymour Hoffman as Plutarch Heavensbee, Sam Claflin as Finnick Odair, Jena
Malone as Johanna Mason and Jeffrey Wright as Beetee ([SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/528746/the-hunger-games-catching-fire-cost-50-million-more-than-the-original-plus-new-imax-poster/);
[TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/hunger-games-catching-fire-ready-blaze-box-office-record-book/)).
Producers Nina Jacobson (Color Force) and Jon Kilik returned; the source
novel — the trilogy's second book — had debuted at No. 1 on the *USA Today*,
*New York Times*, *Wall Street Journal* and *Publishers Weekly* bestseller
lists and was a *Time* Top Ten Fiction Book of 2009
([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/bwwtv/article/THE-HUNGER-GAMES-CATCHING-FIRE-Tops-400-Million-at-Domestic-Box-Office-Now-2013s-1-FILM-20140110);
[Scholastic](https://eu-shop.scholastic.co.uk/products/The-Hunger-Games-number-2-Catching-Fire-Suzanne-Collins-9781407132099)).

## The best-reviewed Hunger Games film

Early reviews ran 93% positive on Rotten Tomatoes with a 78 on Metacritic —
better than the original's — and the finished consensus reads: "Smart, smoothly
directed, and enriched with a deeper exploration of the franchise's
thought-provoking themes, *Catching Fire* proves a thoroughly compelling second
installment in the *Hunger Games* series" ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/hunger-games-catching-fire-ready-blaze-box-office-record-book/);
[Rotten Tomatoes](https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_hunger_games_catching_fire)).
The film's current 90/100 from 292 reviews tops the franchise on the same page:
*The Hunger Games* at 84%, each *Mockingjay* at 70% ([Rotten Tomatoes](https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_hunger_games_catching_fire)).
The story picks up with Katniss and Peeta's Victor's Tour after the 74th Games,
as a simmering rebellion meets President Snow's announcement of the 75th Hunger
Games — the Third Quarter Quell, drawing tributes from the existing pool of
victors ([SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/528746/the-hunger-games-catching-fire-cost-50-million-more-than-the-original-plus-new-imax-poster/);
[Rotten Tomatoes](https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_hunger_games_catching_fire)).
Rated PG-13, it runs 2 hours 26 minutes and was released theatrically in the US
on 22 November 2013 through Lionsgate ([Rotten Tomatoes](https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_hunger_games_catching_fire)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is *Catching Fire* still Lionsgate's highest-grossing film?**
No. *Michael* passed it at $977 million on 28 June 2026 (Variety via MBW), ending a 13-year run, and went on to $1.001 billion — Lionsgate's first billion-dollar release. Catching Fire's $865 million remains the franchise best and was the studio's all-time top film from 2013 to 2026.

**Why didn't Gary Ross direct *Catching Fire*?**
Ross turned the sequel down in April 2012, citing the tight production schedule — Lionsgate wanted to start shooting in August. Francis Lawrence, director of *I Am Legend* and *Water for Elephants*, was officially named his successor on 3 May 2012.

**What is the Quarter Quell in *Catching Fire*?**
The 75th Annual Hunger Games: a special edition of the Games with modified rules. In *Catching Fire*, the twist forces previous victors — including Katniss and Peeta — back into the arena, raising the stakes as rebellion simmers in the districts.

**Is *Catching Fire* the best-reviewed Hunger Games movie?**
Yes. It holds 90/100 on Rotten Tomatoes from 292 reviews, ahead of *The Hunger Games* (84%) and both *Mockingjay* films (70% each). The site's Critics Consensus calls it "smart, smoothly directed" with "a deeper exploration of the franchise's thought-provoking themes."
