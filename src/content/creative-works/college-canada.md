## Overview

*College Canada* was the flagship newsmagazine of the [Association of Canadian Community Colleges](/organizations/colleges-and-institutes-canada/) (ACCC), now Colleges and Institutes Canada (CICan). Launched in October 1976 as a quarterly of the association's Canadian Studies Bureau, it documented college–industry research partnerships, was shut down by about 1990, then reappeared as an annual newsmagazine — volumes 6 and 7 in 2001 and 2002 — and was still being handed to Canadian senators in 2007 ([Colleges and Institutes Canada](https://www.collegesinstitutes.ca/cicans-50th-anniversary/); [Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/home/record?idnumber=6218575&app=FonAndCol)).

The magazine matters because it is the paper trail of a long fight: for decades Canada's federal research money went almost exclusively to universities, while colleges — the institutions training the skilled workforce — were shut out of the major granting councils. *College Canada* recorded the applied research colleges were doing anyway, years before Ottawa relented ([Colleges and Institutes Canada](https://www.collegesinstitutes.ca/cicans-50th-anniversary/advocacy/)). It is not the "College Canada '75" conference, a Vancouver gathering that used the name a year earlier, nor the separate journal *College Quarterly*.

## A Canadian Studies quarterly, 1976–c.1990

### Launch under the Kellogg grant

*College Canada* began with American money and a Canadian-studies mission. In 1976 the ACCC received critical funding from the [Kellogg Foundation](/organizations/w-k-kellogg-foundation/) under "PROJECT BREAKTHROUGH" and used it to establish the Canadian Studies Bureau, the International Bureau, a series of mini-conferences — and its quarterly publication, *COLLEGE CANADA* ([Colleges and Institutes Canada](https://www.collegesinstitutes.ca/cicans-50th-anniversary/)). The launch came four years after the association itself was formally established in 1972 ([Colleges and Institutes Canada](https://www.collegesinstitutes.ca/cicans-50th-anniversary/)). Library and Archives [Canada](/places/canada/) holds the evidence of the launch: Vol. 1, No. 1 from October 1976 and Vol. 1, No. 2 from November 1976, filed in the ACCC fonds ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/home/record?idnumber=6218575&app=FonAndCol)). The Bureau's journals were a small, volunteer-reliant operation promoting Canadian Studies programs "from coast to coast (and almost) to coast", in the words of a contributor who edited them ([College Quarterly](http://collegequarterly.ca/2012-vol15-num04-fall/doughty4.html)).

### The turn to applied research

The magazine's editorial turn came in the mid-1980s, when the ACCC began pressing for college access to federal research funding. In 1986 and 1987 the flagship publication was already focusing on "the vital relationships between colleges and local industry partners, and the value that these applied research initiatives provided to their communities, to students, and to industry" ([Colleges and Institutes Canada](https://www.collegesinstitutes.ca/cicans-50th-anniversary/advocacy/)). It also carried editorial op-eds on Indigenous education in the 1980s, part of the association's early advocacy for Indigenous learners ([Colleges and Institutes Canada](https://www.collegesinstitutes.ca/cicans-50th-anniversary/advocacy/)). But the Canadian Studies era was ending: as the ACCC grew more entrepreneurial and moved toward international work, it shut down its early publications, and *College Canada* "was the last to go" by about 1987 ([College Quarterly](http://collegequarterly.ca/1995-vol02-num04-summer/doughty.html)).

## The annual newsmagazine, 2001–2007

### A newsmagazine returns

A magazine with the same title, the same publisher and the same ISSN reappeared at the turn of the millennium — but no source says whether it was a relaunch or a quiet continuation, so the two eras are best treated separately. Volume 6, Issue 1 (Fall/Winter 2001) announced itself on the masthead as "The Newsmagazine of the Association of Canadian Community Colleges", published once a year, provided to all member institutions through ACCC membership fees, and intended to inform "the college and institute community and its partners of the Association's activities" ([Association of Canadian Community Colleges](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA41153605_0636/micro_IA41153605_0636.pdf)). Its masthead names Chair Pat Felker, President Gerald Brown, Editor-in-Chief Lorna Malcolmson and French Editor Debby Wilson, with the association's office at 1223 Michael Street North in Ottawa ([Association of Canadian Community Colleges](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA41153605_0636/micro_IA41153605_0636.pdf)).

### Still in senators' kits in 2007

Volume 7, No. 1 (Fall/Winter 2002) kept the same editor, the same ISSN 1203-4789 and the same bilingual format, running 62 pages against the 2001 issue's 82 ([ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED469885)). The magazine was still in active use five years later: on 4 December 2007, ACCC Vice-President Terry Anne Boyles — a former college president in Saskatoon — told the Senate Committee on Agriculture and Forestry in Ottawa, at its hearings on rural poverty in Canada, that senators' kits contained "various issues of our *College Canada* magazine" showcasing how colleges and communities worked together on poverty alleviation and community sustainability ([Senate of Canada](https://sencanada.ca/en/Content/SEN/Committee/392/agri/02eva-e)). She was speaking for an association of more than 150 colleges, institutes of technology, polytechnics, university-colleges and CEGEPs, with campuses in at least 1,000 communities ([Senate of Canada](https://sencanada.ca/en/Content/SEN/Committee/392/agri/02eva-e)).

## What the issues actually covered

### The rural-colleges issue, 2001

The two surviving later issues show a magazine organized around one theme per year, with each issue assembling case studies from across the country. The 2001 issue took rural and remote colleges as its theme: Michael Hall on anchoring community colleges in cyberspace, Brent Mills on the National Task Force on Rural and Remote Colleges, Pamela Walsh on the College of the North Atlantic in Newfoundland and Labrador, Lynden Johnson on the Canadian Rural Partnership, Gilles Bacon on regional colleges in Québec, Fay Myers on Parkland Regional College in Saskatchewan, and Terry Weninger revisiting the 1997 British Columbia Small Colleges Report ([Association of Canadian Community Colleges](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA41153605_0636/micro_IA41153605_0636.pdf)). Its editorial argued that colleges in 900 communities were the hub of local socioeconomic wellbeing, not just training providers ([Association of Canadian Community Colleges](https://archive.org/download/micro_IA41153605_0636/micro_IA41153605_0636.pdf)).

### Workplace learning, 2002

The 2002 issue turned to workplace learning: Perrin Beatty on industry and colleges as key partners in meeting the skills challenge, Conrad Murphy and Michael Herzog on the TOWES workplace-skills test, Donna Allan reporting from Lethbridge Community College, Michael Kidney and Sue Boutlier on training technicians who fix the space shuttle, Bea Clark chronicling the General Motors of Canada–Niagara College partnership, and Chantale Perreault on transferring applied research to the workplace through training in a new degree program ([ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED469885)). The numbers behind that industry-partnership story were striking: in a CICan-sponsored survey in 1988, respondents submitted 286 proposals for research projects and 88% said they wanted to pursue them ([Colleges and Institutes Canada](https://www.collegesinstitutes.ca/cicans-50th-anniversary/advocacy/)), which means the demand for college research was documented years before federal funding followed.

## FAQ

### What was College Canada?

*College Canada* was the flagship newsmagazine of the Association of Canadian Community Colleges (ACCC), now Colleges and Institutes Canada. Launched in October 1976 as a quarterly of the association's Canadian Studies Bureau, it later became an annual newsmagazine documenting college–industry partnerships, applied research and community development across Canada's college system.

### Who published College Canada?

The Association of Canadian Community Colleges published it from Ottawa, Ontario, with Lorna Malcolmson as editor-in-chief of the 2001 and 2002 issues. The association rebranded as Colleges and Institutes Canada (CICan) in 2014, and its 50th-anniversary history calls *College Canada* its flagship publication.

### When did College Canada run?

It ran in two eras that may or may not connect: a quarterly launched in October 1976 that was shut down by about 1987, with all such ACCC journals gone by 1990; and an annual newsmagazine with volumes 6 and 7 in 2001 and 2002, still being distributed to senators in December 2007. No source states a relaunch date or an end date.

### What did College Canada cover?

College–industry research partnerships, workplace learning and vocational education, rural and remote colleges, and community economic development. The 2001 issue covered rural colleges from Newfoundland and Labrador to British Columbia; the 2002 issue covered skills partnerships including General Motors and Niagara College.

### Is College Canada the same as the "College Canada '75" conference?

No. "College Canada '75" was the ACCC's 1975 annual conference in Vancouver, attended by almost 1,000 delegates — it used the name a year before the magazine's first issue appeared in October 1976. The magazine is also distinct from the journal *College Quarterly*, whose editor wrote the first-hand accounts of the magazine's early years used in this profile.
