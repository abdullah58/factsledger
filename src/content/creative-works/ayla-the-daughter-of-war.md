# Ayla: The Daughter of War

## The film that became a state-backed Oscar campaign and a soft-power case study

What nobody on the first page of search results tells you about *Ayla: The Daughter of War* is how big it was at home — and what it became afterward. In [Turkey](/places/turkey/), *Ayla* was the second most popular film of 2017, selling more than five million tickets, with financial support from the [Turkish Culture and Tourism Ministry](/organizations/turkish-culture-and-tourism-ministry/) [^1^]. Its bid for the [Academy Award](/organizations/academy-awards/) for Best Foreign Language Film was not a quiet submission: [Turkish Airlines](/organizations/turkish-airlines/) served as the main sponsor of the Oscar public-relations campaign, with the Culture and Tourism Ministry as a main supporter, funding a campaign that took the film to [South Korea](/places/south-korea/) in October 2017 — timed to what Hürriyet Daily News described as the 60th anniversary of Turkish soldiers landing in [Busan](/places/busan/) — and then to [Los Angeles](/places/los-angeles/) [^2^]. And eight years later, the 2025 [Film Diplomacy Conference](/events/film-diplomacy-conference/) in [Istanbul](/places/istanbul/) presented *Ayla* as a successful application of film diplomacy, crediting it with tangibly improving cultural and diplomatic relations between Türkiye and South Korea [^3^].

## The true story

The film is adapted from the true story of Turkish sergeant [Süleyman Dilbirliği](/people/suleyman-dilbirligi/), who served with the Turkish brigade under [United Nations](/organizations/united-nations/) command in the [Korean War](/events/korean-war/) of 1950–1953 [^4^]. During the war, Dilbirliği found a five-year-old girl who had lost both her parents and took her under his wing, naming her Ayla — Turkish for moonlight. After about fifteen months together, his brigade was ordered home; he fought the Korean bureaucracy to take her with him and failed, and Ayla was placed in an orphanage [^2^][^4^].

The story only became public decades later. During the Korean President's visit in 2010, Turkey's state broadcaster [TRT](/organizations/trt/) produced a documentary about Turkish soldiers in the Korean War, and Dilbirliği's story made headlines; he and Ayla — by then [Kim Eun-ja](/people/kim-eun-ja/) — were reunited in tears and spoke about what they had lived through [^2^]. After that first 2010 reunion, Kim told reporters, they met two or three times and wrote letters when they could not meet [^4^]. In November 2017, with Dilbirliği 91 and in an Istanbul hospital, Kim flew in to visit her foster father; witnesses said a man who had stopped reacting to anything responded when she took his hands [^4^].

## Production and release

Directed by [Can Ülkay](/people/can-ulkay/) and written by [Yiğit Güralp](/people/yigit-guralp/), the film stars [İsmail Hacıoğlu](/people/ismail-hacioglu/) as the young Dilbirliği and [Çetin Tekindor](/people/cetin-tekindor/) as the elderly one, with Korean actresses [Kim Seol](/people/kim-seol/) and [Lee Kyung-jin](/people/lee-kyung-jin/) as the young and adult Ayla; [Cade Carradine](/people/cade-carradine/) and [Eric Roberts](/people/eric-roberts/) appear in supporting roles [^2^][^5^]. Under Academy rules it had a limited screening in Turkey on 8 September 2017 before its Turkish theatrical release on 27 October 2017 [^2^][^6^].

Not everything behind the scenes was harmonious. Güralp, the screenwriter, publicly criticized the production team for blocking him on social media and keeping him away from the entire PR process, writing on Instagram that "for four years, I did not have a single, peaceful, healthy day" — adding, "'Ayla' belongs to the audience now" [^2^].
