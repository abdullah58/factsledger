## The film that became a state-backed Oscar campaign and a soft-power case study

In [Turkey](/places/turkey/), *Ayla* was the second most popular film of 2017, selling more than five million tickets, with financial support from the [Turkish Culture and Tourism Ministry](/organizations/turkish-culture-and-tourism-ministry/) [^1^]. Its [Academy Award](/organizations/academy-awards/) submission also had a promotional campaign. A September report identified [Turkish Airlines](/organizations/turkish-airlines/) as main sponsor and the ministry as a supporter, and announced plans for visits to [South Korea](/places/south-korea/), mentioning [Busan](/places/busan/), and [Los Angeles](/places/los-angeles/) [^2^]. Eight years later, the 2025 [Film Diplomacy Conference](/events/film-diplomacy-conference/) in [Istanbul](/places/istanbul/) presented *Ayla* as a successful application of film diplomacy, crediting it with tangibly improving cultural and diplomatic relations between Türkiye and South Korea [^3^].

## The true story

The film is adapted from the true story of Turkish sergeant [Süleyman Dilbirliği](/people/suleyman-dilbirligi/), who served with the Turkish brigade under [United Nations](/organizations/united-nations/) command in the [Korean War](/events/korean-war/) of 1950–1953 [^4^]. During the war, Dilbirliği found a five-year-old girl who had lost both her parents and took her under his wing, naming her Ayla — Turkish for moonlight. After about fifteen months together, his brigade was ordered home; he fought the Korean bureaucracy to take her with him and failed, and Ayla was placed in an orphanage [^2^][^4^].

The story only became public decades later. During the Korean President's visit in 2010, Turkey's state broadcaster [TRT](/organizations/trt/) produced a documentary about Turkish soldiers in the Korean War, and Dilbirliği's story made headlines; he and Ayla — by then [Kim Eun-ja](/people/kim-eun-ja/) — were reunited in tears and spoke about what they had lived through [^2^]. After that first 2010 reunion, Kim told reporters, they met two or three times and wrote letters when they could not meet [^4^]. In November 2017, with Dilbirliği 91 and in an Istanbul hospital, Kim flew in to visit her foster father; witnesses said a man who had stopped reacting to anything responded when she took his hands [^4^].

## Production and release

Directed by [Can Ülkay](/people/can-ulkay/) and written by [Yiğit Güralp](/people/yigit-guralp/), the film stars [İsmail Hacıoğlu](/people/ismail-hacioglu/) as the young Dilbirliği and [Çetin Tekindor](/people/cetin-tekindor/) as the elderly one, with Korean actresses [Kim Seol](/people/kim-seol/) and [Lee Kyung-jin](/people/lee-kyung-jin/) as the young and adult Ayla; [Cade Carradine](/people/cade-carradine/) and [Eric Roberts](/people/eric-roberts/) appear in supporting roles [^2^][^5^]. The September report announced a limited screening for 8 September; Box Office Türkiye records 27 October as the first theatrical release date [^2^][^6^]. On 28 October, Daily Sabah confirmed that the film had opened that week [^5^].

The box-office reporting periods also need separating. Box Office Türkiye lists a second release on 15 June 2018 and cumulative totals over 46 weeks of 5,589,872 admissions and ₺66,055,256 in gross revenue. Those cumulative figures are not a 2017-only total, and the lira gross is not converted into a dollar estimate here [^6^].

Not everything behind the scenes was harmonious. Güralp, the screenwriter, publicly criticized the production team for blocking him on social media and keeping him away from the entire PR process, writing on Instagram that "for four years, I did not have a single, peaceful, healthy day" — adding, "'Ayla' belongs to the audience now" [^2^].

## What this profile does not claim

- The film was Turkey's official entry for Best Foreign Language Film at the [90th Academy Awards](/events/90th-academy-awards/); it was not nominated [^4^].
- A dollar box-office figure is deliberately omitted: aggregator sites publish conflicting numbers, while the verified commercial fact is the ministry-reported ticket count of over five million [^1^].
- This profile does not repeat filming locations, runtime, or festival-premiere details, because the only pages stating them were not personally verified for this profile.
