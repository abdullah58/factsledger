## Overview

Adobe XD was [Adobe](/organizations/adobe/)'s user-interface and user-experience design tool, previewed in March 2016 as a fast, purpose-built way to design app and web interfaces and wire them into clickable prototypes — and it is the rare design tool whose real-world story matters more than its features ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/16/03/15/adobes-experience-design-cc-brings-app-web-ux-and-prototyping-to-mac)). By 2023 XD had become a regulatory exhibit: the product Adobe itself pointed to when it told antitrust regulators it did not meaningfully compete with [Figma](/organizations/figma/), a standalone app that had lost $25 million over three years ([Reuters](https://www.brandiconimage.com/2023/12/adobe-figma-end-20-billion-acquisition.html)). When Adobe's $20 billion Figma acquisition collapsed that December, XD was left in maintenance mode, and in January 2024 Adobe confirmed it had "no plans to further invest" in the product ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2024/01/31/adobe-no-plans-to-invest-in-xd-despite-its-figma-failure/1119427)).

## Project Comet becomes XD

XD began life under the name Project Comet, demonstrated at Adobe MAX in October 2015, and was released as Adobe Experience Design CC (Preview) on March 14, 2016, open to anyone with an Adobe ID ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/16/03/15/adobes-experience-design-cc-brings-app-web-ux-and-prototyping-to-mac)). It was a mobile-app and webpage user-experience and prototyping tool with a streamlined interface, hooks into Adobe's Creative Cloud products and numerous preview options ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/16/03/15/adobes-experience-design-cc-brings-app-web-ux-and-prototyping-to-mac)). It came with user-interface assets built in for the major mobile operating systems of the day — iOS, Android and Windows Phone, including fonts, buttons, images and colour palettes — and supported [Illustrator](/creative-works/illustrator/) CC and [Photoshop](/creative-works/photoshop/) CC files, so designers could move work between Adobe's established tools and the new one ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/16/03/15/adobes-experience-design-cc-brings-app-web-ux-and-prototyping-to-mac)).

The workflow XD sold was speed through simplification. A demonstration at launch built an iOS app mockup, complete with graphics, text, links, transitions and more, in about ten minutes from a blank canvas ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/16/03/15/adobes-experience-design-cc-brings-app-web-ux-and-prototyping-to-mac)). Drag-and-drop auto-masking image boxes required far fewer steps than manually inserting, measuring and masking images; lists auto-populated; and wireframing worked by drawing a wire from one on-screen asset, such as a button or image, to a second page, with transitions such as page slides to make the prototype feel like a real app ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/16/03/15/adobes-experience-design-cc-brings-app-web-ux-and-prototyping-to-mac)). A dedicated prototype mode let designers test interactive hotspots, while a desktop preview mode showed changes in real time, and a finished prototype could be posted to the web and shared for remote assessment ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/16/03/15/adobes-experience-design-cc-brings-app-web-ux-and-prototyping-to-mac)). Adobe expected a shipping version later in 2016, with companion apps for iOS and Android and deeper Photoshop and Illustrator integration to follow ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/16/03/15/adobes-experience-design-cc-brings-app-web-ux-and-prototyping-to-mac)).

XD launched into a market that was already moving past it. Figma, which had launched its flagship browser-based design program in late 2015, let teams collaborate on interface projects entirely in the browser ([VC News Daily](https://vcnewsdaily.com/access/getarticle.php?aid=kqbvzbqffz)), while XD remained a desktop program tied to Adobe's ecosystem. The competition was not decided by feature checklists — XD could prototype, wireframe and preview capably — but by where design work happened, and design work was moving into the browser.

## The $25 million exhibit

When Adobe agreed in September 2022 to buy Figma for about $20 billion, XD became central to the antitrust argument in an unexpected way: as proof that Adobe was not a real competitor. Adobe argued it did not compete with Figma in any meaningful way, and said in November 2023 that its only product relevant to the antitrust question was Adobe XD, "which lost $25 million as a standalone app over the last three years" ([Reuters](https://www.brandiconimage.com/2023/12/adobe-figma-end-20-billion-acquisition.html)). The scale matched the rest of the portrait: Adobe general counsel Dana Rao told TechCrunch in 2022 that XD generated no more than $15 million to $17 million a year in revenue and had just a handful of full-time employees working on it ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2024/01/31/adobe-no-plans-to-invest-in-xd-despite-its-figma-failure/1119427)).

The regulators were not persuaded. Britain's [Competition and Markets Authority](/organizations/competition-and-markets-authority/) said the deal would harm innovation for software used by the vast majority of UK digital designers, echoing similar concerns from the [European Union](/organizations/european-union/)'s Commission about reduced competition ([Reuters](https://www.brandiconimage.com/2023/12/adobe-figma-end-20-billion-acquisition.html)). British regulators had indicated they would require remedies for Adobe to divest Figma Design, a core asset of the acquisition, and Adobe had refused to offer fixes to the CMA on grounds that no remedy preserving the deal's benefits would satisfy its concerns ([Reuters](https://www.brandiconimage.com/2023/12/adobe-figma-end-20-billion-acquisition.html)). On December 18, 2023, the companies terminated the deal for lack of a clear path to approval, and Adobe paid Figma a $1 billion termination fee ([Reuters](https://www.brandiconimage.com/2023/12/adobe-figma-end-20-billion-acquisition.html)).

## Maintenance mode, and no way back

XD's fate was sealed before the deal's collapse, not by it — at least on Adobe's account. XD went into maintenance mode in May 2023, which some developers thought was in anticipation of the Figma purchase being waved through, but Adobe told The Register it had disinvested in XD years before any Figma agreement was reached ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2024/01/31/adobe-no-plans-to-invest-in-xd-despite-its-figma-failure/1119427)). In January 2024, after the deal's failure, Adobe confirmed the position in the bluntest terms: "Adobe XD is currently in maintenance mode to support the needs of existing customers," a spokesperson said, and "Adobe disinvested [in XD] years before an agreement was reached on Figma. It is no longer being sold as a single application to new customers, and Adobe has no plans to further invest in the product" ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2024/01/31/adobe-no-plans-to-invest-in-xd-despite-its-figma-failure/1119427)).

Adobe's own published wording for customers was equally final: "We want to remind you that Adobe XD continues to be in maintenance mode. This means that we're not investing in ongoing development or shipping new features within the product. We continue to support existing customers by addressing bugs and updating any security or privacy needs while in maintenance mode" ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2024/01/31/adobe-no-plans-to-invest-in-xd-despite-its-figma-failure/1119427)). XD was no longer available for purchase as a single application, remaining available only to existing users through Adobe's All Apps subscription — a point Adobe's own support staff confirmed to users in June 2023. The irony noted by developers at the time was sharp: the deal Adobe had pursued to dominate interface design had been killed partly because regulators feared a monopoly, leaving the market open to Figma — the company Adobe had tried to buy — while Adobe's own challenger sat in maintenance mode with no investment coming.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim XD is formally discontinued or give a final version or end-of-support date: no source read in full verified one, and Adobe's stated position as of January 2024 is maintenance mode for existing customers, not shutdown. It does not assert the "five full-time staff" figure as independently confirmed; it reports the $25 million loss and Rao's "handful" wording as best attested, and flags the five-staff variant in the open items. It does not accept developer speculation that maintenance mode began "in anticipation of the Figma buy" as Adobe's reason, since Adobe's stated position is the opposite chronology. It does not profile Creative Cloud pricing, bundles or subscription terms, which are out of scope, and it does not claim XD failed for any single technical reason: the sources establish a competitive and regulatory story — a desktop tool overtaken by a browser-native rival, then disinvested — not a verdict on XD's design quality, which contemporary launch coverage described as fast and capable.
