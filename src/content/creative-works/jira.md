## Overview

Jira is [Atlassian](/organizations/atlassian/)'s issue-tracking and agile project-management software, first released in 2002 as a bug tracker for software developers and now the company's flagship work-management product, used by more than 300,000 of Atlassian's customers ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/the-next-era-of-jira)). Its name is a play on Gojira, the Japanese name for Godzilla, nodding to Bugzilla, the bug tracker Atlassian's developers used before building their own ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/atlassian-founders-20-years-20-lessons)). Since May 2024 it has been a single product covering both software and business teams, after Jira Software and Jira Work Management were merged ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/the-next-era-of-jira)).

Jira is not Atlassian itself — it is the product that made the company. Atlassian began in 2002 as a services business supporting other companies' customer-support teams, and Jira was the internal tool its founders built because the tracker they were using was not good enough ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/atlassian-founders-20-years-20-lessons)). Over two decades it grew from a developer bug tracker into a family of products spanning software delivery, IT service management and enterprise agile planning, survived a two-week outage in 2022 that deleted hundreds of customer sites, and is now being moved entirely to the cloud, with its last self-managed edition ending in 2029.

## From bug tracker to platform

Atlassian was founded in 2002 by Mike Cannon-Brookes and Scott Farquhar, two graduates of the University of New South Wales, who started out providing customer-support services to other companies. The bug-tracking software they were using was poor, so they built their own tracker, called it JIRA, and quickly decided they were better off selling Jira than providing support services ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/atlassian-founders-20-years-20-lessons)). The name was a deliberate joke: Bugzilla was the leading bug tracker of the day, and Jira is a play on "Gojira," the Japanese title for the original 1954 Godzilla movie ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/atlassian-founders-20-years-20-lessons)).

The founders chose to sell software rather than build a sales team, betting on a self-service purchase experience even though most of their target market was across the Pacific in the [United States](/places/united-states/). According to company lore, the bet paid off in 2003, when they woke one morning to find an order from American Airlines lying on the fax machine — a customer that had simply found Jira on Atlassian's website and bought it ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/atlassian-founders-20-years-20-lessons)). The same account records Atlassian passing 200,000 customers by 2022.

Growth came with acquisitions that extended Jira beyond software teams. Atlassian bought Trello for $425 million in 2017 and incident-management company Opsgenie for $295 million in 2018 ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1721768/atlassian-targets-agile-development-at-scale-with-jira-align.html)). Jira Service Management, the service-desk product built on Jira, originated in Atlassian's ShipIt quarterly hackathon tradition and was, by 2022, Atlassian's fastest-growing product ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/atlassian-founders-20-years-20-lessons)). In March 2020 Atlassian shut all its global offices during the [COVID-19 pandemic](/events/covid-19-pandemic/) and declared that team members could work from an office, from home, or hybrid going forward, on the view that distributed work would endure ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/atlassian-founders-20-years-20-lessons)).

## The Jira family

By 2019 Jira had become a family of products rather than a single tool, and Atlassian filled the last gap — planning at enterprise scale — by acquisition. In March 2019 it agreed to buy AgileCraft, a provider of enterprise agile planning software founded in 2013, for approximately $166 million; the deal closed in early April, and at the company's Summit in Las Vegas that month AgileCraft was rebranded Jira Align ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1721768/atlassian-targets-agile-development-at-scale-with-jira-align.html); [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/softwaredev/atlassian-announces-jira-align-and-confluence-opsgenie-updates/)). Jira Align connects business strategy to the work of engineering teams, supporting scaled-agile frameworks including SAFe, LeSS and Spotify, and was aimed at Atlassian's 1,000 largest customers — companies with more than 500 developers — while the existing Portfolio for Jira stayed positioned for small- to mid-sized businesses ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1721768/atlassian-targets-agile-development-at-scale-with-jira-align.html)). [Gartner](/organizations/gartner/) research director Thomas Murphy said at the time that Jira Align filled a "gap in the communication" with the C-suite and helped Atlassian take a leadership position in enterprise agile ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1721768/atlassian-targets-agile-development-at-scale-with-jira-align.html)).

| Product | Who it is for | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jira | Software and business teams | Backlogs, sprints, Scrum and Kanban boards, timelines, goals and reporting |
| Jira Service Management | IT and service teams | Service desk, incident and change management, asset management |
| Jira Align | Enterprise leadership | Connects team-level work to business strategy and portfolio planning |

In May 2024 Atlassian simplified the lineup, merging Jira Software and Jira Work Management — the business-team edition — into a single product called simply Jira, with no pricing change for existing Jira Software subscribers ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/the-next-era-of-jira)). The unified product added goal tracking, AI-powered work breakdown and automation through Atlassian Intelligence, list views for every project, shared release dates in calendars, and deeper integrations with Confluence and Loom ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/the-next-era-of-jira)). [Roblox](/organizations/roblox/) saved more than $150,000 a year by consolidating multiple tools into Jira ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/the-next-era-of-jira)). Atlassian has since begun renaming Jira's core vocabulary, with "issues" becoming "work items" and "projects" becoming "spaces" ([simplify-software.org](https://simplify-software.org/blog/is-jira-being-phased-out)).

## Scale and market position

Jira's scale is best read as a series of snapshots. Atlassian reported more than 200,000 customers in 2022, more than 290,000 when Server support ended in February 2024 — including [NASA](/organizations/nasa/), Reddit, Rivian and PayPal — and more than 300,000 by September 2025, when it said 99% of its customers already used its cloud platform ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/atlassian-founders-20-years-20-lessons); [Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/company-news/farewell-to-server); [simplify-software.org](https://simplify-software.org/blog/is-jira-being-phased-out)). By February 2024, 94% of all Atlassian customers had a Jira Software, Confluence and/or Jira Service Management presence on Cloud ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/company-news/farewell-to-server)).

That dominance is exactly what rivals pitch against. [ClickUp](/organizations/clickup/) sells itself as a single platform to replace the "polyglot mixture" of task managers, email clients, chat apps, time trackers, and tools like Atlassian's Jira — a positioning that only makes sense because Jira is the incumbent in the category. Jira's breadth is also its most common criticism: teams praise its custom workflows and reporting while complaining that the same configurability makes it heavy to administer, a tension Atlassian's 2024 unification and AI features were explicitly designed to ease ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/the-next-era-of-jira)).

## The April 2022 outage

Jira's worst incident came not from an attack but from a routine cleanup. On Tuesday, April 5, 2022, starting at 07:38 UTC, a maintenance script deleted 883 customer cloud sites representing 775 customers ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/technology/atlassian-two-week-long-cloud-outage-impacted-775-customers/)). The script was meant to remove a legacy standalone app, Insight – Asset Management, which had been folded into Jira Service Management in 2021; instead of the app's IDs, the team running the deletion was given the IDs of the entire cloud sites where the app was installed, and the script ran in permanent-deletion mode rather than mark-for-deletion ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/technology/atlassian-two-week-long-cloud-outage-impacted-775-customers/)).

The first affected customers were restored on April 8, and all sites were progressively restored by April 18 — up to 14 days offline for a subset ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/technology/atlassian-two-week-long-cloud-outage-impacted-775-customers/)). The entire Jira family, Confluence, Atlassian Access, Opsgenie and Statuspage were unavailable to those customers. Atlassian said the incident was not a cyberattack, that there was no unauthorized access to customer data, that no customer lost more than five minutes of data, and that over 99.6% of its customers were undisrupted ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/technology/atlassian-two-week-long-cloud-outage-impacted-775-customers/)). The company first estimated about 400 affected customers out of more than 200,000 cloud customers, then nearly doubled the figure to 775 after including inactive, free and small accounts; its CTO, Sri Viswanath, said Atlassian should have communicated what it knew earlier instead of waiting for a full picture ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/technology/atlassian-two-week-long-cloud-outage-impacted-775-customers/)).

## The end of self-managed Jira

The outage landed in the middle of Atlassian's move to the cloud. In October 2020 the company announced it would stop selling licences for on-premises products from February 2021 ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/technology/atlassian-two-week-long-cloud-outage-impacted-775-customers/)), and Server support ended on February 15, 2024 ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/company-news/farewell-to-server)). One detail is unsettled: BleepingComputer, attributing co-founder Scott Farquhar, gives February 2, 2024 as the date support for active licences would end, while Atlassian's own post says February 15, 2024. This profile uses February 15, 2024.

In September 2025 Atlassian announced the end of the remaining self-managed edition, Data Center: new customers cannot buy Data Center subscriptions after March 30, 2026, existing customers can buy until March 30, 2028, and on March 28, 2029 Data Center subscriptions and their Marketplace apps expire and the products become read-only ([simplify-software.org](https://simplify-software.org/blog/is-jira-being-phased-out)). Bitbucket and Bamboo Data Center and Jira Align Data Center are excluded from the end of life ([simplify-software.org](https://simplify-software.org/blog/is-jira-being-phased-out)). Jira itself is not ending — Jira Cloud continues, and is where Atlassian puts new features — but the Jira a team runs on its own servers is.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Jira?** Jira is Atlassian's software for tracking issues and managing projects, used by software teams for bug tracking and agile work (Scrum and Kanban boards, backlogs, sprints) and increasingly by business teams for any kind of planned work ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/the-next-era-of-jira)).

**Why is it called Jira?** The name is a play on Gojira, the Japanese name for Godzilla: Atlassian's developers built Jira to replace Bugzilla, the leading bug tracker of the time, and the Godzilla reference was the in-house joke ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/atlassian-founders-20-years-20-lessons)).

**What happened in the April 2022 outage?** A maintenance script meant to delete a retired app was given whole cloud-site IDs instead of app IDs and permanently deleted 883 sites belonging to 775 customers; the last were restored on April 18, up to 14 days later, with no customer losing more than five minutes of data ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/technology/atlassian-two-week-long-cloud-outage-impacted-775-customers/)).

**Is Jira being discontinued?** No. Jira Cloud continues and is where new features land. What is ending is self-managed Jira: Server support ended in February 2024 and Data Center becomes read-only on March 28, 2029 ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/company-news/farewell-to-server); [simplify-software.org](https://simplify-software.org/blog/is-jira-being-phased-out)).

**What is the difference between Jira, Jira Service Management and Jira Align?** Jira is the core work-tracking product for software and business teams; Jira Service Management is the service-desk and IT service-management product built on it; Jira Align is the enterprise layer that connects team work to business strategy ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1721768/atlassian-targets-agile-development-at-scale-with-jira-align.html); [Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/the-next-era-of-jira)).
