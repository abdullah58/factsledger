## Overview

Rush University is a private nonprofit academic health university on Chicago's Near West Side, in the Illinois Medical District — an institution founded in 1972 whose oldest college predates the city around it ([Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/rush-university-receives-heed-award-for-excellence-in-diversity)). Its predecessor, Rush Medical College, received its charter on March 2, 1837, two days before Chicago itself was chartered, making it Chicago's first health-care institution and one of the first medical schools west of the Alleghenies ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/rankings-and-ratings/rush-university-medical-center-15-2/)). Today the university enrolls more than 2,500 students across Rush Medical College, the College of Nursing, the College of Health Sciences and the Graduate College, as the academic arm of RUSH University System for Health ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/care-coordination/data-drives-down-readmissions-at-chicago-health-system/)).

## A medical college older than its city

Founder Daniel Brainard named the school for Benjamin Rush — the physician-statesman who signed the Declaration of Independence — and ran it, as most nineteenth-century medical schools were run, as a proprietary institution owned by its physician faculty ([University of Chicago Library](https://www.lib.uchicago.edu/e/scrc/findingaids/view.php?eadid=ICU.SPCL.RUSHMEDICAL)). By 1900 it was among the nation's largest and most distinguished medical schools, and its faculty accepted an affiliation with the then-new University of Chicago that both sides called mutually beneficial ([University of Chicago Library](https://www.lib.uchicago.edu/e/scrc/findingaids/view.php?eadid=ICU.SPCL.RUSHMEDICAL)). That relationship lasted until World War II, when Rush instead aligned with the University of Illinois College of Medicine, its neighbor in the West Side medical district ([University of Chicago Library](https://www.lib.uchicago.edu/e/scrc/findingaids/view.php?eadid=ICU.SPCL.RUSHMEDICAL)). In the late 1960s the dormant Rush charter was merged into Presbyterian-St. Luke's Hospital — itself a 1956 merger of St. Luke's (1864) and Presbyterian (1883) hospitals — to form Rush-Presbyterian-St. Luke's Medical Center and resume full medical instruction ([University of Chicago Library](https://www.lib.uchicago.edu/e/scrc/findingaids/view.php?eadid=ICU.SPCL.RUSHMEDICAL)). Rush University followed in 1972, growing from a single college with fewer than 100 students to four colleges ([Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/rush-university-receives-heed-award-for-excellence-in-diversity)).

## The academic health system

RUSH University System for Health now comprises RUSH University Medical Center, RUSH Copley Medical Center and RUSH Oak Park Hospital, plus an extensive provider network and outpatient facilities, with the university embedded as its education and research arm ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/care-coordination/data-drives-down-readmissions-at-chicago-health-system/)). The medical center's record includes several Chicago firsts: the city's first cardiac catheterization lab in 1948, the first surgery to reattach a severed hand in the 1960s, and in 2014 the nation's first use of a newly approved brain stimulator with a unique placement-planning system for epilepsy patients ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/rankings-and-ratings/rush-university-medical-center-15-2/)). It is a Magnet-recognized hospital, ranked No. 2 in Chicago and Illinois by U.S. News & World Report ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/rankings-and-ratings/rush-university-medical-center-15-2/)).
