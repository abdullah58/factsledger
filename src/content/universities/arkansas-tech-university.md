## Overview

Arkansas Tech University is a public regional university in Russellville, Arkansas, whose history tracks the state's agricultural-school experiment of 1909 ([Encyclopedia of Arkansas](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/arkansas-tech-university-4138/)). Created by Act 100 of the Arkansas General Assembly as the Second District Agricultural School, it opened on October 26, 1910, with 186 students after Russellville outbid Ozark, Fort Smith and Morrilton for the site — pledging, among other inducements, free electricity and water for three years ([Encyclopedia of Arkansas](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/arkansas-tech-university-4138/)). It became Arkansas Polytechnic College in 1925 and Arkansas Tech University on July 9, 1976 ([Encyclopedia of Arkansas](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/arkansas-tech-university-4138/)). Preliminary fall 2026 enrollment stands at 9,516 across its Russellville and Ozark campuses ([Southwest Times Record](https://www.swtimes.com/story/news/2026/09/03/arkansas-tech-sees-freshman-transfer-enrollment-gains-in-2026/91593555007/)).

## A name chosen to dodge teacher training

The 1925 renaming carries the institution's most distinctive historical quirk. When the legislature renamed the four district schools that February, the other three became Agricultural and Mechanical (A&M) schools — but Russellville's leaders wanted the word "polytechnic" precisely to remain free of the "normal" (teacher-training) courses offered at Conway, and the school styled itself "Tech" for short ([Encyclopedia of Arkansas](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/arkansas-tech-university-4138/)). The irony, the Encyclopedia notes, is that the curriculum drifted toward teacher training and the liberal and fine arts anyway. The school survived an existential scare in the Depression — legislators discussed abolishing the agricultural schools outright — and a wartime collapse to just 133 students in fall 1943, when federal contracts to train Women's Army Auxiliary Corps members and naval air personnel kept it solvent ([Encyclopedia of Arkansas](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/arkansas-tech-university-4138/)). The GI Bill then more than doubled enrollment in a single year, to 1,493 in fall 1946 ([Encyclopedia of Arkansas](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/arkansas-tech-university-4138/)).

## Boom, bust, and the access mission

Under president Robert C. Brown (1993–2014), ATU ran one of the longest enrollment growth streaks in American public higher education: seventeen consecutive annual records, topping 10,000 in 2011 and peaking at 12,054 in fall 2015, when it was Arkansas's third-largest institution ([Encyclopedia of Arkansas](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/arkansas-tech-university-4138/)). The decade after was contraction — down to 9,397 in fall 2023, with the Russellville campus shrinking while Ozark grew ([Arkansas Senate](https://senate.arkansas.gov/senate-news/posts/2023/10/most-arkansas-colleges-report-enrollment-growth/)). Leadership churned too: Robin Bowen, the first woman to lead a four-year public college in Arkansas, was fired without cause in 2023, and Russell Jones took office on July 1, 2024 ([Encyclopedia of Arkansas](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/arkansas-tech-university-4138/)).

The fall 2026 figures suggest a turnaround built on access rather than selectivity. Enrollment rose 7% to 9,516, freshman enrollment climbed 9% and transfers 11%, and the Ozark satellite — absorbed from Arkansas Valley Technical Institute in 2003–04 — set its own record at 2,669 students ([Southwest Times Record](https://www.swtimes.com/story/news/2026/09/03/arkansas-tech-sees-freshman-transfer-enrollment-gains-in-2026/91593555007/)). The student body is 94% Arkansas residents, 43% first-generation college students and 57% from low-income backgrounds ([Southwest Times Record](https://www.swtimes.com/story/news/2026/09/03/arkansas-tech-sees-freshman-transfer-enrollment-gains-in-2026/91593555007/)). Recent capital investment includes the new Ferguson Student Union and the Farm Credit Agriculture Building ([Southwest Times Record](https://www.swtimes.com/story/news/2026/09/03/arkansas-tech-sees-freshman-transfer-enrollment-gains-in-2026/91593555007/)). The men's teams compete as the Wonder Boys and the women's as the Golden Suns in NCAA Division II's Gulf South Conference ([Encyclopedia of Arkansas](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/arkansas-tech-university-4138/)).
