## Overview

Texas A&M University is a public land-grant research university in College Station, [Texas](/places/texas/), opened on October 4, 1876 as the Agricultural and Mechanical College of Texas — the first public institution of higher education in the state ([Texas State Historical Association](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/texas-a-m-university)). It is the flagship of the Texas A&M University System, the largest university in Texas with 81,354 students across all its campuses, and a member of the Association of American Universities since 2001 ([Texas A&M University](https://www.tamu.edu/about/faq.html); [Association of American Universities](https://www.aau.edu/resource-library/aau-member-universities-by-year-of-entry)).

The university's research enterprise spent a record $1.433 billion in fiscal year 2025, ranking 13th among public universities in the National Science Foundation's Higher Education Research and Development survey ([Texas A&M University](https://inside.provost.tamu.edu/story/texas-am-reports-record-1-433-billion-in-fy25-research-expenditures/)). In the 2026–27 U.S. News & World Report Best Colleges rankings it placed No. 24 among public universities and No. 59 nationally ([Texas A&M University](https://tamu.campusesp.com/posts/3795)).

## From military college to the state's largest university

Texas A&M's origin is federal land policy. Congress passed the Morrill Land-Grant Act in 1862; the Texas Legislature accepted its terms in 1866 and, in 1871, organized the Agricultural and Mechanical College, appropriating $75,000 for buildings on 2,416 acres donated by citizens of Brazos County near Bryan ([Texas State Historical Association](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/texas-a-m-university)). When the college opened in 1876 it had 106 students and six faculty under Thomas S. Gathright, and it was an all-male military institution in which every student was required to join the Corps of Cadets.

The school nearly failed in its first decade — enrollment fell to about eighty students by 1883, the year the University of Texas opened in Austin — but it recovered under Governor Lawrence Sullivan Ross, who became president in 1891, and under the 1931 settlement that gave the college one-third of the revenues of the Permanent University Fund, the oil-rich endowment shared with the University of Texas ([Texas State Historical Association](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/texas-a-m-university)). The modern university was made in the 1960s under President James Earl Rudder: military training became optional in 1965, women were admitted on a limited basis in 1963 and on an equal basis in 1971, and the Texas Legislature renamed the school Texas A&M University effective August 23, 1963 — the "A&M" retained as a symbolic link to the past rather than an abbreviation ([Texas State Historical Association](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/texas-a-m-university); [Texas A&M University](https://www.tamu.edu/about/faq.html)). Enrollment surged from about 8,000 in 1963 to more than 25,000 by the 1976 centennial.

Today the College Station campus spans 5,200 acres, the largest university footprint in Texas, and the university operates branch campuses in Galveston and Doha, Qatar, a law school in Fort Worth, and a higher-education center in McAllen ([Texas A&M University](https://www.tamu.edu/about/faq.html)). The campus is also home to the George H. W. Bush Presidential Library and Museum ([Texas State Historical Association](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/texas-a-m-university)). The Corps of Cadets remains central to the university's identity: though membership is voluntary, it forms the largest uniformed body of students outside the U.S. military academies ([Texas A&M University](https://www.tamu.edu/about/faq.html)). In athletics, Texas A&M left the Big 12 for the Southeastern Conference on July 1, 2012 ([Texas A&M University](https://stories.tamu.edu/news/2012/07/02/making-history-texas-am-joins-the-sec/)).

## A research engine that outgrew its football image

Texas A&M is often introduced through football and tradition; its research numbers tell a different story. The university generated $1.433 billion in research expenditures in fiscal year 2025 — a record, up $39 million on the year before — with federal funding supplying $562 million (39%), institutional funds $420 million (29%), and state and local government $322 million (23%) ([Texas A&M University](https://inside.provost.tamu.edu/story/texas-am-reports-record-1-433-billion-in-fy25-research-expenditures/)). The National Science Foundation's HERD survey ranked its fiscal 2024 spending of $1.394 billion 13th among public universities and 22nd among all 681 U.S. degree-granting institutions — No. 1 in Texas and in the SEC. Texas A&M was the first research institution in Texas to pass $1 billion in annual research spending, in fiscal 2021. It holds land-grant, sea-grant, and space-grant designations simultaneously.

The scale shows up in rankings beyond research spending. In the 2026–27 U.S. News rankings, Texas A&M's undergraduate petroleum engineering program remained the nation's highest-ranked; the university ranked No. 3 among public universities for study abroad, No. 5 for service learning, and No. 8 for the first-year student experience ([Texas A&M University](https://tamu.campusesp.com/posts/3795)). A Lightcast analysis put the university's statewide economic impact above $22 billion for fiscal 2022–23, with research spending alone contributing $1.2 billion in added income to the state economy.

Leadership has been unusually unsettled. Susan Ballabina became the 28th president on May 11, 2026, after a unanimous Board of Regents vote — the third woman to hold the post, after Elsa Murano and M. Katherine Banks, and a 32-year veteran of the A&M System who had served as its executive vice chancellor ([KBTX](https://gray-kbtx-prod.gtv-cdn.com/2026/05/06/susan-ballabina-becomes-texas-ams-28th-president/)). She succeeded interim president Tommy Williams, a former state senator appointed in October 2025 after Mark A. Welsh III resigned on September 19, 2025, following a controversy over a classroom discussion about gender identity.

## The Baidu connection: the biologist co-founder

[Baidu](/organizations/baidu/) was founded by two men who met in the United States, and Texas A&M supplied the one who was not a computer scientist. Eric Xu (Xu Yong) earned his bachelor's and master's degrees in biology at Peking University, then came to the United States as a Rockefeller Foundation fellow and obtained his PhD in biology from Texas A&M University, followed by a postdoctoral fellowship at the University of California, Berkeley ([The Buffalo News](https://cedar.Buffalo.EDU/pub_docs/article125.pdf)). In 1998 Xu introduced his friend [Robin Li](/people/robin-li/) to Yahoo co-founder Jerry Yang; in 2000 Li and Xu started Baidu in a hotel room in Beijing with $1.2 million from U.S. venture firms, and Baidu's search engine launched in 2001 ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/robin-li)). As the Buffalo News reported at Baidu's 2005 IPO, the company "was founded in 2000 by Robin Yanhong Li, 36, who earned a master's degree in computer science from UB in 1994... and Eric Xu, who has a PhD from Texas A&M and is a veteran of American biotech firms. Xu later left the company" ([The Buffalo News](https://cedar.Buffalo.EDU/pub_docs/article125.pdf)).

The pairing is the point: Baidu's search technology came from Li's RankDex work, but its founding capital of expertise included a biologist who had crossed from bench science into Silicon Valley deal-making. Texas A&M's role in that story is a reminder that the university's research training fed not only Texas industry but, through its international graduates, the founding of China's dominant search engine.
