## Overview

California State University, [Los Angeles](/places/los-angeles/) is a public university in Los Angeles, founded in 1947 by an act of the California legislature as Los Angeles State College ([U-S-History.com](https://www.u-s-history.com/pages/h3067.html)). It occupies an unusual position in American higher education: a 2017 study published in *The New York Times* ranked it number one in the United States for the upward mobility of its students — ahead of every Ivy League university — while a decade later the campus is cutting division budgets 12.4% to close a $32.4 million deficit as enrollment slides ([Hispanic Outlook](https://www.hispanicoutlook.com/articles/cal-state-la-ranked-number-one-in-the-nation-for-u); [EdSource](https://edsource.org/2024/budget-cuts-begin-to-surface-at-california-state-university/718699)). The engine of social mobility is, itself, financially strained.

## The mobility engine

The ranking comes from the Equality of Opportunity Project, which studied records from more than 2,000 colleges and universities using anonymous federal tax filings and tuition records following 30 million students from 1999 to 2013; the research team included scholars from UC Berkeley, Stanford, and Brown ([Hispanic Outlook](https://www.hispanicoutlook.com/articles/cal-state-la-ranked-number-one-in-the-nation-for-u)). A college's mobility rate is defined as the fraction of students from families in the bottom fifth of the income distribution who end up in the top fifth — the product of access (how many poor students it admits) and success rate (how many reach the top fifth) ([Hispanic Outlook](https://www.hispanicoutlook.com/articles/cal-state-la-ranked-number-one-in-the-nation-for-u)). Cal State LA's rate was 9.9%, ahead of second-place Pace University-New York at 8.4%, and higher than Ivy League universities that admit far fewer low-income students ([Hispanic Outlook](https://www.hispanicoutlook.com/articles/cal-state-la-ranked-number-one-in-the-nation-for-u)). Of 161 California colleges, it ranked first on the overall mobility index; on a broader bottom-40-to-top-40 measure it ranked sixth nationally at 35.7% ([Hispanic Outlook](https://www.hispanicoutlook.com/articles/cal-state-la-ranked-number-one-in-the-nation-for-u)). The university is a federally designated Hispanic-Serving Institution, with 58.4% of students identifying as Latino ([Hispanic Outlook](https://www.hispanicoutlook.com/articles/cal-state-la-ranked-number-one-in-the-nation-for-u)).

## The budget crisis

By fall 2024, the financial picture had darkened. Cal State LA carried a $32.4 million deficit and directed every division to cut budgets 12.4%; it budgeted on the assumption enrollment would come in 5.3% below its in-state full-time-equivalent target ([EdSource](https://edsource.org/2024/budget-cuts-begin-to-surface-at-california-state-university/718699)). Headcount enrollment in fall 2023 was 24,673 — up 6% from a decade earlier but below the pre-pandemic peak of 28,253 ([EdSource](https://edsource.org/2024/budget-cuts-begin-to-surface-at-california-state-university/718699)). Students felt it in course availability: course sections were down almost 7% year over year, the history department's enrollment fell from more than 1,800 to fewer than 1,700 students, and English faculty reported a narrowing range of electives ([EdSource](https://edsource.org/2024/budget-cuts-begin-to-surface-at-california-state-university/718699)). President Berenecea Johnson Eanes described a double bind: when the campus overenrolled, it absorbed the costs of extra students without additional state funding; now declining enrollment "feels like a budget reduction, because of the lost tuition, even though our funding per student is up" ([EdSource](https://edsource.org/2024/budget-cuts-begin-to-surface-at-california-state-university/718699)). The strain is systemwide — Cal State officials projected a $1 billion budget gap for 2025-26 on uncertain state funding, enrollment declines, and rising costs ([EdSource](https://edsource.org/2024/budget-cuts-begin-to-surface-at-california-state-university/718699)).

## History and organization

The university opened in 1947 as Los Angeles State College, sharing the Los Angeles City College campus; it became the Los Angeles State College of Applied Arts and Sciences in 1949, joined the state college system as California State College at Los Angeles in 1964, and gained university status and its present name in 1972 ([U-S-History.com](https://www.u-s-history.com/pages/h3067.html)). It is organized into six colleges — Arts and Letters; Business and Economics; Education; Engineering, Computer Science, and Technology; Health and Human Services; and Natural and Social Sciences ([U-S-History.com](https://www.u-s-history.com/pages/h3067.html)).
