## Overview

The Austin Community College District is a public community college system based in Austin, Texas, that pairs a conventional two-year college with an unusual civic structure: it is also a taxing district whose boundaries have grown, piece by piece, through voter-approved annexations ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-community-college)). The college was established late in 1972 to serve the Texas capital area, and classes opened in September 1973 in several Austin locations with about 2,200 students ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-community-college)). In its early years the school board of the Austin Independent School District served as the college's governing body, and Thomas M. Hatfield was named its first president ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-community-college)).

The district's modern identity is defined less by its founding than by two things page-one search results tend to underplay: a property-tax-funded Free Tuition Pilot Program that has measurably reversed an enrollment decline, and a governance model in which neighboring communities vote themselves into the tax base. In fall 2021 the district enrolled 35,609 students, making it one of the largest public two-year institutions in the country ([NCES](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/digest/d22/tables/dt22_312.10.asp)).

## A college that is also a taxing district

Texas community colleges operate under a state framework in which local property taxes are a core revenue source, and ACC leans on that base more explicitly than most. The district's free-tuition program relies on property taxes and enrollment-growth revenue to stay funded, according to the college's deputy chief financial officer ([The Daily Texan](https://thedailytexan.com/2026/07/20/acc-free-tuition-program-continues-for-2027/)). That funding mix was reshaped statewide in 2023, when Texas House Bill 8 replaced the old enrollment-based formula with one tying state dollars to degree and certificate completions, transfers to four-year universities, and high-school dual-credit participation ([Governing](https://www.governing.com/policy/five-ways-a-funding-overhaul-transformed-texas-community-colleges)). Lawmakers appropriated a historic $683 million to two-year institutions under the new model ([Governing](https://www.governing.com/policy/five-ways-a-funding-overhaul-transformed-texas-community-colleges)).

The district's boundaries are not fixed; they expand when a community inside ACC's state-designated service area petitions to join and voters approve the annexation, accepting the college's property tax in exchange for in-district tuition and a vote in district elections. That mechanism is about to be tested again: Pflugerville ISD voters are scheduled to decide on November 3, 2026 whether to join the ACC taxing district, with early voting running October 19–30 ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/why-im-voting-yes-acc-pflugerville-amelia-folkes-apr-mps-932nc)). A local fiscal analysis of the measure estimates the annexation would extend ACC's property tax across the Pflugerville ISD territory in exchange for in-district tuition and expanded dual-credit and workforce programs ([Pflugerville Pfamilies Pfirst](https://pfpfpf.org/acc-annexation-analysis)). Pflugerville previously voted on ACC annexation in 2018 ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/why-im-voting-yes-acc-pflugerville-amelia-folkes-apr-mps-932nc)).

## The Free Tuition Pilot Program

ACC launched its Free Tuition Pilot Program in 2024 to reduce financial barriers and reverse declining enrollment ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/student-success/college-experience/2026/06/11/how-one-college-rethinking-free-tuition)). The program covers tuition for eligible students living within ACC's tax-base district who enroll after graduating from high school or earning a Texas GED, offering up to three years of free tuition for credit-bearing coursework and up to five years for students pursuing a bachelor's degree at the college ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/student-success/college-experience/2026/06/11/how-one-college-rethinking-free-tuition)). Crucially, it is a "first-dollar" program: ACC covers tuition and general fees before financial aid is applied, so eligible students can direct need-based grants and scholarships toward housing, transportation, food, and other living costs ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/student-success/college-experience/2026/06/11/how-one-college-rethinking-free-tuition)).

The documented results are striking. Two years after launch, enrollment among eligible students had grown 59 percent, from 3,461 in fall 2023 to 5,504 in fall 2025, and college leaders said the initiative reversed a decade-long enrollment decline and brought enrollment to its highest point since 2011 ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/student-success/college-experience/2026/06/11/how-one-college-rethinking-free-tuition)). The program also appears to change how students engage once enrolled: in fall 2025, 51 percent of free-tuition students attended full time, compared with 28 percent of the overall student population ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/student-success/college-experience/2026/06/11/how-one-college-rethinking-free-tuition)). Since the launch, ACC has reported an 87 percent increase in degrees and awards earned among participating students compared with pre-program levels ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/student-success/college-experience/2026/06/11/how-one-college-rethinking-free-tuition)).

The pilot was seeded in part by state money: ACC directed $6.8 million it received through HB 8 into the free-tuition effort, with the first class of beneficiaries starting in 2024 ([Governing](https://www.governing.com/policy/five-ways-a-funding-overhaul-transformed-texas-community-colleges)). The program carries no financial or GPA requirements, began with the high-school graduating class of 2024, and is currently designed to run through the class of 2028 ([The Daily Texan](https://thedailytexan.com/2026/07/20/acc-free-tuition-program-continues-for-2027/)). In July 2026 the Board of Trustees approved a $583 million budget for fiscal year 2027 to continue free tuition while holding the tuition rate flat — the same rate the college has charged for 13 years ([The Daily Texan](https://thedailytexan.com/2026/07/20/acc-free-tuition-program-continues-for-2027/)). Many students use the program to complete core requirements before transferring to the University of Texas at Austin or another university ([The Daily Texan](https://thedailytexan.com/2026/07/20/acc-free-tuition-program-continues-for-2027/)).
