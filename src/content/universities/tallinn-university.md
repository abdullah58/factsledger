## Overview

Tallinn University is a public research university in [Tallinn](/places/tallinn/), [Estonia](/places/estonia/), formed in 2005 by the merger of several institutions, the oldest of which — the Tallinn Teachers Institute — was founded in 1919 ([Times Higher Education](https://timeshighereducation.com/world-university-rankings/tallinn-university)). It is the largest university of humanities in Tallinn and the third-biggest public university in Estonia, with more than 7,500 students, 9.5 percent of them international, and over 800 employees ([University of Glasgow](https://www.gla.ac.uk/explore/internationalisation/ourpartners/partners/talinnuniversityestonia/)). Its campus sits in the city centre, between the harbour and Kadriorg Park, with affiliated regional colleges in Haapsalu and Rakvere ([Times Higher Education](https://timeshighereducation.com/world-university-rankings/tallinn-university)).

This profile is about Tallinn University (TLU), the humanities-led university formed in 2005 — not Tallinn University of Technology (TalTech), the separate technical university in Tallinn's Mustamäe district, and not the University of Tartu, Estonia's oldest university. The university was founded by an Act of the parliament of the Republic of Estonia in 2005 ([Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/history)). Its predecessor, the Tallinn Teachers' Seminar, was established in 1919 and carried a succession of names — Tallinn Teachers' Institute from 1947 to 1952, Tallinn Pedagogical Institute from 1952 to 1992, and Tallinn Pedagogical University from 1992 to 2005 — before the merger ([Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/history)).

## A merger university built on teacher training

Tallinn University's identity is rooted in teacher training: QS describes it as the central institution for teacher-education in Estonia, "with 100 years of experience in teacher training" ([QS TopUniversities](https://www.topuniversities.com/universities/tallinn-university)). The 2005 merger brought together the Tallinn Teachers' Institute, the Academic Library, the Institutes of Humanities and of History, and the Film and Media School ([Times Higher Education](https://timeshighereducation.com/world-university-rankings/tallinn-university)). Two older strands fed into the new university as well: the Institute of History, established under the Estonian Academy of Sciences in 1947, and the Estonian Institute of Humanities, founded by a group of intellectuals in 1988 and described by the university as the first private university in the eastern part of Europe, a counterweight to state-controlled, Soviet-style universities ([Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/history)).

The university's research is organised in five interdisciplinary focus fields — educational innovation, digital and media culture, cultural competences, healthy and sustainable lifestyle, and society and open governance — and delivered through six schools: the School of Educational Sciences; the Baltic Film, Media, Arts and Communication School (BFM); the School of Humanities; the School of Natural Sciences and Health; the School of Governance, Law and Society; and the School of Digital Technologies, which supplies services and expertise across the whole university ([QS TopUniversities](https://www.topuniversities.com/universities/tallinn-university); [Times Higher Education](https://timeshighereducation.com/world-university-rankings/tallinn-university)). The emphasis is on the humanities, through schools of education, arts and communication, government, law and society, humanities, and film and media ([Times Higher Education](https://timeshighereducation.com/world-university-rankings/tallinn-university)). The campus buildings carry Latin names: the oldest, Terra, dates to 1938, while Mare was completed in 2006 and Nova and Astra in 2012 ([Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/history)). All university activities operate on the "academic quarter", starting at 15 minutes past the hour ([Times Higher Education](https://timeshighereducation.com/world-university-rankings/tallinn-university)).

The humanities focus shows up in the details: the Asian studies section of the School of Humanities runs courses in Japanese, Chinese, Turkish, Arabic, Sanskrit, Korean and Persian, and the Institute of Population Studies is regarded as a national lead body ([Times Higher Education](https://timeshighereducation.com/world-university-rankings/tallinn-university)). The university's digital technologists developed three serious games as partners in the LEAP (Lean and Agile Practice linking Engineering Higher Education to Industry) programme ([Times Higher Education](https://timeshighereducation.com/world-university-rankings/tallinn-university)). One of Estonia's biggest international summer and winter schools is organised by the university, drawing around 400 participants from all over the world each year ([QS TopUniversities](https://www.topuniversities.com/universities/tallinn-university)).

## Film, media and the rankings climb

The Baltic Film, Media, Arts and Communication School is the university's most distinctive unit: QS calls BFM the only higher-educational institution in Northern Europe teaching film, television and media studies in English and the most contemporary film school in the region ([QS TopUniversities](https://www.topuniversities.com/universities/tallinn-university)). Times Higher Education adds that the film and media school claims to offer the widest range of English-language programmes in its disciplines in Northern Europe ([Times Higher Education](https://timeshighereducation.com/world-university-rankings/tallinn-university)). BFM was established within the university in 2005, and the university states it is the only university college in northern Europe in which it is possible to study film, television and media in English ([Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/history)).

On rankings, the trajectory is a slow climb followed by a recent step up. QS placed the university at 801–1000 in 2019, 2020 and 2021, then at 1001–1200 in 2022 through 2025, before moving it to 901–950 in both 2026 and 2027 ([QS TopUniversities](https://www.topuniversities.com/universities/tallinn-university)). The stat that anchors the ranking story is the improvement itself: after four years in the 1001–1200 band, the university moved up a band in the two most recent editions, which the university's own ranking page — still showing the 1001–1200 figure from the 2024 and 2025 editions — has not yet caught up with ([QS TopUniversities](https://www.topuniversities.com/universities/tallinn-university); [Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/ranking-positions)). In the 2021 edition of the QS rankings, the university was ranked among the top 5 percent of universities in the world and in the world top 300 in Education and Training and top 350 in Sociology ([QS TopUniversities](https://www.topuniversities.com/universities/tallinn-university)). By subject in the 2024 editions, it stood at 301–350 in QS Education and Training, 401–500 in THE Education and in THE Arts and Humanities, and 601–800 in THE Social Sciences, with a THE Emerging Economies ranking of 301–350 in 2024 and a QS Northern European Region ranking of 131 in 2025 ([Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/ranking-positions)).

## Social entrepreneurship — and the Banned Books Museum link

The university's most direct connection to this site's other profiles runs through its Social Entrepreneurship master's programme (SEMA), housed in the School of Governance, Law and Society ([Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/yti/social-entrepreneurship)). The university describes SEMA as the only curriculum for Social Entrepreneurship in the region that gives not only an academic degree but also a supportive environment for development, following project-based learning, with core topics including Social Entrepreneurship, Contemporary Social Problems, Social Capital and Social Impact, and Civil Society and Social Innovations ([Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/yti/social-entrepreneurship)). Students complete a master's thesis that may focus on a business model for a social enterprise or on measuring social impact ([Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/yti/social-entrepreneurship)).

That programme is where [Joseph Maximillian Dunnigan](/people/joseph-maximillian-dunnigan/) studied: Tallinn University's own blog describes him as "founder of the [Banned Books Museum](/organizations/banned-books-museum/) and a student of Tallinn University's Social Entrepreneurship Master's Programme (SEMA)" ([Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/yti/mediahub/blogs/realities-running-social-enterprise-joe-dunnigan-founder-banned-books-museum)). Dunnigan went on to open the museum in Tallinn's Old Town in December 2020, turning what he learned in the programme into a small non-profit museum of censored books. The SEMA programme was launched in 2018 and has been entirely online since 2022, with around 90 percent of its applicants international students, according to a Veriff case study ([Veriff](https://www.veriff.com/case-studies/tallinn-university-partners-with-veriff)).
