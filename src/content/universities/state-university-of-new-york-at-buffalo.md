## Overview

The State University of New York at Buffalo — the University at Buffalo, or UB — is a public research university in Buffalo and Amherst, New York, founded in 1846 as a private medical college and incorporated into the State University of New York (SUNY) system in 1962 ([University at Buffalo](https://www.buffalo.edu/community/about-us/about-the-university.html)). It is the largest and most comprehensive university in the SUNY system, a Carnegie-classified R1 institution, and an Association of American Universities member since 1989 ([State University of New York](https://www.suny.edu/suny-news/press-releases/6-26/6-2-26-4/buffalo.html)).

UB enrolls more than 31,000 students, including 21,188 undergraduates, across 13 schools and colleges on three campuses, and counts more than 313,000 alumni in 150 countries ([University at Buffalo](https://www.buffalo.edu/admissions/academics/about-ub.html)). In the 2027 U.S. News & World Report Best Colleges rankings it placed No. 40 among public universities and 85th overall ([University at Buffalo](https://www.buffalo.edu/news/releases/2026/09/ub-us-news-ranking-2026.html)).

## From medical college to SUNY flagship

UB began as a small private medical school founded to train physicians for Buffalo, Niagara Falls, and surrounding communities, and it remained private for more than a century. Its founders included Millard Fillmore, the future 13th president of the United States, who served as the university's first chancellor from 1846 until his death in 1874 ([University at Buffalo](https://www.buffalo.edu/news/releases/2020/08/001.html)). The state acquired the university in 1962 under Governor Nelson Rockefeller, and the new public institution built a second, much larger campus in the suburb of Amherst — today's North Campus, opened in the early 1970s and now the center of most university activity.

The university now operates on three campuses with distinct roles. North Campus in Amherst holds most core academic programs, residence halls, and athletic venues; South Campus in Buffalo, a 1920s-era landmark, houses the schools of Architecture and Planning, Dental Medicine, Nursing, Pharmacy and Pharmaceutical Sciences, and Public Health and Health Professions; and the Downtown Campus is anchored by the Jacobs School of Medicine and Biomedical Sciences, which moved into a new building on Buffalo's medical corridor in 2017 ([University at Buffalo](https://www.buffalo.edu/community/about-us/about-the-university.html)). UB describes itself as SUNY's flagship and its primary center for professional education, and it was the first public university in New York State invited to join the Association of American Universities, in 1989 ([State University of New York](https://www.suny.edu/suny-news/press-releases/6-26/6-2-26-4/buffalo.html); [Association of American Universities](https://www.aau.edu/resource-library/aau-member-universities-by-year-of-entry)).

Leadership changed in 2026. Caroline Attardo Genco, previously provost and senior vice president at Tufts University, was named UB's 16th president on June 2, 2026, and took office on August 10 — the first woman to lead the university in its 180-year history and the first Buffalo native to lead it in its modern era ([State University of New York](https://www.suny.edu/suny-news/press-releases/6-26/6-2-26-4/buffalo.html)). She succeeded Satish K. Tripathi, the 15th president, who served from 2011 and is now President Emeritus and University Professor.

## Research, rankings, and Empire AI

UB's research identity rests on scale and on a distinctive state-level bet: artificial intelligence. The university is one of the 71 members of the Association of American Universities, the group of leading North American research universities, and it ranks among the top 40 public universities in the United States ([University at Buffalo](https://www.buffalo.edu/admissions/academics/about-ub.html)). Its undergraduate engineering program ranks 32nd among public universities and its computer science program 40th among publics in the 2027 U.S. News rankings ([University at Buffalo](https://www.buffalo.edu/news/releases/2026/09/ub-us-news-ranking-2026.html)).

The information-gain story is Empire AI. Launched in 2024 by Governor Kathy Hochul, Empire AI is New York State's AI research consortium, backed by more than $500 million in public and private funding, and UB hosts its supercomputing center ([University at Buffalo](https://www.buffalo.edu/ubnow/stories/2026/08/empire-ai-beta.html)). In 2026 the consortium's second-phase system, Empire AI Beta — a $40 million NVIDIA-powered supercomputer housed at UB — went fully online, giving researchers at New York's leading public and private universities what the governor called the most powerful academic research computer in the country. Beta delivers an 11-fold increase in AI training capacity over the consortium's first system, with more than 300 research projects already queued to use it, spanning brain and gastrointestinal surgery tools, sustainable food systems, and financial-market data. A permanent, full-scale Gamma facility at UB is expected to be completed by the end of 2027, with waste heat from the system used to heat campus buildings ([University at Buffalo](https://www.buffalo.edu/ubnow/stories/2026/08/empire-ai-beta.html)). The AI focus is not new: UB's senior vice president for research notes the university has more than five decades of leadership in AI and data science.

## The Baidu connection, and a reckoning with the founder

UB's most consequential alumnus for the technology world is [Robin Li](/people/robin-li/), co-founder, chairman, and chief executive of [Baidu](/organizations/baidu/). Li arrived at SUNY-Buffalo in the fall of 1991 to study computer science, earned his master's degree in 1994 — he had planned a doctorate but left for industry — and studied document analysis at UB's Center of Excellence for Document Analysis and Recognition, known as CEDAR ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/robin-li); [University at Buffalo](https://www.buffalo.edu/ubnow/spotlight.host.html/content/shared/university/news/news-center-releases/2013/06/043.detail.html?ref=ppppp.dev)). In 1996, while working at Dow Jones's IDD Information Services, he developed RankDex, a link-analysis method for ranking websites that received a U.S. patent in 1999; in 2000 he co-founded Baidu with Eric Xu, and Baidu's search engine launched in 2001 ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/robin-li)). In 2013 Li and his wife Melissa Dongmin Ma gave $200,000 to fund the Robin Li Data Mining and Machine Learning Laboratory in UB's Davis Hall ([University at Buffalo](https://www.buffalo.edu/ubnow/spotlight.host.html/content/shared/university/news/news-center-releases/2013/06/043.detail.html?ref=ppppp.dev)).

UB has also publicly reckoned with the founder who gave it its first chancellor. On August 3, 2020, the UB Council voted to remove the names of Millard Fillmore, James O. Putnam, and Peter B. Porter from four campus locations, a decision the university said aligned with its commitment to fight systemic racism ([University at Buffalo](https://www.buffalo.edu/news/releases/2020/08/001.html)). The Millard Fillmore Academic Center became simply the Academic Center, and Putnam Way was renamed Mary Talbert Way for the Buffalo civil-rights pioneer ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/quicktakes/2020/08/04/buffalo-removes-name-millard-fillmore-building)). Fillmore, UB's founder and first chancellor, had as president supported the Compromise of 1850, including the Fugitive Slave Act — a decision the university called deeply hurtful, especially for African Americans. UB had already stopped co-sponsoring the annual ceremony at Fillmore's gravesite in 2019.
