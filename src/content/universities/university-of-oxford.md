## Overview

The University of Oxford is not a university in the conventional sense — it is a federation. There is no central campus: Oxford is made up of 36 colleges, three societies and four permanent private halls spread across the city, related to the central University through a federal system ([University of Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/admissions/undergraduate/college-life/college-overview)). Each college is independent and self-governing, holding a charter approved by the Privy Council, and the colleges — not the University — select and admit undergraduates, house them, and are responsible for their undergraduate tutorial teaching and welfare ([University of Oxford](http://www.ox.ac.uk/about/the-university)). The University itself determines course content, organises lectures and seminars, provides the libraries, laboratories and museums, sets and marks examinations, and awards the degrees ([University of Oxford](http://www.ox.ac.uk/about/the-university)). This split — students belong simultaneously to a small college and to a world-class university — is the structural fact that most summaries of "Oxford" skip past, and it shapes everything from how tutorials work to who actually admits you.

Teaching is attested at Oxford from 1096, and the university received its Royal Charter in 1248, making it one of the oldest universities in the world; the oldest colleges — University, Balliol and Merton — were established by the 13th century, while the newest, Green Templeton, dates only to a 2008 merger and Reuben College to 2019 ([IAU/UNESCO WHED](https://www.whed.net/detail_institution.php?KDo2MF0sQ2BTLVMoYApgCg=%3D); [University of Oxford](http://www.ox.ac.uk/about/the-university)). It is an independent and self-governing institution consisting of the University, its divisions, departments and faculties, and the colleges ([University of Oxford](http://www.ox.ac.uk/about/the-university)).

## Colleges, tutorials and degrees

The college is the unit of student life. Every Oxford student belongs to a college as well as to a course department and the University, and undergraduates typically live in college accommodation in their first and final years ([University of Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/admissions/undergraduate/college-life/college-overview)). Most tutorial teaching happens in colleges, and college tutors — who usually research as well as teach — assess applicants, conduct admissions interviews, and effectively decide who comes to their college and whom they will teach ([University of Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/admissions/undergraduate/college-life/college-overview)). The permanent private halls are similar to colleges but smaller, founded by particular Christian denominations, with two admitting undergraduates ([University of Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/admissions/undergraduate/college-life/college-overview)).

Oxford-educated figures profiled in this reference include [Tony Blair](https://factsledger.com/people/tony-blair) (law at St John's College), [James Cuff](https://factsledger.com/people/james-cuff) (D.Phil. in molecular biophysics), [Ghislaine Maxwell](https://factsledger.com/people/ghislaine-maxwell) (Balliol College), [Reuven Yaron](https://factsledger.com/people/reuven-yaron) (who wrote his doctoral thesis at Oxford), and [Helen Toner](https://factsledger.com/people/helen-toner) (a research affiliate at Oxford's Centre for the Governance of AI).

## Scale, research and standing

Oxford enrols more than 26,000 students — 12,460 undergraduates and 13,755 postgraduates — with 43% of the student body, around 12,565 students, coming from more than 170 countries and territories ([University of Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/about/the-university/facts-and-figures)). Entry is intensely competitive: there are usually only about 3,300 undergraduate places, and more than 23,000 people applied for 2024 entry — roughly seven applicants per place — while over 66% of UK undergraduates admitted in 2024 came from state schools ([University of Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/about/the-university/facts-and-figures)).

On research, Oxford's submission to the 2021 Research Excellence Framework had the highest volume of world-leading research in UK higher education, and its total research income reached £865.7 million in 2021/22 ([University of Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/about/the-university/facts-and-figures)). Oxford University Innovation is the UK leader in generating spinout companies — more than 300 to date, more than any other UK institution — and the most prolific university patent filer in the country ([University of Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/about/the-university/facts-and-figures)). The university contributes around £16.9 billion to the UK economy and supports more than 90,400 full-time jobs ([University of Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/about/the-university/facts-and-figures)).

The rankings record is singular: Oxford was ranked first in the world in the Times Higher Education World University Rankings for 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 — a record ten consecutive years — and in the 2026 subject tables was first in the world for medical and health subjects for the 15th year running and for computer science for the eighth year in a row ([University of Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/about/the-university/facts-and-figures)). Oxford University Press, a department of the University, is the largest and most successful university press in the world, publishing in some 100 languages with offices in more than 50 countries ([University of Oxford](https://www.ox.ac.uk/about/the-university/facts-and-figures)).
