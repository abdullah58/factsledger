## Overview

This profile covers **Saint Mary's University in Halifax, Nova Scotia, Canada** — the public university recorded as IAU-014271 in the International Association of Universities' World Higher Education Database ([IAU WHED](https://www.whed.net/institutions/IAU-014271)). It is not the private Lasallian Saint Mary's University of Minnesota in Winona, founded in 1912 ([Visit Winona](https://visitwinona.com/directory/saint-marys-university-of-minnesota/)), nor the private Marianist St. Mary's University in San Antonio, Texas, founded in 1852 ([University Guru](https://www.universityguru.com/university/st.-mary's-university-san-antonio)).

The Halifax institution was founded in 1802 as Saint Mary's College, acquired its present status and title in 1841, and has been a public, non-denominational university since 1970 ([IAU WHED](https://www.whed.net/institutions/IAU-014271)). It enrolls 5,990 students (2024–25) at 923 Robie Street in Halifax, led by president Robert Summerby-Murray since 2015, with faculties of Arts, Science, Education and the Sobey School of Business, plus doctoral programs in fields including astronomy and business ([IAU WHED](https://www.whed.net/institutions/IAU-014271)).

## The province's $25-million health-data bet

In March 2023 the Government of Nova Scotia made Saint Mary's the centerpiece of a workforce bet: a $25-million investment for the university to build health-care data analytics and health system administration programs, announced March 8 by Advanced Education Minister Brian Wong ([Government of Nova Scotia](https://news.novascotia.ca/en/2023/03/08/province-supports-cutting-edge-healthcare-data-analytics-training-saint-marys)). The money was earmarked for a diploma in health-care analytics to bring data-informed decision-making to the province's health system, a business administration program for health-care administrators, office directors and family doctors, and expanded articulation with Nova Scotia Community College so college diplomas could ladder into degrees ([Government of Nova Scotia](https://news.novascotia.ca/en/2023/03/08/province-supports-cutting-edge-healthcare-data-analytics-training-saint-marys)).

The investment sat inside a wider health-spending surge: the same budget cycle committed $59 million for a medical school at Cape Breton University and $37 million for a health institute at St. Francis Xavier University, with health-related spending up $750 million to $6.5 billion — about 45% of provincial spending ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/nova-scotia-leaves-tax-rates-alone-but-provides-relief-to-nurses/)). The SMU funding came as a last-minute "additional appropriation" outside the previous year's budget ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/nova-scotia-leaves-tax-rates-alone-but-provides-relief-to-nurses/)).
