## Overview

Jacksonville State University (JSU, "Jax State") is a public university in Jacksonville, [Alabama](/places/alabama/), founded in 1883 as a state normal school for training teachers ([Encyclopedia of Alabama](https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/jacksonville-state-university-jsu/)). From that two-year teacher-training origin it has grown into a regional university on a 459-acre campus in the Appalachian foothills of northeast Alabama, enrolling a record 10,433 students in fall 2026 ([Encyclopedia of Alabama](https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/jacksonville-state-university-jsu/); [Gadsden Messenger](https://gadsdenmessenger.com/jax-state-sets-enrollment-record-for-sixth-year/)). Its recent history has been unusually dramatic: a devastating tornado in 2018, a landmark football bowl victory in 2023, and eight consecutive years of enrollment growth.

## From normal school to university

The institution was established on February 22, 1883, when Governor Edward O'Neal signed the bill creating the State Normal School at Jacksonville, which acquired the 12 acres and two-story brick building of the closed Calhoun College ([Encyclopedia of Alabama](https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/jacksonville-state-university-jsu/)). The first president, James C. Ryals Jr., opened the school with three instructors; he died of pneumonia in 1885 at age 30 ([Encyclopedia of Alabama](https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/jacksonville-state-university-jsu/)).

The school became a four-year institution in 1929-30 and was renamed Jacksonville State Teachers College, earning regional accreditation from the Southern Association of Colleges and Schools five years later ([Encyclopedia of Alabama](https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/jacksonville-state-university-jsu/)). The name changed to Jacksonville State College in 1957 with the creation of the first graduate program, and in August 1966 the State Board of Education elevated it to university status; a year later the legislature established an independent Board of Trustees ([Encyclopedia of Alabama](https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/jacksonville-state-university-jsu/)).

## The tornado and the rebound

On March 19, 2018, an EF-3 tornado struck the campus during spring break — timing that kept injuries to very few — and damaged many buildings, cancelling classes for about three weeks ([Encyclopedia of Alabama](https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/jacksonville-state-university-jsu/)). Merrill Hall, home of the School of Business and Industry, and the Alumni House were the hardest hit and were later demolished; a fundraising concert headlined by the band Alabama, led by JSU alumnus Randy Owen, helped the recovery ([Encyclopedia of Alabama](https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/jacksonville-state-university-jsu/)).

What followed was a sustained renaissance. Enrollment has grown for eight consecutive years, from 8,479 in fall 2018 to the fall 2026 record of 10,433 — the sixth straight all-time record — including 9,090 undergraduates and 1,343 graduate students ([Gadsden Messenger](https://gadsdenmessenger.com/jax-state-sets-enrollment-record-for-sixth-year/)). The university credits expanded academic offerings — fall 2026 brought the first students in new Aviation and Entrepreneurship bachelor's programs — and campus investment including the West Village housing complex with 700-plus beds ([Gadsden Messenger](https://gadsdenmessenger.com/jax-state-sets-enrollment-record-for-sixth-year/)).

## Athletics: from small-college titles to an FBS bowl win

JSU's athletic tradition runs deep: it claims NCAA national titles in football (1992), men's basketball (1985), baseball (1990, 1991) and women's gymnastics (1984, 1985), and in 2001 placekicker Ashley Martin became the first woman to score in an NCAA football game ([Encyclopedia of Alabama](https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/jacksonville-state-university-jsu/)). The Gamecocks play in red and white, and the school calls itself "The Friendliest Campus in the South" ([Encyclopedia of Alabama](https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/jacksonville-state-university-jsu/)).

The football program's modern peak came in its first season at the FBS level. On December 16, 2023, Jacksonville State beat Louisiana 34-31 in overtime in the New Orleans Bowl — Zion Webb's tying touchdown pass with 1:46 left in regulation, then freshman Garrison Rippa's 27-yard field goal in overtime — for the first bowl win in program history ([Associated Press](https://sportsfly.cbsistatic.com/college-football/news/jacksonville-state-rodriguez-celebrate-unlikely-new-orleans-bowl-win-over-louisiana-lafayette/)). The Gamecocks had only received a bowl bid because of a shortage of eligible teams, yet outgained Louisiana and converted five of six fourth downs ([Associated Press](https://sportsfly.cbsistatic.com/college-football/news/jacksonville-state-rodriguez-celebrate-unlikely-new-orleans-bowl-win-over-louisiana-lafayette/)).
