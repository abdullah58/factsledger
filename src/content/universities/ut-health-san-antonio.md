## Overview

UT Health San Antonio was the public academic health science center of San Antonio, Texas — formally The University of Texas Health Science Center at San Antonio — until September 1, 2025, when it merged with The University of Texas at San Antonio to form a single institution called UT San Antonio ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/ut-health-ut-san-antonio-complete-merger/)). Page-one search results still largely describe it as a standalone university; as a separate institution, it no longer exists. The merged UT San Antonio is now Texas' third-largest public research university by research expenditures, comprising 15 colleges and schools across six campuses with around 40,000 students and 17,000 employees ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/ut-health-ut-san-antonio-complete-merger/)).

The institution began in 1959, when the Fifty-sixth Texas Legislature authorized a medical school originally named South Texas Medical School ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/university-of-texas-health-science-center-at-san-antonio)). After heated debate over its location, a site on the city's periphery was chosen: 100 acres donated by the San Antonio Medical Foundation in the South Texas Medical Center complex ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/university-of-texas-health-science-center-at-san-antonio)). Construction was not completed until July 1968; the first class in San Antonio numbered 104 students, and 33 students received the first doctor of medicine degrees in 1970 ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/university-of-texas-health-science-center-at-san-antonio)). A dental school followed, authorized by the Sixty-first Texas Legislature in 1969 and opened in September 1970 in temporary facilities, with its first graduation in June 1974 ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/university-of-texas-health-science-center-at-san-antonio)). In 1972 the UT Board of Regents formally established The University of Texas Health Science Center at San Antonio, composed of the medical school, the dental school, the graduate school of biomedical sciences, and a school of allied health sciences, with Frank Harrison as its first president ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/university-of-texas-health-science-center-at-san-antonio)). A nursing school authorized in 1969 was placed administratively within the health science center in 1976 ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/university-of-texas-health-science-center-at-san-antonio)).

## The merger that failed in 2010 — and succeeded in 2025

The idea of combining the health science center with UTSA was studied and rejected fifteen years before it happened. In October 2009 the UT System Board of Regents charged a special advisory group chaired by Peter T. Flawn — president emeritus of UT Austin and a former UTSA president — with studying the feasibility of a merger ([UT System](http://utsystem.edu/news/2010/05/13/special-advisory-group-reports-merger-feasibility-of-ut-san-antonio-and-ut-health-science-center-at-san-antonio)). On May 13, 2010, the group reported its conclusion: there was "no compelling reason for, and no compelling cost benefit from" merging the two institutions ([UT System](http://utsystem.edu/news/2010/05/13/special-advisory-group-reports-merger-feasibility-of-ut-san-antonio-and-ut-health-science-center-at-san-antonio)). Its key findings held that a merger's benefit to UTSA's national stature would be marginal at best, that the three-to-five-year integration would inflict significant near-term damage on the health science center through costs, conflicts and distractions, and that both institutions were on strong positive trajectories a merger would only delay ([UT System](http://utsystem.edu/news/2010/05/13/special-advisory-group-reports-merger-feasibility-of-ut-san-antonio-and-ut-health-science-center-at-san-antonio)). The Regents accepted the report, and the institutions remained separate ([UT System](http://utsystem.edu/news/2010/05/13/special-advisory-group-reports-merger-feasibility-of-ut-san-antonio-and-ut-health-science-center-at-san-antonio)).

The calculus changed. The UT System shared plans in late August 2024 to merge the two entities into a unified institution ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/ut-health-ut-san-antonio-complete-merger/)), and on September 1, 2025 the merger took effect ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/ut-health-ut-san-antonio-complete-merger/)). The combined university spans more than 320 undergraduate and graduate degree programs, holds over $486 million in annual research expenditures and a $1.3 billion endowment, and joins two Carnegie R1 universities — a pairing Becker's described as the nation's largest university integration by research dollars and endowment since 2013 ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/ut-health-ut-san-antonio-complete-merger/)). The new entity is expected to contribute up to $7 billion annually to San Antonio's $41.1 billion biosciences and healthcare sector; UT Health San Antonio's clinical enterprise sees over 2.5 million patient visits per year and trains more than 1,000 fellows and residents ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/finance/ut-health-ut-san-antonio-complete-merger/)).

## A health system built for the AI era

In the months before the merger closed, the health science center opened the UT Health Multispecialty and Research Hospital in late 2024 — a facility designed from construction as AI infrastructure rather than merely added capacity ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ai/what-ut-health-san-antonio-discovered-by-building-a-hospital-for-the-ai-era/)). Every patient room was built with a video platform and wall-mounted camera, speaker and microphone, letting virtual nurses observe patients, verify medications and guide discharges from a centralized location ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ai/what-ut-health-san-antonio-discovered-by-building-a-hospital-for-the-ai-era/)). The hospital's electronic health record includes ambient documentation, three AI agents for scheduling and visit preparation, a revenue-cycle agent, and clinical decision support for nurses and physicians ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ai/what-ut-health-san-antonio-discovered-by-building-a-hospital-for-the-ai-era/)). After the merger, the health system began pairing AI and computer-science PhDs with clinicians through the newly formed College of Artificial Intelligence, Cyber, and Computing ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ai/what-ut-health-san-antonio-discovered-by-building-a-hospital-for-the-ai-era/)).

The institution's reach extended well beyond San Antonio. In 1998 the legislature authorized a Regional Academic Health Center in the lower Rio Grande Valley, administered by the San Antonio medical school, with a medical-education division in Harlingen and a research division in Edinburg ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/university-of-texas-health-science-center-at-san-antonio)). Its nursing school ran a B.S.N.-to-Ph.D. program in partnership with the [Texas Tech University Health Sciences Center](/universities/texas-tech-university-health-sciences-center/) School of Nursing in Lubbock ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/university-of-texas-health-science-center-at-san-antonio)).
