## Overview

Centennial College is Ontario's first community college — a public college in [Toronto](/places/toronto/), [Ontario](/places/ontario/), [Canada](/places/canada/), established in 1966 and named for Canada's then-upcoming 100th birthday. It primarily serves the eastern portion of the Greater Toronto Area through five campuses and seven satellite locations, and is recognised as one of Canada's most culturally diverse post-secondary institutions, with almost 100 ethno-cultural groups represented and 80 languages spoken on campus ([Centennial College](https://www.centennialcollege.ca/about-centennial/college-overview/); [Centennial College](https://www.centennialcollege.ca/about-centennial/60th-anniversary/centennial-college-celebrates-its-60th-anniversary/)).

The college's stated mission is to educate students for career success, and its vision is to transform lives and communities through learning ([Centennial College](https://www.centennialcollege.ca/about-centennial/college-overview/)). Its president and chief executive officer is Craig Stephenson ([Office of the Commissioner of Lobbying of Canada](https://lobbycanada.gc.ca/app/secure/ocl/lrs/do/vwRg?cno=348246&regId=916931)), and it is a member of [Colleges and Institutes Canada](/organizations/colleges-and-institutes-canada/), the national association of publicly supported colleges ([Colleges and Institutes Canada](https://www.collegesinstitutes.ca/colleges-and-institutes-in-your-community/our-members/?_protocols=sdg-accord)). In 2026 the college marked its 60th anniversary ([Centennial College](https://www.centennialcollege.ca/about-centennial/60th-anniversary/centennial-college-celebrates-its-60th-anniversary/)).

## From a radar factory to Ontario's first college

Centennial's story starts with a proposal, not a building. In 1965, Ontario Education Minister William Davis proposed a network of public colleges to help Ontario compete in an increasingly technological world, and a team of local volunteers found an empty radar instrument factory in Toronto's east end and persuaded the federal government to lease it as a temporary campus ([Centennial College](https://www.centennialcollege.ca/about-centennial/50th-anniversary)). The inaugural documents were signed in May 1966, and the plant was converted into the province's first college campus in just four months — when the doors opened on 17 October 1966, professors still had to shout over jackhammers and drills outside ([Centennial College](https://www.centennialcollege.ca/about-centennial/50th-anniversary)). The college was named Centennial for Canada's then-upcoming 100th birthday ([Centennial College](https://www.centennialcollege.ca/about-centennial/60th-anniversary/centennial-college-celebrates-its-60th-anniversary/)).

Its first instructors were recruited from industry and other schools to teach applied courses in business, technology, public relations, journalism, welfare services and early childhood education — the disciplines of an emerging service-based economy ([Centennial College](https://www.centennialcollege.ca/about-centennial/50th-anniversary)). Centennial opened months before its sister colleges, which followed in 1967, and it marked its 50th anniversary first, with celebrations running into 2017 for Ontario's 24 public colleges ([Centennial College](https://www.centennialcollege.ca/about-centennial/50th-anniversary)).

## Campuses, programmes and a campus in China

Centennial's five campuses are Ashtonbee, Downsview, Morningside, Progress and Story Arts Centre, with two further learning sites at Eglinton and Pickering ([Transition Resource Guide](https://www.transitionresourceguide.ca/post-secondary-education/research-schools/colleges/centennial-college)). Its programmes span business, media and arts, community and consumer services, engineering, health care and transportation, delivered full-time, part-time and through apprenticeships and joint programmes ([Transition Resource Guide](https://www.transitionresourceguide.ca/post-secondary-education/research-schools/colleges/centennial-college)). A federal filing describes more than 400 diploma, certificate and degree programmes ([Office of the Commissioner of Lobbying of Canada](https://lobbycanada.gc.ca/app/secure/ocl/lrs/do/vwRg?cno=348246&regId=916931)). The college's reach extends beyond Ontario: in October 2016 it opened Suzhou Centennial College, the first Canadian institution approved by China's Ministry of Education to deliver a Canadian curriculum in China, run with Suzhou University of Science and Technology about 100 km northwest of Shanghai ([The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/centennial-college-opens-campus-china/)). The Suzhou campus opened with 1,000 enrolled students and a target of 5,000 within five years, building on 20 years of the college's educational partnerships in China ([The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/centennial-college-opens-campus-china/)).

## The international-enrolment arc: warning, cap and cuts

Centennial's recent history is a case study in how Ontario's colleges came to depend on international tuition. The province's Auditor General warned in a December 2021 audit that international students made up only 30% of enrolment at Ontario's public colleges yet generated 68% of tuition revenue — $1.7 billion in 2020/21 — after international enrolment grew 342% between 2012/13 and 2020/21 while domestic enrolment fell 15% ([Toronto Metropolitan University](https://www.torontomu.ca/content/dam/global-migration-institute/Policy/pdfs/CERCMigration_PolicyBrief18_FEB2024.pdf)). The audit recommended a strategy to diversify revenue, but a 2023 follow-up found little or no progress on that front ([Toronto Metropolitan University](https://www.torontomu.ca/content/dam/global-migration-institute/Policy/pdfs/CERCMigration_PolicyBrief18_FEB2024.pdf)). Then the federal government capped international study permits on 22 January 2024, cutting Ontario's allotment roughly in half ([Canadian Press](https://hrnewscanada.com/centennial-college-suspends-49-programs-as-international-student-enrolment-drops/)).

In January 2025 Centennial suspended 49 full-time programmes for the 2025–26 academic year — 16 in its business school, 14 in its media school and seven in its engineering school — about 28% of its previous 177 full-time programmes, while 128 programmes continued to admit new students ([Canadian Press](https://hrnewscanada.com/centennial-college-suspends-49-programs-as-international-student-enrolment-drops/)). The college expected new international enrolments to fall 43% in the current academic year, nearly 5,000 fewer students than in 2023–24, and said job cuts were unavoidable ([Canadian Press](https://hrnewscanada.com/centennial-college-suspends-49-programs-as-international-student-enrolment-drops/)). In July 2025 the college clarified that, counting by programme title, it had suspended 54 programmes in 2025 — 49 in January and five in March — and still offered more than 100 programmes ([Centennial College](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/a-statement-from-centennial-college-clarifying-program-suspension-figures-834268356.html)).

International students were 30% of Ontario college enrolment but generated 68% of tuition revenue, $1.7 billion in 2020/21 ([Toronto Metropolitan University](https://www.torontomu.ca/content/dam/global-migration-institute/Policy/pdfs/CERCMigration_PolicyBrief18_FEB2024.pdf)) — which meant Centennial's finances rested on a revenue stream the province's own auditor had already flagged as a risk, years before the federal cap forced the 2025 cuts.

## FAQ

### When was Centennial College founded, and why is it called "Centennial"?

It was established in 1966 as Ontario's first community college, named for Canada's then-upcoming 100th birthday. Its first campus was a converted radar instrument factory in Toronto's east end, and its doors opened on 17 October 1966, months before the province's other public colleges opened in 1967.

### Where are Centennial College's campuses?

Its five campuses are Progress, Morningside, Ashtonbee, Downsview and the Story Arts Centre, all in Toronto, plus Eglinton and Pickering learning sites. Story Arts Centre programmes are moving to Progress Campus in summer 2026, so students start the Fall 2026 semester at Progress.

### Why did Centennial College suspend programmes in 2025?

The federal cap on international study permits cut new international enrolments, which the college expected to fall 43% — nearly 5,000 fewer students. It suspended 49 full-time programmes in January 2025 and five more in March, 54 in total, while 128 programmes continued admitting students.

### What is Suzhou Centennial College?

It is Centennial's learning site in Suzhou, China, opened in October 2016 with Suzhou University of Science and Technology. It was the first Canadian institution approved by China's Ministry of Education to deliver a Canadian curriculum in China, opening with 1,000 students and 18 programmes.
