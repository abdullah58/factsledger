## Overview

The University of Toronto is [Canada](/places/canada/)'s largest university, a public research institution founded by royal charter in 1827 as King's College, with 103,140 students across three campuses in the Greater [Toronto](/places/toronto/) Area ([University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/about-u-of-t/quick-facts)). It earns more research income than any other Canadian university — CA$1.59 billion in fiscal 2024 — and its discoveries range from insulin to deep learning ([Research Infosource](https://www.researchinfosource.com/news/2025)).

The scale is what first strikes you: 81,412 undergraduates and 21,728 graduate students in Fall 2025-26, with international students from 176 countries, the largest group from China at 14,050 ([University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/about-u-of-t/quick-facts)). It offers more than 700 undergraduate and 200 graduate programmes, and its 44 libraries hold 19.5 million physical volumes — the third-largest library system in North America ([Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/student/best-universities/best-universities-toronto)). The university ranks 21st in the world in the 2026 Times Higher Education rankings, first in Canada, and holds the same top national spot in the U.S. News, Shanghai and QS tables ([University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/about-u-of-t/quick-facts)).

## From King's College to a secular university

The university began as an instrument of colonial power. On 15 March 1827, a royal charter from King George IV established King's College at York, at the urging of John Strachan, the Archdeacon of York ([University of Toronto Libraries](https://collections.library.utoronto.ca/explore/heritage/exhibits/chronology)). It was the first institution of higher learning in Upper Canada, and it was closely controlled by the Church of England. Teaching did not begin for another sixteen years: King's College opened on 8 June 1843 in a renovated Parliament building on Front Street, with just 26 students signing the roll ([University of Toronto Libraries](https://collections.library.utoronto.ca/explore/heritage/exhibits/chronology)).

The break came quickly after that. Reformist politicians fought the clergy's control of the college, and on 30 May 1849 a provincial act abolished King's College and created the non-denominational University of Toronto, effective 1 January 1850 ([University of Toronto Libraries](https://collections.library.utoronto.ca/explore/heritage/exhibits/chronology)). John McCaul became its first president that day. An 1853 act then separated the university's administrative and teaching functions, assigning teaching to the newly created University College ([University of Toronto Libraries](https://collections.library.utoronto.ca/explore/heritage/exhibits/chronology)). Over the following decades the university arranged federation with several church colleges — Victoria, Trinity and St. Michael's among them — producing the collegiate system that still shapes the St. George campus, where each college keeps substantial autonomy over its own affairs.

## Three campuses, one university

The modern university is tri-campus. St. George, the downtown campus around Queen's Park, is the oldest and largest, with 69,921 students; Mississauga has 17,361 and Scarborough 15,858 ([University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/about-u-of-t/quick-facts)). The suburban campuses opened in the 1960s — Scarborough in 1964 and Erindale, now Mississauga, in 1967 — and together they now teach nearly half of the university's undergraduates.

Growth has been rapid even by recent standards: enrolment rose 22 per cent between 2013-14 and 2024-25, when 102,431 students attended, and graduate enrolment grew 36 per cent over the same period ([U of T Magazine](https://magazine.utoronto.ca/campus/what-numbers-say-last-12-years/)). The university earmarked $380 million in financial support to students in 2024-25, and 47 per cent of its full-time [Ontario](/places/ontario/) students received provincial OSAP assistance in 2023-24 ([U of T Magazine](https://magazine.utoronto.ca/campus/what-numbers-say-last-12-years/)).

## Canada's research-income leader

The ranking tables measure reputation; the research-income tables measure money, and there the gap is wide. In fiscal 2024 the University of Toronto reported $1.59 billion in research income, up 10.8 per cent, leading Canada's Top 50 Research Universities and roughly doubling the second-place institution ([Research Infosource](https://www.researchinfosource.com/news/2025)). Research Infosource named it Research University of the Year 2025 in the medical category ([Research Infosource](https://www.researchinfosource.com/news/2025)). The university and its partner hospitals hold 330 Canada Research Chairs — significantly more than any other university ([U of T Magazine](https://magazine.utoronto.ca/campus/what-numbers-say-last-12-years/)).

That funding base shows up in prizes: U of T researchers take 48 per cent of the major international prizes awarded to faculty at Canadian universities, and 25 per cent of the most prestigious national awards, including Killam and Steacie Prizes ([U of T Magazine](https://magazine.utoronto.ca/campus/what-numbers-say-last-12-years/)). The university also ranks first in the world for sustainability and third for health-sciences research output with its partner hospitals ([U of T Magazine](https://magazine.utoronto.ca/campus/what-numbers-say-last-12-years/)).

## Insulin, deep learning and the Nobel record

Two discoveries define the university's scientific reputation, a century apart. In John MacLeod's laboratory in 1921, Frederick Banting and Charles Best treated dogs so that they no longer produced trypsin, allowing insulin to be extracted and used to treat diabetes ([NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1923/banting/facts/)). Banting shared the 1923 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with MacLeod "for the discovery of insulin," with the University of Toronto listed as his affiliation ([NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1923/banting/facts/)). The medical school's later breakthroughs — James Till and Ernest McCulloch's discovery of stem cells, and Lap-Chee Tsui's discovery of the gene responsible for cystic fibrosis — extended the same tradition ([University of Toronto Alumni](https://alumni.utoronto.ca/news/university-toronto-receives-single-largest-gift-canadian-history-james-and-louise-temerty)).

The second came from computer science. In 1983–1985 Geoffrey Hinton used tools from statistical physics to create the Boltzmann machine, which can learn to recognise characteristic elements in a set of data ([NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2024/hinton/facts/)). In 2024 he shared the Nobel Prize in Physics with John Hopfield "for foundational discoveries and inventions that enable machine learning with artificial neural networks," again with the University of Toronto as his affiliation ([NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2024/hinton/facts/)). Hinton had already shared the 2018 ACM A.M. Turing Award — computing's equivalent of the Nobel — with Yoshua Bengio and Yann LeCun for the deep-learning breakthroughs that made neural networks a critical component of computing ([ACM](https://www.acm.org/articles/bulletins/2019/march/turing-award-2018)). The university's own magazine notes Hinton was the fourth U of T faculty member to receive a Nobel Prize ([U of T Magazine](https://magazine.utoronto.ca/campus/what-numbers-say-last-12-years/)).

The alumni record runs through public life as well. Lester B. Pearson enrolled in history at the university, took his BA in 1919 after war service, and later taught modern history there before entering the foreign service in 1928 ([NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1957/ceremony-speech/)). He received the 1957 Nobel Peace Prize for his work on the Suez Crisis, and went on to become Canada's 14th prime minister. In all, five Canadian prime ministers count among the university's alumni, along with the writers Margaret Atwood and Michael Ondaatje ([Times Higher Education](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/student/best-universities/best-universities-toronto)).

## The startup university

The university's newest identity is as a company factory. Its 12 business incubators and accelerators support almost 1,000 entrepreneurial teams a year, and in the past five years entrepreneurs from the U of T community have launched 941 startups, which have secured over $12 billion in funding and created 17,000 jobs ([U of T Magazine](https://magazine.utoronto.ca/campus/what-numbers-say-last-12-years/)). More than 1,000 patent applications were filed in the past decade, and 80 per cent of inventions name a post-doctoral researcher or student as an inventor or co-inventor ([U of T Magazine](https://magazine.utoronto.ca/campus/what-numbers-say-last-12-years/)).

The cleanest example is [Blue J](/organizations/blue-j/). [Benjamin Alarie](/people/benjamin-alarie/), Professor of Law and Osler Chair in Business Law, is co-founder and CEO of Blue J, a University of Toronto spinout founded in 2015 ([University of Toronto Faculty of Law](https://jackmanlaw.utoronto.ca/people/benjamin-alarie)). He launched it with two other Faculty of Law professors to apply artificial intelligence to tax research, first with predictive analytics and, after a 2023 relaunch, with large language model technology from [OpenAI](/organizations/openai/) ([University of Toronto News](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/u-t-startup-blue-j-legal-raises-167-million-globe-and-mail)). In August 2025 Blue J raised US$122 million (C$167 million) in a Series D round led by Sapphire Ventures and Oak HC/FT, valuing the company at more than US$300 million — and its platform is now used by major firms and the Canada Revenue Agency itself ([University of Toronto News](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/u-t-startup-blue-j-legal-raises-167-million-globe-and-mail)). The tax authority's own research tool was, in other words, built in a law faculty seminar room.

Philanthropy has followed the same scale. On 24 September 2020 James and Louise Temerty gave $250 million to the Faculty of Medicine — the single largest gift ever made in Canada — and the faculty was renamed the Temerty Faculty of Medicine in their honour ([University of Toronto Alumni](https://alumni.utoronto.ca/news/university-toronto-receives-single-largest-gift-canadian-history-james-and-louise-temerty)). The gift funds a new Centre for AI Research and Education in Medicine, a new faculty building, and $10 million for the Dean's COVID-19 Priority Fund ([University of Toronto Alumni](https://alumni.utoronto.ca/news/university-toronto-receives-single-largest-gift-canadian-history-james-and-louise-temerty)).

## Frequently asked questions

**How big is the University of Toronto?** It is Canada's largest university by enrolment, with 103,140 students in Fall 2025-26 — 81,412 undergraduates and 21,728 graduate students — across its St. George, Mississauga and Scarborough campuses ([University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/about-u-of-t/quick-facts)). International students come from 176 countries.

**When was the University of Toronto founded?** By royal charter on 15 March 1827, as King's College, the first institution of higher learning in Upper Canada ([University of Toronto Libraries](https://collections.library.utoronto.ca/explore/heritage/exhibits/chronology)). It became the non-denominational University of Toronto on 1 January 1850 ([University of Toronto Libraries](https://collections.library.utoronto.ca/explore/heritage/exhibits/chronology)).

**What is the University of Toronto best known for?** The discovery of insulin by Banting and Best in 1921, which won the 1923 Nobel Prize in Medicine, and deep learning — Geoffrey Hinton's Boltzmann machine work of 1983–85, which won the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics and the 2018 Turing Award ([NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1923/banting/facts/); [NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2024/hinton/facts/)). It also leads Canada in research income, at $1.59 billion in fiscal 2024 ([Research Infosource](https://www.researchinfosource.com/news/2025)).

**What is Blue J's connection to the University of Toronto?** Blue J is a University of Toronto spinout founded in 2015 by three Faculty of Law professors, led by Benjamin Alarie, and it applies AI to tax research ([University of Toronto Faculty of Law](https://jackmanlaw.utoronto.ca/people/benjamin-alarie)). Its platform is used by major firms and the Canada Revenue Agency ([University of Toronto News](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/u-t-startup-blue-j-legal-raises-167-million-globe-and-mail)).

**How does the University of Toronto rank globally?** 21st in the 2026 Times Higher Education World University Rankings, first in Canada; it also ranks 20th in U.S. News, 24th in the Shanghai ranking and 32nd in QS ([University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/about-u-of-t/quick-facts)).
