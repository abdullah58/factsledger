## Overview

Arizona State University is the testing ground for the most ambitious — and most argued-over — idea in American public higher education: that a university can be both enormous and excellent. Founded in 1885 and based in Tempe, Arizona, ASU enrolls more than 180,000 students across its Phoenix-area campuses and online programs, making it one of the largest universities in the United States ([AZ Big Media](https://azbigmedia.com/business/asu-takes-top-spot-in-innovation-for-10th-year-in-a-row/)). Since 2002 it has been led by president Michael M. Crow, who set out to rebuild it as a "New American University" — "an institution combining the highest levels of academic excellence, inclusiveness to a broad demographic, and maximum societal impact" ([EDUCAUSE Review](https://er.educause.edu/articles/2012/7/no-more-excuses-michael-m-crow-on-analytics)).

The results, by the numbers, are striking. Between 2002 and 2011, enrollment grew 30% to a record 72,254, research expenditures nearly tripled, degrees awarded rose 51.5%, and the six-year graduation rate climbed nearly 20% ([EDUCAUSE Review](https://er.educause.edu/articles/2012/7/no-more-excuses-michael-m-crow-on-analytics)). U.S. News & World Report has ranked ASU the #1 Most Innovative School every year since it created the category — ten straight years through 2025, ahead of MIT and Stanford each time — in a peer vote of college presidents, provosts and admissions deans ([AZ Big Media](https://azbigmedia.com/business/asu-takes-top-spot-in-innovation-for-10th-year-in-a-row/)).

## The model and its critics

Crow's method was analytics at industrial scale: a "Student 360" system giving advisors a full view of each student's academic, financial and behavioral data, predictive models steering students toward completable majors, and standardized, software-assisted instruction for giant introductory courses ([EDUCAUSE Review](https://er.educause.edu/articles/2012/7/no-more-excuses-michael-m-crow-on-analytics)). Crow's own defense: elite institutions "make up a small percentage of the higher education enterprise," and serving everyone else requires knowing "exactly where we are" through data ([EDUCAUSE Review](https://er.educause.edu/articles/2012/7/no-more-excuses-michael-m-crow-on-analytics)).

Critics see a corporation. In a widely discussed 2015 Inside Higher Ed essay, a commentator called ASU "pretty clearly set up as a factory of credentialing," pointing to software-taught math, writing instructors carrying double the recommended loads, relentless student-data mining, and enrollment growth even as state funding fell — concluding the university was "indistinguishable from Amazon" ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/blogs/just-visiting/asu-new-american-university-its-terrifying)). The essay is polemical, but it names the genuine tension the model creates: whether access at this scale can coexist with the labor-intensive teaching the humanities, in particular, require.

## What the rankings capture

Beyond the innovation streak, ASU has stacked up parallel #1s: five straight years atop Times Higher Education's U.S. ranking for work aligned with the UN Sustainable Development Goals, #1 in the U.S. for sustainability practices, #1 public university for hosting international students, and top national spots in supply-chain management, anthropology research spending and journalism awards ([AZ Big Media](https://azbigmedia.com/business/asu-takes-top-spot-in-innovation-for-10th-year-in-a-row/)). Its research footprint includes the Psyche mission — the first NASA deep-space mission led by ASU — and in 2024 it became the first university to collaborate formally with OpenAI ([AZ Big Media](https://azbigmedia.com/business/asu-takes-top-spot-in-innovation-for-10th-year-in-a-row/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim a single definitive enrollment figure — on-campus, total, and online-inclusive counts differ across sources and years. It does not endorse either the boosterish or the dystopian reading of the New American University model; the critical view is one commentator's, presented as such. The 1885 founding as a territorial teachers' college is standard institutional history not re-verified here. Rankings cited are peer-voted or index-based, not objective measures of educational quality.
