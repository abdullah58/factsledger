## Overview

The Albert Einstein College of Medicine is a medical college in New York, United States, whose institutional independence was earned in stages rather than granted at birth: founded by Yeshiva University in 1955, transferred to Montefiore in 2015, and granted independent degree-granting authority through an absolute charter on March 4, 2019 ([St. Louis Jewish Light](https://stljewishlight.org/world-news/jewish-philanthropist-donates-1b-eliminating-med-school-tuition-for-new-lucky-students/), [Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/albert-einstein-college-of-medicine-achieves-independent-degree-granting-authority)). Around the same period it was a significant research operation, drawing $172M in NIH funding in 2018 ([Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/albert-einstein-college-of-medicine-achieves-independent-degree-granting-authority)). In February 2024 Ruth Gottesman's $1B gift — the largest ever to a US medical school — made tuition free for its students ([St. Louis Jewish Light](https://stljewishlight.org/world-news/jewish-philanthropist-donates-1b-eliminating-med-school-tuition-for-new-lucky-students/)). The gift dominated national coverage, but the independence arc underneath it is what gives the profile its structure — and what distinguishes a medical college with a real institutional history from a one-time headline.

## From Yeshiva to Montefiore to independence

The college was founded by Yeshiva University in 1955 and later transferred to Montefiore in 2015 ([St. Louis Jewish Light](https://stljewishlight.org/world-news/jewish-philanthropist-donates-1b-eliminating-med-school-tuition-for-new-lucky-students/)). For six decades the college's identity was bound to its parent institutions; the 2015 transfer to Montefiore shifted that anchor without granting the college its own credentials. The key institutional milestone came on March 4, 2019, when the college received an absolute charter granting it independent degree-granting authority — the formal legal step that let its academic credentials stand on their own, separate from its parent institutions ([Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/albert-einstein-college-of-medicine-achieves-independent-degree-granting-authority)). This profile dates full independence precisely to that charter and does not describe the college as fully independent before it ([Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/albert-einstein-college-of-medicine-achieves-independent-degree-granting-authority)). The institutional story runs parallel to a research one: the college drew $172M in NIH funding in 2018, a scale of federal research support that underscores its standing as a research institution, not merely a teaching one ([Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/albert-einstein-college-of-medicine-achieves-independent-degree-granting-authority)).

## The billion-dollar gift

In February 2024 Ruth Gottesman gave the college $1B, described as the largest gift ever made to a US medical school, on terms that made tuition free ([St. Louis Jewish Light](https://stljewishlight.org/world-news/jewish-philanthropist-donates-1b-eliminating-med-school-tuition-for-new-lucky-students/)). At the time, annual tuition exceeded $59,000, and the gift covered the 737 MD students then enrolled ([St. Louis Jewish Light](https://stljewishlight.org/world-news/jewish-philanthropist-donates-1b-eliminating-med-school-tuition-for-new-lucky-students/)). The tuition-free move was corroborated by higher-education trade coverage days later ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/quick-takes/2024/02/27/billion-dollar-gift-makes-tuition-free-ny-medical-college)). Despite the transformation of its financial foundation, the Einstein name was retained — a continuity signal amid a radical change in what attending costs ([St. Louis Jewish Light](https://stljewishlight.org/world-news/jewish-philanthropist-donates-1b-eliminating-med-school-tuition-for-new-lucky-students/)). The gift arrived five years after the 2019 charter: an independent degree-granting institution, now tuition-free. In sequence, the college's modern history reads as two kinds of liberation — first the legal autonomy of the absolute charter, then the financial removal of tuition as a barrier for its MD students ([Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/albert-einstein-college-of-medicine-achieves-independent-degree-granting-authority), [St. Louis Jewish Light](https://stljewishlight.org/world-news/jewish-philanthropist-donates-1b-eliminating-med-school-tuition-for-new-lucky-students/)).
