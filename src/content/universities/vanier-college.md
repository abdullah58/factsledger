## Overview

Vanier College is a publicly funded English-language CEGEP in Saint-Laurent, [Montreal](/places/montreal/), Quebec, [Canada](/places/canada/), named in honour of General Georges P. Vanier, a Canadian soldier, diplomat and former Governor General of Canada. It opened on 8 September 1970 with close to 1,400 students in its first cohort, and today its student population is just over 6,000 ([Vanier College](https://vanier-prod.okam.one/about)).

That short description hides an unusual record: Vanier was the second English-language CEGEP to open in Quebec, it briefly housed the national office of the country's college association, and its alumni include a future Vice President of the United States, a future leader of Canada's New Democratic Party, and the core of a Polaris Music Prize-winning band — all from a single campus in Saint-Laurent.

## A CEGEP, and the second English one

Vanier only makes sense inside Quebec's CEGEP system, a structure no other Canadian province has. A CEGEP — *Collège d'enseignement général et professionnel* — is a public school providing the first level of post-secondary education, and Quebec's first CEGEPs opened in 1967, a few months after the General and Vocational Colleges Act ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://development.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/cegep)). The network now comprises 48 public CEGEPs, five of them English-language and 43 French-language ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://development.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/cegep)). Vanier was the second English-language CEGEP to open, and a significant percentage of its staff came from Dawson College, the first, to its Sainte-Croix campus ([McGill Journal of Education](https://mje.mcgill.ca/article/download/7993/5921/0)). The college describes itself as recognised for academic and athletic excellence, for leadership in sustainability, and for commitment to the Indigenous Education Protocol ([Vanier College](https://vanier-prod.okam.one/about)). It also acknowledges that its campus sits on the traditional unceded territory of the Kanien'kehá:ka (Mohawk) Nation of the Six Nations Haudenosaunee Confederacy ([Vanier College](https://vanier-prod.okam.one/about)).

## Two years to university, three years to work

Vanier's programmes follow the CEGEP split between pre-university study, which prepares students for university, and technical training, which prepares them for the labour market — both recognised by the government-sanctioned Diploma of College Studies (DEC), with shorter programmes leading to an Attestation of College Studies ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://development.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/cegep)). The college offers a wide range of pre-university, career and Continuing Education programmes in a diverse campus environment, with a mission "to provide a life-enriching learning experience that prepares students to succeed academically and professionally as engaged citizens of the world" ([Vanier College](https://vanier-prod.okam.one/about)). On the career side it offers 16 programmes leading to a Diploma of College Studies, each three years long and designed to prepare students directly for the workforce, with the option to continue to university ([Vanier College](https://www.vaniercollege.qc.ca/programs/career?program_length=64)). Named programmes span health, technology, business and the arts, including Nursing, Animal Health Technology, Architectural Technology, Computer Science Technology and Business Administration ([Vanier College](https://www.vaniercollege.qc.ca/programs/career?program_length=64)).

| Programme route | Length | Credential | Destination |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-university | Two years | Diploma of College Studies (DCS/DEC) | University |
| Career / technical | Three years | Diploma of College Studies (DCS/DEC) | Workforce, with university option |
| Continuing Education | Varies | DCS or Attestation of College Studies (ACS/AEC) | Workforce or further study |

More than 169,240 students were enrolled in regular education at CEGEPs across Quebec in 2016–17 ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://development.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/cegep)) — and that scale is the point: Vanier is one campus in a 48-college public network built to make post-secondary education widely accessible, not a standalone private college.

## Alumni and a national role

Vanier's best-known alumni arrived within a decade of its opening and then scattered in very different directions. Kamala Harris graduated from high school in 1981, enrolled at Vanier after her mother took a research position at McGill University, and later transferred to Howard University in Washington, D.C. ([National Museum of African American History and Culture](https://nmaahc.si.edu/explore/stories/black-women-race-white-house)). Thomas Mulcair took his pre-university diploma in social sciences at Vanier, helped lead a student strike there, entered law school at McGill at 18, and went on to lead the New Democratic Party from 2012 to 2017 ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/thomas-mulcair/)). Patrick Watson's career began shortly after he graduated from Vanier's music programme, and his band's original lineup included three other Vanier music alumni — Simon Angell, Robbie Kuster and Mishka Stein — before *Close to Paradise* won the Polaris Music Prize in 2007 ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://development.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/patrick-watson-musician-and-band)). Vanier also played a brief national role: the national office of the Association of Canadian Community Colleges sat at Vanier before moving to [Centennial College](/universities/centennial-college/) in Toronto in 1973 ([Colleges and Institutes Canada](https://www.collegesinstitutes.ca/cicans-50th-anniversary/)), the association now known as [Colleges and Institutes Canada](/organizations/colleges-and-institutes-canada/). Its women's D1 soccer team brought home a national bronze medal in November 2026 ([Vanier College](https://www.vaniercollege.qc.ca/)).

## FAQ

### What is Vanier College?

Vanier College is a publicly funded English-language CEGEP in Saint-Laurent, Montreal, Quebec, named for General Georges P. Vanier, a soldier, diplomat and former Governor General of Canada. It opened on 8 September 1970 with close to 1,400 students and today has just over 6,000 students.

### What is a CEGEP?

A CEGEP is a publicly funded Quebec college providing the first level of post-secondary education. Quebec is the only province with the system: 48 public CEGEPs, five English-language and 43 French-language, offering pre-university and technical programmes leading to a Diploma of College Studies.

### How long do Vanier programmes take?

Pre-university programmes take two years and prepare students for university. Career programmes take three years, lead to a Diploma of College Studies, and are designed to prepare students directly for the workforce, with the option to continue to university.

### Did Kamala Harris attend Vanier College?

Yes. She graduated from high school in 1981, enrolled at Vanier College in Montreal, and later transferred to Howard University in Washington, D.C., according to the Smithsonian's National Museum of African American History and Culture.

### Who else studied at Vanier?

Thomas Mulcair took a social-sciences diploma there before studying law at McGill and later leading the New Democratic Party, and musician Patrick Watson graduated from its music programme, as did three of his original bandmates.
