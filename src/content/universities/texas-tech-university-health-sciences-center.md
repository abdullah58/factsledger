## Overview

Texas Tech University Health Sciences Center (TTUHSC) is a public health-sciences university headquartered in Lubbock, Texas — a separate state institution from Texas Tech University, created to answer West Texas's doctor shortage ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/texas-tech-university-health-sciences-center)). Authorized by the 61st Texas Legislature in May 1969 as the Texas Tech University School of Medicine and renamed the Health Sciences Center in 1979, it operates as a multi-campus institution across West Texas with a statutory mission centered on rural health care ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/texas-tech-university-health-sciences-center)).

## A medical school born of a three-city war

Lubbock began seeking a medical division for Texas Tech in the early 1960s, but the 1965 bill died on Governor John Connally's veto — and the veto detonated a regional fight. Lubbock, Amarillo and El Paso each claimed the school: El Paso cited the largest patient pool for teaching, Amarillo a new medical park, Lubbock the advantage of a major university campus ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/texas-tech-university-health-sciences-center)). West Texans argued the rest of Texas was served by University of Texas and Baylor schools while their region had twice the national patient-to-doctor ratio and a high infant-mortality rate ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/texas-tech-university-health-sciences-center)). The Coordinating Board authorized two new medical schools — one in Houston under the University of Texas, one in Lubbock under Texas Technological College — and Lubbock's legislators pushed House Bill 498 through the Sixty-first Legislature in 1969, signed by Governor Preston Smith, a supporter of the idea since 1949 ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/texas-tech-university-health-sciences-center)).

A legal quirk shaped the institution permanently: because ad valorem taxes cannot be used to construct educational facilities, the medical school could not be part of Texas Tech University itself — it had to be chartered as a separate state institution, even though both shared the same regents and president ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/texas-tech-university-health-sciences-center)). The School of Medicine opened in August 1972 with 36 freshmen and 25 junior transfers and graduated its first class in May 1974; nursing and allied-health schools followed in the 1980s, all aimed at West Texas's underserved counties ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/texas-tech-university-health-sciences-center)). The Lubbock School of Nursing also ran a B.S.N.-to-Ph.D. program in partnership with the [UT Health San Antonio](/universities/ut-health-san-antonio/) School of Nursing — nineteen students were enrolled in 1993 ([Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/university-of-texas-health-science-center-at-san-antonio)).

## 2026: the canceled lecture

In January 2026 TTUHSC canceled a scheduled January 26 talk by Dr. Shelley Sella, a retired OB-GYN and author, on medical and ethical considerations in late-pregnancy abortion — an event organized by the campus chapter of Medical Students for Choice ([Texas Tribune](https://gmg-kprc-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/news/texas/2026/02/18/texas-tech-medical-school-cancels-talk-on-health-ethical-considerations-in-late-pregnancy-abortions/)). The cancellation followed public pressure from anti-abortion activist Mark Lee Dickson and the campus chapter of Turning Point USA, who argued the event would be illegal on a public university campus — a claim abortion-rights advocates dispute, noting Texas law does not restrict medical education or discussion of abortion care ([Texas Tribune](https://gmg-kprc-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/news/texas/2026/02/18/texas-tech-medical-school-cancels-talk-on-health-ethical-considerations-in-late-pregnancy-abortions/)). University officials said only that the center "evaluated the request and determined it was not in the best interest of the university to host this event on campus" ([Texas Tribune](https://gmg-kprc-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/news/texas/2026/02/18/texas-tech-medical-school-cancels-talk-on-health-ethical-considerations-in-late-pregnancy-abortions/)). PEN America called the cancellation "yet another example of the dangerous pattern of censorship spreading across Texas universities" ([PEN America](https://pen.org/press-release/texas-tech-gives-into-political-pressure-to-cancel-a-talk/)).
