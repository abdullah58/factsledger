## Overview

The University of Southern Maine (USM) is a public university in the University of Maine System, holding classes in Portland, Gorham and Lewiston, Maine ([Mainebiz](https://mainebiz.biz/article/rebrand-reversal-usm-tables-name-change-for-now/)). Its roots go back to 1878, when the state opened Gorham Normal School — a teachers' college — in the leased buildings of the closed Gorham Academy; the Academy's 1806 building is still in university use, housing arts offices and classrooms ([SAH Archipedia](https://sah-archipedia.org/buildings/ME-01-005-0056)). After a 1970 merger and a 1978 renaming, the university became nationally known a decade ago for one of the most wrenching retrenchments in American public higher education — and then, quietly, stabilized ([Mainebiz](https://mainebiz.biz/article/rebrand-reversal-usm-tables-name-change-for-now/); [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/2014/10/07/new-southern-maine-cuts-announced-after-months-protest-over-old-ones)).

## Two schools, one university

USM has used several names since its beginnings in 1878. From 1957 to 1970, the campus in Maine's largest city was known as the University of Maine at Portland; in 1970 it merged with Gorham State College to form the University of Maine at Portland-Gorham, which was renamed the University of Southern Maine in 1978 ([Mainebiz](https://mainebiz.biz/article/rebrand-reversal-usm-tables-name-change-for-now/)). The Gorham campus, with its 19th-century academy buildings, remains the more residential of the university's sites ([SAH Archipedia](https://sah-archipedia.org/buildings/ME-01-005-0056)).

## The 2014 retrenchment

In October 2014, USM announced plans to eliminate between a fifth and a sixth of its faculty — about 50 of roughly 300 full-time faculty — and roughly 100 of its 750 staff, to close what the administration said was a $16 million budget deficit ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/2014/10/07/new-southern-maine-cuts-announced-after-months-protest-over-old-ones)). President David Flanagan, a former power-company CEO who had taken office that August, said enrollment had declined by nearly a third in five years while costs had not adjusted; the alternative, he said, was raising tuition from $7,700 to $10,000 a year, "too much for Maine students to bear" ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/2014/10/07/new-southern-maine-cuts-announced-after-months-protest-over-old-ones)).

The academic restructuring was sweeping: English, philosophy and history would merge into a single humanities department of 22.5 professors (down from 29); chemistry, math and physics could become a single major; music, art and theater would combine; and remaining faculty would teach four courses a semester instead of three ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/2014/10/07/new-southern-maine-cuts-announced-after-months-protest-over-old-ones)). Faculty disputed the deficit figure, filed union grievances, and warned the cuts would accelerate enrollment declines — and the fight made USM, in Inside Higher Ed's words, "a national cause célèbre for opponents of sharp cuts to higher education" ([Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/2014/10/07/new-southern-maine-cuts-announced-after-months-protest-over-old-ones)).

## Stabilization

A decade later, the university's numbers tell a steadier story. Official University of Maine System data puts USM's fall headcount at 6,190 in 2023, 6,257 in 2024 and 6,168 in 2025, with 6,325 projected for fall 2026 — up 2.5% — and 72.9% of students full-time ([University of Maine System Board of Trustees](https://public.onboardmeetings.com/Meeting/qpRiuKxn8GpWkrSY1EpjQ0sgspu0Q1G7Pak1pWiR6McA/Fuz4GJlSs%2F3rNH3ysFJkR6wIbRMq1a%2Fz0FlrVVd7j4IA/Y%2FMRIrg9WS7UNu7%2Fp9vdzOtkOk4I26C9d1o23b43neoA/uyXIaiCu5PVImXM6s9WHUJthFaKPJWXIG36j4iRdg8kA/Agenda Document)). Mainebiz reported in 2026 that USM had more than doubled its doctoral enrollment over five years, with significant growth in undergraduate and graduate education and counselor education, along with cybersecurity and marketing programs ([Mainebiz](https://mainebiz.biz/article/spring-enrollment-gains-ground-at-maines-public-universities/)).
