## Overview

Caltech is having the most turbulent year in its modern history, and none of the reference pages about it say so. In 2026 the Pasadena institute installed a new president, learned that [NASA](https://factsledger.com/organizations/nasa/) will put the management contract for the Jet Propulsion Laboratory — a relationship Caltech has held without challenge since the lab joined NASA in 1958 — up for competitive bidding for the first time, and watched its flagship laboratory lay off another 550 employees, the fourth workforce reduction in under two years ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-to-compete-contract-for-jet-propulsion-laboratory-management/)). The California Institute of Technology remains one of the world's most decorated research universities — 48 [Nobel](https://factsledger.com/events/nobel-prize/) laureates and a 3:1 student-faculty ratio, by its own October 2025 count — but it enters its second century-plus under its tenth president at a moment when funding, politics, and its defining partnership with NASA are all in motion at once ([Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/documents/149/At_a_Glance_MASTER_101525.pdf)).

## 2026: a new president, a bidding war, and a shrinking lab

Ray Jayawardhana began his tenure as Caltech's tenth president on July 1, 2026, succeeding Thomas Rosenbaum, who had led the institute since 2014 ([NASA JPL](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/jpl/caltech-welcomes-astrophysicist-ray-jayawardhana-as-new-president/)). He arrived from Johns Hopkins University, where he served as provost overseeing ten schools, after earlier stints as dean of arts and sciences at Cornell and on the faculty at the University of Toronto ([Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/ray-jayawardhana-appointed-caltech-tenth-president)). As a scientist he studies how planets and planetary systems form, sits on the core science team for the Near Infrared Imager and Slitless Spectrograph aboard the James Webb Space Telescope, and has co-authored 180 refereed papers with over 10,000 citations ([NASA JPL](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/jpl/caltech-welcomes-astrophysicist-ray-jayawardhana-as-new-president/)). He earned his Ph.D. in astronomy from Harvard and his B.S. in astronomy and physics from Yale ([NASA JPL](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/jpl/caltech-welcomes-astrophysicist-ray-jayawardhana-as-new-president/)).

The choice was openly about communication as much as scholarship. Search committee chair Jonas Zmuidzinas said the Caltech community had asked for "a strong communicator" who could be "an effective steward of JPL" and inspire confidence among philanthropic partners ([Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/ray-jayawardhana-appointed-caltech-tenth-president)). His honors include a Guggenheim Fellowship, the Rutherford Medal in Physics, and the Carl Sagan Medal; asteroid 4668 Rayjay is named after him ([Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/ray-jayawardhana-appointed-caltech-tenth-president)). JPL director Dave Gallagher called his arrival "a pivotal moment for Caltech, JPL, and NASA" ([NASA JPL](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/jpl/caltech-welcomes-astrophysicist-ray-jayawardhana-as-new-president/)).

The JPL announcement landed just before. In May 2026, NASA said it would compete the next contract for managing and operating the Jet Propulsion Laboratory, ending more than six decades in which every NASA contract for the lab was awarded sole-source to Caltech ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-to-compete-contract-for-jet-propulsion-laboratory-management/)). The current agreement began October 1, 2018 and runs through September 30, 2028, with a potential maximum value of $30 billion if all options are exercised ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-to-compete-contract-for-jet-propulsion-laboratory-management/)). Administrator Jared Isaacman framed the move as stewardship: the growth of the space economy, he said, means there may now be a competitive market for running a federally funded research and development center ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-to-compete-contract-for-jet-propulsion-laboratory-management/)). Caltech, which has managed JPL since the lab's 1958 transfer from the U.S. Army, said the announcement came as "no surprise" and that a bid team had been in place since the prior summer, according to contemporary press reporting ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-to-compete-contract-for-jet-propulsion-laboratory-management/)).

The first challenger moved within months. At its September 2–4, 2026 quarterly meeting, the Texas A&M University System Board of Regents authorized building a team to prepare a rival proposal ([The Battalion](https://thebatt.com/news/regents-approve-am-system-to-bid-on-management-of-nasas-jet-propulsion-lab/)). Chancellor Glenn Hegar pointed to the system's co-management of Los Alamos National Laboratory and the Pantex nuclear weapons plant, plus a research partnership with JPL dating to 2019 ([The Battalion](https://thebatt.com/news/regents-approve-am-system-to-bid-on-management-of-nasas-jet-propulsion-lab/)). A&M is also nearing completion of a $200 million Space Institute near NASA's Johnson Space Center in Houston ([The Battalion](https://thebatt.com/news/regents-approve-am-system-to-bid-on-management-of-nasas-jet-propulsion-lab/)).

Meanwhile the lab itself was shrinking. In October 2026 JPL laid off 550 employees — about 11% of its workforce — the fourth reduction since the start of 2024 ([SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/more-layoffs-at-jpl/)). The earlier rounds: roughly 100 contractors in January 2024, 530 employees plus 40 contractors in February 2024, and 325 employees in November 2024 ([SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/more-layoffs-at-jpl/)). Director Dave Gallagher said the October cuts were part of a restructuring begun in July and "not a response to the ongoing government shutdown," while noting the White House's fiscal 2026 budget request had proposed steep cuts to NASA science programs, including JPL's Mars Sample Return mission ([SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/more-layoffs-at-jpl/)). Gallagher himself had taken over in May 2026 when director Laurie Leshin stepped down for personal reasons, after 36 years at the lab as, most recently, associate director for strategic integration ([SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/more-layoffs-at-jpl/)). A detail that matters to Pasadena: JPL's staff are Caltech employees, not federal civil servants ([SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/more-layoffs-at-jpl/)).

The cuts landed in a community still counting its losses from the January Eaton Fire. Representative Judy Chu, whose district includes JPL, called the layoffs "an absolute tragedy," noting they came nine months after the fire destroyed thousands of homes ([Chemistry World](https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/nasas-jet-propulsion-lab-and-home-of-mars-rovers-loses-10-of-its-staff/4022320.article)). More than 200 JPL employees lost their homes in the fire, by then-director Leshin's account as reported at the time ([Chemistry World](https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/nasas-jet-propulsion-lab-and-home-of-mars-rovers-loses-10-of-its-staff/4022320.article)).

## From Throop to Caltech: an unlikely rise with a darker side

The institute began on November 2, 1891, as Throop University, a school founded in Pasadena by Chicago businessman Amos Throop that former Caltech archivist Judith Goodstein later called a "thoroughly undistinguished little college" that barely survived its first year ([Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/then-and-now-52898)). Two further name changes followed before it became the California Institute of Technology in 1920 ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/history-society/california-triangulation-science-religion-industry-and-the-birth-of-the-research-park)).

The transformation was the work of three men, and it was ruthless. Astronomer George Ellery Hale joined the board in 1907 and set about rebuilding the school along engineering lines; according to historian Mike Davis's account, relayed by PBS SoCal, Hale effectively "booted" the 500 students attending Throop and reopened the school in the fall of 1910 with just 31 pupils ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/history-society/california-triangulation-science-religion-industry-and-the-birth-of-the-research-park)). With chemist Arthur Amos Noyes and physicist Robert A. Millikan, Hale formed the "triumvirate" that turned a local trade school into a research powerhouse ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/history-society/california-triangulation-science-religion-industry-and-the-birth-of-the-research-park)).

Millikan — a Nobel laureate who became Caltech's most famous president — also preached ideas the institute no longer advertises. PBS SoCal's history documents him promoting "Aryan supremacy on the shores of the Pacific," calling Southern California "the westernmost outpost of Nordic civilization," and aligning Caltech with "aristocracy and patronage" against "meddling congressman" ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/history-society/california-triangulation-science-religion-industry-and-the-birth-of-the-research-park)). That same patronage model, fused with military money — over $80 million in military research during World War II alone — made Caltech the hub of Southern California's postwar science economy, spinning off the Jet Propulsion Laboratory, Hughes Aircraft, Aerojet General, and the Rand Institute ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/history-society/california-triangulation-science-religion-industry-and-the-birth-of-the-research-park)).

The strangest figure in Caltech's past is Jack Parsons, the rocket researcher who helped found JPL while leading a double life: by day he worked in Caltech labs and at the Devil's Gate test range, and by night he led the Pasadena chapter of Aleister Crowley's Ordo Templi Orientis, the Agape Lodge ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/history-society/california-triangulation-science-religion-industry-and-the-birth-of-the-research-park)). Parsons took a young naval officer and science-fiction writer, L. Ron Hubbard, as his occult apprentice; Hubbard later repaid the mentorship by running off with Parsons's mistress before founding Scientology, while Parsons died in 1952 when his mansion exploded ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/history-society/california-triangulation-science-religion-industry-and-the-birth-of-the-research-park)).

## Einstein on campus, and the institute today

Albert Einstein visited Caltech as a scientist on three occasions in the early 1930s, and seventy years later his presence was formally restored: the Einstein Papers Project — the official effort to collect, edit, and publish his writings in the multi-volume *Collected Papers of Albert Einstein* — installed its offices on campus in the summer of 2000 and has been centered there ever since ([Einstein Papers Project](http://www.einstein.caltech.edu/who-we-are/history-of-the-project); [Reuters](https://www.ciol.com/einstein-s-web/)). The project, led by general editor Diana Kormos-Buchwald, operates out of a converted cottage at 363 South Hill Ave. on the campus's eastern edge, working with the Albert Einstein Archives at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem ([Einstein Papers Project](http://www.einstein.caltech.edu/who-we-are/history-of-the-project); [Reuters](https://www.ciol.com/einstein-s-web/)).

By its own October 2025 figures, Caltech is tiny and decorated in roughly equal measure: 1,023 undergraduates, 1,440 graduate students, 323 professorial faculty, 558 postdoctoral scholars, and the famous 3:1 student-faculty ratio ([Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/documents/149/At_a_Glance_MASTER_101525.pdf)). The institute claims 48 Nobel laureates, 66 National Medal of Science recipients, 14 National Medal of Technology and Innovation recipients, and 120 National Academies memberships ([Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/documents/149/At_a_Glance_MASTER_101525.pdf)). Its observatories stretch from the W. M. Keck Observatory in Hawaii and Palomar Observatory in California to the Owens Valley Radio Observatory, LIGO, and the Chajnantor Observatory in Chile ([Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/documents/149/At_a_Glance_MASTER_101525.pdf)). More than 25,000 living alumni are counted worldwide ([Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/documents/149/At_a_Glance_MASTER_101525.pdf)).
