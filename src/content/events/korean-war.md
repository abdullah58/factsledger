The Korean War (June 25, 1950 – July 27, 1953) was the first war fought under the United Nations flag — a UN-led defense of [South Korea](/places/south-korea/) after North Korea, supplied and advised by the Soviet Union, invaded across the 38th parallel ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Korean-War)). Twenty-two nations served under the U.S.-led United Nations Command ([UNC](https://www.unc.mil/History/1950-1953-Korean-War-Active-Conflict/)); at least 2.5 million people died ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Korean-War)), and the fighting ended in an armistice, not a peace treaty ([UNC](https://www.unc.mil/History/1950-1953-Korean-War-Active-Conflict/)).

## Overview

The war's roots lay in the 1945 collapse of the Japanese empire, which had annexed Korea in 1910 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Korean-War)). The United States and the Soviet Union divided Korea at the 38th parallel for administrative purposes; the division hardened, and in 1948 two rival states formed — the Republic of Korea in the south under Syngman Rhee and the Democratic People's Republic of Korea in the north under Kim Il-sung, who had Soviet backing ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Korean-War)). On June 25, 1950, roughly 135,000 North Korean soldiers crossed the parallel and invaded the south ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/world/asia/korean-war-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). The UN Security Council passed resolutions 82 and 83 condemning the invasion and calling members to assist South Korea, then resolution 84 on July 7 establishing a unified command under the United States, authorized to fly the UN flag ([UNC](https://www.unc.mil/History/1950-1953-Korean-War-Active-Conflict/)). The conflict widened when China sent its People's Volunteer Army across the Yalu River in November 1950 ([UNC](https://www.unc.mil/History/1950-1953-Korean-War-Active-Conflict/)).

## The Turkish brigade under UN Command

Few war overviews give [Turkey](/places/turkey/) its due — the first country after the United States to answer the UN's call for troops ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkey-remembers-its-fallen-soldiers-in-korean-war-136964)). Parliament approved Adnan Menderes's proposal, and on September 17, 1950, the first Turkish brigade sailed from Mersin under Brigadier General Tahsin Yazıcı, reaching [Busan](/places/busan/) in mid-October ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkey-remembers-its-fallen-soldiers-in-korean-war-136964); [Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/asia-pacific/turkish-war-veteran-recounts-heroic-struggles-of-comrades-in-korean-war/3003829)). Turkey fielded four brigades totaling 21,212 soldiers — the fourth-largest of 16 national contributions — and ranked third in casualties: Anadolu Agency reports 774 soldiers martyred under UN command between 1950 and 1953 ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkey-remembers-its-fallen-soldiers-in-korean-war-136964); [Anadolu Agency](https://www.anews.com.tr/turkey/2017/12/07/turkish-foster-father-of-korean-war-orphan-dies-at-91)). Nicknamed "the distinguished unit" for stealth and bravery ([TRT World](https://www.trtworld.com/article/14204554/amp)) and a victor at the Battle of Kumyangjang-ni at Yongin, which Turkey's ambassador credits for enabling a UN counter-attack ([TRT World](https://www.trtworld.com/article/14204554/amp)). Turkish troops also built a school for Korean war orphans ([TRT World](https://www.trtworld.com/article/14204554/amp)), and Sgt. [Süleyman Dilbirliği](/people/suleyman-dilbirligi/) found the orphan he named Ayla — the story behind [Ayla: The Daughter of War](/creative-works/ayla-the-daughter-of-war/) ([Anadolu Agency](https://www.anews.com.tr/turkey/2017/12/07/turkish-foster-father-of-korean-war-orphan-dies-at-91)).

## How the war unfolded

The war's tide turned repeatedly. After the UN established the Busan Perimeter through August and mid-September 1950, General Douglas MacArthur's amphibious landing at Incheon on September 15–16 recaptured Seoul and drove North Korean forces back across the 38th parallel ([UNC](https://www.unc.mil/History/1950-1953-Korean-War-Active-Conflict/); [History](https://www.history.com/articles/korean-war)). UN troops then advanced north toward the Yalu River — China's border — and in late November 1950 the Chinese People's Volunteer Army crossed it, driving UNC forces back and surrounding them at the Changjin (Chosin) Reservoir ([UNC](https://www.unc.mil/History/1950-1953-Korean-War-Active-Conflict/)). Evacuation from Hungnam in December also carried 98,000 Korean civilians to safety ([UNC](https://www.unc.mil/History/1950-1953-Korean-War-Active-Conflict/)). A political crisis followed: MacArthur's March 1951 letter to Congressman Joseph Martin demanding wider war against communism led President Harry S. Truman to fire him on April 11 for insubordination ([History](https://www.history.com/articles/korean-war)). By May 1951 Seoul was retaken and the front stalled near the 38th parallel, where sporadic fighting continued for two more years while truce talks ground on ([UNC](https://www.unc.mil/History/1950-1953-Korean-War-Active-Conflict/)).

## The armistice that never became peace

Truce talks began at Kaesong in July 1951 and moved to Panmunjom that October, deadlocking for two years and 17 days — chiefly over whether prisoners of war should be forcibly repatriated ([UNC](https://www.unc.mil/History/1950-1953-Korean-War-Active-Conflict/); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/world/asia/korean-war-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). On July 27, 1953, the [United Nations](/organizations/united-nations/) Command signed the Korean Armistice Agreement with North Korea and the Chinese People's Volunteers; South Korea's government refused to sign ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/world/asia/korean-war-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). The agreement let prisoners choose where to live, redrew the boundary near the 38th parallel — giving South Korea about 1,500 extra square miles — and created the two-mile-wide Demilitarized Zone that still divides the peninsula ([History](https://www.history.com/articles/korean-war)). Critically, it was a ceasefire, not a peace treaty: none was ever signed, so the war has technically never ended ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/world/asia/korean-war-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). Negotiations in 1954 produced no further agreement, and the front line has served as the de facto boundary ever since ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Korean-War)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why did the Korean War start?** After Japan's 1945 defeat, the U.S. and Soviet Union divided Korea at the 38th parallel, and rival states formed in 1948. Convinced reunification required war, Soviet-backed North Korean leader Kim Il-sung invaded the South on June 25, 1950, prompting the UN Security Council to authorize a U.S.-led unified command ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Korean-War); [UNC](https://www.unc.mil/History/1950-1953-Korean-War-Active-Conflict/)).

**Who won the Korean War?** No one. The armistice of July 27, 1953, restored the front near the 38th parallel — South Korea gained about 1,500 square miles — but the peninsula stayed divided and no peace treaty followed. Historians call it a stalemate: North Korea failed to conquer the South, and the UN Command failed to unify the peninsula ([History](https://www.history.com/articles/korean-war); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/world/asia/korean-war-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)).

**How many Turks served and died in the Korean War?** Turkey sent 21,212 soldiers in four brigades — the fourth-largest contribution of 16 troop-sending nations — and ranked third in casualties. Anadolu Agency reports 774 Turkish soldiers martyred under UN command between 1950 and 1953; 462 are buried at the UN Memorial Cemetery in Busan ([Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/asia-pacific/turkish-war-veteran-recounts-heroic-struggles-of-comrades-in-korean-war/3003829); [TRT World](https://www.trtworld.com/article/14204554/amp)).

**Has the Korean War officially ended?** No. The July 27, 1953 armistice was a ceasefire, and South Korea never signed it. No peace treaty has ever been signed, so the two Koreas technically remain at war, separated by the Demilitarized Zone — one of the world's most fortified borders, maintained to this day by the armistice's military commission machinery ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/world/asia/korean-war-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)).
