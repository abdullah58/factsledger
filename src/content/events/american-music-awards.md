## Overview

The American Music Awards (AMAs) are an annual music awards show in the [United States](/places/united-states/) created by television producer Dick Clark in 1973 and first presented on 19 February 1974 at the Earl Carroll Theatre in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/). Unlike the Grammy Awards, decided by recording-industry members, AMA winners are determined entirely by fan voting, with nominees drawn from fan-engagement data tracked by Billboard. [Dick Clark Productions](/organizations/dick-clark-productions/) has produced it ever since ([American Music Awards](https://www.theamas.com/about/); [Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/602398-most-american-music-awards-won-by-a-male-artist)).

The show was born from a network rivalry: ABC lost the Grammy broadcast rights to CBS, and Clark built a competing ceremony that measured what fans actually bought and listened to rather than what industry peers preferred ([American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/american-music-awards-skip-another-year-will-air-2025/); [Billboard Explains](https://www.musiciansterritory.com/billboard-explains-the-american-music-awards/)). That fan-first premise has held for half a century, even as the mechanics changed: nominees now come from streaming, sales, airplay and touring data tracked by Billboard and Luminate, while winners are picked entirely by fans, with tabulation and verification handled by a third-party independent company ([American Music Awards](https://www.theamas.com/about/)). The show bills itself as "the world's largest fan-voted award show", a description [Variety](/organizations/variety/) has also used ([American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/american-music-awards-skip-another-year-will-air-2025/)). The 50th anniversary special underlined that staying power, reaching more than 13 million people and averaging over 6.1 million viewers ([American Music Awards](https://www.theamas.com/2025/05/winners-announced-for-the-2025-american-music-awards/)). Dick Clark Productions, owned by Penske Media Corporation in a joint venture with Eldridge, also produces the Billboard Music Awards and the Golden Globes ([American Music Awards](https://www.theamas.com/2025/05/winners-announced-for-the-2025-american-music-awards/)).

The distinction that matters most is the vote. The AMAs and the Grammys both celebrate the year's music, but only the AMAs hand the decision to fans, while the [Billboard Music Awards](/events/billboard-music-awards/) — the AMAs' sister show under Dick Clark Productions — decide their honorees from chart performance rather than any vote at all ([Billboard Explains](https://www.musiciansterritory.com/billboard-explains-the-american-music-awards/); [The Wrap](https://www.thewrap.com/american-music-awards-cbs-paramount-plus-return-partnership/)).

## How the winners are chosen

The show has always called itself the fans' awards, but the way popularity is measured has changed over fifty years ([Billboard Explains](https://www.musiciansterritory.com/billboard-explains-the-american-music-awards/)). Today the process has two halves. Nominees are drawn from fan interactions reflected on the Billboard charts — streaming, album and song sales, radio airplay and tour grosses — tracked by Billboard and its data partner Luminate over a defined eligibility period; for the 2025 ceremony that period ran from 22 March 2024 to 20 March 2025 ([American Music Awards](https://www.theamas.com/about/); [American Music Awards](https://www.theamas.com/2025/05/winners-announced-for-the-2025-american-music-awards/)). Winners are then determined entirely by fan voting, with tabulation and verification managed by a third-party independent company ([American Music Awards](https://www.theamas.com/about/)). The 2018 edition shows how open that vote is: fans could cast 100 votes a day for New Artist of the Year and Collaboration of the Year, and one vote a day in every other category ([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/bwwtv/article/AMERICAN-MUSIC-AWARDS-to-Honor-Aretha-Franklin-with-Special-Tribute-20180927)). By design, the ceremony rewards commercial popularity directly.

## From ABC to CBS: five decades on television

The AMAs aired on ABC from 1974 until the network's contract ran out in 2022 — nearly five decades on one network ([American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/american-music-awards-skip-another-year-will-air-2025/)). It began in January or February, switched to November in 2003 — a year with two ceremonies — and stayed in autumn until the network change ([American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/american-music-awards-skip-another-year-will-air-2025/); [Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/602398-most-american-music-awards-won-by-a-male-artist)). With no new broadcast home, 2023 passed with no ceremony, the first miss in nearly fifty years ([American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/american-music-awards-skip-another-year-will-air-2025/)). CBS picked up the rights in 2024, aired a 50th anniversary special that October, and brought the show back on Memorial Day, 26 May 2025, from Fontainebleau Las Vegas ([American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/american-music-awards-skip-another-year-will-air-2025/); [The Wrap](https://www.thewrap.com/american-music-awards-cbs-paramount-plus-return-partnership/)). In August 2025 the two signed a five-year deal keeping it on CBS and Paramount+ through 2030, with the 52nd edition set for Memorial Day weekend 2026 in Las Vegas ([The Wrap](https://www.thewrap.com/american-music-awards-cbs-paramount-plus-return-partnership/)). It completes a neat reversal: the Grammys, the show the AMAs were created to rival, leave CBS for Disney and ABC in 2027 after 54 consecutive years there ([The Wrap](https://www.thewrap.com/american-music-awards-cbs-paramount-plus-return-partnership/)).

## Records and the 2025 comeback

Taylor Swift is the most-awarded artist in the show's history, reaching 29 with six wins at the 2019 ceremony and passing Michael Jackson, whose total [Guinness](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/602398-most-american-music-awards-won-by-a-male-artist) counts as 26 trophies including four honorary awards, while most reporting credits him with 24. [Aretha Franklin](/people/aretha-franklin/) won six AMAs, the first in 1976, and hosted in 1976 and 1983 ([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/bwwtv/article/AMERICAN-MUSIC-AWARDS-to-Honor-Aretha-Franklin-with-Special-Tribute-20180927)). [Nicki Minaj](/people/nicki-minaj/) won two 2015 AMAs for *The Pinkprint* ([BMI](https://www.bmi.com/news/bmi_congratulates_its_2015_american_music_award_winners)), and [Lil Wayne](/people/lil-wayne/) shared in the 2017 Favorite Song – Rap/Hip-Hop win for "I'm the One" ([uDiscoverMusic](https://www.udiscovermusic.com/news/winners-2017-american-music-awards/)).

Billie Eilish, the second-most-nominated artist, owned the comeback, sweeping all seven categories, including Artist, Album and Song of the Year ([American Music Awards](https://www.theamas.com/2025/05/winners-announced-for-the-2025-american-music-awards/)). Eminem won his first AMAs in 15 years, Janet Jackson took the ICON Award, Rod Stewart the Lifetime Achievement Award, and Gracie Abrams New Artist of the Year ([American Music Awards](https://www.theamas.com/2025/05/winners-announced-for-the-2025-american-music-awards/)). The 2025 ceremony drew more than 10 million unique viewers, up 38% on the last ABC airing in 2022 ([The Wrap](https://www.thewrap.com/american-music-awards-cbs-paramount-plus-return-partnership/)) — a rebound that persuaded CBS to lock the show into a five-year deal through 2030.

## FAQ

**Who created the American Music Awards?**
Television producer Dick Clark created the show in 1973 for ABC, after the network lost the Grammy broadcast rights to CBS. The first ceremony was presented on 19 February 1974 at the Earl Carroll Theatre in New York City, and Dick Clark Productions has produced it ever since ([Billboard Explains](https://www.musiciansterritory.com/billboard-explains-the-american-music-awards/); [Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/602398-most-american-music-awards-won-by-a-male-artist)).

**How are American Music Awards winners chosen?**
Winners are determined entirely by fan voting, with tabulation and verification handled by a third-party independent company. Nominees come from fan-engagement data — streaming, album and song sales, radio airplay and tour grosses — tracked by Billboard and Luminate over a set eligibility period ([American Music Awards](https://www.theamas.com/about/)).

**How are the AMAs different from the Grammys and the Billboard Music Awards?**
The AMAs are decided by fans, the Grammys by recording-industry members voting as peers, and the Billboard Music Awards by chart performance rather than any vote. The AMAs and the Billboard Music Awards are both produced by Dick Clark Productions ([Billboard Explains](https://www.musiciansterritory.com/billboard-explains-the-american-music-awards/); [The Wrap](https://www.thewrap.com/american-music-awards-cbs-paramount-plus-return-partnership/)).

**Who has won the most American Music Awards?**
Taylor Swift, with 29 wins after her six-award night in 2019. She passed Michael Jackson, whose total is reported as 26 trophies including honorary awards by Guinness World Records and as 24 wins in most contemporary reporting; the two counts use different bases ([Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/602398-most-american-music-awards-won-by-a-male-artist)).

**When are the next American Music Awards?**
The 52nd edition is scheduled for Memorial Day weekend 2026 from Las Vegas, airing on CBS and streaming on Paramount+ under the five-year deal signed in August 2025. No host, nominees or performers had been announced at the time of writing ([The Wrap](https://www.thewrap.com/american-music-awards-cbs-paramount-plus-return-partnership/)).
