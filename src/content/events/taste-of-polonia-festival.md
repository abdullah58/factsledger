## Overview

The Taste of Polonia Festival is an annual four-day Polish cultural festival held every Labor Day weekend at the [Copernicus Center](/organizations/copernicus-center/) in [Chicago](/places/chicago/)'s Jefferson Park neighbourhood, founded in 1980 by the [Copernicus Foundation](/organizations/copernicus-foundation/). It is billed as the largest Polish festival in the [United States](/places/united-states/), drawing tens of thousands of visitors for Polish food, live music, dance and cultural exhibits ([Choose Chicago](https://www.choosechicago.com/articles/itineraries/polish-heritage-chicago-neighborhood-guide/); [Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/about-us/)). The festival takes place at 5216 W. Lawrence Ave., in and around the former Gateway Theatre, and the Foundation presents it as part of fulfilling its mission for education and entertainment ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/taste-of-polonia-2025/); [Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/about-us/)). Chicago's Polish community, which began forming around the 1850s, is one of the largest Polish populations in the world outside [Poland](/places/poland/), and the festival is its best-known annual gathering in the city ([Choose Chicago](https://www.choosechicago.com/articles/itineraries/polish-heritage-chicago-neighborhood-guide/)).

## From a statue campaign to a festival

The festival grew out of the Copernicus Foundation's origin story, which began in 1970 when the [City of Chicago](/organizations/city-of-chicago/) asked the Polish community to place a statue of [Nicolaus Copernicus](/people/nicolaus-copernicus/) at the entrance to the [Adler Planetarium](/organizations/adler-planetarium/) for the 500th anniversary of the astronomer's birth; the campaign raised over $300,000 and the statue was dedicated in 1973 ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/about-us/)). The Foundation was chartered as a non-profit in July 1971, and in 1979 it bought the old Gateway cinema near Milwaukee and Lawrence avenues, the first Chicago theatre built exclusively for the "talkies", remodelling it into the Copernicus Center ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/about-us/)). In 1985 the building gained Solidarity Tower, a scale replica of the clock tower of the Royal Castle in [Warsaw](/places/warsaw/), visible from the Kennedy Expressway, and its 1,852-seat theatre was named for Mitchell P. Kobelinski ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/about-us/)).

## Food, music and three stages

The 2025 edition ran Friday 29 August to Monday 1 September, opening at 4:30 p.m. on Friday and at 11:30 a.m. on the other days, closing at 10:30 p.m. except Monday, when it closed at 9:30 p.m. ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/taste-of-polonia-2025/)). Its programme featured over 60 bands and artists across three stages — pop, rock, latin, classical, dance, alternative and polka — with the historic Copernicus Center Theater Stage hosting Polish performers, folk ensembles and community dance groups ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/taste-of-polonia-2025/)). Visitors could eat traditional Polish cuisine and drink Polish beer, browse Outdoor Merchant Row, watch movie screenings and cultural exhibitions, and use the Kids Zone, while a Catholic Mass was held on the Theater Stage at 11 a.m. on Sunday ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/taste-of-polonia-2025/); [Secret Chicago](https://secretchicago.com/taste-of-polonia-chicago-2025/)). Secret Chicago's guide counted more than 30 food and craft beer vendors, plus carnival rides, games and bounce houses, and the organizer billed Polish singer Kamil Bednarek for 30 August and 1 September ([Secret Chicago](https://secretchicago.com/taste-of-polonia-chicago-2025/); [Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/taste-of-polonia-2025/)).

## How big it is — and what the numbers disagree on

Every full-read source agrees the festival is the largest Polish festival in the country, but they disagree on how many people attend: Choose Chicago puts annual attendance at over 35,000, the Copernicus Center's own history page puts it at over 40,000, and its 2025 page puts it at over 45,000 ([Choose Chicago](https://www.choosechicago.com/articles/itineraries/polish-heritage-chicago-neighborhood-guide/); [Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/about-us/); [Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/taste-of-polonia-2025/)). The University of Chicago's Chicago Studies guide independently calls it the largest Polish festival in the United States, without giving a number ([University of Chicago](https://chicagostudies.uchicago.edu/neighborhoods)). That 10,000-person gap between the lowest and highest published figures implies the numbers are organizer and tourism estimates rather than audited counts, so this profile reports the range instead of a single attendance figure. The same caution applies to the stage count, which is given as four stages by Choose Chicago and three by the organizer's 2025 page.

Tickets for 2025 cost $10 for Friday only, $15 for any one day and $45 for a four-day pass, with children 12 and under entering free ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/taste-of-polonia-2025/)). The site is a short walk from the Jefferson Park CTA Blue Line stop, and drivers could use free parking at the Irish American Heritage Center, 4626 N. Knox Ave., with a shuttle to the festival grounds ([Secret Chicago](https://secretchicago.com/taste-of-polonia-chicago-2025/)). Among the 2025 sponsors were the Polish & Slavic Federal Credit Union, Verizon, Visit Małopolska, Aetna and the Illinois Lottery ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/taste-of-polonia-2025/)).

## FAQ

**When and where is the Taste of Polonia Festival?**
It is held every Labor Day weekend, Friday to Monday, at the Copernicus Center, 5216 W. Lawrence Ave. in Chicago's Jefferson Park neighbourhood. The 2025 edition ran 29 August to 1 September 2025, opening at 4:30 p.m. on Friday and 11:30 a.m. on the other days.

**Who organizes the Taste of Polonia Festival?**
The Copernicus Foundation organizes it through the Copernicus Center, the former Gateway Theatre it bought in 1979. The Foundation was chartered in July 1971 after a campaign to place a Copernicus statue at the Adler Planetarium, and it has sponsored the festival since 1980.

**How many people attend the Taste of Polonia Festival?**
Published estimates disagree: Choose Chicago says over 35,000 a year, the Copernicus Center's history page says over 40,000, and its 2025 page says over 45,000. No audited attendance count was found, so the honest answer is a range of over 35,000 to over 45,000.

**What is there to do at the festival?**
The 2025 programme offered over 60 bands and artists across three stages, Polish food and beer, dance performances, cultural exhibitions, a Kids Zone, movie screenings, Outdoor Merchant Row and a Sunday morning Catholic Mass in the theatre. Secret Chicago also lists more than 30 food and craft beer vendors plus carnival rides.

**How much did the 2025 festival cost to attend?**
The organizer listed Friday-only tickets at $10, any-one-day tickets at $15 and a four-day pass at $45, with children 12 and under entering free. Note that Secret Chicago's 2025 guide listed admission as "Free", which conflicts with the organizer's prices.
