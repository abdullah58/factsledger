## Overview

Chovqan is a traditional horse-riding game from the Karabakh region of [Azerbaijan](/places/azerbaijan/), played by two teams
of five riders — two fullbacks and three forwards — mounted on [Karabakh horses](/species/karabakh-horse/) on a flat grass field.
Players use wooden mallets to drive a small leather or wooden ball into the opposing team's goal; there
is no goalkeeper, and the ball may be stopped with any part of the body but struck only with the club.
Matches are interspersed with instrumental folk music called janghi, and players traditionally wear
large astrakhan hats, long tight-fitting coats with a high waist, and specialized trousers and shoes
([UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage](https://ich.unesco.org/en/USL/chovqan-a-traditional-karabakh-horse-riding-game-in-the-republic-of-azerbaijan-00905);
[AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/nation/62698.html)). Azerbaijan's [National Commission for UNESCO](/organizations/national-commission-azerbaijan-unesco/)
presents the game as the direct predecessor of modern polo, stating that the word "chovqan" is of
Turkic origin meaning "to strike, waving a stick"
([National Commission of Azerbaijan for UNESCO](https://unesco.az/index.php/en/articles/intangible_cultural_heritage/chovqan-traditional-karabakh-horse-riding-game-republic-azerbaijan)).

## History

The Commission traces chovqan's origins to the middle of the first millennium, with documentary
evidence including a vessel depicting the game found during excavations near Beylagan, taken to confirm
its existence in the 11th century; the game is also mentioned in [Nizami](/people/nizami-ganjavi/)'s poem "[Khosrov and Shirin](/creative-works/khosrov-and-shirin/)"
and in the epic "[Kitabi Dede Gorgud](/creative-works/kitabi-dede-gorgud/)," with 11th-century miniatures and Tabriz miniatures illustrating
its scenes
([National Commission of Azerbaijan for UNESCO](https://unesco.az/index.php/en/articles/intangible_cultural_heritage/chovqan-traditional-karabakh-horse-riding-game-republic-azerbaijan)).
Historically it was played by Azerbaijan's aristocracy and members of the Shah's family, with Karabakh
breeders concentrating on the quick, short horses the game requires — above all the Karabakh breed
itself ([National Commission of Azerbaijan for UNESCO](https://unesco.az/index.php/en/articles/intangible_cultural_heritage/chovqan-traditional-karabakh-horse-riding-game-republic-azerbaijan)).
The Commission's account follows the game along the [Silk Road](/places/silk-road/) to [India](/places/india/) and states that the British,
encountering it there in the 19th century, renamed it "polo" — the name under which it appeared at the
[1900 Paris Olympics](/events/1900-summer-olympics/) ([National Commission of Azerbaijan for UNESCO](https://unesco.az/index.php/en/articles/intangible_cultural_heritage/chovqan-traditional-karabakh-horse-riding-game-republic-azerbaijan)).

During the [Soviet](/places/soviet-union/) period, chovqan competitions were regularly held in Karabakh and other regions of
Azerbaijan and were routinely included in national and union-level festivals, remaining popular in
Karabakh until the 1980s
([National Commission of Azerbaijan for UNESCO](https://unesco.az/index.php/en/articles/intangible_cultural_heritage/chovqan-traditional-karabakh-horse-riding-game-republic-azerbaijan)).
A specialist traditional-sports reference, drawing on nomination-era material, attributes the game's
subsequent decline to the departure of experienced trainers, the abolition of the collective-farm
system after the Soviet collapse — which drove players from [Ganja](/places/ganja/), Karabakh, [Khyzy](/places/khyzy/), [Shamakhi](/places/shamakhi/) and
[Gedabey](/places/gedabey/) to migrate to cities — and a growing shortage of Karabakh horses; Azerbaijan's national
register counted only 137 practitioners in 2010, falling to 118 by early 2012
([Traditional Sports](https://traditionalsports.org/traditional-sports/asia/chovquan-chovqan-azerbaijan.html)).

The revival began in the 2000s: the first annual national tournament for the [President's Cup](/events/presidents-cup-chovgan/) was held
in 2006, and by the 8th edition in December 2013 the games at Sheki's Republican Equestrian Tourism
Center drew 20 regional teams
([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/nation/62698.html)). In September 2013 Baku hosted the [Polo World
Cup](/events/2013-polo-world-cup/), on whose margins an exhibition game was played between Azerbaijan's polo and chovqan teams
([National Commission of Azerbaijan for UNESCO](https://unesco.az/index.php/en/articles/intangible_cultural_heritage/chovqan-traditional-karabakh-horse-riding-game-republic-azerbaijan)).

## Rules and equipment

A standard game lasts 30 minutes in two 15-minute halves with a 10-minute break, after which the teams
change ends; amateur games run 20 minutes. In case of a draw, extra time of no more than 8 minutes
follows, then penalty kicks, and if the score is still level the winner is determined by drawing lots
([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/nation/62698.html)). A specialist reference describes the field as
200 metres long and 120 metres wide with 3-metre goals, officiated by three referees — a main referee
and two side referees — usually chosen from the most experienced players
([Traditional Sports](https://traditionalsports.org/traditional-sports/asia/chovquan-chovqan-azerbaijan.html)).
The wooden mallets are traditionally prepared from elm, pomegranate, mulberry, dogwood or hornbeam
wood ([Traditional Sports](https://traditionalsports.org/traditional-sports/asia/chovquan-chovqan-azerbaijan.html)).
Competitions are accompanied by janghi music, played at the beginning and end of the game and
sometimes after each goal, performed on folk instruments such as the zurna, saz, nagara and def
([Traditional Sports](https://traditionalsports.org/traditional-sports/asia/chovquan-chovqan-azerbaijan.html)).
[UNESCO](/organizations/unesco/)'s inscription record notes that the game's players and trainers are traditionally local male
farmers and skilled riders, and that its rules and techniques pass from experienced players to
beginners through collective training
([UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage](https://ich.unesco.org/en/USL/chovqan-a-traditional-karabakh-horse-riding-game-in-the-republic-of-azerbaijan-00905)).

## UNESCO recognition and safeguarding

In December 2013, UNESCO inscribed Chovqan on its List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of
Urgent Safeguarding (decision 8.COM 7.a.1), the decision taken at the [Intergovernmental Committee](/organizations/intergovernmental-committee-intangible-heritage/)'s
8th session held in Baku from December 2 to 8, 2013
([UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage](https://ich.unesco.org/en/USL/chovqan-a-traditional-karabakh-horse-riding-game-in-the-republic-of-azerbaijan-00905);
[Presidential Library of Azerbaijan](https://republic.preslib.az/en_a7-9.html)). The inscription
documented specific threats: diminishing interest among younger generations, urbanization,
rural-to-urban migration, and shortages of players, trainers and Karabakh horses
([UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage](https://ich.unesco.org/en/USL/chovqan-a-traditional-karabakh-horse-riding-game-in-the-republic-of-azerbaijan-00905)).
In May 2015 an exhibition on the chovqan game opened at UNESCO headquarters in [Paris](/places/paris/), alongside the
presentation of French writer [Jean-Louis Gouraud](/people/jean-louis-gouraud/)'s book "Karabakh Horses"
([Presidential Library of Azerbaijan](https://republic.preslib.az/en_a7-9.html)).

Azerbaijan submitted its second periodic safeguarding report on time in December 2021, and the
committee examined it in December 2022 (decision 17.COM 6.a.8), noting efforts including training
programs and competitions, the sustaining of janghi music playing, participatory monitoring, and
success in increasing practitioner numbers and raising awareness among youth; it encouraged the
country to continue developing policy frameworks, training and competitions, and the long-term
breeding of Karabakh horses
([UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage](https://ich.unesco.org/en/Decisions/17.COM/6.a.8)).
That decision set the next report deadline at 15 December 2025, while UNESCO's country page
currently lists 2029 as the next reporting milestone
([UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage](https://ich.unesco.org/en/Decisions/17.COM/6.a.8);
[UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage](https://ich.unesco.org/en/state/azerbaijan-AZ?info=periodic-reporting&cp=AZ&topic=es-estado)).

## International competition era

In November 2023 Baku hosted a six-nation World Chovqan Championship — Azerbaijan, [Kazakhstan](/places/kazakhstan/),
[Morocco](/places/morocco/), [Poland](/places/poland/), [Türkiye](/places/turkey/) and [Uzbekistan](/places/uzbekistan/) — played four-a-side, with women riding for the Polish and
Uzbek teams and a Hungarian judging panel; the final on November 8 was timed to [Victory Day](/events/victory-day-azerbaijan/), and
Azerbaijan defeated Morocco 4:0 to take the first title
([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/sports/216997.html);
[Idman.biz](https://www.idman.biz/en/news/horse-riding/7400)).

In February 2024 the founding General Assembly of the [International Chovgan Federation](/organizations/international-chovgan-federation/) (ICF) took
place at Baku's [Bina Equestrian Center](/places/bina-equestrian-center/), with 29 delegates from 19 nations; Bahruz Nabiyev was
elected president and Poland's Maciej Michel Olbrich general secretary
([AJMEDIA](https://en.ajmedia.jp/baku-hosting-general-assembly-on-establishment-of-international-chovgan-federation/);
[Idman.biz](https://www.idman.biz/en/news/fencing/16397)). The same year a [Professional Chovgan
League](/organizations/professional-chovgan-league/) with 10 club teams was inaugurated at the Bina Equestrian Center
([Idman.biz](https://www.idman.biz/en/news/horse-riding/29071)).

The first ICF World Championship followed in June 2024 in Baku: 10 national teams playing
five-a-side, with Azerbaijan beating Morocco 2:0 in the final and [Nigeria](/places/nigeria/) taking bronze with a 3:1
win over Uzbekistan ([Idman.biz](https://www.idman.biz/en/news/horse-riding/32431)). The 2nd
Chovqan World Championship was held in Baku from June 7 to 14, 2026, again at the Bina Equestrian
Center; Azerbaijan defeated [Chile](/places/chile/) 3:0 in the final while [Uruguay](/places/uruguay/) beat Nigeria 3:2 for bronze
([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/sports/254624.html);
[AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/sports/259863.html)).

## A separate Iranian element

[Iran](/places/iran/)'s "[Chogān](/events/chogan/), a horse-riding game accompanied by music and storytelling" is a distinct UNESCO
element: inscribed in 2017 (12.COM) on the Representative List — not the Urgent Safeguarding List —
it has a history of over 2,000 years in Iran, was mostly played in royal courts and urban fields,
and its bearers include three primary groups: the players, the storytellers and the musicians, with
techniques traditionally transmitted informally within families or workshops
([UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chogan-a-horse-riding-game-accompanied-by-music-and-storytelling-01282)).
UNESCO treats the two as separate national elements on separate lists; this profile does not merge
them.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not settle the historiography of polo's origins: the account of chovqan's journey
along the Silk Road and its renaming by the British is Azerbaijan's National Commission's stated
position, presented as such. It does not resolve the dating of the first international competition in
Baghdad — the Commission says the 12th century, a specialist reference says the 2nd — and makes no
claim about the game's current practitioner numbers, since the only figures found are the
nomination-era register counts. It does not confirm whether Azerbaijan's 2025 safeguarding report
was submitted or examined, and it asserts nothing from the 2015 CNN feature an earlier version
referenced, since that piece could not be read directly.
