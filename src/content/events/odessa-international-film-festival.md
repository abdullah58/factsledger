## Overview

**The Odesa International Film Festival (OIFF)** is an annual film festival in Odesa, Ukraine, founded in 2010 — one of the largest-audience film festivals in Eastern Europe ([Filmitalia](https://filmitalia.org/en/festival/86979)). Its top honor, the Grand Prix of the International Competition — the Golden Duke, decided by audience vote — went to Adam Rehmeier's *Dinner in America* (USA) at the 11th edition, held September 25–October 3, 2020 ([Cineuropa](https://cineuropa.org/newsdetail/393331/)).

## Ukraine's flagship summer film festival

OIFF was founded in 2010 to popularize high-level intellectual films among Ukrainian viewers and to support Ukrainian cinema at home and abroad, and became the country's brightest summer film event ([Filmitalia](https://filmitalia.org/en/festival/86979)). The nine-day program runs three competitions — International, National (Ukrainian films), and European Documentary — with a selection focused on "art mainstream": films of refined artistic quality that remain accessible to broad audiences ([Filmitalia](https://filmitalia.org/en/festival/86979)). The audience, not a jury, awards the Grand Prix–Golden Duke; the international jury separately names Best Film, Best Director and Best Acting winners ([Filmitalia](https://filmitalia.org/en/festival/86979)). The festival's scale is its calling card: its Potemkin Stairs open-air screenings — on the steps made famous by Eisenstein's *Battleship Potemkin* — seat 15,000 viewers, which implies the audience-voted Grand Prix carries genuine mass-audience weight rather than narrow jury taste ([Filmitalia](https://filmitalia.org/en/festival/86979)). Alongside the screenings, the festival runs a Summer Film School, a pitching and work-in-progress program, and Ukraine's only professional Film Market for distribution and exhibitor deals ([Filmitalia](https://filmitalia.org/en/festival/86979)).

## The 2020 hybrid edition: the Grand Prix for Dinner in America

The 11th OIFF ran September 25–October 3, 2020 as a hybrid edition, with its closing ceremony held online rather than at the traditional Odessa Opera and Ballet Theater ([Cineuropa](https://cineuropa.org/newsdetail/393331/)). The audience-voted Grand Prix went to the US film *Dinner in America* by Adam Rehmeier, who thanked the organizers and said he was "extremely touched" by the audience's reaction ([Cineuropa](https://cineuropa.org/newsdetail/393331/)). The international jury — which included composer Howard Shore — gave its Best Film award to *Atlantis* by Valentyn Vasyanovych, named Fabrice du Welz Best Director for *Adoration*, and awarded Best Acting to the ensemble of Maura Delpero's *Maternal*; Roman Balayan received the honorary Golden Duke for lifetime achievement, and the Audience Choice award went to the Ukrainian animated film *Viktor_Robot* ([Cineuropa](https://cineuropa.org/newsdetail/393331/)). The win capped the film's post-Sundance run of more than 20 directing and audience awards at festivals including Tallinn, Neuchâtel, Dublin and Nightstream ([MovieWeb](https://movieweb.com/snack-shack-adam-rehmeier-intervie/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Where and when is the Odesa International Film Festival?**
The festival is held annually in the summer in Odesa, Ukraine. The 11th edition ran September 25–October 3, 2020, in a hybrid format; the festival's 2026 listing places its 17th edition in Kyiv (August 27–September 4, 2026), per Filmitalia's database.

**When was OIFF founded?**
In 2010, to popularize high-level intellectual films among Ukrainian viewers and to support the development of Ukrainian cinema nationally and internationally. It quickly became Ukraine's brightest summer film event.

**What is the OIFF Grand Prix?**
The festival's top award in the International Competition — the Golden Duke — is decided by audience vote. The international jury separately awards Best Film, Best Director and Best Acting prizes.

**Which year did Dinner in America win the OIFF Grand Prix?**
2020, at the 11th edition. The award was announced on October 3, 2020; some secondary listings attribute it to 2021, but Cineuropa's dated contemporaneous report pins it to the 2020 edition, which this profile follows.

**Did OIFF leave Odesa?**
The 2026 edition is listed in Kyiv rather than Odesa in Filmitalia's festival database. The database does not state the reason, and the 2021–2025 edition history was not covered by full-read sources in this pass, so this profile does not speculate.
