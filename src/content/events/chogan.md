## Overview

Chogān is a traditional horse-riding game accompanied by music and storytelling, practised in [Iran](/places/iran/) with a history of over 2,000 years, and inscribed by [UNESCO](/organizations/unesco/) in 2017 on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. In Chogān, two rider teams compete, aiming to pass the ball through the opposing team's goal posts using a wooden stick, while a musical performance and storytelling accompany the main game ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chogan-a-horse-riding-game-accompanied-by-music-and-storytelling-01282)).

Chogān has mostly been played in royal courts and urban fields, and its bearers fall into three primary groups: the players, the storytellers and the musicians ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chogan-a-horse-riding-game-accompanied-by-music-and-storytelling-01282)). UNESCO describes it as a cultural, artistic and athletic element with a strong connection to the identity and history of its bearers and practitioners, with a strong presence in literature, storytelling, proverbs, handicrafts and ornaments ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chogan-a-horse-riding-game-accompanied-by-music-and-storytelling-01282)). It is a separate UNESCO element from Azerbaijan's Chovqan, which was inscribed in 2013 on a different list — a distinction this profile states explicitly and does not blur.

## The game and its bearers

Chogān is not a game alone but a composite performance: the main game, a corresponding musical performance, and storytelling, all staged together ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chogan-a-horse-riding-game-accompanied-by-music-and-storytelling-01282)). On the field, two rider teams compete to pass the ball through the opposing team's goal posts using a wooden stick, while storytellers narrate and musicians perform alongside the play ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chogan-a-horse-riding-game-accompanied-by-music-and-storytelling-01282)). The bearers include three primary groups — the players, the storytellers and the musicians — and UNESCO's inscription decision adds texture: depending on the region, the game differs in the size of the Meydan (open area), the number of Chukken (rounds) and the number of Choganbazan (players) ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/Decisions/12.COM/11.b.14)). UNESCO describes Chogān as an element that promotes the health of the body and soul and establishes a connection between nature, humankind and horses, and notes its strong presence in the literature, storytelling, proverbs, handicrafts and ornaments that form part of its practitioners' symbolism ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chogan-a-horse-riding-game-accompanied-by-music-and-storytelling-01282)).

## History and transmission

Chogān's history in Iran stretches back over 2,000 years, with the game played mostly in royal courts and urban fields ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chogan-a-horse-riding-game-accompanied-by-music-and-storytelling-01282)). Traditionally, transmission occurred informally within the family or in workshops, and Chogān techniques continue to be actively safeguarded by families and local practitioners ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chogan-a-horse-riding-game-accompanied-by-music-and-storytelling-01282)). Over the last decades, Chogān associations have also been established, which hold training courses, support local masters and provide assistance in transmitting all aspects of Chogān while safeguarding local diversity ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chogan-a-horse-riding-game-accompanied-by-music-and-storytelling-01282)). The nomination file was compiled after an Inscription Committee formed in February 2009 from representatives of five provincial associations, the Office for Inscription of Heritage and the Iranian National Commission for UNESCO, and the element was included in Iran's National Representative Inventory in 2010 ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/Decisions/12.COM/11.b.14)). Safeguarding is substantially community-funded: 70 per cent of the cost of safeguarding activities is covered by local bearers, under a five-year plan covering festivals, research, booklets, tribute ceremonies, an oral-history archive, youth training and apprentice workshops ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/Decisions/12.COM/11.b.14)).

## A separate element from Chovqan

Chogān must not be merged with [Chovqan](/events/chovgan/), Azerbaijan's traditional horse-riding game: UNESCO treats them as two separate inscribed elements, on two different lists, for two different countries. Chogān, for Iran, was inscribed in 2017 (12.COM) on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/chogan-a-horse-riding-game-accompanied-by-music-and-storytelling-01282)). Chovqan, for Azerbaijan, was inscribed in 2013 (8.COM) on the List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding, and is played on Karabakh horses by two teams of five riders — two fullbacks and three forwards — using wooden mallets to drive a small leather or wooden ball into the opponents' goal, interspersed with instrumental folk music called janghi ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/USL/chovqan-a-traditional-karabakh-horse-riding-game-in-the-republic-of-azerbaijan-00905)). The two elements share the horse, the mallet and the goal, and both are described as ancestors of modern polo — but their inscriptions, bearer communities, lists and safeguarding regimes are distinct, and this profile keeps them that way.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Chogān?**
Chogān is a traditional Iranian horse-riding game accompanied by music and storytelling, with a history of over 2,000 years. Two rider teams compete to pass a ball through the opposing team's goal posts using a wooden stick, while storytellers narrate and musicians perform alongside the game.

**Who takes part in Chogān?**
Three primary groups of bearers: the players, the storytellers and the musicians. Traditionally, skills were transmitted informally within families or in workshops; today, Chogān associations also hold training courses, support local masters and help transmit all aspects of the game.

**When did UNESCO recognise Chogān?**
UNESCO inscribed Chogān on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2017, at its 12th committee session (12.COM), under reference 01282. The element had been included in Iran's National Representative Inventory of Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2010.

**Is Chogān the same as Azerbaijan's Chovqan?**
No. They are two separate UNESCO elements for two different countries, on two different lists: Chogān (Iran, 2017, Representative List) and Chovqan (Azerbaijan, 2013, List in Need of Urgent Safeguarding). They share the horse, mallet and goal, but their inscriptions, bearer communities and safeguarding regimes are distinct.

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not give a single standard set of rules: UNESCO's decision states that Meydan size, Chukken rounds and player numbers vary by region.
- It does not cover Chogān's modern competitive record (tournament results, federation championships), which was not verified from sources read in full.
- It does not present the 2,000-year history as independently established archaeology; it is UNESCO's nomination-file account, reported as such.
- It does not adjudicate whether Chogān and Chovqan share a common historical origin; it states only that UNESCO treats them as separate elements.
- It does not merge the two games' safeguarding regimes, bearer communities or inscriptions in any way.
