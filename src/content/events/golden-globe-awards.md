## Overview

The organization that created the Golden Globe Awards is suing the company that now owns them. On July 28, 2026, the Hollywood Foreign Press Association — the group that created the Globes in 1944 — filed a federal lawsuit accusing Penske Media, its chairman Jay Penske and other defendants of orchestrating a "clandestine" plot to seize control of the show ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/movies/2026/07/28/hfpa-lawsuit-over-control-of-golden-globe-awards-seeks-150m/91081363007/)). The complaint alleges an "illicit scheme" that ended with the dissolution of the 80-year-old HFPA, and seeks "at least" $150 million in damages plus a court order canceling the acquisition of the Golden Globes' assets, rights and properties ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/movies/2026/07/28/hfpa-lawsuit-over-control-of-golden-globe-awards-seeks-150m/91081363007/)). The Golden Globes responded that the suit "continues the absurdity and irrationality that the industry has come to expect from the defunct organization formerly known as the HFPA" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/movies/2026/07/28/hfpa-lawsuit-over-control-of-golden-globe-awards-seeks-150m/91081363007/)).

That is where the 80-year-old awards show stands in 2026: born in a Fox studio lot, raised by a small club of foreign journalists, exiled from its longtime network, and now a for-profit property at the center of a nine-figure legal fight over who stole it.

## An 80-year-old ceremony

The first Golden Globe Awards were held on January 20, 1944, at the 20th Century-Fox studios, honoring the best achievements of 1943 filmmaking; the Hollywood Foreign Press Association itself had been formed the year before ([City of Jackson history document](https://ww2.jacksonms.gov/browse/95BGlO/4OK087/history__of-the_golden-globes.pdf)). The ceremony was first broadcast on television in 1958, and television categories were added in the decades that followed, making the Globes one of the few major awards shows to span both film and television ([City of Jackson history document](https://ww2.jacksonms.gov/browse/95BGlO/4OK087/history__of-the_golden-globes.pdf)). For decades the show aired on NBC — until the HFPA's lack of diversity and ethical controversies detonated. After the Los Angeles Times reported that the HFPA had no Black members, Hollywood boycotted the group, publicists withheld talent, and NBC pulled the 2022 ceremony off the air ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/movies/2026/07/28/hfpa-lawsuit-over-control-of-golden-globe-awards-seeks-150m/91081363007/); [Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/article/golden-globes-score-5-year-deal-cbs-broadcast-paramount-streaming)).

The aftermath remade the institution. In June 2023, Dick Clark Productions and Eldridge Industries announced they had taken over the awards show from the HFPA; the HFPA was subsequently dissolved and the Globes became a for-profit venture, with DCP owned by Penske Media ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/movies/2026/07/28/hfpa-lawsuit-over-control-of-golden-globe-awards-seeks-150m/91081363007/); [Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/article/golden-globes-score-5-year-deal-cbs-broadcast-paramount-streaming)). CBS stepped in as the new broadcast home, and in March 2024 the two sides announced a five-year deal with live streaming on Paramount+ ([Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/article/golden-globes-score-5-year-deal-cbs-broadcast-paramount-streaming)). The January 2024 telecast — the 81st Golden Globes — averaged 9.4 million viewers, up about 50% from the diminished 2023 show, stabilizing an event that had briefly teetered on the brink ([Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/article/golden-globes-score-5-year-deal-cbs-broadcast-paramount-streaming)).

The HFPA's 2026 lawsuit claims the whole sequence was engineered: that around 2021, Penske and billionaire associate Todd Boehly used the Hollywood trade publications they control — The Hollywood Reporter, Variety, Deadline, Rolling Stone — to instigate the boycott, devalue the organization, and make it vulnerable to acquisition ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/movies/2026/07/28/hfpa-lawsuit-over-control-of-golden-globe-awards-seeks-150m/91081363007/)). No court has ruled on the claims.

## The people it made

The Globes' own archive is a ledger of Hollywood careers. [Amy Adams](https://factsledger.com/people/amy-adams/) — whom the Golden Globes' site describes as "one of the most accomplished and versatile actresses in Hollywood today" — had been nominated for nine Golden Globes and won two, as of a December 2018 profile on the official site ([Golden Globes](https://goldenglobes.com/articles/amy-adams-2/)). Those two wins were for *American Hustle* and *Big Eyes*, per Agence France-Presse ([AFP](https://saudigazette.com.sa/article/521555/Life/Life-Style/Hollywood-takes-break-from-scandal-to-honor-Amy-Adams)). [Cameron Diaz](https://factsledger.com/people/cameron-diaz/) is a four-time Golden Globe nominee whose films the Globes' own site catalogues — *There's Something About Mary*, *Being John Malkovich*, *Vanilla Sky* and *Gangs of New York* among them ([Men's Journal](https://www.mensjournal.com/entertainment/cameron-diaz-confirmed-to-star-in-beloved-80s-movie-sequel); [Golden Globes](https://goldenglobes.com/person/cameron-diaz/)).
