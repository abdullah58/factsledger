## Overview

The 1900 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the II Olympiad, were an international multi-sport event held in [Paris](/places/paris/), France, from 14 May to 28 October 1900 as part of the Exposition Universelle world's fair. They were the second modern Olympic Games, the first to include women, and among the most disorganised, with no opening or closing ceremonies and many athletes unaware they had competed in an Olympic event ([International Olympic Committee](https://olympics.com/en/olympic-games/paris-1900/)).

The International Olympic Committee (IOC) records 997 athletes — 22 women and 975 men — from 24 National Olympic Committees competing in 95 events, with the competitions spread over nearly five and a half months ([International Olympic Committee](https://olympics.com/en/olympic-games/paris-1900/)). The Games were folded into the World's Fair programme and their Olympic status was so under-promoted that many athletes never knew they had taken part in the Olympic Games at all ([International Olympic Committee](https://olympics.com/en/olympic-games/paris-1900/); [NBC](https://www.nbc.com/nbc-insider/where-are-the-olympics-2024-paris-games-sites)). There were no organised national teams in the modern sense, since National Olympic Committees were not yet organised ([NBC](https://www.nbc.com/nbc-insider/where-are-the-olympics-2024-paris-games-sites)).

## A world's fair swallowed the Games

The Paris Games were staged inside the Exposition Universelle, a world's fair celebrating the achievements of the past century, and that integration cost them a distinct identity ([Athletics Weekly](https://dev.athleticsweekly.com/meeting/1900-paris-olympic-games/)). The programme stretched over five months from May to October, suffered from poor organisation and inconsistent signage, and left athletes and spectators confused about what was Olympic and what was merely fair entertainment ([Athletics Weekly](https://dev.athleticsweekly.com/meeting/1900-paris-olympic-games/)). NBC's history of the Games, citing Olympic historian Bill Mallon's research, calls them disorganised and chaotic, overshadowed by the ongoing World's Fair, whose own sporting events blurred the line around which contests counted as official Olympic Games ([NBC](https://www.nbc.com/nbc-insider/where-are-the-olympics-2024-paris-games-sites)). The confusion extended to prizes: official Olympic medallists took home rectangular medals — the only rectangular Olympic medals ever issued — while other winners received cups or trophies ([NBC](https://www.nbc.com/nbc-insider/where-are-the-olympics-2024-paris-games-sites)). Many of the current Olympic traditions had not yet been established: there were no opening or closing ceremonies, no athletes' oath, and no flame ([International Olympic Committee](https://olympics.com/en/olympic-games/paris-1900/)).

## Women, mixed teams and firsts

Women made their first appearance in the modern Games at Paris 1900, and the IOC records that the first women to compete were Mrs Brohy and Miss Ohnier of France, in croquet ([International Olympic Committee](https://olympics.com/en/olympic-games/paris-1900/)). Athletics Weekly credits Hélène de Pourtalès of Switzerland as the first woman to win an Olympic gold medal, as part of the winning sailing team in the mixed 1–2 ton class ([Athletics Weekly](https://dev.athleticsweekly.com/meeting/1900-paris-olympic-games/)). Mixed-nationality teams were a defining feature of these Games: tennis, football, polo, rowing and tug of war all featured athletes from different nations competing on the same team ([International Olympic Committee](https://olympics.com/en/olympic-games/paris-1900/)). The star of the Games was the American Alvin Kraenzlein, who won the 60 metres, the 110 metres hurdles, the 200 metres hurdles and the long jump ([International Olympic Committee](https://olympics.com/en/olympic-games/paris-1900/)). His long-jump win came with a famous subplot: his American rival Myer Prinstein, leading after the preliminaries, refused to contest the final because it was scheduled for a Sunday ([International Olympic Committee](https://olympics.com/en/olympic-games/paris-1900/)).

## Polo, horses and the programme

The 1900 Games were the first modern Olympics to include horse sports, and the programme carried five Olympic-medal equestrian events — individual jumping, high jump, long jump, hacks and hunter combined, and mail coach — alongside polo matches ([Horse Illustrated](https://www.horseillustrated.com/2024-paris-olympics-equestrian-sports-close-ties-with-paris/)). Polo was included in the programme of the second Olympic Games following a British initiative, and its ancestor game was [chovgan](/events/chovgan/), the traditional [Karabakh horse](/species/karabakh-horse/)-riding game regarded as the predecessor of modern polo ([AzerNews](http://www.azernews.az/culture/59653.html)). Three female riders competed in the hacks and hunter combined event: Italian Elvira Guerra and Frenchwomen Jane Moulin and Blanche de Marcigny ([Horse Illustrated](https://www.horseillustrated.com/2024-paris-olympics-equestrian-sports-close-ties-with-paris/)). The wider programme ranged from cricket, golf, rowing, rugby union, sailing and shooting to pigeon shooting, in which live birds were used as targets ([Athletics Weekly](https://dev.athleticsweekly.com/meeting/1900-paris-olympic-games/)). Swimming took place in the River Seine, sailing regattas were held at Meulan and Le Havre, and the gymnastics all-round title demanded 16 movements, including 50-kilogram stone lifting and rope climbing ([Athletics Weekly](https://dev.athleticsweekly.com/meeting/1900-paris-olympic-games/); [International Olympic Committee](https://olympics.com/en/olympic-games/paris-1900/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When and where were the 1900 Summer Olympics held?**
The Games took place in Paris, France, from 14 May to 28 October 1900, as part of the Exposition Universelle world's fair. Competitions were spread over nearly five and a half months rather than concentrated in a single stadium fortnight, and no opening or closing ceremonies were held.

**Why are the 1900 Games called chaotic?**
Because they were folded into the World's Fair, their Olympic status was under-promoted, organisation was poor, and many athletes never knew they had competed in an Olympic event. Prizes varied — rectangular medals for some winners, cups or trophies for others — and National Olympic Committees did not yet exist.

**Who were the standout athletes at Paris 1900?**
American Alvin Kraenzlein won four events: the 60 metres, 110 metres hurdles, 200 metres hurdles and long jump. Swiss shooter Konrad Stäheli won three gold medals. Hélène de Pourtalès of Switzerland became the first woman to win an Olympic gold medal, in sailing's mixed 1–2 ton class.

**Was polo an Olympic sport in 1900?**
Polo was on the programme of the second Olympic Games, included following a British initiative, and the IOC lists it among five sports with mixed-nationality teams at these Games. One specialist source describes the matches as exhibition matches; this profile records polo as on the programme without settling that characterisation.
