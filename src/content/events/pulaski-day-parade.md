## Overview

[Chicago](/places/chicago/)'s Pulaski Day Parade is the city's annual tribute to Casimir Pulaski, the Polish-born Revolutionary War cavalry officer known as the "Father of American Cavalry." It began as October death-anniversary marches in the 1930s and today centres on a first-Monday-in-March ceremony at the Polish Museum of America, honouring a hero who never set foot in Illinois but became a symbol of Chicago's Polish community. The earliest Chicago celebrations covered by the Chicago Tribune took place in October, on the anniversary of Pulaski's death, with the earliest found in 1933 ([Chicago Public Library](https://www.chipublib.org/blogs/post/who-was-casimir-pulaski-and-when-did-we-start-celebrating-his-birthday/)). A Pulaski Day Parade was televised on Channel 9 in October 1958, according to a Tribune headline of the time ([Chicago Public Library](https://www.chipublib.org/blogs/post/who-was-casimir-pulaski-and-when-did-we-start-celebrating-his-birthday/)). Pulaski himself was born in [Warsaw](/places/warsaw/) on 6 March 1745 and died on 11 October 1779, two days after being mortally wounded at the Siege of Savannah ([Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/discover-the-short-life-and-long-legacy-of-casimir-pulaski-a-polish-cavalry-officer-who-became-an-american-revolutionary-hero-180986162/)).

## From October marches to a March holiday

Chicago's Pulaski observance changed its season entirely over the twentieth century. In 1935, President Franklin D. Roosevelt vetoed a bill that would have created a federal commemoration of Pulaski, yet local October celebrations continued ([Chicago Public Library](https://www.chipublib.org/blogs/post/who-was-casimir-pulaski-and-when-did-we-start-celebrating-his-birthday/)). The first Tribune mention of a March observance came in 1947, when Governor Dwight Green set 4 March as Pulaski Day for the 200th anniversary of the hero's birth, though Chicagoans kept marking the October death anniversary too ([Chicago Public Library](https://www.chipublib.org/blogs/post/who-was-casimir-pulaski-and-when-did-we-start-celebrating-his-birthday/)). Governor Dan Walker then signed legislation making the first Monday in March a commemorative holiday, an event the Polish Museum of America dates to 9 September 1973 ([Polish Museum of America](https://www.polishmuseumofamerica.org/the-rise-of-casimir-pulaski-day/)). In 1985, Governor James R. Thompson signed legislation making that Monday a bank and school holiday, and it was first observed as a public holiday in 1986 ([Chicago Public Library](https://www.chipublib.org/blogs/post/who-was-casimir-pulaski-and-when-did-we-start-celebrating-his-birthday/)).

## The ceremony at the Polish Museum of America

Today the parade tradition lives on as a ceremony, not a street march. The Polish Museum of America is the official State of Illinois location for the Pulaski Day celebration on the first Monday in March, and the museum calls it the largest public event it holds each year ([Polish Museum of America](https://www.polishmuseumofamerica.org/polcon2us/)). The museum has observed the day since the 1980s ([Military.com](https://www.military.com/army/2026/03/04/annual-chicago-ceremony-honors-polish-american-father-of-american-cavalry.html)). For the 280th anniversary of Pulaski's birth in 2025, the president of [Poland](/places/poland/), the senior United States senator from Illinois and Chicago political figures gathered beneath Stanisław Kaczor Batowski's painting *Pulaski at Savannah*, amid a Polish feast, folk dancers and survivors of the 1944 Warsaw Uprising ([Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/discover-the-short-life-and-long-legacy-of-casimir-pulaski-a-polish-cavalry-officer-who-became-an-american-revolutionary-hero-180986162/)). The 2026 ceremony raised $10,000 for the museum through a fundraising drive organised by Cook County Treasurer Maria Pappas, who has attended the event since 1989 ([Military.com](https://www.military.com/army/2026/03/04/annual-chicago-ceremony-honors-polish-american-father-of-american-cavalry.html); [Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/discover-the-short-life-and-long-legacy-of-casimir-pulaski-a-polish-cavalry-officer-who-became-an-american-revolutionary-hero-180986162/)) — a modest sum that shows the day now doubles as a fundraiser sustaining the museum itself.

## Chicago's parade is not New York's

The name invites confusion, because the famous Pulaski Day Parade marches in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/), not Chicago. New York's parade was first held in 1937 and runs up Fifth Avenue on the first Sunday of October, close to the 11 October federal General Pulaski Memorial Day; its 89th edition marched on 4 October 2026 from 35th to 56th Street ([poland.us](https://poland.us/en/pulaski-parade-2026-in-nyc-dr-iwona-korga-to-lead-polonia-down-fifth-avenue/)). Chicago's tradition is the mirror image: an October parade that faded, replaced by a March ceremony tied to Pulaski's birthday and to Illinois law, which recognises the holiday nowhere else in the [United States](/places/united-states/) ([Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/discover-the-short-life-and-long-legacy-of-casimir-pulaski-a-polish-cavalry-officer-who-became-an-american-revolutionary-hero-180986162/)). The Illinois–Poland bond runs deeper than one day: the Illinois National Guard has run a State Partnership Program with Poland since 1993, and 200 Polish men enlisted in the Illinois militia during the [American Civil War](/events/american-civil-war/) ([National Guard](https://www.nationalguard.mil/News/State-Partnership-Program/Article/575093/illinois-guard-members-consul-general-of-poland-celebrate-pulaski-day/index.html)).

## FAQ

**When is Chicago's Pulaski Day Parade?**
Today it is observed on the first Monday in March, around Pulaski's 6 March birthday, as a ceremony at the Polish Museum of America. Historically, Chicago marked the day in October, on the anniversary of Pulaski's death, and a parade was televised on Channel 9 in October 1958.

**Who was Casimir Pulaski?**
Pulaski was a Polish-born cavalry officer who fought for American independence, arriving in 1777 after meeting Benjamin Franklin in Paris. He fought at Brandywine, saved George Washington's army, became a brigadier general, and died of wounds from the Siege of Savannah in 1779.

**Is there still a street parade in Chicago?**
No current annual marching parade in Chicago was verified in the sources read for this profile. The modern observance is a ceremony at the Polish Museum of America, with speeches, folk dancers, a Polish feast and a colour guard presenting the American and Polish flags.

**How is Chicago's parade different from New York's?**
New York's Pulaski Day Parade has marched up Fifth Avenue on the first Sunday of October since 1937. Chicago's October parade tradition faded decades ago, and its modern observance falls on the first Monday in March, tied to Pulaski's birthday and Illinois state law.

**Why does Illinois celebrate a man who never visited the state?**
Pulaski never set foot in Illinois, but Chicago's large Polish community pushed for recognition for much of the twentieth century before the state adopted the holiday. The celebration endures as a statement of Polish-American identity, pride and dignity in the city.
