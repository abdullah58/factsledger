## Overview

The National Games on Chovgan for the President's Cup are Azerbaijan's annual national [chovgan](/events/chovgan/) tournament, contested for the President's Cup and held since 2006. The tournament brings regional and club teams together each December to play chovgan — the traditional Karabakh horse-riding game regarded as the predecessor of modern polo — with the stated aims of preserving and developing the national horse-riding games and promoting equestrian tourism in Azerbaijan ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/nation/62698.html)).

The games began in the Sheki region, at the Republican Equestrian Tourism Center, under the organisation of the Ministry of Culture and Tourism and the Sheki Executive Power, and later moved to the [Bina Equestrian Center](/places/bina-equestrian-center/) in Baku under the Azerbaijan Equestrian Federation ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/nation/62698.html); [AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/sports/218979.html)). Chovgan tournaments in independent Azerbaijan started from the games held in Shaki in December 2006, and since then the Chovgan Presidential Cup has been held every December ([AzerNews](http://www.azernews.az/culture/59653.html)). The tournament is played on [Karabakh horses](/species/karabakh-horse/), the breed on which chovgan is traditionally mounted ([UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/USL/chovqan-a-traditional-karabakh-horse-riding-game-in-the-republic-of-azerbaijan-00905)).

## Origins and purpose

The tournament's purpose, as stated at the 2013 edition, is to preserve and develop the national horse-riding games — including chovgan, described as Azerbaijan's cultural heritage — as well as to promote equestrian tourism in the country ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/nation/62698.html)). A later edition's opening ceremony put the goals in similar terms: to create an impetus for the development of horse breeding in Azerbaijan and to promote the sport among young people ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/sports/218979.html)). The format follows the traditional game: two teams of five to six players each plus one odd player, with no goalkeeper — five-player teams field three attackers and two fullbacks, six-player teams add a halfback — and the ball may be stopped with any part of the body but struck only with a special club ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/nation/62698.html)). Games last 30 minutes in two 15-minute halves, with up to 8 minutes of extra time, then penalty kicks, and if the score is still level, the winner is determined by drawing lots ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/nation/62698.html)).

## The Sheki editions

The best-documented editions are the 8th and 9th National Games, held at the Republican Equestrian Tourism Center in Sheki. The 8th Games opened on 16 December 2013 and ran to 20 December, organised by the Culture and Tourism Ministry and the Sheki Executive Power, bringing together 20 regional teams ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/nation/62698.html)). The opening game saw Sheki beat Lachin 1:0, and the edition coincided with a landmark month for the sport: the culture minister linked the games to chovgan's inclusion that same month in UNESCO's List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding, at the committee's 8th session held in Baku on 2–7 December ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/nation/62698.html)). The 9th competition followed on 17–22 December, with teams from Gabala, Agstafa, Agcabedi, Goranboy, Gakh, Balakan, Yevlakh, Sheki, Oguz, Agdam, Ismailli, Lachin and Xachmaz, plus Baku clubs Serhedchi, Polad and Kovsar ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/sports/75206.html)). In the final, held on 22 December, Serhedchi beat Kovser 4:0 to win the Presidential Cup, with Agtstafa third ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/sports/75206.html)).

## The Baku era

Later editions moved to the Bina Equestrian Center in Baku, organised by the Azerbaijan Equestrian Federation, with a more club-based field. One Bina edition opened with a parade of participating teams, a concert, and a cavalry display by the State Border Service with Garabagh horses, before a first match between Sarhadchi-Garabagh and Polad ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/sports/218979.html)). In another Bina final, the Polad team won the President's Cup, beating Sarhadchi-Karabakh 4:1, after Sarhadchi-Dilbaz had beaten Omar 2:1 for bronze; eight teams took part, including Kohlen, Shirvan, Zafar and Embawood, and the winners received medals, diplomas and a trophy ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/sports/219413.html)). The most recent edition documented here is the 12th President's Chovqan Cup, dedicated to the birthday of President Ilham Aliyev, at which six teams competed, Sarhadchi beat Embavud 2:1 for third place, and Elit overcame Polad 3:2 in the final ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/sports/252176.html)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the National Games on Chovgan for the President's Cup?**
It is Azerbaijan's annual national chovgan tournament, played for the President's Cup each December since 2006. Regional and club teams compete in the traditional Karabakh horse-riding game, with the stated aims of preserving the national horse-riding games and promoting equestrian tourism and horse breeding.

**Where is the tournament held?**
The early editions were held at the Republican Equestrian Tourism Center in Sheki, organised by the Ministry of Culture and Tourism and the Sheki Executive Power. Later editions moved to the Bina Equestrian Center in Baku, organised by the Azerbaijan Equestrian Federation.

**What are the rules of chovgan as played in the tournament?**
Two teams of five to six players plus one odd player, with no goalkeeper; five-player teams field three attackers and two fullbacks. Games last 30 minutes in two 15-minute halves, with up to 8 minutes of extra time, then penalties, and drawing lots if the score is still level.

**Who has won the President's Cup?**
Documented winners include Serhedchi, who beat Kovser 4:0 in the 9th edition's final; Polad, who beat Sarhadchi-Karabakh 4:1 in a later Bina final; and Elit, who beat Polad 3:2 in the 12th Cup. The 8th edition's winner is not reported in the sources read.
