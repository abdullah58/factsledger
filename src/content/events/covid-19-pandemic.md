## Overview

The COVID-19 pandemic was a global health emergency caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus, first reported as a cluster of pneumonia cases in Wuhan, China, on 31 December 2019. The World Health Organization declared it a public health emergency on 30 January 2020, characterised it as a pandemic on 11 March 2020, and ended the emergency designation on 5 May 2023. About 7 million deaths were officially reported to the WHO, while excess-mortality estimates put the true toll at 22.1 million.

The pandemic reshaped daily life on a scale no living generation had seen: by early April 2020 more than half of the world's population was under lockdown, and the shutdowns that followed destroyed small businesses in large numbers, including neighbourhood cafes and restaurants that never reopened. Vaccines developed in under a year — the fastest in history — eventually allowed the emergency to end, but the virus itself kept circulating as an established health issue.

## From Wuhan to a pandemic

On 31 December 2019 the Wuhan Municipal Health Commission reported a cluster of pneumonia cases in Wuhan, Hubei province, and a novel coronavirus was eventually identified as the cause ([WHO](https://www.who.int/NEWS/ITEM/27-04-2020-WHO-TIMELINE---COVID-19)). China publicly shared the virus's genetic sequence on 12 January 2020, and the first case outside China was confirmed in Thailand the next day ([WHO](https://www.who.int/NEWS/ITEM/27-04-2020-WHO-TIMELINE---COVID-19)). By 30 January, when the WHO declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern, its situation report counted 7,818 confirmed cases worldwide, 82 of them in 18 countries outside China ([WHO](https://www.who.int/NEWS/ITEM/27-04-2020-WHO-TIMELINE---COVID-19)). On 11 March 2020 the WHO said it was "deeply concerned both by the alarming levels of spread and severity, and by the alarming levels of inaction" and assessed that COVID-19 could be characterised as a pandemic ([WHO](https://www.who.int/NEWS/ITEM/27-04-2020-WHO-TIMELINE---COVID-19)). The virus, SARS-CoV-2, causes the disease COVID-19, and the two names are often used interchangeably in public discussion ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/05-05-2023-statement-on-the-fifteenth-meeting-of-the-international-health-regulations-(2005)-emergency-committee-regarding-the-coronavirus-disease-(covid-19)-pandemic)).

## Lockdowns and the economic shock

The response to the virus was as historic as the virus itself. By early April 2020 more than half of the world's population was under lockdown, with [India](/places/india/)'s lockdown alone covering more than 1.3 billion people by late April ([Risk Management and Healthcare Policy](https://www.DovePress.com/the-ldquoquarantine-dry-eyerdquo-the-lockdown-for-coronavirus-disease--peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-RMHP)). The shutdowns froze economic life: the World Bank forecast in June 2020 that the global economy would shrink 5.2% that year, the deepest recession since the Second World War ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2020/06/08/covid-19-to-plunge-global-economy-into-worst-recession-since-world-war-ii?ref=timesnext.com)). The realised damage was smaller but still severe — IMF figures show world output contracted 3.1% in 2020 before rebounding 6.1% in 2021 ([The Business Standard](https://www.tbsnews.net/world/global-economy/imf-cuts-global-growth-forecasts-warns-high-inflation-threatens-recession?amp)). Governments responded with rescue spending, including the [Paycheck Protection Program](/events/paycheck-protection-program/) in the [United States](/places/united-states/), a roughly $800 billion loan scheme to keep small-business workers paid.

## The small-business toll

Small businesses bore the worst of the shutdowns. Between 1 March and 31 August 2020, 163,735 businesses listed on Yelp in the United States closed, and 97,966 of them — 60% — never reopened ([FEE](https://fee.org/articles/yelp-60-of-business-closed-during-pandemic-are-permanent/)). Restaurants were hit hardest: 32,109 closed and 19,590 of those closures were permanent ([FEE](https://fee.org/articles/yelp-60-of-business-closed-during-pandemic-are-permanent/)). Retail followed close behind, with 30,374 closures, 58% of them permanent, and bars and nightlife saw 6,451 closures, 54% of them permanent ([FEE](https://fee.org/articles/yelp-60-of-business-closed-during-pandemic-are-permanent/)). Permanent closures had already risen 34% since Yelp's previous report in mid-July. The figures cover businesses listed on Yelp, a large but partial window on the country's small firms. The pattern explains why so many neighbourhood ventures — cafes, restaurants and bars among them — vanished during 2020 even where their owners had done nothing wrong: months of forced closure drained reserves that small firms rarely hold.

## Vaccines and the end of the emergency

The way out of the pandemic came with unprecedented speed. On 8 December 2020, 90-year-old Margaret Keenan became the first person in the world to receive a COVID-19 jab outside a clinical trial, at Coventry University Hospital, where NHS matron May Parsons gave her the [Pfizer](/organizations/pfizer/)/BioNTech vaccine ([Science Museum Group](https://blog.sciencemuseum.org.uk/marking-two-years-since-margaret-keenan-and-may-parsons-made-history/)). Created in less than a year, the COVID-19 vaccines are the fastest ever developed — the previous record, the mumps vaccine, took four years ([Science Museum Group](https://blog.sciencemuseum.org.uk/marking-two-years-since-margaret-keenan-and-may-parsons-made-history/)). By the time the emergency ended, 13.3 billion doses had been administered worldwide, with 89% of health workers and 82% of adults over 60 having completed their primary vaccination course ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/05-05-2023-statement-on-the-fifteenth-meeting-of-the-international-health-regulations-(2005)-emergency-committee-regarding-the-coronavirus-disease-(covid-19)-pandemic)). On 5 May 2023 the WHO determined that COVID-19 was "an established and ongoing health issue which no longer constitutes a public health emergency of international concern" ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/05-05-2023-statement-on-the-fifteenth-meeting-of-the-international-health-regulations-(2005)-emergency-committee-regarding-the-coronavirus-disease-(covid-19)-pandemic)). The virus kept circulating, but the emergency phase was over.

## Frequently asked questions

**When did the WHO declare COVID-19 a pandemic?**
On 11 March 2020, when the WHO said it was "deeply concerned both by the alarming levels of spread and severity, and by the alarming levels of inaction" and assessed that COVID-19 could be characterised as a pandemic ([WHO](https://www.who.int/NEWS/ITEM/27-04-2020-WHO-TIMELINE---COVID-19)). It had already declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern on 30 January 2020.

**How many people died in the pandemic?**
About 7.0 million deaths were officially reported to the WHO between 2020 and 2023, but WHO excess-mortality estimates put the true toll at 22.1 million over the same period ([WHO](https://www.who.int/data/stories/world-health-statistics-2026--a-visual-summary)). Excess deaths peaked in 2021 at 10.4 million, driven by more lethal variants such as Delta and severe strain on health systems.

**When did the pandemic end?**
The WHO ended the global health emergency on 5 May 2023, determining that COVID-19 no longer constituted a Public Health Emergency of International Concern ([WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/05-05-2023-statement-on-the-fifteenth-meeting-of-the-international-health-regulations-(2005)-emergency-committee-regarding-the-coronavirus-disease-(covid-19)-pandemic)). The virus itself did not disappear; it continues to circulate as an established and ongoing health issue.

**How badly did lockdowns hit small businesses?**
Very badly. Yelp's September 2020 report found 163,735 US businesses on its platform had closed between March and August 2020, and 97,966 of them — 60% — never reopened ([FEE](https://fee.org/articles/yelp-60-of-business-closed-during-pandemic-are-permanent/)). Restaurants were worst hit, with 32,109 closures, 61% of them permanent.

**Who received the first COVID-19 vaccine?**
Margaret Keenan, a 90-year-old from Coventry, received the first COVID-19 jab outside a clinical trial on 8 December 2020, at Coventry University Hospital ([Science Museum Group](https://blog.sciencemuseum.org.uk/marking-two-years-since-margaret-keenan-and-may-parsons-made-history/)). NHS matron May Parsons administered the Pfizer/BioNTech vaccine, days after UK regulators had approved it.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not state a single settled death toll: the WHO's own figures disagree by design — 7.0 million deaths were reported, while excess-mortality modelling estimates 22.1 million deaths for 2020–2023, a gap driven by underreporting and by indirect deaths from disrupted health care. It does not state the date of the first COVID-19 death, which was not verified from a fully-read source. It does not present the World Bank's 5.2% contraction as the realised outcome for 2020; that was a June 2020 forecast, while the IMF's realised figure is a 3.1% contraction. It does not claim the pandemic "ended" on 5 May 2023 in the sense of the virus disappearing — the WHO ended the emergency designation while the virus continued to circulate. It does not cover the medical science of COVID-19 in depth, the origin debate beyond the verified reporting timeline, or country-by-country policy comparisons, none of which were within the scope of the sources read for this profile.
