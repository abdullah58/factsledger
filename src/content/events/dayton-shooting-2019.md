## Overview

In the early hours of 4 August 2019, 24-year-old Connor Betts opened fire outside bars in the Oregon District of [Dayton](/places/ohio/), Ohio, killing nine people and injuring 27 others before police officers already patrolling the area shot him dead within about 30 seconds ([Associated Press](https://www.ndinsider.com/story/news/2019/08/04/dayton-ohio-shooting/1914438001/)). It was the second mass shooting in the [United States](/places/united-states/) in less than a day, following the [El Paso shooting](/events/el-paso-shooting-2019/) hours earlier ([Associated Press](https://www.ndinsider.com/story/news/2019/08/04/dayton-ohio-shooting/1914438001/)). The FBI's final report, released in November 2021, concluded that Betts acted alone, was not directed by any organisation, and was driven by a long-standing fascination with mass violence rather than by an ideology ([Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/cincinnati/news/press-releases/investigative-report-on-the-august-4-2019-attack-in-dayton-ohio)).

The shooting began at around 1 a.m. in the 400 block of East Fifth Street, a nightlife area of bars and restaurants, as crowds were leaving the clubs ([Associated Press](https://www.ndinsider.com/story/news/2019/08/04/dayton-ohio-shooting/1914438001/)). Betts wore body armour and a mask and carried a .223-calibre "assault-style" rifle with extra magazines capable of firing up to 100 rounds ([Associated Press](https://www.ndinsider.com/story/news/2019/08/04/dayton-ohio-shooting/1914438001/)). Dayton Mayor Nan Whaley called it a "senseless act of violence", and Ohio Governor Mike DeWine said the officers' response "saved many, many lives" ([Associated Press](https://www.ndinsider.com/story/news/2019/08/04/dayton-ohio-shooting/1914438001/)).

## The victims

The nine people killed were Megan K. Betts, 22, Lois L. Oglesby, 27, Saeed Saleh, 38, Derrick R. Fudge, 57, Logan M. Turner, 30, Nicholas P. Cumer, 25, Thomas J. McNichols, 25, Beatrice N. Warren-Curtis, 36, and Monica E. Brickhouse, 39 ([Associated Press](https://www.ndinsider.com/story/news/2019/08/04/dayton-ohio-shooting/1914438001/)). They ranged in age from 22 to 57; four were women and five were men, six were black and three were white ([Associated Press](https://www.ndinsider.com/story/news/2019/08/04/dayton-ohio-shooting/1914438001/)). Among the dead was Megan Betts, the shooter's own sister. Betts had driven to the Oregon District earlier in the evening with his sister, but the two separated after arrival; police said Megan was "one of the initial victims" but not the first ([Associated Press](https://www.ndinsider.com/story/news/2019/08/04/dayton-ohio-shooting/1914438001/)). Five area hospitals treated the injured, with Miami Valley Hospital receiving 16 patients ([Associated Press](https://www.ndinsider.com/story/news/2019/08/04/dayton-ohio-shooting/1914438001/)).

The speed of the police response defined the night. Officers already in the area heard the gunshots, ran toward them, and killed Betts within about 30 seconds of the first shots; Dayton Police Chief Richard Biehl said officers engaged the gunman about halfway through that span ([Associated Press](https://www.ndinsider.com/story/news/2019/08/04/dayton-ohio-shooting/1914438001/)). The Congressional Record puts the interval at 32 seconds and notes that the officers "didn't receive the call in 32 seconds" and "didn't show up in 32 seconds" — they killed him in 32 seconds ([Congressional Record](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-165/issue-143/house-section/article/H7548-1)). Six officers who stopped the shooter — Sergeant Knight and Officers Jeremy Campbell, Vincent Carter, David Denlinger, Ryan Nabel and Brian Rolfes — received the Medal of Valor at the White House ([Congressional Record](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-165/issue-143/house-section/article/H7548-1)).

## Investigation and motive

Because Betts was killed at the scene, there was no trial — and the question of why he did it fell to investigators. The FBI and the Dayton Police Department released their final report on 29 November 2021, after more than 125 interviews in multiple states, review of over 950 surveillance videos amounting to about 400 hours of footage, and analysis of electronic devices and social media ([Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/cincinnati/news/press-releases/investigative-report-on-the-august-4-2019-attack-in-dayton-ohio)). Their conclusion was that Betts was solely responsible, acted alone, and "was not directed by any organization or aligned to any specific ideological group" ([Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/cincinnati/news/press-releases/investigative-report-on-the-august-4-2019-attack-in-dayton-ohio)).

The FBI's Behavioural Analysis Unit assessed that Betts's "enduring fascination with mass violence" and his inability to cope with a convergence of personal factors — including a decade-long struggle with multiple mental health stressors and the successive loss of significant stabilising anchors before 4 August 2019 — "likely were the primary contributors to the timing and finality of his decision" ([Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/cincinnati/news/press-releases/investigative-report-on-the-august-4-2019-attack-in-dayton-ohio)). He had fantasised about mass shootings, serial killings and murder-suicide for at least a decade without sharing specific details with friends or family, and there were no specific warnings that he intended to commit a crime ([Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/cincinnati/news/press-releases/investigative-report-on-the-august-4-2019-attack-in-dayton-ohio)). Writings found at his family home in Bellbrook showed an interest in killing people but did not indicate a racial or political motive, according to law-enforcement sources, and former schoolmates said he had made a high-school "hit list" ([CNN](https://www.nbcpalmsprings.com/entertainment-report/2019/08/05/the-dayton-gunman-killed-9-people-in-30-seconds-families-dont-know-why)).

The investigation also established how Betts was armed. He had bought the .223-calibre rifle legally from a local firearms dealer, which had ordered it online from Texas; he had no criminal record other than traffic offences and would not have been prevented from buying the gun by a background check ([Associated Press](https://www.ndinsider.com/story/news/2019/08/04/dayton-ohio-shooting/1914438001/)). The FBI found he likely violated federal law by lying about his drug use when he purchased the firearm in early 2019, and that the additional firearm parts, body armour and 100-round magazine were acquired on open-market internet sites with the help of an associate, Ethan Kollie, who pleaded guilty and was sentenced to 32 months in prison in February 2020 for illegally possessing certain firearms and lying on a federal firearms form ([Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/cincinnati/news/press-releases/investigative-report-on-the-august-4-2019-attack-in-dayton-ohio)).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many people were killed and injured in Dayton?

Nine people were killed and 27 others were injured ([Associated Press](https://www.ndinsider.com/story/news/2019/08/04/dayton-ohio-shooting/1914438001/)). The shooter, Connor Betts, was also killed — by police — so some tallies list ten deaths including the perpetrator; this profile counts the nine victims separately from Betts.

### How quickly did police stop the shooter?

Within about 30 seconds of the first shots, according to the Associated Press, or 32 seconds according to the Congressional Record ([Associated Press](https://www.ndinsider.com/story/news/2019/08/04/dayton-ohio-shooting/1914438001/); [Congressional Record](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-165/issue-143/house-section/article/H7548-1)). Officers were already patrolling the Oregon District and ran toward the gunfire rather than arriving after a call.

### What was Connor Betts's motive?

The FBI's final report found no ideological or organisational motive: Betts acted alone and was not aligned to any specific ideological group ([Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/cincinnati/news/press-releases/investigative-report-on-the-august-4-2019-attack-in-dayton-ohio)). Its Behavioural Analysis Unit pointed to a decade-long fascination with mass violence, mental health stressors, and the loss of stabilising personal anchors as the likely contributors.

### Was his sister Megan deliberately targeted?

That was not resolved by the sources read for this profile. Police said Megan Betts was one of the initial victims but not the first, and that she and her brother had separated after arriving together ([Associated Press](https://www.ndinsider.com/story/news/2019/08/04/dayton-ohio-shooting/1914438001/)). The profile does not assert that she was deliberately targeted.

### What happened to the man who helped Betts get weapon parts?

Ethan Kollie, the associate who helped Betts acquire firearm parts, body armour and a 100-round magazine, pleaded guilty to illegally possessing certain firearms and lying on a federal firearms form, and was sentenced to 32 months in prison in February 2020 ([Federal Bureau of Investigation](https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/cincinnati/news/press-releases/investigative-report-on-the-august-4-2019-attack-in-dayton-ohio)).

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not give a single definitive motive for the shooting: the FBI's own conclusion is that the motive was a convergence of personal factors, not an ideology or a grievance, and the profile reports that finding rather than a simpler explanation.
- It does not state whether Betts deliberately targeted his sister; investigators did not resolve that question in the sources read here.
- It does not use the injured figure of 26 quoted in one contemporaneous report; it uses 27, the figure in the Associated Press report and the Congressional Record, and flags the variance.
- It does not describe the shooting in graphic detail; it reports the sequence, the casualties, and the investigation.
