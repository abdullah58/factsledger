## Overview

The Coupe de France is the rare national cup competition whose own rulebook keeps score of underdogs. Since 1976, the French Football Federation has maintained an official honor roll — the *Classement des petits poucets* — ranking each season's best-performing non-professional clubs on the rounds they passed, the level they reached, and the higher-tier sides they knocked out, with tiebreaks favoring the lower-division club and Federation awards for the top three ([Fédération Française de Football](https://media.fff.fr/uploads/documents/reglement-de-la-coupe-de-france-20252026-ok.pdf)).

Born during the First World War on the initiative of Henri Delaunay, the competition was the first in France to unite clubs of every level in a single-elimination format; its first final, in 1918, saw Olympique de Pantin beat FC Lyon 3–0 ([Ligue 1](https://ligue1.com/en/articles/l1_article_1508-coupe-de-france-history-format-all-you-need-to-know)). Paris Saint-Germain are its record winners with 16 titles, ahead of Marseille with 10 and Saint-Étienne with 6, out of 36 different winning clubs as of 2025 ([Khel Now](https://khelnow.com/football/2023-04-world-football-coupe-de-france-list-all-winners)).

## A cup engineered for the underdog

The competition's machinery is designed to give small clubs a genuine chance. The early rounds are regional qualifiers, run locally to keep costs down; clubs from National 3 (the fifth tier) join from the third round, and the professional sides only enter at the seventh round ([Ligue 1](https://ligue1.com/en/articles/l1_article_1508-coupe-de-france-history-format-all-you-need-to-know)). Clubs from the overseas departments and territories — Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana, Réunion — hold their own knockout competitions whose winners advance to the seventh round and integrate into the national draw ([Ligue 1](https://ligue1.com/en/articles/l1_article_1508-coupe-de-france-history-format-all-you-need-to-know)).

The regulations even codify the underdog's home advantage: when the second-drawn club is at least two divisions below its opponent, it becomes the home team — so a Ligue 1 side can be forced to travel to a National 1 or lower ground ([Fédération Française de Football](https://media.fff.fr/uploads/documents/reglement-de-la-coupe-de-france-20252026-ok.pdf)). The trophy itself is an *objet d'art* donated by Dr. Michaux and owned by the Federation; both finalists receive replicas, and the winner takes a trophy definitively ([Fédération Française de Football](https://media.fff.fr/uploads/documents/reglement-de-la-coupe-de-france-20252026-ok.pdf)). France Télévisions, the competition's broadcaster, describes it as gathering more than 7,000 amateur and professional clubs — and the dream, in its presentation, is to receive the trophy "from the hands of the President of the Republic" ([France Télévisions](https://www.francetvpub.fr/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/cdf2019.pdf)).

## The 1926 final: Montbéliard's underdogs

The cup's giant-killing folklore has deep roots. In 1926, [[AS Valentigney](/organizations/as-valentigney/)](https://factsledger.com/organizations/as-valentigney) — a small club from the Montbéliard region — reached the final against Olympique de Marseille, and were beaten 4–1 at the Stade de Colombes on May 9, 1926, in front of 30,000 spectators ([OM4ever](http://om4ever.com/GrandesFinales/26OMValentigney.htm)).

The contemporary report in *Le Miroir des Sports* treated the finalists as the heroes regardless of the score: "les gars du pays de Montbéliard" had the entire crowd's sympathy, ran themselves into the ground, and never despaired despite Marseille's crushing superiority of class. Marseille, the report concluded, "won the Cup, but did not carry away the admiration of those who love beautiful football" ([OM4ever](http://om4ever.com/GrandesFinales/26OMValentigney.htm)). It is the archetypal *petit poucet* story — the kind the Federation's honor roll was created to celebrate fifty years later.

## Records and rituals

The modern giant-killer benchmark is Calais RUFC's run to the 2000 final as a fourth-tier amateur side; in the competition's history, a side from outside the top tier has reached the final on 15 occasions ([Ligue 1](https://ligue1.com/en/articles/l1_article_1508-coupe-de-france-history-format-all-you-need-to-know)). More recently, Les Herbiers were the "petit poucet" finalists of 2017–18 before falling to PSG ([France Télévisions](https://www.francetvpub.fr/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/cdf2019.pdf)).

The final is played in May at the Stade de France, and since 1927 the French President has traditionally attended and handed the trophy to the winning captain ([Khel Now](https://khelnow.com/football/2023-04-world-football-coupe-de-france-list-all-winners)). The winner also earns a place in the UEFA Europa League — as Nantes did in 2022 and Toulouse in 2023 ([Ligue 1](https://ligue1.com/en/articles/l1_article_1508-coupe-de-france-history-format-all-you-need-to-know)). PSG's dominance of the modern era culminated in their 16th title in 2024–25, a 3–0 victory over Stade de Reims ([Khel Now](https://khelnow.com/football/2023-04-world-football-coupe-de-france-list-all-winners)).
