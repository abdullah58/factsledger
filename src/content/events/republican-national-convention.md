## Overview

The Republican National Convention broke a 170-year pattern in September 2026. On September 9–10, the party gathered at the American Airlines Center in Dallas for its first-ever midterm convention — the first major-party midterm convention of any kind since the Democrats held one in 1982 ([The American Star News](https://theamericanstarnews.com/trump-pledges-5000-dividend-to-voters-at-dallas-midterm-convention/)). There were no voting delegates and no nominations to decide; the two-day event was a messaging push ahead of the November 3 midterm elections ([The American Star News](https://theamericanstarnews.com/trump-pledges-5000-dividend-to-voters-at-dallas-midterm-convention/)). Donald Trump headlined the first night with a nearly two-hour keynote, promising a $5,000 "Trump dividend" to every adult American if Republicans kept control of both chambers of Congress ([The American Star News](https://theamericanstarnews.com/trump-pledges-5000-dividend-to-voters-at-dallas-midterm-convention/)); on night two, Vice President [JD Vance](/people/jd-vance/) delivered the keynote to "JD! JD!" chants, and Trump closed the convention with a repeat-after-me pledge to vote ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/international/donald-trump-implores-republican-convention-goers-to-go-and-vote-as-party-faces-midterm-risks/article71454802.ece)).

At its traditional core, the convention is the Republican Party's quadrennial nominating convention — the gathering, held every four years since 1856, where delegates formally name the party's presidential and vice-presidential candidates, adopt a platform, and conduct party business ([Republican National Committee](https://prod-static.gop.com/media/documents/2024_Call_of_the_Convention_as_adopted_11.20.23_1700517775.pdf)). It is run by the Republican National Committee, which issues the official "Call" convening each convention ([Republican National Committee](https://prod-static.gop.com/media/documents/2024_Call_of_the_Convention_as_adopted_11.20.23_1700517775.pdf)).

The most recent nominating convention met July 15–18, 2024, in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, where Donald Trump was nominated for president and named Senator JD Vance of Ohio as his running mate ([Medill News Service](https://dc.medill.northwestern.edu/blog/2024/07/25/photos-recap-of-the-2024-republican-national-convention/)). The next nominating convention is set for Houston, Texas, in the summer of 2028 — dates not yet final ([Houston First](http://www.houstonfirst.com/news/rnc-selects-houston-for-2028-republican-national-convention)).

## The first midterm convention (2026)

Conventions have traditionally belonged to presidential years, but the party wanted a midterm edition too: the September 9–10, 2026 gathering in Dallas was the first Republican midterm convention ever held, and the first by either major party since the Democrats' 1982 midterm conference ([The American Star News](https://theamericanstarnews.com/trump-pledges-5000-dividend-to-voters-at-dallas-midterm-convention/)). RNC chair Joe Gruters billed it as a "Trump-a-palooza," per the Minnesota Reformer ([The American Star News](https://theamericanstarnews.com/trump-pledges-5000-dividend-to-voters-at-dallas-midterm-convention/)). Unlike a nominating convention, there were no voting delegates on the floor — it was a two-day platform for party messaging and for rallying Trump's base ahead of the November 3 midterms ([The American Star News](https://theamericanstarnews.com/trump-pledges-5000-dividend-to-voters-at-dallas-midterm-convention/)).

The headliner was Trump's opening-night keynote, which ran nearly two hours and centered on a new economic promise: a $5,000 "Trump dividend" to every adult American if Republicans retained both chambers of Congress, with the one condition that the money be spent in the United States ([The American Star News](https://theamericanstarnews.com/trump-pledges-5000-dividend-to-voters-at-dallas-midterm-convention/)). The proposal could cost more than $1 trillion and was roundly panned by budget experts — and even by some Republicans ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/international/donald-trump-implores-republican-convention-goers-to-go-and-vote-as-party-faces-midterm-risks/article71454802.ece)). It followed an earlier unfulfilled pledge: in November 2025 Trump had promised $2,000 checks funded by tariff revenue, which never materialized after the Supreme Court struck down his emergency tariffs ([The American Star News](https://theamericanstarnews.com/trump-pledges-5000-dividend-to-voters-at-dallas-midterm-convention/)).

Night two belonged to Vice President JD Vance, whose keynote was watched closely as a possible signal of a post-Trump future — the crowd greeted him with "JD! JD!" chants — while Trump returned to close the convention with a voter-turnout appeal, leading the crowd in a repeat-after-me pledge to vote on November 3 ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/international/donald-trump-implores-republican-convention-goers-to-go-and-vote-as-party-faces-midterm-risks/article71454802.ece)). The evening included a video tribute to Charlie Kirk, the conservative activist assassinated a year earlier, and even a video appearance by Democratic Senator John Fetterman praising Republican Senator Dave McCormick ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/international/donald-trump-implores-republican-convention-goers-to-go-and-vote-as-party-faces-midterm-risks/article71454802.ece); [The American Star News](https://theamericanstarnews.com/trump-pledges-5000-dividend-to-voters-at-dallas-midterm-convention/)).

## Born as an anti-slavery institution

The convention predates the modern campaign rally by a long way. The Republican Party formed in 1854 as the Whig Party fractured over slavery; abolitionists, former Whigs and Democrats, and Free Soilers united around opposition to the Kansas–Nebraska Act of 1854 ([PhillyVoice](https://www.phillyvoice.com/republicans-first-adopted-anti-slavery-platform-philly/)). In February 1856, a national plenary meeting in Pittsburgh affirmed opposition to slavery's westward expansion and support for a slavery-free Kansas ([PhillyVoice](https://www.phillyvoice.com/republicans-first-adopted-anti-slavery-platform-philly/)).

Four months later came the first convention: June 17–19, 1856, at Musical Fund Hall (808 Locust Street) in Philadelphia, with about 600 delegates putting anti-slavery opposition — specifically blocking slavery's spread westward — at the center of the new party's platform ([PhillyVoice](https://www.phillyvoice.com/republicans-first-adopted-anti-slavery-platform-philly/)). John Frémont, "The Pathfinder," got the presidential nod and William Dayton of New Jersey the vice-presidential nod — chosen over Abraham Lincoln — under the slogan "Free Soil, Free Labor, Free Men" ([PhillyVoice](https://www.phillyvoice.com/republicans-first-adopted-anti-slavery-platform-philly/)). Frémont lost that November to Democrat James Buchanan, 45% to 33%, with former president Millard Fillmore taking 22% for the American Party ([PhillyVoice](https://www.phillyvoice.com/republicans-first-adopted-anti-slavery-platform-philly/)).

## How the nomination actually works

The RNC's official Call spells out the mechanics. Delegations come from all 50 states, DC, and five territories — American Samoa, Guam, the Northern Mariana Islands, Puerto Rico, and the Virgin Islands ([Republican National Committee](https://prod-static.gop.com/media/documents/2024_Call_of_the_Convention_as_adopted_11.20.23_1700517775.pdf)). Each state's delegation is built from a formula: 10 at-large delegates, three delegates per House member, the state's RNC committeeman, committeewoman, and chair, plus bonus delegates for voting Republican in the last presidential election, having a Republican governor, a House majority, legislative-chamber majorities, and Republican senators ([Republican National Committee](https://prod-static.gop.com/media/documents/2024_Call_of_the_Convention_as_adopted_11.20.23_1700517775.pdf)).

Delegates won through state preference votes must be allocated and bound — proportionally or winner-take-all — for at least one ballot, and states calling the roll do so alphabetically through their delegation chair ([Republican National Committee](https://prod-static.gop.com/media/documents/2024_Call_of_the_Convention_as_adopted_11.20.23_1700517775.pdf)). To have a name placed in nomination, a candidate must show plurality support from delegates of five or more states — the rule since the 2020 convention; the nominee is whoever wins a majority of the votes entitled to be cast, with the roll call repeated until someone does ([Republican National Committee](https://prod-static.gop.com/media/documents/2024_Call_of_the_Convention_as_adopted_11.20.23_1700517775.pdf)). A lone qualifying vice-presidential candidate can be nominated by acclamation — which is exactly what happened with Mike Pence in 2016 ([Republican National Committee](https://prod-static.gop.com/media/documents/2024_Call_of_the_Convention_as_adopted_11.20.23_1700517775.pdf); [NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/politics/2016-07-19/donald-trump-officially-wins-gop-nomination)).

Four convention committees do the working business: Platform, Credentials, Rules and Order of Business, and Permanent Organization ([Republican National Committee](https://prod-static.gop.com/media/documents/2024_Call_of_the_Convention_as_adopted_11.20.23_1700517775.pdf)). And the rules include an emergency provision letting the RNC move the convention or hold the roll call remotely if the convention cannot meet in its city — the clause that covered the 2020 scramble ([Republican National Committee](https://prod-static.gop.com/media/documents/2024_Call_of_the_Convention_as_adopted_11.20.23_1700517775.pdf)).

One thing worth saying plainly: by the time delegates gather, state primaries and caucuses have usually settled the race, so the roll call formalizes an outcome under binding rules rather than choosing from scratch.

## Three recent conventions

**2016, Cleveland.** The roll call on July 19 at Quicken Loans Arena made Trump's nomination official, with a brief Alaska recount dispute and Washington state's delegation casting all its votes for Trump despite earlier talk of unbinding ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/politics/2016-07-19/donald-trump-officially-wins-gop-nomination)). Donald Trump Jr. announced the New York delegation's votes that pushed his father past the majority, and House Speaker Paul Ryan chaired ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/politics/2016-07-19/donald-trump-officially-wins-gop-nomination)).

**2020, Charlotte/Jacksonville/Washington.** The only modern convention reshaped by a pandemic. Trump and the RNC pulled it out of Charlotte on June 2, 2020, after Governor Roy Cooper refused to guarantee a full-capacity crowd without COVID-19 measures; Jacksonville, Florida, was named the replacement ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2020/7/23/trump-cancels-in-person-republican-convention-events-in-florida)). On July 23 Trump canceled the Jacksonville events, saying "the timing for this event is not right," as Florida suffered a sharp coronavirus spike ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2020/7/23/trump-cancels-in-person-republican-convention-events-in-florida)). What remained was a hybrid: on August 24, just 336 delegates gathered in Charlotte for a roughly four-hour session to formally renominate Trump, while the rest of the 2,500+ delegates voted by proxy; prime-time programming originated from the Andrew W. Mellon Auditorium in Washington, DC, with Trump accepting the nomination from the White House South Lawn under the theme "Honoring the Great American Story" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2020/7/23/trump-cancels-in-person-republican-convention-events-in-florida); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2020/8/23/republicans-prepare-for-trump-heavy-hybrid-convention)).

**2024, Milwaukee.** July 15–18 at Fiserv Forum, shadowed by the July 13 assassination attempt in Butler, Pennsylvania — Trump attended wearing a bandage on his ear as delegates chanted "fight, fight, fight" ([Medill News Service](https://dc.medill.northwestern.edu/blog/2024/07/25/photos-recap-of-the-2024-republican-national-convention/)). Trump was formally nominated on opening day and announced Vance as his running mate the same day; his acceptance speech was described as the longest such acceptance in U.S. convention history ([Medill News Service](https://dc.medill.northwestern.edu/blog/2024/07/25/photos-recap-of-the-2024-republican-national-convention/)). Unity-themed speeches came from former rivals including Nikki Haley and Ron DeSantis, and the Sunday after the convention, [Joe Biden](/people/joe-biden/) dropped out of the race and endorsed Kamala Harris ([Medill News Service](https://dc.medill.northwestern.edu/blog/2024/07/25/photos-recap-of-the-2024-republican-national-convention/)).

## 2028: Houston

The RNC voted on August 25, 2023, to select Houston for the 2028 convention — an unusually early pick, made possible by a 2022 rules change letting the party choose the 2028 city before the 2024 election; Houston had submitted its bid in June 2022 ([Houston First](http://www.houstonfirst.com/news/rnc-selects-houston-for-2028-republican-national-convention)). The bid centers on the Toyota Center, the George R. Brown Convention Center, and Minute Maid Park (the bid-era name), with about 50,000 attendees and roughly $200 million in economic impact expected ([Houston First](http://www.houstonfirst.com/news/rnc-selects-houston-for-2028-republican-national-convention)). Houston last hosted the RNC in 1992, when it renominated George H. W. Bush ([Houston First](http://www.houstonfirst.com/news/rnc-selects-houston-for-2028-republican-national-convention)). Dates are not yet final; the host-city release said likely August 2028, and a June 2026 commentary piece said late August ([Houston First](http://www.houstonfirst.com/news/rnc-selects-houston-for-2028-republican-national-convention); [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/restoring-america/courage-strength-optimism/4611265/2028-campaign-what-will-trump-do/)).

## What this profile does not claim
