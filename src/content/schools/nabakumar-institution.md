## Overview

Nabakumar Institution is a school-and-college in Bakshi Bazar, Old Dhaka, founded in 1916 and named for a
Dhaka zamindar called Nabakumar; its college division, added in 1973, is named Dr. Shahidullah College
after the educator Dr. Muhammad Shahidullah
([Banglapedia](https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Nabakumar_Institute);
[institution site](https://niadsc.edu.bd/)). The school and college divisions share one campus at 16 Umesh
Dutta Road, Bakshibazar, and run under a single administration
([institution site](https://niadsc.edu.bd/)). The school's own profile describes a 1,800-student institution
with 68 teachers, two buildings of six and four storeys, morning and day shifts, and science, humanities
and business-studies streams ([institution site](https://niadsc.edu.bd/)).

The institution's national profile rests on 24 January 1969, when a Standard IX student, Matiur Rahman
Mallik, was shot dead by police during a demonstration in Dhaka — one of the killings that galvanised the
mass-upsurge movement against the Ayub Khan government later that year
([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/city/mass-upsurge-day-today-1524313);
[Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/303385/historic-mass-upsurge-day-tuesday)).

## History

The institute was established in 1916 in Bakshi Bazar and operated as a school even though it was named an
institute ([Banglapedia](https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Nabakumar_Institute)). In 1973 it opened a
college branch, which was named for Dr. Muhammad Shahidullah, the educationist after whom the present
college division is still named
([Banglapedia](https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Nabakumar_Institute);
[institution site](https://niadsc.edu.bd/)). The institution's own site dates the school branch to 1916 and
the college branch to 1973, and states that both divisions now operate from the same campus under one
administration ([institution site](https://niadsc.edu.bd/)).

## The 1969 mass upsurge

On 24 January 1969, Matiur Rahman Mallik, a Standard IX student of Nabakumar Institution, and Rustam Ali,
a rickshaw-puller, were killed in police firing on demonstrators in Dhaka as the Pakistani authorities
tried to suppress the popular uprising
([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/city/mass-upsurge-day-today-1524313);
[The Business Standard](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/historic-mass-upsurge-day-today-780254)). The
killings spread protests across the country and are credited with helping to bring down the Ayub regime
([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/city/mass-upsurge-day-today-1524313)). In a message quoted
by Dhaka Tribune, the prime minister named further victims of the day's firing, including "Makbul of
Nabakumar Institute," alongside Matiur, Anwar, Rustam, Milon and Alamgir — this wider casualty list rests
on that quoted message alone and is not independently corroborated here
([Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/303385/historic-mass-upsurge-day-tuesday)).

## Memorials and annual commemoration

Banglapedia records that the government granted 10 million taka in 1998 toward memorial facilities at the
school, leading to Matiur Rahman memorial structures on campus
([Banglapedia](https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Nabakumar_Institute)). Every 24 January, the campus
becomes a site of national commemoration: the Awami League places wreaths at the "Shaheed Matiur Rahman
Smiriti Saudha" at the Bakshibazar campus
([The Business Standard](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/historic-mass-upsurge-day-tuesday-572826);
[The New Nation](https://dailynewnation.com/historic-mass-upsurge-day-today-2/)), while The Daily Star
describes tributes being paid at "his monument at Nabakumar Institution in the capital's Bakshibazar"
([The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/city/mass-upsurge-day-today-1524313)). The New Nation
separately reports Bangabandhu Sangskritik Jote laying wreaths at the campus monument and holding a
discussion at the institute
([The New Nation](https://dailynewnation.com/news/411057/)). Whether the "Smiriti Saudha," the "monument,"
and the library and museum described by Banglapedia are one structure or several is not resolved by the
sources.

## The institution today

The school's own site presents a full present-day picture: 1,800 students taught by 68 teachers with two
librarians and 12 other staff, housed in a six-storey and a four-storey building, with a morning shift
from 7.45am to noon and a day shift from noon to 4pm ([institution site](https://niadsc.edu.bd/)). Streams
offered are science, humanities and business studies; facilities include a library of several thousand
books, physics, chemistry, biology and computer laboratories, and a student "honesty store"
([institution site](https://niadsc.edu.bd/)). Co-curricular life covers a cultural week, speech, debate and
essay competitions, study tours, annual sports, national-day observances, freshers' receptions and parents'
days, and the campus is under CCTV with round-the-clock guards
([institution site](https://niadsc.edu.bd/)). The principal is listed as Brigadier General Md. Abdul Mannan
Bhuiyan (retd.), who describes 29 years of service in the army's education corps before joining the
institution ([institution site](https://niadsc.edu.bd/)).
