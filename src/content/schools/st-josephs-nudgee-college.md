## Overview

On 12 September 2026, St Joseph's Nudgee College defeated Brisbane Boys' College 31-24 at Ross Oval before close to 10,000 spectators to claim the school's 47th GPS First XV rugby premiership — a fifth consecutive title, the fourth of them outright — extending what match coverage called the most dominant run in the competition's history. Nudgee had led the competition for all nine rounds and sealed it outright ([CODE Sports](https://www.europesays.com/australia/94571/)).

The flyhalf who steered the title, Chilli Smith, played the season in the months after the death of his father, former Wallaby scrumhalf Brian Smith, and carried his name on his arm. Smith's chip-and-chase just before half-time sparked the raid that restored Nudgee's lead 19-14, and afterwards he spoke of the weight of the No. 10 jersey — worn before him by Elton Flatley, Reesjan Pasitoa, Archie Mesritz and James O'Connor ([CODE Sports](https://www.europesays.com/australia/94571/)).

The college behind that run is a Catholic day and boarding school for boys in Years 5 to 12 at 2199 Sandgate Road, Boondall, on Brisbane's northside ([St Joseph's Nudgee College](https://cathnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Information_Package_DP_Nudgee.pdf)). Founded in 1891 by the Congregation of Christian Brothers, it is a school in the Edmund Rice tradition administered under Edmund Rice Education Australia, with more than 1,700 students — over 300 of them boarders — on a 136-hectare campus about 16 kilometres from the Brisbane CBD ([St Joseph's Nudgee College](https://media.digistormhosting.com.au/sjnc-au-qld-165-website-2023/content/NC-Prospectus_FEB_2025_WEB.pdf?v=1774568346); [Brisbane City Council](https://heritage.brisbane.qld.gov.au/heritage-places/254)).

## History: the Irish-Catholic boarding mission

Nudgee began in 1891 as the boarding campus of St Joseph's College, Gregory Terrace, which had run out of room to house its boarders. The Boondall site was chosen by Brother Patrick Ambrose Treacy, founder of the Christian Brothers' Australian Province, at the request of Brisbane's Archbishop Robert Dunne, and Brother D. F. Bodkin was appointed the first headmaster ([Mapping Brisbane History](https://mappingbrisbanehistory.com.au/location/022-st-josephs-college-nudgee-collegeboondall/)). In 1938 the college extended its reach with Nudgee Junior College, opened on a separate campus at Indooroopilly ([Mapping Brisbane History](https://mappingbrisbanehistory.com.au/location/022-st-josephs-college-nudgee-collegeboondall/)).

Peer-reviewed research has shown the founding mission ran deeper than accommodation. The college was established to give the children of Irish Catholics in regional and remote Queensland and northern New South Wales an education that could act as a vehicle for socio-economic advancement — and its first decades were shaped by the tension between a belligerent determination to retain an Irish identity and the awareness that a "ghetto mentality" would block that advancement ([University of Southern Queensland Research Repository](https://research.usq.edu.au/item/q6v00/divided-loyalties-st-joseph-s-nudgee-college-the-great-war-and-anzac-day-1915-39)). That tension surfaced in the college's mixed reaction to the outbreak of war in 1914 and its subsequent reticence to celebrate Anzac Day between 1916 and 1939 — a reticence documented through the college's own Annuals ([University of Southern Queensland Research Repository](https://research.usq.edu.au/item/q6v00/divided-loyalties-st-joseph-s-nudgee-college-the-great-war-and-anzac-day-1915-39)).

At the college's Golden Jubilee in 1941, Archbishop Sir James Duhig described it as "the jewel in the crown of the Christian Brothers' Schools in Queensland" ([Mapping Brisbane History](https://mappingbrisbanehistory.com.au/location/st-josephs-college-nudgee/)). The split into two St Joseph's colleges fostered a sporting culture that, in one historical account, prioritised sport over scholarship — and the "Battle of the Colours" rivalry with Terrace turned the old school tie into a passion ([Mapping Brisbane History](https://mappingbrisbanehistory.com.au/location/st-josephs-college-nudgee/)). The college's buildings were entered on the Queensland Heritage Register on 6 November 2006 ([Brisbane City Council](https://heritage.brisbane.qld.gov.au/heritage-places/254)).

## Boarding, campus and the Edmund Rice mission

Boarding is the institution's defining thread: the college's own prospectus calls it "the beating heart of Nudgee College" for more than 130 years, and counts 320 boarders drawn from regional, rural and remote communities across Australia and overseas — including Papua New Guinea, the Pacific Islands, Asia, the Middle East and the United Kingdom ([St Joseph's Nudgee College](https://media.digistormhosting.com.au/sjnc-au-qld-165-website-2023/content/NC-Prospectus_FEB_2025_WEB.pdf?v=1774568346)). Boarding life is guided by the values of faith, family, courage and trust, with House Mums and supervisors forming the daily care network ([St Joseph's Nudgee College](https://media.digistormhosting.com.au/sjnc-au-qld-165-website-2023/content/NC-Prospectus_FEB_2025_WEB.pdf?v=1774568346)).

In 2024 that program won the Australian Boarding Schools Association's Boarding School of the Year award at the Australian Education Awards ([St Joseph's Nudgee College](https://media.digistormhosting.com.au/sjnc-au-qld-165-website-2023/content/NC-Prospectus_FEB_2025_WEB.pdf?v=1774568346)). Its physical centre is the Bathersby Boarding Village, a castle-like complex designed by m3architecture that brings all four boarding houses together in one building for the first time in the school's history — a three-to-four storey wall enclosing a private courtyard, with common rooms, kitchens and vertical-garden bathrooms, home to 330 boys and their supervisors ([Archello](https://archello.com/project/st-josephs-nudgee-college-bathersby-boarding)).

The school describes its academic mission in the Edmund Rice frame: a liberating education built on gospel spirituality within an inclusive community committed to justice and solidarity ([St Joseph's Nudgee College](https://media.digistormhosting.com.au/sjnc-au-qld-165-website-2023/content/NC-Prospectus_FEB_2025_WEB.pdf?v=1774568346)). That frame shows in concrete programs — social justice and sustainability work, year-level retreats, liturgies, immersion programs — and in the school's Reconciliation Action Plan ([St Joseph's Nudgee College](https://cathnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Information_Package_DP_Nudgee.pdf)). The student formation program targets wellbeing through a curriculum covering men's health, digital citizenship and positive relationships, and the House system is explicitly designed to make a big school feel small ([St Joseph's Nudgee College](https://media.digistormhosting.com.au/sjnc-au-qld-165-website-2023/content/NC-Prospectus_FEB_2025_WEB.pdf?v=1774568346)).

The co-curricular roll reads like a country town: alongside rugby union, cricket, rowing, swimming and esports sits a Cattle Club, with cattle yards on the campus itself ([St Joseph's Nudgee College](https://media.digistormhosting.com.au/sjnc-au-qld-165-website-2023/content/NC-Prospectus_FEB_2025_WEB.pdf?v=1774568346)). The college's motto is *Signum Fidei* — "a Sign of Faith" — and its principal is Mr Scott Thomson ([St Joseph's Nudgee College](https://media.digistormhosting.com.au/sjnc-au-qld-165-website-2023/content/NC-Prospectus_FEB_2025_WEB.pdf?v=1774568346)).

## The rugby dynasty

No section of the college's life is more documented than rugby. As one of the nine member schools of Queensland's Great Public Schools Association, Nudgee has converted its boarding pipeline into the competition's most successful program: [the association's own account](/organizations/great-public-schools-queensland-rugby-union/) records 47 GPS First XV premierships, five straight to 2026.

The 2026 season was the dynasty's sharpest expression. Nudgee led the competition from the opening round, took the title in a final-round showdown, and closed it out on Ross Oval on 12 September ([CODE Sports](https://www.europesays.com/australia/94571/)). The decider against Brisbane Boys' College swung on the details — hooker Aiden Luke's pilfers and pick-and-drives, No. 8 Xavier Hill's match-winning breakdown steal on full time, college captain and halfback Ethan Andrews' 50-metre dart — before Nudgee's notorious maul landed the dagger and the school celebrated its fifth title in a row ([CODE Sports](https://www.europesays.com/australia/94571/)).
