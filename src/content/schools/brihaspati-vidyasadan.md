## Overview

Brihaspati Vidyasadan (BVS) is a private day and boarding school in Naxal, Kathmandu, operated by Brihaspati
Vidyasadan Management Pvt Ltd. A 2015 survey of Nepal's post-SLC education landscape described it as one of the
country's "old and reputed schools" and "the choice of bright and diligent students"
([New Spotlight](https://www.spotlightnepal.com/2015/07/03/after-slc-search-for-quality-education/)).
It was named a **Full Award** recipient in the [British Council](https://factsledger.com/organizations/british-council)'s 2019–20 International School Award programme
([British Council](https://www.britishcouncil.org.np/sites/default/files/press_release_isa-20_october_2020.pdf)).
Separately, the land its campus occupies — leased for more than three decades from the Nepal Children's
Organisation, a state-linked charity also known as Bal Mandir — is the subject of an unresolved fraud case in
Nepal's courts, following a government inquiry that found the lease had improperly favoured private interests.

## The school

### Standing and scale

The school dates its own founding to 1985: a company-profile page carrying the institution's own copyright gives
that year, while the school's current website displays a "42 Years of Excellence" counter, which would imply
roughly 1984
([VocalPanda](https://www.vocalpanda.com/c/brihaspati-vidyasadan);
[Brihaspati Vidyasadan](https://bvs.edu.np)).
Neither figure has been independently confirmed. The school's own site reports 116 teaching staff, more than 120
non-teaching staff, over 400 Plus 2 students and over 1,000 students in Grades 1–10, and its student testimonials
reference Plus 2 streams including science, management and psychology
([Brihaspati Vidyasadan](https://bvs.edu.np)).
Its own news posts name Kumar Thapa as principal, in office at least from 2023 into 2024
([Brihaspati Vidyasadan](https://bvs.edu.np/brihaspati-vidyasadan-successfully-conducted-its-7th-open-house-session-at-brihaspati-school-premises/)).

## The Bal Mandir land dispute

### Origins of the lease

The Nepal Children's Organisation (NCO), widely known as Bal Mandir, was established in 1964 under Nepal's
National Guidance Act, and the government transferred it a large tract of land in Naxal, Kathmandu — 119 ropanis,
12 annas and 3 paisas, following the nationalization of Sita Bhawan
([Nepalnews](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/the-bal-mandir-land-scandal-from-charity-land-to-corruption-case/);
[myRepublica](https://www.myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/cases-filed-against-17-persons-over-below-par-lease-rental-of-bal-mandir-la-74-23.html)).
In February 1992, NCO leased 9 ropani of that land to Brihaspati Vidyasadan; further parcels were added in
2001–2002, bringing the total to roughly 28–29 ropani
([Nepalnews](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/the-bal-mandir-land-scandal-from-charity-land-to-corruption-case/);
[The Himalayan Times](https://english.himalayatimes.com.np/2023/09/76323/)).
The Himalayan Times reported that the school itself was founded in order to secure this land, describing
**Purushottam Raj Joshi** as its founder — put forward, the paper says, by a network of Indian-origin merchants
it calls "Vishal Group," which holds a majority interest while the Joshi family retains a modest stake — a
characterization not repeated in later, more detailed reporting (see
[What this profile does not claim](#what-this-profile-does-not-claim)).

The lease was repeatedly renegotiated and extended over the following decades, at rents Nepal's anti-corruption
body, the CIAA, and multiple news outlets described as far below market rates. In 2009 the CIAA directed NCO to
cancel the lease over this concern, but the directive was not enforced; instead, the two parties jointly went to
the Supreme Court and reached a settlement through which the school regained the land on further extended terms
([Nepalnews](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/the-bal-mandir-land-scandal-from-charity-land-to-corruption-case/)).
A supplementary agreement signed on June 21, 2015 extended the lease again — later accounts describe the
extension as running 43 years beyond a 2032 expiry, though the exact lease years vary between outlets (see
[What this profile does not claim](#what-this-profile-does-not-claim)).
Reporting on the rents likewise varies: myRepublica's 2025 charge-sheet report put the renewed annual rent at Rs
11 million (roughly Rs 393,000 per ropani), "significantly lower than the prevailing market rates," while
Nepalnews's 2026 explainer described the school as paying the NCO Rs 9 million a year for the entire holding even
as it collected Rs 6 million a year from a sub-tenant — Rai School, affiliated with India's Central Board of
Secondary Education — for just five ropanis
([myRepublica](https://www.myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/cases-filed-against-17-persons-over-below-par-lease-rental-of-bal-mandir-la-74-23.html);
[Nepalnews](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/the-bal-mandir-land-scandal-from-charity-land-to-corruption-case/)).

### The Bhattarai Commission and the voided lease

NCO's government-appointed leadership changed around 2019–2020, and in December 2019 the Nepali government formed
a five-member inquiry commission, headed by former appellate chief justice Haribabu Bhattarai, to examine the
lease. The commission submitted its report to the Ministry of Women, Children and Senior Citizens in May 2020,
concluding that since 1992, NCO officials had colluded to hand the charity's assets to private interests, and
that the lease agreements with Brihaspati were legally improper, procedurally defective and financially harmful
— but the report went unimplemented for years
([Nepalnews](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/the-bal-mandir-land-scandal-from-charity-land-to-corruption-case/)).

On April 27, 2024, NCO's board officially cancelled the lease agreement with the school, declaring it legally
void, after the organisation's president alleged the original arrangement was "selfish and dishonest"
([Nepalnews](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/the-bal-mandir-land-scandal-from-charity-land-to-corruption-case/);
[Ukaalo](https://www.ukaalo.com/ukaalo-vishesh/20240426-balmandir-terminated-agreement-with-brihaspati/16861/)).
The NCO presidency at the centre of the scandal had been held for nearly three decades by **Rita Singh Vaidya**
— daughter of democratic leader Ganeshman Singh and elder sister of Nepali Congress leader Prakash Man Singh —
appointed in 1992 and in office until 2019 (per Nepalnews; the Himalayan Times dates her ouster to 12 Shrawan
2077, i.e. mid-2020). She died in December 2024, months before the charge sheet was filed
([Nepalnews](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/the-bal-mandir-land-scandal-from-charity-land-to-corruption-case/);
[The Himalayan Times](https://english.himalayatimes.com.np/2023/09/76323/)).

### Investigation and charges

Nepal's Central Investigation Bureau opened a formal probe in May 2024, after NCO's leadership filed complaints
alleging systemic misuse of the organisation's fixed assets. Courts issued arrest warrants for 20 people
connected to the case, independently reported by Kantipur/Ekantipur
([10 May 2024](https://ekantipur.com/news/2024/05/10/en/arrest-warrant-for-20-people-for-embezzlement-of-bal-mandir-land-00-27.html))
and Online Khabar
([9 May 2024](https://english.onlinekhabar.com/bal-mandir-land-case-arrest-warrant-issued-against-members-of-vishal-group.html)).

On May 7, 2025, prosecutors filed fraud charges against 17 individuals at Kathmandu District Court under Section
249 of the Criminal Code 2074 (Nepal Penal Code 2017), seeking about Rs 603.9 million in damages; those convicted
at this scale face up to seven years' imprisonment plus a fine. Three of the original 20 warrant targets were
excluded from the charge sheet, having died before it was filed: Chiranjeevi Tiwari, then-chairperson of
Brihaspati Vidyasadan Management Pvt Ltd; Manohar Gopal Shrestha, then NCO treasurer; and Suman Shakya, then NCO
deputy general secretary
([myRepublica](https://www.myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/cases-filed-against-17-persons-over-below-par-lease-rental-of-bal-mandir-la-74-23.html);
[Nepalnews](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/the-bal-mandir-land-scandal-from-charity-land-to-corruption-case/)).
Among the 17 charged, Republica names the school's chairperson, **Purushottam Raj Joshi** (82), alongside six
other directors of Brihaspati Vidyasadan Management Pvt Ltd; the remaining ten held positions at NCO itself and
are not affiliated with the school. Nepalnews adds that three of the accused are Indian nationals, and that the
Attorney General's Office overruled a district-level recommendation against prosecution to send the case to trial
([myRepublica](https://www.myrepublica.nagariknetwork.com/news/cases-filed-against-17-persons-over-below-par-lease-rental-of-bal-mandir-la-74-23.html);
[Nepalnews](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/the-bal-mandir-land-scandal-from-charity-land-to-corruption-case/)).
As of the most recent reporting found, from April 2026, the case remained before the Kathmandu District Court,
and the government had forwarded the long-dormant 2020 commission report to police for further action
([Nepalnews](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/the-bal-mandir-land-scandal-from-charity-land-to-corruption-case/)).

### Other reported scrutiny

Separately from the land case, a 2018 report by the Nepali outlet Naya Page alleged that the school charged fees
above its officially declared tuition through bundled mandatory charges for items such as food and transport
([Naya Page](https://www.nayapage.com/archives/44169)). This profile does not treat that allegation as resolved
either way; it is noted as a separate, independent line of reporting about the school, distinct from the land case.

## What this profile does not claim

**This profile makes no finding, and draws no inference, about the guilt or innocence of any individual named in
the fraud case described above.** Charges are allegations that a court has not yet resolved, not findings of fact;
this profile treats them as such throughout, and will be updated if the case's outcome is independently reported.

The characterization of a "Vishal Group" or "Bishal Group" as the school's controlling interest appears in three of
the independent sources used here (The Himalayan Times, Online Khabar and New Business Age) but not in the most
comprehensive later account (Nepalnews, 2026), which instead refers to named individuals and to Brihaspati
Vidyasadan Management Pvt Ltd. This profile therefore attributes that label to the specific outlets that used it,
rather than asserting it as an established, formally registered entity name — and notes it has no connection to the
unrelated apparel and retail company of the same name.

Of the 17 people charged in May 2025, only the school's chairperson is named here; the other six charged directors
of Brihaspati Vidyasadan Management Pvt Ltd are referred to collectively rather than individually, since they do
not otherwise appear to be public figures. No claim is made about the school's current enrollment, academic
results or day-to-day operating status beyond the self-reported figures on its own site, since no independent
source addressed these during this research.

No claim is made that the school currently offers a Cambridge A Level programme: older tertiary profiles describe
one as launched in 2008–09, but the British Council's current A Level Partner Schools list for Nepal does not
include Brihaspati Vidyasadan, and the school's own site foregrounds its Plus 2 programmes instead. The three
alumni named above come from the school's own alumni page and are presented as the school's own account, not as
independently verified fact. Early-history details found only in uncited tertiary profiles (a Dhobi Khola start, a
first SLC batch in 1990, a 1998–99 Birendra National Shield) could not be independently verified and are not
stated here.
