## Overview

Tunisia is a North African republic of 12.3 million people, bordered by Algeria and Libya on the Mediterranean Sea, with Tunis as its capital. It was the birthplace of the 2011 Arab Spring, adopted a democratic constitution in 2014, then saw President Kais Saied suspend parliament in 2021, adopt a presidential constitution in 2022, and win re-election in 2024 with 90.7% of the vote on 28.8% turnout. Its economy — textiles, agriculture, tourism and phosphates — grew 2.5% in 2025 ([World Bank](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099525208062610753/pdf/Bulk-9d23615b-0898-4d4d-bb20-c2e1003eaa13.pdf); [United States Senate](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-resolution/310/text)).

This profile is about Tunisia the country — officially the Republic of Tunisia — not Tunis, the capital city from which the country takes its name. The country covers 163,610 square kilometres, with Arabic as the official language alongside French and Tamazight, and the Tunisian dinar as its currency ([Institute for Security Studies](https://futures.issafrica.org/geographic/guide.pdf?geography=TN&topic=current-path); [World Bank](https://www.developmentaid.org/api/frontend/cms/file/2025/12/IDU-355dc59a-9397-4eb4-b360-811cfc74993f.pdf)). Its deep past is unusually visible: Tunisia holds ten [UNESCO](/organizations/unesco/) World Heritage properties, nine cultural and one natural, from the archaeological site of Carthage and the Amphitheatre of El Jem to the Medina of Tunis and, most recently, the Village of Sidi Bou Saïd, inscribed in 2026 ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/statesparties/tn/)).

Abroad, Saied has tilted away from Western partners, signing a "strategic partnership" with [China](/places/china/) in May 2024 and straining relations with [Morocco](/places/morocco/) after welcoming the Polisario Front leader in 2022, while the [United States](/places/united-states/) — a partner since a 1797 treaty — has criticised the repression ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/RS21666)). At home, the AMEN social programme reaches over 1.48 million vulnerable people with cash transfers, the ELMED interconnector — a $268 million, 600-megawatt undersea cable to [Italy](/places/italy/) — will tie Tunisia's grid to Europe, and Canada committed $5 million over five years for [Colleges and Institutes Canada](/organizations/colleges-and-institutes-canada/) to build environment-related academic programmes with Tunisian higher institutes ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/tunisia/overview); [Academica Group](https://academica.ca/top-ten/cican-receives-5m-to-strengthen-environmental-programs-in-tunisia/)).

## From independence to revolution

Tunisia gained independence from [France](/places/france/) on 20 March 1956, with Habib Bourguiba as Prime Minister before becoming the country's first President in 1957 ([United States Senate](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-resolution/310/text)). Bourguiba led Tunisia for the next 30 years, a period of vast social reforms — including an ambitious state-led development strategy emphasising secularism and gender equity — alongside restrictions on civil society and democratic participation ([United States Senate](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-resolution/310/text); [Institute for Security Studies](https://futures.issafrica.org/geographic/guide.pdf?geography=TN&topic=current-path)). In 1987 Prime Minister Zine El Abidine Ben Ali deposed Bourguiba, citing his failing health, and named himself President; he was elected in 1989 and 1994 without genuine opposition and re-elected in 1999, 2004 and 2009 by implausibly high margins ([United States Senate](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-resolution/310/text)). On 17 December 2010, street vendor Mohamed Bouazizi set himself on fire in Sidi Bouzid after police harassment, and the protests that followed forced Ben Ali to flee to Saudi Arabia on 14 January 2011, ending 23 years of rule and igniting the wider Arab Spring ([United States Senate](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-resolution/310/text)).

## The 2021 power grab and the new order

After the revolution Tunisia alone among Arab Spring countries built a constitutional democracy: a Constituent Assembly adopted a new constitution on 26 January 2014, four civil society groups shared the 2015 Nobel Peace Prize for mediating the transition, and Béji Caïd Essebsi won the first genuinely free presidential election that year ([United States Senate](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-resolution/310/text); [Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/RS21666)). That order unravelled when Kais Saied, a law professor elected president in 2019, dismissed Prime Minister Hichem Mechichi on 25 July 2021, suspended parliament and assumed full executive authority ([United States Senate](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-resolution/310/text)). He then seized the electoral commission, dissolved the High Judicial Council, and in July 2022 won a referendum approving a constitution centralising power in the presidency — 94% of the vote on 31% turnout amid an opposition boycott ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/RS21666)). Saied won a second term on 6 October 2024 with 90.7% of the vote on 28.8% turnout, the lowest since the revolution, with nearly all would-be challengers jailed or barred; mass convictions of opposition figures followed in April 2025 ([United States Senate](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-resolution/310/text)).

## Economy: a slow, uneven recovery

Tunisia has a diverse economy built on textiles, agriculture, tourism and phosphate mining, though a net energy importer; the [European Union](/organizations/european-union/) is its top trading partner, taking over half of its trade, and its leading exports are garments, insulated wire, olive oil and refined petroleum ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/RS21666)). Growth has been modest and volatile: the economy contracted by 9.0% in 2020, stalled at 0.2% in 2023, then recovered to 1.6% in 2024 and 2.5% in 2025, driven by agriculture, up 9.5% in the first nine months of 2025 on strong olive oil and cereal harvests, and a construction revival, while tourism arrivals passed pre-pandemic levels ([World Bank](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099525208062610753/pdf/Bulk-9d23615b-0898-4d4d-bb20-c2e1003eaa13.pdf); [World Bank](https://www.developmentaid.org/api/frontend/cms/file/2025/12/IDU-355dc59a-9397-4eb4-b360-811cfc74993f.pdf)). The recovery has not yet reached the labour market: unemployment was 15.2% in late 2025, youth unemployment stayed above 40%, and women's labour force participation was 27.7% against 64.8% for men ([World Bank](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099525208062610753/pdf/Bulk-9d23615b-0898-4d4d-bb20-c2e1003eaa13.pdf)). Public debt, 82.2% of GDP in 2025, is increasingly financed at home, and the government has leaned on domestic borrowing and the Central Bank after Saied rejected an IMF loan in 2023 ([World Bank](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099525208062610753/pdf/Bulk-9d23615b-0898-4d4d-bb20-c2e1003eaa13.pdf); [Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/RS21666)).

## FAQ

### Where is Tunisia?

Tunisia sits in North Africa on the Mediterranean Sea, bordered by Algeria to the west and south-west and Libya to the south-east. It covers 163,610 square kilometres, and its capital is Tunis, on the north-eastern coast. Arabic is the official language, French and Tamazight are widely spoken, and the currency is the Tunisian dinar.

### What happened in Tunisia in 2011?

In December 2010 street vendor Mohamed Bouazizi set himself on fire in Sidi Bouzid to protest police harassment, sparking nationwide protests against corruption, repression and unemployment. President Zine El Abidine Ben Ali fled to Saudi Arabia on 14 January 2011 after 23 years in power, and the uprising ignited the wider Arab Spring across the region.

### Who is Kais Saied and what changed after 2021?

Kais Saied is a constitutional law professor elected president in 2019. On 25 July 2021 he dismissed the prime minister, suspended parliament and assumed full executive authority. A 2022 referendum then approved a constitution centralising power in the presidency, and he won re-election in October 2024 with 90.7% of the vote on 28.8% turnout.

### What is Tunisia's economy based on?

Textiles, agriculture, tourism and phosphate mining are the key sectors, with garments, insulated wire, olive oil and refined petroleum among the top exports. The European Union takes over half of Tunisia's trade. Growth reached 2.5% in 2025, but unemployment was 15.2% and public debt stood at 82.2% of GDP.

### What are Tunisia's UNESCO World Heritage sites?

Tunisia has ten World Heritage properties: nine cultural and one natural. The cultural sites include Carthage, the Medina of Tunis, the Amphitheatre of El Jem, Kairouan, Dougga, the Medina of Sousse, Kerkuane, Djerba and the Village of Sidi Bou Saïd, inscribed in 2026. The single natural site is Ichkeul National Park, listed in 1980.
