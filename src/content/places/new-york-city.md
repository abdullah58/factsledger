## Overview

New York City is the most populous city in the [United States](/places/united-states/) — 8,804,190 residents in the 2020 census, over 20 million across the metro area — a city and port at the mouth of the Hudson River in southeastern New York state, and, in Britannica's phrase, "the largest and most influential American metropolis" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/New-York-City)). It has held the rank of America's largest city since the first U.S. census in 1790, and it is the most international city in the country. Less known: the city is also the founding ground of America's engineering-accreditation system — the body now known as [ABET](/organizations/abet/) was founded and headquartered here from 1932 until 1996 ([ABET](https://www.abet.org/about-abet/history/)).

## The five boroughs

The modern city is really "a collection of many neighbourhoods scattered among the city's five boroughs," each with its own lifestyle — moving between them "may be like passing from one country to another" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/New-York-City)). The boroughs and their counties:

| Borough | County | Character in brief |
|---|---|---|
| Manhattan | New York County | The island core: Wall Street, Broadway, Fifth Avenue |
| Brooklyn | Kings County | Once an independent city; approved consolidation by 277 votes |
| The Bronx | Bronx County | The only borough mainly on the mainland |
| Queens | Queens County | The city's largest borough by land area |
| Staten Island | Richmond County | The harbor borough, reached across the Narrows |

The five-borough shape is the product of consolidation, not geography. In 1868 the politician and planner Andrew Haswell Green — later called the "Father of Greater New York" — proposed bringing the city and its surrounding communities under one municipal government to coordinate transportation and infrastructure; after years of debate a nonbinding referendum passed in 1894, and on January 1, 1898, "Greater New York" was established ([WorldAtlas](https://www.worldatlas.com/county/why-new-york-city-is-five-different-counties.html)). The vote was closest in Brooklyn, where consolidation won by just 277 votes — a reminder that the metropolis was assembled politically, borough by borough.

The city's government is strong mayor–council: the mayor serves a four-year term and may hold office for a maximum of two consecutive terms ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/New-York-City)). Its seal, adopted in 1686, carries a beaver and a flour barrel — images that document Manhattan's first economic phase, the fur trade and flour exports.

## A harbor that made a metropolis

New York's site explains its rise. It sits where the Hudson and East rivers empty into "one of the world's premier harbours," making it "both the gateway to the North American continent and its preferred exit to the oceans of the globe" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/New-York-City)). In 1524 the Italian navigator Giovanni da Verrazzano was the first European to enter the harbor; the bridge dedicated to him in 1964, spanning the Narrows, was then the world's longest suspension bridge.

The Dutch settlement named New Amsterdam in 1626 (chartered as a city in 1653) came under English control in 1664 and was renamed New York after Charles II granted the lands to his brother, the Duke of York; the name was permanently adopted in 1674. The city was "once the capital of both its state and the country" before commerce eclipsed politics as its defining force ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/New-York-City)).

Trade did the rest. Only the third-largest American port at the Revolution, New York by the mid-1800s handled more than half of the country's oceangoing travelers and commercial trade; after 1900 it was the world's busiest port, a distinction it held until the 1950s. Containerization, obsolete piers, and labor costs shifted cargo to the New Jersey side after the 1960s, but the Port Authority of New York and New Jersey still dominated northeastern U.S. water trade at the start of the 21st century ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/New-York-City)). The rivers themselves are estuaries of the Atlantic — the Hudson is tidal as far north as Troy — and the city's roughly 600 miles of shoreline are "locked in constant combat with the ocean."

## The city's cultural shorthand

Few cities have contributed more images to the collective consciousness: Wall Street means finance, Broadway is synonymous with theatre, Fifth Avenue is paired with shopping, Madison Avenue means advertising, Greenwich Village connotes bohemian lifestyles, Seventh Avenue signifies fashion, Tammany Hall defines machine politics, and Harlem evokes the Jazz Age and African American aspirations ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/New-York-City)). Its symbol is the Statue of Liberty, and for three decades the World Trade Center was the most prominent symbol of the city's global prowess. As James Fenimore Cooper observed, the city was inhabited by strangers who were nonetheless "essentially national in interest, position, pursuits" — no one thinks of the place as belonging to a particular state "but to the United States."

## The engineering cradle: from New Amsterdam to ABET

Here is the thread the encyclopedias skip. In 1932, seven engineering societies founded the Engineers' Council for Professional Development (ECPD) in New York City — first headquartered at the Engineering Societies Building, then at the United Engineering Center — and the council stayed in the city until it relocated to [Baltimore, Maryland](/places/baltimore/) in 1996 ([ABET](https://www.abet.org/about-abet/history/)). The founding societies were the [American Society of Civil Engineers](/organizations/asce/), the American Institute of Mining and Metallurgical Engineers (now [AIME](/organizations/aime/)), the [American Society of Mechanical Engineers](/organizations/asme/), the [American Institute of Electrical Engineers](/organizations/aiee/) (now [IEEE](/organizations/ieee/)), the Society for the Promotion of Engineering Education (now the [American Society for Engineering Education](/organizations/asee/)), the [American Institute of Chemical Engineers](/organizations/aiche/), and the National Council of State Boards of Engineering Examiners (now [NCEES](/organizations/ncees/)).

From that New York base, ECPD evaluated its first engineering degree programs in 1936 and by 1947 had accredited 580 undergraduate engineering programs at 133 institutions — the administrative seed of the accreditation system that, renamed ABET in 1980, now covers 4,863 programs in 42 countries and areas ([ABET](https://www.abet.org/about-abet/history/)). The city's harbor made it America's commercial gateway; its engineering societies made it the profession's standard-setter.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the five boroughs of New York City?**
Manhattan, Brooklyn, the Bronx, Queens, and Staten Island. Each is coextensive with a New York county (New York, Kings, Bronx, Queens, and Richmond counties respectively), and they were combined into a single city on January 1, 1898.

**Why is New York City the largest U.S. city?**
It has been the largest since the first census in 1790. Britannica attributes the rise to its site: the mouth of the Hudson, one of the world's premier harbors, made it the gateway for people and goods — by the mid-1800s it handled more than half of America's oceangoing trade.

**When did New York City get its current shape?**
On January 1, 1898, when the five boroughs consolidated into "Greater New York" after an 1894 referendum. The idea dated to an 1868 proposal by Andrew Haswell Green, and Brooklyn approved consolidation by just 277 votes.

**What is the connection between New York City and ABET?**
ABET was founded in New York City in 1932 as the Engineers' Council for Professional Development by seven engineering societies, headquartered first at the Engineering Societies Building and then the United Engineering Center. It relocated to Baltimore, Maryland, in 1996.

**How long is a New York City mayoral term?**
Four years, with a maximum of two consecutive terms.
