## Overview

Irvine is a master-planned city in central Orange County, [California](/places/california/), [United States](/places/united-states/), built by a single land company around a university campus and incorporated on December 28, 1971. It had 307,670 residents in the 2020 census and an estimated 318,764 by July 2025, on 65.61 square miles of land ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/irvinecitycalifornia)). Irvine is also the safest large city in America by FBI-based crime costs, a city where Asian residents are the largest group, and the headquarters city of telematics company [CalAmp](/organizations/calamp/).

## From cattle ranch to planned city

The land that became Irvine was ranch country for a century before it was a city. Gabrielino people had lived in the area for some 2,000 years before Spanish explorer Gaspar de Portolà arrived in 1769, and three large Spanish and Mexican land grants — Rancho Santiago de Santa Ana, Rancho San Joaquin, and Rancho Lomas de Santiago — later made up the Irvine Ranch ([City of Irvine](https://cityofirvine.gov/about-irvine/history-city)). In 1864, in the year of the Great Drought, José Sepúlveda sold 50,000 acres of Rancho San Joaquin to James Irvine, Benjamin and Thomas Flint, and Llewellyn Bixby for $18,000; two years later the partners added the 47,000-acre Rancho Lomas de Santiago for $7,000 ([City of Irvine](https://cityofirvine.gov/about-irvine/history-city)). In 1878 Irvine bought out his partners for $150,000, assembling 110,000 acres stretching 23 miles from the Pacific Ocean to the Santa Ana River ([City of Irvine](https://cityofirvine.gov/about-irvine/history-city)). His son incorporated the holdings as The Irvine Company in 1894, and by 1918 the ranch grew some 60,000 acres of lima beans — billed as the largest bean field in the world under one management ([City of Irvine](https://cityofirvine.gov/about-irvine/history-city); [City of Irvine](https://cityofirvine.gov/news-media/news-article/take-our-city-irvine-history-quiz)).

The town itself was an afterthought of the ranch. Because another town called Irvine already existed in California, the community was first named Myford, for Irvine's infant son; only in 1914, after the northern town became Carson Hill, did it adopt the name Irvine ([City of Irvine](https://cityofirvine.gov/news-media/news-article/take-our-city-irvine-history-quiz)). The pivot came in 1959, when the University of California asked The Irvine Company for 1,000 acres for a new campus. Architect William Pereira proposed a "town and gown" university town on undeveloped company land, and a decade of planning converted 10,000 of the ranch's 93,000 acres into a suburban community of mixed-use residences, business sectors, greenbelts, parks, and bike trails — a plan UC Irvine's archives say became "the model for innumerable master-planned communities nationwide" ([UC Irvine Libraries](http://special.lib.uci.edu/collections/anteater-chronicles/community/the-city-of-irvine); [City of Irvine](https://cityofirvine.gov/about-irvine/history-city)). The first homes were completed by 1965, the first villages by 1970, and on December 28, 1971 residents voted to incorporate a city substantially larger than Pereira's plan had envisioned, to control the area's future and protect its tax base ([UC Irvine Libraries](http://special.lib.uci.edu/collections/anteater-chronicles/community/the-city-of-irvine); [City of Irvine](https://cityofirvine.gov/about-irvine/history-city)).

## A city that outgrew its own plan

Irvine's growth outran every projection made for it. The 2020 census counted 307,670 residents, up from 212,375 in 2010 — a 44.9% increase in a single decade — and the Census Bureau's July 2025 estimate of 318,764 already exceeds the roughly 318,000 residents the city projected in 2015 for build-out sometime after 2035 ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/irvinecitycalifornia); [City of Irvine](https://cityofirvine.gov/about-irvine/history-city)). That growth was not just numerical: Asian residents are the city's largest group at 44.8% of the population, 39.8% of residents are foreign-born, and 51.6% speak a language other than English at home — a majority-minority, immigrant-majority planned city ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/irvinecitycalifornia)).

The city is affluent and highly educated by national measures: 70.6% of residents age 25 and older hold a bachelor's degree or higher, median household income is $136,719, and the median owner-occupied home is valued at $1,191,500 — while the owner-occupied rate of 44.5% and median gross rent of $2,997 show most households rent ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/irvinecitycalifornia)). Retail sales reached $10.37 billion in 2022, and the city counted more than 200,000 jobs and more than 21,000 licensed businesses in 2015, with Broadcom building a permanent campus ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/irvinecitycalifornia); [City of Irvine](https://cityofirvine.gov/about-irvine/history-city)). One of those businesses is CalAmp, the telematics company whose principal executive offices are at 15635 Alton Parkway in Irvine and which filed for Chapter 11 in Delaware in June 2024 under a restructuring agreement with Lynrock Lake Master Fund LP ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000095017024067683/camp-20240522.htm)).

Safety is Irvine's most repeated distinction, and the numbers behind it are specific. MoneyGeek's 2026 ranking, built on FBI 2024 crime data for 315 cities over 100,000 people, puts Irvine's estimated crime cost at $447 per resident — the lowest of any American city over 300,000 people — with a violent crime rate of 84 per 100,000 and property crime of 1,390 per 100,000 ([MoneyGeek](https://www.moneygeek.com/resources/safest-cities/)). The same study ranks Irvine 19th overall among all 315 cities, ahead of every other large city in the country ([MoneyGeek](https://www.moneygeek.com/resources/safest-cities/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was Irvine incorporated?**
December 28, 1971. Residents of the new villages voted to incorporate a city substantially larger than the one William Pereira's master plan had envisioned, to control the area's future and protect its tax base.

**Why is Irvine called a planned city?**
Because it was drawn up before it was built: in 1959 architect William Pereira and Irvine Company planners designed a university town around the new UC Irvine campus, converting 10,000 of the ranch's 93,000 acres into villages, business districts, greenbelts, parks, and bike trails.

**How big is Irvine?**
The 2020 census counted 307,670 residents on 65.61 square miles of land; the Census Bureau's July 2025 estimate is 318,764. The city itself describes its total area as 66 square miles.

**Is Irvine really the safest large city in America?**
On MoneyGeek's 2026 cost-of-crime ranking (FBI 2024 data), yes: at $447 per resident it has the lowest crime cost of any U.S. city over 300,000 people, with a violent crime rate of 84 per 100,000. Rankings depend on methodology, and the FBI cautions against ranking communities from its data.

**What company is headquartered in Irvine?**
Among others, CalAmp, the telematics company, lists its principal executive offices at 15635 Alton Parkway, Suite 250, Irvine, in its SEC filings. It filed for Chapter 11 in Delaware in June 2024.
