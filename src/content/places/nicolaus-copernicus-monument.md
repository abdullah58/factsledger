## Overview

The Nicolaus Copernicus Monument is a bronze statue of the Polish astronomer [Nicolaus Copernicus](/people/nicolaus-copernicus/) (1473–1543) standing on Solidarity Drive in front of the [Adler Planetarium](/organizations/adler-planetarium/) in [Chicago](/places/chicago/), Illinois. Dedicated on 14 October 1973 to mark the 500th anniversary of Copernicus's birth, it is a replica of Bertel Thorvaldsen's 1830 monument in [Warsaw](/places/warsaw/), recast by Polish sculptor Bronislaw Koniuszy with $150,000 raised by the [Copernicus Foundation](/organizations/copernicus-foundation/).

The seated figure holds an armillary sphere in his left hand and a pair of dividers in his right, the tools of the astronomer who argued that the Earth orbits the Sun ([Clio](https://theclio.com/entry/129659)). The 100,000-pound bronze stands on a tall pedestal of eight granite blocks in Northerly Island Park, on Chicago's Museum Campus ([Clio](https://theclio.com/entry/129659)). The monument is both a tribute to a scientific innovator and a symbol of pride for the city's large and active Polish community ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)).

## A replica of the Warsaw original

The Chicago statue copies a monument that has stood in Warsaw, [Poland](/places/poland/), since 1830. Danish sculptor Bertel Thorvaldsen, who ran a large workshop in Rome and built heroic neo-classical monuments for clients including the Pope and Napoleon, designed the Warsaw Copernicus in 1822 and executed it by 1830 ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)). It stands at the Palace Staszic, home of the Polish Academy of Sciences ([Adler Planetarium](https://www.adlerplanetarium.org/blog/copernicus-the-astronomer-the-city/)). The original was damaged in the destruction of Warsaw during World War II, and although the Nazis planned to melt it down, Poles recovered the sculpture; after full repairs and conservation, it was rededicated in 1949 ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)). Polish sculptor Bronislaw Koniuszy, who had served in the Polish Underground Army before graduating from the Academy of Fine Arts in Warsaw in 1953, recast the work for Chicago from Thorvaldsen's original plaster model ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)).

## The 1973 dedication and the voyage from Gdynia

The Adler Planetarium erected the monument to mark the 500th anniversary of the birth of Copernicus, described by the Chicago Park District as the father of modern astronomy ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)). The Copernicus Foundation of Chicago raised $150,000 to produce the replica, and Koniuszy shipped the finished 100,000-pound bronze statue on the Zawiercie, the first ship to travel regularly between Chicago and the Polish port of Gdynia ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)). At the dedication ceremony on 14 October 1973, Mayor [Richard J. Daley](/people/richard-j-daley/) credited the monument with symbolically linking Chicago and Poland, and pronounced 14 October Nicolaus Copernicus Day ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)). The Foundation raised $150,000 for the replica ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)), a community-funded sum — not a city commission — that put a 100,000-pound bronze on Chicago's lakefront as a permanent Polish-American landmark.

## The astronomer, and why Chicago claimed him

Copernicus was born on 19 February 1473 in Torun, in what is now Poland, and studied liberal arts including astronomy at the University of Krakow, then at the University of Bologna, medicine at the University of Padua, and canon law at the University of Ferrara, where he took his doctorate in 1503 ([Clio](https://theclio.com/entry/129659)). Shortly before his death in 1543 he published *De revolutionibus orbium coelestium*, proposing that the Earth orbits the Sun with the other planets — a claim that contradicted the cosmology of Aristotle and the astronomical models of Ptolemy ([Adler Planetarium](https://www.adlerplanetarium.org/blog/copernicus-the-astronomer-the-city/)). His theory gained wide acceptance through the work of Galileo, Johannes Kepler and Isaac Newton, and with Newtonian physics from the late 17th century ([Adler Planetarium](https://www.adlerplanetarium.org/blog/copernicus-the-astronomer-the-city/)). For Chicago's Polish community, the statue is more than a science monument: the Adler notes that the bronze Copernicus seems to present his ideas keenly to the city, holding his dividers and armillary sphere — the sphere itself a symbol of the old geocentric universe he displaced ([Adler Planetarium](https://www.adlerplanetarium.org/blog/copernicus-the-astronomer-the-city/)).

## FAQ

### Where is the Nicolaus Copernicus Monument in Chicago?

It stands on Solidarity Drive in front of the Adler Planetarium, on Chicago's Museum Campus in Northerly Island Park ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)). The seated bronze figure faces the Chicago skyline, greeting visitors as they walk up to the planetarium ([Adler Planetarium](https://www.adlerplanetarium.org/blog/copernicus-the-astronomer-the-city/)).

### Who made the Chicago statue?

Polish sculptor Bronislaw Koniuszy recast it from Bertel Thorvaldsen's original plaster model for the Warsaw monument ([Adler Planetarium](https://www.adlerplanetarium.org/blog/copernicus-the-astronomer-the-city/)). Koniuszy had served in the Polish Underground Army before graduating from the Academy of Fine Arts in Warsaw in 1953 ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)).

### What happened to the original Warsaw monument?

It was damaged during the destruction of Warsaw in World War II, and the Nazis planned to melt it down ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)). Poles recovered the sculpture, and after full repairs and conservation it was rededicated in 1949 ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)).

### Why was the Chicago monument dedicated in 1973?

The Adler Planetarium erected it to mark the 500th anniversary of the birth of Nicolaus Copernicus (1473–1543), the father of modern astronomy ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)). Mayor Richard J. Daley attended the 14 October 1973 dedication and proclaimed the date Nicolaus Copernicus Day ([Clio](https://theclio.com/entry/129659)).
