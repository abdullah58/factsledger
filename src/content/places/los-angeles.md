Los Angeles is the second-largest city in the United States, a municipality of 3,898,747 people counted at the 2020 Census, spread over 469 square miles of Southern California ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/glendalecitycalifornia,losangelescitycalifornia,CA/POP010220)). It is the permanent home of the American film industry — host of the Academy Awards, center of the union-drawn 30-mile studio zone — and, less famously, the busiest container port in the country ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/port-los-angeles-sets-september-volume-record-executive-director-says-2026-10-09/)).

## Overview

This profile is about the **City of Los Angeles, California** — not Los Angeles County, the 10-million-person jurisdiction that contains it, and not the Los Ángeles in Chile or same-named places. The city began September 4, 1781, when 44 settlers — 14 families of Native American, African, and European heritage from northern Mexico — founded El Pueblo de Nuestra Señora la Reina de los Ángeles de Porciúncula under Governor Felipe de Neve ([Down To Earth](http://downtoearth.org.in/governance/the-town-of-the-virgin-how-los-angeles-began-as-a-pueblo-in-colonial-spanish-america)). The pueblo was laid out under Spain's Law of the Indies atop existing Tongva villages, as a square measuring four square leagues ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/shows/lost-la/los-angeles-original-borders)). It grew under the flags of Spain, Mexico, and the United States into the nation's second-largest city ([Down To Earth](http://downtoearth.org.in/governance/the-town-of-the-virgin-how-los-angeles-began-as-a-pueblo-in-colonial-spanish-america)). The 2020 Census counted 3,898,747 city residents (up from 3,792,621 in 2010) and 10,014,009 in the county ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/losangelescountycalifornia/PST040221)), the country's most populous ([123ru.net](https://m.123ru.net/english/292685284/)). Citywide, 47.2% are Hispanic or Latino, 35.7% foreign-born, and 56.4% speak a language other than English at home ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/glendalecitycalifornia,losangelescitycalifornia,CA/POP010220)).

## The thirty-mile map: Hollywood is a labor contract, not just a place

Hollywood is a district of Los Angeles, not a city — incorporated in 1903, annexed to Los Angeles in 1910 after running short of water, a neighborhood ever since ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/shows/lost-la/studio-labor-and-the-origins-of-hollywoods-thirty-mile-zone-or-tmz)). The film industry draws its own map over the city: the "thirty-mile zone," a 30-mile radius centered on Beverly Boulevard and La Cienega Boulevard — the former headquarters of the Association of Motion Picture and Television Producers ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/shows/lost-la/studio-labor-and-the-origins-of-hollywoods-thirty-mile-zone-or-tmz)). Informal since 1917, it was formalized as a six-mile circle in a 1934 agreement and expanded to 30 miles in 1970 to reclaim "runaway" productions ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/shows/lost-la/studio-labor-and-the-origins-of-hollywoods-thirty-mile-zone-or-tmz)). Inside the circle, crews transport themselves; outside it, studios owe transport, meals, and extra compensation — and in 1973 the National Labor Relations Board ruled the expansion an unfair labor practice against the studio drivers, never consulted on the change ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/shows/lost-la/studio-labor-and-the-origins-of-hollywoods-thirty-mile-zone-or-tmz)). Powdermaker called Hollywood "a state of mind"; the zone gives it a payroll geography — and its initials named TMZ ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/shows/lost-la/studio-labor-and-the-origins-of-hollywoods-thirty-mile-zone-or-tmz)).

## The Oscars' mall-theater era ends in 2028

The Academy Awards have been presented at the Dolby Theatre since 2002 — a venue purpose-built for the telecast, sitting inside the Hollywood & Highland (now Ovation Hollywood) shopping complex ([Smithsonian magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smartnews-arts-culture/oscars-are-held-mall-180962226/)). Each year a 250-person labor force spends two weeks dressing the mall, and lease agreements require every store to close on Oscar Sunday ([Smithsonian magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smartnews-arts-culture/oscars-are-held-mall-180962226/)). First the Kodak Theatre, it was renamed after Kodak's 2012 bankruptcy ([Smithsonian magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smartnews-arts-culture/oscars-are-held-mall-180962226/)). That era is ending: in March 2026 the Academy announced the ceremony moves to the Peacock Theater at L.A. Live downtown in 2029 under a ten-year AEG deal, with the telecast shifting from ABC to YouTube — the Dolby stays home through 2028, the 100th Oscars ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/26/entertainment/oscars-new-venue)). The city's Oscar gravity predates the move: in October 2017, Turkey's Oscar entry [Ayla: The Daughter of War](/creative-works/ayla-the-daughter-of-war/) — the story of [Süleyman Dilbirliği](/people/suleyman-dilbirligi/) — headlined the first Turkish Film Festival in Los Angeles as its Academy campaign passed through the city ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)).

## The working port, the third Olympics, and the 2025 fires

Under the film geography sits a working trade city. The Port of Los Angeles is the busiest in the US for containers ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/port-los-angeles-sets-september-volume-record-executive-director-says-2026-10-09/)). In September 2026 it handled 1.04 million TEUs — a record for the month ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/port-los-angeles-sets-september-volume-record-executive-director-says-2026-10-09/)); the implication is that drought restrictions on the Panama Canal are actively rerouting Asia–US trade through Los Angeles' West Coast docks. Seroka noted it moved more cargo than during the COVID-19 surge, without the hundred-ship backlog ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/port-los-angeles-sets-september-volume-record-executive-director-says-2026-10-09/)). Sport adds a second civic geography: Los Angeles hosted the Summer Olympics in 1932 and 1984, and the 2028 Games will make it the third city ever to host three Olympics, after London and Paris, with the Memorial Coliseum expected back as a stadium ([NBC Sports](https://olympics.nbcsports.com/2017/07/31/los-angeles-2028-olympics-la2028/)). The city's current status carries a scar: the Palisades and Eaton fires that began January 7, 2025 — centered on Pacific Palisades and Altadena — killed at least 28 people and destroyed more than 16,000 structures ([UCLA](https://lewis.ucla.edu/research/the-palisades-and-eaton-fires-neighborhood-data-and-potential-housing-market-effects/)).

## FAQ

**Is Hollywood a separate city?** No — Hollywood is a district, not a city. It was incorporated as an independent municipality in 1903 but, short of water, annexed itself to Los Angeles in 1910 and remains a neighborhood whose boundaries are drawn on official city maps ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/shows/lost-la/studio-labor-and-the-origins-of-hollywoods-thirty-mile-zone-or-tmz)).

**Where are the Academy Awards held?** At the Dolby Theatre in Hollywood since 2002; it stays home through the 100th Oscars in 2028. In 2029 the ceremony moves to the Peacock Theater at L.A. Live in downtown Los Angeles, with the telecast shifting from ABC to YouTube under a ten-year AEG deal ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/03/26/entertainment/oscars-new-venue)).

**What is the Thirty-Mile Zone?** A 30-mile-radius "studio zone" centered on Beverly Boulevard and La Cienega Boulevard in Los Angeles, defined by union contracts. It sets pay scales, per-diem rates, and transport obligations for film and TV crews — shooting outside it costs productions extra ([PBS SoCal](https://www.pbssocal.org/shows/lost-la/studio-labor-and-the-origins-of-hollywoods-thirty-mile-zone-or-tmz)).

**How big is Los Angeles compared to Los Angeles County?** The city counted 3,898,747 residents on 469 square miles in 2020; the county counted 10,014,009 on 4,059 square miles, making it the most populous county in the country ([123ru.net](https://m.123ru.net/english/292685284/)). The city is its seat but holds less than 40% of the county's people ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/glendalecitycalifornia,losangelescitycalifornia,CA/POP010220); [U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/losangelescountycalifornia/PST040221)).

**When does Los Angeles next host the Olympics?** In 2028, when it becomes the third city ever to host three Olympic Games, after London and Paris. It previously hosted the Summer Games in 1932 and 1984, with the Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum expected to return as a stadium ([NBC Sports](https://olympics.nbcsports.com/2017/07/31/los-angeles-2028-olympics-la2028/)).
