Israel is 27 days from a national election its own coalition history has barely seen, and it is presiding over a "ceasefire" that keeps killing people. On September 30, 2026, Israeli strikes killed at least five to seven Palestinians in Gaza — a drone attack on a passenger van in Tal al-Hawa, a strike on a residential apartment near al-Shifa Hospital — as the territory's health officials put the death toll since the October 2025 ceasefire began at at least 1,431 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/israeli-strikes-kill-five-people-gaza-medics-say-2026-09-30/); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/30/israeli-attacks-on-gaza-kill-several-as-ceasefire-violations-continue)). The State of Israel — 22,072 square kilometres, an estimated 9,141,000 people, its proclaimed capital Jerusalem — holds national elections on October 27 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Israel); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/12/israel-to-hold-national-elections-on-october-27-parliament-says?traffic_source=rss)).

## The machinery of the ceasefire

The daily violation wires almost never explain what the October 2025 ceasefire was supposed to build. On September 29, 2025, President Donald Trump announced a 20-point plan to end the Gaza war — hostage release, a partial Israeli withdrawal, a technocratic government, an international stabilization force, and Hamas's disarmament — and its first phase entered into force on October 10, 2025 ([Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/board-of-peace-envoy-says-gaza-governing-body-dissolution-highlights-need-to-finalize-roadmap-talks/3987407)). The plan's start promised humanitarian aid, fuel, shelter supplies, and reopened border crossings before any move to the next phase ([Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/board-of-peace-envoy-says-gaza-governing-body-dissolution-highlights-need-to-finalize-roadmap-talks/3987407)). Oversight sits with the U.S.-backed [Board of Peace](/organizations/board-of-peace/), chaired by Trump, alongside a Gaza Executive Council and the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza — the NCAG ([Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/board-of-peace-envoy-says-gaza-governing-body-dissolution-highlights-need-to-finalize-roadmap-talks/3987407)).

The NCAG is the technocratic heart of the design: a non-political body of Palestinian national figures meant to manage Gaza's day-to-day civil affairs. It has operated from Cairo since mid-January 2026 and has not yet begun carrying out its duties from inside the [Gaza Strip](/places/gaza-strip/) ([Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/board-of-peace-envoy-says-gaza-governing-body-dissolution-highlights-need-to-finalize-roadmap-talks/3987407)). On July 6, 2026, Gaza's government dissolved its Emergency Committee and its acting chairman resigned, saying the move was meant to facilitate the transfer of governance to the NCAG under a roadmap agreed by Palestinian factions; Israel's public broadcaster KAN, citing an unnamed Israeli official, dismissed the move as "deception with no practical significance" ([Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/board-of-peace-envoy-says-gaza-governing-body-dissolution-highlights-need-to-finalize-roadmap-talks/3987407)).

The implementation blueprint is a 15-point roadmap that the board's Gaza envoy, Nickolay Mladenov — a former UN special coordinator for the Middle East peace process — unveiled on May 21, 2026. It sets out mechanisms for reconstruction, the decommissioning of weapons, the withdrawal of Israeli forces, the deployment of an international stabilization force, and the rebuilding of Gaza's police service ([Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/board-of-peace-envoy-says-gaza-governing-body-dissolution-highlights-need-to-finalize-roadmap-talks/3987407)).

## Verification, not trust — and a prime minister's rejection

Mladenov's August 2026 Channel 12 interview laid out the roadmap's logic with unusual precision. "Nobody is required to do anything – last of all Israel – before we actually have verified steps on the ground," he said: verified decommissioning of weapons — taken away from the factions, stored, and ultimately rendered unusable — is the trigger, and then "Israel then withdraws from Gaza" ([The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/board-of-peace-envoy-says-full-execution-of-gaza-plan-only-guarantee-oct-7-wont-recur/)). The Yellow Line, which divided Gaza roughly evenly between Israeli and Hamas control under the October 2025 ceasefire, "will move further back once there is decommissioning in Gaza": each geographical sector's weapons must be certified as taken over and stored by the NCAG and the Board of Peace, and only then is Israel required to move back progressively, "step by step, in stages, ultimately to the [border] fence in Gaza" ([The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/board-of-peace-envoy-says-full-execution-of-gaza-plan-only-guarantee-oct-7-wont-recur/)). Israel has since taken territory beyond the original Yellow Line and now controls more than 60 percent of Gaza; the plan does not require an IDF withdrawal from the line at this stage, only that troops not push further in and not shoot across "unless they are a real threat," and it leaves Israel unrestricted in responding to "imminent" threats ([The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/board-of-peace-envoy-says-full-execution-of-gaza-plan-only-guarantee-oct-7-wont-recur/)).

The design is deliberately built on verification rather than trust between the parties, with a verification mechanism and a provision that the whole process stops if commitments are not honored ([The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/board-of-peace-envoy-says-full-execution-of-gaza-plan-only-guarantee-oct-7-wont-recur/)). Mladenov credited the mediators — "if it wasn't for Egypt and for the other mediators, Turkey and Qatar, we would have not been able to get to a position in which Hamas would agree to the roadmap" — even as Israeli concern grew over Turkey and Qatar joining the planned International Stabilization Force ([The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/board-of-peace-envoy-says-full-execution-of-gaza-plan-only-guarantee-oct-7-wont-recur/)). He also conceded the scale: destroying all of Hamas's tunnel network would take "more than a decade," though the board could accelerate it ([The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/board-of-peace-envoy-says-full-execution-of-gaza-plan-only-guarantee-oct-7-wont-recur/)).

Days after Hamas approved the roadmap, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu announced he categorically rejected the 15-point plan and halted a small IDF pullout from three areas near Rafah in southern Gaza — a withdrawal he had personally approved in consultations with the IDF chief of staff and the United States ([The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/board-of-peace-envoy-says-full-execution-of-gaza-plan-only-guarantee-oct-7-wont-recur/)). The IDF and the security establishment learned of the reversal from the media — Channel 12 called it "a major shock" — and Netanyahu told the cabinet Israel would not withdraw from any part of Gaza unless or until Hamas gives up all its weapons ([The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/board-of-peace-envoy-says-full-execution-of-gaza-plan-only-guarantee-oct-7-wont-recur/)). The White House brushed the rejection aside as campaign rhetoric ahead of the October 27 election: "We are not troubled by those comments from Netanyahu. We understand his political needs [in this election period]. We have no problem with that, as long as he continues to do what we ask — especially as regards reining in the strikes in Gaza" ([The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/board-of-peace-envoy-says-full-execution-of-gaza-plan-only-guarantee-oct-7-wont-recur/)). U.S. officials noted Israel had in fact halted strikes in Gaza for the previous five days, and that Netanyahu had told Trump's envoy Jared Kushner he was prepared to give the plan a chance ([The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/board-of-peace-envoy-says-full-execution-of-gaza-plan-only-guarantee-oct-7-wont-recur/)). Mohammad Dahlan, the former Fatah official and close adviser to the UAE president who has served as a key Palestinian go-between, called the rejection "challenge, disdain, and deception toward the American administration and President Donald Trump's team," saying mediators "now recognize Netanyahu as the obstacle to achieving security and stability" ([The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/board-of-peace-envoy-says-full-execution-of-gaza-plan-only-guarantee-oct-7-wont-recur/)).

## The September state of the ceasefire

On September 30, the violence was back in the daily wire. Six people were killed in an Israeli drone attack on a passenger van in Tal al-Hawa in southwest Gaza City, according to witnesses and medical sources; an earlier strike on a residential apartment near al-Shifa Hospital killed at least one woman; four Palestinians, including two children, were wounded when a drone hit a shelter for displaced people in southern Gaza; two more were seriously wounded southeast of Deir el-Balah; and tank fire was reported in southern Khan Younis ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/30/israeli-attacks-on-gaza-kill-several-as-ceasefire-violations-continue)). The Palestine Red Crescent Society said its crews took 13 wounded people to al-Quds Hospital ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/30/israeli-attacks-on-gaza-kill-several-as-ceasefire-violations-continue)). Reuters' count was five killed in two attacks; the Israeli military did not immediately comment on either reported strike ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/israeli-strikes-kill-five-people-gaza-medics-say-2026-09-30/)).

Israel says [Hamas](/organizations/hamas/) has been rearming and launching attacks in violation of the October ceasefire and has stepped up strikes in recent weeks; Hamas denies trying to carry out attacks or rebuild its arsenal and accuses Israel of undermining the Gaza peace deal ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/israeli-strikes-kill-five-people-gaza-medics-say-2026-09-30/)). The ceasefire calls for eventual reconstruction of the territory, but little progress has been made: negotiators have failed to agree terms for Hamas to disarm and for Israel to withdraw its troops ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/israeli-strikes-kill-five-people-gaza-medics-say-2026-09-30/)).

The running totals are stark. At least 1,431 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire began, with 5,026 wounded over the same period; Israel says four of its soldiers have been killed ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/30/israeli-attacks-on-gaza-kill-several-as-ceasefire-violations-continue); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/israeli-strikes-kill-five-people-gaza-medics-say-2026-09-30/)). Since October 2023, at least 74,032 people have been killed and 175,142 wounded, according to health officials ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/30/israeli-attacks-on-gaza-kill-several-as-ceasefire-violations-continue)). In August, the United Nations Satellite Centre found that the number of destroyed structures across Gaza had increased by 9 percent since the "ceasefire" took effect; about 82 percent of structures in Gaza — roughly 200,000 buildings — have been damaged, and about two-thirds of those destroyed since the start of the war ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/30/israeli-attacks-on-gaza-kill-several-as-ceasefire-violations-continue)).

## The Abraham Accords at six

A second diplomatic thread runs parallel to Gaza. On September 15, 2026, Israel, the UAE, and Bahrain marked six years since the Abraham Accords were signed at the White House on September 15, 2020 ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/history/article-908689)). Since then, Morocco, Kazakhstan, Sudan, and Somaliland have joined; the addition of Saudi Arabia — the accords' original roadmap objective — was suspended indefinitely after the Hamas-led October 7 attacks and the Gaza war ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/history/article-908689)). The current objectives, the Post reports, are the Saudis, a peace treaty with Lebanon, and diplomatic relations with the new Syrian government ruled by Ahmed al-Sharaa ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/history/article-908689)). Axios reported on September 5 that the U.S. has begun developing a post-war strategy for containing Iran and normalizing relations between Israel and U.S. allies, still in its early stages ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/history/article-908689)).

The anniversary brought a concrete upgrade. On September 16, 2026, Israel and Morocco agreed to upgrade their diplomatic missions to full embassies and exchange ambassadors, in a trilateral meeting in New York between U.S. Ambassador to the UN Mike Waltz, Moroccan Foreign Minister Nasser Bourita, and Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar — one day after the sixth anniversary ([Fox News](https://foxnews.com/world/us-backed-israel-morocco-deal-deepens-abraham-accords-amid-growing-regional-threats)). Morocco's normalization had come through a separate U.S.-Morocco-Israel Joint Declaration signed in Rabat on December 22, 2020, committing the countries to full diplomatic relations, direct flights, and expanded economic cooperation ([Fox News](https://foxnews.com/world/us-backed-israel-morocco-deal-deepens-abraham-accords-amid-growing-regional-threats)). The September 16 joint statement advanced those commitments: full embassies and ambassador exchange, direct flights by Israeli and Moroccan airlines before the end of 2026, investment-protection and double-taxation agreements, and cooperation across trade, finance, technology, tourism, water, agriculture, food security, energy, telecommunications, and regional security ([Fox News](https://foxnews.com/world/us-backed-israel-morocco-deal-deepens-abraham-accords-amid-growing-regional-threats)). It also reaffirmed U.S. and Israeli recognition of Moroccan sovereignty over Western Sahara and support for Morocco's autonomy proposal ([Fox News](https://foxnews.com/world/us-backed-israel-morocco-deal-deepens-abraham-accords-amid-growing-regional-threats)).

Morocco has used the relationship as a working channel, not just a ceremony: during the Gaza war it secured a land route for 40 tons of humanitarian assistance into northern Gaza in March 2024, and it participates in Gaza stabilization efforts ([Fox News](https://foxnews.com/world/us-backed-israel-morocco-deal-deepens-abraham-accords-amid-growing-regional-threats)). "We don't agree with Israel 100%. Each one may have its policy," Bourita said, arguing that Morocco uses the "Abraham Accords spirit" to "promote peace" and "promote stability in the region" ([Fox News](https://foxnews.com/world/us-backed-israel-morocco-deal-deepens-abraham-accords-amid-growing-regional-threats)).

## The October 27 election

The [government of Israel](/organizations/government-of-israel/) faces voters on October 27, 2026 — the latest date legally allowed — in an election widely viewed as a referendum on Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's leadership ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/12/israel-to-hold-national-elections-on-october-27-parliament-says?traffic_source=rss)). The Knesset is set to hold the final session of its current term on July 17, making Netanyahu's far-right coalition the first in half a century to complete a full four-year term ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/12/israel-to-hold-national-elections-on-october-27-parliament-says?traffic_source=rss)). Netanyahu, 76 and Israel's longest-serving prime minister, confirmed in June that he will run again ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/12/israel-to-hold-national-elections-on-october-27-parliament-says?traffic_source=rss)).

His main rival has emerged from inside his own wartime leadership: former army chief of staff Gadi Eisenkot, whose Yashar party is narrowly ahead of Netanyahu's Likud in a Channel 13 poll ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/12/israel-to-hold-national-elections-on-october-27-parliament-says?traffic_source=rss)). Eisenkot served in Netanyahu's war cabinet and resigned in June 2024, saying the government "had completely failed" to achieve any of its objectives in Gaza; his son was killed in December 2023 at the start of Israel's war on Gaza ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/12/israel-to-hold-national-elections-on-october-27-parliament-says?traffic_source=rss)). The government's pre-recess legislative push included judiciary reform and a bill to suspend the detention of ultra-Orthodox Jews who evade mandatory military service ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/12/israel-to-hold-national-elections-on-october-27-parliament-says?traffic_source=rss)).

The case against Netanyahu runs on two tracks. Critics blame him for the security failures of October 7, 2023 — the deadliest attack in Israel's history, when armed groups from Gaza breached advanced border defenses and took 251 people hostage — and for a war that, nearly three years in, has not destroyed Hamas as promised ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/12/israel-to-hold-national-elections-on-october-27-parliament-says?traffic_source=rss)). He is also on trial for corruption and could face up to 10 years in prison if convicted, with critics accusing him of using the premiership to evade justice ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/12/israel-to-hold-national-elections-on-october-27-parliament-says?traffic_source=rss)). ## Land, people, and government

Israel sits at the eastern end of the Mediterranean Sea, bounded by Lebanon to the north, Syria to the northeast, Jordan to the east and southeast, Egypt to the southwest, and the Mediterranean to the west ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Israel)). It is a small country — about 290 miles north-to-south and 85 miles at its widest — with four geographic regions: the Mediterranean coastal plain, the northern and central hill regions, the Great Rift Valley, and the Negev desert that covers the sparsely populated south ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Israel)). Jerusalem is the seat of government and the proclaimed capital, "although the latter status has not received wide international recognition" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Israel)).

The population is estimated at 9,141,000 in 2026 (excluding Israelis in the West Bank and Gaza Strip), spread over 22,072 square kilometres (also excluding the West Bank and Gaza) ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Israel)). Israel is a multiparty republic with one legislative house, the 120-seat Knesset; Hebrew is the official language with Arabic holding special status; there is no official religion; and the currency is the new Israeli shekel ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Israel)). It is a wealthy, long-lived, highly urbanized society: life expectancy of 81.1 years for men and 85.4 for women (2024), GNI per capita of $52,910 (2024), and 92.9 percent urban population ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Israel)). Its diversity carries tensions — between Ashkenazim and Sephardim, over religious influence, and around the rights and integration of Arab citizens and other minority groups ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Israel)).

## A compact history

The region that now falls within Israel's borders passed through Roman and Byzantine rule, the Islamic caliphate from the 7th century, the Crusades, and Ottoman rule until World War I, when it was placed under British mandate from the League of Nations ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Israel)). The 1917 Balfour Declaration committed Britain to facilitating a Jewish homeland in Palestine; Jewish immigration grew dramatically amid persecution and the Holocaust, tensions with Palestinian Arabs flared, and the UN plan to partition Palestine into Jewish and Arab sectors was followed by Israel's declaration of statehood on May 14, 1948 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Israel)). Israel fought a series of wars against neighboring Arab states over the next 35 years; the 1967 Six-Day War left it in control of the Sinai Peninsula, the West Bank, the Gaza Strip, the Golan Heights, and East Jerusalem; and in the final quarter of the 20th century it concluded peace treaties with several neighboring Arab states ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Israel)). The Oslo Accords aimed to grant Palestinians self-rule in the Gaza Strip and the West Bank toward a negotiated peace ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Israel)).
