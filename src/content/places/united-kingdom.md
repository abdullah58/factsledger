## Overview

The United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland is a constitutional monarchy of about 69.5 million people off the northwestern coast of mainland Europe, made up of four nations — [England](/places/england/), Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland — and governed from London, where a sovereign Parliament sits alongside the monarch, King Charles III ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/United-Kingdom); [Royal Central](https://royalcentral.co.uk/uk/king/king-charles-iii-is-proclaimed-in-ancient-ceremony-filled-with-emotion-and-history-180634/)). In 2026 the head of government is Prime Minister Andy Burnham, the former mayor of Manchester, who took the helm that summer after Keir Starmer resigned — making Burnham the country's seventh prime minister in a decade ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/29/uk/andy-burnham-what-next-labour-conference-intl?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)).

The UK is a unitary state with an unusual shape: one sovereign Parliament at Westminster, but separate law-making bodies in Edinburgh, Cardiff and Belfast for devolved matters such as health and education ([UK Cabinet Office](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/691afaec84a267da57d705ab/Devolution_factsheet.pdf)). Its population stood at an estimated 69,483,900 in mid-2025, with growth slowing to its weakest rate since mid-2020 as immigration fell sharply ([Office for National Statistics](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/populationandmigration/populationestimates/bulletins/annualmidyearpopulationestimates/mid2025)).

## A union built over centuries

The United Kingdom was assembled piece by piece, not drawn on a map. Wales was formally united with England by the Acts of Union of 1536 and 1542; Scotland, ruled from London since 1603, joined England and Wales in 1707 to form the Kingdom of Great Britain; Ireland was united with Great Britain through the Act of Union of 1800; and after the Irish republic gained independence in 1922, six of Ulster's nine counties remained in the UK as Northern Ireland ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/United-Kingdom)). The adjective "British" came into use with the 1707 union, and London — on the tidal River Thames in southeastern England — became the seat of the whole arrangement ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/United-Kingdom)).

The union has been tested repeatedly. In September 2014, Scotland voted against independence by 55.4% to around 45% on a turnout above 84%, with Glasgow and Dundee voting Yes and Edinburgh voting decisively No; the first minister, Alex Salmond, accepted the verdict the next morning ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/world/scots-spurn-independence-vote-to-stay-within-uk/article64244237.ece)). In Northern Ireland, power-sharing collapsed for two years before the devolved assembly was restored on 3 February 2024, when Sinn Féin's Michelle O'Neill became the first nationalist First Minister, with Emma Little-Pengelly of the DUP as her deputy ([The Straits Times](https://straitstimes.com/world/europe/o-neill-to-make-history-as-n-ireland-s-first-nationalist-leader)). As of late 2026, Scotland's first minister John Swinney has publicly predicted that Burnham will be "the last prime minister of the United Kingdom" — a claim Burnham says he is "utterly determined" to prove wrong ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/29/uk/andy-burnham-what-next-labour-conference-intl?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)).

## How the UK is governed

The UK is a constitutional monarchy with parliamentary democracy: the head of state is the reigning monarch — Charles III, proclaimed at St James's Palace on 10 September 2022 after the death of his mother, Queen Elizabeth II, two days earlier — while the head of government is the prime minister, who leads the majority party in the House of Commons ([Royal Central](https://royalcentral.co.uk/uk/king/king-charles-iii-is-proclaimed-in-ancient-ceremony-filled-with-emotion-and-history-180634/); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/United-Kingdom)). Parliament has two legislative houses: the 774-member House of Lords and the 650-member House of Commons ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/United-Kingdom)). Westminster is sovereign — it can make laws on any issue — but by convention it does not normally legislate in devolved areas without the consent of the relevant devolved parliament ([UK Cabinet Office](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/691afaec84a267da57d705ab/Devolution_factsheet.pdf)).

Devolution, in its current form, dates to the late 1990s: voters chose to create a Scottish Parliament and the Senedd Cymru in 1997, and devolution in Northern Ireland was a key element of the Belfast (Good Friday) Agreement, backed by a 1998 referendum ([UK Cabinet Office](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/691afaec84a267da57d705ab/Devolution_factsheet.pdf)). England has no separate parliament of its own; English devolution — mayors and combined authorities — involves no law-making or tax-raising powers ([UK Cabinet Office](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/691afaec84a267da57d705ab/Devolution_factsheet.pdf)). Intergovernmental work runs through the Council of the Nations and Regions, bringing the Prime Minister together with the First Ministers of Scotland and Wales, Northern Ireland's First and deputy First Ministers, and English mayors ([UK Cabinet Office](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/691afaec84a267da57d705ab/Devolution_factsheet.pdf)).

The UK joined the European Union in 1973; in June 2016, 52% of voters chose to leave in a referendum, and after years of negotiation the UK withdrew on 31 January 2020 — the first country ever to leave the EU ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/United-Kingdom)). ## The UK in 2026: people, money and the screen

The UK's population reached an estimated 69,483,900 in mid-2025, up 227,700 (+0.33%) on the year before — the lowest rate of increase since mid-2020, and a population that has nonetheless grown every year since mid-1982 ([Office for National Statistics](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/populationandmigration/populationestimates/bulletins/annualmidyearpopulationestimates/mid2025)). Net international migration collapsed to 222,900 from 651,700 the previous year, while births fell to 652,400 — the lowest number in at least 43 years — against 647,500 deaths ([Office for National Statistics](https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/populationandmigration/populationestimates/bulletins/annualmidyearpopulationestimates/mid2025)). In other words, immigration still drove almost all of the UK's growth, but at roughly a third of the previous year's level — the 2026 immigration debate is playing out against a demographic slowdown, not a surge.

The economy remains large — 2024 gross national income was $3.42 trillion, or $49,470 per capita — and London ranks among the world's leading commercial, financial and cultural centres, with Birmingham, Manchester and Liverpool as England's other major cities ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/United-Kingdom)). The UK is a member of NATO and retains links with parts of its former empire through the Commonwealth; its greatest post-war export, Britannica argues, has been cultural — literature, theatre, film, television and popular music — and above all the English language itself ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/United-Kingdom)). That cultural pipeline ran in reverse in April 2026, when Antoine Fuqua's [Michael](/creative-works/michael-2026-film/) — starring Jaafar Jackson — opened in the UK on 22 April, two days before its US release ([NME](https://www.nme.com/news/film/michael-jackson-biopic-reshoots-cost-15million-removed-all-mention-of-child-abuse-allegations-3939096)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the UK the same as Great Britain?**
No. Great Britain is the island containing England, Scotland and Wales; the United Kingdom adds Northern Ireland, on the island of Ireland. The name "Britain" is sometimes used loosely for the whole UK, and the full name is the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland.

**What are the four countries of the UK?**
England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland each have their own devolved legislature — the Scottish Parliament, the Senedd Cymru and the Northern Ireland Assembly — while England is governed directly from Westminster. Each of the four parts maintains its own customs, traditions and cuisine.

**Who is the UK's prime minister in 2026?**
Andy Burnham, the former mayor of Manchester, who became prime minister in summer 2026 after Keir Starmer resigned as Labour leader and PM. He is the UK's seventh prime minister in a decade, backed into office by 379 of Labour's 403 MPs.

**When did the UK leave the EU?**
After a June 2016 referendum in which 52% voted to leave, the UK withdrew from the European Union on 31 January 2020 — the first country ever to do so. In 2026, Prime Minister Burnham says Brexit has "done more harm than good" and wants Britain to consider closer ties.
