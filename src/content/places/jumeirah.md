## Overview

Jumeirah is the historic coastal district of [Dubai](/places/dubai/) on the Arabian Gulf shore, a low-rise residential area whose villas hide a 1,000-year-old Abbasid trading port, whose mosque is one of the UAE's most recognisable landmarks, and whose Union House is where the [United Arab Emirates](/places/united-arab-emirates/) was formed on December 2, 1971 ([Gulf News](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Jumeirah+Mosque+a+key+FEATURE+of+old+dubai.-a0263816380); [XPRESS](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Jumeirah%3a+Digging+up+the+past.-a0180786256)). It is also, since March 30, 2026, one of the two Dubai districts where [Baidu](/organizations/baidu/)'s [Apollo Go](/organizations/apollo-go/) driverless taxis carry paying passengers ([What's On](https://whatson.ae/2026/03/driverless-taxis-dubai-how-to-book/)).

This profile is about the mainland coastal district — not Palm Jumeirah, the artificial island, nor the Jumeirah Beach Residence towers, nor the Jumeirah hotel group, which share the name but are separate developments. The district's own name is disputed: it may be a miniaturised form of *jumra*, meaning "small ember", or it may mean "stone's throw" or "pebble", and no other place in the Arab world carries the name ([XPRESS](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Jumeirah%3a+Digging+up+the+past.-a0180786256)).

Long before the restaurants, cosmetic-surgery centres, and fashion boutiques sprang up along Jumeirah Beach Road, the area was a serene residential neighbourhood whose crowd-pulling monuments in the 1980s were the Chicago Beach Hotel and Jumeirah Mosque ([Gulf News](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Jumeirah+Mosque+a+key+FEATURE+of+old+dubai.-a0263816380)). The hotel was demolished and renamed Jumeirah Beach Hotel in 1997; the mosque remains, meticulously maintained, in Jumeirah 1 ([Gulf News](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Jumeirah+Mosque+a+key+FEATURE+of+old+dubai.-a0263816380)).

## A trading port under the villas

The fact most Jumeirah profiles miss is what lies beneath it. Nestled between the villas of Jumeirah 2 is a fenced-off 80,000-square-metre plot holding the relics of a 1,000-year-old trading port ([XPRESS](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Jumeirah%3a+Digging+up+the+past.-a0180786256)). The Jumeirah Archaeological Site belongs to the Abbasid era, between the 9th and 11th centuries, when the settlement served as a caravan stop along a trading route connecting Iraq and Oman to India and [China](/places/china/) ([Dubai Media Office](https://mediaoffice.ae/news/2020/Jan/09-01/Mohammed-bin-Rashid-visits-Jumeirah-Archaeological-Site)). It was discovered in 1969 and first explored by a team from the American University of Beirut; an Iraqi archaeological team continued the work in 1974, when the first buildings and artefacts were found, and the Department of Tourism and Commerce Marketing has excavated there since 1993 ([XPRESS](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Jumeirah%3a+Digging+up+the+past.-a0180786256)).

Seven structures have been excavated and identified: a souq, a workshop that was later turned into a mosque, a caravanserai or inn, and a large residential building believed to be the Ruler's House ([XPRESS](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Jumeirah%3a+Digging+up+the+past.-a0180786256)). The finds prove the trading-port reading: Abbasid-era currency, pottery from southern Iraq, and terracotta and stonecraft from China ([XPRESS](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Jumeirah%3a+Digging+up+the+past.-a0180786256)). The old city was large — archaeologists believe it spread from the beach to Al Safa Park and as far as the Iranian Hospital — and only nine of its structures survived Dubai's glass-and-metal development ([XPRESS](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Jumeirah%3a+Digging+up+the+past.-a0180786256)). Many of the artefacts are on exhibit at the Dubai Museum in Bur Dubai ([XPRESS](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Jumeirah%3a+Digging+up+the+past.-a0180786256)). In January 2020, Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum visited the site and toured its museum of pottery, coins, and tools, and the ancient homes, rest houses, mosque, and public facilities ([Dubai Media Office](https://mediaoffice.ae/news/2020/Jan/09-01/Mohammed-bin-Rashid-visits-Jumeirah-Archaeological-Site)).

## The mosque, Union House, and the driverless present

Jumeirah Mosque — "the two minaret mosque" — is featured on the Dh500 note, was built under the patronage of Sheikh Rashid bin Saeed Al Maktoum, and is one of only three mosques in the UAE open to non-Muslims, the others being the Al Farooq Omar Bin Al Khattab Mosque in Al Safa and the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque in [Abu Dhabi](/places/abu-dhabi/) ([Gulf News](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Jumeirah+Mosque+a+key+FEATURE+of+old+dubai.-a0263816380)). Its Fatimid-style architecture sets it apart from the neighbourhood's other mosques, and it can accommodate up to 1,300 people ([Gulf News](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Jumeirah+Mosque+a+key+FEATURE+of+old+dubai.-a0263816380)). For over a decade the Sheikh Mohammed Centre for Cultural Understanding has organised visits for non-Muslims, with tours four times a week lasting around 75 minutes ([Gulf News](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Jumeirah+Mosque+a+key+FEATURE+of+old+dubai.-a0263816380)).

Union House, also in the district, used to hold the office of Sheikh Rashid himself ([Gulf News](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Jumeirah+Mosque+a+key+FEATURE+of+old+dubai.-a0263816380)). On the afternoon of Thursday, December 2, 1971, Sheikh Zayed, Sheikh Rashid, and the rulers of Sharjah, Ajman, Fujairah, and Umm Al Quwain met in its circular building to sign the declaration forming the union of the Arab emirates and elected Sheikh Zayed president of the newly formed nation; Ras Al Khaimah joined in February 1972 ([Gulf News](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Jumeirah+Mosque+a+key+FEATURE+of+old+dubai.-a0263816380)). A 120-metre flagpole flies over Union House, marking the place where the UAE flag was first unveiled and raised to a 21-gun salute, although the gold flagpole itself was not constructed until 2001 ([Gulf News](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Jumeirah+Mosque+a+key+FEATURE+of+old+dubai.-a0263816380)).

The district's present is driverless. On March 30, 2026, Dubai's Roads and Transport Authority launched commercial driverless taxi operations in Jumeirah and Umm Suqeim, in partnership with Baidu's Apollo Go and WeRide, starting with 100 autonomous taxis ([What's On](https://whatson.ae/2026/03/driverless-taxis-dubai-how-to-book/)). Apollo Go rides are booked through the Apollo Go app with [Dubai Taxi Company](/organizations/dubai-taxi-company/), and since August 19, 2026, Apollo Go cars have also been available on [Uber](/organizations/uber/) ([Devo Dubai](https://devodubai.com/article/2026-10-26/dubai-robotaxis-where-you-can-ride-one-now-which-apps-and-what-it-costs)). The RTA set a fare of Dh5 per ride from July 15, 2026, and by August the service had a fleet of 144 vehicles, 48 in active operation, completing about 50 trips a day, with 7,613 passenger trips and 97 percent customer satisfaction since launch ([Devo Dubai](https://devodubai.com/article/2026-10-26/dubai-robotaxis-where-you-can-ride-one-now-which-apps-and-what-it-costs)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim a single settled date for Jumeirah Mosque: Gulf News says it has been maintained since its launch in 1975, while other references give an opening in November 1979 — the profile says "late 1970s" for that reason. It does not claim the Union House documents were signed in 1972: one line of the Gulf News article says so, but the article's own detailed account and the UAE Ministry of Foreign Affairs give December 2, 1971, with Ras Al Khaimah joining in February 1972. It does not claim a population or area figure for the district, because no verified figure was found in a tier1 or tier2 source. It does not claim the archaeological site is open to the public — it is fenced off, and its artefacts are displayed at the Dubai Museum. And it does not settle the etymology of "Jumeirah": the candidate meanings are reported, not chosen between.
