**Jharkhand** is a state in eastern India, formed on 15 November 2000 when 18 districts of southern Bihar were carved out under the Bihar Reorganisation Act, 2000, making it India's 28th state ([CaseMine](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5a979d984a93263ca60b70f0); [Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/ranchi-news/jkhand-foundation-day-birsa-memorial-park-universal-pension-scheme-launch-mark-day-101636989802528-amp.html)). Its capital is Ranchi; it is governed by an 81-member Legislative Assembly; Scheduled Tribes account for 26.21% of its population; and it holds around 40% of the country's mineral reserves ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/elections/jharkhand-assembly/jharkhand-assembly-election-battleground-ready-for-direct-contest-between-jmm-and-bjp/article68819182.ece); [The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/elections/assembly-elections/jharkhand/jharkhand-assembly-elections-how-tribals-and-women-will-shape-the-outcome/articleshow/114663154.cms); [IBEF](https://ibef.org/states/jharkhand)).

## Statehood: carved out of Bihar on 15 November 2000

Section 3 of the Bihar Reorganisation Act, 2000 states that "on and from the appointed day, there shall be formed a new State to be known as the State of Jharkhand" from 18 named districts of the existing State of Bihar — Bokaro, Chatra, Deogarh, Dhanbad, Dumka, Garhwa, Giridih, Godda, Gumla, Hazaribagh, Kodarma, Lohardaga, Pakur, Palamau, Ranchi, Sahebganj, and East and West Singhbhum — and Section 5 inserted it as entry 28 in the First Schedule of the Constitution ([CaseMine](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5a979d984a93263ca60b70f0)). The "appointed day" was fixed as 15 November 2000 by notification S.O. 829(E) of 14 September 2000, and the state has since grown from 18 districts to 24 ([CaseMine](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5a979d984a93263ca60b70f0); [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/elections/jharkhand-assembly/jharkhand-assembly-election-battleground-ready-for-direct-contest-between-jmm-and-bjp/article68819182.ece)). The date was deliberately chosen: Jharkhand was carved out of Bihar on the birth anniversary of 19th-century freedom fighter and tribal leader [Birsa Munda](/people/birsa-munda/), and Prime Minister Narendra Modi has credited the "strong will" of Atal Bihari Vajpayee for the state's creation ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/ranchi-news/jkhand-foundation-day-birsa-memorial-park-universal-pension-scheme-launch-mark-day-101636989802528-amp.html)).

## Government and representation

Jharkhand has a unicameral legislature. The Act fixed the Legislative Assembly at 81 seats — 243 went to Bihar and 81 to Jharkhand — and today those 81 constituencies span the state's 24 districts: 44 general, 28 reserved for Scheduled Tribes and 9 reserved for Scheduled Castes ([CaseMine](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5a979d984a93263ca60b70f0); [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/elections/jharkhand-assembly/jharkhand-assembly-election-battleground-ready-for-direct-contest-between-jmm-and-bjp/article68819182.ece)). In Parliament the Act allocated Jharkhand 14 seats in the [Lok Sabha](/organizations/lok-sabha/) and 6 in the Rajya Sabha ([CaseMine](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5a979d984a93263ca60b70f0)). Administratively the state is grouped into five divisions — Kolhan, Santhal Pargana, North Chotanagpur, South Chotanagpur and Palamu — with North Chotanagpur alone holding 25 of the 81 assembly seats, the highest of any division ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/elections/assembly-elections/jharkhand/jharkhand-assembly-elections-how-tribals-and-women-will-shape-the-outcome/articleshow/114663154.cms)). The electoral roll counted 2.59 crore voters ahead of the November 2024 polls ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/elections/jharkhand-assembly/jharkhand-assembly-election-battleground-ready-for-direct-contest-between-jmm-and-bjp/article68819182.ece)). Both 2024 election coverage and IBEF's 2026 state brief identify JMM leader Hemant Soren as Chief Minister; officeholders are not asserted as current beyond those sources ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/elections/jharkhand-assembly/jharkhand-assembly-election-battleground-ready-for-direct-contest-between-jmm-and-bjp/article68819182.ece); [IBEF](https://ibef.org/industry/jharkhand-presentation)).

## People: census and tribal weight

The Census of India counted 32,988,134 residents of Jharkhand in 2011 — 16,930,315 males and 16,057,819 females — up from 26,945,829 in 2001, a decadal growth of 22.42% (down from 23.19% the previous decade), and 2.72% of India's total population ([Jagran Josh](https://www.jagranjosh.com/general-knowledge/jharkhand-population-as-per-census-2011-1373872122-1)). What gives that number political weight is the tribal share: Scheduled Tribes account for 26.21% of the state's population, and 28 of the 81 assembly seats are reserved for ST candidates, a higher reservation share than the population share ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/elections/assembly-elections/jharkhand/jharkhand-assembly-elections-how-tribals-and-women-will-shape-the-outcome/articleshow/114663154.cms)). The state's literacy rate stands at 66.4%, and its listed state languages are Hindi, Santhali, Urdu, Bengali and English ([IBEF](https://ibef.org/states/jharkhand)). The Kolhan and Santhal Pargana divisions are the tribal-dominated regions, while North Chotanagpur is the industrialised coal belt ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/elections/assembly-elections/jharkhand/jharkhand-assembly-elections-how-tribals-and-women-will-shape-the-outcome/articleshow/114663154.cms)). Jharkhand borders Bihar to the north, Uttar Pradesh and Chhattisgarh to the west, Odisha to the south and West Bengal to the east ([IBEF](https://ibef.org/states/jharkhand)).

## A mineral economy

Jharkhand holds around 40% of India's mineral reserves, with itemized shares of 27.3% of the country's coal, 26% of its iron ore (hematite) — second among all states — and 18.5% of its copper ore, plus uranium, mica, bauxite, granite, limestone, silver, graphite, magnetite and dolomite ([IBEF](https://ibef.org/industry/jharkhand-presentation)).

| Mineral | Share of India's reserves | Position |
| ------- | ------------------------- | -------- |
| Coal | 27.3% | Largest coal reserves of any state |
| Iron ore (hematite) | 26% | Second among Indian states |
| Copper ore | 18.5% | Among the top holders |
| Coking coal, uranium, pyrite | Sole producer | Only state in India producing these |

Jharkhand is the only Indian state to produce coking coal, uranium and pyrite, and mineral production stood at 138.15 million tonnes in FY23 ([IBEF](https://ibef.org/industry/jharkhand-presentation)). The state accounts for an estimated 20–25% of India's total steel production, fed by the country's largest coking-coal resources ([IBEF](https://ibef.org/industry/jharkhand-presentation)). Gross state domestic product is estimated at Rs 6.24 trillion (US$66.92 billion) in FY27, growing 10.57% annually between FY16 and FY27 ([IBEF](https://ibef.org/industry/jharkhand-presentation)). The resource dependence is visible in the budget: the FY27 state budget expects Rs 14,656 crore from cess on mineral-bearing land alone, and proposes a Rs 1,209 crore Budget Stabilisation Fund to cushion state finances against mining-revenue fluctuations — which implies Jharkhand's public finances are structurally tied to extraction ([IBEF](https://ibef.org/industry/jharkhand-presentation)). Beyond minerals, the state is India's largest producer of tussar (non-mulberry) silk with a 76.4% output share, exports totalled Rs 20,847 crore in FY26, and about 80% of the rural population depends on agriculture, with rice on 80% of the cropped area ([IBEF](https://ibef.org/industry/jharkhand-presentation); [IBEF](https://ibef.org/states/jharkhand)).

## The Birsa Munda thread

No other Indian state's founding is so visibly tied to a single historical figure. Jharkhand's formation date — 15 November 2000 — is the birth anniversary of Birsa Munda, the 19th-century tribal leader, and the state marks that date as both its foundation day and Janjatiya Utsav ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/ranchi-news/jkhand-foundation-day-birsa-memorial-park-universal-pension-scheme-launch-mark-day-101636989802528-amp.html)). On the 2021 foundation day, Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated the Bhagwan Birsa Munda Smriti Udyan cum Freedom Fighters Museum in Ranchi by video conferencing, calling it a living establishment of tribal culture ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/ranchi-news/jkhand-foundation-day-birsa-memorial-park-universal-pension-scheme-launch-mark-day-101636989802528-amp.html)). The cultural memory now extends to children's media: at the 2024 [International Film Festival of India](/events/international-film-festival-of-india/) in [Goa](/places/goa/), Season 2 of "[Krish, Trish and Baltiboy](/creative-works/krish-trish-and-baltiboy/): Bharat Hain Hum" was launched with "Birsa Munda from Jharkhand" among its featured unsung heroes, released in 12 Indian and 7 international languages and streaming on [Prasar Bharati](/organizations/prasar-bharati/)'s Waves platform ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/entertainment/second-season-of-animated-series-krish-trish-and-baltiboy-bharat-hain-hum-launched-at-iffi/)). This profile goes no further into Munda's biography than its sources support; his leadership belongs to his own profile.

## Frequently asked questions

**When and how was Jharkhand formed?**
Jharkhand was formed on 15 November 2000 under the Bihar Reorganisation Act, 2000, which carved 18 districts out of southern Bihar; the Act inserted it as the 28th entry in the First Schedule of the Constitution. The date was fixed by notification S.O. 829(E) and coincides with Birsa Munda's birth anniversary.

**What is the capital of Jharkhand?**
Ranchi. The Hindu's 2024 election coverage explicitly identifies the Ranchi assembly seat as "state capital of Jharkhand," and IBEF's state fact file lists Ranchi as the capital. Jamshedpur is the state's largest city, but it is not the capital.

**Why does Jharkhand reserve so many assembly seats for Scheduled Tribes?**
Scheduled Tribes make up 26.21% of Jharkhand's population, and 28 of the 81 Legislative Assembly seats are reserved for ST candidates, with 9 more reserved for Scheduled Castes and 44 unreserved. The reservation share slightly exceeds the population share, reflecting the state's tribal character.

**What is Jharkhand's economy based on?**
Mining and minerals. The state holds around 40% of India's mineral reserves — including the largest coal reserves and the sole domestic production of coking coal, uranium and pyrite — and accounts for 20–25% of India's steel output. Its GSDP is estimated at Rs 6.24 trillion (US$66.92 billion) in FY27.

**Why is Birsa Munda so important to Jharkhand?**
The state's foundation day, 15 November, is his birth anniversary; the Prime Minister opened a museum to him in Ranchi in 2021 and the day is observed as Janjatiya Utsav; and Season 2 of "Krish, Trish and Baltiboy: Bharat Hain Hum" dramatises "Birsa Munda from Jharkhand" as an unsung freedom fighter.
