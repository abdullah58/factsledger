# Tennessee

**Tennessee is a state in the southeastern United States with an estimated
population of 7,315,076 (July 1, 2025) and an all-industry gross domestic
product of $589.8 billion (2025).** Admitted to the Union on June 1,
1796, as the 16th state, it is known as "The Volunteer State," has its
capital at Nashville, and is home to the most-visited national park in
the country — Great Smoky Mountains National Park, with more than 12
million recreational visits in 2024.

## A migration-driven growth story

Between 2016 and 2025, Tennessee's population grew 10% — from about
6.6 million to 7.2 million people, an average of 1.0% per year — making it
the 9th fastest-growing state in the country (Sycamore Institute analysis
of Census estimates). Growth was overwhelmingly migration-driven: 74% of
the decade's gain came from net domestic migration, another 20% from net
international migration, with the rest from natural increase. The gains
were uneven: 85 of 95 counties grew, from a 32% increase in Wilson
County to a 17% decline in Lake County.

The state's population is concentrated. As of 2020, about 93% of
Tennessee's land area was classified rural, but roughly 66% of residents
lived in urban areas; in 2025, about 55% of Tennesseans lived in the 10
most populous counties and one-third in the 10 largest cities. The
population is also aging: the number of residents 65 and older grew 30%
over the decade — faster than any other age group — rising from 15.7% to
18.6% of the population. And it grew more diverse: the non-white share
rose from 25.7% to 30%, driven by Hispanic and multiracial growth; in
2025 about 70% of Tennesseans were white non-Hispanic, 15.2% Black
non-Hispanic, and 8.6% Hispanic.

## Economy

Tennessee's $589.8 billion economy (2025, BEA) rests on auto
manufacturing — major Nissan, General Motors, and Volkswagen plants —
agriculture (top crops soybeans, corn, cotton), and health care companies
including HCA Healthcare, plus tourism anchored by the Smoky Mountains,
Graceland, and the Grand Ole Opry (HISTORY.com). Census figures
(2020–2024 ACS): 62.0% of residents 16+ are in the civilian labor force,
median household income is $69,595, per capita income $39,437, and 13.5%
live in poverty. The state has 150,129 employer establishments employing
nearly 3 million people with a $175.8 billion annual payroll (2023), and
43,374 building permits were issued in 2025.

## State facts and history

Tennessee takes its name from the Cherokee village Tanasi. Paleo-Indian
evidence dates back 12,000–15,000 years; at European contact the Cherokee
held the southeast and the Chickasaw the west (Hernando de Soto, 1540).
The 1830 Indian Removal Act forced tribes from the state; the Trail of
Tears to Oklahoma killed thousands. Initially part of North Carolina,
then the Southwest Territory from 1790, Tennessee entered the Union on
June 1, 1796, with Knoxville as its first capital.

A slave state and the last to secede, Tennessee was the first readmitted
during Reconstruction. Its modern milestones include the 1925 Scopes
Trial in Dayton, the April 4, 1968 assassination of Martin Luther King
Jr. in Memphis, and the 1982 Knoxville World's Fair (the 266-foot
Sunsphere survives). Great Smoky Mountains National Park spans 522,427
acres across Tennessee and North Carolina, holds 848 miles of trails,
and generated over $2.2 billion in economic activity in 2023 while
supporting 33,748 jobs. Notable Tennesseans include Presidents Andrew
Jackson, James Polk, and Andrew Johnson; Vice President Al Gore; Elvis
Presley, Dolly Parton, and Tina Turner; and authors Alex Haley and Ida
B. Wells. The state is home to the NBA's Memphis Grizzlies, NHL's
Nashville Predators, NFL's Tennessee Titans, and MLS's Nashville SC.

## FAQ

**Why is Tennessee called "The Volunteer State"?**
The nickname comes from the overwhelming volunteer response of
Tennesseans at the start of the War of 1812 (HISTORY.com).

**What is the capital of Tennessee?**
Nashville. Knoxville was the state's first capital.

**How fast is Tennessee growing?**
The state added about 669,000 people (10%) between 2016 and 2025,
mostly from people moving in from other states — the 9th fastest growth
rate in the country.

**What is the most-visited national park?**
Great Smoky Mountains National Park — more than 12 million recreational
visits in 2024, per the National Park Service.

**Who is the governor of Tennessee?**
Bill Lee, the 50th governor, in office since 2019.
