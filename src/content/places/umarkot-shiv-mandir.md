## Overview

The Umarkot Shiv Mandir — also rendered Umerkot Shiv Mandir or Amarkot Shiv Mandir — is a Shiva temple about 6
km from Umerkot in Sindh, Pakistan, near Rana Jaageer Goth ([DOAM](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/10373)). It
is one of the most sacred Hindu places of worship in lower Sindh, and the centerpiece of what Dawn called "one
of the biggest religious festivals in the country" ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1392074)).

The verified-but-unshared fact that this profile leads with is this: the federal Department of Archaeology and
Museums, the very authority that keeps the temple's official record, explicitly rejects the popular claim that
the temple is 5,000 years old — calling it "purely exaggeration" — and files the site under a relative
chronology of 1857–1947 CE, with a legal status of "Not Protected" and private ownership
([DOAM](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/10373)). No competing coverage of the temple examines the
archaeology department's own chronology or its rebuttal of the antiquity narrative.

## The official record: chronology, status, and the antiquity question

The department's site record lists the temple as "Monument - Temple" in Sindh's Umerkot district, period
"Historic", relative chronology "1857 - 1947 CE" ([DOAM](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/10373)). Its ownership
field reads "Private" and its legal status field "Not Protected" — the same record notes the temple is "one of
the oldest and revered temples for Hindus living in Sindh"
([DOAM](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/10373)).

The record is unusually candid about the age claims. It reports that local Hindu leader Cheetan Bhagat says
the temple is 5,000 years old, then states directly that "this is purely exaggeration and has nothing to do
with facts, because there was no such concept five thousand years from now"
([DOAM](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/10373)). That official rebuttal matters because the 5,000-year figure
circulates unchallenged elsewhere: The Express Tribune repeated Bhagat's claim in 2021
([Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2320096/umerkot-qila-stands-tall-in-history)), and a 2024 report noted a
local community leader making the same claim alongside the more common "more than 2,000 years" belief
([IANS](https://www.illustrateddailynews.com/world/sindh-govt-proposes-kartarpur-like-religious-corridor-for-hindu-jain-pilgrims-551062)).

Dawn put the antiquity question in careful terms: "It is said that this temple is perhaps the oldest in Sindh"
([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1392074)) — a report of local tradition, not an archaeological finding. The
oldest figure given in 2024 reporting was 2,000 years ([Times
Now](https://www.timesnownews.com/spiritual/2000-year-old-shiva-temple-in-pakistan-houses-a-shivling-that-grows-in-height-article-110389587)).
The one firm structural fact all sources share is that the current structure was built or expanded by a Muslim
man about a century ago — reported by Dawn, The Express Tribune, the department, and Times Now alike
([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1392074); [DOAM](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/10373)). His name is given
in none of them.

## The temple, the legends, and the Durga shrine

A neatly tiled staircase painted in yellow and red — saffron and red, in one account — leads to a middle-sized
courtyard and then to the main temple ([DOAM](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/10373);
[Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2320096/umerkot-qila-stands-tall-in-history)). Inside, the space is
described as filled with the musky fragrance of amber, with the Shiva Lingam placed in the middle
([Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2320096/umerkot-qila-stands-tall-in-history)). The Sindhi-style
architecture is modest yet striking: a dome crowning the sanctum, a rising shikhara, and murals illustrating
episodes from Hindu mythology ([Sindh Renaissance](https://sindhrenaissance.com/umerkot-shiv-temple%F0%9F%94%B1%F0%9F%9B%95-a-timeless-beacon-of-faith-in-sindh%F0%9F%87%B5%F0%9F%87%B0/)).

Two legends attach to the lingam. In the cowherd story, a man grazing his cows noticed one animal repeatedly
leaving the herd to empty her milk over a particular stone; people investigated, concluded it was a Shiva
Lingam, and built the temple ([DOAM](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/10373); [Times
Now](https://www.timesnownews.com/spiritual/2000-year-old-shiva-temple-in-pakistan-houses-a-shivling-that-grows-in-height-article-110389587)).
The second holds that the lingam kept growing until locals marked its height to literally observe the growth
([DOAM](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/10373)).

Right behind the main temple stands a recently-constructed shrine to Durga, the Mother Goddess associated with
protection, strength, motherhood, destruction and wars, with a mural depicting her painted on the main wall of
the temple's main hall ([DOAM](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/10373);
[Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2320096/umerkot-qila-stands-tall-in-history)).

## The Shivratri festival

The department records that the temple hosts a three-day Shiva festival each March, drawing some 250,000
people, with pilgrims coming from lower and upper Sindh and most staying all three days
([DOAM](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/10373)). Dawn's 2018 account describes devotees singing "Jai Shiva
Shankar" and "Bolo Har Har Mahadev Ki Jai" as they passed through Muslim villages, where they were served
drinks — "interfaith harmony is still alive in Sindh in general and Tharparkar in particular"
([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1392074)). Members of Hindu communities including the Maheswari, Lohana,
Mahraj, Khatri, Malhi, Koli, Bhil, Meghwar, Charan, Giri, Oad, Jatia, Kariam Gavaria and Jogis attend to pay
homage to Shiva ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1392074)).

The logistics are substantial: for all three days the Panchayat arranged meals of rice, vegetables, chicken and
sweets, with the person in charge of food reporting that more than 3,000 cauldrons were prepared; women were
served in a separate hall decorated with images of Shiva with Parvati, Ganpati and Kartikeya
([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1392074)). All expenses were borne by the All Hindu Panchayat of Umarkot,
which also manages the temple and has built a guesthouse, community hall and other amenities for the growing
numbers of pilgrims ([DOAM](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/10373);
[Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1392074)). *Om* symbols welcome the yatris at the temple gate, and its walls
are decorated with posters of Lord Shiva and other saints of Thar
([DOAM](https://doam.gov.pk/public/sites/10373)).

The festival's interfaith character is a recurring theme: local Muslim villagers participate and provide
hospitality to pilgrims, an enduring symbol of Sindh's inclusive traditions ([Sindh Renaissance](https://sindhrenaissance.com/umerkot-shiv-temple%F0%9F%94%B1%F0%9F%9B%95-a-timeless-beacon-of-faith-in-sindh%F0%9F%87%B5%F0%9F%87%B0/)).

## A corridor on the horizon

In June 2024, Sindh Tourism Minister Zulfiqar Ali Shah proposed developing a Kartarpur-like religious corridor
from contiguous areas of Gujarat and Rajasthan to Umarkot and Nagarparkar, to let Hindu and Jain devotees
worship at the region's ancient temples — naming the Shri Shiv Mandir among them
([IANS](https://www.illustrateddailynews.com/world/sindh-govt-proposes-kartarpur-like-religious-corridor-for-hindu-jain-pilgrims-551062)).
Shah made the proposal at a tourism promotion event in Dubai, arguing that legions of Hindus and Jains were
willing to visit Sindh's temple regions, and separately suggested a weekly flight from India to Sukkur or
Larkana for religious tourists
([IANS](https://www.illustrateddailynews.com/world/sindh-govt-proposes-kartarpur-like-religious-corridor-for-hindu-jain-pilgrims-551062)).

The proposal framed the stakes: Umarkot is described as the only Hindu-majority district in Pakistan, and the
[Kartarpur Corridor](/places/kartarpur-corridor/) — opened on November 9, 2019, for visa-free Sikh pilgrimage to Gurdwara Darbar Sahib
Kartarpur — as the model ([IANS](https://www.illustrateddailynews.com/world/sindh-govt-proposes-kartarpur-like-religious-corridor-for-hindu-jain-pilgrims-551062)).
Meanwhile, heritage advocates have called for stronger governmental protection of the temple itself, with some
advocates urging UNESCO World Heritage inclusion ([Sindh Renaissance](https://sindhrenaissance.com/umerkot-shiv-temple%F0%9F%94%B1%F0%9F%9B%95-a-timeless-beacon-of-faith-in-sindh%F0%9F%87%B5%F0%9F%87%B0/)) — a notable aspiration given the temple's
current "Not Protected" status.

## What this profile does not claim

No founding date, construction period or architectural dating is given, because no source read for this
profile supplies one from a heritage authority; the archaeology department's own record assigns the site to
1857–1947 CE and rejects the 5,000-year claim. The age figures in circulation (2,000 and 5,000 years) are
reported as attributed claims, not findings. The name of the Muslim benefactor who expanded the structure
about a century ago is not given in any source read. Attendance figures come from 2018 reporting and the
department's record; no official multi-year attendance series was located. The 2024 religious-corridor
proposal is recorded as a ministerial proposal; no construction or approval was reported. The profile makes no
claim about the temple's physical condition or any conservation work, as none was located.
