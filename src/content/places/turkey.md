Turkey is a transcontinental republic of about 86 million people, with its capital in Ankara and its economic center in Istanbul. It fields NATO's second-largest military, and it is the world's second-largest exporter of television series after the United States — a cultural-soft-power export that no standard country profile explains, but that now shapes how hundreds of millions of people see the country ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Turkey); [NPR](https://www.weku.org/npr-news/2026-09-29/as-the-u-s-signals-a-smaller-middle-east-footprint-turkey-is-ready-to-step-in?_amp=true); [Express Tribune/AFP](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2593550/turkey-bets-big-on-dizi-diplomacy)).

## Overview

Turkey, officially Türkiye Cumhuriyeti (the Republic of Türkiye), sits at the crossroads of the Balkans, Caucasus, and Middle East — most of it on the Anatolian peninsula in Asia, with Turkish Thrace in Europe ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Turkey)). The capital is Ankara and the largest city is Istanbul. Britannica puts the population at 85,954,000 in a 2026 estimate, 77.9% urban; Turkish is the official language and the lira the currency ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Turkey)). The Turkish Straits — the Bosporus, the Sea of Marmara, the Dardanelles — are the Black Sea's only outlet, and Turkey's control of them has shaped its foreign relations ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Turkey)). The modern republic was founded in 1923 after the Ottoman Empire's collapse, under Mustafa Kemal Atatürk ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Turkey)). Constitutional amendments implemented in 2018 expanded the Grand National Assembly to 600 seats, eliminated the prime minister's post, and expanded the president's role ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Turkey)). At the [United Nations](/organizations/united-nations/), Türkiye became official in June 2022, after a letter from Foreign Minister Mevlüt Çavuşoğlu to Secretary-General António Guterres ([NPR](https://www.wcbe.org/npr-news/npr-news/2022-06-02/turkey-will-now-be-known-as-turkiye-at-least-at-the-u-n)).

## Dizi diplomacy: the screen as foreign policy

In February 2026, Culture and Tourism Minister Mehmet Nuri Ersoy announced incentives worth up to the equivalent of $100,000 in lira per episode for producers of dizis — the soap-style dramas Turkey sells to 170 countries — provided they promote Turkey and the Turkish language abroad ([Express Tribune/AFP](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2593550/turkey-bets-big-on-dizi-diplomacy)). Qualifying productions must target priority tourism markets and show strong ratings; in exchange, the ministry accelerates filming permits and opens historical and cultural sites free of charge ([Express Tribune/AFP](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2593550/turkey-bets-big-on-dizi-diplomacy)). Global demand rose 184% between 2020 and 2023, according to Parrot Analytics ([Express Tribune/AFP](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2593550/turkey-bets-big-on-dizi-diplomacy)). AFP's reporting puts Turkey second only to the United States as an exporter of television series by hours sold, with annual export revenues past the $1 billion mark; Daily Sabah's cited academic ranks it third by production volume ([Express Tribune/AFP](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2593550/turkey-bets-big-on-dizi-diplomacy); [Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/turkiye/turkish-dramas-expand-turkiyes-soft-power-amid-image-debate/news)). An hour of dizi sells for $20,000–$30,000 against roughly $1 million for prime U.S. or British drama, while production teams deliver up to 140 minutes of content a week ([Express Tribune/AFP](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2593550/turkey-bets-big-on-dizi-diplomacy)).

## A state-backed cinema industry

The Culture and Tourism Ministry has financed Turkish cinema at scale. The number of Turkish films screened nationwide rose from nine in 2002 to 180 in 2018, while the audience for Turkish films jumped from 2 million to 44 million filmgoers in the same period — and with 63% of moviegoers choosing domestic films in 2018, Turkey ranked first in Europe for preferring its own productions ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkish-films-soaring-with-support-143623)). Between 2010 and 2017 the ministry granted roughly 235 million lira to film productions ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkish-films-soaring-with-support-143623)). The pipeline's flagship is [Ayla: The Daughter of War](/creative-works/ayla-the-daughter-of-war/), which sold 5-million-plus tickets in 2017 and became Turkey's official Oscar entry ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkish-films-soaring-with-support-143623); [Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/amp/ayla-a-story-of-love-turkeys-oscar-entry-121072)). Directed by [Can Ülkay](/people/can-ulkay/) and based on the real-life story of [Sergeant Süleyman Dilbirliği](/people/suleyman-dilbirligi/), it stars [İsmail Hacıoğlu](/people/ismail-hacioglu/), Çetin Tekindor, and [Lee Kyung-jin](/people/lee-kyung-jin/) ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/amp/ayla-a-story-of-love-turkeys-oscar-entry-121072)). Ministry-backed films also traveled: Nuri Bilge Ceylan's The Wild Pear Tree competed for the Palme d'Or at Cannes 2018, Buğday (Grain) won the Grand Prix in Tokyo, and Anons took the Horizons Special Jury Prize at the 75th Venice Film Festival ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkish-films-soaring-with-support-143623)).

## Hard power and a record tourism engine

Turkey pairs cultural export with a large conventional footprint: it fields NATO's second-largest military, maintains bases in Somalia, Qatar, and Libya, and has cast itself as a mediator in conflicts from Ukraine to Iran ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/npr-news/2026-09-29/as-the-u-s-signals-a-smaller-middle-east-footprint-turkey-is-ready-to-step-in?_amp=true)). NPR's reporting notes that Turkey's TV shows are watched across the Middle East — the same screens the state now subsidizes — alongside a broadening defense-industrial base ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/npr-news/2026-09-29/as-the-u-s-signals-a-smaller-middle-east-footprint-turkey-is-ready-to-step-in?_amp=true)). In 2025, Türkiye drew a record 63.94 million visitors generating $65.23 billion in tourism income ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkiyes-2025-tourism-income-up-6-8-percent-to-65-2-billion-218417)) — more than sixty times the industry's estimated television-series export revenues, which means the soft-power push doubles as one of the state's largest hard-currency earners. Russia sent the most foreign tourists (6.9 million), followed by Germany (6.75 million); Istanbul absorbed 18.97 million foreign arrivals and Antalya more than 16 million ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkiyes-2025-tourism-income-up-6-8-percent-to-65-2-billion-218417)). The state feeds the funnel directly: [Turkish Airlines](/organizations/turkish-airlines/) offers transit passengers in Istanbul complimentary half-day tours of major dizi studio sets ([Express Tribune/AFP](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2593550/turkey-bets-big-on-dizi-diplomacy)).

## FAQ

**Why is the country called Türkiye instead of Turkey?**
The United Nations adopted "Türkiye" as the official name in all languages in June 2022, after Foreign Minister Mevlüt Çavuşoğlu wrote to Secretary-General António Guterres ([NPR](https://www.wcbe.org/npr-news/npr-news/2022-06-02/turkey-will-now-be-known-as-turkiye-at-least-at-the-u-n)). The rebrand began in December 2021 with President Erdoğan's memorandum asking the world to use Türkiye — pitched as brand-building and a way to stop confusing the country with the bird ([NPR](https://www.wcbe.org/npr-news/npr-news/2022-06-02/turkey-will-now-be-known-as-turkiye-at-least-at-the-u-n)).

**How big is Turkey's TV-drama export industry?**
Turkish dizis are sold to more than 170 countries, with global demand up 184% between 2020 and 2023 (Parrot Analytics) and annual export revenues past $1 billion ([Express Tribune/AFP](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2593550/turkey-bets-big-on-dizi-diplomacy)). By hours sold, Turkey is the world's second-largest exporter of television series after the United States ([Express Tribune/AFP](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2593550/turkey-bets-big-on-dizi-diplomacy)).

**What is Ayla: The Daughter of War?**
The 2017 Turkish film directed by Can Ülkay, based on the true story of Sgt. Süleyman Dilbirliği and the Korean orphan he sheltered during the Korean War ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/amp/ayla-a-story-of-love-turkeys-oscar-entry-121072)). Backed by the Culture and Tourism Ministry, it sold more than 5 million tickets — Turkey's second most-watched film of 2017 — and was the country's official Oscar entry ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkish-films-soaring-with-support-143623)).

**Where do Turkey's tourists come from?**
Russians were the largest foreign group in 2025 at 6.9 million, followed by Germans (6.75 million), Britons (4.27 million), and Americans (1.58 million, up nearly 10%) ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkiyes-2025-tourism-income-up-6-8-percent-to-65-2-billion-218417)). Istanbul took 18.97 million foreign arrivals — 36% of the national total — and Antalya more than 16 million ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkiyes-2025-tourism-income-up-6-8-percent-to-65-2-billion-218417)).

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not reconcile the TV-export ranking: sources report second-largest by hours sold (AFP) and third-largest by production volume (Daily Sabah's academic interviewee) — both are attributed, not averaged.
- It does not cover 2026 macroeconomic indicators (inflation, interest rates, lira valuation); those were not part of this research pass.
- It does not assert the recent, fast-moving geopolitics in the September 2026 NPR piece (defense agreements, regional wars); only its stable Turkey-specific facts are used.
- Ayla's box-office figures and the domestic-preference statistic are 2017–2018-era ministry-compiled data, and are dated as such.
