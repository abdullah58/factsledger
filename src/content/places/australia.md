## Overview

Australia, officially the Commonwealth of Australia, is the smallest continent and one of the largest countries on Earth, lying between the Pacific and Indian oceans in the Southern Hemisphere, with its capital at Canberra and a federal parliamentary system, formally a constitutional monarchy, formed in 1901 from six British colonies ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Australia)). Its parliament has two houses — a 76-seat Senate and a 150-seat House of Representatives — and its head of state is the British monarch, represented by a governor-general ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Australia)). Its population was 27.6 million at 30 June 2025 ([Australian Bureau of Statistics](https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/population-15-june-2025)). It is also the home country of [Afterpay](/organizations/afterpay/), the [Sydney](/places/sydney/)-founded buy-now-pay-later pioneer whose US$29 billion sale became the biggest buyout of an Australian firm ([Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/twitters-dorsey-leads-blockbuster-29-billion-buyout-of-australian-bnpl-giant-afterpay/)).

## From First Peoples to federation

Australia's human history begins long before its written one. Aboriginal explorers arrived from Asia at least 60,000 years before European explorers sailed into the South Pacific, and by 20,000 years ago they had spread throughout the mainland and its chief island outlier, Tasmania ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Australia); [Parliamentary Education Office](https://peo.gov.au/understand-our-parliament/history-of-parliament/federation/federation)). When Captain Arthur Phillip landed with the First Fleet at Botany Bay in 1788, there may have been between 250,000 and 500,000 Aboriginal people on the continent ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Australia)).

British settlement grew into six colonies, each with its own parliament, railway system, postage stamps and taxes by the late 1800s, but all still under the British Parliament ([Parliamentary Education Office](https://peo.gov.au/understand-our-parliament/history-of-parliament/federation/federation)). The case for uniting them was practical: removing internal taxes and borders would increase trade, and a national government could organise defence and control immigration ([Parliamentary Education Office](https://peo.gov.au/understand-our-parliament/history-of-parliament/federation/federation)). After conferences, conventions and referendums sparked by Henry Parkes's 1889 Tenterfield oration, the British Parliament passed the Commonwealth of Australia Constitution Act 1900, signed by Queen Victoria in July 1900 ([Parliamentary Education Office](https://peo.gov.au/understand-our-parliament/history-of-parliament/federation/federation)). On 1 January 1901 the six colonies united as the states of the Commonwealth, with the declaration ceremony at Centennial Park in Sydney, Lord Hopetoun sworn in as first Governor-General and Edmund Barton as first Prime Minister, and the first Parliament opening in Melbourne on 9 May 1901 ([Parliamentary Education Office](https://peo.gov.au/understand-our-parliament/history-of-parliament/federation/federation)). The same account is frank about whose voices were missing: women in most colonies could not vote in the federation referendums, and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples were mostly excluded from voting and the proceedings ([Parliamentary Education Office](https://peo.gov.au/understand-our-parliament/history-of-parliament/federation/federation)).

## A federation built around a compromise capital

Federation created an immediate problem: where should the new nation's parliament sit? The Constitution answered it only in outline — section 125 required the seat of government to be in Commonwealth territory of at least 100 square miles, within New South Wales and at least 100 miles from Sydney ([National Capital Authority](https://www.nca.gov.au/canberra-seat-government)). Rivalry between the two most influential colonies meant the capital would be in neither Sydney nor Melbourne, so the parliament, sitting temporarily in Melbourne, had to choose a site; after investigating alternatives in 1908, a committee chose modern-day Canberra, officially named by Lady Denman on 12 March 1913 ([National Capital Authority](https://www.nca.gov.au/canberra-seat-government)). The Constitution set up a House of Representatives elected on population — the "people's house" of 150 members — and a Senate designed as the "states' house", with 12 senators from each state and two from each territory ([National Capital Authority](https://www.nca.gov.au/canberra-seat-government)). Neither territory existed in 1901: the Australian Capital Territory and the Northern Territory were both created in 1911, the latter previously part of South Australia, and neither holds the same legal status as a state ([Parliamentary Education Office](https://peo.gov.au/understand-our-parliament/history-of-parliament/federation/federation)). Parliament moved from Melbourne to Canberra in 1927, the permanent new Parliament House opened in 1988, the Australian Capital Territory held its first Legislative Assembly election in March 1989, and the Northern Territory had become self-governing in 1978 ([National Capital Authority](https://www.nca.gov.au/canberra-seat-government); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Australia)).

## An economy built on resources — and a fintech export

Mining has been a cornerstone since the gold rushes of the 1850s, contributing about 13.8 per cent of GDP as of 2025 in a predominantly export-oriented sector ([US International Trade Administration](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/australia-mining-and-critical-minerals-0)). Australia is the world's largest producer of lithium and among the top five producers of gold, iron ore, lead, zinc and nickel, holds the world's largest uranium reserves, and counts iron ore — 953 million metric tons exported in 2024, rising to 954 million in 2025 — as its top export earner ([US International Trade Administration](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/australia-mining-and-critical-minerals-0)). Iron ore export earnings, however, fell from US$93.4 billion in 2023–24 to US$67 billion in 2024–25 as prices softened while volumes rose ([US International Trade Administration](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/australia-mining-and-critical-minerals-0)).

The story most country profiles miss is that Australia also exported a financial idea. Afterpay, created in 2014 by [Anthony Eisen](/people/anthony-eisen/) and [Nick Molnar](/people/nick-molnar/) — Molnar built it in his Sydney home — let shoppers split purchases into interest-free instalments, with merchants paying about 4 per cent of each transaction's value and Australian customers facing late fees of up to 25 per cent of the purchase price, capped at A$68 per order ([AFP](https://techxplore.com/news/2021-08-afterpay-billion-aussie-startup.html?deviceType=mobile)). By 2021 it had more than 16 million customers worldwide and almost 100,000 retailers, and its sale to the US payments firm [Square](/organizations/block/) for US$29 billion was the biggest buyout of an Australian firm, eclipsing the previous record, the US$16 billion sale of Westfield's global shopping mall empire in 2018 ([Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/twitters-dorsey-leads-blockbuster-29-billion-buyout-of-australian-bnpl-giant-afterpay/)). The regulatory footnote matters as much as the price: because buy-now-pay-later firms generally make money from merchant commission and late fees rather than interest, they sidestepped the legal definition of credit, were not required to run background checks, and normally asked only for a name, address and birth date — a gap that drew a voluntary industry code from eight Australian companies in March 2021 and, in California, an almost US$1 million settlement over operating a lending business without a licence ([Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/twitters-dorsey-leads-blockbuster-29-billion-buyout-of-australian-bnpl-giant-afterpay/); [AFP](https://techxplore.com/news/2021-08-afterpay-billion-aussie-startup.html?deviceType=mobile)).
