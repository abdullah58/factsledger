## Overview

Ohio is a Midwestern state of the [United States](/places/united-states/) with nearly 11.9 million people, the 17th state admitted to the Union in 1803, whose capital and largest city is Columbus ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)). It is where *Billboard* published its first issue in 1894, where the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame stands in Cleveland, and where, in November 2026, voters elected Amy Acton as the state's first woman governor ([Sam Houston State University](https://profiles.shsu.edu/lis_fwh/book/other_notable_genres/support/Billboard2.htm); [Signal Ohio](https://signalohio.org/amy-acton-wins-ohio-governors-race-setting-up-a-new-era-of-divided-government/)). This profile is about the state of Ohio — not the Ohio River, which forms its southern border and gives it its name, and not Ohio University or Ohio State University ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)).

Ohio sits on the northeastern edge of the Midwest, bordered by Lake Erie to the north, Pennsylvania to the east, West Virginia and Kentucky to the south, Indiana to the west and Michigan to the northwest ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)). It is the 34th-largest state by area at 44,826 square miles, but near the top in population: the 2020 census counted 11,799,448 residents, and the 2024 estimate was 11,883,304 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)). Its nickname is the Buckeye State, its motto "With God, All Things Are Possible", its bird the northern cardinal and its song "Beautiful Ohio" ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)). Ohio has supplied eight U.S. presidents by birth or residence — William H. Harrison, Ulysses S. Grant, Rutherford B. Hayes, James A. Garfield, Benjamin Harrison, William McKinley, William H. Taft and Warren G. Harding ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)).

## From Northwest Territory to the 17th state

Ohio was the first state carved from the Northwest Territory, becoming the 17th member of the Union on March 1, 1803 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)). Its name comes from the Ohio River, which traces its name to an Iroquoian word meaning "great water" ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)). The capital moved twice in the state's early years — Chillicothe, then Zanesville — before settling in newly founded, centrally located Columbus in 1816, a city the legislature had planned in 1812 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)). The canal feeder branch of 1831, the National Road of 1836 and the first railroad of 1850 then turned Columbus into the state's largest city ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)). Ohio played a major role in the Civil War, supplying substantial support to the Union Army, was a key battleground in the War of 1812, and sat on the Underground Railroad, helping escaped slaves reach freedom ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)).

## The 2026 governor's race: a first and a flip

On November 3, 2026, Democrat Amy Acton defeated Republican Vivek Ramaswamy for governor of Ohio, 52.3% to 44.4%, with Libertarian Don Kissick at 3.3% ([MultiState](https://www.multistate.us/elections/governor-race/ohio-2026)). Acton, a doctor who led Ohio's Department of Health at the start of the coronavirus pandemic, is the first woman to be elected governor of Ohio, and the first Democrat to win the office since Ted Strickland in 2006 ([Reason](https://reason.com/2026/11/03/amy-acton-beats-vivek-ramaswamy-in-ohio-governors-race/); [Signal Ohio](https://signalohio.org/amy-acton-wins-ohio-governors-race-setting-up-a-new-era-of-divided-government/)). She takes office in January, replacing Republican Governor Mike DeWine, who could not seek reelection because of term limits ([Signal Ohio](https://signalohio.org/amy-acton-wins-ohio-governors-race-setting-up-a-new-era-of-divided-government/)). The race turned ugly in its final weeks over a Ramaswamy campaign ad featuring Acton's estranged mother calling her a liar — an ad applauded by President Donald Trump and Vice President J.D. Vance but condemned by other prominent Republicans, and one that private polling showed coincided with a swing toward Acton ([Reason](https://reason.com/2026/11/03/amy-acton-beats-vivek-ramaswamy-in-ohio-governors-race/); [Signal Ohio](https://signalohio.org/amy-acton-wins-ohio-governors-race-setting-up-a-new-era-of-divided-government/)). More than $200 million in advertising ran against Acton, a state record, against at least $44 million on her side ([Signal Ohio](https://signalohio.org/amy-acton-wins-ohio-governors-race-setting-up-a-new-era-of-divided-government/)).

## An economy built on making things

Manufacturing is one of Ohio's most important economic activities, producing motor vehicles, metal products, chemicals and processed foods and accounting for nearly a fifth of the state's gross product ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)). That scale matters: with over half the U.S. population within 500 miles, Ohio acts as a crucial industrial hub, supported by lake ports, railways and a location between the Eastern Seaboard and the Midwest ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)). The service sector — trade, government, finance and health services — has grown to contribute over half of Ohio's gross state product, while agriculture in the Maumee valley and Lake Plains regions balances the economy with soybeans, corn and livestock ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)). The newest layer is artificial intelligence: OpenAI, Oracle and SoftBank announced in September 2025 that one of five new Stargate data-centre sites will be in Lordstown, Ohio, where SoftBank has broken ground on an advanced design on track to be operational the following year ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/five-new-stargate-sites/?ref=bayarealetters.com)). The Lordstown site and a Texas counterpart can scale to 1.5 gigawatts over 18 months ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/five-new-stargate-sites/?ref=bayarealetters.com)).

## Music: from Billboard to the Rock Hall

Ohio's hold on American popular culture starts in Cincinnati, where *Billboard* published its first issue on November 1, 1894 — an eight-page monthly called *Billboard Advertising*, "devoted to the interests of advertisers, poster printers, bill posters, advertising agents & secretaries of fairs", founded by William Donaldson and James Hennegan ([Billboard](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/11/01/the-first-billboard-all-that-was-new-bright-and-interesting-on-the-boards/); [Sam Houston State University](https://profiles.shsu.edu/lis_fwh/book/other_notable_genres/support/Billboard2.htm)). The magazine renamed itself *The Billboard* in 1897 and *Billboard* in 1961, and its charts became the industry standard: the first national music chart appeared in July 1940, the Billboard 200 became a weekly chart in March 1956, and the Hot 100 premiered in August 1958 ([Billboard](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/11/01/the-first-billboard-all-that-was-new-bright-and-interesting-on-the-boards/)). The brand has since grown to encompass the [Billboard Music Awards](/events/billboard-music-awards/) and other extensions ([Billboard](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/11/01/the-first-billboard-all-that-was-new-bright-and-interesting-on-the-boards/)). Cleveland holds the other half of the story: the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Foundation, established in 1983 by Atlantic Records cofounder Ahmet Ertegun and *Rolling Stone* publisher Jann Wenner, chose Cleveland over rival bids from New Orleans, Memphis, Chicago, San Francisco and [New York City](/places/new-york-city/), because disc jockey Alan Freed had popularised the term "rock and roll" there in the 1950s ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rock-and-Roll-Hall-of-Fame-and-Museum)). The I.M. Pei-designed museum opened on the Lake Erie shore in 1995, and [Aretha Franklin](/people/aretha-franklin/) was inducted in its second class, in 1987 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rock-and-Roll-Hall-of-Fame-and-Museum)).

## Flight and football

Dayton claims aviation: Wilbur and Orville Wright designed their flying machines at home in Dayton, built a gasoline engine there with bicycle-shop mechanic Charlie Taylor, and on December 17, 1903 flew 120 feet in 12 seconds — a flight that, the National Park Service says, "effectively launched the age of human flight" ([U.S. National Park Service](https://home.nps.gov/articles/wright-brothers-national-memorial-site-of-the-first-controlled-powered-flight-teaching-with-historic-places.htm)). Their fourth flight that day covered 852 feet in 59 seconds ([U.S. National Park Service](https://home.nps.gov/articles/wright-brothers-national-memorial-site-of-the-first-controlled-powered-flight-teaching-with-historic-places.htm)). Canton claims professional football: the league now called the NFL was founded there on September 17, 1920, as the American Professional Football Association, and the Pro Football Hall of Fame has stood there since it opened on September 7, 1963 ([Pro Football Hall of Fame](https://www.profootballhof.com/visit/hall-of-fame-history)). The Canton Bulldogs, an early pro power, were the league's first two-time champions in 1922 and 1923, and Jim Thorpe played his first pro football with the Bulldogs starting in 1915 ([Pro Football Hall of Fame](https://www.profootballhof.com/visit/hall-of-fame-history)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Ohio known for?**
Ohio is known as the Buckeye State, for its manufacturing economy, for supplying eight U.S. presidents, and for its outsized role in American culture: *Billboard* began in Cincinnati in 1894, the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame stands in Cleveland, the Wright brothers built the first airplane in Dayton, and the NFL was founded in Canton ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)).

**When did Ohio become a state?**
Ohio became the 17th state on March 1, 1803, the first state carved from the Northwest Territory ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)). Its capital moved from Chillicothe to Zanesville in the early years before settling in Columbus in 1816, and the state takes its name from the Ohio River ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)).

**Who won the 2026 Ohio governor's race?**
Democrat Amy Acton won on November 3, 2026, with 52.3% of the vote against Republican Vivek Ramaswamy's 44.4% ([MultiState](https://www.multistate.us/elections/governor-race/ohio-2026)). She is the first woman elected governor of Ohio and the first Democrat to win the office since 2006 ([Signal Ohio](https://signalohio.org/amy-acton-wins-ohio-governors-race-setting-up-a-new-era-of-divided-government/)).

**Why is the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in Cleveland?**
The foundation considered bids from New Orleans, Memphis, Chicago, San Francisco and New York City, then chose Cleveland, where disc jockey Alan Freed had popularised the term "rock and roll" in the 1950s and which offered a public and private funding package ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rock-and-Roll-Hall-of-Fame-and-Museum)). The museum opened in 1995.

**What is Ohio's biggest industry?**
Manufacturing is one of Ohio's most important economic activities, producing motor vehicles, metal products, chemicals and processed foods and accounting for nearly a fifth of the state's gross product ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)). The service sector, however, now contributes over half of gross state product ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Ohio-state)).
