## Overview

Tokyo is the capital of [Japan](/places/japan/) and the core of Greater Tokyo, the world's largest metropolitan area, with an estimated agglomeration population of 41.2 million in 2025 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Tokyo)). The city proper — the 23 special wards — had 9,733,276 residents in 2020. Formerly Edo, the seat of the Tokugawa shogunate, it became the imperial capital in 1868 and was renamed Tokyo, meaning "eastern capital". It is Japan's political, economic and cultural centre, and the seat of the emperor and the national government.

## From Edo to imperial capital

The site of Tokyo had been inhabited since ancient times, and the small fishing village of Edo existed there for centuries before its development into a city began in the Tokugawa period (1603–1867), when it became the capital of the Tokugawa shogunate ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Tokyo)). During that period the imperial family remained in Kyoto, the ancient imperial capital. With the Meiji Restoration of 1868, which ended the shogunate, the capital was moved to Edo and the city was renamed Tokyo. Edo had already been Japan's largest city since the 17th century, its population exceeded one million in the late 19th century, and as Japan's political, economic and cultural centre it became one of the world's most populous cities in the 20th century ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Tokyo)). Tokyo rebuilt twice after catastrophe — the earthquake of 1923 and the firebombing of March 1945 — and hosted the Summer Olympic Games in 1964, the first time the Games were held in Asia ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/facts/Tokyo)).

## The political, financial and cultural centre

Tokyo is Japan's largest industrial, commercial and financial centre, and many domestic and international financial institutions and other businesses are headquartered in central Tokyo ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Tokyo)). The city is built around the Imperial Palace, the home of the emperor of Japan, which lies at its heart encircled by stone-walled moats and broad gardens. East of the palace is Marunouchi, the financial hub and a major centre of Japanese business; south lies Kasumigaseki, with many national government offices, and west of that Nagatacho, where the National Diet Building stands ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Tokyo)). Tokyo is also Japan's major cultural centre — the Tokyo National Museum stands in Ueno Park, theatrical works from traditional Kabuki to modern drama are performed regularly, and the University of Tokyo heads a long list of major universities ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Tokyo)). It is the country's chief transportation hub, served by a dense network of electric railways, subways, bus lines and highways, with Tokyo Station as the central rail terminal for the Shinkansen bullet trains, Narita airport for international flights and Haneda on the bay for domestic service ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Tokyo)).

## The 2025 manga-piracy ruling

In November 2025 Tokyo became the venue for a landmark internet-copyright judgment. On 19 November, the [Tokyo District Court](/organizations/tokyo-district-court/) held [Cloudflare](/organizations/cloudflare/) liable for aiding the infringement of publication rights in four manga titles, including the globally popular *ONE PIECE* and *Attack on Titan*, in a lawsuit brought by Japan's "Big Four" publishers — KADOKAWA, [Kodansha](/organizations/kodansha/), [Shueisha](/organizations/shueisha/) and [Shogakukan](/organizations/shogakukan/) ([AIPPI](https://www.aippi.org/news/dont-aid-pirates-the-court-found-cloudflare-liable-for-aiding-and-abetting-copyright-infringement-of-one-piece/)). It was the first time a global content delivery network had been held financially responsible for damages arising from third-party piracy. The infringement ran through two massive piracy sites distributing more than 4,000 manga titles without authorisation and attracting more than 300 million monthly accesses at their peak, using Cloudflare's CDN service ([AIPPI](https://www.aippi.org/news/dont-aid-pirates-the-court-found-cloudflare-liable-for-aiding-and-abetting-copyright-infringement-of-one-piece/)). The publishers had sent DMCA notices and obtained US information-disclosure orders in 2020 and 2021 before filing suit on 1 February 2022, and the case was heard in one of the court's Intellectual Property Divisions ([Shueisha Inc.](https://www.shueisha.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/notice20251119eng.pdf)).

The court's reasoning matters beyond the parties. It found the aiding lay in Cloudflare's business practices, not in CDN technology itself: a systemic lack of "Know Your Customer" verification and a policy of simplifying account creation gave piracy operators a "high degree of anonymity", and the court declined safe-harbour protection because Cloudflare could have known of the infringement from the notices and could technically have stopped serving the sites ([AIPPI](https://www.aippi.org/news/dont-aid-pirates-the-court-found-cloudflare-liable-for-aiding-and-abetting-copyright-infringement-of-one-piece/)). Total damages for the four works were calculated at approximately ¥3.6 billion (about $24 million), but because the plaintiffs filed a partial claim the court ordered payment of about ¥500 million (about $3.2–3.3 million) plus interest ([AIPPI](https://www.aippi.org/news/dont-aid-pirates-the-court-found-cloudflare-liable-for-aiding-and-abetting-copyright-infringement-of-one-piece/)). The publishers welcomed the decision; Cloudflare expressed strong disappointment, warning it could affect the efficiency, security and reliability of the internet and stifle innovation ([AIPPI](https://www.aippi.org/news/dont-aid-pirates-the-court-found-cloudflare-liable-for-aiding-and-abetting-copyright-infringement-of-one-piece/)).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Tokyo a city or a prefecture?

Both, in a sense. Tokyo is officially the Tokyo Metropolis (Tokyo-to), a prefecture-level entity, but the name is also used for the city formed by its 23 special wards, which had 9,733,276 residents in 2020 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Tokyo)). The wider Greater Tokyo agglomeration, spilling into neighbouring prefectures, is estimated at about 41.2 million people in 2025.

### What was Tokyo called before 1868?

Edo. It was a small fishing village for centuries before becoming the capital of the Tokugawa shogunate in the Tokugawa period (1603–1867), and it was renamed Tokyo — "eastern capital" — when the imperial capital moved there in the Meiji Restoration of 1868 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Tokyo)).

### Why is Tokyo important to Japan?

As the national capital it is the centre of government and home to the Imperial Palace; economically it is Japan's largest industrial, financial and commercial hub, with many domestic and international corporations headquartered there; and culturally it dominates with its museums, theatres and universities ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Tokyo)).

### What happened in the Tokyo District Court manga case?

On 19 November 2025 the court held Cloudflare liable for aiding copyright infringement by providing CDN services to two manga piracy sites, and ordered about ¥500 million in damages to four major publishers, after finding Cloudflare had failed to verify customer identities or act on infringement notices ([AIPPI](https://www.aippi.org/news/dont-aid-pirates-the-court-found-cloudflare-liable-for-aiding-and-abetting-copyright-infringement-of-one-piece/)).

### How big is Tokyo compared with other cities?

By its Greater Tokyo agglomeration estimate of 41.2 million in 2025, Britannica ranks it the third-largest urban agglomeration in the world, behind Guangzhou and Shanghai ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Tokyo)).
