## Overview

Sudan is a country in north-eastern Africa, officially the Republic of the Sudan, with its capital at Khartoum and a population of 50,467,278 (2024 estimate) across 1,861,484 square kilometres ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)). It gained independence from Anglo-Egyptian co-rule on 1 January 1956, and military regimes have dominated its politics for most of the period since ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)). Since April 2023 it has been gripped by a war between the Sudan Armed Forces (SAF) and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces (RSF) that has fuelled the world's largest displacement crisis and largest hunger crisis, displacing more than 15 million people ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)). The [United States](/places/united-states/) has determined that both sides committed war crimes, and in January 2025 declared that RSF members and allied militias committed genocide ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)).

## Land and people

Sudan lies in north-eastern Africa, bordering the Red Sea between Egypt and Eritrea, with land boundaries totalling 6,819 km shared with the Central African Republic, Chad, Egypt, Eritrea, Ethiopia, Libya, and South Sudan ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)). The Nile is Sudan's primary water source: its major tributaries, the White Nile and the Blue Nile, meet at Khartoum to form the River Nile, which flows northward through Egypt to the Mediterranean Sea ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)). The terrain is generally a flat, featureless plain, with desert dominating the north and the highest point, Jabal Marrah, at 3,042 metres ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)).

Sudanese Arabs make up approximately 70 percent of the population, alongside Fur, Beja, Nuba, Ingessana, Uduk, Fallata, Masalit, and many others — there are over 500 ethnic groups ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)). The official languages are Arabic and English, and the population is Sunni Muslim with a small Christian minority ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)). Settlement is concentrated along the Nile, around Khartoum, between the Blue and White Nile rivers, and in South Darfur; the north is sparsely populated ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)).

## From independence to Bashir

Modern Sudan's history begins long before independence: the area was the site of the Kingdom of Kerma (c. 2500–1500 B.C.) and later the Kingdom of Kush, and after Kush fell the Nubians formed three Christian kingdoms, two of which endured until around 1500 ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)). Arab nomads settled much of Sudan between the 14th and 15th centuries, and an 1899 agreement set up a joint British-Egyptian government that was effectively a British colony ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)). Military regimes favouring Islamic-oriented governments have dominated national politics since independence in 1956 ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)).

During most of the second half of the 20th century Sudan was embroiled in two prolonged civil wars rooted in northern domination of the largely non-Muslim, non-Arab south; the first ended in 1972 and another broke out in 1983 ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)). Peace talks gained momentum in 2002–04, and the final North/South Comprehensive Peace Agreement in 2005 granted southern rebels autonomy for six years, followed by a referendum; South Sudan became independent in 2011 ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)). Sudan also faced conflict in Darfur, Southern Kordofan, and Blue Nile starting in 2003 ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)).

Islamist military leader Omar al-Bashir, who took power in a 1989 coup, armed Sudanese Arab militias known as the Janjaweed to help the SAF counter rebels in Darfur in the early 2000s; the United States, among others, labelled their atrocities against non-Arab communities genocide ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)). Bashir formed the RSF from the Janjaweed to counter other insurgencies, allowing it to seize gold mines and other assets ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)). In 2019, after months of nationwide protests, Bashir's 30-year rule ended when the military forced him out, and economist Abdalla Hamdok was selected to lead a transitional government ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)). The transitional government led reforms and secured US sanctions relief and international debt relief, but in late 2021 the SAF and RSF generals usurped power from it — Sudan's sixth coup since independence — and violently suppressed protests ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)). General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan, chair of the Sovereign Council and commander-in-chief of the SAF, serves as de facto head of state and government ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)).

## The war since April 2023

The war began as a fight for power between the SAF, led by Burhan, and the RSF, led by Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo, known as Hemedti, after pressure to restore civilian authority and merge their forces in security-sector reforms ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)). The RSF seized the capital and much of Darfur in 2023 and advanced into the south-east in 2024, disrupting farming in Sudan's agricultural heartland ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)). Early 2025 was a turning point: the SAF recaptured the south-east and reclaimed war-torn Khartoum in March, while the RSF intensified efforts to take El Fasher, the besieged North Darfur capital, and in May launched a drone assault on Port Sudan, the country's interim capital and main gateway for aid ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)).

The humanitarian scale is the war's defining fact: more than 15 million people displaced, including over 11 million internally, 17 million children out of school, and more than half the population — roughly 25 million people — acutely food insecure, with famine spreading ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)). Mortality figures are not reliable, but by some estimates as many as 150,000 people may have died in the first year of the conflict alone ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)). Experts confirm famine, possibly the worst in decades, is spreading, and both warring parties have been accused of using starvation as a weapon and of obstructing aid ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)).

## Sanctions and international response

In late 2023 the State Department issued a determination that the RSF and SAF had committed war crimes and that members of the RSF and allied militia had committed crimes against humanity and ethnic cleansing; the [United Nations](/organizations/united-nations/)-authorised Independent International Fact-Finding Mission for Sudan reached similar findings ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)). On 7 January 2025 the State Department declared that members of the RSF and allied militias had committed acts of genocide, and the administration sanctioned RSF leader Hemedti for his role in systemic atrocities and mass rape in Darfur; a week later it sanctioned SAF leader Burhan, citing the SAF's atrocities and aid obstruction ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)). The Prosecutor of the International Criminal Court is investigating the situation in Darfur under a 2005 United Nations Security Council referral ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)).

The international response has been widely described as inadequate: the humanitarian response for 2025, estimated at USD 4.2 billion, was 13% funded, and the United States — Sudan's largest humanitarian donor, providing over USD 1 billion in FY2024 aid — had announced no new US aid pledges in 2025 at the time of the CRS report ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)). Peace initiatives have proliferated without stopping the fighting, and some observers warn that Africa's third-largest country risks becoming a failed state ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)). Foreign powers have reportedly taken sides, including the UAE, named by UN sanctions monitors in 2024 as involved in aiding the RSF, and [Iran](/places/iran/), Qatar, Egypt, and [Turkey](/places/turkey/), which have reportedly supported the SAF ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the capital of Sudan?**
Khartoum, at the meeting of the White Nile and Blue Nile, with an urban population of 6.344 million in 2023. It is Sudan's capital and largest city ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)).

**When did Sudan become independent?**
On 1 January 1956, from Anglo-Egyptian co-rule. Military regimes have dominated its politics for most of the period since ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/sudan.html)).

**What caused the war that began in 2023?**
A fight for power between the Sudan Armed Forces and the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces, after the two generals who seized power in 2021 came under pressure to restore civilian rule and merge their forces. Its roots lie in how Sudan has been ruled since independence ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)).

**How large is the humanitarian crisis?**
More than 15 million people are displaced, including over 11 million inside Sudan, and roughly 25 million face acute food insecurity, with famine spreading. It is the world's largest displacement and hunger crisis ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)).

**Has the United States sanctioned Sudan's warring leaders?**
Yes. In January 2025 the US sanctioned RSF leader Hemedti over genocide and atrocities in Darfur, and a week later sanctioned SAF leader Burhan over atrocities and aid obstruction ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF12816)).
