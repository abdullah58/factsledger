## Overview

Silicon Valley is a technology region in Northern [California](/places/california/), in the southern San Francisco Bay Area of the [United States](/places/united-states/). It is not a city and has no official boundary: the regional think tank Joint Venture defines it broadly, as a phenomenon extending from [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) to San Jose and Fremont, taking in at least 40 cities across five counties ([Joint Venture Silicon Valley](https://jointventure.org/about-us/faq)). Its global reputation rests on a century of reinvention — radio, vacuum tubes, semiconductors, personal computing, networking and the internet — with each wave building on the skills and infrastructure of the last.

## A century of new industries

Silicon Valley did not happen overnight. Its roots trace to the area around Stanford University in the late 1900s, when early work in radio communications led to a succession of technology businesses, and the region has since "thrived by inventing a significant new industry about every 10 years since the 1930s" ([Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/blog/silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation/?key=silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation)). In 1908 Stanford graduate Cyril Elwell demonstrated wireless conversation over almost a mile in Palo Alto, and in 1909 he founded the Federal Telegraph Company, which installed the US Navy's first global-scale radio system. The 1930s brought vacuum-tube manufacturing, the 1940s defence and microwave work, and in 1956 IBM's San Jose laboratory created RAMAC, the world's first disk drive — about a ton in weight, storing some 4 MB ([Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/blog/silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation/?key=silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation)).

The semiconductor era gave the region its name. In 1956 William Shockley, co-inventor of the transistor, brought silicon from Bell Labs to his Mountain View startup; a year later eight of his scientists left to found Fairchild Semiconductor in Palo Alto, where Robert Noyce adapted the planar process into the integrated circuit and Gordon Moore's observation about transistor density became Moore's Law ([Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/blog/silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation/?key=silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation)). In 1971 journalist Don Hoefler used the nickname "Silicon Valley" for the first time in print, and the name stuck. That same year Intel, a Fairchild spin-off, introduced the microprocessor, launching personal computing; Apple followed with the Apple II and Macintosh, Xerox's Palo Alto Research Center produced the Alto's mouse and graphical interface and the Ethernet networking system, and in the 1990s Cisco Systems — founded by Stanford's Leonard Bosack and Sandy Lerner — came to dominate routers as the internet grew ([Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/blog/silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation/?key=silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation)).

## Why the valley kept reinventing itself

The pattern was not luck but infrastructure that compounded. Stanford University sat at the centre of it: engineering professor Frederick Terman urged students to start businesses locally instead of moving east, and in 1936 a $1,000 donation from vacuum-tube pioneer Charles Litton funded the assistant position that, in Terman's words, "ultimately financed David Packard to come back to Stanford", where he and William Hewlett founded Hewlett-Packard in 1939 ([Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/blog/silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation/?key=silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation)). Stanford created the Stanford Industrial Park — Varian Associates became its first tenant — leasing university land to technology companies and tightening the loop between academic research and commercial products. Each generation also trained the next: Federal Telegraph "bred other startups and trained engineers and technicians for a growing industry cluster", and Fairchild alone produced more than 120 spin-off companies ([Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/blog/silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation/?key=silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation)). Employee stock ownership, introduced at Fairchild, became a pattern for future entrepreneurs, and early venture financing from Stanford faculty may have been the valley's first high-tech venture capital deal.

## Silicon Valley and the wider Bay Area

The name now means more than the Santa Clara Valley. Joint Venture Silicon Valley, the region's think tank, is explicit that the phenomenon "is taking place throughout the entire Bay Area", and its own footprint runs from San Francisco to San Jose and Fremont ([Joint Venture Silicon Valley](https://jointventure.org/about-us/faq)). That breadth matters for the companies of the internet era: [Cloudflare](/organizations/cloudflare/), for example, is headquartered in San Francisco, at the northern end of that Bay Area footprint ([Shueisha Inc.](https://www.shueisha.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/notice20251119eng.pdf)), operating in the same regional ecosystem of Stanford, venture capital and serial founders that the valley built. The 2000s mobile era illustrates the continuity: Apple dropped "Computer" from its name and moved from the iPod in 2001 through the iPhone and iPad, while more than 80% of devices sold worldwide run on Google's Android operating system — both lineages tracing back to the valley's earlier waves ([Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/blog/silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation/?key=silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation)).

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is Silicon Valley?

In the southern San Francisco Bay Area of Northern California, centred on the southern end of the bay. Joint Venture Silicon Valley defines it broadly, from San Francisco to San Jose and Fremont, taking in at least 40 cities and five counties ([Joint Venture Silicon Valley](https://jointventure.org/about-us/faq)).

### Is Silicon Valley a city?

No. It is not a city or an official administrative region, and it has no official boundary. It is a name for a technology ecosystem made up of many cities, including Palo Alto, Mountain View, San Jose and Menlo Park — all named in the Computer History Museum's history of the region ([Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/blog/silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation/?key=silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation)).

### How did Silicon Valley get its name?

From silicon, the material of the semiconductor chips that its companies manufactured. Journalist Don Hoefler first used the nickname "Silicon Valley" in print in 1971, in a series of articles in the trade paper *Electronic News* ([Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/blog/silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation/?key=silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation)).

### What role did Stanford University play?

A central one. Stanford's Frederick Terman encouraged students to found companies locally, the university leased land for the Stanford Industrial Park in 1951, and its research labs fed companies from Hewlett-Packard in 1939 to Cisco in the 1980s ([Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/blog/silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation/?key=silicon-valley-a-century-of-entrepreneurial-innovation)).

### Is Cloudflare based in Silicon Valley?

Cloudflare is headquartered in San Francisco ([Shueisha Inc.](https://www.shueisha.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/notice20251119eng.pdf)), at the northern end of the Bay Area footprint that Joint Venture counts as Silicon Valley — part of the same regional tech ecosystem, though outside the narrow Santa Clara Valley definition.

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not fix a single boundary for Silicon Valley: the broad Joint Venture footprint (San Francisco to San Jose and Fremont) and the narrower Santa Clara County definition both exist, and the profile reports both.
- It does not assert current figures for the region's population, employment or venture funding — the sources read in full are historical, and no current index figures were verified this pass.
- It does not claim the "85% of internet traffic" figure for Cisco is current; it is an analysts' claim cited by the Computer History Museum from 2015-era sourcing.
- It does not cover the 2020s AI era in detail: the museum history used here was updated in 2019.
- "Silicon Valley" here means the Northern California technology region — not the television series of the same name.
