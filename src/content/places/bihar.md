## Overview

**Bihar** is a state in eastern [India](/places/india/) — bordered by Nepal to the north, West Bengal to the northeast, Uttar Pradesh to the west, and [Jharkhand](/places/jharkhand/) to the south — whose capital and largest city is Patna. The 2011 census counted 104,099,452 people, making it India's most densely populated state and one of its least urbanized. The Ganges cuts it in two, and its soil holds Magadha, Bodh Gaya, Nalanda and the Champaran satyagraha.

That combination — deep antiquity and present-day strain — is the thread of this profile. Bihar was for centuries the seat of imperial powers and the focus of Indian culture and civilization, and its name comes from the Sanskrit *vihara*, a Buddhist monastery, reflecting how prominent such communities were here in ancient times ([Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Bihar/317496)). Today most of its people still live in rural areas, mainly in compact villages in farming regions, and most workers still depend on agriculture ([Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Bihar/317496)).

## Land, rivers and floods

Most of Bihar lies within India's vast Indo-Gangetic Plain. The Ganges flows through the center of the state from west to east and divides it into two regions: the North Bihar Plain, flat and low-lying and prone to flooding, and the South Bihar Plain, which has many hills ([Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Bihar/317496)). Rivers running down from the Himalayas of Nepal — the Ghaghara, the Gandak, the Baghmati, the Kosi and the Mahananda — join the Ganges from the north; in the south, except for the Son River, the rivers are small ([Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Bihar/317496)).

Bihar has three seasons — a hot summer, a mild winter, and a monsoon in between — with almost all of the year's rain falling between June and September, an average of 100–150 centimeters a year, the north being wettest ([Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Bihar/317496)). Flooding is a structural fact of the state, not just bad luck: Himalayan rivers carry rock and soil that settles on riverbeds, raising them and shrinking channel capacity; the flat land drains slowly; and embankments built along rivers, especially the Kosi, have eroded and sometimes failed ([Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Bihar/317496)). Global warming has intensified monsoon rainstorms and sped up Himalayan glacier melt, adding to river volumes ([Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Bihar/317496)).

## Magadha, the Buddha and Gandhi

The area that is now Bihar was the site of the ancient kingdoms of Videha and Magadha in the 2nd and 1st millennia BCE. It was ruled by the Mauryan emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE and the Gupta dynasty in the 4th–6th centuries CE, and a series of Muslim dynasties controlled the area for several hundred years starting about 1200 ([Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Bihar/317496)). The British took control in 1765 and governed the region as part of Bengal; Bihar was an important center of the Indian Mutiny of 1857–58, and it was where Mohandas K. Gandhi launched his nonviolent resistance movement in the early 20th century ([Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Bihar/317496)). Bihar became a separate province of British India in 1936 and a state of independent India in 1950; in 2000 most of its southern region became the new state of Jharkhand ([Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Bihar/317496)).

Gandhi's campaign was the Champaran satyagraha. Bihar leaders approached him at the Congress meeting in Lucknow in 1916, asking him to take up the atrocities on farmers in Champaran; he arrived in Patna on April 10, 1917, reached Motihari five days later, and on April 17 set off on the satyagraha to better the lot of the indigo farmers ([The Hindu](http://www.thehindu.com/news/national/where-is-gandhis-satyagraha-centenary-in-champaran/article17759420.ece)). He stayed for months at the Bhitiharwa ashram with his wife Kasturba, one of the sites later listed on a Gandhian tourist circuit ([The Hindu](http://www.thehindu.com/news/national/where-is-gandhis-satyagraha-centenary-in-champaran/article17759420.ece)).

The sacred geography is as layered as the political history. Gaya is an important center for Hindu pilgrims; nearby Bodh Gaya is the most sacred place in Buddhism, where the Buddha attained enlightenment, and its Mahabodhi temple complex is a UNESCO World Heritage site ([Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Bihar/317496)). At Nalanda are the ruins of a renowned ancient Buddhist monastery complex that long served as an important center of learning, and Pawapuri, sacred to Jains, is where the reformer Mahavira achieved enlightenment ([Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Bihar/317496)).

Nalanda's scale explains why it still anchors Bihar's identity: founded by Kumaragupta I of the Gupta dynasty in the 5th century CE, it drew patronage from Harshavardhana of Kannauj in the 7th century and the Pala rulers of the 8th–12th centuries, and was patronized for an unbroken 800 years ([Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=147142&reg=48&lang=2)). Its curriculum covered Buddhism alongside logic, grammar, science and medicine, and admission followed rigorous evaluation ([Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=147142&reg=48&lang=2)). The Archaeological Survey of India excavated and consolidated the site from 1915 to 1937 and again from 1974 to 1982 ([Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=147142&reg=48&lang=2)).

## The growth paradox: fast growth, lowest incomes

Agriculture remains the base: the major staple crops are rice, wheat, maize and pulses, with sugarcane, jute, chilies and tobacco as cash crops, and Bihar is one of India's leading producers of fruits and vegetables ([Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Bihar/317496)). Its most important manufactures include processed foods, steel, chemicals, petroleum products, cement and textiles ([Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Bihar/317496)).

The Bihar Economic Survey 2025–26 presents a paradox: Bihar's GSDP grew 13.1% at current prices in 2024–25, against India's 9.8%, and 8.6% at constant prices against India's 6.5% — yet its per-capita income stayed the country's lowest, at just over Rs 70,000 — fast growth that has not yet translated into average incomes ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/patna-news/bihar-eco-survey-presents-paradox-higher-growth-than-nation-but-lowest-per-capita-income-101770034774078.html)). The outperformance is not new: GSDP grew 17.9% in 2022–23 and 14.9% in 2023–24 at current prices, against India's 14.0% and 12.0% ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/patna-news/bihar-eco-survey-presents-paradox-higher-growth-than-nation-but-lowest-per-capita-income-101770034774078.html)). The gap inside the state is stark — Patna district recorded the highest annual per-capita GSDP at Rs 1.31 lakh, while Sheohar recorded the lowest at Rs 24,332 ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/patna-news/bihar-eco-survey-presents-paradox-higher-growth-than-nation-but-lowest-per-capita-income-101770034774078.html)). Capital expenditure has risen to 22.3% of total spending, per-capita energy consumption has risen from 134 kWh in 2011–12 to 374 kWh in 2024–25, and road transport has grown 13.4% a year over 15 years ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/patna-news/bihar-eco-survey-presents-paradox-higher-growth-than-nation-but-lowest-per-capita-income-101770034774078.html)).

Two recent counts frame Bihar's politics. The state government's 2023 caste-based survey counted 130,725,310 people and found 63% in the Other Backward Class (27.12%) and Extremely Backward Classes (36.01%) categories, with Scheduled Castes at 19.65%, Scheduled Tribes at 1.68% and unreserved groups at 15.52% ([News18](https://www.news18.com/explainers/caste-data-in-centres-census-how-is-it-different-from-state-surveys-how-will-it-help-ws-kl-9318939.html)). Bihar was the first state in independent India to enumerate all castes — caste data apart from SCs and STs had not been collected in the national census since 1931 ([News18](https://www.news18.com/explainers/caste-data-in-centres-census-how-is-it-different-from-state-surveys-how-will-it-help-ws-kl-9318939.html)).

In the 2020 Assembly election, the NDA won 125 of 243 seats — BJP 74, JD(U) 43, VIP 4, HAM 4 — just above the 122 majority mark, while the Mahagathbandhan won 110, with the RJD the single largest party at 75 seats on over 23% of the popular vote ([Firstpost](https://www.firstpost.com/politics/bihar-election-result-2020-nda-wins-bihar-with-clear-majority-rjd-emerges-single-largest-party-with-75-seats-mgb-bag-110-9000041.html)). The count was contested: the RJD alleged pressure on district officials in close seats, and the Election Commission responded that where the victory margin is smaller than the number of rejected postal ballots, reverification of those ballots is mandatory and must be videographed ([Firstpost](https://www.firstpost.com/politics/bihar-election-result-2020-nda-wins-bihar-with-clear-majority-rjd-emerges-single-largest-party-with-75-seats-mgb-bag-110-9000041.html)). The profile of the [2020 Deputy Chairman election](/events/2020-deputy-chairman-rajya-sabha-election/) covers how that year's Rajya Sabha contest was framed against Bihar's election.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Bihar known for?**
Bihar is known for the ancient kingdoms of Magadha and Videha, the Mauryan and Gupta empires, the Buddha's enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, Mahavira at Pawapuri, the Nalanda university, Gandhi's 1917 Champaran satyagraha, and the Ganges plain — a history that makes it one of India's oldest continuously significant regions.

**How many people live in Bihar?**
The 2011 Census of India counted 104,099,452 people in Bihar, making it India's most densely populated state. The state government's 2023 caste survey counted 130,725,310 people, but that is a survey figure, not a Census of India count, so the two totals are not directly comparable.

**Why does Bihar flood almost every year?**
Himalayan rivers carry sediment that raises riverbeds and shrinks channels, the flat North Bihar Plain drains slowly, and embankments — especially on the Kosi — have eroded and sometimes failed. Monsoon rain from June to September, intensified by warming and faster glacier melt, turns that geography into annual flooding.

**What happened in Bihar's 2020 Assembly election?**
The NDA won 125 of 243 seats — BJP 74, JD(U) 43, VIP 4, HAM 4 — just clearing the 122 majority mark. The Mahagathbandhan won 110 seats, with the RJD the single largest party at 75 seats. The count was disputed, with the RJD alleging pressure on officials and the Election Commission citing mandatory postal-ballot reverification.

**What did Bihar's 2023 caste survey find?**
The survey counted 130,725,310 people and found 63% in the OBC (27.12%) and EBC (36.01%) categories, with Scheduled Castes at 19.65%, Scheduled Tribes at 1.68% and unreserved groups at 15.52%. Bihar was the first state in independent India to enumerate all castes.
