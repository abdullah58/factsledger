# Iowa

**Iowa is a Midwestern U.S. state of about 3.24 million people (July 1,
2025 estimate), admitted to the Union as the 29th state on December 28,
1846.** More than nine-tenths of its land is devoted to agriculture, and
it remains the nation's leading producer of corn — with a record
2.77-billion-bushel harvest in 2025 — while also growing into a major
insurance and financial-services hub centered on Greater Des Moines.

## A Midwestern state built on agriculture

Iowa is bounded by the Mississippi River on its entire eastern border and
the Missouri River (with its tributary the Big Sioux) on the west —
the only U.S. state with two parallel rivers as borders. The capital is
Des Moines, the state's largest city (214,133 in 2020); other large
cities include Cedar Rapids, Iowa City, Waterloo, and Sioux City. The
state name derives from the Iowa Native American people who once
inhabited the area.

The numbers tell the farm story. In 2022, Iowa generated about $46.6
billion in agricultural cash receipts — corn $16.2B, hogs $11.2B,
soybeans $9.0B, cattle and calves $5.5B, chicken eggs $2.0B — from 30
million acres of farmland (84% of the state) spread over 86,800 farms
averaging 346 acres. Agricultural production and processing industries
accounted for 12.6% of total state GDP (University of Arkansas Division
of Agriculture). Top farm exports: soybeans, corn, and pork.

2025 set records. Despite derecho winds, disease, and record rainfall,
Iowa farmers harvested a record 2.77 billion bushels of corn — beating
the 2016 record of 2.74B — at an average yield of 210 bushels per acre,
and 595.6 million bushels of soybeans, the third-largest Iowa harvest on
record, with a record yield of 63.5 bushels per acre. Iowa remains the
largest U.S. corn grower, 418 million bushels ahead of second-place
Illinois, and the second-biggest soybean grower. About half of the
state's corn crop goes to biofuel: Iowa produced 4.6 billion gallons of
ethanol in 2025, the most nationally (USDA data via the Des Moines
Register, January 2026).

## Beyond the farm: insurance, finance, and diversification

The 1980s collapse of farm and land values forced Iowa to diversify, and
by the end of the 20th century the state was emphasizing banking,
insurance, biotechnology, and R&D (Britannica). The centerpiece is
Greater Des Moines: finance and insurance contributed $25.3 billion of
the region's $90 billion GDP in 2024, and the region has the highest
concentration of insurance employment among U.S. metros, with more than
80 of Iowa's insurance companies headquartered there — including
Principal, Nationwide, EMC, Athene, and F&G. Iowa's insurance premium
tax is being lowered from 1% to 0.9% by 2027, among the lowest in the
nation (Greater Des Moines Partnership).

## Politics and public life

Since 1972, Iowa's caucuses have been the first in the nation to select
delegates for presidential nominating conventions, giving the state an
outsized role in U.S. presidential politics every four years. The state
holds 4 of 435 seats in the U.S. House of Representatives and sits in the
Central time zone. The University of Iowa was the first public
university in the country to admit men and women on an equal basis.

## FAQ

**Why is Iowa called the Hawkeye State?**
It is one of two official nicknames (the other is the Corn State), per
Britannica; the state motto is "Our Liberties We Prize and Our Rights We
Will Maintain."

**How big is Iowa's corn harvest?**
A record 2.77 billion bushels in 2025 — enough to keep Iowa the largest
corn grower in the United States, 418 million bushels ahead of Illinois.

**What does Iowa export?**
Mainly soybeans, corn, and pork — chiefly to Canada, Mexico, and Japan.

**Is Iowa only about farming?**
No. Agriculture is the signature, but finance and insurance are the
other pillar: Greater Des Moines is one of the top U.S. insurance
centers, with finance/insurance contributing $25.3 billion to the
region's 2024 GDP.

**When is Iowa's first-in-the-nation political role?**
Iowa's caucuses have kicked off presidential-nominating delegate
selection since 1972.
