## Overview

Paris is the capital and largest city of France, standing on the Seine in the north-central part of the country at the centre of the Île-de-France region. The city proper covers 105 square kilometres and had an estimated 2,145,906 residents in 2020, while its urban agglomeration held about 10,858,874. Paris serves as France's leading centre of commerce and culture ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)).

For centuries Paris has ranked among the world's most important and attractive cities, prized for business, study, culture and entertainment ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). Its nickname, the 'City of Light' ('la Ville Lumière'), dates to the Enlightenment and still fits a city that remains a centre of education and intellectual life ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). This profile covers its history, layout, government, culture and green spaces.

## History

People were living on the site of present-day Paris by about 7600 BCE, and under Roman administration in the 1st century BCE the original settlement on the Île de la Cité became the capital of the Parisii tribe and territory ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). The Frankish king Clovis I had taken Paris from the Gauls by 494 CE and later made it his capital ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). Under Hugh Capet, who ruled from 987 to 996, and the Capetian dynasty that followed, the preeminence of Paris was firmly established, and the city became the political and cultural hub as modern France took shape ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). France has long been highly centralised, and Paris came to be identified with a powerful central state that drew talent and vitality from the provinces ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). The years 1358, 1382, 1588, 1648, 1789, 1830, 1848 and 1871 stand out for great social and political violence in the city ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)).

## Geography and layout

Paris occupies a central position in the Paris Basin, a rich agricultural region, and forms one of eight départements of the Île-de-France administrative region ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). The city proper is small: no corner lies farther than about 6 miles (10 km) from the square in front of Notre-Dame Cathedral ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). It sits in a depression hollowed out by the Seine, with heights around it treated as the city's limits; elevation runs from 130 metres at the butte of Montmartre in the north to 26 metres at Grenelle in the south-west ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). The Seine flows for about 8 miles (13 km) through the centre of the city and through 10 of its 20 arrondissements, entering at the south-east corner and leaving at the south-west ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). Parisians call its banks the Right Bank and the Left Bank, facing downstream, because the river's bends change which compass direction each bank faces ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). Paris weather is changeable: the annual average is about 12 °C, with July averaging about 19 °C and January about 3 °C ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)).

## Government and the arrondissements

Paris comprises 20 arrondissements, or municipal districts, each with its own mayor, town hall and particular features ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). Their numbering begins in the heart of Paris and continues in the spiralling shape of a snail shell, ending in the far east, and Parisians refer to them by number — the first, second, third and so on ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). The Île de la Cité at the centre is the seat of religious and temporal authority, the nucleus of the ancient city ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). The city's boundaries have spread outward over the centuries to absorb surrounding towns, usually built around monasteries or churches and often the site of a market ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). Growth ran mainly eastward from the mid-14th to the mid-16th century and has run westward since ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). Adapting to urbanisation — immigration, housing, social infrastructure, public utilities, suburban development and zoning — has produced the vast urban agglomeration around the small historic core ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)).

## Culture, commerce and the 'City of Light'

Paris is by far France's most important centre of commerce and culture ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). Its gastronomy, haute couture, painting, literature and intellectual community enjoy an enviable reputation, and the city is appreciated for the opportunities it offers for business, commerce, study, culture and entertainment ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). The Seine divides historic Paris into three parts: the Île de la Cité at its centre, the seat of religious and temporal authority; the Left Bank, traditionally the seat of intellectual life; and the Right Bank, home to the heart of the city's economic life — though those distinctions have blurred in recent decades ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). The fusion of all these functions at the centre of France, and later at the centre of an empire, produced a tremendously vital environment ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). That blend of commerce, intellect and authority is what the 'City of Light' name points to: a reputation built over centuries, not a single monument or era.

## Parks, the Seine and green space

The garden effect of the Seine's open waters and tree-lined banks helps make Paris look well endowed with green spaces, and tens of thousands of trees — mostly plane trees, with a scattering of chestnuts — line its streets ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). Most parks and gardens of the modern central city stand on land once reserved for kings on the old city's outskirts ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). Under Napoleon III, who had been impressed by [London](/places/london/)'s parks while living in Britain, two ancient royal military preserves became 'English' parks: the Bois de Boulogne in the west and the Bois de Vincennes in the east ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). His reign also laid out large areas of promenades and garden squares, and later mayors, including Jacques Chirac in the late 20th century, continued creating new parks ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)). The Promenade Plantée, a partially elevated parkway along an abandoned rail line and viaduct in the 12th arrondissement, was the world's first elevated park, its first phase completed in 1994 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Paris)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Where is Paris located?**
Paris stands in north-central France on the Seine, about 375 km upstream from the river's mouth on the English Channel, at the centre of the Île-de-France region. The city proper covers 105 sq km, and no corner lies farther than about 10 km from the square in front of Notre-Dame Cathedral.

**Why is Paris called the City of Light?**
The nickname 'la Ville Lumière' was earned during the Enlightenment, when Paris stood at the centre of European intellectual life. It remains appropriate because Paris has kept its importance today as a centre for education and intellectual life and inquiry.

**How is Paris governed?**
Paris comprises 20 arrondissements, each with its own mayor, town hall and particular features. Their numbering starts in the heart of the city and spirals outward like a snail shell, ending in the far east. Paris is also one of eight départements of the Île-de-France administrative region.

**What is the Promenade Plantée?**
The Promenade Plantée is a partially elevated parkway built along an abandoned rail line and viaduct in the 12th arrondissement, on the right bank of the Seine. It was the world's first elevated park, with its first phase completed in 1994, and it runs about 4.5 km from the Opéra Bastille to the Bois de Vincennes.
