## Overview

Sydney is the capital of New South Wales and [Australia](/places/australia/)'s largest city, built around the deep harbour of Port Jackson on the country's southeastern coast ([Britannica](http://www.britannica.com/EBchecked/topic/577475/Sydney)). It began as a British penal colony at Sydney Cove on 26 January 1788, on the clan lands of the Gadigal people, and grew into a multicultural metropolis of 5,638,800 people at 30 June 2025 ([State Library of New South Wales](https://www2.sl.nsw.gov.au/archive/events/exhibitions/2012/macquarie/02_settlement/index.html); [Australian Bureau of Statistics](https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/capital-city-growth-slows)). Its harbour — a maze of bays and inlets — still defines it: the city is dominated by the bulk of the Harbour Bridge and the white shell roofs of the Opera House, and its port ranks among the most important in the South Pacific ([Britannica](http://www.britannica.com/EBchecked/topic/577475/Sydney)).

## A colony built on Gadigal land

The colony's beginning is well dated but its purpose is not. On 26 January 1788, Governor Arthur Phillip and the First Fleet arrived in Port Jackson to establish a penal colony at Sydney Cove; a fleet of 11 ships carrying just over 700 prisoners, plus guards and administrators, had stepped ashore to peg out what became the most remote European colony on earth ([State Library of New South Wales](https://www2.sl.nsw.gov.au/archive/events/exhibitions/2012/macquarie/02_settlement/index.html); [Museums of History NSW](https://mhnsw.au/stories/convict-sydney/why-sydney/)). Why Britain chose this spot is not known for sure: business, a naval foothold, overcrowded jails, and reform through hard work are competing theories, and the museum account refuses to pick one ([Museums of History NSW](https://mhnsw.au/stories/convict-sydney/why-sydney/)).

The human cost was immediate. Aboriginal peoples had lived in Australia for at least 40,000 years, and the Sydney region supported a dense population of clans, while the new colony struggled for food on infertile soil ([State Library of New South Wales](https://www2.sl.nsw.gov.au/archive/events/exhibitions/2012/macquarie/02_settlement/index.html)). A smallpox epidemic in 1789 left the Cadigal clan, on whose land the settlement stood, with only three people by 1791 ([State Library of New South Wales](https://www2.sl.nsw.gov.au/archive/events/exhibitions/2012/macquarie/02_settlement/index.html)). Yet Sydney survived as a port: while still a small convict settlement in the early 19th century, it had already established trade with the Pacific Islands, India, China, South Africa and the Americas ([Britannica](http://www.britannica.com/EBchecked/topic/577475/Sydney)).

Geography shaped what followed. Greater Sydney stretches from the Blue Mountains to the Pacific and from Lake Macquarie to south of Botany Bay, with a compact business district on a rocky peninsula between two arms of the harbour, in contrast to sprawling suburbs ([Britannica](http://www.britannica.com/EBchecked/topic/577475/Sydney)). Its climate — a mean of about 22 °C in January and 13 °C in July, with about 1,200 mm of rain a year — encouraged sailing, swimming and surfing ([Britannica](http://www.britannica.com/EBchecked/topic/577475/Sydney)).

## The harbour's two icons — and the Games that remade a suburb

Two structures, built forty years apart, became Sydney's shorthand. The Harbour Bridge was approved in 1922 and built from 1924; about 1,400 men worked on it, and it opened on 19 March 1932 with a deck 1,149 metres long and 48.8 metres wide, then the widest long-span bridge in the world ([Australian Screen Online](https://aso.gov.au/titles/documentaries/construction-sydney-harbour/clip3/)). The Opera House followed a different path to the same status: Danish architect Jorn Utzon won a 1957 design competition, construction took 14 years, and the centre opened on 20 October 1973 on the land of the Gadigal people of the Eora nation, becoming a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007 ([National Film and Sound Archive](https://www.nfsa.gov.au/collection/curated/sydney-opera-house)).

The 2000 Olympic Games gave the city a third, less photographed legacy. More than 10,000 athletes from 199 nations competed in 300 medal events, with new sports including trampoline, taekwondo and triathlon ([U.S. Olympic & Paralympic Museum](https://usopm.org/sydney-2000-olympic-games-american-gold-medalists/)). Beyond the medal moments — Cathy Freeman's 400 m gold remains a defining image, particularly as an Indigenous Australian on home soil — the Games transformed the former industrial site at Homebush Bay into Sydney Olympic Park, with green spaces, housing and venues still serving elite sport and the community, while the Sydney Marathon, first run as a test event in 2000, is now part of the World Marathon Majors ([Inside the Games](https://www.insidethegames.biz/articles/ioc-kirsty-coventry-sydney-2000-spark)).

## A multicultural city that now exports companies

Postwar migration remade Sydney more thoroughly than any single building. The influx turned Australia from a previously monocultural British-oriented society into one of the world's most multicultural societies, the big cities received the bulk of postwar immigration, and Sydney eventually proved more attractive than Melbourne, with the overseas-born accounting for about one-third of its population at the start of the 21st century ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Australia/The-advent-of-multicultural-society)). That growth has not stopped: Sydney's population reached 5,638,800 at 30 June 2025, up 75,200 (1.4 percent) in a year, with net overseas migration remaining the main driver and the largest gains in outer-suburban areas ([Australian Bureau of Statistics](https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/capital-city-growth-slows)).

The least-covered part of that economy is the one this profile's source article points to. [Afterpay](/organizations/afterpay/) was co-founded in Sydney in 2014 by [Anthony Eisen](/people/anthony-eisen/) and [Nick Molnar](/people/nick-molnar/), who built it in his Sydney home, as a buy-now-pay-later service charging retailers rather than interest to shoppers ([AFP via TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2021-08-afterpay-billion-aussie-startup.html?deviceType=mobile)). By March 2018 its main office was in Sydney's Surry Hills, it offered payment in four instalments, and it was already a $1 billion "unicorn" ([University of Sydney](http://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2018/03/26/a-breakthrough-business-thats-changing-how-we-shop.html)). The founders had met as neighbours, and their company went on to be bought by [Square](/organizations/block/) in a US$29 billion deal announced in 2021 ([AFP via TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2021-08-afterpay-billion-aussie-startup.html?deviceType=mobile)). Sydney, in other words, does not only import people and export postcards: it exports companies too.
