## Overview

Mexico, officially the United Mexican States, is a federal republic in southern North America with an estimated 130.7 million people in 2024 — about 135.7 million on Britannica's 2026 estimate — making it the world's tenth most populous country and home to the largest population of Spanish speakers anywhere, roughly two and a half times that of [Spain](/places/spain/) or Colombia ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Mexico); [CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). Its capital, Mexico City, is one of the most populous metropolitan areas in the world, with 22.281 million people in 2023, and about a quarter of all Mexicans live in and around it ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). Mexico is the [United States](/places/united-states/)' second-largest goods trading partner, after [Canada](/places/canada/), under the US-Mexico-Canada Agreement that entered into force in 2020 ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). For the European booking-platform story, Mexico matters as the one market [Booksy](/organizations/booksy/) entered by acquisition rather than by launch: it merged with Polish rival Versum in December 2020, and the deal carried Booksy into Mexico ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)).

Mexico covers 1,964,375 square kilometres — slightly less than three times the size of Texas — bounded by the Pacific Ocean to the west and south, the Gulf of Mexico and the Caribbean Sea to the east, the United States to the north, and Guatemala and Belize to the southeast ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Mexico); [CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). Its official name reflects its structure: 31 socially and physically diverse states plus the Federal District, governed as a federal republic with a 128-seat Senate and a 500-seat Chamber of Deputies ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Mexico)). More than four-fifths of Mexicans now live in urban areas, and the population is young by rich-country standards, with a median age of 31 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Mexico); [CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)).

## From ancient civilisations to a modern republic

Mexico was the site of several advanced Amerindian civilisations — including the Olmec, Toltec, Teotihuacan, Zapotec, Maya and Aztec — until Spain conquered and colonised the area in the early 16th century ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). Administered as the Viceroyalty of New Spain for three centuries, it achieved independence early in the 19th century, after the war that began in 1810 ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). The cultural remnants of those pre-Columbian civilisations — Teotihuacán, the Mayan pyramids at Chichén Itzá and Tulum — still stand alongside colonial towns such as Taxco and Querétaro, and the capital itself has been continually built and rebuilt on the rubble of past civilisations ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Mexico)).

The 20th century turned on the Mexican Revolution of 1910, and its political order lasted most of the century: elections in 2000 marked the first time since the Revolution that an opposition candidate, Vicente Fox of the National Action Party, defeated the governing Institutional Revolutionary Party ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). The left-leaning Andrés Manuel López Obrador, of the National Regeneration Movement (MORENA), became president in 2018, and his successor Claudia Sheinbaum won the June 2024 election with nearly 60% of the vote — the highest share ever for a Mexican presidential candidate — taking office on October 1, 2024 as the country's first woman president and its first president of Jewish heritage ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Claudia-Sheinbaum)).

## An economy wired into the United States

Mexico is an upper-middle-income economy, highly integrated with the US through trade and nearshore manufacturing, with weak domestic demand, fiscal consolidation and trade uncertainty contributing to sluggish growth ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). The numbers show both the scale and the dependence: real GDP on a purchasing-power-parity basis was $2.883 trillion in 2024, growing 1.5%, with GDP per capita at $22,000; on official exchange rates GDP was $1.853 trillion ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). Services account for 58.2% of output, industry 31.6% and agriculture 3.8%, and the labour force stood at 60.959 million with unemployment of just 2.8% in 2024 ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)).

The trade relationship is the defining fact. Mexico exported $680.798 billion of goods in 2024, of which 76% went to the United States, led by cars, vehicle parts, crude petroleum, trucks and computers; it imported $697.067 billion, 46% of it from the US ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). That integration is the point of the USMCA, which replaced NAFTA in 2020, and it is also why "nearshoring" — manufacturing moving closer to the US market — is the central economic story of the 2020s ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). The dependence cuts both ways: the Factbook lists low real wages, high underemployment, inequitable income distribution and few advancement opportunities, particularly for the largely Indigenous population in the impoverished southern states, among the ongoing concerns, alongside cartel violence that has produced tens of thousands of drug-related homicides since 2007 ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). Remittances from Mexicans abroad equalled 3.7% of GDP in 2024, and 36.3% of the population lived below the poverty line in 2022 ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)).

## Why Mexico mattered to Booksy

Booksy's six markets as of January 2021 were the US — its largest — the [United Kingdom](/places/united-kingdom/), [Poland](/places/poland/), Spain, [Brazil](/places/brazil/) and [South Africa](/places/south-africa/), with a reported 13 million consumers on the app ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)). Mexico arrived differently from the others: not through a launch, but through the December 2020 merger with Versum, a leading Polish competitor, which "allow[ed] it to enter Mexico" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)). That made Mexico the test case for the roll-up strategy TechCrunch identified as Booksy's next phase — using its funding "to roll-up other local players" rather than out-marketing them city by city ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)).

The logic is visible in the demography. A booking platform for hairdressers, barbers and beauty salons needs dense, urban, phone-first consumers — and Mexico offers 81.6% urbanisation, a 22-million-strong capital, and Guadalajara (5.420 million) and Monterrey (5.117 million) as second-tier metros ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). Mobile connectivity supports the model too: Mexico had 140 million mobile cellular subscriptions in 2023, about 100 per 100 inhabitants, and 81% of the population used the internet ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). The same figures also explain the risk: a market where over 90% of appointments were still processed by phone and email, as Booksy's founder argued of the beauty industry generally, is both the opportunity and the adoption challenge.

## Culture, land and heritage

Mexico's physical setting is one of the most dynamic on Earth: it sits on the Ring of Fire, a belt containing about 75% of the world's volcanoes, and its highest point, Citlaltépetl (also called Pico de Orizaba), rises to 5,610 metres, with the active Popocatépetl at 5,465 metres southeast of Mexico City ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Mexico); [CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). The country holds 36 UNESCO World Heritage Sites — 28 cultural, 6 natural and 2 mixed — including the historic centre of Mexico City, Teotihuacán, Chichén Itzá and the Monarch Butterfly Biosphere Reserve ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). Tourism is a significant contributor to the economy, with the capital's historic centre and Teotihuacán designated UNESCO sites in 1987, and beach resorts such as Cancún, Cozumel and Cabo San Lucas now more popular than Acapulco and Puerto Vallarta ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Mexico)).

The population's composition reflects the colonial blending Britannica describes: mestizos — the mixture of Indigenous and European peoples — account for more than three-fifths of the population, with Indigenous peoples under one-tenth and Mexicans of European heritage a significant remainder ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Mexico); [CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). Spanish is spoken by 93.8% of the population, with 5.4% speaking Spanish and Indigenous languages, and Roman Catholics made up 77.7% in 2020 ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)). Everyday culture runs on an ancient trinity of staples — corn, beans and squash — and the calendar mixes Catholic feasts such as the Day of the Dead (November 1–2) and Our Lady of Guadalupe (December 12) with secular holidays including Independence Day on September 16 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Mexico)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is Mexico important in the Booksy story?**
Mexico was the market Booksy entered through acquisition rather than launch: its December 2020 merger with Versum, a leading Polish competitor, carried it into Mexico, making the country the test case for the roll-up strategy behind the $70 million Series C ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)).

**How large is Mexico?**
It covers 1,964,375 square kilometres, slightly less than three times the size of Texas, and its population was estimated at 130.7 million in 2024 (CIA World Factbook) and 135.7 million in 2026 (Britannica) — the world's tenth largest ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Mexico)).

**Who leads Mexico?**
Claudia Sheinbaum, who took office on October 1, 2024 as Mexico's 66th president and its first woman president, after winning the June 2024 election with nearly 60% of the vote ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Claudia-Sheinbaum)).

**How integrated is Mexico's economy with the United States?**
Very: 76% of Mexico's $680.8 billion in 2024 exports went to the US, and the USMCA trade agreement, in force since 2020, replaced NAFTA as the framework for that trade ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)).

**What is Mexico City's scale?**
The capital's metropolitan population was 22.281 million in 2023, and about a quarter of all Mexicans live in and around the capital ([CIA World Factbook](https://archive.govglance.org/factbook/mexico)).
