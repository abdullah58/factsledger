## Overview

Leicestershire is an administrative, geographic and historic county in the East Midlands region of [England](/places/england/), bordered by Nottinghamshire, Lincolnshire, Rutland, Northamptonshire, Warwickshire, Staffordshire and Derbyshire ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/leicestershire)). The administrative county comprises seven districts — Blaby, Harborough, North West Leicestershire, and the boroughs of Charnwood, Hinckley and Bosworth, Melton, and Oadby and Wigston — while the geographic county adds the unitary authority of [Leicester](/places/leicester/) ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/leicestershire)). The administrative county held an estimated 745,573 people in mid-2024, and adding Leicester city's estimated 388,348 gives a geographic county of about 1.13 million ([CityPopulation](http://citypopulation.de/en/uk/admin/E10000018__leicestershire/); [CityPopulation](https://www.citypopulation.de/en/uk/england/admin/east_midlands/E06000016__leicester/)). Its central axis, containing most of its population and industry, is the valley of the River Soar, which crosses the county from south to north on its way to join the River Trent ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/leicestershire)).

Leicestershire is a county where deep time and dinner tables collide. In Charnwood Forest in the west, Precambrian gritstone exposes some of Britain's oldest bedrock, and the 1957 discovery of the Charnia fossil overturned the belief that rocks this old could hold no fossils ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/leicestershire); [Charnwood Forest Geopark](https://www.charnwoodforest.org/discovering-charnia/)). In 1485, Bosworth Field in the county saw the final battle of the Wars of the Roses, killing Richard III and putting the Tudor dynasty on the throne ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/leicestershire)). And in Melton Mowbray, pork pies made since the 1700s carry protected geographical indication status, the edible end of a cheesemaking economy whose whey once fattened the pigs ([National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/what-are-melton-mowbray-pork-pies)).

## A county shaped by the Soar

Leicestershire's geography is a three-part arrangement around its central river. East of the Soar valley lies an upland reaching 300 to 500 feet, a deeply rural and scenic area that is the territory of some of England's most famous foxhunts, such as the Fernie Hunt ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/leicestershire)). West of the valley lie the hills of Charnwood Forest, where Precambrian gritstone — some of Britain's oldest bedrock — is exposed at the surface, with Bardon Hill reaching 912 feet ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/leicestershire)). North and west of the forest lies the Leicestershire coalfield, the site of some of the earliest developments of the Industrial Revolution in canal and rail transport, where mining declined in the second half of the twentieth century and had ceased by the 1980s ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/leicestershire)). The administrative county covers 806 square miles and the geographic county 833 square miles ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/leicestershire)).

The county's human history runs from prehistoric settlement, evidenced by archaeology much of it from Charnwood Forest, through a Roman settlement underlying Leicester, an Angle invasion in the sixth century, membership of the kingdom of Mercia in the seventh and eighth centuries, Danish settlement in the ninth century, and Norman nobles and religious orders sharing control of the land in the later Middle Ages ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/leicestershire)). The administrative, geographic and historic counties occupy slightly different areas: the historic county is nearly coterminous with the geographic county but excludes two small areas, one east of Wymeswold that lies in historic Nottinghamshire and one south of the River Welland at Market Harborough that belongs to historic Northamptonshire ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/leicestershire)).

## Bosworth, fossils and food

The Battle of Bosworth, fought on 22 August 1485, is the county's hinge event: in the final battle of the Wars of the Roses, the Yorkist King Richard III led a cavalry charge and was killed, making his adversary Henry Tudor king of England ([University of Leicester](https://le.ac.uk/richard-iii/grey-friars)). For years historians debated the battlefield's exact location, until a Leicestershire County Council archaeological survey announced the site straddling the Roman road known as Fenn Lane, three kilometres south-west of Ambion Hill, proven by systematic metal detecting that recovered Richard III's own silver-gilt boar badge beside a small medieval marsh — the king, the survey concluded, was killed when his horse became stuck in a mire ([National Lottery Heritage Fund](https://www.heritagefund.org.uk/news/true-location-bosworth-battlefield-pinpointed)). Bosworth Battlefield Heritage Centre, owned by the county council, now exhibits the finds ([National Lottery Heritage Fund](https://www.heritagefund.org.uk/news/true-location-bosworth-battlefield-pinpointed)).

Charnwood's fossils are the county's other world-changing discovery. The forest's rocks had long been recognised as Precambrian and were therefore thought too old to contain fossils, since the oldest fossils known to Victorian geologists came from the Cambrian period ([Charnwood Forest Geopark](https://www.charnwoodforest.org/discovering-charnia/)). In 1956 and 1957, two schoolchildren — Tina Negus and Roger Mason — independently found the same frond-like impression; Trevor Ford published the discovery in 1958, naming the fossil Charnia after the forest, and Negus's role was publicly recognised only at the fiftieth anniversary in 2007 ([Charnwood Forest Geopark](https://www.charnwoodforest.org/discovering-charnia/)). Charnia remains some of the oldest evidence of animal life, key to understanding how animals first evolved more than 560 million years ago ([Charnwood Forest Geopark](https://www.charnwoodforest.org/discovering-charnia/)). The county's food heritage is less ancient but better eaten: Leicestershire's agriculture has traditionally been pastoral and livestock-based, famous for sheep, Stilton cheese and pork pies, while framework knitting, introduced at Hinckley in 1640, made hosiery an industry of special importance ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/leicestershire)).

## The county today

Leicestershire today is a county of country houses rather than of great buildings, with fine medieval churches at Leicester, Lutterworth — where the reformer John Wycliffe was parish priest in the 1380s — Melton Mowbray and Ashby-de-la-Zouch, a cathedral at Leicester, and modern universities at both Leicester and Loughborough ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/leicestershire)). Its population keeps growing: the administrative county rose from 651,179 in 2011 to an estimated 712,632 in 2021 and 745,573 in 2024, with Charnwood the largest district at 188,385 and Harborough the fastest-growing in proportional terms ([CityPopulation](http://citypopulation.de/en/uk/admin/E10000018__leicestershire/)). The National Forest, spanning 200 square miles across Leicestershire, Derbyshire and Staffordshire, passed ten million trees planted in 2026, lifting forest cover in the area from 6 per cent to more than 25 per cent, with a target of 33 per cent ([Forestry Journal](https://www.forestryjournal.co.uk/news/26019668.national-forest-reaches-10-million-trees-landmark-milestone/)). The county also carries the scars of its railway history: on 1 February 2008, at [Barrow upon Soar](/places/barrow-upon-soar/), a tipper lorry demolished a footbridge and the [Nottingham](/places/nottingham/) to [Norwich](/places/norwich/) train derailed — the [Barrow upon Soar rail crash](/events/barrow-upon-soar-rail-crash/), investigated by the Rail Accident Investigation Branch ([RAIB](https://www.gov.uk/raib-reports/collision-of-a-train-with-a-demolished-footbridge-barrow-upon-soar)). [Barrow upon Soar railway station](/places/barrow-upon-soar-railway-station/) stands on the [Midland Main Line](/places/midland-main-line/) through the county, and the county remains, in the [United Kingdom](/places/united-kingdom/)'s railway geography, a corridor county between London, the East Midlands and the North.

Bradgate Park, the county's best-loved country park, condenses Leicestershire's history into 830 acres of publicly accessible countryside close to Leicester city centre. It was first enclosed as a deer park around 800 years ago, and its deer herd of about 550 animals — three-quarters of them fallow deer — descends from a park first recorded in 1241 ([Bradgate Park Trust](https://www.bradgatepark.org/bradgate-park)). Old John Tower, an eighteenth-century folly on the park's highest point, is often called the Icon of Leicestershire, while the ruins of Bradgate House, one of the earliest brick-built country houses in England and the childhood home of Lady Jane Grey, Queen of England for Nine Days in 1553, are protected as a Scheduled Ancient Monument ([Bradgate Park Trust](https://www.bradgatepark.org/bradgate-park)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the county town of Leicestershire?**
Leicester. It is a city and unitary authority within the geographic and historic county, situated on the River Soar and the Grand Union Canal, with a history running from a Roman settlement to the 2012 discovery of Richard III's skeleton under a car park.

**Is Leicester part of Leicestershire?**
Yes and no. Leicester is inside the geographic and historic county of Leicestershire, but it is a separate unitary authority, administered by Leicester City Council rather than Leicestershire County Council. The administrative county covers the seven districts outside the city.

**Where was the Battle of Bosworth fought?**
At Bosworth in Leicestershire, on 22 August 1485. A county council archaeological survey pinpointed the battlefield on Fenn Lane, three kilometres south-west of Ambion Hill, after finding Richard III's silver-gilt boar badge beside a medieval marsh where his horse stuck.

**What is Leicestershire famous for?**
Bosworth Field and the Tudor dynasty, the Charnia fossil in Charnwood Forest, Stilton cheese, and the Melton Mowbray pork pie, which holds protected geographical indication status. Its traditional industries were hosiery, footwear and engineering, and its coalfield was an early Industrial Revolution site.

**How big is Leicestershire?**
The administrative county covers 806 square miles with about 745,573 people (2024 estimate); the geographic county, including Leicester city, covers 833 square miles with about 1.13 million people. Charnwood is the largest district at about 188,385 people, followed by Hinckley and Bosworth at about 116,682.
