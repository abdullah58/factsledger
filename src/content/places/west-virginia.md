## Overview

West Virginia, the Mountain State, is the 35th state of the [United States](/places/united-states/), admitted to the Union on June 20, 1863, with Charleston as its capital and a 2024 estimated population of 1,769,979 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/West-Virginia)). It is the state whose birth was a direct refusal of secession: its western counties defied Virginia's secession convention in 1861 and chose to remain in the Union ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/West-Virginia)).

The state justifies its nickname in every direction. With an average elevation of about 1,500 feet, it is the highest state east of the Mississippi River, and all of it lies within the Appalachian Mountain system, split between the Appalachian Plateau Province in the west and the Ridge and Valley Province in the east ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/West-Virginia)). Spruce Knob, at 4,863 feet, is its highest point, and Harpers Ferry, at 247 feet where the Shenandoah meets the Potomac, is its lowest ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/West-Virginia)). It is bordered by Pennsylvania to the north, Maryland and Virginia to the east, Kentucky to the southwest, and Ohio to the northwest, and it has two panhandles — one reaching north between Pennsylvania and Ohio, the other east between Maryland and Virginia ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/West-Virginia)).

## Born of the Civil War

West Virginia began as the northwestern portion of Virginia, but its inhabitants defied the state's secession convention in 1861, choosing instead to remain within the Union ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/West-Virginia)). Delegates from the western counties met at Wheeling, declared the Ordinance of Secession illegal, established a Restored Government of Virginia aligned with the Union, and called elections in the west ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/West-Virginia)). In October 1861 voters in the proposed new state overwhelmingly approved its creation, and in 1863 Congress consented to its admission on the condition that the state gradually emancipate its slaves ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/West-Virginia)). The new state originally included 48 Virginia counties; in 1866 Congress officially added Berkeley and Jefferson counties, giving West Virginia generally its present boundary ([US Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/geographies/reference-files/2010/geo/state-local-geo-guides-2010/west-virginia.html)). The state motto adopted from that tradition, "Montani semper liberi" — "Mountaineers are always free" — still frames how West Virginians describe themselves ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/West-Virginia)). Charleston, originally named Charles Town in 1794 and renamed in 1819, was designated the state capital in 1870, with the move from Wheeling taking place in 1885; its State Capitol, completed in 1932, features a gold-leafed dome larger than that of the United States Capitol ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/West-Virginia)).

## Mountains, coal, and a shrinking population

For decades the rich coal beds underlying West Virginia made it a leading producer of bituminous coal in North America, and the state remains tied economically and socially to the mountain spines that span it and the rivers that enclose it on many sides ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/West-Virginia)). The gnarled terrain long locked West Virginians into small communities in narrow valleys and posed obstacles, literal and symbolic, to people from the outside world ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/West-Virginia)). Since World War II large numbers of residents have left for places offering greater employment; the 1970s energy crisis and coal boom briefly reversed the outflow, and from the 1980s population loss from the coalfields and heavy manufacturing was partly offset by an influx of urban professionals and retirees in the eastern panhandle ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/West-Virginia)). The census numbers tell the same story in miniature: the state counted 1,852,994 people in 2010, 1,793,716 in 2020, and an estimated 1,769,979 in 2024 ([US Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/geographies/reference-files/2010/geo/state-local-geo-guides-2010/west-virginia.html); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/West-Virginia)). The implication is a state still working to diversify beyond coal, turning to education and telecommunications among other strategies to build a more modern social and economic climate ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/West-Virginia)). Its other traditional industries — chemicals, primary metals, lumber and wood products — remain relatively stable, and tourism is a growing service sector, with cattle, dairy, and eastern-panhandle apples rounding out agriculture ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/West-Virginia)).

## The 2026 Supreme Court chapter: West Virginia v. B.P.J.

Most state profiles stop at coal and mountains. West Virginia's most consequential recent chapter is a courtroom one. In April 2021 the state passed the Save Women's Sports Act (H.B. 3293), which ensures female athletes do not have to compete against males in school sports ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/case/state-of-west-virginia-v-bpj/)). The law was challenged by the ACLU, and [Alliance Defending Freedom](/organizations/alliance-defending-freedom/) intervened on behalf of Lainey Armistead, a former women's soccer player at West Virginia State University; the district court allowed her intervention in December 2021 ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/case/state-of-west-virginia-v-bpj/)). A federal district judge granted summary judgment in favor of the law's defenders on January 5, 2023, but the US Court of Appeals for the 4th Circuit reversed part of that decision in April 2024 ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/case/state-of-west-virginia-v-bpj/)). The state, with ADF attorneys as co-counsel alongside Attorney General J.B. McCuskey, asked the [US Supreme Court](/organizations/us-supreme-court/) to hear the case in July 2024; the Court agreed in July 2025 and heard arguments in January 2026 ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/case/state-of-west-virginia-v-bpj/); [Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/press-release/us-supreme-court-levels-playing-field-for-womens-sports/)).

On June 30, 2026, the Court ruled 9-0 that Title IX — the federal law ensuring equal opportunities for women in education — and 6-3 that the Equal Protection Clause of the 14th Amendment allow states to protect female athletes through sex-specific sports ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/press-release/us-supreme-court-levels-playing-field-for-womens-sports/)). The Title IX holding was unanimous: "sex" in Title IX means biological sex, not gender identity, based on the ordinary meaning of the term when the statute was enacted in 1972 ([LexBlog](https://www.lexblog.com/2026/08/11/what-the-supreme-courts-transgender-sports-ruling-means-for-education-institutions/)). On Equal Protection, the majority applied intermediate scrutiny and held that safety and competitive fairness are important government interests to which limiting women's sports to biological females is substantially related ([LexBlog](https://www.lexblog.com/2026/08/11/what-the-supreme-courts-transgender-sports-ruling-means-for-education-institutions/)). The Court expressly distinguished Bostock v. Clayton County, its 2020 employment-discrimination decision, without overruling it: Title VII concerns employment, while Title IX authorizes sex-separated sports teams in a fundamentally different context ([Quarles](https://www.quarles.com/newsroom/publications/supreme-court-upholds-state-authority-to-define-womens-sports-teams-by-biological-sex-under-title-ix-key-implications-for-employers)). ADF also names a second client, Adaleia Cross, and characterizes the plaintiff's record — defeating more than 470 girls over 1,400 times and winning a women's state shot-put championship — as the human stakes of the case; those figures are ADF's characterizations, not findings quoted in the independent legal analyses ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/press-release/us-supreme-court-levels-playing-field-for-womens-sports/)). Critically, the ruling says what states MAY do, not what they must: the Court did not decide whether schools may permit transgender athletes on teams aligned with their gender identity, leaving that question for future litigation ([LexBlog](https://www.lexblog.com/2026/08/11/what-the-supreme-courts-transgender-sports-ruling-means-for-education-institutions/)).
