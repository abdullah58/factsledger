## Overview

Montreal is Quebec's largest city and Canada's second-largest, a French-speaking metropolis of 1,762,949 people (2021 census) on an island in the St. Lawrence River ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal); [Statistics Canada](https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2021/as-sa/fogs-spg/Page.cfm?lang=e&topic=1&dguid=2021S0503462)). Founded in 1642 as Ville-Marie, it was Canada's largest metropolitan centre from Confederation until Toronto overtook it in the 1970s; today it is a global centre for artificial-intelligence research and home to the headquarters of the International Civil Aviation Organization ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/diplomacy-and-international-relations/international-civil-aviation-organization)).

The city occupies about three-fourths of Montreal Island (Île de Montréal), the largest of the 234 islands of the Hochelaga Archipelago, near the confluence of the Ottawa and St. Lawrence rivers ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal)). It is built around and up Mont Royal, which rises to 763 feet (233 metres), and the St. Lawrence — draining the Great Lakes — gave the city a natural waterway to the heart of the North American continent, making it an ideal staging point for the fur trade west and north to Hudson Bay ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal)). The Lachine Canal, which let ships bypass the rapids just west of the city, is preserved as a National Historic Site ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal)).

Montreal's climate is continental: January averages in the mid-teens Fahrenheit (about −9 °C) and July in the low 70s (about 22 °C), with about 41 inches (1,050 mm) of precipitation a year and winter snowfalls that often exceed 7 feet (about 2.2 metres) ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal)). The 1998 ice storm took a number of lives, made road travel impossible and caused major damage to hydroelectric transmission lines and trees ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal)).

## From Hochelaga to Ville-Marie

Europeans did not found Montreal on empty land. The fortified Iroquois village of Hochelaga stood on the island when Jacques Cartier visited it in 1535, home to about 1,500 people living in some 50 longhouses, according to Cartier's log for October of that year ([Parks Canada](https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/dfhd/page_nhs_eng.aspx?id=645)). The village was abandoned before 1600, its exact location is unknown, and the site — marked by a cairn at the main gates of McGill University — was designated a National Historic Site of Canada in 1920 ([Parks Canada](https://www.pc.gc.ca/apps/dfhd/page_nhs_eng.aspx?id=645)).

A century later, in 1642, French settlers founded Ville-Marie as a missionary settlement; it soon became a fur-trading centre, a role enhanced after the British conquest of New France in 1763 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal)). The city had surrendered peacefully to British forces in 1760 and became part of British North America in 1763 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal)). Its position at the meeting of two rivers made it the entrepôt for a trade that ran through the Great Lakes and, via Lake Michigan, to the Mississippi–Missouri river system ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal)).

From the Confederation of Canada in 1867, Montreal was the country's largest metropolitan centre, until Toronto overtook it in the 1970s ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal)). The twentieth century stamped the city with set pieces: Place Ville-Marie (1962), a cruciform tower of more than 40 storeys above underground shops and theatres; Expo 67, the World's Fair marking the centenary of Confederation, built on Île Sainte-Hélène and the entirely man-made Île Notre-Dame; and the city hosted the 1976 Summer Olympic Games ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal)).

## A French-speaking metropolis

French Canadians are the majority population in Montreal, and French speakers account for about half the population in the city proper and about two-thirds on the island ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal)). The city is often said to be the second-largest French-speaking city in the world, after [Paris](/places/paris/) — though Britannica notes the accuracy of that statement is sometimes questioned, with the same claim made for Kinshasa and Algiers ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal)). Montreal is widely considered one of North America's most cosmopolitan cities and a destination for many immigrants ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal)).

That French colonial history, dating to the 16th century, is visible in the streetscape. In Place d'Armes, the old commercial centre, stands the Neo-Gothic Notre-Dame Basilica, founded in 1683 and rebuilt in 1829; on Mont Royal, near the University of Montreal, stands St. Joseph's Oratory, whose white dome marks the highest point in Montreal at 856 feet (260 metres) above sea level, and whose 99 front stairs pious pilgrims climb on their knees ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal)). The Quartier des Spectacles hosts the city's best-known festivals — the Just for Laughs Festival, the International Jazz Festival and the Francofolies, devoted to Francophone music ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal)). Montrealers are also great sports enthusiasts, and the Montreal Canadiens are one of the most storied franchises in the National Hockey League ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal)).

## Economy: from commercial capital to AI hub

Montreal's economy was long dominated by an Anglophone minority, but the city remained a transportation, manufacturing and financial centre even after Toronto overtook it as Canada's largest metropolitan centre in the 1970s ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Montreal)). The scale is still considerable: the census metropolitan area's GDP at basic prices was CA$279.5 billion in 2022, up from CA$228.7 billion in 2020 — a recovery and growth run that, divided by the 2021 CMA population, works out to roughly CA$65,100 per person ([Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=3610046801); [Statistics Canada](https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2021/as-sa/fogs-spg/Page.cfm?lang=e&topic=1&dguid=2021S0503462)).

Two international anchors mark the modern economy. Montreal was designated a [UNESCO](/organizations/unesco/) City of Design in June 2006, the first North American city to join the network, after Buenos Aires (August 2005) and Berlin (November 2005); at the time, design accounted for 20,356 jobs in the metropolitan area and economic spin-offs of more than $750 million ([Canadian Architect](https://canadianarchitect.com/montreal-designated-as-a-city-of-design-by-unesco/)). And the city hosts the headquarters of the International Civil Aviation Organization, a [United Nations](/organizations/united-nations/) specialized agency created under the 1944 Convention on International Civil Aviation; Montreal was chosen as its permanent home in part because of the city's status as an international cosmopolitan city, and the organization had more than 190 member states by the mid-2020s ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/diplomacy-and-international-relations/international-civil-aviation-organization)).

The newest pillar is artificial intelligence. Yoshua Bengio, a professor at the University of Montreal and scientific director of Mila, Quebec's Artificial Intelligence Institute, shared the 2018 ACM A.M. Turing Award — often called the "Nobel Prize of Computing" — with Geoffrey Hinton and Yann LeCun for breakthroughs that made deep neural networks a critical component of computing ([ACM](https://www.acm.org/articles/bulletins/2019/march/turing-award-2018)). Mila, founded by Bengio from a partnership between the Université de Montréal and McGill University, describes itself as the world's largest academic AI research centre specialized in deep learning, with a community of over 1,500 members, supported by the Government of Canada through the Pan-Canadian AI Strategy and by the Government of Quebec ([Mila](https://mila.quebec/en/news/mila-and-inovia-capital-are-partnering-to-launch-the-venture-scientist-fund-propelling-canadas)). In January 2026 Mila and Inovia Capital launched the Venture Scientist Fund, targeting US$100 million to turn Canadian AI research into AI-native companies, integrated with the national AI institutes Amii, Mila and Vector ([Mila](https://mila.quebec/en/news/mila-and-inovia-capital-are-partnering-to-launch-the-venture-scientist-fund-propelling-canadas)).

## Frequently asked questions

**How many people live in Montreal?**
The city of Montréal had 1,762,949 residents in the 2021 census, and the census metropolitan area had 4,291,732 — the second-largest metropolitan area in Canada, after Toronto. The city grew 3.4% and the metropolitan area 4.6% between the 2016 and 2021 censuses.

**When was Montreal founded?**
The city dates to 1642, when French settlers founded Ville-Marie as a missionary settlement. An Iroquois village, Hochelaga, stood on the island more than a century earlier — Jacques Cartier visited it in 1535 — but it was abandoned before 1600.

**Why is Montreal important for artificial intelligence?**
Montreal is home to Mila, the Quebec AI Institute founded by Yoshua Bengio, which describes itself as the world's largest academic AI research centre specialized in deep learning. Bengio shared the 2018 Turing Award — computing's highest honour — with Geoffrey Hinton and Yann LeCun.

**Is Montreal the second-largest French-speaking city in the world, after Paris?**
It is often said to be, but the claim is contested: Britannica notes the same claim is made for Kinshasa and Algiers. What is settled is that French Canadians are the majority population and about half the city proper speaks French.

**What is Montreal's connection to Behavox?**
Behavox's June 2026 announcement of a US$175 million investment from HPS Investment Partners, part of BlackRock, was datelined "London & Montreal". The company is headquartered in London, with offices across North America, EMEA and APAC.
