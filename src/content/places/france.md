## Overview

France is a country of northwestern Europe — officially the French Republic — whose capital is [Paris](/places/paris/), whose currency is the euro, and whose population was estimated at 66,590,000 in metropolitan France in 2026 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/France)). It is the [European Union](/organizations/european-union/)'s second-largest economy, a permanent member of the [United Nations](/organizations/united-nations/) Security Council, and the world's most visited country. What sets France apart is the state's habit of owning finance: its public investor, the [Caisse des Dépôts](/organizations/caisse-des-depots-et-consignations/), founded in 1816, anchors a state-built bancassurance group that absorbed [CNP Assurances](/organizations/cnp-assurances/) in 2022 ([Groupe Caisse des Dépôts](https://www.caissedesdepots.fr/en/know-us/our-model/our-history); [Eurex](https://www.eurex.com/ex-en/rules-regs/corporate-actions/corporate-action-information/CNP-Assurances-S.A.-Takeover-Updated-06-June-2022--2827330)).

Britannica calls France "among the globe's oldest nations, the product of an alliance of duchies and principalities under a single ruler in the Middle Ages," with central authority vested in the state ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/France)). The French "look to the state as the primary guardian of liberty," and the state provides free education, health care and pension plans ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/France)). Geographically France bridges northern and southern Europe, and it is Europe's most important agricultural producer and a leading industrial power ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/France)).

## History: from Revolution to the Fifth Republic

France's modern identity begins with the Revolution of 1789, which overthrew the ancien régime and produced the Declaration of the Rights of Man, "which expresses the nation's ideals to this day" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/France)). Its democratic ideals inspired reformist and revolutionary movements for generations ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/France)). Napoleon I then built the state's durable machinery: prefects, the Napoleonic Code, the judicial system, the Banque de France, the centralised university and the military academies ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/France)).

The present Fifth Republic has enjoyed notable stability since its promulgation on 28 September 1958, "marked by a tremendous growth in private initiative and the rise of centrist politics" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/France)). Paris was remade under Napoleon III by Baron Haussmann; beyond the capital, Lyon, Marseille, Nantes and Bordeaux anchor the other regions ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/France)).

## Government and institutions

France is a unitary semi-presidential republic: a directly elected president shares executive power with a prime minister, under a bicameral Parliament of a 348-seat Senate and a 577-seat National Assembly ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/France)). President Emmanuel Macron won re-election to a second five-year term in April 2022, defeating Marine Le Pen — the first French president in 20 years to win re-election, since Jacques Chirac in 2002. Allies welcomed the continuity for France's European and NATO course during the war in Ukraine ([Associated Press](https://www.kdlg.org/as-heard-on-npr/2022-04-23/french-president-emmanuel-macron-beats-his-far-right-rival-to-win-reelection)).

The second Macron term has been politically unstable at home. After Prime Minister François Bayrou resigned on 9 September 2025, Macron appointed Sébastien Lecornu, his fifth prime minister in under two years, to lead a minority government and forge a 2026 budget ([Reuters](https://www.tbsnews.net/worldbiz/europe/frances-macron-names-loyalist-lecornu-new-prime-minister-1232846)). Lecornu's first cabinet, unveiled on 5 October 2025, collapsed within fourteen hours — the shortest-lived government in the history of the Fifth Republic — and Macron reappointed him on 10 October 2025; the Lecornu II government announced on 12 October survived no-confidence motions on 16 October ([AmCham France](https://amchamfrance.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Whos-Who-Lecornu-oct25.pdf)). France's deficit stood at nearly double the EU ceiling of 3% of GDP ([Reuters](https://www.tbsnews.net/worldbiz/europe/frances-macron-names-loyalist-lecornu-new-prime-minister-1232846)).

## Economy: the EU's second-largest

France's GDP was €2.92 trillion in 2024 ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/NY.GDP.MKTP.CN?locations=EU-FR)). Within the European Union it ranks second: EU GDP was €18.8 trillion in 2025, Germany the largest at €4.5 trillion (23.8%), "followed by France (15.8%) and [Italy](/places/italy/) (12.0%)" ([Eurostat](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/wdn-20260806-1)). GNI per capita was $45,160 in 2024, and life expectancy reached 80.3 years for men and 85.9 for women in 2025 — a high-income market whose 80.4% urban population (2018) is served by a state that still funds education, health care and pensions ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/France)).

France remained the world's most visited country in 2024 with 100 million international tourists — a record, up 2% on 2023 — generating €71 billion in revenue, up 12%, boosted by the Paris Summer Olympics ([World Tourism Forum](https://live.worldtourismforum.net/news/france-leads-global-tourism-with-100m-visitors-in-2024)). [Spain](/places/spain/), its closest competitor, drew 94 million visitors in the same year ([World Tourism Forum](https://live.worldtourismforum.net/news/france-leads-global-tourism-with-100m-visitors-in-2024)).

## The state as owner-operator: the public financial pole

### A public investor since 1816

The story starts in 1816. After the fall of Napoleon, France faced a financial crisis and savers distrusted its institutions, so in April 1816 the government created the Caisse des Dépôts under Parliament's protection and supervision to manage private funds and restore public faith ([Groupe Caisse des Dépôts](https://www.caissedesdepots.fr/en/know-us/our-model/our-history)). Two centuries on, the institution calls itself "the leading public financial hub," fulfilling "public interest functions in support of the policies pursued by the French State and local authorities" ([Groupe Caisse des Dépôts](https://www.caissedesdepots.fr/en/know-us/our-model/our-history)).

### The CNP takeover, 2018–2022

CNP Assurances was born inside that public world: the Caisse Nationale de Prévoyance was formed in 1959 from two Caisse des Dépôts funds, became a public industrial-and-commercial establishment in 1987, and was converted into a joint-stock company by law 92-665 of 16 July 1992, remaining a subsidiary of the Caisse des Dépôts group — which French law defines as "a public group serving the general interest" ([Assemblée Nationale](https://questions.assemblee-nationale.fr/q15/15-199QE.htm)). In August 2018, Economy and Finance Minister [Bruno Le Maire](/people/bruno-le-maire/) announced the project to combine [La Poste](/organizations/la-poste/), [La Banque Postale](/organizations/la-banque-postale/) and CNP Assurances into a "grand pôle financier public" ([Allnews](https://allnews.ch/content/corporate/la-banque-postale-veut-monter-%C3%A0-100-dans-cnp-assurances)). The competition authority approved the pole on 30 December 2019 without any conditions ([Autorité de la concurrence](https://www.autoritedelaconcurrence.fr/en/print/pdf/node/6782)), and the transactions closed on 4 March 2020: the Caisse des Dépôts took 66% of La Poste (the state 34%), and La Banque Postale took 62.1% of CNP Assurances ([La Banque Postale](https://www.labanquepostale.com/content/dam/lbp/documents/communiques-de-presse/en/2020/CP-creation-large-public-financial-pole-2020.pdf)).

The final act was a takeover, not a privatisation. On 28 October 2021 La Banque Postale agreed to buy [Groupe BPCE](/organizations/groupe-bpce/)'s entire 16.1% CNP stake at €21.90 a share, then offer minorities the same price, targeting a squeeze-out and delisting ([Allnews](https://allnews.ch/content/corporate/la-banque-postale-veut-monter-%C3%A0-100-dans-cnp-assurances)). The €21.90 price was a 36.2% premium over the pre-announcement price and valued CNP at about €15 billion ([Boursorama](https://www.boursorama.com/bourse/actualites/opa-des-primes-attractives-pour-cnp-assurances-prodware-et-s-t-dupont-739164beaf965dd9e1850c864ef9dcd1)). On 3 June 2022 La Banque Postale announced all offer conditions were fulfilled, holding 97.79% of the capital and 98.56% of the voting rights, and moved to request the mandatory squeeze-out ([Eurex](https://www.eurex.com/ex-en/rules-regs/corporate-actions/corporate-action-information/CNP-Assurances-S.A.-Takeover-Updated-06-June-2022--2827330)).

## France in the world

France is a leading member of the European Union and its predecessors, and one of the five permanent members of the United Nations Security Council — alongside the [United States](/places/united-states/), Russia, the [United Kingdom](/places/united-kingdom/) and China — with the right to veto council decisions ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/France)). Its relationship with [NATO](/organizations/nato/) has been deliberately independent-minded: from 1966 to 1995 France stayed out of the alliance's integrated military structure, retaining full control of its own forces; it returned to the NATO Military Committee in 1995, and in 2009 President Nicolas Sarkozy announced France would rejoin the military command ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/France)). That combination — EU insider, nuclear-armed veto holder, and sometimes awkward NATO ally — is why allies read Macron's 2022 re-election as a decision about Europe's security architecture as much as French domestic politics ([Associated Press](https://www.kdlg.org/as-heard-on-npr/2022-04-23/french-president-emmanuel-macron-beats-his-far-right-rival-to-win-reelection)).

## FAQ

### What type of government does France have?

France is a unitary semi-presidential republic under the Fifth Republic, promulgated in 1958. President Emmanuel Macron, re-elected in 2022, shares executive power with Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu, under a bicameral Parliament: a 348-seat Senate and a 577-seat National Assembly.

### How large is France's economy?

France's GDP was €2.92 trillion in 2024 (World Bank) — the EU's second-largest economy at 15.8% of EU GDP in 2025, behind Germany at 23.8% (Eurostat). GNI per capita was $45,160 in 2024; life expectancy was 80.3 years for men and 85.9 for women.

### What is the "grand pôle financier public"?

The large public financial group announced in August 2018 by Economy Minister Bruno Le Maire, combining La Poste, La Banque Postale and CNP Assurances under the Caisse des Dépôts. Completed in 2020, it ended with La Banque Postale's 2022 takeover of CNP at €21.90 a share.

### Why is France the world's most visited country?

France welcomed 100 million international tourists in 2024, a record, generating €71 billion in revenue, helped by the Paris Summer Olympics. Its draw is Paris, its gastronomy and heritage, and its Mediterranean and Atlantic coasts.
