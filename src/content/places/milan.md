## Overview

Milan, capital of the Lombardy region in northern [Italy](/places/italy/), is the country's second-largest city behind Rome, its leading financial centre and its most prosperous manufacturing and commercial city ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Milan-Italy)). With 1.37 million inhabitants in the city proper, it is Italy's second-largest city after Rome and ahead of Naples, and the capital of Lombardy, the country's most populous and richest region ([Statista](https://www.statista.com/topics/12103/milan/)).

It lies north of the Po river, on the plain between the Ticino to the west and the Adda to the east, at 122 metres above sea level with the Alps rising to the north ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Milan-Italy)). Milanese call their city Italy's "moral capital," a claim Britannica traces not to government but to work: Milan set itself up in the late 19th century as a capital of innovation, production and efficiency, and the rest of Italy, Rome included, accepts the label because of the city's contributions in every field since unification ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Milan-Italy)).

## From duchy to industrial capital

The Visconti family established a signoria, with Gian Galeazzo Visconti becoming the first duke in 1395, and the Sforza family founded a new dynasty in 1450, ushering in prosperity and cultural flourishing during the Italian Renaissance ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Duchy-of-Milan)). The duchy then changed hands repeatedly, falling under French and then Spanish rule before passing to Austrian control in 1706 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Duchy-of-Milan)). Milan became the capital of Napoleon's Kingdom of Italy in 1805, returned to Austrian rule after Napoleon's fall, played a significant role in the Risorgimento, and after 1859 became an integral part of the unified Italian state, developing into Italy's industrial and financial centre ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Duchy-of-Milan)).

The city's stones record that long arc. Milan Cathedral, whose construction began in 1386 and took five centuries to complete, with final details on the facade finished only in 1965, is one of the world's largest Roman Catholic cathedrals, its roof bursting with spires, gargoyles and marble statues ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Milan-Cathedral)). Inside, five aisles run beneath brick pillars encased in marble, and the walls hold 3,159 images — more statuary than any other building in the world — topped by the Madonnina, covered in 3,900 pieces of gold leaf ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Milan-Cathedral)).

## Fashion, finance and the arts

Milan is known as a world capital of fashion, design, finance, business services, and media and publishing, and as the home of Leonardo da Vinci's Last Supper and the celebrated Gothic cathedral, the Duomo ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Milan-Italy)). The Last Supper itself sits in the refectory of the Dominican convent of Santa Maria delle Grazie, in a complex begun in 1463 by Guiniforte Solari and reworked at the end of the 15th century by Bramante, who added semi-circular apses, a drum-shaped dome, a cloister and the refectory ([UNESCO / Italian Ministry of Culture](https://unesco.cultura.gov.it/en/projects/chiesa-e-convento-domenicano-di-santa-maria-delle-grazie-con-l-ultima-cena-di-leonardo-da-vinci/)). Leonardo painted it between 1495 and 1497, depicting the moment immediately after Christ said, "One of you will betray me," with the apostles in four groups of three — painted not in fresco but in tempera on plaster that did not absorb paint, a flaw Vasari noted as early as 1568 ([UNESCO / Italian Ministry of Culture](https://unesco.cultura.gov.it/en/projects/chiesa-e-convento-domenicano-di-santa-maria-delle-grazie-con-l-ultima-cena-di-leonardo-da-vinci/)).

Opera gives Milan its other great stage. Teatro alla Scala was founded under the auspices of Empress Maria Theresa of Austria to replace the Royal Ducal Theatre, destroyed by fire on 26 February 1776, and, designed by Giuseppe Piermarini, it opened on 3 August 1778 with Antonio Salieri's L'Europa riconosciuta ([Teatro alla Scala](https://www.teatroallascala.org/en/the-theater/history.html)). With Rossini's arrival in 1812 it became the appointed place of Italian opera seria, and Verdi's cycle there, from Oberto in 1839 to Nabucco's triumph in 1842, tied the house to Italy's patriotic awakening ([Teatro alla Scala](https://www.teatroallascala.org/en/the-theater/history.html)). Fashion completes the trio: Milan Fashion Week takes place semiannually, in February and September, with Versace and Armani among the Milan-based brands on show ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Milan-Italy)).

## Milan in 2026: ice, and a new kind of acquirer

The most recent chapter postdates most city profiles. In the 2026 Winter Olympics, Milan hosted events in figure skating, ice hockey and speed skating ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Milan-Italy)), and the opening ceremony was staged at San Siro, held simultaneously across four venues — San Siro as hub, with elements in Livigno, Predazzo and Cortina d'Ampezzo — with two Olympic cauldrons lit simultaneously for the first time in Olympic history ([European Olympic Committees](https://www.eurolympic.org/olympic-winter-games-milano-cortina-2026-begins-with-historic-opening-ceremony/)). The ceremony featured Mariah Carey, Laura Pausini and Andrea Bocelli, and Italian President Sergio Mattarella declared the Games open ([European Olympic Committees](https://www.eurolympic.org/olympic-winter-games-milano-cortina-2026-begins-with-historic-opening-ceremony/)).

The quieter 2026 story is corporate: the city is now home base of a serial acquirer of the software era. [Bending Spoons](/organizations/bending-spoons/), the Milan-based company founded in 2013, had completed more than 50 acquisitions by mid-2026, with a portfolio including AOL and Eventbrite that served over 500 million monthly active users and more than 9 million monthly paying customers in March 2026, on $1.6 billion of revenue for the twelve months ended 31 March 2026 ([Renaissance Capital](https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/News/119648/italy-based-tech-company-bending-spoons-files-for-an-estimated-1.5-billion)). That is the "moral capital" thesis in its newest clothes — production and efficiency, applied to apps rather than factories.

The counterweight is demographic: Milan's population has progressively aged, while rising living costs not compensated by higher salaries have pushed many residents to the outskirts ([Statista](https://www.statista.com/topics/12103/milan/)). A city staging the world's events while its own residents drift outward is a tension, not a triumph.
