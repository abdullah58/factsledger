## Overview

Palasa-Kasibugga is a twin-town municipality in Srikakulam district of [Andhra Pradesh](/places/andhra-pradesh/), India, formed from the adjoining towns of Palasa and Kasibugga ([City Population](https://citypopulation.de/en/india/andhrapradesh/srikakulam/2841101000__palasa_kasibugga/)). At the 2011 Census of India, its population was recorded at 57,507, with a literacy rate of 75.68%, above the state average of 67.02% ([City Population](https://citypopulation.de/en/india/andhrapradesh/srikakulam/2841101000__palasa_kasibugga/)).

For decades, Palasa has been synonymous with cashew across Andhra Pradesh, and Palasa-Kasibugga sits at the heart of that identity: the two localities are the biggest cashew processing centres in the state, with over 300 units together producing more than 60,000 kg of cashew kernels per day and an annual turnover of between Rs 250 and Rs 300 crore ([The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/visakhapatnam/cashew-processing-units-feel-the-crunch-in-andhra-pradesh/articleshow/98054890.cms)). The Palasa variety of cashew kernel is famous across the country for its rich taste, which the Times of India attributes to the region's soil and climate conditions ([The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/visakhapatnam/cashew-processing-units-feel-the-crunch-in-andhra-pradesh/articleshow/98054890.cms)). That national renown rests on a fragile supply chain: the units require at least 70,000 to 80,000 tonnes of raw cashew a year, far more than local growers produce, so processors import raw nuts from Ghana, Ivory Coast, Benin, Tanzania, Indonesia and other countries by sea route ([The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/visakhapatnam/cashew-processing-units-feel-the-crunch-in-andhra-pradesh/articleshow/98054890.cms)). Nearly 350 cashew processing factories were concentrated in Palasa–Kasibugga as of the latest reporting, many running at only 50–60% capacity as West Asia unrest has driven up import costs and disrupted logistics, payments and exports ([The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/vijayawada/aps-cashew-industry-hit-as-west-asia-unrest-drives-up-import-costs/articleshow/129783445.cms)).

## Municipal status

The municipality's grade was raised by the Andhra Pradesh government: under G.O.Ms.No.308, dated 12 December 2019, Palasa Kasibugga Municipality was upgraded from Third Grade to Second Grade with immediate effect, in exercise of the powers under the Andhra Pradesh Municipalities Act, 1965 ([Andhra Pradesh Gazette](http://ia803104.us.archive.org/2/items/in.gazette.andhra.2019-12-12.6079/6079.pdf)). That notification — the highest-tier administrative evidence available for this profile — confirms the twin towns' municipal standing as Second Grade, even though no government source read in full pins down the year the two towns were merged.

## 2025 temple stampede and its aftermath

On 1 November 2025, a stampede at the privately run Sri Venkateswara Swamy temple in Kasibugga killed nine people, eight women and a boy, according to India's official government news service ([News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/stampede-at-venkateswara-swamy-temple-at-kashibugga-in-andhra-pradesh-claims-9-lives)). The temple, built to hold around 3,000 people, was overwhelmed by roughly 25,000 devotees who had gathered for Karthika Ekadashi observances; an iron grill along the single shared entry-and-exit passage collapsed near a stairway, triggering the crush, according to the state's medical and health minister as reported by The Federal ([The Federal](https://thefederal.com/category/states/south/andhra-pradesh/venkateswara-temple-stampede-srikakulam-214173)).

Investigators found a compound of failures. The shrine sat on the first floor, requiring devotees to climb around 20 steps for darshan, and the railing that gave way was reportedly in poor condition and not properly constructed ([NewsMeter](https://newsmeter.in/top-stories/srikakulam-negligence-blamed-as-stampede-at-kasibugga-temple-claims-nine-lives-757500)). The temple was privately managed and not registered under the Endowments Department, and its administrators had neither sought the mandatory permissions nor requested police protection for the event ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/day-after-9-deaths-in-stampede-police-bar-entry-at-kasibugga-temple)). Srikakulam District Collector Swapnil Dinkar Pundkar called the incident "complete negligence" on the part of the temple authorities, and said the district would move to ensure CCTV surveillance at temples for better crowd management ([NewsMeter](https://newsmeter.in/top-stories/srikakulam-negligence-blamed-as-stampede-at-kasibugga-temple-claims-nine-lives-757500)). Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu described the incident as a result of poor planning and lack of communication, ordered a full-fledged inquiry, and promised strict action against those responsible ([NewsMeter](https://newsmeter.in/top-stories/srikakulam-negligence-blamed-as-stampede-at-kasibugga-temple-claims-nine-lives-757500)).

What sets this profile apart from the day-one coverage is the accountability arc that followed. Police registered an FIR against the temple management, and charges under Sections 105 and 106 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita — causing death by negligence and endangering human life through rash acts — were directed at the temple's management committee and a private builder involved in ongoing construction ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/andhrapradesh/outrage-fades-grief-and-questions-linger-after-kasibugga-temple-stampede/)). A Special Investigation Team was constituted to examine crowd-control lapses, structural failures and accountability, though its report had not been made public ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/andhrapradesh/outrage-fades-grief-and-questions-linger-after-kasibugga-temple-stampede/)). Temple founder Hari Mukunda Panda — who had built the 12-acre complex as a "mini Tirupati" after a personal setback at the original Tirupati shrine, funded by his savings and the sale of coconut groves — expressed remorse but denied culpability, and was placed under house arrest ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/andhrapradesh/outrage-fades-grief-and-questions-linger-after-kasibugga-temple-stampede/)). The Indian Express reported that Panda faced a culpable homicide case, that Endowments Minister Anam Ramanarayana Reddy had announced a full-fledged inquiry committee, and that the government was fully prepared to take over and develop the temple, which was not fully completed ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/hyderabad/after-stampede-left-9-dead-andhra-pradesh-govt-could-take-over-management-of-private-temple-10342476/)).

The tragedy also acquired statewide significance: it was the third such temple-related disaster in Andhra Pradesh in 2025, after a January stampede at Bairagi Patteda in Tirupati that claimed six lives and an April wall collapse at Simhachalam's Sri Varaha Lakshmi Narasimha Swamy Temple that killed seven — together leaving 22 people dead and nearly 100 injured ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/andhrapradesh/outrage-fades-grief-and-questions-linger-after-kasibugga-temple-stampede/)). The victims named in the days after were all from nearby mandals — eight women and one 13-year-old boy — and of the 26 injured, most were treated at Palasa's own hospitals, the Kidney Research Centre and Super Speciality Hospital and the Community Health Centre ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/andhrapradesh/outrage-fades-grief-and-questions-linger-after-kasibugga-temple-stampede/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not state the municipality's formation date or merger history, which circulate on secondary reference sites but were not confirmed against a government source read in full — it cites only the 2019 grade upgrade, verified from the gazette itself. It treats Kasibugga as one of the two towns forming Palasa-Kasibugga Municipality, consistent with the municipality's name, without asserting ward-level administrative detail beyond what news coverage of the 2025 stampede specifies. A privately circulated 2026 population projection is omitted as an extrapolation rather than a measured census figure. The stampede account relies on reporting from the first two weeks after the incident; the SIT's final findings were not public at the last update, so nothing about its conclusions is asserted.
