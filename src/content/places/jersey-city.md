## Overview

Jersey City is New Jersey's second-largest city, with 292,449 residents in the 2020 census, and the seat of Hudson County, sitting on a peninsula between the Hudson and Hackensack rivers directly across the river from Lower Manhattan ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/jerseycitycitynewjersey/PST040225); [Britannica](http://www.britannica.com/place/Jersey-City)). Founded as a Dutch settlement in the 17th century, it grew into an industrial port and rail terminus, then reinvented its waterfront as "Wall Street West," a finance hub. Today it is also one of America's most diverse cities — 41.7% of residents were born outside the United States — and the headquarters city of the fintech [BlueVine](/organizations/bluevine/) ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/jerseycitycitynewjersey/PST040225); [Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)).

The city's standing in New Jersey is clear: Newark topped the state in the 2020 census with 311,549 residents, and Jersey City followed with 292,449, far ahead of third-placed Paterson at 159,732 ([ROI-NJ](https://www.roi-nj.com/2021/08/12/lifestyle/census-newark-311549-edges-jersey-city-292449-as-states-biggest-city/)). That count was up 44,852 — 18.1% — from 247,597 in 2010, and the Census Bureau's estimate for July 1, 2025 put the population at 302,013 ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/jerseycitycitynewjersey/PST040225)). At 19,835 people per square mile across 14.74 square miles of land, it is a notably dense city ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/jerseycitycitynewjersey/PST040225)). Its link to [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) is physical as well as economic: the Holland Tunnel and the Port Authority Trans-Hudson (PATH) rapid transit system connect it to Manhattan, and the Hudson and Manhattan tubes first made that crossing in 1908 ([Britannica](http://www.britannica.com/place/Jersey-City)).

## From Pavonia to a port city

The site was originally inhabited by the Delaware (Lenape) people, and Henry Hudson visited it in 1609 while seeking a route to East Asia for the Dutch East India Company ([Britannica](http://www.britannica.com/place/Jersey-City)). The Dutch foothold became formal with a land grant to Michael Pauw dated November 22, 1630 — the earliest known conveyance for what is now Jersey City — and the patroonship of Pavonia on the west bank of the Hudson ([City of Jersey City](http://cityofjerseycity.org/docs/grundy.shtml)). The first houses followed in 1633, at Communipaw for the colony's superintendent Jan Evertsen Bout and at Harsimus for his successor Cornelius Van Vorst ([City of Jersey City](http://cityofjerseycity.org/docs/grundy.shtml)).

After early conflict with the Lenape destroyed the scattered settlements, the Dutch regrouped behind walls: the fortified village of Bergen was established in 1660, the first permanent settlement and the first local civil government in what became New Jersey ([City of Jersey City](http://cityofjerseycity.org/docs/grundy.shtml); [Britannica](http://www.britannica.com/place/Jersey-City)). English forces seized New Netherland in September 1664 and renamed the colony New Jersey, confirming Bergen's charter ([City of Jersey City](http://cityofjerseycity.org/docs/grundy.shtml)). During the Revolutionary War the British held the strategic point at the mouth of the Hudson, until Henry "Light-Horse Harry" Lee stormed their fortifications at Paulus Hook on August 19, 1779 — a raid of limited military significance but a great psychological boost for the Americans ([Britannica](http://www.britannica.com/place/Jersey-City)).

The modern city took shape in the 19th century. The City of Jersey was incorporated in 1820, and on February 22, 1838 it was reincorporated as the "Mayor and Common Council of Jersey City," a municipality separate from the Township of Bergen ([City of Jersey City](http://cityofjerseycity.org/docs/grundy.shtml)). Railroads and the Morris Canal arrived in the 1830s, and the city emerged as a major transportation centre sharing a communications complex with New York City ([Britannica](http://www.britannica.com/place/Jersey-City)). Growth came by annexation as much as by birth: Van Vorst merged in 1851, and a consolidation referendum on October 5, 1869 led to a new charter on March 17, 1870 joining Jersey City with Hudson City and the Town of Bergen, with Greenville following in 1873 ([City of Jersey City](http://cityofjerseycity.org/docs/grundy.shtml)). The city changed its government twice in the 20th century — to a commission form in 1913, then to the mayor-council Plan C it still uses in 1961 — while its City Hall, opened January 1, 1896, has remained the seat of municipal government ([City of Jersey City](http://cityofjerseycity.org/docs/grundy.shtml)).

## "Wall Street West": the waterfront reinvention

Jersey City's second act began when its rail yards, factories and warehouses gave way to offices. By 2002 the city had "lured enough financial services firms across the Hudson River to earn the nickname Wall Street West," according to Institutional Investor, with an estimated 30,000 financiers working there — more than in the Stamford, Connecticut area ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btgi8hse53m0xbrnzjeo/home/capital-flight)). The transformation was physical: a 3.2-kilometre waterfront financial district centred on Exchange Place, where Merrill Lynch, J.P. Morgan Chase, UBS PaineWebber, Charles Schwab and Lord Abbett took space in new towers and converted warehouses ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btgi8hse53m0xbrnzjeo/home/capital-flight)).

The shift was also a change in kind. In the 1980s the firms that crossed the river came mostly for back-office work; by 2002, as the Jersey City Economic Development Corporation's real estate director put it, "in some cases, the whole company is moving here; in other cases, an entire income-producing division is coming" ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btgi8hse53m0xbrnzjeo/home/capital-flight)). Merrill Lynch's 42-story building was then the tallest in New Jersey, and Goldman Sachs' new 250-metre tower at 30 Hudson Street was due to open in late 2004 ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btgi8hse53m0xbrnzjeo/home/capital-flight)). The finance cluster has since broadened into technology: BlueVine, founded in 2013 and headquartered in Jersey City, serves about 175,000 small businesses with $2.1 billion in digitally sourced deposits, and [Valley National Bancorp](/organizations/valley-national-bancorp/) announced in September 2026 that it would acquire the company for approximately $340 million ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)).

## One of America's most diverse cities — and the gateway to Ellis Island

Jersey City's population is unusually mixed by every measure the Census Bureau publishes. In 2020-2024 figures, 41.7% of residents were born outside the United States and 50.9% speak a language other than English at home ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/jerseycitycitynewjersey/PST040225)). No single group dominates: Asian residents are 26.8% of the population, White residents 26.2%, Hispanic or Latino residents 25.7% and Black residents 20.4%, with 15.0% identifying with two or more races ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/jerseycitycitynewjersey/PST040225)). The city is also comparatively well educated and well paid, with 54.8% of residents age 25 and over holding a bachelor's degree or higher and median household income of $97,710, against a median gross rent of $2,007 ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/jerseycitycitynewjersey/PST040225)).

That diversity earns the city a high national ranking, but the familiar superlative needs a correction. WalletHub's 2026 study of more than 500 of the largest U.S. cities ranks Jersey City second overall for ethnic diversity — behind Germantown, Maryland — and first among midsize cities, with a score of 72.69 ([WalletHub](https://wallethub.com/edu/cities-with-the-most-and-least-ethno-racial-and-linguistic-diversity/10264)). Its breakdown puts Asians as the largest group at over 26%, Hispanics at nearly 26%, white residents at 23% and Black residents at 19%, with less than half the population speaking only English at home ([WalletHub](https://wallethub.com/edu/cities-with-the-most-and-least-ethno-racial-and-linguistic-diversity/10264)). The city is, fittingly, the departure point for the country's great immigration landmark: ferries to Liberty Island and Ellis Island leave from Liberty State Park in Jersey City, with tickets sold at the park's historic railroad terminal, as well as from the Battery in Manhattan ([U.S. National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/elis/planyourvisit/directions.htm)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim Jersey City was first settled in 1618: Britannica gives that date, but the city historian's account dates the Pauw land grant to November 22, 1630 and the first houses to 1633, and the profile follows the latter. It does not claim the city was "renamed Jersey City in 1820": the City of Jersey was incorporated in 1820, and the name Jersey City dates to the February 22, 1838 charter. It does not repeat the label "most ethnically diverse city in the United States" as a current fact — WalletHub's 2026 study ranks the city second overall, and the ranking depends on the study and the year. It makes no claim about the tallest building in New Jersey, since that title has changed hands since the 2002 source used here. The 2025 population estimate and the 2020 census count are different Census Bureau vintages and are not presented as a growth rate. Finally, the profile does not cover the city's contemporary politics, its school system, or the post-2020 housing and affordability debates in detail — those were not verified from fully-read sources and are left for a future refresh.
