## Overview

Delaware is the First State — the first of the original 13 to ratify the U.S. Constitution, on December 7, 1787 — and, two and a half centuries later, the legal home of corporate America ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/geographies/reference-files/2010/geo/state-local-geo-guides-2010/delaware.html)). The numbers are the story: 2.2 million registered business entities in a state of barely a million people, nearly 300,000 new entities in 2023 alone, about two-thirds of the Fortune 500 incorporated here, and a corporate franchise tax generating $2.2 billion a year — more than a third of the state budget ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2025/03/21/why-delaware-is-home-to-corporate-america-by-the-numbers)). A company "organized under the laws of Delaware," as thousands of SEC filings put it, is legally domiciled in Delaware — it does not mean it is headquartered or operates there; many such companies list the same Wilmington address, 1209 North Orange Street, while their workforces sit elsewhere ([Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/management/2016/10/delaware-incorporation/132066/?oref=rf-topic-lander-river)). In 2024–25 that dominance faced its most serious challenge in a century — and Delaware rewrote its own rules to defend it ([UC Berkeley Law](https://sites.law.berkeley.edu/thenetwork/2025/04/14/could-the-mighty-fall-why-companies-are-considering-reincorporating-out-of-delaware-and-delawares-response/)).

## Why corporate America lives in Delaware

The mechanism is specific, not mystical. Delaware's own account names five reasons companies choose it: the Delaware General Corporation Law, which imposes few mandatory investor protections and otherwise gives corporations full flexibility; the specialized, jury-free, bipartisan Court of Chancery with jurisdiction over corporate disputes; a deep body of precedent, including the "business judgment rule" under which courts don't second-guess business decisions made in good faith and with due care; an expert corporate bar that reviews and recommends updates to the law; and a Secretary of State's office offering expedited service ([UC Berkeley Law](https://sites.law.berkeley.edu/thenetwork/2025/04/14/could-the-mighty-fall-why-companies-are-considering-reincorporating-out-of-delaware-and-delawares-response/)). The Court of Chancery dates to 1792 — established to rule on corporate-law disputes, it has no jury; judges with corporate expertise hear the cases ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2025/03/21/why-delaware-is-home-to-corporate-america-by-the-numbers)). More than half of all publicly traded U.S. companies are incorporated in the state ([Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/management/2016/10/delaware-incorporation/132066/?oref=rf-topic-lander-river)).

The origin story is an accident of New Jersey politics. In the late 19th century New Jersey had liberalized its incorporation statute and was winning the race for charters; Delaware adopted much of the New Jersey statute as its own general corporate law in 1899 ([UC Berkeley Law](https://sites.law.berkeley.edu/thenetwork/2025/04/14/could-the-mighty-fall-why-companies-are-considering-reincorporating-out-of-delaware-and-delawares-response/)). Then, in 1910–11, New Jersey's Governor Woodrow Wilson reversed course and cracked down on corporations and trusts — and companies fled across the river to Delaware, where the un-abolished Court of Chancery was waiting to hear their disputes ([UC Berkeley Law](https://sites.law.berkeley.edu/thenetwork/2025/04/14/could-the-mighty-fall-why-companies-are-considering-reincorporating-out-of-delaware-and-delawares-response/); [Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/management/2016/10/delaware-incorporation/132066/?oref=rf-topic-lander-river)). They never left. The tax base followed: corporate franchise revenue offsets the state's lack of a sales tax and its low property taxes ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2025/03/21/why-delaware-is-home-to-corporate-america-by-the-numbers)).

## 2025: the first real challenge in a century

The threat arrived in the person of Elon Musk. Since 2024 he has moved three companies — SpaceX, Neuralink, and Tesla — to Texas, prompted in large part by adverse rulings from Delaware Chancery Chancellor Kathaleen St. J. McCormick, who rescinded his $55.8 billion Tesla compensation package over board process and conflicts of interest; Musk posted, "Never incorporate your company in the state of Delaware" ([UC Berkeley Law](https://sites.law.berkeley.edu/thenetwork/2025/04/14/could-the-mighty-fall-why-companies-are-considering-reincorporating-out-of-delaware-and-delawares-response/)). Meta — which first incorporated in Delaware in 2004, when it was still Facebook — was reported by the Wall Street Journal to be weighing a move of its legal residence, with Texas in the mix ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2025/03/21/why-delaware-is-home-to-corporate-america-by-the-numbers); [UC Berkeley Law](https://sites.law.berkeley.edu/thenetwork/2025/04/14/could-the-mighty-fall-why-companies-are-considering-reincorporating-out-of-delaware-and-delawares-response/)). Rival forums are under construction: Texas launched a business court in August 2024 modeled on the Chancery, and Nevada has promoted itself as a corporate haven while considering a constitutional amendment for its own business court ([UC Berkeley Law](https://sites.law.berkeley.edu/thenetwork/2025/04/14/could-the-mighty-fall-why-companies-are-considering-reincorporating-out-of-delaware-and-delawares-response/)).

Delaware answered with legislation. Senate Bill 21, signed March 25, 2025 after passing the Senate 20–0 and the House 32–7, creates a safe harbor for directors, officers, and controlling stockholders in conflicted transactions and sets new limits on stockholders' inspections of corporate books and records — the kind of shareholder suit that drove Tesla out would become harder to bring ([UC Berkeley Law](https://sites.law.berkeley.edu/thenetwork/2025/04/14/could-the-mighty-fall-why-companies-are-considering-reincorporating-out-of-delaware-and-delawares-response/); [Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2025/03/21/why-delaware-is-home-to-corporate-america-by-the-numbers)). Berkeley Law's April 2025 assessment: Delaware's swift response, plus other companies' caution, suggests its preeminence "won't change anytime soon — despite other jurisdictions' efforts" ([UC Berkeley Law](https://sites.law.berkeley.edu/thenetwork/2025/04/14/could-the-mighty-fall-why-companies-are-considering-reincorporating-out-of-delaware-and-delawares-response/)).

## The First State: basics and history

The state itself is small — the second-smallest after Rhode Island, 1,948.5 square miles of land, 989,948 residents in 2020 — occupying the northeast of the Delmarva Peninsula with the Delaware River and Bay along its entire eastern edge ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/geographies/reference-files/2010/geo/state-local-geo-guides-2010/delaware.html); [FactMonster](https://www.factmonster.com/encyclopedia/places/north-america/us/delaware-state-united-states/revolution-and-statehood)). Its northern border with Pennsylvania is the Twelve-Mile Circle, a twelve-mile arc centered on the New Castle courthouse cupola ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/geographies/reference-files/2010/geo/state-local-geo-guides-2010/delaware.html)). The area began as Pennsylvania's Three Lower Counties (New Castle, Kent, Sussex), won a separate charter in 1701, declared itself a separate government in 1776, and ratified first in 1787 — hence "First State" ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/geographies/reference-files/2010/geo/state-local-geo-guides-2010/delaware.html)). The land itself is named for Thomas West, Baron De la Warr, via the bay and river; its first peoples were the Lenape, including the Nanticoke in the south ([FactMonster](https://www.factmonster.com/encyclopedia/places/north-america/us/delaware-state-united-states/revolution-and-statehood)).

Delaware's other history is less celebrated: a slave state that stayed loyal to the Union, it refused Lincoln's 1861 emancipation proposal and did not ratify the Thirteenth, Fourteenth, and Fifteenth Amendments until 1901 ([FactMonster](https://www.factmonster.com/encyclopedia/places/north-america/us/delaware-state-united-states/revolution-and-statehood)). Its economy beyond incorporation runs on the Du Pont chemical legacy (Eleuthère Irénée Du Pont's 1802 gunpowder mill on the Brandywine), banking subsidiaries drawn by 1980s usury-law liberalization, Dover Air Force Base — the state's largest military facility — broiler chickens, soybeans, and corn below the Chesapeake & Delaware Canal, Atlantic-beach tourism, and gambling ([FactMonster](https://www.factmonster.com/encyclopedia/places/north-america/us/delaware-state-united-states/revolution-and-statehood)). The state bird, fittingly for a place that punches above its weight, is the blue hen chicken — named for a gamecock-breeding hen of Kent County carried by the Delaware 1st Regiment, the "Blue Hen's Chickens" of the Revolution ([FactMonster](https://www.factmonster.com/encyclopedia/places/north-america/us/delaware-state-united-states/revolution-and-statehood)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do so many companies incorporate in Delaware?**
Five reasons the state itself cites: a flexible corporation law with few mandatory investor protections, a specialized jury-free Court of Chancery for business disputes, a deep body of precedent (including the business judgment rule), an expert corporate bar, and fast Secretary of State service. About two-thirds of the Fortune 500 are incorporated there.

**What is the Delaware Court of Chancery?**
A court established in 1792 to rule on corporate-law disputes. It has no jury — judges with corporate expertise hear cases — which is the central reason companies want their disputes decided under Delaware law.

**Why did Elon Musk move Tesla out of Delaware?**
Chancery Chancellor Kathaleen St. J. McCormick rescinded Musk's $55.8 billion Tesla compensation package over board process and conflicts of interest. Musk moved Tesla, SpaceX, and Neuralink to Texas and posted "Never incorporate your company in the state of Delaware."

**What was Senate Bill 21?**
Delaware legislation signed March 25, 2025, creating a safe harbor for directors, officers, and controlling stockholders in conflicted transactions and limiting stockholders' inspections of corporate books and records — a response to the wave of reincorporations out of the state.

**When did Delaware become a state?**
December 7, 1787 — the first of the original 13 states to ratify the U.S. Constitution, which is why it is called the First State.
