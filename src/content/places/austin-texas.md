## Overview

Austin is the capital of [Texas](/places/texas/) and the seat of Travis County, a city of 961,855 people at the 2020 census on the Colorado River in Central Texas, founded in 1839 as the planned capital of the Republic of Texas on the site of the village of Waterloo ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Austin-Texas); [Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)). It is the state's political centre, home to the University of Texas at Austin, and the self-styled "Live Music Capital of the World" ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Austin-Texas)).

The city sits where the Colorado River crosses the Balcones Escarpment, about 80 miles (130 km) northeast of San Antonio, at the eastern edge of the American Southwest ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Austin-Texas)). Its metropolitan area takes in Hays, Williamson, Bastrop and Caldwell counties, including Round Rock and San Marcos ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Austin-Texas)). Austin spreads across Travis, Hays and Williamson counties and counts 300 days of sunshine a year ([City of Austin](https://www.austintexas.gov/resident/about-city-austin)). What sets Austin apart from other state capitals is how often it has had to fight to stay one — and how completely it then reinvented itself, first as a university town, then as a technology centre, then as a music city, without ever ceasing to be the seat of Texas government.

## A capital chosen on the frontier, then defended

Austin began as Waterloo. Settlers founded the village in 1837 on the banks of the Colorado River, the first permanent settlement in the area ([City of Austin](https://www.austintexas.gov/resident/about-city-austin)). Two years later the young Republic of Texas chose the site for its permanent capital: a commission appointed by the Texas Congress in January 1839, acting on President Mirabeau B. Lamar's instruction, bought 7,735 acres along the river, including Waterloo and adjacent lands ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)). The town was renamed for Stephen F. Austin, founder of Anglo-American Texas, and laid out by surveyors L. J. Pilie and Charles Schoolfield under Edwin Waller as a 640-acre grid, fourteen blocks square, bisected by Congress Avenue and running north from the river to "Capitol Square" ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)).

The first lots were auctioned on 1 August 1839, the government arrived that October, and Austin was incorporated on 27 December 1839; Waller was elected its first mayor on 13 January 1840 ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)). By 1840 the town had 856 inhabitants, including 145 slaves, and diplomatic representatives from France, England and the [United States](/places/united-states/) ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)). The capital nearly moved almost at once. In 1842 President Sam Houston ordered the republic's archives transferred to Houston after Mexican troops captured San Antonio, and when Austinites refused, the government moved anyway — first to Houston, then to Washington-on-the-Brazos ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)). In December 1842 armed men sent to seize the General Land Office records were foiled by the citizens of Austin and Travis County, an episode remembered as the Archives War ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)). Deprived of its political function, Austin languished, its population dropping below 200, until a constitutional convention meeting in the city in 1845 approved annexation to the United States and named Austin the state capital ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)). Austin officially became the state capital on 19 February 1846, and its status was settled for good when it won a statewide election in 1872, turning back challenges from Houston and Waco ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)). The pink granite State Capitol that crowns Congress Avenue today was dedicated in 1888, modelled after the U.S. Capitol ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Austin-Texas)).

## Government, university, and the tech turn

The railroad remade Austin a second time. The Houston and Texas Central Railway arrived on 25 December 1871, making Austin the westernmost railroad terminus in Texas, and the population more than doubled in five years to 10,363 ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)). In 1881 Austin was chosen in a hotly contested statewide election as the site for the new University of Texas, which began instruction two years later, and the same year Tillotson Collegiate and Normal Institute opened and the Austin public school system started ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)). The university is now the city's second anchor: in fall 2025 it enrolled 55,000 students, the largest total in its 142-year history, after a record 90,690 freshman applications ([UT News](https://news.utexas.edu/2025/09/18/ut-sets-all-time-highs-for-enrollment-and-student-performance/)). In 1956 it became the first major university in the South to admit Black students as undergraduates ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)).

Technology arrived deliberately. The Chamber of Commerce had promoted high technology since the 1950s as a way to widen the city's narrow economic base, and the bet paid off when [IBM](/organizations/ibm/) located in Austin in 1967, followed by Texas Instruments in 1969 and Motorola in 1974 ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)). Two research consortiums, the Microelectronics and Computer Technology Corporation and Sematech, followed in the 1980s, and by the early 1990s the metropolitan area had about 400 high-technology manufacturers ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)). Business, research and development and manufacturing in computers, semiconductors, telecommunications, software and biotechnology are now among the city's major employers, alongside government, education and health care ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Austin-Texas)). The newest arrivals underline the shift: Tesla's corporate headquarters is in Austin ([Associated Press](https://www.ktsa.com/austin-is-largest-us-city-to-challenge-2020-census-numbers/)), and [Asure Software](/organizations/asure-software/), founded in 1985 as Forgent Networks, is headquartered in the city ([Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/company-profiles/asure-software/)).

## Live music, bats, and a city that outgrew its own count

Austin's official slogan promotes it as the "Live Music Capital of the World", and more than 100 live music venues are centred mainly around the Warehouse District and Sixth Street ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Austin-Texas)). The claim rests on institutions, not just venues: Austin City Limits was born in 1974 when Willie Nelson taped its pilot episode, premiered on PBS in 1975, and is the longest-running music series in television history ([Austin City Limits](https://acltv.com/2025/02/12/acl-presents-willie-nelson-family/)). South by Southwest began in 1987, founded by Nick Barbaro, Louis Black and Roland Swenson of the Austin Chronicle with Louis Meyers to promote the local music scene; its first year drew 700 registrants and about 200 bands at fifteen venues, and film and interactive events were added in 1994 ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/south-by-southwest)). By 2017 it showcased 2,085 artists at more than 100 venues ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/south-by-southwest)).

The city's stranger landmarks are just as old. The Congress Avenue Bridge is renowned for its population of Mexican free-tailed bats, hundreds of thousands of which fly from their roosts on summer nights ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Austin-Texas)). Barton Springs, a spring-fed pool the city acquired in 1918, became the symbol of the residential city, and Zilker Park along the river is the site of a nature centre, botanical gardens and the springs themselves ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Austin-Texas)). Seventeen moonlight towers — 165-foot structures erected in 1894 and lit by mercury vapour lamps — still light parts of the central city ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Austin-Texas)); the Handbook of Texas records 31 such towers of 150 feet, a discrepancy noted in the open items below.

Austin's growth has repeatedly outrun the official record of it. Between 2010 and 2020 the city grew 21.7%, the second-largest rate among the 50 biggest U.S. cities, trailing only Fort Worth ([Associated Press](https://www.ktsa.com/austin-is-largest-us-city-to-challenge-2020-census-numbers/)). In 2022 Austin became the largest U.S. city to challenge its 2020 census figures, saying it had more than the 961,855 residents tallied, because city officials believed about 7,000 housing units were missed, mostly in newly developed neighbourhoods ([Associated Press](https://www.ktsa.com/austin-is-largest-us-city-to-challenge-2020-census-numbers/)). At about 2.4 people per household, that is roughly 16,800 people — and a share of the $1.5 trillion in annual federal funding that census counts distribute ([Associated Press](https://www.ktsa.com/austin-is-largest-us-city-to-challenge-2020-census-numbers/)).

The growth story has a harder edge that the booster histories leave out. By 1900 segregation characterised many aspects of city life, and a 1906 ordinance required separate compartments on streetcars ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)). The 1928 city plan, the first since 1839, recommended that East Austin be designated a "Negro district", and municipal services such as schools, sewers and parks were made available to Black residents in East Austin only ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)). By 1930 Black residents were heavily concentrated on the east side of town, although their share of the population fell from 33% in 1880 to 17% in 1940 as the city grew ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Austin known for?**
Austin is the capital of Texas, the seat of Travis County, and home to the University of Texas at Austin, and it promotes itself as the "Live Music Capital of the World", with more than 100 live music venues and the South by Southwest festival each March ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Austin-Texas)).

**When was Austin founded?**
Settlers founded the village of Waterloo in 1837; in 1839 the Republic of Texas chose the site for its permanent capital, renamed it Austin for Stephen F. Austin, and incorporated the town on 27 December 1839 ([City of Austin](https://www.austintexas.gov/resident/about-city-austin); [Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)).

**Why did Austin stay the capital of Texas?**
Because its citizens refused to give it up: in 1842 they foiled an armed attempt to seize the republic's archives, and in 1872 Austin won a statewide election settling the capital's location once and for all, turning back challenges from Houston and Waco ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/austin-tx-travis-county)).

**How big is Austin?**
The 2020 census counted 961,855 residents, up 21.7% from 2010, making Austin the 11th most-populous U.S. city in July 2021; the Austin–Round Rock–Georgetown metro area counted 2,283,371 people, and the city challenged the count, saying about 7,000 housing units were missed ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Austin-Texas); [Associated Press](https://www.ktsa.com/austin-is-largest-us-city-to-challenge-2020-census-numbers/)).

**What is South by Southwest?**
South by Southwest (SXSW) is the music, film and interactive media conference and festival held in Austin each March. It began in 1987 with 700 registrants and about 200 bands at fifteen venues, and by 2017 it showcased 2,085 artists at more than 100 venues ([Handbook of Texas, TSHA](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/south-by-southwest)).
