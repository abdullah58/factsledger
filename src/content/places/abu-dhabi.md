## Overview

Abu Dhabi is the largest emirate of the [United Arab Emirates](/places/united-arab-emirates/) and its federal capital, a Gulf coast territory of 67,340 square kilometres whose 2024 census counted 4,135,985 people — up 7.5 percent on 2023 ([UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi/)). The name covers two things at once: the emirate, which holds more than three-fourths of the federation's land area and is the centre of its oil industry, and the city of the same name that serves as the capital of both the emirate and the federation ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/United-Arab-Emirates)).

The emirate lies on the coast of the Arabian Gulf, bordered by Oman to the east, Saudi Arabia to the south and west, and the emirate of [Dubai](/places/dubai/) to the northeast ([UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi/)). Its coastline stretches 700 kilometres and it has more than 200 natural islands ([UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi/)). It is organised into three regions: the city of Abu Dhabi, Al Ain in the east, and Al Dhafra in the west ([UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi/)). The city is home to most ministries and foreign embassies, as well as Zayed Port and Zayed International Airport, and is connected to the mainland by four bridges — Maqta, Mussafah, Sheikh Zayed, and Sheikh Khalifa ([UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi/)). Al Ain, the "Garden City", sits in the foothills of Jebel Hafeet, which at 1,240 metres is the emirate's highest peak and the second-highest in the UAE ([UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi/)).

## From pearls to oil — and now to renewables

Before 1958, the emirate's main industry was the export of pearls; since the discovery of oil, Abu Dhabi has become one of the world's leading producers of energy ([UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi/)). The ruling Al Nahyan family descends from the Al Bu Falah section of the Bani Yas tribe, originally settled in the Liwa oasis; they settled in Abu Dhabi in 1793 and lived in Qasr Al Hosn for almost two centuries ([UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi/)). The emirates were formally united as the United Arab Emirates in 1971, with the city of Abu Dhabi serving as the capital ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/United-Arab-Emirates)).

Oil wealth remade the emirate, but the current story is diversification. GDP grew 3.8 percent in 2024 to AED 1.2 trillion, and foreign investment has risen 300 percent since 2011 ([UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi/)). The emirate is also a pioneer in alternative energy: it is home to the world's largest single-site solar photovoltaic plant, Al Dhafra Solar PV, as well as Masdar City and the Barakah Nuclear Energy Plant, the Arab region's first nuclear energy facility, and it plans to rely on renewable energy for 50 percent of its power needs by 2030 ([UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi/)). Through the Abu Dhabi Global Market, the emirate is advancing as a financial centre; the ADGM hosts the Abu Dhabi Securities Exchange, the largest stock exchange in the UAE by market capitalisation, and a crude-oil derivatives exchange trading ADNOC's flagship Murban futures ([UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi/)). S&P Global reaffirmed its AA/A-1+ sovereign ratings on Abu Dhabi in May 2025 ([UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi/)).

The emirate's population has grown 51 percent over the past decade, from 2.7 million in 2014 to over 4.1 million in 2024, and it is home to more than 200 nationalities ([UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi/)). The census counted 2,767,060 males (66.9 percent) and 1,368,925 females (33.1 percent), and 2,762,715 employed residents, up 9.1 percent on 2023 ([UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi/)). Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan has been President of the UAE since May 14, 2022 — the third president, succeeding his late brother Sheikh Khalifa, who ruled from 2004 to 2022 — and his son Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan became Crown Prince of Abu Dhabi on March 29, 2023 ([UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi/)).

## Culture, and the Gulf's first driverless Baidu service

Abu Dhabi presents itself as a centre of tolerance and culture: the Louvre Abu Dhabi is described as the first museum in the world to explore shared humanity across civilisations, the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque is open to people of all faiths, and the Abrahamic Family House puts a mosque, a church, and a synagogue on the same ground ([UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi/)). It also hosts the Formula 1 Etihad Airways Abu Dhabi Grand Prix, the Abu Dhabi Desert Challenge, and the Abu Dhabi HSBC Championship ([UAE Government portal](https://u.ae/en/about-the-uae/the-seven-emirates/abu-dhabi/)).

The fact most profiles of Abu Dhabi miss is its role in autonomous driving. On January 17, 2026, [Baidu](/organizations/baidu/)'s [Apollo Go](/organizations/apollo-go/) and K2's AutoGo launched a fully autonomous commercial ride-hailing service on Yas Island, bookable through the AutoGo app, after securing one of the emirate's earliest permits for fully autonomous commercial operations in mid-November 2025 ([just-auto](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Baidu%27s+Apollo+Go+and+AutoGo+launch+driverless+rides+in+Abu+Dhabi.-a0871566818)). The partnership, announced in March 2025, aims to build the emirate's largest fully driverless fleet, expanding to several hundred vehicles by 2026, with phased expansion next to Reem Island, Al Maryah Island, and Saadiyat Island ([just-auto](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Baidu%27s+Apollo+Go+and+AutoGo+launch+driverless+rides+in+Abu+Dhabi.-a0871566818)). The stat that frames the scale: Apollo Go reported more than 240 million autonomous kilometres worldwide, including over 140 million kilometres driven without a safety driver, across 22 cities, with weekly ride volumes recently passing 250,000 ([just-auto](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Baidu%27s+Apollo+Go+and+AutoGo+launch+driverless+rides+in+Abu+Dhabi.-a0871566818)). The implication is that Abu Dhabi — not a Chinese city — hosted Apollo Go's first public driverless deployment outside [China](/places/china/), making the emirate a test case for Gulf autonomous mobility.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim a city-only population figure — 4,135,985 is the emirate total from the 2024 census. It does not claim a nominal GDP figure; the AED 1.2 trillion quoted is real GDP as reported by the UAE portal, and a lower nominal figure in other summaries is flagged rather than reconciled. It does not state the exact year oil was discovered, because the sources read give only "before 1958" for pearls and "since the discovery of oil" for what followed. It does not present Baidu's kilometre and ride totals as independently audited — they are the company's own figures as reported by just-auto. And it does not claim the Apollo Go service covers the whole emirate: as of the launch it operates on Yas Island, with Reem, Al Maryah, and Saadiyat named as the next phases.
