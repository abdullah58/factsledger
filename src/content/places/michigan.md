## Overview

Michigan, the Great Lakes State, is the 26th state of the [United States](/places/united-states/), admitted to the Union on January 26, 1837, as the fourth state carved from the Northwest Territory ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan)). Its capital is Lansing, in the south-central part of the state, and its largest city is [Detroit](/places/detroit/), whose name has become a byword throughout the world for the American automotive industry ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan)). The state's name comes from *michi-gama*, an Ojibwa (Chippewa) word meaning "large lake" ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan)). Michigan counted 10,077,331 people in the 2020 census, with a Census Bureau estimate of 10,127,884 on July 1, 2025 ([US Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/MI/PST045224)).

Michigan is the only state split into two large land segments: the sparsely populated but mineral-rich Upper Peninsula, which slices eastward from northern Wisconsin between Lakes Superior and Michigan, and the mitten-shaped Lower Peninsula, which reaches northward from Indiana and Ohio ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan)). By land area alone Michigan ranks only 22nd of the 50 states, but counting the Great Lakes waters under its jurisdiction lifts its total area to 11th ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan)). For most residents, an upturned right hand serves as a ready-made map of the Lower Peninsula, locating towns, routes, and regions ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan)). The state is also known as the Wolverine State, and its motto is "Si Quaeris Peninsulam Amoenam Circumspice" — "If You Seek a Pleasant Peninsula, Look About You" ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan)).

## A state made of water

Michigan's identity is its water. The state borders four of the five Great Lakes, and its roughly 11,000 inland lakes — most of them glacial in origin — range from under an acre to the nearly 20,000 acres of Houghton Lake in the north-central Lower Peninsula ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan)). About 500 islands dot the state's lakes and rivers, including Belle Isle and Grosse Ile in the Detroit River, Mackinac Island near the Straits of Mackinac — a resort on which motor vehicles are prohibited — and Isle Royale, a near-wilderness national park of almost 900 square miles in western Lake Superior, near Canada ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan)). In the southeast, the Lower Peninsula is separated from the Canadian province of Ontario by Lake St. Clair and the St. Clair and Detroit rivers, while the St. Marys River, flowing from Lake Superior to Lake Huron, forms the international boundary between the Upper Peninsula and Ontario ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan)).

The two peninsulas were separate worlds until 1957, when the 5-mile Mackinac Bridge — "Big Mac" — opened across the Straits of Mackinac, which separate Lake Michigan on the west from Lake Huron on the east ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan)). The bridge is more than infrastructure: it is the physical answer to the state's defining oddity, a state that needs a five-mile bridge to hold itself together. The same water that divides the state also feeds it — its inland lakes, its Great Lakes borders, and its wilderness tracts have made Michigan one of the country's leading tourist destinations ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan)).

## From copper and pine to cars — and then to software

Michigan's economy has been remade three times, and each remaking left a layer. Statehood itself was delayed until 1837 by the Toledo War, a boundary dispute with Ohio over the Toledo Strip, a narrow piece of land running westward from Toledo on Lake Erie to the Indiana border ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan/Statehood-and-growth)). Michigan relinquished its claims to Toledo and to the mouth of the Maumee River and was awarded the western Upper Peninsula instead — an exchange widely scorned at the time that ultimately proved a boon, because it handed Michigan the vast copper and iron riches of the Upper Peninsula ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan/Statehood-and-growth)). In the settlement frenzy of the 1840s and 1850s known as "Michigan Fever," thousands of settlers arrived from New York and New England via the Erie Canal and Lake Erie, and iron and copper discovered in the Upper Peninsula in the 1840s drew more immigrants still ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan/Statehood-and-growth)). The state capital moved from Detroit to the more central Lansing in 1847 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan/Statehood-and-growth)).

Mining and forestry jump-started the economy: iron ore came from the Marquette, Gogebic, and Menominee ranges, copper was centered in the Keweenaw Range in the northernmost Upper Peninsula, and lumbering of the vast pine forests was the mainstay of the late 1800s, with the Saginaw Valley the leading lumbering area between 1840 and 1860 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan/Statehood-and-growth)). By 1900, however, most of the pine in the Lower Peninsula was gone ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan/Statehood-and-growth)). Before 1900 a diverse base of agriculture, lumbering, mining, and manufacturing had propelled the state; through much of the 20th century, the economy was dominated by the automotive industry instead ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan/Statehood-and-growth)). The political shape of the state was set in the same era: the present-day [Republican Party](/organizations/republican-party/) was formed at Jackson in July 1854, Michigan sent 90,000 men to the Union cause in the Civil War and lost some 14,000, and in 1932 the state departed from the Republican fold to become one of the indeterminate "swing" states, while organized labor became a powerful political and economic force ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan/Statehood-and-growth)). In 1937 the United Automobile Workers became the bargaining agent for production workers at General Motors, and by the outbreak of World War II it was the dominant union in all automotive plants ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan/Statehood-and-growth)). During the war, Detroit became a major producer of military rather than commercial vehicles and was known as the Arsenal of Democracy ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan/Statehood-and-growth)).

The auto century did not end cleanly. A severe recession in the late 1970s and early 1980s caused widespread unemployment, business failures, and cuts in state services, and government, business, and education pooled their efforts to attract new enterprises and promote tourism ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan/Statehood-and-growth)). The 1990s brought a renaissance built on high-technology industries and a revival of automobile manufacturing, with unemployment dropping to low levels and tourism, manufacturing, agriculture, and services dominating the economy more evenly than in the past ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan/Statehood-and-growth)). By the early 21st century, however, the auto industry was struggling again and unemployment was among the highest in the country, and economic diversification into high-technology industries continued to be viewed as the long-term solution ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan/Statehood-and-growth)). One concrete example of that diversification sits in [Troy, Michigan](/places/troy-michigan/): [Altair Engineering](/organizations/altair-engineering/), headquartered in Troy, Michigan, provides software and cloud solutions in data analytics, product development, and high-performance computing ([Inc. Magazine](https://www.inc.com/altair/from-start-up-to-publicly-traded-how-altair-continues-to-innovate.html)). Altair began as a consultancy started by three friends who met while working at General Motors in the late 1970s and early 1980s, and its first product, HyperMesh (1989), used large-memory computers to model cars and airplanes and identify where a design could be lightened, strengthened, or improved ([Inc. Magazine](https://www.inc.com/altair/from-start-up-to-publicly-traded-how-altair-continues-to-innovate.html)). The stat that anchors the diversification story is a research one: in 2008 the US Department of Energy awarded a $550 million nuclear physics research project to Michigan State University ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan/Statehood-and-growth)).

Agriculture, the oldest layer, never went away. Michigan grows 70% of the United States' supply of tart cherries, ranking first in production, and is the leading producer in the world for Montmorency tart cherries, producing 179.56 million pounds of tart cherries in 2024 ([Michigan Agricultural Council](https://michigangrown.org/cherries/)). Traverse City is home to the annual National Cherry Festival and is known as the Cherry Capital of the World ([Michigan Agricultural Council](https://michigangrown.org/cherries/)). The state's population remains primarily urban, concentrated in the industrialized centers of the southern Lower Peninsula, and its system of public higher education has consistently remained among the strongest, most diverse, and most widely respected in the country ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan)). Racial polarization increased in Michigan during the mid-20th century, with major riots in Detroit in 1943 and 1967 — yet the 1963 Michigan constitution was the first in the country to provide for a Department of Civil Rights ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Michigan/Statehood-and-growth)).
