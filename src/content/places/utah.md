## Overview

Utah, the Beehive State, is a constituent state of the [United States](/places/united-states/) in the Mountain West, admitted to the Union on January 4, 1896, as the 45th state ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Utah)). Its capital and largest city is Salt Lake City, and its 2020 population was 3,271,616, with a 2024 estimate of 3,503,613 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Utah)). The state takes its name from the Ute people who lived there before the Mormon pioneers arrived in 1847, and its motto — "Industry" — still fits: Utah is simultaneously a canyon-and-desert state of five national parks, the fastest-growing state of the 2010s, and the quiet legal home of much of American fintech lending.

Utah lies in the heart of the West, bounded by [Idaho](/places/idaho/) to the north, Wyoming to the northeast, [Colorado](/places/colorado/) to the east, [Arizona](/places/arizona/) to the south, and Nevada to the west ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Utah)). At Four Corners in the southeast, Utah meets Colorado, [New Mexico](/places/new-mexico/), and Arizona at right angles — the only such meeting of states in the country ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Utah)). Its total area is 84,898 square miles, of which 82,169.62 square miles is land ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Utah); [U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/geographies/reference-files/2010/geo/state-local-geo-guides-2010/utah.html)).

## A religious settlement that became a state

Utah's story is unlike any other American state's: it is the story of a religious group that trekked across three-fourths of the continent in search of a "promised land" where it could be free from persecution ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Utah)). Mormon pioneers led by Brigham Young arrived in the Salt Lake Valley in 1847 and founded Salt Lake City ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Utah)). Irrigation was among their first efforts, and they built a cooperative economy of agriculture, handicrafts, and small industry, founding more than 150 self-sustaining communities with 40,000 residents by 1860 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Utah)). Salt Lake City is the world headquarters of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, and Mormons make up nearly seven-tenths of the state's population ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Utah)).

The land itself changed hands first. The United States acquired the area from Mexico in 1848, and Utah Territory was organized on September 9, 1850, originally covering all of present-day Utah plus most of Nevada and parts of western Colorado and southwestern Wyoming ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/geographies/reference-files/2010/geo/state-local-geo-guides-2010/utah.html)). The territory was carved down in stages — Colorado and Nevada in 1861, Idaho in 1863, Wyoming in 1868 — until it reached generally its present boundary ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/geographies/reference-files/2010/geo/state-local-geo-guides-2010/utah.html)). Disputes between the Mormon community and the federal government delayed statehood for decades: the federal government refused to make Utah a state while polygamy was practiced, and after church president Wilford Woodruff's 1890 manifesto ended the practice, there were no more obstacles — Utah was admitted as the 45th state in 1896, after seven attempts ([Utah State Archives](https://archives.utah.gov/2021/05/27/utahs-road-to-statehood-the-obstacle-of-polygamy/)).

## The Golden Spike and the railroad

Utah holds one of the defining moments in American history. On May 10, 1869, at Promontory Summit northwest of Ogden, a single telegraphed word — "done" — signaled to the nation the completion of the first transcontinental railroad ([Utah History Encyclopedia](https://historytogo.utah.gov/post/golden-spike/)). Union Pacific crews of 8,000 to 10,000 Irish, German, and Italian immigrants had pushed west from Omaha; at Promontory they met Central Pacific crews that had included more than 10,000 Chinese laborers building east from Sacramento ([Utah History Encyclopedia](https://historytogo.utah.gov/post/golden-spike/)). Congress had to set the meeting point itself, because the chartering legislation had not provided that the tracks join ([Utah History Encyclopedia](https://historytogo.utah.gov/post/golden-spike/)).

The ceremony was, by the Utah History Encyclopedia's account, somewhat disorganized: Union Pacific's No. 119 and Central Pacific's "Jupiter" lined up facing each other, Leland Stanford brought four ceremonial spikes — the famed Golden Spike from San Francisco magnate David Hewes, a second golden spike, a silver spike from Nevada, and an iron-silver-gold spike from Arizona — and no spike represented Utah, while Mormon church leaders were conspicuous by their absence ([Utah History Encyclopedia](https://historytogo.utah.gov/post/golden-spike/)). At 12:47 p.m. the actual last spike — an ordinary iron spike — was driven into a regular tie; Stanford and Thomas Durant both missed it, and the telegraph operator clicked three dots: "done" ([Utah History Encyclopedia](https://historytogo.utah.gov/post/golden-spike/)).

## Canyons, lakes, and the Mighty Five

More than half of Utah lies within the Colorado Plateau, a high region cut by brilliantly colored canyons; the western third is part of the Great Basin, a broad, flat, desertlike area, and the northeast holds the Middle Rockies — the Uinta Mountains, one of the few American ranges running east-west, and the Wasatch Range ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Utah)). The Great Salt Lake lies in the northeastern Great Basin; southwest of it spreads the Great Salt Lake Desert, about 4,000 square miles, including the Bonneville Salt Flats, the site of many automobile and motorcycle land-speed trials ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Utah)). Elevations run from 13,528 feet at Kings Peak in the Uintas to about 2,350 feet in the southwestern corner ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Utah)). The Great Salt Lake, saline Sevier Lake, and freshwater Utah Lake are the major remnants of ancient Lake Bonneville, which during the Pleistocene covered an area as large as Lake Michigan ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Utah)).

Utah's five national parks — Arches, Bryce Canyon, Canyonlands, Capitol Reef, and Zion — are marketed collectively as "The Mighty 5" and draw millions of visitors a year ([Visit Utah](https://www.visitutah.com/articles/dining-between-the-mighty-five)). Arches alone contains more than 2,000 natural arches, the greatest concentration in the country ([Visit Utah](https://www.visitutah.com/articles/dining-between-the-mighty-five)). Zion is noted for its colorful, deep canyons, high cliffs, and mesas, with Zion Canyon — carved by the Virgin River — about 15 miles long and 2,000 to 3,000 feet deep ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Utah)). The present-day economy rests on manufacturing, tourism, and services, in addition to agriculture and mining ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Utah)).

## The fastest-growing state — and the bank behind the fintechs

Utah grew faster than any other state in the decade to 2020, its population rising 18% — about half a million more people — according to Census numbers analyzed by the Salt Lake Tribune ([Salt Lake Tribune](https://www.shane.news/articles/utah-changing.html)). There are now three times as many people in Utah as fifty years ago, driven by both migration and a high fertility rate; four in ten Utahns were born somewhere else, and about 20% of newcomers in the past decade came from California ([Salt Lake Tribune](https://www.shane.news/articles/utah-changing.html)). Utah remains the youngest state in the nation — the median Utahn is just over 31 years old, almost four years younger than the next youngest state — and median income rose 18% between 2010 and 2019 after inflation ([Salt Lake Tribune](https://www.shane.news/articles/utah-changing.html)). Technology firms have branded the Wasatch Front corridor "Silicon Slopes," and Gardner Policy Institute analysts project the population to at least double within 50 years ([Salt Lake Tribune](https://www.shane.news/articles/utah-changing.html)).

Less visible, but at least as consequential for the national economy, is Utah's role as the charter home of American fintech lending. Under Title 7 of the Utah Code, the Utah Department of Financial Institutions is authorized to charter and regulate banks, industrial banks, and credit unions — and [WebBank](/organizations/webbank/) was chartered by the department in 1997 as an industrial bank ([Utah Department of Financial Institutions](https://dfi.utah.gov/?p=163)). As of December 2015 the department regulated 42 banks with $259 billion in assets, all subject to regular recurring examinations ([Utah Department of Financial Institutions](https://dfi.utah.gov/?p=163)). That Utah industrial-bank charter is the legal foundation on which [Avant](/organizations/avant/) and other online lenders operate nationally: Avant's branded credit products are originated by WebBank, a Utah-chartered industrial bank, under a lending-program agreement — a structure litigated in federal court in *Meade v. Avant* in 2018. The state that once exported copper and coal now exports the banking charter behind a large share of American consumer fintech.

## Two Winter Games

Salt Lake City hosted the Olympic Winter Games in 2002, and 32 years later the Games are coming back: at the 142nd International Olympic Committee Session in Paris on July 24, 2024, Salt Lake City–Utah was awarded the 2034 Winter Games with 83 affirmative votes out of a possible 89 ([Team USA](https://www.teamusa.com/news/2024/july/24/utahs-olympic-and-paralympic-winter-games-dream-becomes-reality-once-again)). The bid's strengths were its existing world-class venues, 100% political support across all levels, 80% public support, and the legacy of 2002 ([Team USA](https://www.teamusa.com/news/2024/july/24/utahs-olympic-and-paralympic-winter-games-dream-becomes-reality-once-again)). There will be just one Olympic Village, and all venues sit within 60 miles of each other; Utah Olympic Park, Utah Olympic Oval, and the Soldier Hollow Nordic Center — all built for 2002 — will host events again in 2034 ([Team USA](https://www.teamusa.com/news/2024/july/24/utahs-olympic-and-paralympic-winter-games-dream-becomes-reality-once-again)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is Utah called the Beehive State?**
The nickname reflects the state's motto, "Industry," and the cooperative, industrious ethos of its Mormon pioneer settlers, who built a self-sufficient economy in an arid valley after arriving in 1847 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Utah)).

**When did Utah become a state?**
Utah was admitted to the Union on January 4, 1896, as the 45th state, after seven attempts ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Utah); [Utah State Archives](https://archives.utah.gov/2021/05/27/utahs-road-to-statehood-the-obstacle-of-polygamy/)).

**What are Utah's five national parks?**
Arches, Bryce Canyon, Canyonlands, Capitol Reef, and Zion, marketed together as "The Mighty 5" ([Visit Utah](https://www.visitutah.com/articles/dining-between-the-mighty-five)). Arches contains more than 2,000 natural arches, the greatest concentration in the country ([Visit Utah](https://www.visitutah.com/articles/dining-between-the-mighty-five)).

**Why do so many fintech companies use Utah banks?**
Utah's Department of Financial Institutions charters industrial banks, and WebBank, chartered in Utah in 1997, originates loans for online lenders such as Avant under lending-program agreements ([Utah Department of Financial Institutions](https://dfi.utah.gov/?p=163)).

**Will Utah host the Olympics again?**
Yes — Salt Lake City–Utah was awarded the 2034 Winter Games on July 24, 2024, reusing the venues built for 2002 ([Team USA](https://www.teamusa.com/news/2024/july/24/utahs-olympic-and-paralympic-winter-games-dream-becomes-reality-once-again)).
