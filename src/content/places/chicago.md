## Overview

Chicago is the most populous city in Illinois and in the American Midwest, and the third-most populous city in the [United States](/places/united-states/), with 2,746,388 residents at the 2020 census ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Chicago)). It sits at the mouth of the Chicago River on the southwestern shore of Lake [Michigan](/places/michigan/), covers 228 square miles, and anchors a metropolitan area of 9,618,502 people ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Chicago)). Founded as a trading post in 1779 and incorporated as a city in 1837, it grew on a river-and-rail crossroads, burned down in 1871, and rebuilt itself into the birthplace of the skyscraper ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Chicago)).

The city was still growing in the 2020s: the Census Bureau's July 2024 estimate put its population at 2,721,308 — still third, behind [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) (8,478,072) and [Los Angeles](/places/los-angeles/) (3,878,704) — after adding 22,164 residents in a year, the seventh-largest numeric gain of any American city ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.Census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025/vintage-2024-popest.html)). Its nicknames are the Windy City, the Second City, and the City of Big Shoulders ([Choose Chicago](https://www.choosechicago.com/press-media/toolkit/chicago-facts/)).

## From trading post to railroad hub

Chicago's name was first recorded in 1688 as Chigagou, an Algonquian word meaning "onion field" ([Choose Chicago](https://www.choosechicago.com/press-media/toolkit/chicago-facts/)). Its site — a small settlement at the mouth of the Chicago River — was unremarkable, but its position at the southwestern end of the Great Lakes system could not have been more ideal as the country expanded westward in the 19th century ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Chicago)).

The first permanent non-Native resident was Jean Baptiste Point du Sable, who settled in 1779 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Chicago)). Du Sable, a trapper and merchant of French and African parentage from Haiti, settled with his Potawatomi wife Kittihawa and built the trading post that evolved into Chicago ([Choose Chicago](https://www.choosechicago.com/press-media/toolkit/chicago-facts/)). What made the settlement grow was geography: the Chicago River formed a critical link in the great waterway between the Atlantic Ocean and the Mississippi River, and with the rise of railways the young city became the country's railway hub ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Chicago)). Incorporated as a town in 1833 with about 350 people, it grew once work began on the Illinois and Michigan Canal, linking Lake Michigan to the Illinois River; the railway age began in 1848, and Chicago became the focal point of a "golden funnel" for grain, lumber, and meat ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Chicago)). Between 1890 and 1982 it was second only to New York City in population ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Chicago)).

## The fire that rebuilt a city

On the evening of October 8, 1871, a fire began in a barn on De Koven Street, on the city's West Side, belonging to Patrick and Catherine O'Leary ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Chicago-fire-of-1871)). What started it is not known: vandals, milk thieves, spontaneous combustion, even the O'Learys' legendary cow could have started the blaze ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Chicago-fire-of-1871)). Months without rain had parched a city built mainly of wood, and a major fire the previous night had exhausted firefighters and damaged equipment ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Chicago-fire-of-1871); [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/chicago-fire-1871)). A steady southwest wind carried the flames from block to block, and the fire leapt the Chicago River, burning until it reached Lake Michigan thirty hours later ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Chicago-fire-of-1871); [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/chicago-fire-1871)).

The toll was staggering: about 300 lives, some 17,450 buildings destroyed across almost 3.5 square miles, and $200 million in damage, with one-third of the city in ruins and nearly 100,000 people — about a third of the population — homeless ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Chicago-fire-of-1871)). But the city's infrastructure survived, and Chicago rebuilt quickly, reaching a population of half a million by 1880 ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/chicago-fire-1871); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Chicago-fire-of-1871)). The rebuilding drew architects including Louis Sullivan and Daniel H. Burnham, who stayed on to design taller downtown buildings ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Chicago-fire-of-1871)). The payoff was architectural history: the world's first steel-frame skyscraper, the Home Insurance Building, was erected in 1885, and by 1890 Chicago was the second-largest city in America ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/chicago-fire-1871)).

## The White City and its shadow

The rebuilt city's coming-out party was the World's Columbian Exposition of 1893, the "White City" built on the banks of Lake Michigan in and around Jackson Park ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/culture-magazines/worlds-columbian-exposition)). Chicago had pushed aside New York and St. Louis to win the fair, and the "Windy City" nickname itself came not from lake breezes but from the braggadocio of that campaign ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Chicago)). The exposition occupied 630 acres, its buildings designed under Daniel Burnham, with facades of plaster, cement, and jute painted white — hence "White City" ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/culture-magazines/worlds-columbian-exposition)). The only surviving building built for the fair is the Palace of Fine Arts, now the Museum of Science and Industry ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/culture-magazines/worlds-columbian-exposition)).

The fair's most famous attraction stood on the Midway Plaisance, the amusement strip designed by Frederick Law Olmsted: the world's first Ferris wheel, built by 32-year-old engineer George Washington Gale Ferris ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/culture-magazines/worlds-columbian-exposition)). It carried 36 cars of 60 passengers each and logged more than 1.45 million paid admissions by November 1893, for a $395,000 profit ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/culture-magazines/worlds-columbian-exposition)). The fair introduced brand names that became staples of daily life, including Cream of Wheat, Shredded Wheat, Pabst Beer, and Juicy Fruit gum ([Choose Chicago](https://www.choosechicago.com/press-media/toolkit/chicago-facts/); [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/culture-magazines/worlds-columbian-exposition)). But the fair had a shadow the celebratory accounts skip: African Americans, while free to come and go, were made to feel unwelcome, and Frederick Douglass saw the fake "African villages" as an attempt "to exhibit the Negro as a repulsive savage" ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/culture-magazines/worlds-columbian-exposition)).

## A crossroads economy — and the world's busiest airfield

It is known as the United States' railroad capital, with more major railroads serving the city than any other place in America, and its CTA operates the nation's second-largest public transportation system ([Choose Chicago](https://www.choosechicago.com/press-media/toolkit/chicago-facts/)). In 1900 the city reversed the flow of the Chicago River so it would empty into the Mississippi River rather than Lake Michigan ([Choose Chicago](https://www.choosechicago.com/press-media/toolkit/chicago-facts/)).

The financial side of that crossroads is CME Group, the $47 billion global derivatives trading giant headquartered in Chicago ([Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/investing/cme-linchpin-of-chicago-finance-says-it-s-not-tied-to-the-city)). The tie is ambivalent: chief executive Terry Duffy has said the exchange is in Chicago for "legacy reasons" and barely relies on face-to-face pit trading — but that it has no plans to leave ([Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/investing/cme-linchpin-of-chicago-finance-says-it-s-not-tied-to-the-city)). The newer financial Chicago is fintech: [Avant](/organizations/avant/), founded in Chicago in 2012 as AvantCredit by CEO [Al Goldstein](/people/al-goldstein/) with co-founders [John Sun](/people/john-sun/) and [Paul Zhang](/people/paul-zhang/), built a machine-learning lending platform and launched in July 2013 ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/avantcredit-secures-20m-to-make-financial-loans-more-accessible-with-big-data)).

Above it all is O'Hare. In 2025 O'Hare International Airport recorded 860,015 aircraft operations, reclaiming the title of the world's busiest airfield by flight operations for the first time since 2019 ([City of Chicago](https://www.chicago.gov/content/city/en/depts/mayor/press_room/press_releases/2026/april/ohare-worlds-busiest.html)). The measure matters: by passengers O'Hare ranked sixth globally, with 84.8 million in 2025, so the "busiest" claim is about flight operations, not foot traffic ([City of Chicago](https://www.chicago.gov/content/city/en/depts/mayor/press_room/press_releases/2026/april/ohare-worlds-busiest.html)).

## Culture, firsts, and the city of neighborhoods

Chicago's skyline is its signature: Willis Tower (formerly Sears Tower) is the second-tallest building in the Western Hemisphere at 1,730 feet, holding first place until New York's One World Trade Center was built in 2014 ([Choose Chicago](https://www.choosechicago.com/press-media/toolkit/chicago-facts/)). The city is the birthplace of Frank Lloyd Wright's Prairie School of architecture ([Choose Chicago](https://www.choosechicago.com/press-media/toolkit/chicago-facts/)). The Atomic Age began at 3:25 p.m. on December 2, 1942, when an atom was split for the first time under the football stands of Stagg Field at the University of Chicago ([Choose Chicago](https://www.choosechicago.com/press-media/toolkit/chicago-facts/)). In 1943 Ike Sewell invented deep-dish pizza at Pizzeria Uno, and in 1960 the first televised U.S. presidential candidates' debate was broadcast from Chicago's CBS Studios, between John F. Kennedy and Richard Nixon ([Choose Chicago](https://www.choosechicago.com/press-media/toolkit/chicago-facts/)). House music originated on the South Side in 1977 at the club The Warehouse ([Choose Chicago](https://www.choosechicago.com/press-media/toolkit/chicago-facts/)). In the record books: Albert A. Michelson became the first American to receive a Nobel Prize in the sciences in 1907, and Gwendolyn Brooks became the first African-American woman to win a Pulitzer Prize in 1950 ([Choose Chicago](https://www.choosechicago.com/press-media/toolkit/chicago-facts/)). Michael Jordan led the Chicago Bulls to six NBA championships ([Choose Chicago](https://www.choosechicago.com/press-media/toolkit/chicago-facts/)).

The city is governed by a mayor and city council; its mayor is Brandon Johnson, elected on April 4, 2023, defeating Paul Vallas in a runoff ([Associated Press](https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/news/world-policy/article/2023/04/05/johnson-elected-chicago-mayor)). Chicagoans call their city the "city of neighborhoods," even though that description can carry connotations of segregation by race, ethnicity, and social class — and the city is divided into 77 community areas ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Chicago); [Choose Chicago](https://www.choosechicago.com/press-media/toolkit/chicago-facts/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is Chicago called the Windy City?**
Not because of lake breezes. The nickname came from the city's braggadocio — dramatically in the 1890s, when Chicago pushed aside New York and St. Louis to win the World's Columbian Exposition of 1893 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Chicago)).

**How big is Chicago?**
Chicago covers 228 square miles and had 2,746,388 residents at the 2020 census, making it the third-most populous city in the United States; its metropolitan area had 9,618,502 people in 2020 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Chicago)). The Census Bureau's July 2024 estimate put the city at 2,721,308 ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.Census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025/vintage-2024-popest.html)).

**What caused the Great Chicago Fire?**
It is not known. The fire began on October 8, 1871, in the De Koven Street barn of Patrick and Catherine O'Leary; vandals, milk thieves, spontaneous combustion, or the O'Learys' legendary cow could have started it, and city officials never determined the cause ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/event/Chicago-fire-of-1871)).

**Is O'Hare really the world's busiest airport?**
By flight operations, yes: O'Hare recorded 860,015 aircraft operations in 2025, the most of any airport in the world according to Airports Council International data ([City of Chicago](https://www.chicago.gov/content/city/en/depts/mayor/press_room/press_releases/2026/april/ohare-worlds-busiest.html)). By passengers it ranked sixth globally with 84.8 million ([City of Chicago](https://www.chicago.gov/content/city/en/depts/mayor/press_room/press_releases/2026/april/ohare-worlds-busiest.html)).

**What is Chicago's connection to Avant?**
Avant is a Chicago consumer fintech founded in the city in 2012 as AvantCredit by CEO Al Goldstein with co-founders John Sun and Paul Zhang; its machine-learning lending platform launched in July 2013 ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/avantcredit-secures-20m-to-make-financial-loans-more-accessible-with-big-data)).
