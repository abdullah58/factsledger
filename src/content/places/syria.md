## Overview

Syria, officially the Syrian Arab Republic, is a country in West Asia on the east coast of the Mediterranean Sea, with its capital at Damascus and a population of 24,261,882 (2025 estimate) across 187,437 square kilometres ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)). It gained independence in 1946 at the end of the French mandate, was ruled by the Asad family from 1970, and descended into civil war in 2011 ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)). On 8 December 2024 rebels captured Damascus and overthrew President Bashar al-Asad, ending more than 50 years of Asad rule ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)). Ahmed al-Sharaa became president in 2025, and the [United States](/places/united-states/) and the [United Nations](/organizations/united-nations/) have since dismantled most of the sanctions imposed on the former regime ([US Department of State](https://www.state.gov/syria-sanctions/); [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166303)).

## Land and people

Syria is bounded by [Turkey](/places/turkey/) to the north, Iraq to the east and south-east, Jordan to the south, and Lebanon and Israel to the south-west, with a Mediterranean coastline of about 180 km ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Syria)). The Euphrates River is the most important water source and the only navigable river in Syria; Mount Hermon, at 2,814 metres, is the country's highest point ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Syria); [CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)). The capital, Damascus, sits on the Barada River in an oasis at the foot of Mount Qasioun and is thought to be one of the world's oldest continuously inhabited cities ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Syria); [CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)).

The population is Arab about 50 percent, Alawite about 15 percent, Kurd about 10 percent, Levantine about 10 percent, and other groups about 15 percent, including Druze, Ismaili, Assyrian, Turkoman, and Armenian communities ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)). Muslims make up 87 percent (Sunni 74 percent, and Alawi, Ismaili, and Shia 13 percent), Christians 10 percent, and Druze 3 percent ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)). Arabic is the official language, with Kurdish, Armenian, Aramaic, Circassian, French, and English also spoken ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)). Population density is highest along the Mediterranean coast and in the major cities of Damascus, Aleppo, and Homs ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)).

## From mandate to the Asad era

After World War I, France acquired a mandate over the northern portion of the former Ottoman province of Syria and administered the area until granting it independence in 1946 ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)). The new country lacked political stability and experienced a series of military coups; Syria united with Egypt in 1958 to form the United Arab Republic, and in 1961 the two separated and the Syrian Arab Republic was re-established ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)). In the 1967 Arab-Israeli War, Syria lost control of the Golan Heights to Israel ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)).

In 1970 Hafiz al-Asad, a member of the socialist Ba'ath Party and the minority Alawi sect, seized power in a bloodless coup and brought political stability to the country ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)). After his death in 2000, his son Bashar al-Asad became president, continuing his father's authoritarian style of government and using the military and security services to quash dissent ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Syria)). The Asad family's rule, father and son together, lasted more than 50 years ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)).

## Civil war, 2011–2024

In the wake of uprisings elsewhere in the region, antigovernment protests broke out in the southern province of Dar'a in 2011, calling for the legalisation of political parties, the removal of corrupt local officials, and the repeal of the restrictive Emergency Law ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)). Demonstrations spread, and the government responded with concessions but also with military force and detentions that led to extended clashes and eventually civil war ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)). International pressure intensified after 2011 as the Arab League, the EU, Turkey, and the US expanded economic sanctions against the Asad regime ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)). Russia launched a military intervention on behalf of the regime in 2015, and the regime regained opposition-held territory until a stalemate set in around 2020 ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)).

The human cost was immense: the United Nations estimated in 2022 that at least 306,000 people had died during the civil war, approximately 6.7 million Syrians were internally displaced, 14.6 million people were in need of humanitarian assistance, and an additional 5.6 million Syrians were registered refugees in Turkey, Jordan, Iraq, Egypt, and North Africa ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)). The conflict remains one of the two largest displacement crises worldwide, the other being the full-scale invasion of Ukraine ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)). On 8 December 2024 Syrian rebels captured Damascus and overthrew President Bashar al-Asad; the former president and his family fled to Moscow, where they were granted political asylum ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)).

## Transition and the sanctions reversal

Ahmed al-Sharaa, leader of Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham (HTS) — the Islamist armed group whose late-2024 offensive drove al-Asad into exile — became president in 2025 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Syria)). The transition posed a sanctions problem: HTS had been proscribed as a terrorist organisation by the United Nations since 2014, and al-Sharaa himself, under his former name Mohammed al-Jolani, was subject to an asset freeze and travel ban ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166303)). The dismantling of the sanctions architecture then proceeded in stages, and the sequence is the profile's most useful reference for sanctions work:

| Date | Action |
|---|---|
| 13 May 2025 | President Trump announces the US will lift sanctions on Syria |
| 30 June 2025 | Executive Order 14312 revokes six executive orders and terminates the national emergency behind comprehensive US sanctions |
| 8 July 2025 | US revokes HTS's Foreign Terrorist Organization designation |
| 26 August 2025 | Syrian Sanctions Regulations removed from the Code of Federal Regulations |
| 6–7 November 2025 | UN Security Council delists al-Sharaa and Interior Minister Anas Khattab; US removes both from the SDGT list |
| 18 December 2025 | US legislation repeals the Caesar Act |
| 24 August 2026 | US rescinds Syria's State Sponsor of Terrorism designation and removes HTS's SDGT designation |

Executive Order 14312 revoked the six executive orders that formed the foundation of the Syria sanctions programme and terminated the national emergency underlying them; the Syrian Sanctions Regulations were removed from the Code of Federal Regulations on 26 August 2025, and persons designated solely under those orders were removed from the Treasury's SDN List ([US Department of State](https://www.state.gov/syria-sanctions/)). On 8 July 2025 the Secretary of State revoked HTS's Foreign Terrorist Organization designation, and on 7 November 2025 the United States removed al-Sharaa and Interior Minister Anas Khattab from the Specially Designated Global Terrorist list; the UN Security Council similarly delisted both from the 1267 ISIL and Al-Qaida sanctions list on 6 November 2025, with 14 members voting in favour and China abstaining ([US Department of State](https://www.state.gov/syria-sanctions/); [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166303)). On 18 December 2025 President Trump signed legislation repealing the Caesar Act, removing the threat of mandatory sanctions for foreign persons supporting the Government of Syria ([US Department of State](https://www.state.gov/syria-sanctions/)). Finally, on 24 August 2026 the Secretary of State rescinded Syria's State Sponsor of Terrorism designation and removed HTS's SDGT designation ([US Department of State](https://www.state.gov/syria-sanctions/)).

Targeted sanctions remain: Bashar al-Asad and other destabilising regional actors stay on the SDN List under other authorities, including designations for human rights abuses, drug trafficking, and support for terrorism, and the United States maintains sanctions on those supporting terrorism, [Iran](/places/iran/) and its proxies, and Syria's past proliferation activities ([US Department of State](https://www.state.gov/syria-sanctions/)). The US-sponsored UN resolution welcomed the interim government's commitments to allow unhindered humanitarian access, fight terrorism including ISIL and al-Qaeda, and protect human rights ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166303)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the capital of Syria?**
Damascus, located on the Barada River in an oasis at the foot of Mount Qasioun. It is Syria's capital and largest city, with a population of 2.585 million in 2023 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Syria); [CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)).

**When did Syria become independent?**
In 1946, when France ended its mandate over the country ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)).

**When did the Assad government fall?**
On 8 December 2024, when rebels captured Damascus and overthrew President Bashar al-Asad, who fled to Moscow. The Asad regime had ruled Syria for over 50 years ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/syria.html)).

**Who leads Syria now?**
Ahmed al-Sharaa, leader of Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham, became president in 2025 after Assad's overthrow ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Syria)).

**Have US sanctions on Syria been lifted?**
Mostly. Executive Order 14312 (30 June 2025) revoked the comprehensive sanctions, the Caesar Act was repealed in December 2025, and Syria's State Sponsor of Terrorism designation was rescinded in August 2026. Targeted sanctions on Bashar al-Asad and his associates remain ([US Department of State](https://www.state.gov/syria-sanctions/)).
