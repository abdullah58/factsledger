## Overview

Thailand, officially the Kingdom of Thailand, is a Southeast Asian constitutional monarchy of about 71.6 million people, governed from Bangkok, its capital since 1782. It covers 513,120 square kilometres in the centre of mainland Southeast Asia, between Myanmar, Laos, Cambodia and Malaysia, and its economy — worth $577 billion in 2025 — rests on manufacturing, agriculture and tourism ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/?locations=XT-TH); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Thailand); [CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/thailand.html)).

Thailand is the former Siam: it was never brought under European colonial domination, and it has been a constitutional monarchy since the revolution of 1932, though the military has repeatedly taken power through coups ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Thailand)). Its official language is Thai, and the vast majority of its people follow Theravada Buddhism, with a Muslim minority concentrated mainly in the south and around Bangkok ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Thailand)). Thailand was a founding member of ASEAN in 1967, signing the Bangkok Declaration alongside Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines and Singapore ([ASEAN](https://asean.org/about-asean)).

## Geography and regions

Thailand sits wholly within the tropics, and its landscape falls into four broad pieces: the hilly, forested areas of the northern frontier, the fertile rice fields of the central plains, the broad plateau of the northeast, and the rugged coasts along the narrow southern peninsula ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Thailand)). The north is mountainous highland country, topped by Doi Inthanon; the northeast, known as Isan, is the Khorat Plateau, bordered to the east by the Mekong River; the centre is the flat Chao Phraya river valley running into the Gulf of Thailand; and the south is the narrow Kra Isthmus widening into the Malay Peninsula.

Administratively, Thailand is a unitary state divided into provinces, plus Bangkok, which is run as a special administrative area. For planning, the provinces are grouped into six regions — Centre, East, North, Northeast, South and the Bangkok Metropolitan Area — under the country's national development plan ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/multi-dimensional-review-of-thailand-volume-2_9789264307674-en/full-report/component-6.html)). Settlement follows the rivers and the coast, with Bangkok dominant and Chiang Mai, Nakhon Ratchasima, Khon Kaen, Pattaya and Hat Yai the main regional centres ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Thailand)).

## From Siam to a constitutional monarchy

Siam, as Thailand was officially called until 1939, was never brought under European colonial domination — the only Southeast Asian country to avoid direct Western rule — and independent Siam was ruled by an absolute monarchy until a revolution in 1932 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Thailand)). Since then Thailand has been a constitutional monarchy, and all subsequent constitutions have provided for an elected parliament, but political authority has often been held by the military, which has taken power through coups ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Thailand)). The pattern recurred in 2006, when the military overthrew an elected government; new parliamentary elections followed in 2007.

The current settlement dates to the 2017 constitution, Thailand's 20th since 1932, promulgated on 6 April 2017 and signed by King Maha Vajiralongkorn ([Council of State of Thailand](https://En.Wikisource.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Kingdom_of_Thailand_(2017)); [NPR](https://www.khsu.org/regional-interests/2017-04-06/thai-king-signs-military-backed-constitution)). Voters had approved it in a referendum in August 2016, two years after the military seized power in a coup. Today the country is a unitary parliamentary constitutional monarchy with a bicameral National Assembly: a 200-member Senate and a 500-member House of Representatives ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Thailand)).

## Economy: factories, farms and visitors

Thailand is an upper-middle-income economy whose output the World Bank puts at $577.01 billion in 2025, or $8,056.6 per person, growing 2.4% that year ([World Bank](https://data.worldbank.org/?locations=XT-TH)). In purchasing-power terms the CIA World Factbook puts GDP at $1.558 trillion in 2024, with real GDP per capita of $21,700 ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/thailand.html)). Services produce about 60% of GDP and industry about 31%, while agriculture contributes only about 9% — yet agriculture still employs roughly one-third of the workforce, mostly in informal and vulnerable jobs ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/multi-dimensional-review-of-thailand-volume-2_9789264307674-en/full-report/component-6.html)). The export mix tells the same story of a manufacturing hub: electronics, automobiles and parts, processed foods and agricultural commodities. Its main export partners are the [United States](/places/united-states/) (18%), [China](/places/china/) (13%) and Japan (7%), on 2023 figures ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/thailand.html)).

Tourism is the third leg, and it has recovered strongly since the pandemic: Thailand welcomed 35.5 million international tourists in 2024, generating THB 1.7 trillion — about US$54.2 billion — or approximately 9% of GDP ([FinTech News Singapore](https://fintechnews.sg/124222/thailand/top-payment-trends-among-tourists-visiting-thailand/)). Rice, rubber, sugarcane and cassava remain the farm backbone, so the industrial economy still sits on a rural base.

## Bangkok primacy — the gap other country profiles miss

Here is where standard country profiles stop and this one keeps going: Thailand's economy is extraordinarily concentrated in one city. Bangkok, as an urban agglomeration, has a population 29 times larger than the next largest city, Chiang Mai, and a GDP nearly 40 times greater than the next largest, Chon Buri ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/thailand/publication/temjuly2024)). No other urban cluster has more than 500,000 inhabitants, and one-third of all city dwellers live in Bangkok and its vicinity ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/multi-dimensional-review-of-thailand-volume-2_9789264307674-en/full-report/component-6.html)). The 2011 floods exposed how much critical industry sat in one place, and Bangkok's GDP growth now roughly equals its population growth — a mature, saturated economy ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/thailand/publication/temjuly2024)).

The regional income gap is stark: the OECD puts GDP per capita at about $25,000 in the Bangkok metropolitan area, against about $3,700 in the Northeast ([OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/multi-dimensional-review-of-thailand-volume-2_9789264307674-en/full-report/component-6.html)). But poorer regions have grown faster than Bangkok since the early 2000s, and per-capita GDP growth in secondary cities has recently run nearly 15 times Bangkok's rate ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/thailand/publication/temjuly2024)). That convergence is the story to watch in Thailand's next decade.

## FAQ

### What is Thailand's capital?

Bangkok is the capital and largest city. Known locally as Krung Thep, it became the capital in 1782, when Rama I, founder of the Chakri dynasty, moved the court to the Chao Phraya River's east bank. Today it is Thailand's cultural and commercial centre and by far its dominant city.

### Is Thailand a monarchy?

Yes — a unitary parliamentary constitutional monarchy. A hereditary monarch is head of state, while the prime minister heads the government. The legislature is the bicameral National Assembly, with a 200-member Senate and a 500-member House of Representatives. Thailand has had 20 constitutions since 1932; the current one dates to 2017.

### Was Thailand ever colonised?

No. Siam, as Thailand was called until 1939, was never brought under European colonial domination, although it ceded territory and trade privileges through unequal treaties in the 19th century. It is the only Southeast Asian country to have avoided direct Western rule.

### How big is Thailand's economy?

The World Bank puts GDP at $577.01 billion in 2025, or $8,056.6 per person, with 2.4% growth. In purchasing-power terms the CIA World Factbook puts GDP at $1.558 trillion in 2024. Services produce about 60% of GDP, industry about 31%, and agriculture about 9%.

### Why does Bangkok matter so much to Thailand?

Bangkok's urban agglomeration is 29 times the population of the next city, Chiang Mai, and its GDP is nearly 40 times that of Chon Buri. But its growth has stalled relative to its size, while secondary cities' per-capita GDP has recently grown nearly 15 times faster.
