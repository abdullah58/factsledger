## Overview

Madhya Pradesh — "central province," the heart of India — is India's second-largest state by area, spanning 3,08,000 square kilometres ([Scroll.in](https://scroll.in/article/1075103/scroll_in)). Its capital is Bhopal, chosen at the state's formation in 1956 over Jabalpur, which the States Reorganisation Commission had recommended instead ([Scroll.in](https://scroll.in/article/1075103/scroll_in)). The state was formed on 1 November 1956, when the States Reorganisation Act consolidated Madhya Bharat, Vindhya Pradesh and Bhopal into a single Hindi-speaking state ([Scroll.in](https://scroll.in/article/1075103/scroll_in)).

What a page-one search for "Madhya Pradesh" mostly offers is this settled encyclopaedic picture — geography, princely history, the Khajuraho-Sanchi tourist canon. It says nothing about the state's sharpest current turn: in the summer of 2026, Madhya Pradesh began positioning itself as a semiconductor investment destination, landing a USD 2 billion (around ₹19,000 crore) commitment from Spain's Submer Group ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/trends/madhya-pradesh-lands-rs-19-000-crore-semiconductor-investment-from-spanish-firm-13973737.html)) and a ₹6,200 crore chip-packaging facility deal from Paras Semiconductors ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/indias-paras-defences-semicon-unit-set-up-644-million-chip-packaging-facility-2026-07-22/)). That investment wave is the verified-but-under-shared story this profile leads with.

## A semiconductor destination by design

On 14 July 2026, Barcelona-headquartered Submer Group announced its roughly ₹19,000 crore investment in Madhya Pradesh's semiconductor sector at the MP Tech Growth Conclave 3.0 in Bhopal — one of the state's biggest commitments in advanced-technology manufacturing, expected to generate nearly 5,000 direct jobs ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/trends/madhya-pradesh-lands-rs-19-000-crore-semiconductor-investment-from-spanish-firm-13973737.html)). Submer is known globally for advanced liquid-cooling technology used in high-performance data centres, and the company has also sought land for a data centre in the state, with 15 acres allotted in the Acharpura industrial area within six days of the request ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/trends/madhya-pradesh-lands-rs-19-000-crore-semiconductor-investment-from-spanish-firm-13973737.html)). Reports cited by Moneycontrol indicate the Madhya Pradesh facility will also support exports to Asian markets.

Eight days later, the domestic side of the story arrived. Paras Semiconductors, a subsidiary of Paras Defence and Space Technologies, signed a memorandum of understanding with the Madhya Pradesh department of science and technology to jointly set up an outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) facility in the Indore-Ujjain region, backed by a proposed investment of ₹6,200 crore (US$643.79 million) ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/indias-paras-defences-semicon-unit-set-up-644-million-chip-packaging-facility-2026-07-22/)). An OSAT plant packages, assembles and tests foundry-made silicon wafers, turning them into finished chips; the facility will undertake advanced chip-packaging operations and reliability testing of the packaged chips ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/indias-paras-defences-semicon-unit-set-up-644-million-chip-packaging-facility-2026-07-22/)). The project is documented in detail in the FactsLedger profile of [Paras Defence and Space Technologies](https://factsledger.com/organizations/paras-defence), which records the same July 2026 MoU.

The state's political leadership is driving the pitch personally. On 18 September 2026, Chief Minister Mohan Yadav inaugurated a semiconductor lab at the Madhya Pradesh Pavilion at SEMICON India 2026 in New Delhi, where the state was showcasing investment opportunities in semiconductors and electronics ([Punjab Kesari English/IANS](https://english.punjabkesari.com/india/mp-has-great-potential-in-semiconductor-sector-youth-will-get-jobs-cm-yadav/amp/)). Yadav said the state is looking to develop Bhopal and the Indore-Ujjain metropolitan regions as potential semiconductor centres, pointing to investor-friendly policies, a land bank, a skilled workforce, power availability and robust infrastructure — plus, he argued, a central location, power-surplus status and stable law and order that give it an edge over other states ([Punjab Kesari English/IANS](https://english.punjabkesari.com/india/mp-has-great-potential-in-semiconductor-sector-youth-will-get-jobs-cm-yadav/amp/)). He framed the bet in employment terms: the state's youth would get jobs and skill-development opportunities through the sector, and the state set up a dedicated pavilion at SEMICON India for direct discussions with investors ([Punjab Kesari English/IANS](https://english.punjabkesari.com/india/mp-has-great-potential-in-semiconductor-sector-youth-will-get-jobs-cm-yadav/amp/)).

One honest caveat runs through the optimism. Moneycontrol's own reporting notes industry experts warning that semiconductor investments of this scale require long construction timelines, specialised infrastructure and a highly trained workforce before production begins — so the commitments are real, but execution is not yet demonstrated ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/trends/madhya-pradesh-lands-rs-19-000-crore-semiconductor-investment-from-spanish-firm-13973737.html)).

## From princely patchwork to the heart of India

At independence in 1947, central India was a mosaic of princely states of varying power alongside the British-administered Central Provinces and Berar ([Scroll.in](https://scroll.in/article/1075103/scroll_in)). The consolidation began with the big Maratha states: on 28 May 1948, the "United State of Gwalior, Indore and Malwa (Madhya Bharat)" was inaugurated by Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru, with Gwalior as winter capital for six-and-a-half months and Indore as summer capital for the rest ([Scroll.in](https://scroll.in/article/1075103/scroll_in)). In the east, the Rewa-centred princely states were formed into Vindhya Pradesh in 1948, while Bhopal — ruled by Nawab Hamidullah Khan and resistant to merger — was taken over by the Government of India in 1949 as a centrally administered area ([Scroll.in](https://scroll.in/article/1075103/scroll_in)). The British province meanwhile was renamed Madhya Pradesh in 1950, a Part A state with Nagpur as its capital ([Scroll.in](https://scroll.in/article/1075103/scroll_in)).

The final consolidation came with the States Reorganisation Act of 1956: Madhya Bharat, Vindhya Pradesh and Bhopal were merged into the new Madhya Pradesh, the Marathi-speaking Vidarbha region was separated to Bombay State, and Bhopal — not the Commission's choice of Jabalpur — became the capital, with 1 November 1956 as the state's official formation day ([Scroll.in](https://scroll.in/article/1075103/scroll_in)). The boundaries moved again half a century later: the Madhya Pradesh Reorganisation Act of August 2000 carved Chhattisgarh out of the state as an independent state on 1 November 2000, with Raipur as its capital ([Scroll.in](https://scroll.in/article/1075103/scroll_in)). The politics of that division ran through the people who had served the undivided state — [Ramchandra Singh Deo](https://factsledger.com/people/ramchandra-singh-deo), previously Madhya Pradesh's Water Resources Minister, became Chhattisgarh's first Finance Minister when the new state was created.

Beyond industry, the state remains a significant contributor to India's agricultural production, particularly in soybean, wheat, sugar cane and cotton, with manufacturing growth concentrated in Gwalior, Jabalpur, Bhopal and Indore ([Scroll.in](https://scroll.in/article/1075103/scroll_in)). Its agricultural identity has deep roots in figures like rice scientist [R.H. Richharia](https://factsledger.com/people/r-h-richharia), born in Nandanvara village in Hoshangabad district, who collected and catalogued some 17,000 indigenous rice cultivars across undivided Madhya Pradesh in the 1970s.

## What this profile does not claim

It does not state the state's 2011 Census population or name its largest city — both are widely repeated online but could not be verified from any source read in full for this profile. It does not claim the Submer or Paras semiconductor projects have broken ground; both are 2026 announcements and MoUs, and Moneycontrol itself cautions that execution requires long timelines and specialised infrastructure. It does not rank the state nationally beyond "second-largest by area," the one ranking its sources directly confirm. It does not narrate the Bhopal gas tragedy, the state's tiger reserves or its UNESCO sites — major subjects that fell outside the verified scope of this profile's sources.
