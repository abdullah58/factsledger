## Overview

Italy, officially the Italian Republic, is a boot-shaped southern European country with Rome as its capital, about 58.9 million people, and a unitary parliamentary republic founded on unification in 1861 and re-founded as a republic in 1946, and a founding member of the [European Union](/organizations/european-union/) with the euro as its currency ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Italy); [EURAXESS](https://euraxess.ec.europa.eu/worldwide/australia-nz/news/country-profile-italy)).

Italy comprises the boot-shaped peninsula extending far into the Mediterranean Sea, as well as Sicily, Sardinia, and a number of smaller islands, covering 302,069 square kilometres ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Italy)). More than three-fourths of the country is mountainous or highland, with the Alps along its northern boundary and the Apennines running southward the length of the peninsula, while most lowlands lie in the valley of the Po ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Italy)). Britannica's dated 2026 population estimate is 58,851,000, consistent with EURAXESS's rounded figure of about 59 million, which makes Italy the third largest state in the European Union ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Italy); [EURAXESS](https://euraxess.ec.europa.eu/worldwide/australia-nz/news/country-profile-italy)).

## From fragmentation to republic

Italy has been inhabited since Paleolithic times, and its recorded story starts with a civilisation that Rome itself displaced: the Etruscan civilisation arose in the 9th century BCE and was overthrown by the Romans in the 4th–3rd centuries BCE ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Italy)). Barbarian invasions of the 4th–5th centuries CE destroyed the Western Roman Empire, and Italy's political fragmentation lasted for centuries without diminishing its impact on European culture, notably during the Renaissance ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Italy)).

Unification, the Risorgimento, reversed that fragmentation in stages: when Napoleonic rule ended in 1815, Italy was again a grouping of independent states, the Risorgimento united most of Italy, including Sicily and Sardinia, by 1861, and peninsular unification was completed by 1870 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Italy)). Italy joined the Allies in World War I, but social unrest in the 1920s brought Benito Mussolini's Fascist movement to power, and Italy allied itself with Nazi Germany in World War II ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Italy)). Defeated by the Allies in 1943, Italy proclaimed itself a republic in 1946, became a charter member of [NATO](/organizations/nato/) in 1949 and of the European Economic Community, now the European Union, and completed setting up regional legislatures with limited autonomy in the 1970s ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Italy)). After World War II, Britannica notes, Italy experienced rapid changes of government but remained socially stable, as a republic with two legislative houses, a president as head of state and a prime minister as head of government ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Italy)).

## A services-and-manufacturing economy that grows slowly

Italy's economy is based largely on services and manufacturing, and its exports include machinery and transport equipment, chemicals, textiles, clothing and shoes, and food products such as olive oil, wine, fruit, and tomatoes ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Italy)). The current official picture is slow, consumption-led growth: in the second quarter of 2026, GDP grew 0.2 per cent on the previous quarter, driven by private consumption and investments, continuing an expansion that began in the third quarter of 2025 ([ISTAT](https://www.istat.it/en/press-release/note-on-italian-economy-july-august-2026/)). Reuters gives the same quarterly shape — GDP up 0.3 per cent in the first quarter and 0.2 per cent in the second — and adds that in 2025 the economy grew 0.5 per cent, and had not exceeded 1 per cent in the last three years despite tens of billions of euros of EU COVID-19 recovery funds ([Reuters](https://q106fm.com/2026/10/09/italy-industrial-output-dives-unexpectedly-in-august/)).


The labour market tells a quieter story about ageing: in July 2026 employment was broadly unchanged at 24,370,000, and unemployment fell to 5.8 per cent ([ISTAT](https://www.istat.it/en/press-release/note-on-italian-economy-july-august-2026/)). Between 2021 and 2026, ISTAT finds, employment growth mainly concerned the 50–64 age group, up 7.8 percentage points, driven by progressive ageing and pension reforms, with permanent staff increasing and fixed-term contracts falling ([ISTAT](https://www.istat.it/en/press-release/note-on-italian-economy-july-august-2026/)). The implication is stark: an economy whose job growth is concentrated among workers aged 50–64 is adapting to an older population, not just creating new jobs ([ISTAT](https://www.istat.it/en/press-release/note-on-italian-economy-july-august-2026/)).

## Heritage, research and a new acquirer story

Italy currently holds the highest number of [UNESCO](/organizations/unesco/) World Heritage sites in the world, with 62 sites within its borders, according to Italia.it ([Italia.it](https://www.italia.it/en/italy/things-to-do/art-culture/unesco-sites?page=2)). Six are recognised natural sites, including the Dolomites and Mount Etna, and the first Italian sites inscribed were the rock art of the Camonica Valley in 1979 and the historic centre of Rome in 1980 ([Italia.it](https://www.italia.it/en/italy/things-to-do/art-culture/unesco-sites?page=2)). Eight cultural landscapes and 17 intangible traditions are also recognised, including the Amalfi Coast and the art of Neapolitan pizzaiuoli ([Italia.it](https://www.italia.it/en/italy/things-to-do/art-culture/unesco-sites?page=2)). Research depth matches it: EURAXESS records 20 Nobel laureates among Italian citizens, about 161,000 researchers in 2022, fourth in the EU, and about EUR 26 billion spent on research and development in 2021 ([EURAXESS](https://euraxess.ec.europa.eu/worldwide/australia-nz/news/country-profile-italy)). Four of the ten oldest universities in the world are in Italy — Bologna (1088), Padua (1222), Naples Federico II (1224), and Siena (1240) — and small and medium-sized enterprises make up 92 per cent of active businesses ([EURAXESS](https://euraxess.ec.europa.eu/worldwide/australia-nz/news/country-profile-italy)).

Italy's newest export is an acquirer. [Bending Spoons](/organizations/bending-spoons/), based in [Milan](/places/milan/), Italy, acquires and improves digital businesses, and in March 2026 it served over 500 million monthly active users ([Willkie Farr & Gallagher](https://www.willkie.com/news/2026/08/willkie-advises-bending-spoons-on-1-285-billion-acquisition-of-airtable)). On 4 August 2026 it announced a definitive agreement to acquire [Airtable](/organizations/airtable/) in an all-cash transaction valuing Airtable at an enterprise value of $1.285 billion, implying an equity value of approximately $2.25 billion with Airtable's net cash — its first acquisition since its Nasdaq listing on 1 July 2026 ([Willkie Farr & Gallagher](https://www.willkie.com/news/2026/08/willkie-advises-bending-spoons-on-1-285-billion-acquisition-of-airtable)). TechCrunch frames the deal in the same terms: Airtable, founded in 2013, had raised more than $1.4 billion and peaked at over $11 billion in 2021, with annual recurring revenue growing over 20 per cent year over year to approximately $480 million as of June 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/04/bending-spoons-to-buy-airtable-for-1-28b/)). TechCrunch describes Bending Spoons' general playbook — acquiring companies trading at a discount to their private valuations, trimming staff, streamlining products, and trying to make them run profitably — but no source read in this pass states a specific plan for Airtable, so none is claimed here ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/04/bending-spoons-to-buy-airtable-for-1-28b/)).
