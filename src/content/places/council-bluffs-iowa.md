# Council Bluffs, Iowa

**Council Bluffs is a city of about 62,700 in western Iowa, on the east
bank of the Missouri River opposite Omaha, Nebraska — the county seat and
largest city of Pottawattamie County, and the place Congress designated
"Mile 0" of the first transcontinental railroad.** It began as a fur
trading post, became the Mormon town of Kanesville, and today hosts one
of Google's largest data-center campuses alongside its railroad museums.

## From Mile 0 to the cloud

In 1853, a 22-year-old railroad surveyor named Grenville M. Dodge arrived
to find a Missouri River terminus for the Rock Island Railroad — and
chose Council Bluffs. Five years later he met an Illinois railroad lawyer
named Abraham Lincoln here; Lincoln visited again in 1859 to deliver a
campaign speech. After Lincoln signed the Pacific Railroad Act (July 1,
1862), his November 1863 order placed the transcontinental railroad's
eastern terminus in Iowa, and on March 7, 1864, Congress officially chose
Council Bluffs as Mile 0. By 1916 the city had seven railroads and nine
passenger depots (Rails to Trails Conservancy, drawing on Union Pacific
Railroad Museum curator Patricia LaBounty).

The railroad past is still visible: the Union Pacific Railroad Museum sits
downtown in the former 1903 Carnegie library building, and a Great
American Rail-Trail historical marker at 99 S. 25th St. commemorates the
1863 Mile 0 designation on what is now the 3,700-mile cross-country rail
trail. And the same flat, well-connected ground drew a second act:
Google built a $600 million data center here in 2007, expanded it in
2012, 2015, 2024, and 2025, and says it has invested more than $20 billion
in Iowa since 2007, including a $7 billion 2025 investment covering the
Council Bluffs expansion and a new Cedar Rapids campus.

## History

The city's official chronology begins in 1824, when St. Louis businessman
Francois Guittar established Traders Point — "the first white settlement
on the western boundary of Iowa Territory" — trading deer, elk, and
buffalo hides with local tribes. When Iowa became the 29th state on
December 28, 1846, the town was renamed Kanesville after Thomas L. Kane,
who helped secure federal permission for the Mormons' 1846–47 winter
encampment; Brigham Young was sustained as the second prophet and
president of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in the
Kanesville Tabernacle, which 200 Mormon pioneers built in two and a half
weeks.

By 1852 most Mormons had moved west, and the town took the name Council
Bluffs — after the site roughly 20 miles north where the Lewis and Clark
Expedition had sat in council with the Otoe Tribe. Cornelius Voorhis was
elected the first mayor in 1853.

An 1873 travelogue caught the town mid-boom: "some 10,000 people," "the
oldest and largest in Western Iowa," "the seat of Pottawattomie County,"
formerly Kanesville "peopled chiefly by Mormons," and "the western
terminus of the three great railroad lines" which "made a hard fight with
Omaha for the eastern terminus of the Union Pacific." The civic buildout
followed: Squirrel Cage jail (1867), a two-story courthouse (1868), the
Iowa School for the Deaf (1870), the first fire department (1883),
Children's Square USA (1883), the second U.S. city with electric trolleys
(1886), and St. Bernard's Hospital (1887). Notable 19th-century citizens
included Amelia Jenks Bloomer and Lee de Forest.

## Council Bluffs today

The Census Bureau estimates the city's population at 62,680 (July 1,
2025), down 0.2% from the 2020 Census count of 62,799. It is
predominantly white (85.4% white alone, 81.2% white alone not Hispanic or
Latino), with 11.6% Hispanic or Latino, 7.3% two or more races, 2.8%
Black, and 1.1% Asian residents; 5.0% are foreign-born, and 4,030 veterans
live in the city. About 63% of housing units are owner-occupied, with a
median home value of $171,800 and median gross rent of $1,030. Education
levels: 88.5% of adults 25+ are high school graduates, 21.5% hold a
bachelor's degree. Median household income is $64,578, per capita income
$33,937, and 15.1% live in poverty; the mean commute is 17.9 minutes.

## FAQ

**Why is Council Bluffs called "Mile 0"?**
President Lincoln's November 1863 order placed the transcontinental
railroad's eastern terminus in Iowa, and Congress confirmed the choice of
Council Bluffs on March 7, 1864. A rail-trail marker at 99 S. 25th St.
still commemorates the designation.

**Was the Lewis and Clark council actually held in Council Bluffs?**
Not exactly — the 1804 council with the Otoe Tribe took place about 20
miles north, on the west side of the Missouri near present-day Fort
Calhoun, Nebraska. The city borrowed the name from the meeting.

**What is Kanesville?**
The town's name from 1846 to 1852, honoring Thomas L. Kane. It was
overwhelmingly Mormon at the time; the Kanesville Tabernacle is preserved
as a historic site.

**Is Council Bluffs part of Omaha's metro?**
The two cities sit opposite each other across the Missouri River —
Council Bluffs on the Iowa side, Omaha on the Nebraska side — and the
railroad bridge first connected them in 1873 (the current bridge dates to
1916). This profile does not state the city's metro statistical area.

**How big is the Google presence?**
Google says it has invested more than $20 billion in Iowa since opening
its $600 million Council Bluffs data center in 2007, including a $7
billion 2025 investment for the Council Bluffs expansion and a new Cedar
Rapids campus.
