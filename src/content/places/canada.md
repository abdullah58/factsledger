## Overview

Canada is the world's second-largest country by area, a bilingual federal parliamentary democracy under a constitutional monarchy in northern North America. Its capital is Ottawa, its population was an estimated 41,651,653 on July 1, 2025, and its 2024 GDP was about $2.24 trillion. Federated in 1867, it now comprises ten provinces and three territories — and it is also a global hub of artificial-intelligence research ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html); [Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/250924/dq250924a-eng.pdf)).

Canada occupies the northern part of North America, bordering the North Atlantic Ocean on the east, the North Pacific on the west, and the Arctic Ocean on the north, north of the conterminous [United States](/places/united-states/) ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)). At 9,984,670 square kilometres it is the second-largest country in the world after Russia, and slightly larger than the United States — yet about 90% of its people are concentrated within 160 kilometres of the US border ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)). Its name probably comes from the Huron or Iroquois word *kanata*, meaning village or camp ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)). Canada has more fresh water than any other country, and almost 9% of its territory is water, with at least two million lakes — more than all other countries combined ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)). Its three largest metropolitan areas are [Toronto](/places/toronto/) (6.372 million), [Montreal](/places/montreal/) (4.308 million) and Vancouver (2.657 million) ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)).

## From Confederation to full independence

Canada did not become independent in a single moment — it federated first and became fully independent gradually. In 1867, three colonies in British North America — Canada, New Brunswick and Nova Scotia — united to form a "Federal Union" called Canada, consisting of four provinces: [Ontario](/places/ontario/), Quebec, Nova Scotia and New Brunswick ([Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/intergovernmental-affairs/services/provinces-territories.html)). The union then grew: Manitoba became a province in 1870, British Columbia joined in 1871, Prince Edward Island in 1873, Alberta and Saskatchewan were created in 1905, and Newfoundland joined in 1949 ([Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/intergovernmental-affairs/services/provinces-territories.html)). The last piece of the map arrived in 1999, when Nunavut was created as the third territory ([Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/intergovernmental-affairs/services/provinces-territories.html)).

Crucially, the 1867 union did not mark independence from the [United Kingdom](/places/united-kingdom/): most of the acts establishing the federation were acts of the British Parliament ([Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/intergovernmental-affairs/services/provinces-territories.html)). Full authority over foreign policy came with the Statute of Westminster in 1931, and political independence was completed when the Canada Act of 1982 repatriated the Constitution; since 1982, all of Canada's constitutional acts have emanated from the Canadian Parliament ([Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/intergovernmental-affairs/services/provinces-territories.html)). The CIA World Factbook tells the same story in shorthand: self-governing dominion in 1867, legislative independence in 1931, constitutional independence formalized in 1982 ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)).

## A federal parliamentary democracy

Canada's government type is a federal parliamentary democracy under a constitutional monarchy, and a Commonwealth realm ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)). Parliament is bicameral: a 343-seat House of Commons, all of whose members are directly elected, and a 105-seat Senate, all of whose members are appointed ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)). The federation's division of powers is constitutionally entrenched: provinces exercise constitutionally recognized legislative powers, while the three territories — the Northwest Territories, Yukon and Nunavut — exercise powers delegated by the federal government ([Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/intergovernmental-affairs/services/provinces-territories.html)). The territories cover about 40% of Canada's land mass but hold only around 3% of its population ([Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/intergovernmental-affairs/services/provinces-territories.html)).

The legal system is common law throughout the country except in Quebec, where civil law based on the French civil code prevails ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)). At the top of the court pyramid sits the [Supreme Court of Canada](/organizations/supreme-court-of-canada/), the country's final court of appeal, made up of the Chief Justice and eight judges ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)). The Court is bilingual, working and deciding cases in English and French, and bijural, deciding cases from Canada's two major legal traditions — common law and civil law — with hearings typically held in Ottawa and no trials or juries ([Supreme Court of Canada](https://scc-csc.gc.ca/about-apropos/work-travail/judicial-judiciaire/)).

Canada is officially bilingual. Recognition of the equal status of English and French dates to Confederation, when the Constitution Act, 1867 recognized the use of both languages in Parliament and in federal courts; the first Official Languages Act followed in 1969, and the 1982 Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms declared English and French the country's official languages, with the Act revised in 1988 ([Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat/services/values-ethics/official-languages/official-languages-act-and-you.html)). Canadians have the right to communicate with and receive services from federal institutions in either official language ([Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/treasury-board-secretariat/services/values-ethics/official-languages/official-languages-act-and-you.html)). In practice, 87.1% of people speak English and 29.1% speak French (2022 estimates) ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)).

## People: an immigration-shaped country

Canada's population story is, above all, an immigration story. The population reached an estimated 41,651,653 on July 1, 2025, and most of the growth in the second quarter of 2025 — 71.5% of it — came from international migration rather than natural increase ([Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/250924/dq250924a-eng.pdf)). The pattern is not new: in the 2021 Census, more than 8.3 million people — 23.0% of the population, almost one in four — were or had ever been landed immigrants or permanent residents, the highest share since Confederation, topping the previous record of 22.3% in 1921 ([Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/12-581-x/2023001/sec2-eng.htm)). The source of that immigration has shifted decisively: in 2021 the majority of immigrants were born in Asia, including the Middle East (51.4%), and the top birthplace of recent immigrants was [India](/places/india/) (18.6%), followed by the Philippines (11.4%) and [China](/places/china/) (8.9%) ([Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/12-581-x/2023001/sec2-eng.htm)).

Indigenous peoples are a distinct and foundational part of that population: 1,807,250 people reported an Indigenous identity — First Nations, Métis or Inuit — in the 2021 Census ([Statistics Canada](https://www.statcan.gc.ca/en/subjects-start/indigenous_peoples)). In Nunavut, the newest territory, more than 80% of the population is Inuit ([Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/intergovernmental-affairs/services/provinces-territories.html)). The population is also aging: 19.5% were aged 65 and older on July 1, 2025, and the median age was 40.6 years ([Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/250924/dq250924a-eng.pdf)). Most Canadians live in cities — 81.9% of the population was urban in 2023 ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)).

## Economy: resources, services and the American market

Canada is a high-income economy and the second-largest trading partner of the United States ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)). Its GDP stood at $2.241 trillion at official exchange rates in 2024, or $2.341 trillion in purchasing-power-parity terms, with real growth of 1.5% that year ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)). The stat that reframes the country's image: services produced 66.4% of GDP in 2021, against 25.3% for industry and 1.6% for agriculture — so the resource economy Canada is known for sits inside a services-led economy ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)). The resources are nonetheless real and vast: petroleum, natural gas, timber, minerals including iron ore, nickel, zinc, copper, gold, uranium, potash and diamonds, plus fish and hydropower ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)). Key industries include transportation equipment, chemicals, processed and unprocessed minerals, food products, and wood and paper products ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)). Unemployment was 6.5% in 2024 and inflation 2.4% ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)). Canada is a member of the G-7, the G-20, NATO, the OECD and the World Trade Organization, and a party to the USMCA trade agreement ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/canada.html)).

## Canada's AI research ecosystem — the profile no other country page writes

Here is where standard country profiles stop and this one keeps going: Canada is a disproportionate force in artificial-intelligence research. Its Pan-Canadian Artificial Intelligence Strategy, run by CIFAR, is built around three national institutes — Amii in Edmonton, Mila in Montréal, and the Vector Institute in Toronto — which CIFAR calls "the vibrant central hubs of Canada's thriving AI ecosystem" ([CIFAR](https://cifar.ca/cifarnews/2024/09/24/three-new-canada-cifar-ai-chairs-appointed-at-the-vector-institute/)). The strategy funds 132 Canada CIFAR AI Chairs as of September 2024, providing long-term dedicated research funding to university faculty and support to recruit and retain top graduate students ([CIFAR](https://cifar.ca/cifarnews/2024/09/24/three-new-canada-cifar-ai-chairs-appointed-at-the-vector-institute/)). CIFAR's own account of why it works is talent: its executive director notes that incoming chairs received their doctoral training elsewhere but "chose to come to Canada as faculty members because they recognize the value of Canada's strong networks of talent" ([CIFAR](https://cifar.ca/cifarnews/2024/09/24/three-new-canada-cifar-ai-chairs-appointed-at-the-vector-institute/)).

The Toronto–Waterloo corridor makes the point concrete. Two of the three new Vector Institute chairs named in September 2024 — Freda Shi and Victor Zhong — are faculty in the [University of Waterloo](/universities/university-of-waterloo/)'s Cheriton School of Computer Science, working on natural language processing and machine learning while holding Vector appointments in Toronto ([CIFAR](https://cifar.ca/cifarnews/2024/09/24/three-new-canada-cifar-ai-chairs-appointed-at-the-vector-institute/)). That corridor also commercializes research: [Blue J](/organizations/blue-j/), the Toronto-headquartered legal-technology company, was co-founded in 2015 by [University of Toronto](/universities/university-of-toronto/) law professors [Benjamin Alarie](/people/benjamin-alarie/), [Albert Yoon](/people/albert-yoon/) and [Anthony Niblett](/people/anthony-niblett/), using machine-learning algorithms to predict the likely outcomes of tax law cases ([University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/u-t-legal-startup-adds-employment-law-tool-its-ai-powered-product-portfolio)). Its clients include the [Canada Revenue Agency](/organizations/canada-revenue-agency/) and the Department of Justice — Canadian AI research serving Canadian federal institutions ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/blue-j-legal/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Canada the second-largest country in the world?**
Yes. At 9,984,670 square kilometres, Canada is the second-largest country by total area after Russia, and slightly larger than the United States. Despite that size, about 90% of its people live within 160 kilometres of the US border.

**How many provinces and territories does Canada have?**
Ten provinces and three territories. The provinces are Alberta, British Columbia, Manitoba, New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia, Ontario, Prince Edward Island, Quebec and Saskatchewan; the territories are the Northwest Territories, Nunavut and Yukon.

**What is the capital of Canada?**
Ottawa, not Toronto. Toronto is the largest city and the centre of the largest metropolitan area (6.372 million people in 2023), but the national capital is Ottawa, which had a metropolitan population of 1.437 million in 2023.

**What are Canada's official languages?**
English and French, with equality of status under the 1982 Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms. The first Official Languages Act dates to 1969, and Canadians have the right to receive federal services in either language. About 87.1% speak English and 29.1% speak French.

**Is Canada a leader in AI research?**
Yes — disproportionate to its size. The federally funded Pan-Canadian AI Strategy runs three national institutes (Amii in Edmonton, Mila in Montréal, the Vector Institute in Toronto) and funds 132 Canada CIFAR AI Chairs. Toronto's Vector Institute draws faculty from the University of Waterloo, and the ecosystem has produced companies such as Blue J Legal.
