Iran enters the last quarter of 2026 as a country under a new supreme leader, fighting a war its own diplomats are trying to end through Qatar. Mojtaba Khamenei holds the office of *rahbar* after his father, Ali Khamenei, was killed on February 28, 2026, in U.S.-Israeli strikes on Tehran — the opening day of a war now in its seventh month ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Iran); [Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/international/middle-east/724a6qtl8d)). The Islamic Republic of Iran — formerly Persia — sits in the Middle East in southwestern Asia, covers 1,630,848 square kilometres, and holds an estimated 87,312,000 people, with Tehran as its capital ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Iran)).

## The war's shape and the Hormuz lever

The current war began on February 28, 2026, when the United States and [Israel](/organizations/government-of-israel/) launched strikes on Iran — strikes that came, per the Doha Institute's situation assessment, after Geneva and Muscat negotiations had been broken off in favor of war ([Doha Institute](https://www.dohainstitute.org/en/PoliticalStudies/Pages/from-administrating-war-to-administrating-negotiations-the-islamabad-memorandum.aspx)). Khamenei was killed aged 86 at his compound in the centre of Tehran, and power was swiftly passed to his son Mojtaba; his public funeral began at Tehran's Grand Mosalla complex, with burial on July 9 at the shrine of Imam Reza in Mashhad, his birthplace ([Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/international/middle-east/724a6qtl8d)). Iran retaliated with missile and drone strikes across the region ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Iran)).

The bombardment killed senior commanders and the supreme leader and severely damaged strategic facilities — yet the regime did not collapse and did not lose its capacity to govern or continue the war, the Doha Institute's assessment argues, because Tehran raised the cost of the conflict: targeting U.S. bases and installations inside regional allies, and closing the Strait of Hormuz to disrupt international shipping ([Doha Institute](https://www.dohainstitute.org/en/PoliticalStudies/Pages/from-administrating-war-to-administrating-negotiations-the-islamabad-memorandum.aspx)). Iran paid a heavy price of its own, including the destruction of many vital facilities and significant damage to its nuclear program ([Doha Institute](https://www.dohainstitute.org/en/PoliticalStudies/Pages/from-administrating-war-to-administrating-negotiations-the-islamabad-memorandum.aspx)).

The strait is the war's economic center of gravity. A fifth of the world's traded oil and gas passed through Hormuz before the war, and it has become Tehran's main point of pressure in the war ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/9/25/iran-says-it-awaits-us-response-on-seven-day-roadmap-to-end-war)). The Doha Institute goes further: Hormuz, not the nuclear program, was "the true core" of the June ceasefire agreement, with global energy security as the crisis's central axis ([Doha Institute](https://www.dohainstitute.org/en/PoliticalStudies/Pages/from-administrating-war-to-administrating-negotiations-the-islamabad-memorandum.aspx)). None of this began in 2026: the previous June, a surprise Israeli strike on Tehran had derailed U.S.-Iran nuclear talks then under way, and the resulting 12-day war set the precedent for the wider conflict ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/2026/02/26/iran-and-us-consider-detailed-proposal-for-deal-in-geneva-talks/)).

## The Islamabad Memorandum and its collapse

On June 18, 2026, the United States and Iran announced that they had agreed to sign a memorandum of understanding, brokered by Pakistan and Qatar, after more than three months of war ([Doha Institute](https://www.dohainstitute.org/en/PoliticalStudies/Pages/from-administrating-war-to-administrating-negotiations-the-islamabad-memorandum.aspx)). Al Jazeera dates the signing to June 17: a Pakistani-and-Qatari-brokered deal setting a 60-day window for negotiations ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/9/25/iran-says-it-awaits-us-response-on-seven-day-roadmap-to-end-war)). Prothom Alo describes it as a 14-point deal pairing a 60-day ceasefire with the reopening of the blockaded strait, a timeline for permanently ending the war, and talks on Hormuz arrangements, reconstruction funding for Iran, and the future of the nuclear program ([Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/international/middle-east/724a6qtl8d)).

The structure was a security-for-economics swap. Iran agreed to reopen Hormuz, return to nuclear negotiations, and reaffirm its commitment not to pursue nuclear weapons; in return the U.S. agreed to lift the naval blockade on Iranian ports, grant broad sanctions waivers, allow the resumption of Iranian oil exports, and release part of Iran's frozen assets ([Doha Institute](https://www.dohainstitute.org/en/PoliticalStudies/Pages/from-administrating-war-to-administrating-negotiations-the-islamabad-memorandum.aspx)). An implementation mechanism was to oversee compliance and prepare a final agreement within sixty days ([Doha Institute](https://www.dohainstitute.org/en/PoliticalStudies/Pages/from-administrating-war-to-administrating-negotiations-the-islamabad-memorandum.aspx)). On the money, Iran pressed hardest for frozen assets — estimated at tens of billions of dollars — and at the July Doha round officials reviewed the use of part of an initial $6 billion, agreeing that goods Iran needed would be purchased and made available ([Doha Institute](https://www.dohainstitute.org/en/PoliticalStudies/Pages/from-administrating-war-to-administrating-negotiations-the-islamabad-memorandum.aspx); [Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/international/middle-east/724a6qtl8d)).

What the memorandum did not do is nearly as important. It deferred the core nuclear questions — whether Iran retains enrichment capabilities, the fate of its principal nuclear facilities, the future monitoring and inspection regime, and the stockpile of highly enriched uranium "at the heart of the dispute" ([Doha Institute](https://www.dohainstitute.org/en/PoliticalStudies/Pages/from-administrating-war-to-administrating-negotiations-the-islamabad-memorandum.aspx)). The Doha Institute's assessment calls it "a framework for ending the war, managing the crisis, and returning to negotiations" rather than a settlement, and notes the irony the Trump administration faced at home: it released tens of billions in Iranian assets and lifted oil sanctions without securing the clear nuclear commitments — such as renouncing enrichment in principle — that Trump had entered the war demanding ([Doha Institute](https://www.dohainstitute.org/en/PoliticalStudies/Pages/from-administrating-war-to-administrating-negotiations-the-islamabad-memorandum.aspx)).

The agreement unravelled over the strait. Vague wording on who controlled shipping through Hormuz led to disputes as Iran began firing on vessels it accused of ignoring its authorization rules; the two sides traded attacks, and Trump declared the deal "over" on July 7 ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/9/25/iran-says-it-awaits-us-response-on-seven-day-roadmap-to-end-war)). U.S. Central Command said it had attacked 10 Iranian military targets over "continued Iranian aggression against commercial shipping"; Iran said it retaliated with strikes against U.S. bases in Kuwait and Bahrain, which both condemned Tehran ([Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/international/middle-east/724a6qtl8d)). In the agreement's text, Iran had committed only to "best efforts" to ensure safe passage of commercial vessels — without fees — for sixty days between the Persian Gulf and the Sea of Oman, while future management was to be discussed with Oman ([Doha Institute](https://www.dohainstitute.org/en/PoliticalStudies/Pages/from-administrating-war-to-administrating-negotiations-the-islamabad-memorandum.aspx)).

The Lebanon clause showed the deal's regional limits. The memorandum explicitly committed to ending military operations on all fronts, including Lebanon — a clause whose practical effect was limited, since it did not oblige Israel to withdraw from areas it occupied and Israel "does not consider itself bound by any arrangements concerning Lebanon," continuing operations while asserting freedom of action on its northern border ([Doha Institute](https://www.dohainstitute.org/en/PoliticalStudies/Pages/from-administrating-war-to-administrating-negotiations-the-islamabad-memorandum.aspx)). The Doha round that followed the collapse produced a communications channel to report and record alleged violations and an agreement to keep talking after Khamenei's funeral, with Trump saying "the denuclearisation of Iran is moving along well" ([Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/international/middle-east/724a6qtl8d)).

## The seven-day roadmap

On September 25, 2026, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi stood outside the UN Security Council in New York and detailed a new Iranian offer: a seven-day roadmap to end the war, conveyed to Washington through Qatar ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/25/iran-pitches-seven-day-roadmap-to-end-conflict-with-us)). Under the plan, initial steps would take four to five days, the Strait of Hormuz would reopen on the sixth day, and on day seven talks with the U.S. would start "for the final deal on mutually agreed subjects" — Iran's nuclear program ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/9/25/iran-says-it-awaits-us-response-on-seven-day-roadmap-to-end-war)). Washington, in turn, would lift its naval blockade of Iranian ports, waive sanctions on Iranian oil sales, release an estimated $12 billion in frozen Iranian assets, and observe a ceasefire covering the region — ending hostilities on all fronts, including Gaza, where [Hamas](/organizations/hamas/) operates, and Lebanon ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/25/iran-pitches-seven-day-roadmap-to-end-conflict-with-us)).

"The seven-day timeline will start as soon as the United States accepts this plan," Araghchi said, adding: "The choice now rests with the United States" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/9/25/iran-says-it-awaits-us-response-on-seven-day-roadmap-to-end-war)). President Masoud Pezeshkian made the same case on Fox News' 'Special Report with Bret Baier': "When we can resolve issues through dialogue, we shouldn't resort to killing one another. But with the instigations conducted by Israel, they have imposed this war on us" — and "It's America that must choose whether it wants to end this or not" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/25/iran-pitches-seven-day-roadmap-to-end-conflict-with-us)). Araghchi had met Trump's envoy Steve Witkoff on the UNGA sidelines that Tuesday in a "lengthy" meeting Witkoff hoped would "prove constructive and promising" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/9/25/iran-says-it-awaits-us-response-on-seven-day-roadmap-to-end-war)).

The answer came fast. Later that Friday, the Wall Street Journal reported that Trump had privately rejected Iran's proposal — "privately sceptical" Tehran would meet his demands — and had told aides he expects a renewed bombing campaign after the November U.S. midterm elections ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/9/25/iran-says-it-awaits-us-response-on-seven-day-roadmap-to-end-war)). "Basically nothing has really changed," said Cyrus Schayegh, professor of international history and politics at the Geneva Graduate Institute: Iran wants to keep the leverage it gained during the war, Washington resists returning to the June framework, and no significant movement is likely before the midterms ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/25/iran-pitches-seven-day-roadmap-to-end-conflict-with-us)).

## The February Geneva talks

The war's diplomatic prehistory ran through Geneva. On February 26, 2026, the U.S. and Iran ended a third round of "intensive" nuclear talks at the Omani embassy in Geneva with a deal elusive and a fourth, technical-level meeting scheduled for Vienna the following week ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/2026/02/26/iran-and-us-consider-detailed-proposal-for-deal-in-geneva-talks/)). Oman's Foreign Minister Badr Al Busaidi, acting as go-between, declared "significant progress" ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/2026/02/26/iran-and-us-consider-detailed-proposal-for-deal-in-geneva-talks/)). Iran offered a "temporary suspension" of uranium enrichment but rejected demands to hand over its uranium stockpile or negotiate limits on its missiles and defenses; the U.S., the Wall Street Journal reported, brought "tough demands" including dismantling Iran's enrichment sites, handing over enriched uranium to the U.S., and a deal that would "last forever" ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/2026/02/26/iran-and-us-consider-detailed-proposal-for-deal-in-geneva-talks/)).

The public positions were already irreconcilable. Araghchi said a "fair, balanced and equitable deal" was within reach and Pezeshkian reiterated Iran was not seeking nuclear weapons, while supreme-leader adviser Ali Shamkhani said "an agreement could be reached immediately" if the core issue was ensuring Iran did not seek the bomb ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/2026/02/26/iran-and-us-consider-detailed-proposal-for-deal-in-geneva-talks/)). Vice President JD Vance countered that Washington had seen evidence of Iran rebuilding its nuclear program — "Iran cannot have a nuclear weapon" — and Secretary of State Marco Rubio warned Tehran was developing intercontinental ballistic missiles, calling Iran's refusal to discuss missiles "a big, big problem" ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/2026/02/26/iran-and-us-consider-detailed-proposal-for-deal-in-geneva-talks/)). In his State of the Union address that Tuesday, Trump said Iran had already developed missiles threatening Europe and U.S. bases overseas and was building missiles "that could soon reach the US"; Araghchi dismissed the claims as "fake news," saying Iran had capped its missile range below 2,000 kilometres "for defence and deterrence" ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/2026/02/26/iran-and-us-consider-detailed-proposal-for-deal-in-geneva-talks/)). Around the talks, a U.S. aircraft carrier departed a Greek port for the Middle East to join what Trump called an "armada," a second carrier approached, and the IRGC — a week after live-fire drills in the Strait of Hormuz — tested new firepower while Iran and Russia held joint naval exercises ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/2026/02/26/iran-and-us-consider-detailed-proposal-for-deal-in-geneva-talks/)). Two days later, the strikes began.

## Land, people, and government

Iran occupies a high plateau rising higher than 1,500 feet above sea level, largely ringed by mountains; more than half its surface consists of salt deserts, about one-tenth of its land is arable, and another fourth is suitable for grazing ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Iran)). Persians are the largest ethnic group, alongside Azerbaijanians, Kurds, Lurs, Bakhtyari, and Baloch; Persian (Farsi) is the official language, and Islam — predominantly Shi'i — is the official religion, with Zoroastrianism also present ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Iran)). The currency is the rial, and the economy rests on petroleum reserves accounting for about one-tenth of world reserves ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Iran)).

The state is a unitary Islamic republic with one legislative house and several oversight bodies dominated by clergy: the president is head of state and government, but political and religious authority is held by the *rahbar*, the supreme leader, chosen by a body of Islamic jurists and overseeing virtually all functions of government directly or indirectly ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Iran)). Mass protests against the regime, particularly in 2022–23 and 2025–26, were harshly suppressed ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Iran)).

## A compact history

Recorded history in Iran began with the Elamites around 3000 BCE; the Medes flourished from about 728 BCE before the Persians overthrew them in 550, and Alexander the Great conquered Persia in the 4th century BCE ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Iran)). The Parthian empire ran from 247 BCE to 226 CE, when control passed to the Sasanians; Muslim dynasties ruled from the 7th century, the Safavids held power from 1501 to 1736, and the Qajars ruled from 1796 while Russia and Britain economically controlled the country in the 19th century ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Iran)). Reza Khan's 1921 coup founded the Pahlavi line; his son Mohammad Reza Shah Pahlavi, whose modernization program alienated religious leaders, was overthrown in 1979, when Ruhollah Khomeini set up the Islamic republic ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Iran)). The 1980s Iran-Iraq War ended in stalemate; Ali Khamenei succeeded Khomeini in 1989, with popularly elected presidents operating under his authority, and periods of openness have alternated with conservative policy since the 1990s ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/summary/Iran)).
