## Overview

South Africa is the Republic of South Africa — the southernmost country in Africa, a parliamentary republic of about 61 to 63 million people, depending on the source and the estimate year, with three capitals, twelve official languages and the most industrial economy on the continent ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/South-Africa); [CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)). Pretoria is the executive capital, Cape Town the legislative capital and Bloemfontein the judicial capital, while Johannesburg — the largest urban area, with 10.316 million people — is the commercial centre ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/South-Africa)).

In the [Booksy](/organizations/booksy/) story, South Africa matters as the African market. By January 2021 it was one of the six markets Booksy operated in — alongside the United States, the United Kingdom, Poland, Spain and [Brazil](/places/brazil/) — with 13 million consumers across the company's markets ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)). The connectivity base is there: 115 million mobile subscriptions, 167 per 100 inhabitants, and 76% of the population online ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)). The economic context is harder: unemployment of 33.2%, youth unemployment of 60.9%, and growth of 0.6% in 2024 ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)).

## Land, people and economy

South Africa covers 1,220,813 square kilometres according to Britannica — 1,219,090 according to the Factbook — between the Atlantic and Indian Oceans, with the Drakensberg range running some 700 miles and peaks above 11,400 feet, and the Cape Floral Region's fynbos vegetation ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/South-Africa); [CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)). The population is young — median age 30.7 — and 68.8% urban (2023) ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)). The ethnic composition is Black African 80.9%, Colored 8.8%, White 7.8% and Indian/Asian 2.6% (2021), and the most spoken first languages are isiZulu 25.3%, isiXhosa 14.8%, Afrikaans 12.2% and English 8.1% (2018) ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)). There are twelve official languages in total — Afrikaans, English, Ndebele, Pedi, Sotho, South African Sign Language, Swati, Tsonga, Tswana, Venda, Xhosa and Zulu ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/South-Africa)). Literacy is 91.2% (2024) and education spending is 6% of GDP ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)).

Economically, South Africa is an upper-middle-income country and the largest economy in southern Africa ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)). GDP in purchasing-power terms was $870.42 billion in 2024, growing 0.6%, with per-capita PPP income of $13,600; nominal GDP was $400.261 billion ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)). Services dominate at 62.7% of GDP, industry is 24.4% and agriculture 2.9% (2024) ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)). The labour force is 27.766 million, and unemployment is 33.2% (2024), with youth unemployment at 60.9% — the defining structural problem, alongside high income inequality and poverty, which the Factbook lists in its economic overview ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)). Exports were $127.629 billion in 2024 — gold, platinum, coal, cars and iron ore, with China taking 19%, the United States 8%, Germany 7%, India 7% and the United Kingdom 6% ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)). Mining is the country's signature industry — the world's largest producer of platinum, gold and chromium — alongside automobile assembly, metalworking, machinery, textiles, iron and steel, chemicals and foodstuffs ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)). Electricity generation is 87.3% fossil fuels, mostly coal, and the Factbook notes reforms under way to address electricity generation, transport and logistics ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)).

## From Union to apartheid to democracy

The country's modern history begins with the Dutch landing in 1652 and founding Cape Town; the British seized the Cape in 1806, and the Boers — later Afrikaners — trekked north, founding the Transvaal and the Orange Free State ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)). Diamonds were discovered in 1867 and gold in 1886, transforming the economy and drawing migration to the interior, and the Anglo-Zulu War of 1879 and the Second South African War of 1899–1902 followed ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)). The Union of South Africa was formed on 31 May 1910 from four British colonies — Cape Colony, Natal, Transvaal and Orange Free State ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)).

In 1948 the National Party came to power and instituted apartheid — Afrikaans for "apartness" — a system of racial segregation and discrimination that lasted until 1994 ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/South-Africa)). The republic was declared on 31 May 1961 after a whites-only referendum, and South Africa left the Commonwealth, rejoining only in 1994 ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/South-Africa)). The African National Congress resisted, Nelson Mandela spent decades in prison, and the government of F.W. de Klerk (1989–94) repealed the apartheid laws ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/South-Africa)). The first multi-racial elections were held on 27 April 1994 — now Freedom Day — bringing a Mandela-led Black-majority government to power ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/South-Africa)). The constitution drafted in 1996 took effect on 4 February 1997 ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)).

South Africa hosted the FIFA World Cup in 2010, the first African country to do so, and Bafana Bafana have qualified for the 2026 tournament ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/South-Africa)). The current political fact is the Government of National Unity: after the 29 May 2024 election, Cyril Ramaphosa was elected president by the National Assembly unopposed and took office on 19 June 2024, with the next election expected in May 2029 ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)). The country has 12 UNESCO World Heritage Sites, including Robben Island, iSimangaliso Wetland Park, the Fossil Hominid Sites and the Nelson Mandela Legacy Sites ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is South Africa important to Booksy?**
By January 2021 South Africa was one of Booksy's six markets, alongside the US, UK, Poland, Spain and Brazil, with 13 million consumers across the company's markets ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)). Its connectivity — 115 million mobile subscriptions and 76% of the population online — is the base a booking app needs ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)).

**Why does South Africa have three capitals?**
Pretoria is the executive (administrative) capital, Cape Town the legislative capital and Bloemfontein the judicial capital — a division dating to the 1910 Union, when the four colonies each kept a role ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)).

**When did apartheid end?**
The National Party instituted apartheid in 1948; the laws were repealed under F.W. de Klerk's government (1989–94), and the first multi-racial elections on 27 April 1994 brought a Mandela-led government to power ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/South-Africa)).

**Who is South Africa's president?**
Matamela Cyril Ramaphosa, in office since 19 June 2024 after being elected by the National Assembly unopposed following the 29 May 2024 election, leading a Government of National Unity ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)).

**How big is South Africa's economy?**
GDP in purchasing-power terms was $870.42 billion in 2024, growing 0.6%, making it the largest economy in southern Africa; nominal GDP was $400.261 billion, and unemployment was 33.2% ([CIA World Factbook](https://theworldfactbook.org/country/south-africa.html)).
