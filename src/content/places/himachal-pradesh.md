**Himachal Pradesh** is a Western Himalayan state of northern India that became the 18th state of the Indian Union on 25 January 1971 ([The Tribune](https://tribuneindia.com/news/exam-mentor/from-princely-states-to-full-statehood-the-story-of-himachal-pradesh/)). It is governed from two capitals — Shimla in summer and Dharamshala, declared the second capital in 2017 — and its 2011 Census population of 6,864,602 recorded 82.8% literacy ([The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/shimla/dharamshala-no-capital-gain-for-himachal-pradesh/articleshow/91720006.cms); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/news-ians/himachal-literacy-rate-up-6-3-percent-in-decade-census-113052600101_1.html)). It is the home state of freedom fighter Wazir Ram Singh Pathania of Nurpur, whose 1848 revolt against the British was dramatised in Season 2 of the animated series *Krish, Trish and Baltiboy* ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/entertainment/second-season-of-animated-series-krish-trish-and-baltiboy-bharat-hain-hum-launched-at-iffi/)).

## From 30 princely states to India's 18th state

After independence, the region was constituted in 1948 as a Chief Commissioner's Province, created by integrating 30 princely hill states; it became a Part C state in 1950 and a Union Territory in 1956 ([The Tribune](https://tribuneindia.com/news/exam-mentor/from-princely-states-to-full-statehood-the-story-of-himachal-pradesh/)). The decisive expansion came under the Punjab Reorganisation Act, 1966, when the hill areas of Punjab — Kangra, Shimla, Kullu and Lahaul-Spiti — were merged into the territory ([The Tribune](https://tribuneindia.com/news/exam-mentor/from-princely-states-to-full-statehood-the-story-of-himachal-pradesh/)). Parliament then passed the State of Himachal Pradesh Act, 1970, and the territory was elevated to full statehood on 25 January 1971, a date still observed as Statehood Day ([The Tribune](https://tribuneindia.com/news/exam-mentor/from-princely-states-to-full-statehood-the-story-of-himachal-pradesh/)). The state sits in the Western Himalayas, bordered by Punjab, Haryana, Uttarakhand, Jammu & Kashmir, Ladakh and China (Tibet Autonomous Region) ([The Tribune](https://tribuneindia.com/news/exam-mentor/from-princely-states-to-full-statehood-the-story-of-himachal-pradesh/)).

## Two capitals, one 68-seat assembly

Shimla — then Simla — was the summer capital of British India, declared so in 1863 by Viceroy John Lawrence, who moved the entire colonial administration there from Calcutta each year; the town itself was a British creation with little pre-colonial urban history ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/research/shimla-rename-shyamala-himachal-pradesh-shamli-5415941/lite/)). The state's legislature is a unicameral Vidhan Sabha with 68 seats, and the Himachal Pradesh High Court was established in 1971 ([The Tribune](https://tribuneindia.com/news/exam-mentor/from-princely-states-to-full-statehood-the-story-of-himachal-pradesh/); [The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/shimla/dharamshala-no-capital-gain-for-himachal-pradesh/articleshow/91720006.cms)). In Parliament, the state sends four MPs — from the Shimla, Kangra, Hamirpur and Mandi constituencies — and the BJP swept all four in the 2024 general election ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/elections/lok-sabha/election-results-2024-its-a-clean-sweep-for-bjp-in-himachal-congress-makes-a-mark-in-assembly-bypolls/article68251610.ece/amp/)). Dharamshala's second-capital story is more complicated: Chief Minister Virbhadra Singh declared it the state's second capital in the winter of 2017, formalised by a one-line March 2017 notification — but by 2022 nothing had moved: no offices shifted from Shimla, and the "second capital" existed on paper only ([The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/shimla/dharamshala-no-capital-gain-for-himachal-pradesh/articleshow/91720006.cms)). The town does host the assembly's annual winter sessions and is the seat of the Tibetan government-in-exile ([The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/shimla/dharamshala-no-capital-gain-for-himachal-pradesh/articleshow/91720006.cms)).

## The numbers: literacy, income, and a rebounding mountain economy

The 2011 Census put the state's population at 6,864,602 with a decadal growth rate of 12.9%, and literacy at 82.8% — up from 76.5% in 2001 — with male literacy at 89.5% and female at 75.9% ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/news-ians/himachal-literacy-rate-up-6-3-percent-in-decade-census-113052600101_1.html)). The state's own Statistical Year Book 2022-23 confirms the official figures: 55,673 sq km, 12 districts, and a density of 123 people per sq km ([Department of Economic & Statistics, HP](https://himachalservices.nic.in/economics/pdf/YearBook2022-23.pdf)). More than 90% of the population lives in rural areas — 17,882 villages out of 20,690 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/news-ians/himachal-literacy-rate-up-6-3-percent-in-decade-census-113052600101_1.html)). The 2025-26 Economic Survey, presented in the Vidhan Sabha by Chief Minister Sukhvinder Singh Sukhu, projects 8.3% growth, per-capita income of Rs 2,83,626 (9.8% up, Rs 64,051 above the national average), and a GSDP of roughly Rs 2.54 lakh crore at current prices ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/economy-picks-up-pace-as-growth-jumps-to-8-3/)). Tourist arrivals rebounded from 32.13 lakh in 2020 to 3.11 crore in 2025 — a roughly tenfold recovery that implies tourism now matters as much to the state as its historic hydropower base, which earned Rs 1,668 crore from power sales ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/economy-picks-up-pace-as-growth-jumps-to-8-3/)).

## The state's screen moment: Wazir Ram Singh Pathania

The most direct bridge between Himachal Pradesh and this site's existing profiles is a man who died 175 years ago. [Wazir Ram Singh Pathania](/people/wazir-ram-singh-pathania/) — the Nurpur minister who led an armed uprising against the British in 1848, was captured, and died in exile in Rangoon on 11 November 1849 — was named by The Tribune as one of the freedom fighters featured in Season 2 ("Bharat Hain Hum") of [Krish, Trish and Baltiboy](/creative-works/krish-trish-and-baltiboy/), launched at the International Film Festival of India with actor Nagarjuna at the event ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/entertainment/second-season-of-animated-series-krish-trish-and-baltiboy-bharat-hain-hum-launched-at-iffi/)). The state keeps his memory institutionally alive: Governor Shiv Pratap Shukla unveiled Pathania's statue at the Ram Singh Pathania Memorial in Nurpur, noting he hailed from Bassa Waziran village and led the 1848 uprising remembered for the Battle of Shahpur ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/governor-unveils-statue-of-wazir-ram-singh/)). The "first organised armed revolt" description belongs to the Governor's commemorative speech as reported — official recognition, not settled historiography — and the details of Pathania's life sit on his own profile ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/governor-unveils-statue-of-wazir-ram-singh/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When did Himachal Pradesh become a state?**
On 25 January 1971, as India's 18th state under the State of Himachal Pradesh Act, 1970. Before that it was a Chief Commissioner's Province (1948), a Part C state (1950) and a Union Territory (1956); the Punjab Reorganisation Act of 1966 then merged Kangra, Shimla, Kullu and Lahaul-Spiti into it.

**Why does Himachal Pradesh have two capitals?**
Shimla is the summer capital; Dharamshala was declared the second (winter) capital in 2017 by Chief Minister Virbhadra Singh. But as of 2022 no offices had actually shifted, so the second capital existed on paper only — Dharamshala does, however, host the assembly's annual winter sessions.

**What are the state's population and literacy figures?**
The 2011 Census counted 6,864,602 people with a literacy rate of 82.8% — up from 76.5% in 2001 — with male literacy at 89.5% and female at 75.9%. More than 90% of the population lives in rural areas, across 17,882 villages of 20,690 revenue villages.

**How is Himachal Pradesh connected to "Krish, Trish and Baltiboy"?**
The animated series' Season 2, "Bharat Hain Hum," launched at the International Film Festival of India, features Wazir Ram Singh Pathania of Himachal Pradesh among its dramatised freedom fighters. Pathania, the Nurpur minister who led the 1848 uprising against the British, has a memorial statue in Nurpur unveiled by the state's Governor.
