## Overview

California is the most populous state in the [United States](/places/united-states/), with an estimated 39.4 million residents in July 2025, and the third-largest state by area at 163,695 square miles. Admitted as the 31st state on September 9, 1850, it is also the world's fourth-largest economy: its $4.296 trillion output in the third quarter of 2025 edged out Japan's $4.28 trillion ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/CA,US/PST045223?utm_campaign=20240909cnmps2&utm_medium=email&utm_source=govdelivery); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://mendel-preview.dev-ext.britannica.com/place/California-state); [Orange County Register](https://edition.pagesuite.com/tribune/article_popover.aspx?guid=2b7186f4-e84e-402f-bff7-9f12ab8a0b04)). California holds both the highest and the lowest points in the contiguous United States — Mount Whitney and Death Valley — and leads the country in agricultural production while housing the tech industry of Silicon Valley and the entertainment industry of Hollywood ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://mendel-preview.dev-ext.britannica.com/place/California-state)).

## Gold, statehood, and a capital that moved four times

California became a state in a hurry, and gold was the reason. In February 1848 Mexico and the United States signed the treaty ending the Mexican War, ceding California to the United States — several days after gold had been discovered on the American River near Sacramento on January 24, 1848 ([California State Parks](https://www.parks.ca.gov/?lang=en&page_id=23856)). The ensuing Gold Rush brought a huge increase in population and a pressing need for civil government, and in 1849 Californians sought statehood; after heated debate in Congress over slavery, California entered the Union as a free, nonslavery state under the Compromise of 1850, becoming the 31st state on September 9, 1850 ([California State Parks](https://www.parks.ca.gov/?lang=en&page_id=23856)).

Even the capital could not sit still. California first situated its capital in San Jose, but the facilities were unready and the wet winter of 1850–51 turned dirt roads to mud; the legislature moved to Vallejo, then to Benicia, and finally to the riverside port of Sacramento in 1854, where it has stayed ([California State Parks](https://www.parks.ca.gov/?lang=en&page_id=23856)). The state's symbols date from the same era: the current flag, adopted by the legislature in 1911, is based on the Bear Flag raised by American pioneers over Sonoma in 1846, and "Eureka" — "I Have Found It," probably referring to the discovery of gold — has appeared on the state seal since 1849 and became the official motto in 1963 ([California State Parks](https://www.parks.ca.gov/?lang=en&page_id=23856)). The name itself is older and less certain: no version of its origin is fully accepted, though there is wide support for the idea that it came from an early 16th-century Spanish novel, *Las sergas de Esplandián*, describing a paradisiacal island full of gold and precious stones called California ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://mendel-preview.dev-ext.britannica.com/place/California-state)).

## The fourth-largest economy in the world

California's economy is the largest of any U.S. state, and since 2024 it has also been the world's fourth-largest — a ranking with a surprisingly short and contested history. In April 2025 the state announced it had overtaken Japan to become the world's fourth-largest economy, behind only the United States, China, and Germany ([California Environmental Protection Agency](https://calepa.ca.gov/2025/09/23/press-release-california-leaders-in-new-york-this-week-sounding-the-alarm-on-the-need-for-urgent-climate-action/)). Then Japan briefly reclaimed the spot in the IMF's October 2025 update — before fresh Bureau of Economic Analysis data for the third quarter of 2025 put California back ahead at a $4.296 trillion annual rate, up 4.5% on the year, beating Japan's $4.28 trillion by just $16 billion ([Orange County Register](https://edition.pagesuite.com/tribune/article_popover.aspx?guid=2b7186f4-e84e-402f-bff7-9f12ab8a0b04)). The chase pack is close: India sits at $4.125 trillion and the United Kingdom at $3.96 trillion, and India's fast-growing economy is expected to overtake the No. 4 ranking before long ([Orange County Register](https://edition.pagesuite.com/tribune/article_popover.aspx?guid=2b7186f4-e84e-402f-bff7-9f12ab8a0b04)).

The scale is real but unevenly felt. California's third-quarter growth of 4.5% was its fastest since the second quarter of 2023, yet it ranked only 25th among the states — middle of the pack nationally, though ahead of its two main rivals, Texas at 4.2% and Florida at 3.5% ([Orange County Register](https://edition.pagesuite.com/tribune/article_popover.aspx?guid=2b7186f4-e84e-402f-bff7-9f12ab8a0b04)). And the ranking itself is currency-sensitive: a weak U.S. dollar flatters California's rivals when the IMF next updates its figures ([Orange County Register](https://edition.pagesuite.com/tribune/article_popover.aspx?guid=2b7186f4-e84e-402f-bff7-9f12ab8a0b04)). The more durable story may be the pairing of growth with decarbonization: between 2000 and 2022 California's greenhouse-gas emissions fell 20% while its GDP rose 78%, and in 2025, 67% of the state's retail electricity sales came from renewable and zero-carbon generation, up from 61% the year before and around 41% a decade ago ([California Environmental Protection Agency](https://calepa.ca.gov/2025/09/23/press-release-california-leaders-in-new-york-this-week-sounding-the-alarm-on-the-need-for-urgent-climate-action/)). The state leads the nation in zero-emission vehicle adoption, with more than 2 million ZEVs purchased and 34% of new ZEVs sold in the U.S. sold in California ([California Environmental Protection Agency](https://calepa.ca.gov/2025/09/23/press-release-california-leaders-in-new-york-this-week-sounding-the-alarm-on-the-need-for-urgent-climate-action/)).

## A state of extremes

California's geography is a catalogue of superlatives. It is bounded by Oregon to the north, Nevada and [Arizona](/places/arizona/) to the east, the Mexican state of Baja California to the south, and the Pacific Ocean to the west, with a coastline of roughly 1,100 miles ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://mendel-preview.dev-ext.britannica.com/place/California-state)). The Central Valley runs 450 miles through the state's center and is its agricultural heart; the Mojave Desert covers more than 25,000 square miles, a sixth of the state; and the 800-mile San Andreas Fault runs through most of California, producing the San Francisco earthquake of 1906 and the Northridge quake of 1994 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://mendel-preview.dev-ext.britannica.com/place/California-state)). Water is the state's chronic constraint: the California State Water Project, launched in 1960, is the largest water-transfer system ever undertaken, moving water from the Feather River to communities as far south as the Mexican border ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://mendel-preview.dev-ext.britannica.com/place/California-state)).

The people are as concentrated as the geography is spread out. More than three-fourths of Californians live in the [Los Angeles](/places/los-angeles/), San Francisco, and San Diego metropolitan areas, making the state the most urban in the country ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://mendel-preview.dev-ext.britannica.com/place/California-state)). The population is strikingly diverse: 41.2% Hispanic or Latino, 17.5% Asian alone, 27.0% foreign-born, and 44.4% speaking a language other than English at home ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/CA,US/PST045223?utm_campaign=20240909cnmps2&utm_medium=email&utm_source=govdelivery)). Here is the paradox of the moment: the world's fourth-largest economy slightly shrank in people even as it grew in output — the 2020 census counted 39,538,223 residents, while the July 2025 estimate is 39,355,309, a 0.5% decline ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/CA,US/PST045223?utm_campaign=20240909cnmps2&utm_medium=email&utm_source=govdelivery)). The pattern does not hold everywhere inside the state: [Irvine](/places/irvine/), a master-planned city in Orange County, grew 3.6% from the 2020 estimates base to July 2025 while the state as a whole declined 0.5% ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/irvinecitycalifornia)). With 1,029,689 employer establishments and 16.4 million jobs, and retail sales of $809.6 billion in 2022, the economy's weight is not in doubt — only its direction of travel ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/CA,US/PST045223?utm_campaign=20240909cnmps2&utm_medium=email&utm_source=govdelivery)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When did California become a state?**
September 9, 1850 — the 31st state, admitted as a free state under the Compromise of 1850, less than three years after gold was discovered near Sacramento and the territory was ceded by Mexico.

**Why is California called the Golden State?**
The nickname refers to the Gold Rush and the state's golden landscape; the state motto is "Eureka" ("I Have Found It"), which has appeared on the state seal since 1849 and became the official motto in 1963.

**Is California really the world's fourth-largest economy?**
On nominal GDP, yes as of 2025: third-quarter BEA data put California at a $4.296 trillion annual rate, ahead of Japan's $4.28 trillion by $16 billion. But the ranking is a moving target — Japan briefly reclaimed No. 4 in the IMF's October 2025 update, and India ($4.125 trillion) is close behind.

**What is California's capital?**
Sacramento, since 1854. The capital previously sat in San Jose, then Vallejo, then Benicia, before settling in Sacramento.

**What is the highest and lowest point in California?**
Mount Whitney (14,494 feet) is the highest point in the contiguous United States, and Death Valley is the lowest — both are in California.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not present the No. 4 world-economy ranking as settled: Japan briefly retook it in the IMF's October 2025 update, the Q3 2025 margin is only $16 billion, and India is projected to pass California. It does not smooth over the population picture: the 2020 census and the July 2025 estimate show a slight decline, and estimates are not counts. It does not name the state legislature's composition or statewide officeholders beyond Governor Gavin Newsom as of September 2025. It does not assert the name-origin story as certain — Britannica itself says no version is fully accepted. And it does not confuse California with Baja California, the wider Californias region, or the former northern California town of Irvine, renamed Carson Hill in 1914.
