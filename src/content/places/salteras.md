## Overview

Salteras is a municipality of about 5,500 people in the province of [Seville](/places/seville/), Andalusia, [Spain](/places/spain/), set on a small hill in the Aljarafe comarca about 12 km from Seville ([Turismo de la Provincia de Sevilla](https://www.turismosevilla.org/es/la-provincia-de-sevilla/nuestros-pueblos/salteras); [andalucia.com](https://www.andalucia.com/province/sevilla/salteras/home)). It is a town whose recorded history runs from a Roman settlement called Pésula, through a Muslim farmstead and a medieval privilege from King Juan II, to a present day defined by olive groves, Holy Week music and one nationally unique institution: the [Centro Santa Ángela de la Cruz](/organizations/centro-santa-angela-de-la-cruz/), Spain's first — and still only — residential and day centre for deafblind young people and adults, opened in Salteras in 2010 ([Revista Andalucía Económica](https://andaluciaeconomica.com/nuevos-equipos-informaticos-facilitan-el-aprendizaje-de-personas-con-sordoceguera/)).

That combination is what makes a town of this size worth a profile. Most Andalusian municipalities of five thousand people are known, if at all, for one church or one festival; Salteras has a parish church begun in 1540, two bands of national standing in Holy Week music, a ceramics museum and an artisanal hat factory, and it also hosts a centre that draws deafblind residents from across Spain ([Turismo de la Provincia de Sevilla](https://www.turismosevilla.org/es/la-provincia-de-sevilla/nuestros-pueblos/salteras); [SID — Universidad de Salamanca](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/)). It is about half an hour by car from Seville on the A-8077, with a Cercanías train stop on line C5 and a metropolitan bus, the M-175, from Seville's Plaza de Armas ([Turismo de la Provincia de Sevilla](https://www.turismosevilla.org/es/la-provincia-de-sevilla/nuestros-pueblos/salteras)).

## From Pésula to Salteras

Salteras has traditionally been identified with a Turdetanian settlement called Pésula, cited by the Roman historian Pliny, and Roman funerary stones and inscriptions have been found across its municipal area ([Turismo de la Provincia de Sevilla](https://www.turismosevilla.org/es/la-provincia-de-sevilla/nuestros-pueblos/salteras)). The spelling varies by source — Paesula in some accounts, Pésula or Pesula in others — and the town's coat of arms still carries a Roman altar recalling that past ([FOTW Flags of the World](https://www.fahnenversand.de/fotw/flags/es-se-sa.html)). Visigothic presence is attested by sixth-century funerary gravestones, and the parish church still holds a Visigothic funerary slab dated 520 ([andalucia.com](https://www.andalucia.com/province/sevilla/salteras/home); [Turismo de la Provincia de Sevilla](https://www.turismosevilla.org/es/la-provincia-de-sevilla/nuestros-pueblos/salteras)).

Under Muslim rule the settlement was an Arab farmstead, an alquería, which gathered neighbouring hamlets until it formed a town during the Christian Repartimiento and the fourteenth-century repopulation ([Turismo de la Provincia de Sevilla](https://www.turismosevilla.org/es/la-provincia-de-sevilla/nuestros-pueblos/salteras)). After the Reconquista, Fernando III gave the settlement a title ([andalucia.com](https://www.andalucia.com/province/sevilla/salteras/home)), and in 1443 King Juan II granted Salteras the status of "Guarda y Collación de Sevilla" — a privilege the town still carries in the bordure of its coat of arms ([Turismo de la Provincia de Sevilla](https://www.turismosevilla.org/es/la-provincia-de-sevilla/nuestros-pueblos/salteras); [FOTW Flags of the World](https://www.fahnenversand.de/fotw/flags/es-se-sa.html)).

The seventeenth century turned Salteras into a seigneurial property. Its lordship was bought successively by the Sevillian knight Juan Federigui, by Gaspar de Guzmán, Count-Duke of Olivares, in 1641, and by Olivares's nephew Luis de Haro, before passing to the Casa de Alba in the eighteenth century ([Turismo de la Provincia de Sevilla](https://www.turismosevilla.org/es/la-provincia-de-sevilla/nuestros-pueblos/salteras)). The Guzmán arms still occupy the third quarter of the town's coat of arms as a reminder of the 1641 purchase ([FOTW Flags of the World](https://www.fahnenversand.de/fotw/flags/es-se-sa.html)). The town's one famous figure of the Peninsular War is Francisca Pérez-Cerpa, a mother of seven sons who, according to local accounts, asked the general commanding the Spanish troops to enrol her three elder sons, offering to pay the cost herself; the Cortes de Cádiz are said to have rewarded her with a life pension and the military rank of colonel ([andalucia.com](https://www.andalucia.com/province/sevilla/salteras/home); [FOTW Flags of the World](https://www.fahnenversand.de/fotw/flags/es-se-sa.html)). That story rests on tier3 sources only.

The town's symbols were formalised late. The coat of arms — a Roman altar, the Virgin of the Olive, the Guzmán arms and an olive tree, with the 1443 privilege in the bordure — was designed by Manuel Troncoso Jurado and prescribed by decree of 2 September 1986, and the flag of three equal horizontal stripes of green, white and blue, charged with the coat of arms, was adopted on 28 September 1988 and prescribed by Decree No. 8 of 17 January 1990 ([FOTW Flags of the World](https://www.fahnenversand.de/fotw/flags/es-se-sa.html)).

## Olive groves, churches and music

Salteras's heritage is a clear example of the olive-growing tradition of the Aljarafe ([Turismo de la Provincia de Sevilla](https://www.turismosevilla.org/es/la-provincia-de-sevilla/nuestros-pueblos/salteras)). The parish church of Santa María de la Oliva, in the Plaza de España, began construction in 1540 in Mudéjar style; its interior holds a Visigothic slab dated 520, a main altarpiece and Baroque paintings and sculptures ([Turismo de la Provincia de Sevilla](https://www.turismosevilla.org/es/la-provincia-de-sevilla/nuestros-pueblos/salteras)). Beside the church stands the chapel of the Virgen de la Oliva, the town's patroness, built over an old hermitage; a third temple, the chapel of the Vera Cruz, holds the images of that brotherhood ([Turismo de la Provincia de Sevilla](https://www.turismosevilla.org/es/la-provincia-de-sevilla/nuestros-pueblos/salteras)).

Beyond the churches, the town's landmarks are working ones. The Hacienda El Mayorazgo, a traditional seventeenth-century Andalusian farmhouse, is today the Casa de la Cultura, and the Arabic fountain of La Alberquilla stands about three kilometres from the town ([andalucia.com](https://www.andalucia.com/province/sevilla/salteras/home)). Just outside Salteras is the Museo Pickman, the ceramics museum of La Cartuja de Sevilla, holding about 300 pieces from a collection declared of Cultural Interest, begun by the English merchant Charles Pickman ([andalucia.com](https://www.andalucia.com/province/sevilla/salteras/home)). The Fernández y Roche artisanal hat factory still makes hats by hand and is one of only ten factories of its kind left in the world ([Turismo de la Provincia de Sevilla](https://www.turismosevilla.org/es/la-provincia-de-sevilla/nuestros-pueblos/salteras)).

The festivals keep old devotions on the town's own calendar. The fiestas in honour of the Virgen de la Oliva fall in the first week of February, and the Feria and Corpus Christi are celebrated in August — Salteras is one of the Sevillian municipalities, together with Umbrete and Villanueva del Ariscal, that does not hold Corpus Christi in spring ([Turismo de la Provincia de Sevilla](https://www.turismosevilla.org/es/la-provincia-de-sevilla/nuestros-pueblos/salteras)). The Feria runs from 11 to 15 August, and the year also brings the Three Kings procession on 5 January, Carnival in February and Holy Week ([andalucia.com](https://www.andalucia.com/province/sevilla/salteras/home)). Grilled meat is the local speciality, with rice with hare, chickpeas with cod and the sweet barcos de sidra among the typical dishes ([Turismo de la Provincia de Sevilla](https://www.turismosevilla.org/es/la-provincia-de-sevilla/nuestros-pueblos/salteras)).

Music is the town's most widely known export. Salteras is known across the province for its bands, and its two music societies — the Asociación Filarmónica Nuestra Señora de la Oliva and the Sociedad Filarmónica Cultural Nuestra Señora del Carmen — carry recognised prestige throughout Andalusia ([Turismo de la Provincia de Sevilla](https://www.turismosevilla.org/es/la-provincia-de-sevilla/nuestros-pueblos/salteras)). The older of the two, La Oliva, was formally founded as a philharmonic society on 4 January 1913, with roots in a formation documented around 1900 under Manuel Chico Morales ([Junta de Cofradías de la Semana Santa de Cuenca](https://www.juntacofradiascuenca.es/actualidad/La-Sociedad-Filarm-nica-Ntra-Sra-La-Oliva-Salteras-interpretar--Concierto-inicio---1336)). Joaquín de la Orden directed the band from 1923 to 1984 and was awarded the Medalla al Mérito en el Trabajo in 1979 for a lifetime of teaching music to the town's young people ([Junta de Cofradías de la Semana Santa de Cuenca](https://www.juntacofradiascuenca.es/actualidad/La-Sociedad-Filarm-nica-Ntra-Sra-La-Oliva-Salteras-interpretar--Concierto-inicio---1336)). Later directors included Amadora Mercado Pérez, who in 2018 became the band's first woman director and the first woman to direct in Seville's Holy Week, and Irene Gómez Calado; the current director is the composer José Peña Rubio ([Junta de Cofradías de la Semana Santa de Cuenca](https://www.juntacofradiascuenca.es/actualidad/La-Sociedad-Filarm-nica-Ntra-Sra-La-Oliva-Salteras-interpretar--Concierto-inicio---1336)). Today La Oliva accompanies nine brotherhoods of Seville's Holy Week and three more in the province, including the Vera Cruz of Salteras itself, and it has recorded 17 albums and runs a youth band ([Junta de Cofradías de la Semana Santa de Cuenca](https://www.juntacofradiascuenca.es/actualidad/La-Sociedad-Filarm-nica-Ntra-Sra-La-Oliva-Salteras-interpretar--Concierto-inicio---1336)).

## The Centro Santa Ángela de la Cruz

The institution that puts Salteras on the national map is the Centro Santa Ángela de la Cruz, run by the [Asociación Española de Familias de Personas con Sordoceguera (APASCIDE)](/organizations/asociacion-espanola-de-familias-de-personas-con-sordoceguera-apascide/). Queen Sofía inaugurated it on 26 October 2010 as the first centre in Spain for the comprehensive care of young people and adults with deafblindness — combined hearing and visual disability ([SID — Universidad de Salamanca](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/)). It was built for €2.2 million, funded by Plan E, the regional administration and the Obra Social de Caja Madrid, on the El Almuédano estate ceded by the Archbishopric of Seville ([SID — Universidad de Salamanca](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/)).

The centre's scale is deliberately small, because the work is one-to-one. At its opening it had 45 places — 17 residential and 28 day-centre — with 60% of them concerted with the Junta de Andalucía, which invested about €680,000 a year; all the residential places were already assigned and there was a waiting list ([SID — Universidad de Salamanca](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/)). By 2023 the mix had shifted to 26 residential and 19 day-centre places, concerted with three autonomous communities, while the total stayed at 45 ([Revista Andalucía Económica](https://andaluciaeconomica.com/nuevos-equipos-informaticos-facilitan-el-aprendizaje-de-personas-con-sordoceguera/)). Forty staff work there — almost one carer or mediator per user — all knowing sign language, among them special educators, psychologists, social workers and guide-interpreters, plus at least a dozen Apascide volunteers ([SID — Universidad de Salamanca](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/)). The first residents were aged 16 to 40; rooms are differentiated by colour and texture so residents can tell them apart, and the centre has gardens, a sensory-stimulation room and a spa, with at least two leisure outings a week on bus line 176 ([SID — Universidad de Salamanca](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/)).

The centre's national standing was recognised in 2013, when the Colegio Oficial de Enfermería of the province of Seville awarded it the XXI Premio San Juan de Dios; the jury's citation called it unique in Spain in offering quality care to deafblind young people from different regions ([ArteSacro](https://www.artesacro.org/Noticia/Ver/86322/xxi-premio-san-juan-dios-colegio-oficial-enfermeria-ha-recaido-centro)). The need it serves is large: an estimated minimum of 7,000 people in Spain live with deafblindness, about 10% of them totally deafblind, and APASCIDE itself was declared an association of public utility by the [Ministry of the Interior](/organizations/ministry-of-the-interior-spain/) on 4 April 2006 ([Revista Andalucía Económica](https://andaluciaeconomica.com/nuevos-equipos-informaticos-facilitan-el-aprendizaje-de-personas-con-sordoceguera/)). In 2023 the neighbouring Fundación Cobre Las Cruces donated six new adapted computers to modernise a workshop unrenewed for over twelve years ([Revista Andalucía Económica](https://andaluciaeconomica.com/nuevos-equipos-informaticos-facilitan-el-aprendizaje-de-personas-con-sordoceguera/)). The Cobre Las Cruces mining-metallurgical complex, which sits between Gerena, Guillena and Salteras and has belonged to First Quantum Minerals since 2013, has invested over €1,100 million in the project since production began in 2009 ([Revista Andalucía Económica](https://andaluciaeconomica.com/nuevos-equipos-informaticos-facilitan-el-aprendizaje-de-personas-con-sordoceguera/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Where is Salteras?**
Salteras is a municipality in the province of Seville, Andalusia, Spain, in the Aljarafe comarca about 12 km from Seville, reachable by car on the A-8077 in about half an hour, by Cercanías train on line C5, or by bus M-175 from Seville's Plaza de Armas ([Turismo de la Provincia de Sevilla](https://www.turismosevilla.org/es/la-provincia-de-sevilla/nuestros-pueblos/salteras)).

**How many people live in Salteras?**
About 5,500, according to the tourism guide andalucia.com; the town had 5,449 inhabitants in 2013 according to FOTW. No exact current figure is asserted here because the INE's official 2025 figure was not verified in a fully-read INE source ([andalucia.com](https://www.andalucia.com/province/sevilla/salteras/home); [FOTW Flags of the World](https://www.fahnenversand.de/fotw/flags/es-se-sa.html)).

**What is the Centro Santa Ángela de la Cruz?**
It is Spain's first — and still only — residence and day centre specialising in young people and adults with deafblindness, run by APASCIDE in Salteras and inaugurated by Queen Sofía on 26 October 2010, with 45 places in total ([Revista Andalucía Económica](https://andaluciaeconomica.com/nuevos-equipos-informaticos-facilitan-el-aprendizaje-de-personas-con-sordoceguera/); [SID — Universidad de Salamanca](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/)).
