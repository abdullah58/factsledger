## Overview

Leicester is a city and unitary authority in the geographic and historic county of [Leicestershire](/places/leicestershire/), [England](/places/england/), lying on the River Soar and the Grand Union Canal ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Leicester-city-and-unitary-authority-England)). It was the site of a prominent Roman settlement, Ratae Corieltauvorum, where the Fosse Way crossed the River Soar, and the Jewry Wall — a remnant of a Roman bath house and palaestra — still bears witness to that early period ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Leicester-city-and-unitary-authority-England)). The city held 368,572 people at the 2021 census, up from 329,839 in 2011, with a mid-2024 estimate of 388,348, and its 2021 census recorded 159,977 Asian, 150,657 White and 28,766 Black residents, with 91,161 Christians, 86,443 Muslims and 65,821 Hindus ([CityPopulation](https://www.citypopulation.de/en/uk/england/admin/east_midlands/E06000016__leicester/)). Leicester is the county town of Leicestershire, the city where Richard III's skeleton was found under a car park in 2012, home to Diwali celebrations counted among the largest outside India, and the city whose football club won the Premier League in 2016 from odds of 5,000-1 ([University of Leicester](https://le.ac.uk/richard-iii/grey-friars); [InYourArea](https://www.inyourarea.co.uk/news/university-of-leicester-vice-chancellor-switches-on-diwali-lights); [NPR Illinois](https://www.nprillinois.org/2016-05-02/longshot-leicester-city-win-english-premiere-league-title)).

Leicester's story is one of repeated reinvention on the same ground. The Roman town became a Norman borough with a considerable burgess community, then an important medieval town whose timber castle, later reinforced and expanded in stone, had fallen into disrepair by the time Richard III visited before the Battle of Bosworth in 1485; today two gateways to the outer walls are the principal remains of the castle complex ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Leicester-city-and-unitary-authority-England)). The Grand Union Canal linked Leicester to [London](/places/london/) in the early nineteenth century, and in 1832 the railway joined the town with the small Leicestershire coalfield to the north-west; rapid industrial development followed, built first on hosiery and knitwear — the city's oldest industry — then on nineteenth-century footwear manufacture and light engineering ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Leicester-city-and-unitary-authority-England)). Although Leicester lost its city status in the eleventh century, a royal charter of incorporation was granted in 1589, and in 1919 it was again officially recognised as a city ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Leicester-city-and-unitary-authority-England)).

## Two thousand years on the Soar

Leicester's historic fabric survives in fragments that map its whole past. The Church of St Martin — first constructed by the Normans and then rebuilt and enlarged between the thirteenth and fifteenth centuries — became Leicester Cathedral in 1927, after the diocese of Leicester was constituted by the Church of England ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Leicester-city-and-unitary-authority-England)). Among the city's other historic structures are the Guildhall and Trinity Hospital, both dating from the fourteenth century, and Wyggeston School, from the sixteenth century ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Leicester-city-and-unitary-authority-England)). The focal centre of the town is the Clock Tower, from which shopping streets radiate, and the city has undergone much redevelopment since the Second World War ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Leicester-city-and-unitary-authority-England)). The city covers 28 square miles, and its population has grown by more than a tenth in each of the last two census decades, from 279,921 in 2001 to 329,839 in 2011 to 368,572 in 2021 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Leicester-city-and-unitary-authority-England); [CityPopulation](https://www.citypopulation.de/en/uk/england/admin/east_midlands/E06000016__leicester/)).

During the twentieth century Leicester experienced large-scale immigration and became one of Britain's most ethnically diverse cities: in the 1970s in particular, many South Asian refugees settled in Leicester after being forced from East Africa by Africanization policies, most significantly those initiated in Uganda by Idi Amin, followed by significant numbers of citizens of Somali origin in the 1990s and large numbers of immigrants from eastern Europe at the beginning of the twenty-first century ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Leicester-city-and-unitary-authority-England)). The 2021 census made the result concrete: the city recorded 86,443 Muslims, 65,821 Hindus, 16,451 Sikhs and 84,607 people of no religion alongside its Christian plurality ([CityPopulation](https://www.citypopulation.de/en/uk/england/admin/east_midlands/E06000016__leicester/)). Leicester has two universities — the University of Leicester, chartered in 1957 after its founding as a university college in 1918, and De Montfort University — and its cultural venues include Curve Theatre, opened in 2008, and De Montfort Hall ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Leicester-city-and-unitary-authority-England)).

## Richard III, Diwali and a 5,000-1 title

In the final battle of the Wars of the Roses at Bosworth Field in Leicestershire on 22 August 1485, the Yorkist King Richard III led a fateful cavalry charge and was killed, making Henry Tudor king of England; Richard's body was buried with little ceremony in the church of the Franciscan friars, the Grey Friars, in Leicester ([University of Leicester](https://le.ac.uk/richard-iii/grey-friars)). Fifty-three years later the friary was dissolved, the church demolished, and the grave lost, until rumours spread that the skeleton had been dug up and thrown into the River Soar — a belief commonly held until the discovery of the remains in 2012 ([University of Leicester](https://le.ac.uk/richard-iii/grey-friars)). The excavation, launched on 24 August 2012, found a burial in the choir of the Grey Friars church within hours, and on 4 February 2013 the University of Leicester announced that, beyond reasonable doubt, the exhumed individual was Richard III ([University of Leicester](https://le.ac.uk/richard-iii/grey-friars)). He was reburied in Leicester Cathedral on 26 March 2015 after five days of commemorations, during which more than 20,000 people visited his coffin; the service was presided over by the Archbishop of Canterbury, Justin Welby, and attended by Sophie, Countess of Wessex, and the actor Benedict Cumberbatch, a distant relation of the king ([iObserve / Catholic News Service](https://iobserve.org/2015/03/26/richard-iii-catholic-king-of-england-reburied-at-leicester-cathedral/)).

Leicester's modern fame is just as improbable. Its Diwali celebrations on Belgrave Road, the Golden Mile, were illuminated with 6,000 lamps at the 2022 switch-on, an event described as one of the largest Diwali celebrations outside of India, complete with the Wheel of Light, an aerial firework display and a Diwali village ([InYourArea](https://www.inyourarea.co.uk/news/university-of-leicester-vice-chancellor-switches-on-diwali-lights)). In sport, Leicester City — a small club from central England that started the 2015-16 season at 5,000-1 odds — clinched the Premier League title on 2 May 2016, its first top-tier title in the club's 132-year history, when second-placed Tottenham failed to beat Chelsea ([NPR Illinois](https://www.nprillinois.org/2016-05-02/longshot-leicester-city-win-english-premiere-league-title)). The city's National Space Centre, opened in 2001 in a building designed by Nicholas Grimshaw, hosted the control room from which the Beagle 2 mission to Mars was orchestrated and guided, displays an original Russian Soyuz spacecraft, and welcomed Buzz Aldrin in 2005 ([Leicester.co.uk](https://www.leicester.co.uk/spacecentre/)).

## Leicester in the wider county

Leicester's role as the county's hub is practical as well as ceremonial. It is the city where the [Barrow upon Soar rail crash](/events/barrow-upon-soar-rail-crash/) of 1 February 2008 played out in the aftermath: London services terminated at Leicester while all four lines through [Barrow upon Soar](/places/barrow-upon-soar/) were blocked, the injured train driver was taken to [Leicester Royal Infirmary](/organizations/leicester-royal-infirmary/), and the prosecution of Network Rail was heard at [Leicester Crown Court](/organizations/leicester-crown-court/) ([RAIB](https://www.gov.uk/raib-reports/collision-of-a-train-with-a-demolished-footbridge-barrow-upon-soar); [New Civil Engineer](https://www.newcivilengineer.com/archive/network-rail-fined-for-bridge-collapse-and-train-derailment-04-10-2011/)). The city stands on the [Midland Main Line](/places/midland-main-line/), with [Barrow upon Soar railway station](/places/barrow-upon-soar-railway-station/) nine miles to its north on the line towards Loughborough, [Nottingham](/places/nottingham/) and [Norwich](/places/norwich/) ([Rail Engineer](https://www.railengineer.co.uk/in-case-you-missed-it-broken-bridge-at-barrow/)). In the [United Kingdom](/places/united-kingdom/)'s geography, Leicester is an East Midlands city: a Roman crossing point that became a hosiery town, a migration city, a university city, and — twice in four years — the stage for a king's rediscovery and a football miracle.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Leicester known for?**
Leicester is known for its Roman origins as Ratae Corieltauvorum, the 2012 discovery of Richard III's skeleton under a car park and his 2015 reburial in the cathedral, Diwali celebrations among the largest outside India, and Leicester City's 5,000-1 Premier League title win in 2016.

**Why was Richard III found in Leicester?**
Richard III was killed at Bosworth Field in 1485 and buried by friars in their Grey Friars church in Leicester. After the friary's dissolution the grave was lost, until University of Leicester archaeologists excavated the site in 2012 and identified the skeleton beyond reasonable doubt in 2013. He was reburied in Leicester Cathedral after five days of commemorations.

**Is Leicester the county town of Leicestershire?**
Yes. Leicester is the county town of Leicestershire and lies within the geographic county, but it is a separate unitary authority governed by Leicester City Council, not by Leicestershire County Council. The administrative county covers the seven districts outside the city, while the city runs its own county-level services.

**How diverse is Leicester?**
Very. The 2021 census recorded 159,977 Asian, 150,657 White and 28,766 Black residents, with 86,443 Muslims, 65,821 Hindus, 16,451 Sikhs and 84,607 people of no religion. Britannica calls it one of Britain's most ethnically diverse cities, shaped by migration from East Africa, Somalia and eastern Europe.

**When did Leicester become a city?**
Leicester lost its city status in the eleventh century, received a royal charter of incorporation in 1589, and was again officially recognised as a city in 1919. Its Norman-built St Martin's church, rebuilt and enlarged between the thirteenth and fifteenth centuries, became Leicester Cathedral in 1927 after the diocese of Leicester was constituted.
