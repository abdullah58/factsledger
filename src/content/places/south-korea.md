South Korea is the East Asian nation of about 50 million people that hosts the world's only United Nations cemetery: 2,339 Korean War servicemembers from 14 countries rest in Busan under permanent UN management, a legacy of the 1950–53 war that forged the country's "blood brother" bond with Türkiye ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/opinion/preserving-the-un-memorial-cemeterys-balance-between-remembrance-and-public-space/12567489)). Asia's fourth-largest economy and home to memory-chip giants Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/economy/2026/10/1/south-koreas-exports-hit-record-high-on-ai-boom)), it is also the country with the world's lowest fertility rate — 0.72 children per woman in 2023 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/world-news/south-korea-s-fertility-rate-which-is-world-s-lowest-drops-to-fresh-low-124022800454_1.html)).

This profile covers the three stories the standard country overviews skip: the one-of-a-kind UN cemetery in Busan, the Turkish brigade's blood-forged friendship with South Korea — including the true story behind the film [Ayla: The Daughter of War](https://factsledger.com/creative-works/ayla-the-daughter-of-war/) — and the paradox of a chip-export superpower confronting a demographic cliff.

## The world's only UN cemetery

The United Nations Memorial Cemetery in Korea (UNMCK) sits in Daeyeon-dong, in Busan's Nam District, and holds a distinction no other burial ground on earth shares: it is the only cemetery in the world administered by the United Nations ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/opinion/preserving-the-un-memorial-cemeterys-balance-between-remembrance-and-public-space/12567489)). Established in January 1951 by the United Nations Command during the 1950–53 Korean War, the site was donated in perpetuity by Korea's National Assembly in 1955; that December a UN General Assembly resolution placed it under permanent UN management ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/opinion/preserving-the-un-memorial-cemeterys-balance-between-remembrance-and-public-space/12567489)). It holds 2,339 servicemembers from 14 countries who died defending Korea's freedom and peace ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/opinion/preserving-the-un-memorial-cemeterys-balance-between-remembrance-and-public-space/12567489)). The cemetery marked its 75th anniversary in 2026: about 470,000 people visited the previous year, with numbers on track to pass 600,000, and since 2017 the "Turn Toward Busan" ceremony has invited people worldwide to face Busan in a minute of silence every November 11 ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/opinion/preserving-the-un-memorial-cemeterys-balance-between-remembrance-and-public-space/12567489)). An 11-country international committee manages the grounds ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/opinion/preserving-the-un-memorial-cemeterys-balance-between-remembrance-and-public-space/12567489)).

## Blood brothers: the Turkish brigade and Ayla

A bond written in blood and orphans. Turkish soldiers were among the first UN forces in September 1950 ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)). Their brigades fought at Kunwu-ri, Jangseungcheon and Nevada Outpost, earning Korean and US valor awards ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)). Korean counts: 14,936 deployed — 724 killed, 175 missing, 234 captured, 2,493 wounded; Türkiye's 21,212 in four brigades ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350); [Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkish-soldiers-remains-from-korean-war-returned-to-authorities-212562)). Troops sheltered orphans at Suwon's "Ankara School" ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)). Most famous: [Kim Eun-ja](/people/kim-eun-ja/), found in 1950 by Sergeant Süleyman Dilbirliği, who named her "Ayla," the halo of light around the moon, and raised her for a year ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)). Reunited in 2010, their story became 2017's film [Ayla: The Daughter of War](https://factsledger.com/creative-works/ayla-the-daughter-of-war/), Türkiye's Oscar submission, with a PR campaign that October in South Korea ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)). One veteran's verdict: the two are "the first and only nation in the world that calls each other Blood Brothers" ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).

## An export powerhouse facing a demographic cliff

South Korea's economy is a study in concentration: Asia's fourth-largest economy is home to one of the world's largest semiconductor industries, led by SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/economy/2026/10/1/south-koreas-exports-hit-record-high-on-ai-boom)). The two chipmakers control roughly 80 percent of the global high-bandwidth memory market and 60 percent of DRAM, according to Counterpoint Research ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/economy/2026/10/1/south-koreas-exports-hit-record-high-on-ai-boom)). In September 2026 exports hit a monthly record of $120.9 billion, and the January–September total of $814.5 billion already exceeded all of 2025, with the government targeting $1 trillion in annual exports ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/economy/2026/10/1/south-koreas-exports-hit-record-high-on-ai-boom)). Yet the same country faces a demographic squeeze: the fertility rate fell to 0.72 in 2023, the lowest in the world and the only sub-1.0 rate in the OECD, with just 230,000 births — down 7.7 percent ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/world-news/south-korea-s-fertility-rate-which-is-world-s-lowest-drops-to-fresh-low-124022800454_1.html)). The implication is stark: despite more than $270 billion spent on pro-natal policies since 2006, experts cited by the outlet estimate the population could halve by century's end ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/world-news/south-korea-s-fertility-rate-which-is-world-s-lowest-drops-to-fresh-low-124022800454_1.html)).

## FAQ

**Why is the cemetery in Busan the world's only UN cemetery?**
It was established in January 1951 by the UN Command to bury Korean War dead; in 1955 Korea's National Assembly donated the land in perpetuity and a UN General Assembly resolution placed it under permanent UN management. No other burial ground anywhere is administered by the United Nations ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/opinion/preserving-the-un-memorial-cemeterys-balance-between-remembrance-and-public-space/12567489)).

**How many Turkish soldiers fought in the Korean War?**
Korean records count 14,936 men — 724 killed, 175 missing, 234 taken prisoner, 2,493 wounded — while Turkish records count 21,212 soldiers across four brigades, with about 900 fallen or missing. The gap reflects total rotations versus the 1950–53 deployment count; 462 Turkish soldiers rest at the Busan UN cemetery ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350); [Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkish-soldiers-remains-from-korean-war-returned-to-authorities-212562)).

**Who was the real-life Ayla?**
Kim Eun-ja, a Korean girl orphaned in 1950, was found by Turkish Sergeant Süleyman Dilbirliği, who named her Ayla — the halo of light around the moon — and cared for her for a year. Reunited in Seoul in 2010, their story became the 2017 film [Ayla: The Daughter of War](https://factsledger.com/creative-works/ayla-the-daughter-of-war/), Türkiye's Oscar submission ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350); [Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/ayla-kim-eunja-passes-away-at-80-209134)).

**Why does South Korea have the world's lowest fertility rate?**
The 2023 rate was 0.72 children per woman — the only sub-1.0 rate in the OECD — driven by the cost of living and damage to women's career opportunities, according to Statistics Korea data. More than $270 billion in pro-natal spending since 2006 has not reversed the decline, and experts estimate the population could halve by century's end ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/world-news/south-korea-s-fertility-rate-which-is-world-s-lowest-drops-to-fresh-low-124022800454_1.html)).

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not pin Kim Eun-ja's death year; Hürriyet Daily News reports she died at 80 after pneumonia, citing a Turkish Embassy in Seoul statement, but the article's date was not established in this read.
- It does not reconcile the 14,936 vs 21,212 Turkish troop figures; both are reported with their definitional bases and flagged in openItems.
- It does not assert the 2017 Hürriyet campaign framing — "the 60th Anniversary of the Turkish soldiers' landing in Busan" — as chronology; the first Turkish troops arrived in September 1950, and the reunion year 2010 was the actual 60th anniversary. The framing is quoted faithfully and flagged.
- It does not cover the K-pop/Hallyu cultural wave or the Hangul writing system in detail; no source read in full in this pass supported those claims, so they were deliberately excluded rather than asserted thinly.
- It does not give GDP or HDI rankings; those figures were available only in Wikipedia's infobox and were not independently re-verified.
