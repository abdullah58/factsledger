## Overview

Brazil is the Federative Republic of Brazil — the largest country and the largest economy in South America, and the fifth largest country in the world by area after Russia, Canada, China and the United States ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Brazil)). About 221 to 223 million people live there, depending on the source and the estimate year, in a federal presidential republic whose capital is Brasília and whose official language is Portuguese ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Brazil)). It is the only Portuguese-speaking country in the Americas, and it borders every South American country except Chile and Ecuador, with some 4,600 miles of Atlantic coastline ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Brazil)).

In the [Booksy](/organizations/booksy/) story, Brazil matters as scale. By January 2021 it was one of the six markets Booksy operated in — alongside the United States, the United Kingdom, Poland, Spain and [South Africa](/places/south-africa/) — with 13 million consumers across the company's markets ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)). For a beauty-booking platform, Brazil is the prize market of Latin America: 87.8% of its people live in towns and cities, São Paulo alone has 22.62 million people, and there are 216 million mobile subscriptions ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)).

## Land, people and economy

Brazil covers 8,510,418 square kilometres according to Britannica — 8,515,770 according to the Factbook — and contains most of the Amazon basin, the world's largest tropical rainforest ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Brazil); [CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)). Its six biomes — Amazônia, Caatinga, Cerrado, Mata Atlântica, Pampas and Pantanal — run from rainforest to grassland, and forest still covers 58.9% of the land ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Brazil); [CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)). The country's name comes from the brazil tree that once grew plentifully along the coast and was used to produce a deep red dye ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)).

The population is young by European standards — median age 35.4 — and heavily urban: São Paulo (22.62 million), Rio de Janeiro (13.728 million) and Brasília (4.873 million) are the largest cities ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)). The ethnic composition is mixed 45.3%, White 43.5%, Black 10.2%, Indigenous 0.6% and Asian 0.4% (2022), and the religious split is Catholic 56.8% and Evangelical 26.9% ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)). More than 200 Indigenous languages are spoken ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Brazil)). Literacy is 94.8% (2024), and education spending is 5.6% of GDP ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)).

Economically, Brazil is an upper-middle-income country and the largest economy in Latin America, a member of Mercosur, BRICS and the G20 and an OECD accession candidate ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)). GDP in purchasing-power terms was $4.165 trillion in 2024, growing 3.4%, with per-capita PPP income of $19,600; nominal GDP was $2.18 trillion ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)). Services dominate at 59.3% of GDP, industry is 21.3% and agriculture 5.6% (2024) ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)). The labour force is 106.79 million, unemployment 7.7% (2024), and exports were $388.3 billion in 2024 — led by soybeans, crude petroleum, iron ore, raw sugar and corn, with China taking 30% and the United States 10% ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)). Growth is driven by strong domestic consumption, but the Factbook notes "high inequality in income and access to health and education": the Gini coefficient is 51.6 (2023), and the richest 10% take 40.8% of household income against 1.3% for the poorest 10% ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)). Hydroelectricity supplies 60.2% of installed generating capacity, and electricity access is universal ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)).

## From empire to republic to the Lula cycle

Brazil declared independence from Portugal on 7 September 1822 under Dom Pedro I — the "Dia do Fico," "I am staying," when the prince refused to return to Lisbon — and remained a monarchy until slavery was abolished in 1888 and the republic proclaimed in 1889 ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Brazil)). Rio de Janeiro was the capital from 1763 until 1960, when the government moved to the newly built Brasília; Salvador had held the role before Rio, from 1549 to 1763 ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)).

The 20th century was dominated by Getúlio Vargas, in power from 1930 to 1945 and again from 1951 to 1954, and then by the military regime of 1964 to 1985 ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)). The 1988 constitution restored civilian democracy, and the country has held regular elections since ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)). Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva served two terms as president from 2003 to 2010, a period that included the 2014 World Cup and the 2016 Olympics; his successor Dilma Rousseff was removed from office in 2016, Lula himself was jailed in 2018–19 before his conviction was overturned in 2021, and he defeated Jair Bolsonaro in the 2022 election, returning to the presidency on 1 January 2023 with 50.9% of the second-round vote against Bolsonaro's 49.1% ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)). Brazil held the G20 presidency in 2024, and the next presidential election is scheduled for 4 October 2026 ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)).

Brazil's global cultural footprint is outsized: five FIFA World Cups, a record, and figures from Pelé and Marta to Ayrton Senna, Paulo Coelho and Oscar Niemeyer ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Brazil)). It has 24 UNESCO World Heritage Sites, including Brasília, the historic centre of Salvador de Bahia, Iguaçu National Park and the Central Amazon Conservation Complex ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)).

**What language is spoken in Brazil?**
Portuguese — Brazil is the only Portuguese-speaking country in the Americas, and more than 200 Indigenous languages are also spoken ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Brazil)).

**When did Brazil become independent?**
On 7 September 1822, from Portugal, under Dom Pedro I; the monarchy lasted until slavery was abolished in 1888 and the republic was proclaimed in 1889 ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)).

**Who is Brazil's president?**
Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, in office since 1 January 2023 after defeating Jair Bolsonaro in the 2022 runoff with 50.9% of the vote; the next election is scheduled for 4 October 2026 ([CIA World Factbook](https://www.worldfactbooks.com/country/brazil/)).

**How big is Brazil's economy?**
