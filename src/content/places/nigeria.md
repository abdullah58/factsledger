## Overview

Nigeria, officially the Federal Republic of Nigeria, is Africa's most populous country and a federal republic on the Gulf of Guinea in West Africa, with an estimated 239,430,000 people, a capital at Abuja, and Lagos as its leading commercial and industrial city ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Nigeria)). It gained independence from the [United Kingdom](/places/united-kingdom/) on 1 October 1960, is made up of 36 states plus the Federal Capital Territory, and counts English as its official language alongside hundreds of Indigenous languages ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Nigeria); [Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria](https://jurist.ng/constitution/sec-3)).

Nigeria's weight comes from scale and output at once: it is Africa's top oil producer, home to Nollywood — one of the world's largest film industries by number of productions — and a driving force in Afrobeats, now heard worldwide ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/nigeria-union-requests-more-troops-combat-oil-theft-2024-04-05/); [UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2009/05/299102-nigeria-surpasses-hollywood-worlds-second-largest-film-producer-un); [The Lagos Review](https://thelagosreview.ng/burna-boy-performs-at-glastonbury-and-a-brief-history-of-afrobeats/)). Its people belong to at least 250 ethnic groups, the largest being the Hausa-Fulani, the Yoruba and the Igbo ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Nigeria)).

## Geography and people

Nigeria covers 923,768 square kilometres on Africa's western coast, bordered by Niger, Chad and Cameroon, the Gulf of Guinea, and Benin ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Nigeria)). Its terrain runs from plains in the north and south to plateaus and hills in the centre, with the Jos Plateau near the middle and the highest point, Chappal Waddi at 2,419 metres, on the south-eastern border with Cameroon ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Nigeria)). The Niger River, which gives the country its name, joins the Benue, its largest tributary, and drains through a vast delta into the Gulf of Guinea ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Nigeria)).

The delta holds major oil and gas reserves, whose exploitation since the mid-20th century has damaged the environment ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Nigeria)). Climate runs from arid in the north to humid equatorial in the south; about 55% of people lived in urban areas in 2024 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Nigeria)). Besides English, Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo, Fula and Edo are widely spoken, and roughly 70% of Nigerians are under 30 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Nigeria); [NPR](https://www.wamc.org/2023-05-29/nigeria-swears-in-a-new-president-facing-profound-economic-and-security-pressures)).

## From kingdoms to colonial rule

Long before Nigeria existed, its territory held powerful kingdoms: Ife was renowned for terracotta and bronze art, the Oyo Empire grew into one of West Africa's most powerful states on cavalry and trade, and the Kingdom of Benin built a centralised state famous for metalwork and the Benin Bronzes ([Gatmash News](https://gatmash.com/2026/10/12/one-map-many-peoples-how-nigeria-came-to-be/)). In the north, Kanem-Borno held influence around Lake Chad for centuries, while Usman dan Fodio's early-19th-century jihad produced the Sokoto Caliphate, one of West Africa's largest political formations ([Gatmash News](https://gatmash.com/2026/10/12/one-map-many-peoples-how-nigeria-came-to-be/)). Igbo-speaking communities governed through village assemblies, councils of elders and age grades rather than a single monarch ([Gatmash News](https://gatmash.com/2026/10/12/one-map-many-peoples-how-nigeria-came-to-be/)).

The name came later: British journalist Flora Shaw proposed 'Nigeria' in The Times of [London](/places/london/), deriving it from the Niger River ([Gatmash News](https://gatmash.com/2026/10/12/one-map-many-peoples-how-nigeria-came-to-be/)). Britain annexed Lagos in 1861, and on 1 January 1914 the Northern and Southern Protectorates were amalgamated as the Colony and Protectorate of Nigeria — an administrative decision, not a referendum ([Gatmash News](https://gatmash.com/2026/10/12/one-map-many-peoples-how-nigeria-came-to-be/)).

## Independence, civil war and the return to democracy

Nigeria became independent on 1 October 1960 under Prime Minister Abubakar Tafawa Balewa, and in 1963 became a republic under President Nnamdi Azikiwe, staying in the Commonwealth ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Nigeria)). The young federation soon fractured: coups in January and July 1966 were followed by killings of thousands of Igbo people in the north, and more than a million fled east ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/nigeria-biafra-civil-war-ends)). On 30 May 1967 the Eastern Region declared itself the Republic of Biafra under Lieutenant Colonel Chukwuemeka Odumegwu Ojukwu, and federal forces went to war on 6 July ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/nigeria-biafra-civil-war-ends)).

Biafra surrendered on 15 January 1970, after famine and shortages left severe suffering among some 3 million Igbo refugees ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/nigeria-biafra-civil-war-ends)). Soldiers then dominated politics for decades, through coups, the annulled 1993 election and the repressive rule of Sani Abacha ([Africa Facts Zone](https://africafactszone.com/nigeria-returned-civilian-rule-1999/)). After Abacha's death in 1998, General Abdulsalami Abubakar ran a transition, and on 29 May 1999 Olusegun Obasanjo was sworn in, opening the Fourth Republic — Nigeria's longest stretch of civilian rule ([Africa Facts Zone](https://africafactszone.com/nigeria-returned-civilian-rule-1999/)).

## Economy: oil, farming and creative industries

Nigeria is Africa's top oil producer; oil supplies over two-thirds of government earnings and about 90% of foreign-exchange income, exposing public finances to theft, sabotage and price swings ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/nigeria-union-requests-more-troops-combat-oil-theft-2024-04-05/)). Output was 1.48 million barrels per day in February, below a 1.78 million budget target, after theft and sabotage pushed Shell and ExxonMobil out of onshore assets, even as fuel from the new Dangote Refinery reached the domestic market ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/nigeria-union-requests-more-troops-combat-oil-theft-2024-04-05/)). Agriculture is the larger employer, at about 24% of gross domestic product and roughly half the workforce, plus 14% in the wider agro-food sector ([Ecofin Agency](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-agriculture/0104-54340-nigeria-secures-500-million-world-bank-funding-to-strengthen-agricultural-value-chains)).

A 2026 World Bank credit of $500 million backs the AGROW project for rice, maize, cassava and soybeans, aiming to raise smallholder productivity and reach about one million farmers ([Ecofin Agency](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-agriculture/0104-54340-nigeria-secures-500-million-world-bank-funding-to-strengthen-agricultural-value-chains)). That pairing — oil wealth above ground, farming beneath it — explains why Nigeria is at once Africa's largest economy and a country where many still struggle: when Bola Tinubu took office in 2023, unemployment had soared ([NPR](https://www.wamc.org/2023-05-29/nigeria-swears-in-a-new-president-facing-profound-economic-and-security-pressures)).

## Culture: Nollywood, Afrobeats and everyday life

Nollywood overtook Hollywood by output in a 2006 UNESCO survey: 872 productions, all video, against 1,091 from Bollywood, [India](/places/india/)'s Mumbai-based industry, and 485 films from the [United States](/places/united-states/) ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2009/05/299102-nigeria-surpasses-hollywood-worlds-second-largest-film-producer-un)). Low-cost video and informal screenings drove that explosion: 99% of screenings were informal, and 56% of films were in local languages ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2009/05/299102-nigeria-surpasses-hollywood-worlds-second-largest-film-producer-un)). Afrobeats tells a parallel story: it began in Nigeria and Ghana, blending West African sounds with pop, rap and dancehall, and is distinct from Afrobeat, which Fela Kuti pioneered in the 1960s and 1970s ([The Lagos Review](https://thelagosreview.ng/burna-boy-performs-at-glastonbury-and-a-brief-history-of-afrobeats/)).

D'banj's 2012 'Oliver Twist' reached the United Kingdom top 10; Spotify reported a 1,200% rise in Afrobeats streams after 2017 ([The Lagos Review](https://thelagosreview.ng/burna-boy-performs-at-glastonbury-and-a-brief-history-of-afrobeats/)). Burna Boy, Wizkid, Tems and Ayra Starr have since headlined stages; Burna Boy was the first African artist to headline a sold-out United Kingdom stadium show, at London Stadium ([The Lagos Review](https://thelagosreview.ng/burna-boy-performs-at-glastonbury-and-a-brief-history-of-afrobeats/)). Daily life reflects that diversity: hundreds of languages, in a country that still contains Muslim emirates and Christian missionary centres ([Gatmash News](https://gatmash.com/2026/10/12/one-map-many-peoples-how-nigeria-came-to-be/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Nigeria known for?**
Nigeria is known as Africa's most populous country and its top oil producer, and for two global cultural exports: Nollywood, one of the world's largest film industries by output, and Afrobeats, the West African pop sound led by stars such as Burna Boy, Wizkid, Tems and Ayra Starr.

**When did Nigeria gain independence?**
Nigeria gained independence from the United Kingdom on 1 October 1960, with Abubakar Tafawa Balewa as its first prime minister. In 1963 it adopted a new republican constitution, with Nnamdi Azikiwe as president, while remaining a member of the Commonwealth.

**How many states does Nigeria have?**
Nigeria is a federation of 36 states plus the Federal Capital Territory, where the capital, Abuja, is located. The constitution names all 36 states, from Abia and Adamawa in the east to Sokoto and Zamfara in the north-west. Each state has a governor headquartered in its capital city.

**What is Nollywood?**
Nollywood is Nigeria's film industry, built on low-cost video production and informal screenings rather than cinemas. In a 2006 UNESCO survey it released 872 productions, ahead of Hollywood's 485, with 56% of its films in local languages and 44% in English.

**What languages are spoken in Nigeria?**
English is the official language, but hundreds of languages are spoken, including Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo, Fula, Edo, Ibibio and Tiv. The three largest ethnic groups are the Hausa-Fulani in the north, the Yoruba in the south-west and the Igbo in the south-east.

- It does not treat the EBSCO reference work's 1963 dating of independence as correct; the profile uses 1 October 1960, with 1963 as the republic year.
- It does not present the population figure as a census count; 239,430,000 is Britannica's 2026 estimate, and other sources give different totals for different years.
- It does not rank Nollywood by revenue or current output; the UNESCO comparison is a 2006 count of productions, not a claim about today's market.
- It does not cover Nigeria's current security crises, regional politics or the 2023 election dispute in depth; those are context here, not the subject of this profile.
- It does not assert how many local government areas Nigeria has, since the constitutional text and other summaries disagree (768 versus 774).
