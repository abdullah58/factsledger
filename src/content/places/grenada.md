Grenada is the Caribbean country that keeps turning up in other people's stories — a 1979 revolution, a 1983 invasion, a 21st-century crypto billionaire's diplomatic passport — yet its own profile is straightforward. CARICOM, the regional bloc Grenada joined on May 1, 1974, lists it under the nickname "The Spice Isle": an independent state of about 344 square kilometres whose capital is St. George's, whose currency is the Eastern Caribbean dollar, and whose population it puts at 110,821 on a 2009 count ([CARICOM](https://caricom.org/country_profiles/grenada/)). The U.S. [State Department](https://factsledger.com/organizations/us-department-of-state/)'s background note describes it as a parliamentary democracy and independent sovereign state within the Commonwealth, with a governor general, a prime minister and cabinet, and a bicameral parliament ([U.S. Department of State, via the Free Library](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Background%20note:%20Grenada-a0252740795)). Britannica's children's encyclopedia puts it more plainly: a small island country in the eastern Caribbean Sea, called the Isle of Spice, known for growing nutmeg and other spices ([Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/kids/article/Grenada/345697?cmpCountryCode=US&cmpIsGdpr=false&cmpIsCcpa=true&cmpGeoRegion=GA)).

## A colonial handover chain

Before Europeans arrived, Carib Indians had driven the Arawaks from the island; Columbus landed on Grenada in 1498 during his third voyage and named it "Concepcion," though the name "Grenada" — probably taken by Spanish sailors for the city of Granada — was in common use by the 1700s ([U.S. Department of State](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Background%20note:%20Grenada-a0252740795)). The Caribs held the island uncolonized for more than a century after its discovery; in 1650 a French company founded by Cardinal Richelieu purchased Grenada from the English and set up a small settlement, and after several skirmishes, French reinforcements from Martinique defeated the Caribs ([U.S. Department of State](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Background%20note:%20Grenada-a0252740795)). The island stayed under French control until Britain captured it in 1762 during the Seven Years' War, with the Treaty of Paris formally ceding Grenada to Great Britain in 1763 and the Treaty of Versailles confirming it in 1783 after a brief French return ([U.S. Department of State](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Background%20note:%20Grenada-a0252740795)).

The crop that defines Grenada arrived through a personal introduction: in 1782 Sir Joseph Banks, the botanical adviser to King George III, introduced nutmeg to the island, and the soil proved ideal ([U.S. Department of State](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Background%20note:%20Grenada-a0252740795)). The collapse of the sugar estates, combined with nutmeg and cocoa, encouraged the growth of smaller landholdings and a land-owning yeoman farmer class; slavery was outlawed in 1834 ([U.S. Department of State](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Background%20note:%20Grenada-a0252740795)). Grenada's territory also includes the southern Grenadine Islands to the northwest of the main island — the northern Grenadines belong to Saint Vincent and the Grenadines ([Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/kids/article/Grenada/345697?cmpCountryCode=US&cmpIsGdpr=false&cmpIsCcpa=true&cmpGeoRegion=GA)).

## Independence, revolution, intervention

After the Federation of the West Indies collapsed in 1962, Britain and its remaining Eastern Caribbean dependencies developed associated statehood, and Grenada gained full autonomy over its internal affairs in March 1967 ([U.S. Department of State](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Background%20note:%20Grenada-a0252740795)). Full independence came on February 7, 1974, with Sir Eric Gairy as the country's first prime minister — making Grenada one of the smallest independent countries in the Western Hemisphere ([U.S. Department of State](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Background%20note:%20Grenada-a0252740795); [CARICOM](https://caricom.org/country_profiles/grenada/)).

Five years later came the rupture that still defines Grenada's international image. On March 13, 1979, the New Jewel Movement ousted Gairy in a coup and established a People's Revolutionary Government headed by Maurice Bishop, whose Marxist-Leninist government built close ties with Cuba and the Soviet bloc ([U.S. Department of State](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Background%20note:%20Grenada-a0252740795)). In October 1983 a power struggle inside the government ended with the arrest and execution of Bishop and several cabinet members by elements of the People's Revolutionary Army, and the killing of dozens of his supporters ([U.S. Department of State](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Background%20note:%20Grenada-a0252740795)). On October 25, 1983, a U.S.-Caribbean force landed on Grenada in response to an appeal from the Governor General and a request from Barbados and other Eastern Caribbean states; U.S. citizens were evacuated and order restored ([U.S. Department of State](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Background%20note:%20Grenada-a0252740795)). An advisory council ran the country until general elections in December 1984, which the New National Party won with 14 of 15 seats; the constitution suspended in 1979 was restored after those elections, and free elections have continued since ([U.S. Department of State](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Background%20note:%20Grenada-a0252740795)).

## People and economy

About 89% of Grenada's population is of African descent, with the rest of mixed East Indian, African, and Caucasian ancestry, small East Indian and European-descended communities, and traces of the original Arawak and Carib peoples; English is the official language ([U.S. Department of State](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Background%20note:%20Grenada-a0252740795)). Britannica notes the majority has Black African or mixed African and European roots, with small groups of South Asians and whites; most people are Christians, mainly Roman Catholics, and more than 60 percent live in rural areas ([Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/kids/article/Grenada/345697?cmpCountryCode=US&cmpIsGdpr=false&cmpIsCcpa=true&cmpGeoRegion=GA)).

The economy runs on what the land and sea give it: nutmeg, cocoa, bananas, fish, and mace on the agriculture side, with tourism as a very important industry and services alongside ([U.S. Department of State](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Background%20note:%20Grenada-a0252740795); [Britannica Kids](https://kids.britannica.com/kids/article/Grenada/345697?cmpCountryCode=US&cmpIsGdpr=false&cmpIsCcpa=true&cmpGeoRegion=GA)). On September 7, 2004, Hurricane Ivan brought that economy to a near standstill: 37 people were killed, 8,000 to 10,000 left homeless, and 90% of the island's buildings were damaged or destroyed, with overall damage of as much as 2.5 times annual GDP ([U.S. Department of State](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Background%20note:%20Grenada-a0252740795)).

## The WTO ambassadorship

In December 2021, Grenada's government appointed Justin Sun — the founder of the TRON blockchain platform — as its Ambassador and Permanent Representative to the World Trade Organization ([Cryptonews](https://cryptonews.com/news/trx-drops-as-justin-sun-leaves-tron-become-an-ambassador/)). Sun said he was stepping away from day-to-day operations at the Tron Foundation; a photograph distributed at the time showed him with Oliver Joseph, Grenada's minister of foreign affairs ([Cryptonews](https://cryptonews.com/news/trx-drops-as-justin-sun-leaves-tron-become-an-ambassador/)). "I look forward to the opportunity to represent Grenada and work with WTO leadership to reinvigorate global trade in various ways," Sun said, singling out the development of a digital economy internationally ([Cryptonews](https://cryptonews.com/news/trx-drops-as-justin-sun-leaves-tron-become-an-ambassador/)). A TRON DAO announcement dated July 4, 2022 put the official appointment date at December 20, 2021, and recorded Sun's debut on the global stage: he led the Grenada delegation at the WTO's 12th Ministerial Conference (MC12) in Geneva from June 12 to 15, 2022, speaking on the first day about multilateral trade, e-commerce, and the digital economy ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220704005428/en/)).
