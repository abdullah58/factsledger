## Overview

**New Delhi** is the capital of [India](/places/india/): the planned city built south of Old Delhi after the British announced the capital's move from Calcutta in 1911, formally inaugurated in 1931, and capital of independent India since 1947. The name is used three ways — the 42.7 sq km New Delhi Municipal Council area, a revenue district, and shorthand for the whole seat of government — inside the 1,483 sq km National Capital Territory of Delhi.

The distinction matters because the names are constantly conflated. Delhi generally refers to the National Capital Territory, which covers approximately 1,483 square kilometres and includes historic, central, suburban and rural areas — not only the government precinct associated with New Delhi ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/trends/photo/delhi-and-new-delhi-are-not-the-same-administrative-unit-here-is-the-accurate-and-updated-difference-550678-2026-08-22)). New Delhi is one of the territory's revenue districts, and the name is also commonly used for the national administrative core, much of which falls under the New Delhi Municipal Council ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/trends/photo/delhi-and-new-delhi-are-not-the-same-administrative-unit-here-is-the-accurate-and-updated-difference-550678-2026-08-22)). The often-quoted 42.7-square-kilometre figure refers to the NDMC's municipal jurisdiction, not every possible use of "New Delhi" ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/trends/photo/delhi-and-new-delhi-are-not-the-same-administrative-unit-here-is-the-accurate-and-updated-difference-550678-2026-08-22)).

## One name, three meanings

| NDMC municipal area | 42.7 sq km | The jurisdiction of the New Delhi Municipal Council, a statutory municipal body responsible for civic services in its designated central area |
| Revenue district | Part of the NCT | One of the NCT's 13 revenue districts (reorganised from 11 in December 2025, per Business Today) |
| Capital-area shorthand | Not a fixed boundary | The seat of government: Rashtrapati Bhavan, Parliament House, the Secretariat blocks, India Gate and the diplomatic enclave |
| Delhi (NCT) — for contrast, not New Delhi | ~1,483 sq km | The National Capital Territory, with an elected Legislative Assembly and Council of Ministers under Delhi's special constitutional arrangement |

Delhi's longer history explains why the capital sits here at all. The territory comprises the cities of Delhi, popularly known as Old Delhi, and New Delhi, India's capital, plus adjacent rural areas ([Britannica](https://WWW.Britannica.com/summary/Delhi)). Delhi was the capital of a Muslim dynasty from 1206, was sacked by Timur in 1398, was made capital again by the Mughal emperor Babur in 1526, was beautified by Shah Jahan beginning in 1638, was pillaged by Nadir Shah in 1739, surrendered to the Marathas in 1771, and was taken by the British in 1803 ([Britannica](https://WWW.Britannica.com/summary/Delhi)). It was a centre of the Indian Mutiny in 1857 ([Britannica](https://WWW.Britannica.com/summary/Delhi)). In 1911 the British decided to move the capital of British India from Calcutta to Delhi, and in 1912 construction began on New Delhi at a site just south of central Delhi ([Britannica](https://WWW.Britannica.com/summary/Delhi)).

## Building an imperial capital, 1911–1931

The move was politics before it was architecture. Nationalist opposition to colonial rule was building up in Calcutta, which had been the seat of British administration for around 150 years, and Lord Curzon's partition of Bengal had caused uproar, boycotts of foreign goods and political assassinations ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/arts-and-culture/heritage/the-making-of-new-delhi/article23596392.ece/amp/)). Delhi was chosen for a more central location, proximity to the summer capital Shimla, and its weight as a seat of power under the Delhi Sultanate and the Mughals — the government wanted to build on that symbolic association ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/arts-and-culture/heritage/the-making-of-new-delhi/article23596392.ece/amp/)). At the Coronation Durbar of 1911, King-Emperor George V announced the transfer of the seat of government from Calcutta to Delhi ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/arts-and-culture/heritage/the-making-of-new-delhi/article23596392.ece/amp/)).

The site chosen was Raisina Hill, south of Shahjahanabad, seen as an Indian Acropolis with the proposed Viceroy's House as its Parthenon ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/arts-and-culture/heritage/the-making-of-new-delhi/article23596392.ece/amp/)). Edwin Lutyens was appointed consultant to draw up a master plan, with Viceroy Lord Hardinge closely involved; Herbert Baker, who had designed colonial buildings in South Africa, joined in 1913 ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/arts-and-culture/heritage/the-making-of-new-delhi/article23596392.ece/amp/)). It was agreed that Lutyens would design the Viceroy's House and the overall layout of the city, while Baker would take care of the twin secretariat buildings, now the North and South Blocks ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/arts-and-culture/heritage/the-making-of-new-delhi/article23596392.ece/amp/)).

The two men fell out over the slope to the palace: Baker wanted the secretariats up on the acropolis with the Viceroy's House, and the steep gradient he built means the palace disappears from view as one approaches from India Gate, leaving only its dome visible — Lutyens called the result his "Bakerloo" ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/arts-and-culture/heritage/the-making-of-new-delhi/article23596392.ece/amp/)). The style was a mediated compromise: Lutyens and Baker preferred European classicism, Hardinge wanted at least some Indo-Saracenic character, and they settled on Western-style classical architecture with details filled in with Indian motifs ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/arts-and-culture/heritage/the-making-of-new-delhi/article23596392.ece/amp/)). The First World War interrupted construction, so the bulk of the work took place in the 1920s; the new city was formally inaugurated in February 1931 ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/arts-and-culture/heritage/the-making-of-new-delhi/article23596392.ece/amp/)).

The plan's bones are still the city's: monumental classicism, vast ceremonial avenues, open spaces, geometrical symmetry and a grand central axis ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/arts-and-culture/heritage/the-making-of-new-delhi/article23596392.ece/amp/)). King's Way — now Rajpath — ran as the principal imperial axis connecting Purana Qila to the Viceroy's House via India Gate and Vijay Chowk, while Queen's Way — now Janpath — crossed it at right angles toward Connaught Place ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/arts-and-culture/heritage/the-making-of-new-delhi/article23596392.ece/amp/)). Connaught Place, said to be modelled on the Royal Crescent in Bath, was completed around 1935 as two concentric circles of buildings ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/arts-and-culture/heritage/the-making-of-new-delhi/article23596392.ece/amp/)). India Gate, completed in 1921 and designed by Lutyens, is a war memorial dedicated to Indian soldiers killed in the First World War and other wars such as the Afghan War ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/arts-and-culture/heritage/the-making-of-new-delhi/article23596392.ece/amp/)). The Council Chamber, designed by Baker, housed three chambers: the Council of State (now the Rajya Sabha), the Legislative Assembly (now the Lok Sabha) and the Chamber of Princes ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/arts-and-culture/heritage/the-making-of-new-delhi/article23596392.ece/amp/)).

Rashtrapati Bhavan, the former Viceroy's House, is the set piece: designed by Lutyens and constructed from 1912 to 1929, it holds more than 340 rooms on grounds of over 320 acres, making it one of the largest presidential residences in the world ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rashtrapati-Bhavan?oasmId=96422)). Building it took a reported 700 million bricks and more than 30,000 laborers; about 300 families were removed from Raisina and Malcha villages and about 4,000 acres were cleared ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rashtrapati-Bhavan?oasmId=96422)). Its central copper dome, about 180 feet high, is modeled on the Buddhist style of the Sanchi Stupa built by the Mauryan emperor Ashoka, and Lutyens invented his own "Delhi order" fusing Greco-Roman and Indian elements ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rashtrapati-Bhavan?oasmId=96422)). The first Indian-born resident was C. Rajagopalachari, who took over in 1948 as governor-general; Rajendra Prasad was the first president of India to live there, sworn in on January 26, 1950 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rashtrapati-Bhavan?oasmId=96422)).

## The capital since 1947: Central Vista and the air

New Delhi was dedicated in 1931 and became the capital of independent India in 1947 — only 16 years after inauguration, Lord Mountbatten handed sovereignty to an independent India ([Britannica](https://WWW.Britannica.com/summary/Delhi); [Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/arts-and-culture/heritage/the-making-of-new-delhi/article23596392.ece/amp/)). The area's economic and population centre, however, has remained mainly in Old Delhi, and the services sector — including government — is the chief employer ([Britannica](https://WWW.Britannica.com/summary/Delhi)).

The Rajya Sabha has sat in Parliament House in New Delhi since the Council Chamber was built for it; the profile of the [2020 Deputy Chairman election](/events/2020-deputy-chairman-rajya-sabha-election/) covers a contest decided there. The newest layer of the capital is the Central Vista project, a redesign of the historic stretch between India Gate and the presidential palace ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/national/modi-unveils-new-parliament-reshaping-indias-power-centre/article66903477.ece/amp/)). Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated a new four-storey Parliament House on May 28, 2023, built at a cost of 9.7 billion rupees and expected to replace the century-old building where independent India's constitution was adopted ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/national/modi-unveils-new-parliament-reshaping-indias-power-centre/article66903477.ece/amp/)). The project has been controversial: critics say it erases part of a century of heritage and accuse Modi of using it to promote his party's Hindu nationalist ideology, and about a third of federal lawmakers — including the main opposition Congress — boycotted the ceremony, saying India's president should have opened the building ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/national/modi-unveils-new-parliament-reshaping-indias-power-centre/article66903477.ece/amp/)). The old Kingsway, renamed Rajpath after independence, was redeveloped and renamed Kartavya Path — Path of Duty — in 2022 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rashtrapati-Bhavan?oasmId=96422)).

New Delhi's other defining modern fact is its winter air. India's average PM2.5 concentration in 2025 was 48.9 µg/m³, nearly 9.8 times the WHO annual guideline of 5 µg/m³, and Delhi ranked among the most polluted major cities globally ([IQAir](https://www.iqair.com/kw/newsroom/india-air-quality-alert)). As the monsoon withdraws in October, temperature inversions trap pollutants near the surface, sustaining poor-to-severe air from October through February ([IQAir](https://www.iqair.com/kw/newsroom/india-air-quality-alert)). Stubble burning in Punjab and Haryana peaks between late October and mid-November, explaining 78% of Delhi's aerosol variance during that period — which means the capital's worst air is driven substantially by fires outside its borders ([IQAir](https://www.iqair.com/kw/newsroom/india-air-quality-alert)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is New Delhi the same as Delhi?**
No. Delhi generally means the National Capital Territory, about 1,483 sq km with historic, central, suburban and rural areas. New Delhi is one of its revenue districts and the name for the administrative core, much of it under the New Delhi Municipal Council, whose jurisdiction is just 42.7 sq km.

**Why did the British move India's capital to Delhi?**
Calcutta had become inhospitable: nationalist opposition was rising, and Curzon's partition of Bengal had triggered boycotts and assassinations. Delhi offered a central location, proximity to the Shimla summer capital, and the symbolism of the Delhi Sultanate and Mughal seats of power.

**Who designed New Delhi?**
Edwin Lutyens drew the master plan and designed the Viceroy's House, now Rashtrapati Bhavan; Herbert Baker designed the twin Secretariat buildings and the Council Chamber. Their feud over the slope to the palace left the "Bakerloo" — the palace hidden from the approach road until one crests the hill.

**What is the Central Vista project?**
It is the redesign of the historic stretch between India Gate and the presidential palace. Its centerpiece is the new Parliament House, inaugurated May 28, 2023, plus the redeveloped Kartavya Path boulevard, renamed in 2022. Critics call it heritage erasure; supporters call it a modern seat of government.

**Why is New Delhi's air so bad in winter?**
Post-monsoon temperature inversions trap pollutants near the ground from October through February. Stubble burning in Punjab and Haryana peaks in late October to mid-November and explains 78% of Delhi's aerosol variance then, with household emissions, vehicles, industry, dust and Diwali fireworks adding to the load.
