## Overview

The Gaza Strip is a narrow coastal enclave — about 25 miles long and 7 miles wide, holding roughly 2.3 million people in some 360 square kilometers, a density of about 5,500 people per square kilometer that Arab News compares to London's [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/middle-east/the-gaza-strip-tiny-cramped-and-as-densely-populated-as-london-2420961). What the density figures don't capture is what happened to its economy: after [Israel](/places/israel/) and Egypt sealed the territory in 2007, Gaza's trade went literally underground, through a network of smuggling tunnels into Egypt that briefly boomed, then was blown up. This profile starts from that under-reported arc — the economy that tunneled beneath the blockade — before the seventy years of changing rulers that produced it.

## An economy that went underground

Before 1967, Gaza's economy was small but legible: a 1967 Israeli census found 65% of working-age men employed inside the territory, mainly in agriculture, fishing, industry and quarries [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-israel-palestinians-gaza-explainer-idUSKCN1HD16O/?ref=radicalismoffools.com). After Israel captured the Strip, thousands of Gazans shifted to jobs in agriculture, construction and services inside Israel, while Israeli troops administered the territory and guarded the settlements Israel built there [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-israel-palestinians-gaza-explainer-idUSKCN1HD16O/?ref=radicalismoffools.com).

The Oslo Accords of 1993 promised Palestinians a path to economic independence, but two of its concrete instruments were dismantled piece by piece. Gaza International Airport, opened in 1998 as the Palestinians' only direct link to the outside world not controlled by Israel or Egypt, had its radar antenna and runway destroyed by Israel in late 2001 [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-israel-palestinians-gaza-explainer-idUSKCN1HD16O/?ref=radicalismoffools.com). And the fishing zone set by Oslo at 20 nautical miles — a livelihood for tens of thousands — was reduced by Israel to between 3 and 12 nautical miles; Israel said the restrictions were necessary to stop weapons smuggling by boat, while Palestinians accused Israel of reneging on Oslo [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-israel-palestinians-gaza-explainer-idUSKCN1HD16O/?ref=radicalismoffools.com).

The turning point came in August 2005, when the [Government of Israel](/organizations/government-of-israel/) evacuated all its troops and settlers from Gaza in the Disengagement [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-israel-palestinians-gaza-explainer-idUSKCN1HD16O/?ref=radicalismoffools.com). With the settlers gone, "the 'tunnel economy' boomed as armed groups, smugglers and entrepreneurs quickly dug scores of tunnels into Egypt," Reuters reported — even as the pullout removed settlement factories, greenhouses and workshops that had employed Gazans [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-israel-palestinians-gaza-explainer-idUSKCN1HD16O/?ref=radicalismoffools.com). Then, in June 2007, [Hamas](/organizations/hamas/) seized full control of Gaza, and Israel stopped tens of thousands of Palestinian workers from entering the country, closed an industrial zone on the border, and hit Gaza's only electrical power plant with air strikes [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-israel-palestinians-gaza-explainer-idUSKCN1HD16O/?ref=radicalismoffools.com). Arab News notes the blockade's stated aim was to restrict Hamas's military capabilities, "but it also constrained economic activity and impoverished the Palestinian population" [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/news/2007-hamas-takes-over-the-gaza-strip-2597224).

The tunnels were the last valve. In 2013, after Egypt's coup, President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi — viewing Hamas as a threat — closed the border with Gaza and blew up most of the tunnels; "once again isolated, Gaza's economy went into reverse," Reuters wrote [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-israel-palestinians-gaza-explainer-idUSKCN1HD16O/?ref=radicalismoffools.com). The trajectory had been visible years earlier: in 2015, UN Trade and Development warned that Gaza might be unlivable by 2020 [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/news/2007-hamas-takes-over-the-gaza-strip-2597224).

## Seventy years of changing rulers

Gaza sits on ancient Mediterranean trading routes and was held by the Ottoman Empire until 1917, then passed from British to Egyptian to Israeli military rule [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-israel-palestinians-gaza-explainer-idUSKCN1HD16O/?ref=radicalismoffools.com). In 1948, as British rule ended and war broke out between the new State of Israel and its Arab neighbors, tens of thousands of Palestinians fled or were driven from their homes into Gaza; the Egyptian army seized a narrow 40-kilometer coastal strip, and the influx tripled Gaza's population to around 200,000 [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-israel-palestinians-gaza-explainer-idUSKCN1HD16O/?ref=radicalismoffools.com). Egypt held the Strip for two decades under a military governor, and the UN created its refugee agency UNRWA, which today serves 1.3 million registered refugees in Gaza — around 70% of the population [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-israel-palestinians-gaza-explainer-idUSKCN1HD16O/?ref=radicalismoffools.com).

Israel captured Gaza in the 1967 Six-Day War; that year's Israeli census counted 394,000 people, at least 60% of them refugees [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-israel-palestinians-gaza-explainer-idUSKCN1HD16O/?ref=radicalismoffools.com). In December 1987, the First Intifada erupted after an Israeli truck crashed into a vehicle carrying Palestinian workers in the Jabalya refugee camp, killing four — and the Egypt-based Muslim Brotherhood seized the moment to create an armed Palestinian branch, Hamas, with its power base in Gaza [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-israel-palestinians-gaza-explainer-idUSKCN1HD16O/?ref=radicalismoffools.com). The 1993 Oslo Accords created the Palestinian Authority and gave Palestinians limited control in Gaza; Yasser Arafat returned after decades in exile [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-israel-palestinians-gaza-explainer-idUSKCN1HD16O/?ref=radicalismoffools.com).

The modern split came fast. Israel completed its withdrawal on September 22, 2005, leaving Gaza under the Palestinian Authority's control [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/news/2007-hamas-takes-over-the-gaza-strip-2597224). On January 25, 2006, Hamas defeated Fatah in parliamentary elections; on June 15, 2007, it seized Gaza in factional fighting that left 188 people dead and more than 650 wounded, splitting the Palestinian territories in two — the [West Bank](/places/west-bank/) under the PA, Gaza an isolated Hamas stronghold [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/news/2007-hamas-takes-over-the-gaza-strip-2597224). Within months, Israel imposed the land, sea and air blockade that remains in place [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/news/2007-hamas-takes-over-the-gaza-strip-2597224).

Wars punctuated the blockade years: a 22-day offensive in 2008–2009, fighting in 2012, and in 2014 Operation Protective Edge, which killed more than 2,100 Palestinians, mostly civilians, alongside 67 Israeli soldiers and six civilians [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-israel-palestinians-gaza-explainer-idUSKCN1HD16O/?ref=radicalismoffools.com). On October 7, 2023, Hamas attacked Israel, killing more than 1,200 people and taking about 250 hostage [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/news/2007-hamas-takes-over-the-gaza-strip-2597224). Israel's subsequent campaign, which Arab News reports killed more than 47,000 Palestinians and displaced about 90% of Gaza's population, reduced most of the territory to rubble; reconstruction is projected to cost between $80 billion and $200 billion [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/news/2007-hamas-takes-over-the-gaza-strip-2597224).

## The deadliest place to report

Gaza has also become the deadliest place on Earth to be a journalist. [Al Jazeera](/organizations/al-jazeera/), in a September 2026 statement, reported that more than 270 journalists have been killed in Gaza during the war — at least 12 of them reporting for Al Jazeera — and that 706 family members of Palestinian journalists were killed, per the Palestinian Journalists Syndicate [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/9/25/al-jazeera-rejects-netanyahus-attack-on-journalists-in-un-speech). The statement was issued after Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu singled the network out at the UN General Assembly as one of the world's "toxic media outlets" trying to "brainwash young minds to hate Israel" — language Al Jazeera said it "categorically rejects" as part of a sustained campaign against it and its journalists [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/9/25/al-jazeera-rejects-netanyahus-attack-on-journalists-in-un-speech). The network noted that Israel's cabinet had voted two years earlier to shut it down inside the country, closing its offices and banning its broadcasts under a Knesset law allowing the temporary closure of foreign outlets deemed a "threat to security" [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/9/25/al-jazeera-rejects-netanyahus-attack-on-journalists-in-un-speech).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not give a single current population figure — pre-war estimates cluster around 2–2.3 million, and post-war estimates conflict, so the range is stated. It does not resolve the territory's reported area (360, 365, or 375 square kilometers depending on the source). It does not predict who will govern Gaza or which reconstruction plan will prevail; those are unresolved at writing time. Casualty figures from the 2023–2025 war are given as reported by the cited sources and continue to change. Al Jazeera's characterization of the war and its journalist-toll figures are the network's own claims, reported here as attributed, not independently verified.
