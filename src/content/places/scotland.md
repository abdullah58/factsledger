## Overview

Scotland is the most northerly of the four parts of the [United Kingdom](/places/united-kingdom/), occupying about one-third of the island of Great Britain, with Edinburgh as its capital, Glasgow as its largest city, and a record population of 5,545,500 at mid-2025 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland); [National Records of Scotland](https://www.nrscotland.gov.uk/publications/mid-2025-population-estimates/)). Its name comes from the Latin *Scotia*, land of the Scots, a Celtic people from Ireland who settled on the west coast of Great Britain about the 5th century CE ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland)). It is a country that has refused, in Britannica's words, to consider itself as anything other than a separate country — merged with [England](/places/england/) in 1707 to form the United Kingdom of Great Britain, yet governed since 1999 by its own parliament ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland)).

Scotland's economy reached £224.8 billion in onshore GDP in 2025, or £40,537 per person, and £235.3 billion including oil and gas extraction in Scottish waters ([Scottish Government](https://www.gov.scot/publications/gdp-quarterly-national-accounts-2026-q2/pages/gdp-in-nominal-terms/)). Its population story runs in two directions at once: migration keeps the total at a record high, while deaths have exceeded births every year since 2015 ([National Records of Scotland](https://www.nrscotland.gov.uk/publications/mid-2025-population-estimates/)).

## Land and people

Scotland is bounded by England to the south, the Atlantic Ocean to the west and north, and the North Sea to the east ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland)). The west coast is fringed by deep indentations — sea lochs — and by numerous islands, including Lewis and Harris, Skye, and Mull, while Orkney and Shetland lie to the north ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland)). The mainland extends 441 kilometres at its greatest length, from Cape Wrath to the Mull of Galloway, and 248 kilometres at maximum breadth — yet, because of the deep penetration of the sea, most places are within 65 to 80 kilometres of the sea, and only 50 kilometres of land separate the Firth of Clyde and the Firth of Forth ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland)).

The population reached 5,545,500 on 30 June 2025, the highest it has ever been, rising by just 2,200 — under 0.1 percent — in the year, against an average annual increase of around 23,000 in the two decades to mid-2024 ([National Records of Scotland](https://www.nrscotland.gov.uk/publications/mid-2025-population-estimates/)). Migration did all the work: net international migration was +9,300 and net migration from the rest of the UK was +8,600, giving total net migration of +17,900 — well below the recent peak of +83,300 in the year to mid-2023 ([National Records of Scotland](https://www.nrscotland.gov.uk/publications/mid-2025-population-estimates/)). Beneath that total, the natural balance is negative: 45,400 births against 61,600 deaths in the year to mid-2025, a natural change of −16,200, and deaths have exceeded births in every year since the year ending mid-2015 ([National Records of Scotland](https://www.nrscotland.gov.uk/publications/mid-2025-population-estimates/)). The population is ageing — 21.0 percent were 65 and over at mid-2025, up from 16.3 percent in mid-2005, while 15.8 percent were aged 0 to 15, down from 18.2 percent ([National Records of Scotland](https://www.nrscotland.gov.uk/publications/mid-2025-population-estimates/)). Glasgow City is the largest council area at 654,330 people, followed by the City of Edinburgh at 531,370; the smallest are Orkney (21,920), Shetland (22,710), and Na h-Eileanan Siar (25,770), and the population grew in 12 of the 32 council areas and fell in 20 ([National Records of Scotland](https://www.nrscotland.gov.uk/publications/mid-2025-population-estimates/)).

## Union, devolution, and the 2014 referendum

Scotland's relations with England, with which it was merged in 1707 to form the United Kingdom of Great Britain, have long been difficult, and Scotland has long refused to consider itself as anything other than a separate country ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland)). That independent spirit bore fruit in 1996, when the highly symbolic Stone of Scone was returned to Edinburgh from [London](/places/london/), and in 1999 a new Scottish Parliament — the first since 1707 — was elected and given significant powers over Scottish affairs ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland)).

The division of powers is precise. The British Parliament retains foreign affairs, trade, defence, civil service, economic and monetary policy, social security, employment, energy regulation, taxation, and transport, while the Scottish Parliament has powers over health, education, housing, transport, the environment, and agriculture, and can adjust the British income tax rate in Scotland ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland)). Scotland is represented in the British Parliament by 59 members in the House of Commons, and its own parliament comprises 129 members chosen through single-member constituencies and proportional representation ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland)). Unlike England, Scotland has a distinct legal system rooted in Roman law ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland)). The First Minister is John Swinney ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland)).

The question of independence was put to a vote on 18 September 2014. To the question "Should Scotland be an independent country?", 55.4 per cent of voters voted No, choosing to stay in a union they had been part of since 1707, with turnout above 84 per cent and 16- and 17-year-olds voting for the first time ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/world/scots-spurn-independence-vote-to-stay-within-uk/article64244237.ece)). Glasgow and Dundee voted for independence, while nearly two-thirds of Edinburgh's voters chose to remain in the UK ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/world/scots-spurn-independence-vote-to-stay-within-uk/article64244237.ece)). The 1.6 million Yes votes were, in First Minister Alex Salmond's concession words, "a substantial vote for independence" — but he accepted the verdict: "I accept the verdict of the people and I call on all of Scotland to follow suit" ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/world/scots-spurn-independence-vote-to-stay-within-uk/article64244237.ece)).

## Economy and culture

Scotland's economy is anchored by the 2025 figures: onshore GDP of £224.8 billion in current prices, or £40,537 per person, up £10.9 billion (5.1 percent) on 2024 through real growth of 1.2 percent and a GDP deflator increase of 3.8 percent ([Scottish Government](https://www.gov.scot/publications/gdp-quarterly-national-accounts-2026-q2/pages/gdp-in-nominal-terms/)). Including oil and gas extraction in Scottish waters and other extra-regio activity, total GDP was £235.3 billion, or £42,431 per person ([Scottish Government](https://www.gov.scot/publications/gdp-quarterly-national-accounts-2026-q2/pages/gdp-in-nominal-terms/)). The stat that anchors the longer trend is the climb from £188.4 billion of onshore GDP in 2022 to £224.8 billion in 2025 — roughly 19 percent growth in nominal terms in three years ([Scottish Government](https://www.gov.scot/publications/gdp-quarterly-national-accounts-2026-q2/pages/gdp-in-nominal-terms/)).

Culturally, Scotland's contribution far exceeds its size. The Scottish Enlightenment, in the hands of philosophers such as Francis Hutcheson, Adam Smith, and David Hume, forged ideas of progress and the pursuit of happiness that contributed substantially to the development of modern democracy, while Scots inventors and entrepreneurs — Alexander Graham Bell, James Watt, Andrew Carnegie, John McAdam — extended the country's reach far beyond its borders ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland)). In literature, few students of English are unacquainted with historian Thomas Carlyle, poet Robert Burns, and novelist Muriel Spark, and the novelist Sir Walter Scott wrote: "I am a Scotsman; therefore I had to fight my way into the world" ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland)). The visible symbols are familiar: the kilt, a knee-length skirt-like garment with a tartan pattern, serves as the national dress; bagpipes are instantly recognisable; whisky — "water of life" in Gaelic — is a major export and a source of national pride; and haggis is the traditional dish ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland)). Edinburgh, the capital, is one of Europe's chief cultural centres and the seat of the Scottish Parliament; it hosts the annual Edinburgh International Festival each August, and in 2004 UNESCO named it its first City of Literature ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland)). Hogmanay, the New Year's festival, carries customs such as "first-footing" ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Scotland)).
