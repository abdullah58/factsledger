## Overview

Thomas Massie is the Kentucky Republican congressman who, on September 15, 2026, filed eight articles of impeachment against Secretary of Defense [Pete Hegseth](/people/pete-hegseth) — the most direct congressional challenge yet to the administration's war in Iran ([Reason](https://reason.com/2026/09/15/thomas-massie-just-filed-8-articles-of-impeachment-against-pete-hegseth/); [Reuters](http://www.jpost.com/american-politics/article-908729)). Because he filed it as a privileged resolution, House rules required the chamber to take it up within two legislative days; instead, Speaker [Mike Johnson](/people/mike-johnson) canceled the House's remaining votes the next day and sent lawmakers home until after the November 3 midterms, a move Massie denounced as a "Hegseth holiday" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/16/house-republicans-leave-congress-hegseth-impeachment/91790913007/)).

Massie is an unlikely impeacher by party label and a predictable one by record. An MIT-trained engineer who co-invented haptic interface technology and founded a company on 29 patents before entering politics ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/world/us/jack-dorsey-endorses-thomas-massie-who-is-the-kentucky-congressman/articleshow/124699648.cms)), he has spent his House career as a constitutional absolutist on war powers. The impeachment was not a sudden break. It was the endpoint of a campaign that began in June 2025, when he condemned Trump's Iran airstrikes as unconstitutional and introduced a War Powers Resolution alongside Democratic Rep. Ro Khanna ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/who-is-thomas-massie-republican-congressman-condemns-trump-s-iran-airstrikes-backs-war-powers-resolution-101750617849086.html)). He filed the articles as a lame duck — he had lost the May 2026 primary to Trump-backed former Navy SEAL Ed Gallrein after leading the push to force release of the Justice Department's Epstein files ([Reuters](http://www.jpost.com/american-politics/article-908729)).

## The engineer in the House

Massie was born in West Virginia and raised in Lewis County, Kentucky ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/world/us/jack-dorsey-endorses-thomas-massie-who-is-the-kentucky-congressman/articleshow/124699648.cms)). At MIT he earned a bachelor's in Electrical Engineering and a master's in Mechanical Engineering, where he developed haptic technology — interfaces that let users feel and manipulate virtual objects — that later found uses ranging from automotive design to reconstructive surgery for wounded soldiers ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/world/us/jack-dorsey-endorses-thomas-massie-who-is-the-kentucky-congressman/articleshow/124699648.cms); [Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/who-is-thomas-massie-republican-congressman-condemns-trump-s-iran-airstrikes-backs-war-powers-resolution-101750617849086.html)).

He commercialized the work as co-founder of SensAble Technologies, which raised more than $32 million in venture funding and created 70 jobs; Massie himself holds 29 patents ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/world/us/jack-dorsey-endorses-thomas-massie-who-is-the-kentucky-congressman/articleshow/124699648.cms)). He then returned to Kentucky, built an off-grid home on a cattle farm, and entered politics as Lewis County Judge Executive before winning a November 2012 special election for Kentucky's 4th congressional district ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/world/us/jack-dorsey-endorses-thomas-massie-who-is-the-kentucky-congressman/articleshow/124699648.cms)). In the House he sits on the Transportation & Infrastructure and Judiciary committees, and his profile outside Congress grew enough that Jack Dorsey publicly endorsed him for president — an endorsement Massie declined ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/world/us/jack-dorsey-endorses-thomas-massie-who-is-the-kentucky-congressman/articleshow/124699648.cms)).

## War-powers absolutist

The constitutional thread running through Massie's career snapped into focus in June 2025, when Trump ordered strikes on three Iranian nuclear sites without prior congressional approval. Massie appeared on CBS's *Face the Nation* alongside Khanna to condemn the strikes as unconstitutional, arguing that Congress had not been briefed and that there was no imminent threat to the United States that would have authorized the attack. The two lawmakers introduced a War Powers Resolution aimed at blocking U.S. involvement in the escalating Israel-Iran conflict ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/who-is-thomas-massie-republican-congressman-condemns-trump-s-iran-airstrikes-backs-war-powers-resolution-101750617849086.html)).

The war that followed — beginning with February 28 U.S./Israeli attacks on Iran ([Reuters](http://www.jpost.com/american-politics/article-908729)) — kept Massie in open conflict with his own party's president for more than a year. His position stayed constant while the war's costs mounted: fuel prices rising, inflation climbing, and a growing Republican rebellion — seven House Republicans eventually supported a symbolic measure to end the Iran war ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/16/house-republicans-leave-congress-hegseth-impeachment/91790913007/)).

## Eight articles of impeachment

On Tuesday, September 15, 2026, Massie put the constitutional argument into the sharpest form Congress offers: eight articles of impeachment against Hegseth, filed as a privileged resolution ([Reason](https://reason.com/2026/09/15/thomas-massie-just-filed-8-articles-of-impeachment-against-pete-hegseth/); [Reuters](http://www.jpost.com/american-politics/article-908729)). The articles fall into two groups. The first three charge violations of the War Powers Resolution of 1973: launching a war without an imminent threat, continuing it after Congress ordered it ended, and ignoring withdrawal deadlines. Two more articles address the strikes in Iran and Venezuela and the laws governing civilian casualties, tied to the February 28 strike on Shajareh Tayyebeh Elementary School, which the resolution text says killed or injured more than 200 civilians ([Reason](https://reason.com/2026/09/15/thomas-massie-just-filed-8-articles-of-impeachment-against-pete-hegseth/)).

One article targets the campaign of strikes on suspected drug traffickers in the Caribbean, which the resolution text says killed more than 200 people. Article 6 charges Hegseth with retaliation against Senator Mark Kelly — noting that a federal judge had already ruled Hegseth acted unlawfully in stripping Kelly's rank and pension. The final two articles cover the operation that captured Venezuelan leader Nicolás Maduro and the strikes in Yemen ([Reason](https://reason.com/2026/09/15/thomas-massie-just-filed-8-articles-of-impeachment-against-pete-hegseth/)). The casualty figures are Massie's, as stated in the resolution text, not independently established facts. The Pentagon rejected his framing: press secretary Kingsley Wilson said Hegseth "has done nothing wrong" and praised the Iran operation ([Reuters](http://www.jpost.com/american-politics/article-908729)).

## Why Hegseth and not Trump

In an interview the same day, Massie laid out the theory of the case — and answered the obvious question about why he aimed at the defense secretary rather than the president ([Responsible Statecraft](https://responsiblestatecraft.org/massie-impeachment-hegseth/)). "We have exhausted every other legislative remedy to make things right," he said, pointing to an ignored concurrent War Powers resolution, an ignored 60-day limit, and a degraded Pentagon capacity for distinguishing civilian targets. Six of the articles, he explained, cover illegal presidential orders that Hegseth was "under obligation not to follow" — but two categories belong to Hegseth alone: degrading the Defense Department's civilian-casualty-minimization infrastructure, and weaponizing the department to punish Senator Mark Kelly's speech.

On the president, Massie was blunt: "I just don't think it's politically feasible or viable or even advisable to try to impeach Trump." He added a detail only a legislator who reads the paperwork could offer — he had read every White House War Powers communication to Congress, and noticed that at least three of them admitted strike predicates, including an attack on a neutrally-flagged vessel, that do not qualify under the War Powers Resolution, which requires an attack on U.S. soil, soldiers, or infrastructure ([Responsible Statecraft](https://responsiblestatecraft.org/massie-impeachment-hegseth/)). On the administration's insistence that the conflict isn't a war at all, he said: "Somebody better give the president that memo. He calls it a war every week."

## The vote that never happened

The privileged status of the resolution should have forced a floor fight within two legislative days. Instead, on September 16, Johnson abruptly canceled the House's last day of work before a roughly eight-week recess, punting the vote until after the midterm elections ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/16/house-republicans-leave-congress-hegseth-impeachment/91790913007/)). Johnson insisted the scheduling was unrelated to the impeachment push, dismissed it as a "publicity stunt," and said of its fate: "It will immediately be tabled, of course. We will do it when we come back."

Massie erupted on the House floor, asking, "We're calling off Congress? And we're in the majority?" He told reporters the leadership was simply afraid of the debate: "They do not want to debate this." Democrats agreed — Rep. [Melanie Stansbury](/people/melanie-stansbury/) said, "They are afraid that the House of Representatives will vote to impeach Pete Hegseth. So instead, they're just going to send us home." Even some Republicans objected: Rep. Ralph Norman called canceling another day of votes "unacceptable," writing that "the Founders did not envision taking this much time off and still getting paid" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/16/house-republicans-leave-congress-hegseth-impeachment/91790913007/)).

The context for the panic was visible the night before the filing, when three Republicans — Nancy Mace, Zach Nunn, and Mariannette Miller-Meeks — flipped for the first time to vote to end the Iran war, bringing Republican support for that symbolic rebuke of Trump to seven ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/16/house-republicans-leave-congress-hegseth-impeachment/91790913007/)).

## Lame duck, Epstein files, and the road out

Massie's willingness to file the articles is inseparable from his political situation. He had angered Trump by leading the congressional push to release the Justice Department's Epstein-related files, and in May 2026 he lost the Republican primary to Ed Gallrein, a former Navy SEAL backed by Trump — making him a lame duck with nothing left to lose inside the party ([Reuters](http://www.jpost.com/american-politics/article-908729)). Even as the impeachment fight played out, he was collecting signatures for a second Epstein measure, the Epstein Files Transparency Act II, which would give victims standing to sue the Justice Department for allegedly not fully complying with Congress's first law ordering a public accounting of its Epstein documents and investigations ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/16/house-republicans-leave-congress-hegseth-impeachment/91790913007/)).

A Reuters/Ipsos poll at the time had Democrats leading Republicans 44–37 ahead of the November 3 midterms — the electoral backdrop against which Johnson chose to send the House home rather than hold an impeachment vote ([Reuters](http://www.jpost.com/american-politics/article-908729)).
