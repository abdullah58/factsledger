Andrew Witkoff is the young man whose name briefly stood on a Las Vegas Strip tower — and was then taken down. No standalone public profile of him exists; he appears in the record only through what his death set in motion. The documented facts are few and are stated here without embellishment.

## The name on the tower

In the summer of 2017, developer Steve Witkoff and partners bought the unfinished Fontainebleau Las Vegas high-rise from Carl Icahn for $600 million — Icahn had acquired it in 2010 for around $150 million ([Las Vegas Review-Journal](https://www.reviewjournal.com/business/casinos-gaming/how-many-people-have-owned-the-fontainebleau-project-2944181/amp/)). In early 2018, Witkoff and Marriott International announced the resort's new name, Drew Las Vegas, with Edition and JW Marriott brands planned for the property. The Review-Journal reported the fact plainly: Witkoff named it for his son Andrew, who died of an OxyContin overdose in 2011 at age 22 ([Las Vegas Review-Journal](https://www.reviewjournal.com/business/casinos-gaming/how-many-people-have-owned-the-fontainebleau-project-2944181/amp/)).

Witkoff gave the memorial its own terms in a 2019 interview: "Our development in Las Vegas is named after our son Andrew who died. So this is to us a legacy project and a passion project. It's going to allow us to do some wonderful and positive things for the community, for kids in distress. And we're creating what could be 13,000 jobs... It's a $3 billion dollar project, so we have to let our partners know that we are focused on it and it's going to get our total attention" ([GlobeSt](https://www.globest.com/2019/02/07/steve-witkoff-ny-if-you-can-make-it-here/?amp=1)).

The project did not survive the pandemic as the Drew. In January 2020 Witkoff said he was close to securing a roughly $2 billion construction loan; in March he suspended construction as Las Vegas shut down over the coronavirus outbreak, and contractors later filed tens of millions of dollars in liens over unpaid bills ([Las Vegas Review-Journal](https://www.reviewjournal.com/business/casinos-gaming/how-many-people-have-owned-the-fontainebleau-project-2944181/amp/)). In early 2021 the tower's original developer, Jeffrey Soffer, teamed with Koch Industries' real-estate arm to reacquire the property for $350 million through a deed in lieu of foreclosure. The new owners renamed it Fontainebleau Las Vegas in the fall of 2021, and it opened on December 13, 2023, under its original name — the Drew name gone from the building ([Las Vegas Review-Journal](https://www.reviewjournal.com/business/casinos-gaming/how-many-people-have-owned-the-fontainebleau-project-2944181/amp/)).

## The documented facts of a death

Andrew Witkoff died in 2011 at age 22 of an OxyContin overdose at the now-closed Sunset Plaza Drive sober-living facility in California ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/trending/us/steve-witkoff-says-he-feels-late-sons-presence-amid-hamas-hostages-return-what-happened-to-andrew-101760344555182.html)). His parents filed a wrongful-death lawsuit against the facility, alleging that its negligence allowed their son to obtain the drugs ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/trending/us/steve-witkoff-says-he-feels-late-sons-presence-amid-hamas-hostages-return-what-happened-to-andrew-101760344555182.html)). The Real Estate Board of New York marked his passing with a death notice published in the New York Times on August 17, 2011: "The governors, members and staff of the Real Estate Board of New York mourn the passing of Andrew Witkoff, son of former REBNY Governor Steven Witkoff. We express our heartfelt condolences to Steve and his wife Lauren, their sons Alex and Zachary and the entire Witkoff family" ([legacy.com](https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/nytimes/name/andrew-witkoff-obituary?id=26410306)). His mother is Lauren Jill Rappoport; his brothers are Zach and Alexander Witkoff ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/trending/us/steve-witkoff-says-he-feels-late-sons-presence-amid-hamas-hostages-return-what-happened-to-andrew-101760344555182.html)).

## How the loss surfaces publicly

After Andrew's death, Steve Witkoff became an advocate for addiction awareness and recovery efforts, and he addressed the issue in his speech at the 2024 Republican National Convention ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/trending/us/steve-witkoff-says-he-feels-late-sons-presence-amid-hamas-hostages-return-what-happened-to-andrew-101760344555182.html)).

A 2025 [Jerusalem Post](https://factsledger.com/organizations/jerusalem-post/) profile captured how the grief follows him into his diplomatic work. "I'm a member of a terrible club," Witkoff told hostage families, "parents who lost a child" — and he described visiting New Montefiore Cemetery to spend time with Andrew. When freed hostage Edan Alexander came home, Witkoff took the Magen David that had hung on Andrew's chest and placed it around the young man's neck ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/influencers-25/50jews-25/article-867968)). The same profile records the phone call after Andrew's death, when a hospital asked about organ donation: "I had never fainted before... Today, I would have donated. Back then, I did not" ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/influencers-25/50jews-25/article-867968)).
