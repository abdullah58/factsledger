## Overview

Nicolaus Copernicus (1473–1543) was a Polish astronomer who proposed that the Sun, not the Earth, stands at the centre of the solar system — that Earth is a planet orbiting the Sun once a year while turning daily on its own axis. His book *De revolutionibus orbium coelestium*, published in Nuremberg in 1543 just before his death, launched the Copernican Revolution and the Scientific Revolution ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nicolaus-Copernicus)). Born on 19 February 1473 in Toruń, in Royal Prussia in the Crown of the Kingdom of [Poland](/places/poland/), he spent most of his adult life as a canon of the Warmia cathedral chapter at Frombork, doing astronomy in his spare time while administering church estates, practising medicine and advising on money ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nicolaus-Copernicus)). This profile is about the astronomer himself — not the [Copernicus Foundation](/organizations/copernicus-foundation/), the Copernicus Center, or the Chicago monument, which are later commemorations named for him.

## The heliocentric model and De revolutionibus

Copernicus was troubled by the geocentric model of Ptolemy, which kept a motionless Earth at the centre of the universe yet could not satisfy Aristotle's demand that heavenly bodies move in uniform circles. The [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2015/entries/copernicus/) records that he set out to eliminate Ptolemy's equant, an imaginary point used to patch planetary motion, and concluded that only a Sun-centred model could do it. He probably adopted heliocentrism between 1508 and 1514 and sketched it in the *Commentariolus*, a short manuscript circulated among friends but never printed ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2015/entries/copernicus/)). [NASA](/organizations/nasa/) notes that Aristotle's stationary-Earth view had dominated for nearly 1,000 years before Copernicus revived the moving Earth ([NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planetary-motion/)). His full account, *De revolutionibus orbium coelestium*, was printed at Nuremberg in 1543 by Johannes Petreius, after his pupil Georg Joachim Rheticus tested the waters with the *Narratio prima* in 1540 ([Biographical Encyclopedia of Astronomers](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/BEA/copernicus_bea.pdf)).

## Canon, administrator and economist

Copernicus's day job was the church. His uncle Lucas Watzenrode, Bishop of Warmia, became his guardian after his father's death and steered him into a canonry at Frombork, where he was appointed in 1497 and installed in 1501 ([MacTutor](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Copernicus/)). As a canon he collected rents, secured defences, oversaw chapter finances, ran its bakery, brewery and mills, and treated the medical needs of fellow canons — astronomy was his spare-time pursuit ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nicolaus-Copernicus)). He was never ordained a priest, despite a lifetime as a churchman ([Biographical Encyclopedia of Astronomers](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/BEA/copernicus_bea.pdf)). He administered the chapter's estates at Olsztyn in 1516–1519 and 1520–1521, and in January–February 1521 organised the successful defence of Olsztyn against the Teutonic Knights ([MacTutor](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Copernicus/)). At King Sigismund I's request he wrote on coinage, finishing a paper in 1517 stating that debased coin drives good coin out of circulation and prices vary with the money supply — the ideas later called Gresham's law and the quantity theory of money ([Mises Institute](https://mises.org/mises-daily/copernicus-and-quantity-theory-money)).

## Ban, vindication and commemoration

Copernicus's book was not attacked at first, partly because Andreas Osiander, who supervised the printing, added an unsigned preface presenting the model as a mere calculating hypothesis ([Biographical Encyclopedia of Astronomers](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/BEA/copernicus_bea.pdf)). That changed in February–March 1616, when the Catholic Church prohibited the theory of the Earth's motion and the Index suspended *De revolutionibus* until corrected; it stayed on the Index until 1835 ([Ohio State University](https://origins.osu.edu/milestones/february-2016-400-years-ago-catholic-church-prohibited-copernicanism?language_content_entity=en)). Galileo was tried by the Roman Inquisition in 1633 and kept under house arrest for life, while Giordano Bruno was burned at the stake for teaching heliocentrism among other heresies ([NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planetary-motion/)). Yet the evidence mounted: Galileo's observation of the phases of Venus proved Venus orbits the Sun, Kepler's laws described planetary motion, and Newton explained it as gravity in 1687 ([NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planetary-motion/)). In 2005 archaeologists found remains under Frombork Cathedral that DNA matched to hairs in a book Copernicus owned, and he was reburied there on 22 May 2010 under a black granite slab showing the Sun ringed by six planets ([Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2010-05-copernicus-reburied-cathedral.html)).

*De revolutionibus* used only 27 of Copernicus's own observations against 45 taken from Ptolemy's *Almagest* ([Biographical Encyclopedia of Astronomers](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/BEA/copernicus_bea.pdf)), which implies his revolution was won less by new observing than by reinterpreting centuries of existing data. The [Chicago](/places/chicago/) commemoration shows the same staying power in civic life: the Copernicus Foundation raised $150,000 for a replica of Bertel Thorvaldsen's [Warsaw](/places/warsaw/) monument — the original designed in 1822 and executed by 1830 — recast by Bronisław Koniuszy, and it was dedicated in front of the [Adler Planetarium](/organizations/adler-planetarium/) on 14 October 1973, when Mayor [Richard J. Daley](/people/richard-j-daley/) proclaimed Nicolas Copernicus Day ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)). The 100,000-pound bronze statue was shipped from Poland aboard the *Zawiercie*, the first ship to sail regularly between Chicago and the Polish port of Gdynia ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)).

## FAQ

### Who was Nicolaus Copernicus?

Nicolaus Copernicus (1473–1543) was a Polish astronomer and canon of the Warmia cathedral chapter who proposed that the Sun, not the Earth, is the centre of the solar system. His book *De revolutionibus*, published in 1543 just before his death, launched the Copernican Revolution and the Scientific Revolution.

### What is the heliocentric model?

The heliocentric model places the Sun at the centre, with Earth as one planet orbiting it once a year while rotating daily on its axis. It replaced Ptolemy's geocentric model, which held Earth motionless at the centre, and it explained planetary motion more simply and accurately.

### Did Copernicus publish his theory during his lifetime?

Yes, but only just, after Rheticus had persuaded him to publish. The printing was completed in April 1543, and according to a letter from his friend Tiedemann Giese, Copernicus received the final printed pages on the very day he died, 24 May 1543.

### Was Copernicus punished by the Church?

No — he died before any condemnation. The Catholic Church prohibited the theory of the Earth's motion only in 1616, suspending his book until corrected, and it remained on the Index of Prohibited Books until 1835. Galileo, who later defended the theory, was tried in 1633.

### Why is there a Copernicus monument in Chicago?

Chicago's Polish community raised funds through the Copernicus Foundation for a replica of Bertel Thorvaldsen's Warsaw monument. Dedicated on 14 October 1973 outside the Adler Planetarium, it marked the 500th anniversary of Copernicus's birth and honours him as a symbol of Polish pride.
