## Overview

For all the theories about Jeffrey Epstein's death, little has been heard from him in his own words about his final summer — until June 2026, when the New York Times successfully petitioned a federal judge to unseal his jail writings and published them ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/06/16/magazine/jeffrey-epstein-jail-writing.html)). In legal-pad pages filled with chaotic scrawls, Epstein denied he had ever been a hedge fund manager, rejecting the prosecutors' description of him and his work; complained that the case against him was "NOT NEW" — charges resurrected from years earlier, with the government feeling "pressure" to make an "end run" around his decade-old sentencing agreement ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/06/16/magazine/jeffrey-epstein-jail-writing.html)).

He also weighed an escape route: sharing information about President Trump, then serving his first term. But he could not come up with much, scribbling that Trump — with whom he had been close friends from the late 1980s through the early 2000s before a falling out — was a "con artist," "smoke & mirrors," that Trump Organization CFO Allen Weisselberg "knows all," and that the public should see Trump's college transcripts because the president was "not a stable genius," as he had claimed ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/06/16/magazine/jeffrey-epstein-jail-writing.html)). On July 22 he wrote a *cri de coeur* titled "J'Accuse," casting himself as a victim of the #MeToo movement, noting his alleged crimes were from 2002 to 2005 and calling them patronizing "prostitutes" — he had pleaded guilty in Florida to procuring a minor for prostitution; "Jewish — Rich — Politics," he wrote, apparently comparing himself to Alfred Dreyfus, adding "Believe the victim = Believe the Accuser" and "CRAZY!" ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/06/16/magazine/jeffrey-epstein-jail-writing.html)).

Hours later he was found on the floor of his cell with an orange noose around his neck, in an apparent suicide attempt weeks before his death ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/06/16/magazine/jeffrey-epstein-jail-writing.html)). His longer despair runs through the pages too: the future would mean "ONLY PAIN" to himself and others; "Why should people I Lov suffer for my problem"; "Best for all" ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/06/16/magazine/jeffrey-epstein-jail-writing.html)). And the note unsealed this spring, found after what seemed to be an earlier attempt to end his life, reads: "It is a treat to be able to choose one's time to say goodbye" ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/06/16/magazine/jeffrey-epstein-jail-writing.html)).

That voice — resentful, scheming, despairing — is the thread this profile follows, because it is the material no other biography of him covers. It is set against the documented record: the career, the 2008 deal, the 2019 case, and the extraordinary afterlife of that case, which in 2025–2026 produced one of the largest document releases in American history.

## The career he himself disputed

Jeffrey Edward Epstein was born January 20, 1953, in Brooklyn, New York ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)). He studied mathematics and physics at university and briefly taught at Manhattan's Dalton School before entering finance, working at Bear Stearns and later founding his own investment firm ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)). He managed money for billionaires, including retail magnate Leslie Wexner, a relationship that helped him acquire luxury properties and private islands ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)).

The details of the fortune remained partly mysterious even after his death, but court filings and investigative reporting filled in the outline: his primary business was financial advisory — estate, tax, and investment services for the ultra-wealthy — and his two largest patrons were Wexner, founder of Victoria's Secret, and private-equity billionaire [Leon Black](https://factsledger.com/people/leon-black/), who together accounted for the bulk of his fees, over USD 490 million from 1999 to 2018 ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)). He also benefited from tax breaks in the U.S. Virgin Islands that saved him an estimated USD 300 million between 1999 and 2018, and investigators traced more than 4,700 transactions totaling USD 1.9 billion across multiple banks ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)). He used Wexner's Manhattan townhouse and jets to consolidate his wealth and status; after falling out with Wexner in 2007, he rebuilt his fortune through Black, earning tens of millions for tax, estate, and philanthropic advice ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)).

The [Associated Press](https://factsledger.com/organizations/associated-press/)'s 2026 review of internal [Justice Department](https://factsledger.com/organizations/us-department-of-justice/) records adds the closing ledger on those two relationships: Epstein had managed Wexner's finances, but the couple's lawyers told investigators they cut him off in 2007 after learning he had stolen from them, and prosecutors informed Wexner he was "neither a coconspirator nor target in any respect" ([Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/jeffrey-epstein-client-list-sex-trafficking-049c96080a2ca2c12c84ac506437e50b)). Black's lawyer said he had paid Epstein for estate planning and tax advice and that Black had no awareness of Epstein's criminal activities; lawsuits by two women accusing Black of sexual misconduct were dismissed or withdrawn, with one still pending ([Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/jeffrey-epstein-client-list-sex-trafficking-049c96080a2ca2c12c84ac506437e50b)).

The islands themselves were part of the estate's geography: Little St. James (28 hectares), purchased in 1998 for USD 7.95 million through a company he solely controlled, and Great St. James (67 hectares), purchased in 2016 for USD 22.5 million — locals called them "Epstein Island" and worse ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)). FBI searches of the properties recovered hundreds of gigabytes of data and physical evidence ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)).

## The 2008 deal: "the deal of the century"

The criminal record begins in March 2005, when Palm Beach police investigated Epstein after a 14-year-old Royal Palm Beach High School student reported she was molested at his mansion, paying her USD 300 for what she described as a sexualized massage ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)). Police would identify at least 35 girls with similar stories: Epstein paying high-school-age students $200 or $300 to give him sexualized massages ([Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/jeffrey-epstein-client-list-sex-trafficking-049c96080a2ca2c12c84ac506437e50b)).

After the FBI joined the probe, federal prosecutors drafted indictments against Epstein and some of the personal assistants who had arranged the girls' visits and payments ([Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/jeffrey-epstein-client-list-sex-trafficking-049c96080a2ca2c12c84ac506437e50b)). Instead, then-Miami U.S. attorney Alexander Acosta struck a deal letting Epstein plead guilty to state charges of soliciting prostitution from an underage girl; the non-prosecution agreement, signed September 24, 2007, required Epstein to register as a sex offender and shielded four named co-conspirators along with any potential others ([Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/jeffrey-epstein-client-list-sex-trafficking-049c96080a2ca2c12c84ac506437e50b); [Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)).

On June 30, 2008, Epstein pleaded guilty to two felonies — solicitation of prostitution and solicitation of a minor for prostitution — and was sentenced to 18 months in jail ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)). He served 13 months in a private wing of the Palm Beach County Jail, released on work leave six days a week, twelve hours a day; one woman later said she was flown in for sex with him at his foundation offices, formed months before his plea ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)). He was free by mid-2009 ([Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/jeffrey-epstein-client-list-sex-trafficking-049c96080a2ca2c12c84ac506437e50b)), and on July 22, 2009, he was released to one year of house arrest — allowed to travel anywhere, including his private island, provided he returned within 24 hours ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)).

The deal did not stay buried. On November 28, 2018, the Miami Herald published its three-part "Perversion of Justice" series: reporter Julie K. Brown tracked down more than 60 women who said they were abused, identified 80 victims, and exposed the non-prosecution agreement as the "deal of a lifetime" ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/); [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/jeffrey-epstein-client-list-sex-trafficking-049c96080a2ca2c12c84ac506437e50b)). A federal judge later ruled that prosecutors had violated victims' rights by concealing the agreement from them ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)). On December 6, 2018, FBI agents and the U.S. Attorney's Office in Manhattan opened a new investigation into Epstein ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)).

## The 2019 case and his death

On July 6, 2019, Epstein was arrested on new sex-trafficking charges brought by New York prosecutors, who determined they were not bound by the 2007 non-prosecution agreement; Labor Secretary Alexander Acosta resigned days later ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)). Two days after his arrest he was charged with sex trafficking and conspiracy; denied bail, he was held at the Metropolitan Correctional Center in Manhattan ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)). One month after the arrest, he killed himself in his jail cell ([Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/jeffrey-epstein-client-list-sex-trafficking-049c96080a2ca2c12c84ac506437e50b)).

The Times's jail writings fill the weeks between. His 13th day in jail brought the bail denial on July 18 ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/06/16/magazine/jeffrey-epstein-jail-writing.html)). In his notes he drew up battle plans for fighting the case, brainstormed about information on powerful people that might give him an edge in bargaining, and bemoaned his conditions: identified as a "Billionaire Pedophile in jail," he complained of "no sleep, no air, screams," wrote that guards played loud music all night while inmates banged on doors, that the guards told his cellmate they would not file a report if he was beaten, and that he had been "Told to get Depends instead of being able to use the toilet" ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2026/06/16/magazine/jeffrey-epstein-jail-writing.html)).

The criminal network around him outlived him. A year after his death, prosecutors charged his longtime confidant Ghislaine Maxwell, saying she had recruited several of his victims and sometimes joined the sexual abuse ([Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/jeffrey-epstein-client-list-sex-trafficking-049c96080a2ca2c12c84ac506437e50b)). On July 2, 2020, federal prosecutors in New York charged Maxwell with sex crimes; a jury convicted her on December 30, 2021, and on June 28, 2022, she was sentenced to 20 years in prison ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)). One of Epstein's most prominent accusers, Virginia Roberts Giuffre, filed a lawsuit in 2009 claiming Epstein and Maxwell had arranged for her to have sexual encounters with prominent men, and her 2011 and 2014 accounts created a crisis for Britain's royal family ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)); she died by suicide on April 25, 2025, and her posthumous memoir was published on October 21, 2025 ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)). In a related strand, the French modeling agent Jean-Luc Brunel — accused in a separate case of sexually assaulting women in Europe — killed himself in jail while awaiting trial on a rape charge in France ([Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/jeffrey-epstein-client-list-sex-trafficking-049c96080a2ca2c12c84ac506437e50b)). And on October 30, 2025, King Charles III stripped Prince Andrew of his remaining titles and evicted him from his royal residence ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)).

## The files era: 2025–2026

What happened next has no parallel in the commodity biographies. In February 2025, Attorney General Pam Bondi told Fox News that Epstein's never-before-seen "client list" was "sitting on my desk right now," and the Justice Department distributed "declassified" binders to influencers — though much of the material was already public ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/); [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/jeffrey-epstein-client-list-sex-trafficking-049c96080a2ca2c12c84ac506437e50b)). On July 7, 2025, the department reversed course: it stated Epstein had not maintained a "client list" and that it would release no further files related to his sex-trafficking investigation ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)). Internal FBI records show why: on February 19, 2025, a supervisory special agent had written that investigators did not locate any such list during the investigation ([Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/jeffrey-epstein-client-list-sex-trafficking-049c96080a2ca2c12c84ac506437e50b)).

Congress answered with legislation. On July 15, 2025, Representatives Ro Khanna and Thomas Massie introduced the Epstein Files Transparency Act, requiring the Justice Department to make its investigative files public ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)). Along the way, Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche interviewed Maxwell on July 24 and 25, 2025; she denied wrongdoing and said she had never witnessed Trump involved in sexually inappropriate activity ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)). On November 12, 2025, a House committee released Epstein's email correspondence — including a 2019 email in which Epstein wrote that Trump "knew about the girls," without elaboration ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)).

Congress passed the act on November 18, 2025, and President Trump signed it the next day ([Nepal News](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/explainers/everything-about-jeffrey-epstein-key-facts-and-timeline-you-should-know/)). The Senate approved it by unanimous consent, and the House passed it near-unanimously, with Representative Clay Higgins casting the lone dissenting vote on the grounds that the release would "absolutely result in innocent people being hurt" ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/trump-approves-release-of-epstein-files-signs-bill-after-months-of-pressure-101763602025747.html)).
