## Overview

Max Levchin is a Ukrainian-born American entrepreneur and computer scientist, the co-founder and former chief technology officer of [PayPal](/organizations/paypal/), and the founder and chief executive of [Affirm](/organizations/affirm/), the buy-now-pay-later lender. Born in Kyiv in 1975, he arrived in the [United States](/places/united-states/) as a refugee in 1991, studied computer science at the University of Illinois, and built the anti-fraud systems that kept PayPal alive before its $1.5 billion sale to eBay in 2002 ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin)). He later founded Slide, sold to Google for $182 million in 2010, and in 2012 founded Affirm, which went public on Nasdaq in January 2021 ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin); [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1820953/000110465921004835/a20-26663_248k.htm)). He also participated in [Brex](/organizations/brex/)'s $50 million Series B in 2018 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/05/29/less-than-1-year-after-launching-its-corporate-card-for-startups-brex-eyes-2b-valuation/)).

## From Kyiv to Chicago

Levchin was born in Kyiv, then part of the Ukrainian SSR, to a family of physicists, and as a child he struggled with life-threatening respiratory disease; his mother urged him to play the clarinet to build lung capacity ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin)). He was in Kyiv at the time of the 1986 Chernobyl disaster, and his parents sent him and his younger brother by train to Crimea, where they were tested with a Geiger counter on arrival ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin)). In 1991 the family left the Soviet Union under political asylum and settled in Chicago, arriving with $700 in their pockets; by the time they reached the United States, the Soviet Union had collapsed ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin)). Levchin later described the move plainly: "We came as political and religious refugees. It was quite challenging to be Jewish in the Soviet Union" ([The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/business/companies-markets/article/affirms-max-levchin-i-want-to-be-the-money-lender-with-morals-x678ql5qr)).

He enrolled at the University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign in 1993, majoring in computer science, and was already founding companies as a student — NetMeridian Software and SponsorNet New Media, with fellow students Luke Nosek and Scott Banister — before graduating with a B.S. in 1997 ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin)). He stayed connected to the university, creating a computer science scholarship in honour of his grandmother and returning as keynote speaker for its 147th commencement ceremony in May 2018 ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin)).

## PayPal: fraud, CAPTCHA, and a $1.5 billion exit

In 1998 Levchin and [Peter Thiel](/people/peter-thiel/) co-founded Fieldlink, a security company that let users store encrypted data on Palm devices as "digital wallets"; the company was renamed Confinity the same year ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin)). In 2000 Confinity merged with Elon Musk's X.com, and the combined company took the PayPal name and went public in 2002; eBay acquired it the same year for $1.5 billion, with Levchin's 2.3% stake worth about $34 million ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin)). The Times puts PayPal's flotation value in 2002 at $800 million ([The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/business/companies-markets/article/affirms-max-levchin-i-want-to-be-the-money-lender-with-morals-x678ql5qr)).

Levchin's main contribution at PayPal was anti-fraud work: he created the Gausebeck-Levchin test, one of the first commercial implementations of a CAPTCHA, at a time when fraud threatened to sink the young payments business ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin)). In 2002 he was named to MIT Technology Review's TR100 as one of the top 100 innovators in the world under 35, and named Innovator of the Year ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin)). The fraud expertise would become the through-line of his career: years later he described the connection directly, saying "fraud and credit are kind of two sides of the same coin: people who are lying to you, and people who are lying to themselves" ([The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/business/companies-markets/article/affirms-max-levchin-i-want-to-be-the-money-lender-with-morals-x678ql5qr)).

## Slide, Yelp, and the investor years

In 2004 Levchin founded Slide, a personal media-sharing service for MySpace and Facebook, putting $1 million of his own money into the launch; Google bought the company in 2010 for $182 million, and Levchin stayed on for a year as vice president of engineering before Google closed Slide and he left in 2011 ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin)). That same year, 2004, he met fellow Illinois graduates Jeremy Stoppelman and Russell Simmons to launch Yelp, writing a $1 million cheque at a lunch — an investment he later called "the single best investment I made in my entire life, so far" ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin)). He went on to chair Yelp's board from its founding in 2005 until July 2015, and served on Yahoo's board from 2012 to 2015 ([Affirm](https://investors.affirm.com/corporate-governance/management/)).

After leaving Google, Levchin launched HVF, an innovation lab whose name stands for Hard, Valuable, Fun, to explore and fund data-driven projects; he is also founder and general partner of the venture firm SciFi VC, and an active investor in more than 100 startups ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin); [Affirm](https://investors.affirm.com/corporate-governance/management/)). Becoming a father inspired his next venture outside finance: Glow, a data-driven fertility app launched in 2013, which he co-founded and chairs ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin); [Affirm](https://investors.affirm.com/corporate-governance/management/)).

## Affirm: the "money lender with morals"

Affirm grew out of HVF in 2012, founded by Levchin with the goal of building a next-generation credit network; his co-founders on the project included Palantir co-founder Nathan Gettings and Jeff Kaditz of First Data ([Coca-Cola](https://www.coca-colacompany.com/about-us/board-of-directors/max-levchin); [FinTech Magazine](https://fintechmagazine.com/financial-services-finserv/fintech-trailblazer-max-levchin-ceo-and-founder-of-affirm)). The idea traces back to a personal humiliation: in 2002, fresh from PayPal's IPO, Levchin tried to trade his Honda for a Mercedes-Benz convertible and was refused credit because his score was junk after he had "burnt through his credit card at university" ([The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/business/companies-markets/article/affirms-max-levchin-i-want-to-be-the-money-lender-with-morals-x678ql5qr)). He wired the money, bought the car "to impress a girl", and, as he puts it, "married the girl" — but the experience stuck, and ten years later he co-founded Affirm, "a company dedicated to a fairer approach to credit" ([The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/business/companies-markets/article/affirms-max-levchin-i-want-to-be-the-money-lender-with-morals-x678ql5qr)).

Affirm lends in instalments of 3 to 48 months, charges no late-payment fees, and discloses the full cost of borrowing as a single number upfront; merchants pay Affirm a fee for arranging the credit, and underwriting runs on a "soft" credit check that does not affect the applicant's score at first ([The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/business/companies-markets/article/affirms-max-levchin-i-want-to-be-the-money-lender-with-morals-x678ql5qr)). Levchin is blunt about the positioning: "We have a very strong point of view on what isn't a moral way of lending money" ([The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/business/companies-markets/article/affirms-max-levchin-i-want-to-be-the-money-lender-with-morals-x678ql5qr)). The model scaled: Affirm went public on Nasdaq in January 2021, pricing at $49 per share and closing the offering on January 15 with $1.31 billion in proceeds ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1820953/000110465921004835/a20-26663_248k.htm)). By 2026 the company reported $282 million in global profit on $3.7 billion of turnover with 25.8 million active customers, and in October 2026 it signed an exclusive multi-year deal to be the pay-over-time provider for Sam's Club ([The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/business/companies-markets/article/affirms-max-levchin-i-want-to-be-the-money-lender-with-morals-x678ql5qr); [San Francisco Inquirer](https://sfinquirer.com/2026/10/28/max-levchin-affirm-sams-club-pay-over-time/)).

As an investor, Levchin backed Brex's $50 million Series B in April 2018, a round led by [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/) Continuity that brought Brex's total funding to $57 million; the release named PayPal founders Thiel and Levchin among the participants ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/05/29/less-than-1-year-after-launching-its-corporate-card-for-startups-brex-eyes-2b-valuation/); [Brex](https://s3-eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/vox.assets.public/pdfs/a28f0836-a77c-4831-aa70-63e1f1ce2fed.pdf)). Beyond investing, he was appointed to the U.S. Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's Consumer Advisory Board in 2015 — the first Silicon Valley executive to be appointed — and in the same year created the Levchin Prize, which recognises significant contributions to real-world cryptography, with winners so far including the key creators of the AES block cipher, the Signal encryption protocol and SSL ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin)). On October 16, 2025 he was elected a director of The Coca-Cola Company ([Coca-Cola](https://www.coca-colacompany.com/about-us/board-of-directors/max-levchin)). Forbes's real-time estimates put his net worth at about $2.1 billion as of October 28, 2026, attributing most of it to an Affirm stake of more than 8%, and list his home as [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) ([San Francisco Inquirer](https://sfinquirer.com/2026/10/28/max-levchin-affirm-sams-club-pay-over-time/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Max Levchin best known for?**
Co-founding PayPal, where as chief technology officer he built the anti-fraud systems and co-created an early commercial CAPTCHA, and founding Affirm, the buy-now-pay-later lender he still runs as chairman and CEO. He also founded Slide, sold to Google in 2010, and co-founded the fertility app Glow ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin)).

**How did a bad credit score lead to Affirm?**
In 2002, after PayPal's IPO, a car dealer refused Levchin credit for a Mercedes convertible because his university credit-card history had wrecked his score; he paid cash instead, and ten years later co-founded Affirm to offer transparent credit with no late fees ([The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/business/companies-markets/article/affirms-max-levchin-i-want-to-be-the-money-lender-with-morals-x678ql5qr)).

**Did Max Levchin invest in Brex?**
Yes. He participated in Brex's $50 million Series B in April 2018, alongside Peter Thiel, Y Combinator Continuity, Ribbit Capital and others; the round brought Brex's total funding to $57 million. TechCrunch lists the round at a $220 million valuation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/05/29/less-than-1-year-after-launching-its-corporate-card-for-startups-brex-eyes-2b-valuation/); [Brex](https://s3-eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/vox.assets.public/pdfs/a28f0836-a77c-4831-aa70-63e1f1ce2fed.pdf)).

**What is the Levchin Prize?**
A prize Levchin created in 2015 recognising significant contributions to real-world cryptography, celebrating work that protects people in practice rather than theory alone; its winners so far include the key creators of the AES block cipher, the Signal encryption protocol and SSL ([University of Illinois](https://grainger.illinois.edu/news/features/making-of-max-levchin)).

**How much is Max Levchin worth?**
About $2.1 billion, according to Forbes's real-time estimates as of October 28, 2026, mostly from an Affirm stake of more than 8%; Forbes lists his home as San Francisco. The figure moves with Affirm's share price and is a single-date snapshot ([San Francisco Inquirer](https://sfinquirer.com/2026/10/28/max-levchin-affirm-sams-club-pay-over-time/)).
