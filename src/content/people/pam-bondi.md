## From Tampa prosecutor to the top of the Justice Department

Pamela Jo Bondi was born on November 17, 1965, in Tampa, Florida ([[U.S. Department of Justice](/organizations/us-department-of-justice/)](https://www.justice.gov/ag/bio/attorney-general-pamela-bondi)). She received a bachelor's degree from the University of Florida in 1987 and a law degree from the Stetson University College of Law in 1990, and was admitted to the Florida bar in 1991 ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/ag/bio/attorney-general-pamela-bondi)). From 1991 to 2000, she served as Assistant State Attorney within the Office of the State Attorney for the Thirteenth Judicial Circuit ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/ag/bio/attorney-general-pamela-bondi)). A biographical account of her time in office says she spent more than 18 years as a prosecutor, trying cases ranging from domestic violence to capital murder ([SenGov](https://sengov.com/whitehouse/pam-bondi/)).

In 2010, Bondi was elected Attorney General of Florida and served two terms from 2011 to 2019, becoming the first woman to serve in the position ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/ag/bio/attorney-general-pamela-bondi)). When she took office, Florida was known as the pill mill capital of the United States: of the top 100 oxycodone dispensers in the country, 98 were in Florida ([SenGov](https://sengov.com/whitehouse/pam-bondi/)). In her first legislative session, she pushed through legislation that shut down all 98 of those clinics and doctors ([SenGov](https://sengov.com/whitehouse/pam-bondi/)). She was subsequently appointed co-chair of the Substance Abuse Committee for the National Association of Attorneys General and served on President Trump's Opioid and Drug Abuse Commission ([SenGov](https://sengov.com/whitehouse/pam-bondi/)). On human trafficking, she led the push that created the Florida Statewide Human Trafficking Council, which she chaired until the end of her tenure ([SenGov](https://sengov.com/whitehouse/pam-bondi/)).

In 2019, Bondi joined the White House as Special Advisor to President Donald J. Trump in the Office of White House Counsel, working on the defense legal team through the impeachment proceedings ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/ag/bio/attorney-general-pamela-bondi)). In 2020 she was appointed to the Board of Trustees at the Trump Kennedy Center ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/ag/bio/attorney-general-pamela-bondi)). After her White House tenure she moved into private law and policy advocacy, including serving as the Chair of the Center of Litigation, Co-Chair of the Center for Law and Justice, and Chair of the Florida Chapter at the America First Policy Institute ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/ag/bio/attorney-general-pamela-bondi)).

## Confirmation and a 14-month tenure

Bondi was Trump's second pick for the top U.S. law enforcement job: his first choice, former Representative Matt Gaetz, withdrew from consideration ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/us-senate-confirms-trumps-nominee-pam-bondi-attorney-general-2025-02-05/)). The Republican-led Senate confirmed her on February 4, 2025, by a 54–46 vote, with Democratic Senator John Fetterman joining all 53 Republicans ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/us-senate-confirms-trumps-nominee-pam-bondi-attorney-general-2025-02-05/)). She had represented Trump during his first impeachment trial and had echoed some of his claims about voter fraud in the 2020 presidential election ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/us-senate-confirms-trumps-nominee-pam-bondi-attorney-general-2025-02-05/)). She was sworn in as the 87th Attorney General of the United States on February 5, 2025 ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/ag/bio/attorney-general-pamela-bondi)).

Her tenure ended abruptly. Bondi was fired on April 2, 2026, amid mounting criticism over her handling of the files tied to the Jeffrey Epstein investigation ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/29/ex-us-attorney-general-pam-bondi-testifies-in-epstein-files-probe)). The Epstein Files Transparency Act, a law passed in November mandating the release of all Justice Department material related to Epstein within 30 days, became the focal point of the criticism ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/29/ex-us-attorney-general-pam-bondi-testifies-in-epstein-files-probe)). Her deputy, [Todd Blanche](/people/todd-blanche/), became acting attorney general — making Bondi both Blanche's predecessor as attorney general and, during his deputy tenure, his supervising attorney general ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/29/ex-us-attorney-general-pam-bondi-testifies-in-epstein-files-probe)).

## The Epstein files fallout

In March 2026, the House Oversight Committee subpoenaed Bondi, compelling her to testify as part of its inquiries into the Epstein files ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/29/ex-us-attorney-general-pam-bondi-testifies-in-epstein-files-probe)). She and Blanche appeared for a closed-door briefing, but Democrats walked out, arguing the hearing was "fake" because it was not held under oath ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/29/ex-us-attorney-general-pam-bondi-testifies-in-epstein-files-probe)).

On May 29, 2026, Bondi appeared before a closed-door congressional hearing in what was a transcribed interview rather than a sworn, videotaped deposition — a format Chairman James Comer described as necessary to ensure her participation ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/29/ex-us-attorney-general-pam-bondi-testifies-in-epstein-files-probe)). She defended the department's approach, saying it had released nearly three million pages of records during her tenure, including photographs and video evidence, and that "to the best of my knowledge, the department produced everything required under the Epstein Files Transparency Act" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/29/ex-us-attorney-general-pam-bondi-testifies-in-epstein-files-probe)). But she acknowledged flaws: "There were redaction errors," she said — and critics argued the department had released the names and photographs of victims who had not been publicly identified, while continuing to redact information that should have been revealed ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/29/ex-us-attorney-general-pam-bondi-testifies-in-epstein-files-probe)).

Notably, Bondi told the committee she had not personally run the review. "As the head of a large department with broad responsibilities, I did not lead every aspect of this effort or conduct that document review myself," she said, adding that she had "delegated oversight over this process to Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/29/ex-us-attorney-general-pam-bondi-testifies-in-epstein-files-probe)). Some Democrats floated the possibility of contempt charges against her for refusing to fully cooperate with the House investigation ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/29/ex-us-attorney-general-pam-bondi-testifies-in-epstein-files-probe)).

## A cancer diagnosis, then a new White House role

Bondi's appearance came days after she disclosed that she was undergoing treatment for thyroid cancer and recovering from surgery ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/5/29/ex-us-attorney-general-pam-bondi-testifies-in-epstein-files-probe)). She had received the diagnosis shortly after leaving the Justice Department, according to Axios, which cited an anonymous source ([Daily Caller](https://dailycaller.com/2026/05/27/trump-appoints-pam-bondi-cancer-diagnosis-ai-advisory-panel/)).

Within weeks of the diagnosis, Trump appointed her to serve on the Presidential Council of Advisors on Science and Technology (PCAST), the White House advisory panel focused on artificial intelligence policy, again according to Axios ([Daily Caller](https://dailycaller.com/2026/05/27/trump-appoints-pam-bondi-cancer-diagnosis-ai-advisory-panel/)). Her role was announced by billionaire David Sacks, co-chair of the council ([Daily Caller](https://dailycaller.com/2026/05/27/trump-appoints-pam-bondi-cancer-diagnosis-ai-advisory-panel/)). "Winning the AI race means not only beating China but also clearing bureaucratic hurdles thrown up by state legislatures and woke politicians in D.C. No one is better positioned to support PCAST in this mission and to advise the president on legal and regulatory barriers than Pam Bondi," Sacks said ([Daily Caller](https://dailycaller.com/2026/05/27/trump-appoints-pam-bondi-cancer-diagnosis-ai-advisory-panel/)). Vice President JD Vance said Bondi had been "an enormously valuable asset to the president's team" and that he was thrilled she would remain involved in the administration's most important issues ([Daily Caller](https://dailycaller.com/2026/05/27/trump-appoints-pam-bondi-cancer-diagnosis-ai-advisory-panel/)). She was expected to help coordinate "between the government and tech titans on the panel," and was also slated to take on a new role related to national infrastructure initiatives ([Daily Caller](https://dailycaller.com/2026/05/27/trump-appoints-pam-bondi-cancer-diagnosis-ai-advisory-panel/)).
