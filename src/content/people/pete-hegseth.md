## Overview

Peter Brian "Pete" Hegseth — the full name under which he was impeached in the House in September 2026 — is the U.S. secretary of defense, confirmed by the Senate on January 24, 2025, by 51-50, with Vice President JD Vance breaking the tie ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/nomination/119th-congress/11/7)). A former Fox News host and Army National Guard veteran of Guantánamo Bay, Iraq and Afghanistan, he now operates under the secondary title "Secretary of War," authorized by a September 2025 executive order — the department's legal name remains the Department of Defense unless Congress changes it ([CNA](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/world/trump-renaming-lake-ontario-gulf-mexico-us-place-names-power-6366801)). His tenure has been defined by three overlapping storylines: the war against Iran and its legality, a sweeping purge of senior military leadership, and a September 2026 impeachment fight. As of September 29, 2026, he remains in office; speculation that he will be fired is speculation, not confirmed reporting ([AllChronology](https://allchronology.com/2026/09/08/shocking-prediction-reveals-who-might-be-trumps-next-cabinet-casualty/)).

## Military service, veterans advocacy and Fox News

Hegseth graduated from Princeton in 2003 and was commissioned as an infantry captain in the Army National Guard ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/11/13/who-is-pete-hegseth-the-pro-israel-fox-news-host-picked-to-head-pentagon)). He deployed to Afghanistan, Iraq and Guantánamo Bay; Al Jazeera reports two Bronze Stars, crediting his official biography ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/11/13/who-is-pete-hegseth-the-pro-israel-fox-news-host-picked-to-head-pentagon)). He led Concerned Veterans for America, described by Al Jazeera as a Koch-backed advocacy group, and left the military in 2021 after, in his account, being sidelined ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/11/13/who-is-pete-hegseth-the-pro-israel-fox-news-host-picked-to-head-pentagon)).

He joined Fox News as a contributor in 2014 and went on to co-host *Fox & Friends Weekend* and host programs for Fox Nation, becoming a regular on-air defender of Donald Trump ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/11/13/who-is-pete-hegseth-the-pro-israel-fox-news-host-picked-to-head-pentagon)). He authored several books, including *The War on Warriors*, ran unsuccessfully for the U.S. Senate in Minnesota in 2012, and earned a master's in public policy from Harvard Kennedy School ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/11/13/who-is-pete-hegseth-the-pro-israel-fox-news-host-picked-to-head-pentagon)). At the time of his nomination he lived in Tennessee with his wife — a Fox co-producer — and seven children ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/11/13/who-is-pete-hegseth-the-pro-israel-fox-news-host-picked-to-head-pentagon); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/4/pete-hegseths-pentagon-a-list-of-controversies-that-define-his-tenure)).

Al Jazeera's pre-confirmation backgrounder documented his views in detail: a staunch pro-Israel stance, hawkishness toward Iran, skepticism of elements of the NATO alliance and of U.S. policy toward Ukraine and China, opposition to women serving in combat roles, and hostility to military diversity programs ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/11/13/who-is-pete-hegseth-the-pro-israel-fox-news-host-picked-to-head-pentagon)). In 2019 he personally lobbied Trump to pardon or restore the rank of three service members involved in war-crimes cases — two were pardoned and one had his rank reinstated, according to the *Washington Post* account cited by Al Jazeera ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/11/13/who-is-pete-hegseth-the-pro-israel-fox-news-host-picked-to-head-pentagon)).

## Nomination and narrow Senate confirmation

Trump picked Hegseth to be defense secretary about a week after winning the 2024 election ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/11/13/who-is-pete-hegseth-the-pro-israel-fox-news-host-picked-to-head-pentagon)). The nomination — PN11-7, "Peter Hegseth, of Tennessee, to be Secretary of Defense" — was received in the Senate on January 20, 2025, and confirmed four days later by 51-50 on Record Vote 15, after cloture was invoked 51-49 the day before ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/nomination/119th-congress/11/7)).

The confirmation fight was bruising. Hegseth faced questions about marital infidelity, an old allegation of sexual violence and his alcohol use ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/policy/defense/4708253/driscoll-resignation-intensifies-hegseth-questions/)); the Hindustan Times reported the battle also involved allegations of financial mismanagement and heavy drinking, all of which he denied ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/pete-hegseth-is-conquering-the-pentagon-101788337904368.html)). He was confirmed only because Vance broke the Senate deadlock ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/policy/defense/4708253/driscoll-resignation-intensifies-hegseth-questions/)).

Eight months later, Trump marked his 200th executive order since returning to office by authorizing "Department of War" as a secondary title for the Department of Defense and "Secretary of War" as a secondary title for Hegseth ([CNA](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/world/trump-renaming-lake-ontario-gulf-mexico-us-place-names-power-6366801)). "I think it's a much more appropriate name, in light of where the world is right now," Trump said, adding that "it sends a message of victory," according to the BBC account cited by CNA ([CNA](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/world/trump-renaming-lake-ontario-gulf-mexico-us-place-names-power-6366801)). The Pentagon has acted on the order — replacing some "Department of Defense" signage and changing its website domain and title — but only Congress can permanently change the department's legal name; the House approved the change in July as part of its version of the Fiscal Year 2027 National Defense Authorization Act, while the Senate has yet to do so ([CNA](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/world/trump-renaming-lake-ontario-gulf-mexico-us-place-names-power-6366801)).

## Reshaping Pentagon leadership

The defining administrative fact of Hegseth's tenure is the scale of senior-leader turnover. The Hindustan Times reports that he has ousted at least 24 flag or general officers ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/pete-hegseth-is-conquering-the-pentagon-101788337904368.html)). The purge began almost immediately: in February 2025, Trump fired Joint Chiefs of Staff chairman Gen. Charles "CQ" Brown without explanation, and other early dismissals included the heads of the Navy and Coast Guard, the head of the National Security Agency, the Air Force vice chief of staff, a Navy admiral assigned to NATO and three top military lawyers; the Air Force chief of staff announced his retirement two years into a four-year term and the head of U.S. Southern Command retired a year into his tenure ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/hegseth-asked-us-army-chief-of-staff-to-step-down-amid-iran-war-220615)). Hegseth also ordered at least a 20 percent cut in the number of active-duty four-star generals and admirals and a 10 percent cut in the overall number of general and flag officers ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/hegseth-asked-us-army-chief-of-staff-to-step-down-amid-iran-war-220615)).

In early April 2026, Hegseth fired Army chief of staff Gen. Randy George, Transformation and Training Command head Gen. David M. Hodne and the Army's top chaplain, Maj. Gen. William Green Jr. ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/policy/defense/4708253/driscoll-resignation-intensifies-hegseth-questions/)). There was little public explanation: Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell posted on X late on a Thursday that George "will be retiring from his position... effective immediately," and CBS reported that vice chief of staff Gen. Christopher LaNeve would take over in an acting capacity ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/hegseth-asked-us-army-chief-of-staff-to-step-down-amid-iran-war-220615)). George was a highly decorated veteran of a nearly four-decade career with multiple Iraq and Afghanistan deployments who had served as senior military assistant to then-Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/hegseth-asked-us-army-chief-of-staff-to-step-down-amid-iran-war-220615)). Two months later, Hegseth pushed Gen. Chris Donahue — commander of U.S. Army Europe and Africa and the last American soldier on the ground in Afghanistan — into retirement ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/policy/defense/4708253/driscoll-resignation-intensifies-hegseth-questions/); [Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/pete-hegseth-is-conquering-the-pentagon-101788337904368.html)). The Hindustan Times also reports that seven officers were removed from a list of 18 approved for promotion to two-star general ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/pete-hegseth-is-conquering-the-pentagon-101788337904368.html)).

The turnover reached the civilian leadership on August 31, 2026, when Army Secretary Dan Driscoll submitted his resignation after repeated clashes with Hegseth — a departure a Pentagon official told the *Washington Examiner* was "a long time coming" ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/policy/defense/4708253/driscoll-resignation-intensifies-hegseth-questions/)). Driscoll had gone to law school with Vice President Vance and was rumored as a possible Hegseth replacement during the secretary's difficult start ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/policy/defense/4708253/driscoll-resignation-intensifies-hegseth-questions/)). Before leaving, Driscoll reportedly told Trump that Hegseth was "hurting the force and shrinking the pool of officers available for future senior commands," according to ABC News reporting relayed by AllChronology ([AllChronology](https://allchronology.com/2026/09/08/shocking-prediction-reveals-who-might-be-trumps-next-cabinet-casualty/)). The *New York Times* reported that many of the removed officers appeared to have been targeted because of their race, sex or professional associations with officers Hegseth disliked; the Pentagon's stated reasons for individual dismissals were not detailed ([AllChronology](https://allchronology.com/2026/09/08/shocking-prediction-reveals-who-might-be-trumps-next-cabinet-casualty/)). The most frequently tipped successor to Driscoll is Parnell himself — a combat veteran with two Bronze Stars and a Purple Heart who ran unsuccessfully for the House in Pennsylvania in 2020 and dropped out of the 2022 Senate race after abuse allegations from his estranged wife, which he denied ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/policy/defense/4708253/driscoll-resignation-intensifies-hegseth-questions/)).

## Signal, press access and operational controversies

In March 2025, text messages discussing imminent military strikes against Houthi targets in Yemen were shared with a journalist in a Signal group chat ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/4/pete-hegseths-pentagon-a-list-of-controversies-that-define-his-tenure)). Hegseth denied he had shared war plans, and Trump dismissed the episode as fake news ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/4/pete-hegseths-pentagon-a-list-of-controversies-that-define-his-tenure)). The fallout cost Mike Waltz his job as national security adviser ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/policy/defense/4708253/driscoll-resignation-intensifies-hegseth-questions/)).

Al Jazeera's September 2026 overview records a tenure defined by confrontation with the press and with internal dissent: a "maximum lethality" directive, blocked promotions for seven senior officers, journalists escorted out of the Pentagon press office and newly required to be accompanied, and the firing of senior military leaders — including CQ Brown — which critics describe as politically motivated, alongside the cancellation of hundreds of billions of dollars in contracts ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/4/pete-hegseths-pentagon-a-list-of-controversies-that-define-his-tenure)). Reporting compiled by AllChronology adds that dozens of Joint Staff members were subjected to polygraph examinations over leaks, that Hegseth restricted photographers' access and stopped regular briefings, and that accounts from aboard the USS *Abraham Lincoln* described an extended deployment with strained food supplies and photographs of the rust-covered carrier ([AllChronology](https://allchronology.com/2026/09/08/shocking-prediction-reveals-who-might-be-trumps-next-cabinet-casualty/)). The same compilation reports Pentagon funds supporting right-wing social-media influencers tasked with promoting Hegseth's image, a cut to an Army drone-and-robotics battalion despite Driscoll's warnings about unmanned warfare, and pressure on the editorial independence of *Stars and Stripes* — all presented as reported allegations rather than established findings ([AllChronology](https://allchronology.com/2026/09/08/shocking-prediction-reveals-who-might-be-trumps-next-cabinet-casualty/)). Democratic lawmakers separately alleged that Pentagon officials attempted to access funds Congress had appropriated to the National Institutes of Health; that allegation has not been established ([AllChronology](https://allchronology.com/2026/09/08/shocking-prediction-reveals-who-might-be-trumps-next-cabinet-casualty/)). The Pentagon is meanwhile seeking a $1.5 trillion military budget for fiscal 2027 ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/4/pete-hegseths-pentagon-a-list-of-controversies-that-define-his-tenure); [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/policy/defense/4708253/driscoll-resignation-intensifies-hegseth-questions/)).

## Iran war and September 2026 impeachment pressure

The Iran war, which the *New York Post* dates to February 28, 2026, is the gravest charge against Hegseth's tenure ([NY Post](https://nypost.com/2026/09/15/us-news/rep-thomas-massie-moves-to-impeach-pete-hegseth-over-iran-war-will-force-house-vote-on-resolution/)). Although Republicans control both chambers of Congress, the House and Senate passed resolutions earlier in 2026 calling on Trump to end the war; the [White House](https://factsledger.com/organizations/the-white-house) argues the conflict ended after an initial phase of fighting — resetting the War Powers Resolution's 90-day clock — and contends the Resolution unconstitutionally constrains the president ([NY Post](https://nypost.com/2026/09/15/us-news/rep-thomas-massie-moves-to-impeach-pete-hegseth-over-iran-war-will-force-house-vote-on-resolution/)). A cease-fire took effect on April 8 and Trump signed a memorandum of understanding on June 17, but both crumbled in July when Iran attacked commercial ships for using a U.S.-preferred route in the Strait of Hormuz; Trump has sought a deal ending Iran's nuclear program and reopening the strait ([NY Post](https://nypost.com/2026/09/15/us-news/rep-thomas-massie-moves-to-impeach-pete-hegseth-over-iran-war-will-force-house-vote-on-resolution/)).

On September 15, 2026, Rep. Thomas Massie filed articles of impeachment against Hegseth for allegedly committing "high crimes and misdemeanors" by waging the war without congressional approval ([NY Post](https://nypost.com/2026/09/15/us-news/rep-thomas-massie-moves-to-impeach-pete-hegseth-over-iran-war-will-force-house-vote-on-resolution/)). "Peter Brian Hegseth, in his capacity as Secretary of Defense, has executed unlawful orders in contravention of Section 2(c) of the War Powers Resolution of 1973," Massie declared on the House floor, adding that Hegseth "has demonstrated that he will remain a threat to the Constitution if allowed to remain in office, and has acted in a manner grossly incompatible with his duties and the rule of law" ([NY Post](https://nypost.com/2026/09/15/us-news/rep-thomas-massie-moves-to-impeach-pete-hegseth-over-iran-war-will-force-house-vote-on-resolution/)). Massie, 55, is a lame-duck libertarian who lost his May primary to a Trump-backed opponent and is reigniting the war-powers debate seven weeks before the midterms; he vowed to force a House vote on the resolution ([NY Post](https://nypost.com/2026/09/15/us-news/rep-thomas-massie-moves-to-impeach-pete-hegseth-over-iran-war-will-force-house-vote-on-resolution/)).

The administration closed ranks. Attorney General Todd Blanche defended Hegseth at a Rose Garden briefing: "Secretary Hegseth is doing a phenomenal job, and the secretary of war doesn't just go off and do things. He follows the law" ([NY Post](https://nypost.com/2026/09/15/us-news/rep-thomas-massie-moves-to-impeach-pete-hegseth-over-iran-war-will-force-house-vote-on-resolution/)). The Pentagon did not directly address the legality claims but called Hegseth "a transformative leader for the Department of War," with press secretary Kingsley Wilson saying "standards and merit are back, our arsenal is stronger than ever before, and morale and recruitment across every service are at all-time highs" ([NY Post](https://nypost.com/2026/09/15/us-news/rep-thomas-massie-moves-to-impeach-pete-hegseth-over-iran-war-will-force-house-vote-on-resolution/)).

The day after Massie filed, House Republicans canceled the final voting day before an approximately eight-week recess, delaying any vote; Speaker Mike Johnson denied the schedule change was impeachment-related and called the effort a publicity stunt, while Rep. Melanie Stansbury said Republicans were afraid the House would vote to impeach — a vote that could occur when the House returns in November ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/16/house-republicans-leave-congress-hegseth-impeachment/91790913007/)). On September 22, Stansbury appeared on MSNBC vowing to target Trump cabinet secretaries with impeachment if Democrats win the midterms; her quoted allegation about "illegal killings in the illegal war" was presented by the reporting outlet as an allegation she offered without identifying specific operations, laws or supporting evidence ([The Dregs Report](https://thedregsreport.com/2026/09/22/new-mexico-democrat-pledges-to-target-trump-cabinet-secretaries-with-impeachment-if-party-wins-midterms/)). Earlier in the month, outgoing Sen. Thom Tillis had publicly called on Trump to replace Hegseth after Driscoll's departure ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/4/pete-hegseths-pentagon-a-list-of-controversies-that-define-his-tenure)), and journalist Paul Waldman predicted on Substack that Hegseth would be the next cabinet secretary fired or forced to resign — a forecast the piece itself labels speculation, noting no White House confirmation ([AllChronology](https://allchronology.com/2026/09/08/shocking-prediction-reveals-who-might-be-trumps-next-cabinet-casualty/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not adjudicate the legality of the Iran war or of the strikes on alleged drug boats: Massie's articles allege War Powers Resolution violations, while the White House and the Justice Department dispute both the characterization and the constitutional premise, and the question is unresolved. It does not repeat Rep. Stansbury's allegation about "illegal killings" as fact — she identified no specific operations, laws or evidence, and the outlet reporting her remarks presents them as allegations only. It does not state the motives for any officer's removal as fact; motives are disputed, and the *New York Times* account of targeting by race, sex or association is reported through attributed lower-tier relaying. It makes no claim that Hegseth's removal is imminent — Waldman's prediction is explicitly a forecast, and no announcement from Trump or the White House confirms it. It does not present the NIH-funds allegation or the influencer-payments reporting as established. It does not state Hegseth's date of birth, which no citable source verified, and it treats the two Bronze Stars as credited by his official biography. It does not call him legally "the Secretary of War" — that is a secondary title; the department's legal name remains the Department of Defense unless Congress changes it. It makes no claim about his personal life beyond what the sources cover.
