## Overview

Joe Biden is the only modern American president who entered the office with a train station named for him. He earned the nickname "Amtrak Joe" commuting between his home in Wilmington, Delaware, and Washington, D.C., for most of his 36 years in the Senate, and in March 2011 Amtrak rededicated Wilmington's station as the Joseph R. Biden, Jr., Railroad Station after a two-year renovation, saying the renaming honored "a long-time rider and strong advocate of passenger rail" ([Mass Transit magazine](https://www.masstransitmag.com/management/article/21161748/joe-biden-named-next-president-of-the-united-states)). Biden wrote in 2011 that after the 1972 car crash that killed his wife Neilia and infant daughter Naomi, riding Amtrak home every night to his two surviving sons, Beau and Hunter, was the only way he "could have been a Senator at all" — "For 36 years, I was able to make most of those birthday parties, to get home to read bedtime stories, to cheer for my children at their soccer games" ([Mass Transit magazine](https://www.masstransitmag.com/management/article/21161748/joe-biden-named-next-president-of-the-united-states)).

Joseph Robinette Biden Jr. served as the 46th president of the United States from January 20, 2021, to January 20, 2025 — the capstone of a public career that began with his election to the U.S. Senate at age 29 and included 36 years in that chamber and eight years as vice president under Barack Obama ([LegiList / Biographical Directory of the U.S. Congress](https://legilist.com/generate-factsheet.php?bioguide=B000444)). On July 21, 2024, he withdrew from that year's Democratic nomination and endorsed Vice President Kamala Harris ([NPR](https://www.mtpr.org/2024-07-21/want-to-know-what-biden-said-when-he-dropped-out-the-full-letter-is-here)).

He was born November 20, 1942, in Scranton, Pennsylvania, and grew up partly in Claymont, Delaware, after his family moved there in 1953 ([LegiList](https://legilist.com/generate-factsheet.php?bioguide=B000444); [archived White House biography](http://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/The-Official-Biography-of-President-Joe-Biden-1.pdf)).

## Early life and the Senate years

Biden graduated from the University of Delaware in 1965 and Syracuse University College of Law in 1968, joined the Delaware bar in 1969, and practiced in Wilmington before serving on the New Castle County Council from 1970 to 1972 ([LegiList](https://legilist.com/generate-factsheet.php?bioguide=B000444)). Weeks after winning his Senate seat in 1972, his wife Neilia and infant daughter Naomi were killed in a car crash; his sons Beau and Hunter were seriously injured ([archived White House biography](http://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/The-Official-Biography-of-President-Joe-Biden-1.pdf)).

He took his Senate seat on January 3, 1973, and held it until resigning on January 15, 2009, winning reelection in 1978, 1984, 1990, 1996, 2002, and 2008 ([LegiList](https://legilist.com/generate-factsheet.php?bioguide=B000444)). He chaired the Judiciary Committee through the 100th–103rd Congresses and the Foreign Relations Committee in the 107th and 110th, and his Senate record included work on the Violence Against Women Act ([LegiList](https://legilist.com/generate-factsheet.php?bioguide=B000444); [archived White House biography](http://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/The-Official-Biography-of-President-Joe-Biden-1.pdf)). He married Jill Jacobs in 1977; their daughter Ashley was born in 1980 ([archived White House biography](http://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/The-Official-Biography-of-President-Joe-Biden-1.pdf)).

## Amtrak Joe: the Senate commute

The rail commute was not a photo opportunity but the logistical core of Biden's Senate years. Because he could board a train in Washington and be home in Wilmington for dinner, he kept a seat in the Senate he might otherwise have resigned after the 1972 crash — his own telling, in his 2011 "Why America Needs Trains" piece ([Mass Transit magazine](https://www.masstransitmag.com/management/article/21161748/joe-biden-named-next-president-of-the-united-states)). The commute also made him one of the Senate's most persistent advocates for passenger-rail funding, a stance he carried into the vice presidency and the 2020 campaign's transit platform ([Mass Transit magazine](https://www.masstransitmag.com/management/article/21161748/joe-biden-named-next-president-of-the-united-states)).

## Vice presidency (2009–2017)

Elected in 2008 on the ticket headed by Barack Obama and inaugurated January 20, 2009, Biden served as the 47th vice president through January 20, 2017, after reelection in 2012 ([LegiList](https://legilist.com/generate-factsheet.php?bioguide=B000444)). The archived White House biography credits him with work on the passage of the Affordable Care Act and the administration's economic recovery program ([archived White House biography](http://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/The-Official-Biography-of-President-Joe-Biden-1.pdf)). On January 12, 2017, he received the Presidential Medal of Freedom ([LegiList](https://legilist.com/generate-factsheet.php?bioguide=B000444)).

## The 2020 election and presidency (2021–2025)

Biden announced his 2020 campaign on April 25, 2019 ([archived White House biography](http://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/The-Official-Biography-of-President-Joe-Biden-1.pdf)). On November 7, 2020, the Associated Press called Pennsylvania for him — 284 electoral votes — making him president-elect; Kamala Harris became the first woman of color elected vice president, and their ticket drew more than 75 million popular votes, more than any ticket in U.S. history ([NPR / AP](https://www.iowapublicradio.org/news-from-npr/2020-11-07/listen-live-biden-wins-presidency-according-to-ap)). He was inaugurated on January 20, 2021 ([LegiList](https://legilist.com/generate-factsheet.php?bioguide=B000444)).

His first year brought two of the largest spending laws of the era. On March 11, 2021, he signed the $1.9 trillion American Rescue Plan Act: $1,400 direct payments to most Americans, $350 billion for state and local governments, extended unemployment benefits, an expanded child tax credit, and vaccine-distribution funding ([Associated Press](https://tedmag.com/whats-inside-the-1-9t-american-rescue-plan-act/)). In mid-April 2021 he announced the decision to fully withdraw U.S. forces from Afghanistan; the Taliban entered Kabul on August 15, and the last U.S. planes left Kabul airport on August 30, 2021, ending the 20-year war — an evacuation in which an August 26 suicide bombing killed 13 U.S. service members and about 169 Afghans ([Associated Press](https://thisistucson.com/news/nation-world/last-us-troops-exit-afghanistan-ending-americas-longest-war-after-20-years/article_c2654c70-a56e-59ca-9c33-3d7fc21a4f68.html)).

On November 15, 2021, he signed the $1 trillion bipartisan infrastructure bill into law at a South Lawn ceremony, funding roads, bridges, transit systems, and other public works with bipartisan votes in both chambers ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/national-news/2021-11-15/biden-signs-the-1-trillion-bipartisan-infrastructure-bill-into-law)). On February 25, 2022, he nominated D.C. Circuit Judge Ketanji Brown Jackson to the Supreme Court to fill the vacancy created by Justice Stephen Breyer's retirement ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB10702)). On August 9, 2022, he signed the $280 billion CHIPS and Science Act — about $52 billion of it for the U.S. computer-chip sector — which had cleared the Senate 64–33 and the House 243–187 ([Associated Press](https://pbn.com/biden-signs-280b-chips-act-in-bid-to-boost-u-s-over-china/?amp=1)).

## Withdrawing from the 2024 race

On June 27, 2024, Biden debated Donald Trump at [CNN's](/organizations/cnn/) Atlanta headquarters; analysts described him as hoarse and at times inarticulate while Trump dodged questions, and the night reinforced concerns about his age ([Northeastern University](https://news.northeastern.edu/2024/06/27/trump-biden-debate-recap/)). On July 21, 2024, he announced he was leaving the 2024 Democratic nomination to focus on serving the rest of his term, and he endorsed Kamala Harris; NPR linked the decision to Democratic pressure after the debate ([NPR](https://www.mtpr.org/2024-07-21/want-to-know-what-biden-said-when-he-dropped-out-the-full-letter-is-here)).

Harris ran in his place and lost decisively to Trump in November 2024, making Trump the 47th president ([Associated Press](https://www.tricityrecordnm.com/articles/associated-press-new-mexico/biden-will-publish-promise-me-america-memoir-after-the-november-midterm-elections/)).

## Post-presidency (2025–2026)

Within weeks of leaving office, Biden signed with the talent agency Creative Artists Agency (CAA) for representation; he had previously been a CAA client from 2017 to 2020, between the vice presidency and the presidency ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/international/article-840725)).

In May 2025 Biden's office announced he had been diagnosed with prostate cancer that had metastasized to bone, with a Gleason score of 9, described as hormone-sensitive ([The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/biden-says-prostate-cancer-radiation-treatment-worked-as-intended/)). A spokesperson said in October 2025 that he was receiving radiation and hormone treatment ([The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/biden-says-prostate-cancer-radiation-treatment-worked-as-intended/)). On September 8, 2026, Biden said the radiation had "worked as intended" and that doctors had helped control his cancer — he did not say he was cancer-free or in remission ([The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/biden-says-prostate-cancer-radiation-treatment-worked-as-intended/)). His son Hunter described a more difficult health picture in an August 2026 BBC interview, according to the same reporting ([The Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/biden-says-prostate-cancer-radiation-treatment-worked-as-intended/)).

He is writing his memoir, "Promise Me, America," announced July 15, 2026, and scheduled for publication on November 17, 2026, by Little, Brown and Company; Biden said it covers "why I chose to run for reelection and why I chose to step aside" ([Associated Press](https://www.tricityrecordnm.com/articles/associated-press-new-mexico/biden-will-publish-promise-me-america-memoir-after-the-november-midterm-elections/)). The AP also reported that former First Lady Jill Biden wrote in her own book, "View from the East Wing," published in June 2026, that her husband seemed so weak and disoriented during the June 2024 debate that she feared he was having a stroke — noting the White House had initially said he had a cold ([Associated Press](https://www.tricityrecordnm.com/articles/associated-press-new-mexico/biden-will-publish-promise-me-america-memoir-after-the-november-midterm-elections/)).
