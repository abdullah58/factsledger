## Overview

**Anand Sharma** is an Indian politician from Himachal Pradesh who served four terms in the Rajya Sabha, was Union commerce and industry minister under Manmohan Singh, and was deputy leader of the Congress in the Rajya Sabha until he retired in 2022 — before becoming one of the party's most persistent internal critics ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/chandigarh/congress-anand-sharma-kangra-lok-sabha-election-9300544/); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/politics/month-after-opposing-caste-census-congress-leader-anand-sharma-set-to-fight-his-1st-poll-in-42-yrs/2066315/)).

Sharma's career runs against the usual grain of Indian politics: he reached the Union cabinet and the opposition front bench almost entirely through the Rajya Sabha, never winning a direct election, and only contested his first [Lok Sabha](/organizations/lok-sabha/) election in 2024, from Kangra — losing it by 2,51,895 votes ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/chandigarh/congress-anand-sharma-kangra-lok-sabha-election-9300544/); [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!state/lok-sabha-election-2024-result-himachal-pradesh-kangra-seat-winner-rajiv-bhardwaj-bjp-anand-sharma-congress-latest-update-enn24060203487)). He is also the rare senior Congressman who dissented in public while staying in the party: a G-23 reform letter in 2020, a resignation from a Himachal election panel in 2022, and a direct challenge to the party's caste-census plank in 2024 ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/politics/month-after-opposing-caste-census-congress-leader-anand-sharma-set-to-fight-his-1st-poll-in-42-yrs/2066315/); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/politics/caste-census-demand-disrespecting-indira-rajivs-legacy-anand-sharma-questions-congress-poll-plank/2009586/)).

This profile is about Anand Sharma, the Himachal Pradesh Congress politician and former Union commerce minister — not any other person of the same name.

## From student politics to the Union cabinet

Sharma came up through the Congress's student and youth organisations: he was a founding member of the party's student wing, the National Students' Union of India (NSUI), later president of the Himachal Pradesh Youth Congress and chief of the Indian Youth Congress ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/politics/month-after-opposing-caste-census-congress-leader-anand-sharma-set-to-fight-his-1st-poll-in-42-yrs/2066315/)). His first direct election was a defeat: he contested the 1982 Himachal Pradesh Assembly election from Shimla and lost to the BJP's Daulat Ram Chauhan by around 3,000 votes ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/politics/month-after-opposing-caste-census-congress-leader-anand-sharma-set-to-fight-his-1st-poll-in-42-yrs/2066315/)). He was elected to the Rajya Sabha for the first time in April 1984, at 31 — just one year older than the minimum age for the House ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/politics/month-after-opposing-caste-census-congress-leader-anand-sharma-set-to-fight-his-1st-poll-in-42-yrs/2066315/); [The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/chandigarh/congress-anand-sharma-kangra-lok-sabha-election-9300544/)). It was only after the Congress-led United Progressive Alliance won the 2004 general election that he came into the spotlight: he began a second stint in the Rajya Sabha that year, served as junior minister for external affairs, and rose to cabinet minister for commerce, industry and textiles in the Manmohan Singh government ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/politics/month-after-opposing-caste-census-congress-leader-anand-sharma-set-to-fight-his-1st-poll-in-42-yrs/2066315/)). After the UPA lost power in 2014, he became deputy leader of the Congress in the Rajya Sabha, serving until his retirement in 2022 ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/chandigarh/congress-anand-sharma-kangra-lok-sabha-election-9300544/)).

## The Bali negotiator: food security against trade facilitation

Sharma's most consequential ministerial assignment was leading [India](/places/india/)'s delegation at the [World Trade Organization](/organizations/world-trade-organization/)'s Ninth Ministerial Conference in Bali in December 2013, where ministers of 159 countries struck the WTO's first-ever multilateral deal ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/cabinet-approves-india-s-stand-at-wto-meeting-in-bali-113121901147_1.html)). India's red line was food security: the Bali decision gave developing countries interim protection for public stockholding programmes for food security, shielding them from WTO challenge even if support exceeded agreed limits, until a permanent solution was reached — due for adoption by the 11th Ministerial Conference ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/cabinet-approves-india-s-stand-at-wto-meeting-in-bali-113121901147_1.html)). In exchange, India endorsed the Trade Facilitation Agreement, aimed at cutting the transaction cost of trade by simplifying customs procedures ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/cabinet-approves-india-s-stand-at-wto-meeting-in-bali-113121901147_1.html)). The Union Cabinet formally approved the stand India had taken at Bali, endorsing the view that most of India's demands and concerns had been addressed ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/cabinet-approves-india-s-stand-at-wto-meeting-in-bali-113121901147_1.html)). The Bali bargain is the citation-magnet fact of Sharma's career: 159 countries agreed to shield food-security stockholding from WTO challenge, which meant India's subsidised-grain programme could continue without facing a trade dispute while a permanent solution was negotiated ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pti-stories/cabinet-approves-india-s-stand-at-wto-meeting-in-bali-113121901147_1.html)).

## The insider-dissenter: G-23, the steering-committee quit, and the caste-census letter

In August 2020 Sharma was among 23 senior Congress leaders who wrote to then Congress president Sonia Gandhi calling for organisational reforms — the group that became known as G-23 ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/politics/month-after-opposing-caste-census-congress-leader-anand-sharma-set-to-fight-his-1st-poll-in-42-yrs/2066315/)). He kept breaking with the leadership in public: during the 2021 West Bengal assembly polls he opposed the party's alignment with the Indian Secular Front, and in December 2020 he publicly praised the [Narendra Modi](/people/narendra-modi/) government's handling of the COVID-19 crisis at a Federation of Indian Chambers of Commerce and Industry meeting ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/politics/month-after-opposing-caste-census-congress-leader-anand-sharma-set-to-fight-his-1st-poll-in-42-yrs/2066315/)). In August 2022, ahead of the Himachal Pradesh Assembly elections, he quit as chairperson of the Congress steering committee for the state, saying he felt excluded and insulted — though he still campaigned for the party, which went on to win the state polls ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/politics/caste-census-demand-disrespecting-indira-rajivs-legacy-anand-sharma-questions-congress-poll-plank/2009586/)). After being sidelined, he was reinducted into the Congress Working Committee, but he was then overlooked for renomination to the Rajya Sabha from [Himachal Pradesh](/places/himachal-pradesh/) — and the party's replacement candidate, Abhishek Manu Singhvi, lost after cross-voting ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/politics/month-after-opposing-caste-census-congress-leader-anand-sharma-set-to-fight-his-1st-poll-in-42-yrs/2066315/)).

The sharpest break came in March 2024, when Sharma wrote to Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge opposing the party's caste-census plank — a promise strongly backed by Rahul Gandhi — arguing that it would amount to endorsing "identity politics" and was a "fundamental departure" from the party's long-standing policy ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/politics/caste-census-demand-disrespecting-indira-rajivs-legacy-anand-sharma-questions-congress-poll-plank/2009586/)). He invoked Indira Gandhi's 1980 slogan — "Na Jaat Par Na Paat Par, Mohar Lagegi Haath Par" — and quoted Rajiv Gandhi's speech against enshrining casteism after the Mandal Commission report, arguing the move would be "misconstrued as disrespecting the legacy of Indira ji and Rajiv ji" ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/politics/caste-census-demand-disrespecting-indira-rajivs-legacy-anand-sharma-questions-congress-poll-plank/2009586/)). He also asserted that every Census Commissioner since Independence had argued against a national caste census, citing overlap, duplication and doubtful authenticity ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/politics/caste-census-demand-disrespecting-indira-rajivs-legacy-anand-sharma-questions-congress-poll-plank/2009586/)). That last claim is Sharma's assertion as reported by ThePrint, not an independently verified survey of Census Commissioners.

## Kangra 2024: a first direct election, lost

A month after the caste-census letter, the Congress fielded Sharma from Kangra — his first Lok Sabha election and his first poll in 42 years ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/politics/month-after-opposing-caste-census-congress-leader-anand-sharma-set-to-fight-his-1st-poll-in-42-yrs/2066315/)). Kangra looked difficult: the Congress had won the seat only once since 1998 (in 2004), the BJP had held it since 2009, and in 2019 the BJP's Kishan Kapoor had beaten the Congress candidate by over 4.7 lakh votes ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/politics/month-after-opposing-caste-census-congress-leader-anand-sharma-set-to-fight-his-1st-poll-in-42-yrs/2066315/)). Sharma lost to the BJP's Rajeev Bhardwaj, polling 3,80,898 votes against Bhardwaj's 6,32,793 — a margin of 2,51,895 votes — as the BJP won all four of Himachal Pradesh's Lok Sabha seats ([ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!state/lok-sabha-election-2024-result-himachal-pradesh-kangra-seat-winner-rajiv-bhardwaj-bjp-anand-sharma-congress-latest-update-enn24060203487)). ## Frequently asked questions

**Who is Anand Sharma?**
Anand Sharma is a Himachal Pradesh Congress politician, a four-time Rajya Sabha MP first elected in 1984, a former Union commerce minister under Manmohan Singh, and deputy leader of the Congress in the Rajya Sabha until 2022. He later became a prominent internal critic of the party.

**What did Anand Sharma do at the WTO Bali conference?**
Sharma led India's delegation at the 2013 WTO Bali ministerial, where 159 countries agreed to interim protection for food-security public stockholding from WTO challenge, pending a permanent solution. India in turn endorsed the Trade Facilitation Agreement on simpler customs procedures.

**Why did Anand Sharma oppose the caste census?**
In a March 2024 letter to Mallikarjun Kharge, Sharma said a caste census would endorse "identity politics," depart from the Congress's inclusive tradition, and risk disrespecting the legacies of Indira and Rajiv Gandhi. He argued it was no panacea for unemployment or inequality.

**Did Anand Sharma ever win a direct election?**
No. He lost the 1982 Himachal Assembly election from Shimla and the 2024 Lok Sabha election from Kangra by 2,51,895 votes. His parliamentary career ran through four Rajya Sabha terms, beginning with his first election in April 1984, and took him to the Union cabinet without a direct-election win.

**Is Anand Sharma still in the Congress?**
Yes, as of this profile's sources. Despite the G-23 letter, his 2022 steering-committee resignation and his 2024 caste-census dissent, Sharma stayed in the party, was reinducted into the Congress Working Committee, and contested Kangra as a Congress candidate. His dissent stayed inside the tent throughout.
