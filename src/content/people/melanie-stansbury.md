## Overview

On September 21, 2026, Melanie Stansbury appeared on MSNBC's "On the Line" with Alicia Menendez and laid out what she described as a full-spectrum plan for holding Trump cabinet officials accountable if Democrats win the House in the midterms ([The Dregs Report](https://thedregsreport.com/2026/09/22/new-mexico-democrat-pledges-to-target-trump-cabinet-secretaries-with-impeachment-if-party-wins-midterms/)). She said Democrats should use "every tool in the toolbox" to investigate cabinet secretaries, named Defense Secretary [Pete Hegseth](/people/pete-hegseth/) by name, claimed several secretaries had "blatantly broken the law," and warned them directly: "They better get ready, because we'll come for them" ([The Dregs Report](https://thedregsreport.com/2026/09/22/new-mexico-democrat-pledges-to-target-trump-cabinet-secretaries-with-impeachment-if-party-wins-midterms/)). She also played a clip of Representative Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican, saying that "impeaching cabinet members is highly underrated" ([The Dregs Report](https://thedregsreport.com/2026/09/22/new-mexico-democrat-pledges-to-target-trump-cabinet-secretaries-with-impeachment-if-party-wins-midterms/)).

It was a blunt pitch from a congresswoman whose career began far from cable-news confrontation. Stansbury is a Cornell-trained social scientist who describes herself as a "science translator" between physical sciences and policy ([Cornell](https://cals.cornell.edu/news/2021/09/community-based-organizing-us-congress)). As the ranking Democrat on the House's Delivering on Government Efficiency subcommittee — the chamber's mirror of the Musk-era DOGE drive — she has spent 2026 turning that translator's job toward cabinet-level oversight ([GAO](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-109135/index.html)).

## From Cornell to Congress

Stansbury arrived at Cornell in 2006 "searching for new tools and knowledge to make a difference in her hometown community in Albuquerque," and earned an M.S. in Development Studies in 2007 ([Cornell](https://cals.cornell.edu/news/2021/09/community-based-organizing-us-congress)). She later worked toward a Ph.D. with mentors in Native studies and natural resources ([Cornell](https://cals.cornell.edu/news/2021/09/community-based-organizing-us-congress)). Her public-service career spanned the White House Office of Management and Budget, where she worked on science, natural resources, and tribal issues, and a staffer role on the U.S. Senate Committee on Energy and Natural Resources — before she served in the New Mexico state legislature ([Cornell](https://cals.cornell.edu/news/2021/09/community-based-organizing-us-congress)).

In a 2021 interview with Cornell's College of Agriculture and Life Sciences, she framed her politics as applied social science. "I really think of myself as a science translator," she said, describing her job as taking physical, biological, and social sciences and translating them into evidence-based policy ([Cornell](https://cals.cornell.edu/news/2021/09/community-based-organizing-us-congress)). She calls her approach "community-based policymaking": convening groups, letting information from communities shape the policy work, and using the tools of sociology and community organizing every day ([Cornell](https://cals.cornell.edu/news/2021/09/community-based-organizing-us-congress)). She pointed to the stakes back home — one in three children in New Mexico experience food insecurity ([Cornell](https://cals.cornell.edu/news/2021/09/community-based-organizing-us-congress)).

## The 2021 special election

Stansbury came to Congress through the June 1, 2021 special election in New Mexico's 1st district, taking 79,625 votes — 60 percent of the total — according to the Congressional Record's account of the state-certified results ([Congressional Record](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-167/issue-103/house-section/article/H2722-3)). The seat had been vacant since President Biden tapped Deb Haaland as Interior Secretary ([Reuters](https://ktwb.com/2021/06/02/democrat-stansbury-easily-wins-new-mexico-congressional-race/)). Stansbury, then a state representative, defeated Republican Mark Moores, a state senator who ran a law-and-order campaign, by 60 to 36 percent; she was 42 at the time and had raised over $1.3 million, more than twice her opponent's total ([Reuters](https://ktwb.com/2021/06/02/democrat-stansbury-easily-wins-new-mexico-congressional-race/)).

## The oversight role

In the 119th Congress she serves on the [House Oversight Committee](/organizations/house-oversight-committee/)'s Subcommittee on Delivering on Government Efficiency — the subcommittee built to shadow the Department of Government Efficiency. The GAO's June 2026 report on weapons-systems acquisition was formally addressed to "The Honorable Melanie Stansbury, Ranking Member" of that subcommittee ([GAO](https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-26-109135/index.html)).

## The 2026 impeachment push

The MSNBC appearance did not come out of nowhere. Stansbury is one of more than 50 House Democrats signed onto Representative Robin Kelly's articles of impeachment against Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem, introduced after a U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement agent fatally shot Renee Nicole Good, a 37-year-old woman, in Minneapolis; a DHS spokesperson dismissed the effort as "silly" ([Rep. Brittany Pettersen's office](https://pettersen.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=1565)).

The cabinet-impeachment conversation also has a Republican precedent — Massie's. On September 15, 2026, Representative Thomas Massie read eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth on the House floor and filed them as a privileged resolution, which under House rules forces consideration within two legislative days ([Military.com](https://www.military.com/pete-hegseth-avoids-impeachment-vote-as-house-speaker-calls-early-recess)). House Speaker [Mike Johnson](/people/mike-johnson/) canceled the final voting day instead, sending lawmakers home until after the midterms and calling Massie's effort a "publicity stunt by someone who wants attention" ([Military.com](https://www.military.com/pete-hegseth-avoids-impeachment-vote-as-house-speaker-calls-early-recess)). Historically, only two Cabinet secretaries have ever been impeached by the House — War Secretary William Belknap in 1876 and Homeland Security Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas in 2024; no defense secretary has been ([Military.com](https://www.military.com/pete-hegseth-avoids-impeachment-vote-as-house-speaker-calls-early-recess)).

On MSNBC, Stansbury framed her own position in sharper terms than oversight procedure. She said Hegseth should be impeached "because of his illegal killings in the illegal war" and said Trump "has said at multiple times that he's planning to give pardons to everyone in his circle" ([The Dregs Report](https://thedregsreport.com/2026/09/22/new-mexico-democrat-pledges-to-target-trump-cabinet-secretaries-with-impeachment-if-party-wins-midterms/)). Those are her characterizations, not established findings — no court or official investigation has reached those conclusions, and the outlet reporting her remarks noted she offered no specifics for either claim ([The Dregs Report](https://thedregsreport.com/2026/09/22/new-mexico-democrat-pledges-to-target-trump-cabinet-secretaries-with-impeachment-if-party-wins-midterms/)).
