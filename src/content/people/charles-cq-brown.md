Before Charles Q. Brown Jr. became the most senior uniformed officer in the United States military, he was a Texas Tech cadet who wanted to quit. He had signed up for ROTC and decided after a single semester that the military was not for him — it took his father, a retired Army colonel who had served in Vietnam, to talk him into staying ([The American Legion](https://www.legion.org/information-center/news/newsletters/2023/may/biden-taps-butt-kicking-fighter-pilot-gen-cq-brown-for-joint-chiefs-chairman)). He stayed, fell in love with flying fighter jets, and rose to become the 21st chairman of the [Joint Chiefs of Staff](/organizations/joint-chiefs-of-staff/) — the first Black chief of staff of any U.S. military service and only the second Black officer to hold the chairmanship ([The American Legion](https://www.legion.org/information-center/news/newsletters/2023/may/biden-taps-butt-kicking-fighter-pilot-gen-cq-brown-for-joint-chiefs-chairman); [U.S. Air Force](http://www.af.mil/About-Us/Biographies/Display/Article/108485/charles-q-brown-jr/)). In February 2025, 16 months into a four-year term, President Donald Trump fired him ([Associated Press](https://cullmantimes.com/2025/02/21/trump-fires-chairman-of-the-joint-chiefs-of-staff-and-two-other-military-officers/)).

## The pilot

Brown earned his commission in 1984 as a distinguished graduate of the [Air Force](/organizations/air-force/) ROTC program at Texas Tech University, where he studied civil engineering before adding a master's degree in aeronautical science from Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University ([U.S. Air Force](http://www.af.mil/About-Us/Biographies/Display/Article/108485/charles-q-brown-jr/)). What followed was a pilot's career in the purest sense: he became an F-16 instructor, served at the U.S. Air Force Weapons School as an F-16 Fighting Falcon instructor, and eventually commanded the Weapons School itself, along with a fighter squadron and two fighter wings — the 8th at Kunsan Air Base in South Korea and the 31st at Aviano Air Base in Italy ([U.S. Air Force](http://www.af.mil/About-Us/Biographies/Display/Article/108485/charles-q-brown-jr/)).

The detail that best captures his reputation came from President Joe Biden at the Rose Garden ceremony nominating Brown as chairman: in 1991, Brown's F-16 caught fire during a training mission over Florida, and he ejected at more than 300 miles per hour, landing in the Everglades ([The American Legion](https://www.legion.org/information-center/news/newsletters/2023/may/biden-taps-butt-kicking-fighter-pilot-gen-cq-brown-for-joint-chiefs-chairman)). "I'll tell you what — he was back in the cockpit the next week," Biden said ([The American Legion](https://www.legion.org/information-center/news/newsletters/2023/may/biden-taps-butt-kicking-fighter-pilot-gen-cq-brown-for-joint-chiefs-chairman)). By the end of his career he was a command pilot with more than 3,100 flight hours, including 130 combat hours, and had flown 20 additional fixed and rotary-wing aircraft beyond the F-16 ([U.S. Air Force](http://www.af.mil/About-Us/Biographies/Display/Article/108485/charles-q-brown-jr/)). His operational tours spanned Operations Southern Watch, Northern Watch, Enduring Freedom, Odyssey Dawn, Unified Protector and Inherent Resolve ([U.S. Air Force](http://www.af.mil/About-Us/Biographies/Display/Article/108485/charles-q-brown-jr/)).

## The commander

Brown's rise took him through the Air Force's most demanding jobs. He served three tours at U.S. Central Command — as air operations officer, deputy director of operations, and deputy commander from July 2016 to July 2018 — before taking command of U.S. Air Forces Central Command and then Pacific Air Forces, the air component of U.S. Indo-Pacific Command, from July 2018 to July 2020 ([U.S. Air Force](http://www.af.mil/About-Us/Biographies/Display/Article/108485/charles-q-brown-jr/)). In August 2020 he became the Air Force chief of staff, confirmed by the Senate in a 98-0 vote, making him the first Black officer to lead a U.S. military service branch ([The American Legion](https://www.legion.org/information-center/news/newsletters/2023/may/biden-taps-butt-kicking-fighter-pilot-gen-cq-brown-for-joint-chiefs-chairman)).

Weeks into that job, after the police killing of George Floyd, Brown released a five-minute video titled "Here's What I'm Thinking About" that made him a national figure ([Associated Press](https://cullmantimes.com/2025/02/21/trump-fires-chairman-of-the-joint-chiefs-of-staff-and-two-other-military-officers/)). He described pushing himself "to perform error-free" as a pilot and officer his whole life while still facing bias — being questioned about his credentials, he said, "even when he wore the same flight suit and wings as every other pilot" ([Associated Press](https://cullmantimes.com/2025/02/21/trump-fires-chairman-of-the-joint-chiefs-of-staff-and-two-other-military-officers/)). Biden later called him "a fearless leader" for showing the world his unvarnished experience ([The American Legion](https://www.legion.org/information-center/news/newsletters/2023/may/biden-taps-butt-kicking-fighter-pilot-gen-cq-brown-for-joint-chiefs-chairman)). The video, and his support for diversity and equity efforts in the service, would later become ammunition for his critics ([Associated Press](https://cullmantimes.com/2025/02/21/trump-fires-chairman-of-the-joint-chiefs-of-staff-and-two-other-military-officers/)).

## The chairman

Biden nominated Brown in May 2023 to succeed Army Gen. Mark Milley as chairman, calling him "a proud butt-kicking American airman — first and always" and praising his reputation as an unflappable leader who creates "an environment of teamwork, trust and executes with excellence" ([The American Legion](https://www.legion.org/information-center/news/newsletters/2023/may/biden-taps-butt-kicking-fighter-pilot-gen-cq-brown-for-joint-chiefs-chairman)). His path to confirmation was troubled: he was among more than 260 senior military nominations stalled for months by Sen. Tommy Tuberville of Alabama over Pentagon abortion-travel policy ([Associated Press](https://cullmantimes.com/2025/02/21/trump-fires-chairman-of-the-joint-chiefs-of-staff-and-two-other-military-officers/)). When the vote finally came in September 2023, the Senate confirmed him 89-8 ([Associated Press](https://cullmantimes.com/2025/02/21/trump-fires-chairman-of-the-joint-chiefs-of-staff-and-two-other-military-officers/)). His service as chairman made history beyond his own appointment: it was the first time that both the defense secretary, Lloyd Austin, and the chairman of the Joint Chiefs were Black ([Associated Press](https://cullmantimes.com/2025/02/21/trump-fires-chairman-of-the-joint-chiefs-of-staff-and-two-other-military-officers/)).

## The firing

On February 21, 2025, Trump abruptly fired Brown in a social media post, sidelining him 16 months into his four-year term as part of a campaign, led by Defense Secretary [Pete Hegseth](/people/pete-hegseth/), to remove military leaders associated with diversity and equity programs ([Associated Press](https://cullmantimes.com/2025/02/21/trump-fires-chairman-of-the-joint-chiefs-of-staff-and-two-other-military-officers/)). "I want to thank General Charles 'CQ' Brown for his over 40 years of service to our country, including as our current Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff. He is a fine gentleman and an outstanding leader, and I wish a great future for him and his family," Trump posted ([Associated Press](https://cullmantimes.com/2025/02/21/trump-fires-chairman-of-the-joint-chiefs-of-staff-and-two-other-military-officers/)).

The firing had been telegraphed. The previous November, Hegseth had said on a podcast, flatly: "First of all, you gotta fire, you know, you gotta fire the chairman of Joint Chiefs" ([Associated Press](https://cullmantimes.com/2025/02/21/trump-fires-chairman-of-the-joint-chiefs-of-staff-and-two-other-military-officers/)). In one of his books, Hegseth had questioned whether Brown got the job because he was Black, writing: "Was it because of his skin color? Or his skill? We'll never know, but always doubt" ([Associated Press](https://cullmantimes.com/2025/02/21/trump-fires-chairman-of-the-joint-chiefs-of-staff-and-two-other-military-officers/)). Yet on January 27, 2025, his first day as defense chief, Hegseth had patted Brown on the back and said, "Look forward to working with him" ([Associated Press](https://cullmantimes.com/2025/02/21/trump-fires-chairman-of-the-joint-chiefs-of-staff-and-two-other-military-officers/)).

The same day Brown was fired, Hegseth announced the firings of two more senior officers: Chief of Naval Operations Adm. Lisa Franchetti — the first woman to serve on the Joint Chiefs — and Vice Chief of Staff of the Air Force Gen. Jim Slife ([Associated Press](https://cullmantimes.com/2025/02/21/trump-fires-chairman-of-the-joint-chiefs-of-staff-and-two-other-military-officers/)). Trump said he was nominating retired Air Force Lt. Gen. Dan "Razin" Caine, a career F-16 pilot most recently the CIA's associate director for military affairs, as the next chairman; because Caine had never served as a combatant commander or service chief, Trump had to sign a waiver of the legal prerequisites ([Associated Press](https://cullmantimes.com/2025/02/21/trump-fires-chairman-of-the-joint-chiefs-of-staff-and-two-other-military-officers/)).

Congressional reaction split along party lines. Senate Armed Services Chairman Roger Wicker thanked Brown "for his decades of honorable service" and expressed confidence in a qualified successor, while ranking Democrat Jack Reed called the firings "a direct attempt to politicize the military," warning that "elected leaders — especially Senate Republicans — must defend that enduring principle against corrosive attempts to remake the military into a partisan force" ([Associated Press](https://cullmantimes.com/2025/02/21/trump-fires-chairman-of-the-joint-chiefs-of-staff-and-two-other-military-officers/)).
