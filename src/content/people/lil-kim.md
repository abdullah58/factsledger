## Overview

Lil Kim — born Kimberly Denise Jones on July 11, 1974, in Brooklyn, [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) — is an American rapper whose 1996 debut album *Hard Core* made her the first female rapper with three consecutive No. 1 singles on Billboard's Rap Songs chart, and whose 2001 collaboration "Lady Marmalade" topped the Hot 100 and won her a Grammy Award ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/lil-kim-mn0000278439); [The Source](https://thesource.com/2026/08/21/lil-kim-sets-one-night-only-hard-core-symphony-performance-at-brooklyns-kings-theatre/)). A native of Bedford-Stuyvesant, she was discovered by The Notorious B.I.G. and first came to attention as the only woman in his group Junior M.A.F.I.A., before a solo career that produced three consecutive platinum albums and a blueprint for women in hip-hop that later stars still follow ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/lil-kim-mn0000278439); [Revolt](https://www.revolt.tv/article/2024-01-11/347646/11-reasons-lil-kim-is-the-blueprint-for-female-rappers)). In April 2025, Billboard's staff ranked her the fourth-greatest female rapper of all time, behind [Nicki Minaj](/people/nicki-minaj/), Missy Elliott and Lauryn Hill ([Black Enterprise](https://www.blackenterprise.com/nicki-minaj-best-female-rapper-of-all-time/)). She remains active: in November 2026 she presents *Hard Core Symphony*, an orchestral reworking of her debut album, in Brooklyn ([The Source](https://thesource.com/2026/08/21/lil-kim-sets-one-night-only-hard-core-symphony-performance-at-brooklyns-kings-theatre/)).

## Breakthrough and chart records

Her parents split when she was nine; as a teenager she was thrown out of her father's home and lived with friends and on the streets ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/lil-kim-mn0000278439)). The Notorious B.I.G. heard her rhyme and recruited her into Junior M.A.F.I.A., where she stood out on "Player's Anthem" and the 1995 album *Conspiracy* ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/lil-kim-mn0000278439)). Her solo debut, *Hard Core*, arrived in late 1996, debuting at No. 11 — the highest debut in the [United States](/places/united-states/) for a female rapper at the time. Its lead single "No Time", with Sean "Puffy" Combs, topped the rap chart ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/lil-kim-mn0000278439); [Revolt](https://www.revolt.tv/article/2024-01-11/347646/11-reasons-lil-kim-is-the-blueprint-for-female-rappers)). Two more No. 1s followed — "Not Tonight (Ladies Night remix)" and "Crush on You" — the first three consecutive No. 1s by a female rapper on Billboard's Rap Songs chart ([The Source](https://thesource.com/2026/08/21/lil-kim-sets-one-night-only-hard-core-symphony-performance-at-brooklyns-kings-theatre/)). In 2001 she joined Christina Aguilera, Pink and Mýa on "Lady Marmalade" for *Moulin Rouge!*, a Hot 100 No. 1 that won the 2002 Grammy for Best Pop Collaboration with Vocals — the second by a female rapper ([BET Networks](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/grammy-award-winning-multi-platinum-rapper-singer-actress-and-fashion-icon-lil-kim); [The Scroll](https://contentdm.lib.byu.edu/digital/api/collection/BYUIScroll2/id/74139/download); [Revolt](https://www.revolt.tv/article/2024-01-11/347646/11-reasons-lil-kim-is-the-blueprint-for-female-rappers)).

## The 2005 conviction and comeback

On February 25, 2001, Jones's group confronted rap group Capone-N-Noreaga outside the Hot 97 studios in Manhattan; five men fired 22 bullets and one man was shot in the back ([NME](https://www.nme.com/news/music/lil-kim-12-1364664)). When a grand jury investigated in 2003, Jones said her manager Damion Butler was not at the station and denied knowing a bodyguard — but both men admitted it and pleaded guilty, and photos showed Butler opening a door for her ([NME](https://www.nme.com/news/music/lil-kim-12-1364664); [Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2005/07/07/a-year-for-lil-kim/)). A March 2005 conviction on three perjury counts and one conspiracy count left her facing up to 20 years. On July 6, Judge Gerard Lynch gave her one year and one day and a $50,000 fine; in court she admitted, "I testified falsely to the grand jury and during the trial" ([NME](https://www.nme.com/news/music/lil-kim-12-1364664); [Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2005/07/07/a-year-for-lil-kim/)). Her fourth album, *The Naked Truth*, appeared that September while "Lighters Up" dominated urban radio, but the case blocked its promotion ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/lil-kim-mn0000278439)). Ordered to report to prison on September 19, 2005, she was released on July 3, 2006, after ten months ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2005/07/07/a-year-for-lil-kim/); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/lil-kim-mn0000278439)).

## Influence, fashion and later recognition

Her influence runs through the women who followed her: raw, sexually explicit lyrics and unapologetic confidence opened space for lyricists such as [Megan Thee Stallion](/people/megan-thee-stallion/), City Girls and Kash Doll ([Revolt](https://www.revolt.tv/article/2024-01-11/347646/11-reasons-lil-kim-is-the-blueprint-for-female-rappers)). Her fashion risks did the same — Chanel double-"C" wigs and monogram looks, styled with Misa Hylton and Marc Jacobs, were copied by Beyoncé, GloRilla, Teyana Taylor, Nicki Minaj and Miley Cyrus ([Revolt](https://www.revolt.tv/article/2024-01-11/347646/11-reasons-lil-kim-is-the-blueprint-for-female-rappers)). With Mary J. Blige she fronted MAC Cosmetics' 2000 VIVA GLAM campaign, which raised $4 million in one year and $20 million over six years for people with HIV/AIDS ([Revolt](https://www.revolt.tv/article/2024-01-11/347646/11-reasons-lil-kim-is-the-blueprint-for-female-rappers)). She acted in *She's All That*, *Zoolander* and *You Got Served* and released her fifth album, *9*, in late 2019 ([BET Networks](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/grammy-award-winning-multi-platinum-rapper-singer-actress-and-fashion-icon-lil-kim); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/lil-kim-mn0000278439)). In 2019 she received [BET](/organizations/bet/)'s I Am Hip Hop Award, presented by Rick Ross ([NME](https://www.nme.com/news/music/here-are-all-the-winners-from-the-bet-hip-hop-awards-2555401)). In April 2025 Billboard ranked her the fourth-greatest female rapper of all time; on November 19, 2026 she presents *Hard Core Symphony*, an orchestral version of her debut, at Brooklyn's Kings Theatre ([Black Enterprise](https://www.blackenterprise.com/nicki-minaj-best-female-rapper-of-all-time/); [The Source](https://thesource.com/2026/08/21/lil-kim-sets-one-night-only-hard-core-symphony-performance-at-brooklyns-kings-theatre/)).

## FAQ

**What is Lil Kim's real name?**

Lil Kim was born Kimberly Denise Jones on July 11, 1974, in the Bedford-Stuyvesant neighborhood of Brooklyn, New York City ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/lil-kim-mn0000278439)). She adopted the stage name Lil' Kim — also rendered Lil Kim — as a teenager, and is also known by her nickname "Queen Bee" ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/lil-kim-mn0000278439)).

**What was Lil Kim's first No. 1 song?**

Her first No. 1 was "No Time", her 1996 debut single with Sean "Puffy" Combs, which topped Billboard's Rap Songs chart ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/lil-kim-mn0000278439)). Two more rap-chart No. 1s followed, "Not Tonight (Ladies Night remix)" and "Crush on You" ([The Source](https://thesource.com/2026/08/21/lil-kim-sets-one-night-only-hard-core-symphony-performance-at-brooklyns-kings-theatre/)). Her first Hot 100 No. 1 came later, with 2001's "Lady Marmalade" ([BET Networks](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/grammy-award-winning-multi-platinum-rapper-singer-actress-and-fashion-icon-lil-kim)).

**How many Grammy Awards has Lil Kim won?**

One. She won Best Pop Collaboration with Vocals at the 44th Annual Grammy Awards on February 27, 2002, for "Lady Marmalade", recorded with Christina Aguilera, Pink and Mýa for the film *Moulin Rouge!* ([The Scroll](https://contentdm.lib.byu.edu/digital/api/collection/BYUIScroll2/id/74139/download)). It remains her only Grammy win.

**Why did Lil Kim go to prison?**

In March 2005 she was convicted of three counts of perjury and one count of conspiracy for lying to a federal grand jury about a 2001 shooting outside New York's Hot 97 radio station ([NME](https://www.nme.com/news/music/lil-kim-12-1364664)). She was sentenced to one year and one day in prison, fined $50,000, and released on July 3, 2006, after ten months ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2005/07/07/a-year-for-lil-kim/); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/lil-kim-mn0000278439)).

**What is Lil Kim doing now?**

She remains active three decades into her career. In April 2025 Billboard's staff ranked her the fourth-greatest female rapper of all time ([Black Enterprise](https://www.blackenterprise.com/nicki-minaj-best-female-rapper-of-all-time/)), and on November 19, 2026 she presents *Hard Core Symphony*, her first-ever live orchestral performance, reworking her 1996 debut album at the Kings Theatre in Brooklyn ([The Source](https://thesource.com/2026/08/21/lil-kim-sets-one-night-only-hard-core-symphony-performance-at-brooklyns-kings-theatre/)).
