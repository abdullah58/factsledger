## Overview

Richard Joseph Daley was the mayor of [Chicago](/places/chicago/) from 1955 until his death in 1976, serving five full terms and one partial term as the city's longest-serving mayor up to that time. Born on 15 May 1902 in the working-class Irish neighbourhood of Bridgeport, he rose through the Cook County Democratic machine to hold the mayoralty and the party chairmanship at the same time, a dual grip that made him, in Britannica's words, "the last of the big-city bosses" ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Richard-J-Daley)). He rebuilt downtown Chicago, kept the city solvent while other large American cities neared bankruptcy, and was later ranked the best American big-city mayor since 1960 in a poll of 69 urban historians and political scientists ([Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/uic-poll-ranks-best-worst-us-big-city-mayors-since-1960)). His record is inseparable from its controversies: racial segregation, patronage, and the police violence of the 1968 Democratic National Convention. This is Richard Joseph Daley, mayor 1955–1976 — not his eldest son Richard M. Daley, mayor of Chicago from 1989 to 2011 ([University of Illinois Chicago Library](https://exhibits.library.uic.edu/remembering-richard-j-daley/biography-of-mayor-richard-j-daley)).

## From Bridgeport precinct captain to City Hall

Daley was the only child of Michael Daley, an Irish Catholic sheet-metal worker and labour unionist, and Lillian (Dunne) Daley, born in Bridgeport on the city's Near South Side ([University of Illinois Chicago Library](https://exhibits.library.uic.edu/remembering-richard-j-daley/biography-of-mayor-richard-j-daley)). He graduated from De La Salle Institute in 1919 and earned his law degree in 1933 after night classes at DePaul University ([University of Illinois Chicago Library](https://exhibits.library.uic.edu/remembering-richard-j-daley/biography-of-mayor-richard-j-daley)). His political entry came through the Hamburg Athletic Club and alderman Joseph McDonough, who made him a precinct captain in the 11th Ward ([University of Illinois Chicago Library](https://exhibits.library.uic.edu/remembering-richard-j-daley/biography-of-mayor-richard-j-daley)). Elected to the Illinois House in 1936 and the Senate in 1938, he served as senate minority leader, lost his only election — for Cook County Sheriff in 1946 — then served as Governor Adlai Stevenson's state director of revenue from 1948 and as Cook County Clerk from 1950 ([University of Illinois Chicago Library](https://exhibits.library.uic.edu/remembering-richard-j-daley/biography-of-mayor-richard-j-daley)). In 1953 he became chairman of the Cook County Democratic Central Committee, and in 1955 he beat incumbent Mayor Martin Kennelly and Republican Robert Merriam ([University of Illinois Chicago Library](https://exhibits.library.uic.edu/remembering-richard-j-daley/biography-of-mayor-richard-j-daley)).

## Building the city that works

As mayor, Daley moved budget initiation from the City Council to his comptroller and budget director, and never lost a vote in the Council ([University of Illinois Chicago Library](https://exhibits.library.uic.edu/remembering-richard-j-daley/biography-of-mayor-richard-j-daley)). In 1970 he lobbied for and gained "home rule", letting Chicago impose all but income taxes without state approval, and he steadily shifted the cost of city services onto the state, the county and the suburbs, transferring welfare and university costs to the state ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/history/us-history-biographies/richard-joseph-daley)). The building programme included the Northwest, Dan Ryan, Congress and Southwest expressways, McCormick Place, the enlarged O'Hare airport, the Civic Center and the Sears Tower, while the Deep Tunnel Project began in 1975 to relieve flooding ([University of Illinois Chicago Library](https://exhibits.library.uic.edu/remembering-richard-j-daley/biography-of-mayor-richard-j-daley)). Daley also reached beyond downtown civic life: on 14 October 1973 he attended the dedication of the [Copernicus Foundation](/organizations/copernicus-foundation/)'s [Nicolaus Copernicus Monument](/places/nicolaus-copernicus-monument/) outside the [Adler Planetarium](/organizations/adler-planetarium/), credited it with symbolically linking Chicago and [Poland](/places/poland/), and proclaimed the day Nicolaus Copernicus Day ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)).

## 1968, the machine, and a contested legacy

Daley's power rested on the machine, whose ward committeemen controlled 3,400 precinct captains, and he was believed to control over 40,000 patronage jobs, a figure Encyclopedia.com puts at about 35,000 ([University of Illinois Chicago Library](https://exhibits.library.uic.edu/remembering-richard-j-daley/biography-of-mayor-richard-j-daley); [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/history/us-history-biographies/richard-joseph-daley)). He helped deliver Chicago for John F. Kennedy in 1960, and his standing collapsed in 1968: after Martin Luther King Jr.'s assassination he ordered police to "shoot to kill any arsonist … with a Molotov cocktail in his hand", and at the Democratic National Convention of 26–29 August 1968, 5,600 National Guardsmen joined 11,500 police during the "Battle of Michigan Avenue" ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/history/us-history-biographies/richard-joseph-daley); [The Nonviolence Project](https://thenonviolenceproject.wisc.edu/2024/02/04/chaos-in-chicago-the-1968-democratic-national-convention-protests/)). The Walker Report called the police response a "police riot": 641 people were arrested, hundreds were injured, and 63 newspeople reported being attacked ([The Nonviolence Project](https://thenonviolenceproject.wisc.edu/2024/02/04/chaos-in-chicago-the-1968-democratic-national-convention-protests/)). Yet Daley recovered: his fiscal management kept Chicago solvent while New York neared bankruptcy, and in Melvin Holli's poll of 69 experts, 33 ranked him in the top five big-city mayors since 1960, including 14 first-place votes ([Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/uic-poll-ranks-best-worst-us-big-city-mayors-since-1960)). He died of a heart attack on 20 December 1976, aged 74 ([University of Illinois Chicago Library](https://exhibits.library.uic.edu/remembering-richard-j-daley/biography-of-mayor-richard-j-daley)).

## FAQ

### Who was Richard J. Daley?

Richard Joseph Daley was mayor of Chicago from 1955 until his death in 1976, serving five full terms and one partial term. He simultaneously chaired the Cook County Democratic Central Committee from 1953, giving him control of both city government and the local Democratic Party, and was called "the last of the big-city bosses".

### How long was Richard J. Daley mayor of Chicago?

Twenty-one years, from his election in 1955 to his death on 20 December 1976. He was re-elected in 1959, 1963, 1967, 1971 and 1975, winning his largest victory — over 70% of the vote — in 1975, and was the longest-serving Chicago mayor up to that time.

### What did the Walker Report say about the 1968 Democratic National Convention?

The Walker Report, released on 1 December 1968 by the National Commission on the Causes and Prevention of Violence, described the Chicago police response to anti-war demonstrators as a "police riot", while noting that demonstrators had harassed and provoked police. It recorded 641 arrests, hundreds injured and 63 newspeople attacked.

### What is Richard J. Daley's connection to the Copernicus Foundation?

The Copernicus Foundation of Chicago raised $150,000 to bring a replica of [Warsaw](/places/warsaw/)'s Nicolaus Copernicus Monument to Chicago. At its dedication on 14 October 1973 outside the Adler Planetarium, Mayor Daley credited the monument with symbolically linking Chicago and Poland and proclaimed 14 October Nicolaus Copernicus Day.

### Was Richard J. Daley ever accused of corruption himself?

No solid charge was ever made against Daley personally, according to the sources read: scandals in his administration led to investigations and prison terms for some officials, including City Council floor leader Thomas Keane, but Britannica notes that none of the scandals touched Daley himself.
