## Overview

Yahya Sinwar was the leader of [[Hamas](/organizations/hamas/)](https://factsledger.com/organizations/hamas/) in the [Gaza Strip](https://factsledger.com/places/gaza-strip/) from 2017 until his death in 2024, and for the final ten weeks of his life the chairman of the movement's political bureau. His biography is unusual among militant leaders because so much of it was written inside an Israeli prison: convicted in 1989 and sentenced to four life terms, he spent 22 years behind bars, learned fluent Hebrew, followed Israeli newspapers and broadcasts, wrote a novel, and was once saved from a life-threatening brain condition by Israeli surgeons — before walking free in the October 2011 prisoner exchange that released captured Israeli soldier Gilad Shalit ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Yahya-Sinwar); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/yahya-sinwar-hamas-leader-committed-eradicating-israel-is-dead-2024-10-17/)).

Sinwar was born on 29 October 1962 in the Khan Yunis refugee camp in southern Gaza; his parents had been displaced from the Ashkelon area during the 1948 war ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Yahya-Sinwar); [The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-hamas-war/article-813628)). He studied Arabic at the Islamic University of Gaza before his arrest in the late 1980s ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Yahya-Sinwar)). Israeli and American officials identified him as a principal architect of the October 7, 2023 attacks on Israel; he was killed by Israeli forces in Rafah on 16 October 2024, with the IDF announcing his death on October 17 and Hamas confirming it the next day ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/yahya-sinwar-hamas-leader-committed-eradicating-israel-is-dead-2024-10-17/); [Associated Press](https://www.enterprise-journal.com/what-know-about-yahya-sinwar-hamas-leader-who-was-killed-gaza)).

## The prison years: an interrogator, a dentist, and a novel

The least-covered and most-documented stretch of Sinwar's life is the 22 years he spent in Israeli prisons. In 1989 he was convicted and sentenced to four life terms for the killing of two Israeli soldiers and of Palestinians suspected of collaborating with Israel ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Yahya-Sinwar)). He had co-organized al-Majd, the internal security apparatus that hunted suspected informers — Britannica dates its founding to 1985, while the Jerusalem Post places it in the late 1980s ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Yahya-Sinwar); [The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-hamas-war/article-813628)).

The man who interrogated him knew him better than almost anyone. Michael Koubi, a former Shin Bet interrogator, questioned Sinwar for 180 hours following his 1988 arrest and later told the New York Post that he saw "a man with murderous eyes, filled with nothing but hate." According to Koubi, Sinwar said he had killed at least 12 people he suspected of collaborating with Israel, using a butcher's machete — the origin of the nickname "The Butcher of Khan Younis" — and had killed three more men with a razor while in prison ([Matzav](https://matzav.com/former-shin-bet-agent-i-interrogated-oct-7-mastermind-sinwar-for-180-hours-there-can-be-no-peace-as-long-as-he-lives/)). The Associated Press independently reports that Sinwar admitted killing 12 suspected collaborators ([Associated Press](https://www.enterprise-journal.com/what-know-about-yahya-sinwar-hamas-leader-who-was-killed-gaza)).

Prison also nearly killed him — and an Israeli saved him. Dr. Yuval Bitton, then a young dentist working in the clinic at Nafha Prison, examined Sinwar in 2004 after he complained of neck pain and losing his balance; Bitton suspected a stroke caused by a brain tumor and insisted he be hospitalized immediately. Israeli surgeons removed the life-threatening abscess that same day ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-hamas-war/article-807178)). Bitton later said he spent hundreds of hours in conversation with Sinwar; nineteen years later, Bitton's nephew was killed in the October 7 attack that Sinwar is accused of orchestrating ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-hamas-war/article-807178)). An AP explainer separately reports that Israeli doctors treated Sinwar for brain cancer in 2008 — a date that does not match Bitton's 2004 account, and which may describe a separate episode ([Associated Press](https://www.enterprise-journal.com/what-know-about-yahya-sinwar-hamas-leader-who-was-killed-gaza)).

In 2004 Sinwar also completed a semi-autobiographical novel, "The Thorn and the Carnation" (Al-Shawk wa'l Qurunful), written in his cell and smuggled out by fellow prisoners. A study by the Institute for National Security Studies later analyzed the book as an ideological text laying out his worldview, with its protagonist mirroring Sinwar's own life ([Ynet News](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/rjhnrvzdle)). He used the prison years the way his interrogators feared he would: learning Hebrew, reading Israeli newspapers, and studying the society he intended to fight ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Yahya-Sinwar)).

## From prisoner to Gaza's leader

Sinwar was released in October 2011 as part of the exchange for Gilad Shalit — Britannica counts him among 1,026 prisoners freed, while Reuters describes a 1,027-prisoner deal ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Yahya-Sinwar); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/yahya-sinwar-hamas-leader-committed-eradicating-israel-is-dead-2024-10-17/)). He married the same year and had three children ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/yahya-sinwar-hamas-leader-committed-eradicating-israel-is-dead-2024-10-17/)).

His rise inside Hamas was swift. He was elected the movement's leader in Gaza in February 2017, was re-elected in 2021, and survived an assassination attempt that year ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Yahya-Sinwar)). The United States had designated him a Specially Designated Global Terrorist in 2015, and in May 2024 the International Criminal Court's prosecutor sought an arrest warrant for him ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Yahya-Sinwar)). The AP reports that he was believed to be behind the 2016 killing of Hamas commander Mahmoud Ishtewi, an internal purge that reinforced his reputation for ruthlessness toward suspected traitors ([Associated Press](https://www.enterprise-journal.com/what-know-about-yahya-sinwar-hamas-leader-who-was-killed-gaza)).

## October 7 and the end in Rafah

After the assassination of Ismail Haniyeh in Tehran, Hamas named Sinwar chairman of its political bureau on 6 August 2024 — putting the movement's most wanted man at its formal head ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Yahya-Sinwar); [The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-hamas-war/article-813628)). He held the post for barely ten weeks. On 16 October 2024, Israeli forces encountered and killed him in Rafah in southern Gaza; the IDF announced the killing on October 17, and Hamas confirmed his death on October 18 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/yahya-sinwar-hamas-leader-committed-eradicating-israel-is-dead-2024-10-17/); [Associated Press](https://www.enterprise-journal.com/what-know-about-yahya-sinwar-hamas-leader-who-was-killed-gaza)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not settle whether Sinwar was 61 or 62 when he died, whether the Shalit exchange freed 1,026 or 1,027 prisoners, or whether he was arrested in 1988 and convicted in 1989 — sources differ, and the differences are recorded rather than smoothed over. It does not pin the life-saving brain treatment to a single year: Yuval Bitton's account dates it to 2004, while the AP reports a 2008 treatment, and these may be separate episodes. It does not claim that Sinwar personally planned every operational detail of October 7; "architect" reflects how Israeli and U.S. officials characterized his role. The account of the 12 killings rests on his interrogator's and the AP's reporting, presented as their account. And it makes no claims about Hamas's leadership or Gaza's governance after his death.
