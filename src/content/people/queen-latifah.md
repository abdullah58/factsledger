## Overview

Queen Latifah — born Dana Elaine Owens on 18 March 1970 in Newark, New Jersey, in the [United States](/places/united-states/) — is an American rapper, singer and actress whose 1989 debut *All Hail the Queen* helped redefine a male-dominated rap genre, and whose single "U.N.I.T.Y." won the 1995 Grammy for Best Rap Solo Performance ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Queen-Latifah); [Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/queen-latifah/5764/)). She later became an Oscar-nominated actress and a Golden Globe winner, and in 2023 she became the first female rapper to receive a Kennedy Center Honor ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Queen-Latifah)).

That arc — from teenage rapper to the most decorated stages in American culture — is the story of this profile. Across seven studio albums and seven Grammy nominations, Latifah moved from rap into jazz singing, film, television and producing without abandoning the feminist stance of her first records ([Recording Academy](https://iq.grammy.com/news/lgbtq-r-b-artists-rappers-to-know)). Her honours now include a National Medal of Arts, a place in the Library of Congress National Recording Registry, and selection as a 2026 Rock & Roll Hall of Fame inductee ([National Endowment for the Arts](https://www.arts.gov/news/press-releases/2024/president-biden-award-national-medals-arts); [Rock & Roll Hall of Fame](https://rockhall.com/press-release/the-rock-roll-hall-of-fame-reveals-2026-inductees/)).

## The rap breakthrough

As a schoolgirl in New Jersey, Dana Owens was given the nickname Latifah — Arabic for "delicate" or "sensitive" — and later adopted it as her stage name ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Queen-Latifah)). In high school she rapped with the all-female group Ladies Fresh, and while studying communications at the Borough of Manhattan Community College in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/), she recorded a demo tape that caught the attention of Tommy Boy Records, which signed the 18-year-old ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Queen-Latifah)). Her first single, "Wrath of My Madness," arrived in 1988, and her debut album *All Hail the Queen* followed in 1989, mixing soul, reggae and dance with feminist themes to positive reviews and a wide audience ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Queen-Latifah)). Her second album, *Nature of a Sista* (1991), sold less well, and Tommy Boy did not re-sign her — but after signing with Motown she released *Black Reign* in 1993 to critical and commercial success ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Queen-Latifah)).

### "U.N.I.T.Y." and the Billboard record

"U.N.I.T.Y.," the standout single from *Black Reign*, was a direct retort to the sexist language of early-1990s hip-hop, confronting street harassment, domestic violence and the objectification of Black female sexuality ([Refinery29](https://www.refinery29.com/en-us/2015/08/91904/queen-latifah-unity-feminist-legacy)). Latifah has said she was inspired to write it by misogyny she witnessed at a fraternity picnic in Philadelphia, and its challenge — "Who you callin' a bitch?" — was called perhaps the most important question in 1990s pop culture ([Refinery29](https://www.refinery29.com/en-us/2015/08/91904/queen-latifah-unity-feminist-legacy)). It also carried a musical first: at the 37th Annual Grammy Awards in 1995, "U.N.I.T.Y." won Best Rap Solo Performance, the first time a woman had taken that honour — though MC Lyte had been the first female rapper nominated in the category, in 1994 ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/queen-latifah/5764/); [Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/news/10-albums-by-female-rappers-salt-n-pepa-queen-latifah-missy-elliott-eve-nicki-minaj/)). "U.N.I.T.Y." spent 20 weeks on the Billboard Hot 100 and peaked at No. 23, making a feminist protest song her biggest commercial hit — proof that a record demanding respect for women could also be a pop-chart record ([EntertainmentNow](https://entertainmentnow.com/the-voice/queen-latifah-season-30-coach-billboard-music-career/)).

## Screen career: from Jungle Fever to The Equalizer

Latifah made her film debut in Spike Lee's *Jungle Fever* (1991), and from 1993 to 1998 she starred as Khadijah James in the sitcom *Living Single* ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Queen-Latifah)). She hosted a daytime talk show from 1999 and published *Ladies First* that year. She earned an Academy Award nomination for Best Supporting Actress in 2003, for playing Matron Mama Morton in the film musical *Chicago* (2002) ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Queen-Latifah); [Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](http://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/2003/F?qt-honorees=0)). The comedies followed — *Bringing Down the House* (2003), *Barbershop 2: Back in Business* (2004), *Beauty Shop* (2005) and *Last Holiday* (2006) — and she sang as Motormouth Maybelle in *Hairspray* (2007) ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Queen-Latifah)). Her television films earned the top acting prizes: a Golden Globe win for *Life Support* (2007), and a 2015 Emmy nomination and Screen Actors Guild Award for playing Bessie Smith in *Bessie*, which she also produced ([The Kennedy Center](https://www.kennedy-center.org/artists/l/la-ln/queen-latifah/)). From 2021 she starred as vigilante Robyn McCall in CBS's *The Equalizer* through five seasons to 2025, and voiced Ellie in four *Ice Age* films ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Queen-Latifah); [NBC](http://www.thefutoncritic.com/news/2026/05/12/the-voice-announces-grammy-award-winning-music-icon-queen-latifah-as-a-coach-for-season-30-129012/20260512nbc01/)).

## Producer, honours and the 2026 Rock Hall class

Latifah runs Flavor Unit Entertainment with Shakim Compere, behind *Bringing Down the House*, *Bessie* and *Life Support* ([NBC](http://www.thefutoncritic.com/news/2026/05/12/the-voice-announces-grammy-award-winning-music-icon-queen-latifah-as-a-coach-for-season-30-129012/20260512nbc01/)). Producing earned her an Emmy too: *Bessie* won Outstanding Television Movie in 2015 with Latifah as an executive producer ([Television Academy](https://www.televisionacademy.com/shows/bessie)). Her firsts are unmatched among rappers: first rapper at the Super Bowl halftime show (1998), first hip-hop Walk of Fame star (2006), earliest female rapper in the National Recording Registry (2023) ([Recording Academy](https://iq.grammy.com/news/lgbtq-r-b-artists-rappers-to-know); [Library of Congress](https://newsroom.loc.gov/news/national-recording-registry-inducts-music-from-madonna--mariah-carey--queen-latifah--daddy-yankee/s/5a91b115-3825-4a5f-a702-35940b4de958)). In 2023 she became the first female rapper to receive a Kennedy Center Honor; she and [Missy Elliott](/people/missy-elliott/) became the first rappers to receive National Medals of Arts, hers presented by Joe Biden on 21 October 2024 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Queen-Latifah); [Uproxx](https://uproxx.com/music/missy-elliott-queen-latifah-national-medals-of-arts/)). Billboard's 2025 list of the 25 greatest female rappers ranked her No. 5, behind [Nicki Minaj](/people/nicki-minaj/), Missy Elliott, Lauryn Hill and Lil Kim ([Black Enterprise](https://www.blackenterprise.com/nicki-minaj-best-female-rapper-of-all-time/)). In April 2026 she was named a Rock Hall inductee (Early Influence Award), ceremony on 14 November 2026, and joined *The Voice* as a season-30 coach ([Rock & Roll Hall of Fame](https://rockhall.com/press-release/the-rock-roll-hall-of-fame-reveals-2026-inductees/); [NBC](http://www.thefutoncritic.com/news/2026/05/12/the-voice-announces-grammy-award-winning-music-icon-queen-latifah-as-a-coach-for-season-30-129012/20260512nbc01/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is Queen Latifah?**
Queen Latifah, born Dana Elaine Owens on 18 March 1970 in Newark, New Jersey, is an American rapper, singer, actress and producer ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Queen-Latifah)). She broke through with her 1989 debut album *All Hail the Queen*, won a Grammy, earned an Oscar nomination, and became the first female rapper to receive a Kennedy Center Honor ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Queen-Latifah)).

**What is Queen Latifah's biggest hit?**
"U.N.I.T.Y." (1993), from her third album *Black Reign*, is her biggest hit and her most influential song ([Refinery29](https://www.refinery29.com/en-us/2015/08/91904/queen-latifah-unity-feminist-legacy)). It spent 20 weeks on the Billboard Hot 100 and peaked at No. 23, her only Top 30 entry on that chart ([EntertainmentNow](https://entertainmentnow.com/the-voice/queen-latifah-season-30-coach-billboard-music-career/)).

**Did Queen Latifah win a Grammy?**
Yes — one Grammy from seven nominations ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/queen-latifah/5764/)). She won Best Rap Solo Performance at the 37th Annual Grammy Awards in 1995 for "U.N.I.T.Y.", the first time a woman had won that category, though MC Lyte had been the first female nominee in 1994 ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/news/10-albums-by-female-rappers-salt-n-pepa-queen-latifah-missy-elliott-eve-nicki-minaj/)).

**Was Queen Latifah nominated for an Oscar?**
Yes. She was nominated for Best Supporting Actress at the 75th Academy Awards in 2003 for playing Matron Mama Morton in the film musical *Chicago* (2002) ([Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](http://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/2003/F?qt-honorees=0)). Her *Chicago* co-star Catherine Zeta-Jones won the category that night ([Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](http://www.oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/2003/F?qt-honorees=0)).

**What honours has Queen Latifah received most recently?**
In 2023 she received a Kennedy Center Honor and her debut album entered the National Recording Registry ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Queen-Latifah); [Library of Congress](https://newsroom.loc.gov/news/national-recording-registry-inducts-music-from-madonna--mariah-carey--queen-latifah--daddy-yankee/s/5a91b115-3825-4a5f-a702-35940b4de958)). In 2024 she received the 2023 National Medal of Arts, and in 2026 she was announced as a Rock & Roll Hall of Fame inductee ([National Endowment for the Arts](https://www.arts.gov/news/press-releases/2024/president-biden-award-national-medals-arts); [Rock & Roll Hall of Fame](https://rockhall.com/press-release/the-rock-roll-hall-of-fame-reveals-2026-inductees/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not state that Queen Latifah has already been inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame: the ceremony is set for 14 November 2026, one day after this profile's publication, even though the Hall's library page already writes of her as "inducted in 2026" ([Rock & Roll Hall of Fame](https://rockhall.com/press-release/the-rock-roll-hall-of-fame-reveals-2026-inductees/)). It does not date her Grammy win to 1994, as some biographies do: the Recording Academy's own table places the "U.N.I.T.Y." win at the 37th Annual Grammy Awards in 1995, and 1994 is the eligibility year ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/queen-latifah/5764/)). It does not call *Black Reign* the first gold album by a solo female rapper, because no fully-read tier1 or tier2 source confirmed the "first" — only the 1994 gold certification itself ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/news/10-albums-by-female-rappers-salt-n-pepa-queen-latifah-missy-elliott-eve-nicki-minaj/)). It gives years only for the *All Hail the Queen* release and the "Ladies First" single, whose exact dates differ between sources, and it does not itemise *Order in the Court* (1998), whose release details no fully-read tier1 or tier2 source confirmed. It claims nothing about her personal life, net worth or record sales.
