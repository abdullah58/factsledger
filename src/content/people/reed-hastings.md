## Overview

In April 2026, [Netflix](https://factsledger.com/organizations/netflix/) co-founder Reed Hastings wrote shareholders a short note: "I decided to step down this summer to focus on new things." The New York Times reported the letter the same day, and by June the 65-year-old was off the board of the company he had spent 30 years building — the man who had turned a DVD-by-mail rental outfit into a global streaming and content powerhouse was out ([MK](https://www.mk.co.kr/en/world/12019585)). Three years earlier he had already handed the co-CEO role to Greg Peters, joining Ted Sarandos in the top job he himself had held alone for roughly two decades; his January 2023 move to executive chairman and the April 2025 shift to non-executive chairman were framed by the company as the "natural evolution" of its succession ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/netflix-co-founder-reed-hastings-shifts-to-board-chairman-role-12997922.html)).

The exit was not a disappearance. Hastings had been quietly assembling a second act. In 2023 he joined the revamped board of [Bloomberg](https://factsledger.com/organizations/bloomberg/), the financial-data company whose 2023 leadership overhaul put Netflix's co-founder alongside veteran banker Bob Steel and former Bank of England governor Mark Carney ([GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/education/higher-ed/netflix-co-founder-donates-50m-for-ai-at-bowdoin-college)). Then, in 2025, he took a board seat at Anthropic — confirmed in September 2026 when Reuters' read of the AI company's IPO prospectus listed "Reed Hastings | Board Member | 2025 | Chairman and co-founder of Netflix" among its directors ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/anthropic-ipo-prospectus-shows-key-executives-board-2026-09-28/)). The prospectus still called him Netflix's chairman, a title he had given up three months earlier — a small sign of how fast the exit had moved.

## The thirty-year run

Hastings was a software man before he was an entertainment man. In 1991 he founded Pure Software, which made tools for software developers; it went public in 1995 and was acquired by Rational Software in 1997, the same year he and Marc Randolph started [Netflix](https://factsledger.com/organizations/netflix/) ([Stanford](http://engineering.stanford.edu/about/history/heroes/2016-heroes/reed-hastings)). He had come to computing through math — a BA from Bowdoin College in 1983, a master's in artificial intelligence from Stanford in 1988 — with a detour between the two teaching high-school math in Swaziland for the Peace Corps ([Stanford](http://engineering.stanford.edu/about/history/heroes/2016-heroes/reed-hastings)).

At Netflix he was the constant. Sole CEO for about two decades until 2020, when he began sharing the role with Ted Sarandos; the streaming pivot of 2007, the original-content bets, the march to 301.6 million subscribers by the end of 2024 — all happened under his tenure ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/netflix-co-founder-reed-hastings-shifts-to-board-chairman-role-12997922.html)). His board career followed the same arc as his operating one: Microsoft's board from 2007 to 2012, then Facebook's ([Stanford](http://engineering.stanford.edu/about/history/heroes/2016-heroes/reed-hastings)).

## A mountain and a mission

What Hastings says he will do next is already visible. In 2023 he put $100 million into a majority stake in Powder Mountain, the Utah ski resort, took the CEO role himself, and in December 2024 announced plans to run it as a hybrid public/private ski community — partly members-only, partly open ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/netflix-co-founder-reed-hastings-shifts-to-board-chairman-role-12997922.html)). Korean business press reporting on the retirement said he was expected to devote himself to the transformation ([MK](https://www.mk.co.kr/en/world/12019585)).

The other through-line is education, and the money there is even larger. In January 2024 Hastings donated $1.1 billion worth of his Netflix shares to the Silicon Valley Community Foundation ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/business/netflix-co-founder-reed-hastings-shifts-to-board-chairman-role-12997922.html)). A year later he gave $50 million to his alma mater Bowdoin College — the largest gift in its 231-year history — to create the Hastings Initiative for AI and Humanity, which funds 10 new faculty and a program to put ethical frameworks around AI's advance ([GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/education/higher-ed/netflix-co-founder-donates-50m-for-ai-at-bowdoin-college)). "As AI becomes smarter than humans, we are going to need some deep thinking to keep us flourishing," he said in the announcement. The philanthropy is a continuation of his earliest public role: serving on the California State Board of Education from 2000 to 2004, when his Peace Corps years were still the freshest thing on his resume ([GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/education/higher-ed/netflix-co-founder-donates-50m-for-ai-at-bowdoin-college), [Stanford](http://engineering.stanford.edu/about/history/heroes/2016-heroes/reed-hastings)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not state why Hastings left the Netflix board beyond what he said in his letter — "to focus on new things" — and it does not characterize the exit as a dispute or a dispute-free parting; neither was documented. It does not give a net-worth figure, which fluctuates with Netflix's share price. It cites the $1.1 billion Silicon Valley Community Foundation gift and the $50 million Bowdoin gift but not the widely repeated $120 million historically-Black-colleges figure, which could not be tied to a fully read source. It notes his Facebook board service without an end date, which the Stanford page does not supply. And it treats the Anthropic IPO prospectus as evidence of his board seat, not as a guarantee he still holds it — prospectus language lags corporate changes.
