## Overview

Yuri Ushakov is the Kremlin's foreign policy aide to [Vladimir Putin](/people/vladimir-putin/) — in the post since May 2012 — and Russia's lead negotiator in the [Ukraine](/places/ukraine/)-war talks with the United States ([Reuters](https://theprint.in/world/factbox-putin-sends-top-foreign-policy-team-for-talks-with-us/2500169/); [RBC-Ukraine](https://newsukraine.rbc.ua/news/bloomberg-learns-whom-putin-involves-in-us-1739555350.html)). Two facts about him are rarely stated together, though both are verified: he has been sanctioned by Canada, Ukraine and Australia — but not by the United States, the EU or the UK — even as he sits across the table from American envoys through 2025 and 2026 ([OpenSanctions](https://www.opensanctions.org/entities/Q4479563/)). And he is the Kremlin's standing post-meeting narrator: after each encounter between Putin and the US side, it is Ushakov who emerges to brief the press and set Moscow's public terms ([Anadolu Agency](https://f.aa.com.tr/en/world/kremlin-aide-says-putin-witkoff-meeting-in-moscow-useful-constructive/3652146); [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202512/04/WS6930dd3fa310d6866eb2cc94.html); [Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/trumps-envoys-witkoff-kushner-arrive-in-moscow-to-meet-putin/)).

## A 56-year diplomat: Denmark, Vienna, the deputy ministry

Ushakov was born on March 13, 1947, in Moscow, and graduated from the Moscow State Institute of International Relations (MGIMO) in 1970, later earning a Candidate of Historical Sciences degree ([OpenSanctions](https://www.opensanctions.org/entities/Q4479563/)). The Kremlin's official catalog of persons traces his early career through Soviet postings in Denmark from 1970 to 1992 — he rose to Deputy Head of Mission in Copenhagen in 1986 — followed by the directorship of the Foreign Ministry's Department for European Cooperation from 1992 to 1996 ([OpenSanctions](https://www.opensanctions.org/entities/Q4479563/)).

From 1996 to 1998 he was Russia's ambassador to the Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe in Vienna, and in 1998 he served briefly as Deputy Foreign Minister ([OpenSanctions](https://www.opensanctions.org/entities/Q4479563/)). The catalog also records his personal details: he is married with one daughter, speaks English and Danish, holds the diplomatic rank of Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary, is a Merited Worker of the Russian Diplomatic Service, and has been decorated with the Orders of Merit for the Fatherland (classes I–IV), Alexander Nevsky, Honour and Friendship ([OpenSanctions](https://www.opensanctions.org/entities/Q4479563/)).

## Washington, 1998–2008: the decade that made him

In December 1998 Ushakov was appointed ambassador to the United States — and Permanent Observer at the Organization of American States — holding the post until June 2008, which made him the longest-serving post-Soviet Russian ambassador to Washington ([OpenSanctions](https://www.opensanctions.org/entities/Q4479563/); [RBC-Ukraine](https://newsukraine.rbc.ua/news/bloomberg-learns-whom-putin-involves-in-us-1739555350.html)). Bloomberg, reporting on Putin's negotiating team in February 2025, noted that the decade gave him "deep knowledge of dealing with Washington" ([RBC-Ukraine](https://newsukraine.rbc.ua/news/bloomberg-learns-whom-putin-involves-in-us-1739555350.html)).

As ambassador, he once wrote of his frustration with the American attitude: "What offends us is the view shared by some in Washington that Russia can be used when it is needed and discarded or even abused when it is not relevant to American objectives" ([Reuters](https://theprint.in/world/factbox-putin-sends-top-foreign-policy-team-for-talks-with-us/2500169/)). The line has a second life now: the man who wrote it is the one briefing journalists on the current state of US–Russia relations.

## "The man who writes Putin's foreign policy briefing papers"

When Putin moved from the Kremlin to the prime minister's office in 2008, Ushakov followed — becoming Deputy Chief of the Government Staff from June 2008 to May 2012 ([OpenSanctions](https://www.opensanctions.org/entities/Q4479563/)). A June 2, 2009 US embassy cable titled its profile of him "THE MAN WHO WRITES PUTIN'S FOREIGN POLICY BRIEFING PAPERS": Putin had called on the former ambassador to create a foreign-policy office reflecting the prime minister's role, and a three-person team of former foreign-ministry officials drafted the briefing papers and talking points Putin carried into meetings with foreign leaders ([WikiLeaks](https://wikileaks.org/plusd/cables/09MOSCOW1436_a.html)).

His deputy, Yuri Kholkhov, told the Americans that Ushakov was "extremely well informed on American politics" and that Putin "relied upon him to understand developments in the U.S."; Putin had brought him on as his "key foreign policy advisor" ([WikiLeaks](https://wikileaks.org/plusd/cables/09MOSCOW1436_a.html)). Reuters later summarized his function in a single sentence: he "attends Putin's most important international meetings, and speaks for him on major diplomatic issues" ([Reuters](https://theprint.in/world/factbox-putin-sends-top-foreign-policy-team-for-talks-with-us/2500169/)).

## Aide to the president since 2012 — and the "big negotiations"

Ushakov has been aide to the president since May 21, 2012 ([OpenSanctions](https://www.opensanctions.org/entities/Q4479563/)). In February 2025 Bloomberg reported that Putin had assembled a "heavyweight team" for the Ukraine negotiations with the US: Ushakov as the Kremlin's chief foreign policy adviser — "over half a century" in diplomacy — alongside Sergey Naryshkin and Kirill Dmitriev ([RBC-Ukraine](https://newsukraine.rbc.ua/news/bloomberg-learns-whom-putin-involves-in-us-1739555350.html)). Andrey Sushentsov, dean of MGIMO's Faculty of International Relations, called him "the best person for 'big negotiations' in the classical sense," noting he "knows the American establishment well and has big influence in Moscow"; he and Naryshkin had also taken part in the early ceasefire talks with Ukraine shortly after the 2022 invasion ([RBC-Ukraine](https://newsukraine.rbc.ua/news/bloomberg-learns-whom-putin-involves-in-us-1739555350.html)).

His briefings have since become the Kremlin's running public record of the US channel. After the roughly three-hour Putin meeting with US envoy [Steve Witkoff](/people/steve-witkoff/) on August 6, 2025 — Witkoff's fifth Russia visit that year, two days before a US ceasefire deadline — Ushakov called the talks "useful and constructive," said "some signals" had been conveyed and "corresponding signals" received on Trump's behalf, and declined further detail until Trump was briefed ([Anadolu Agency](https://f.aa.com.tr/en/world/kremlin-aide-says-putin-witkoff-meeting-in-moscow-useful-constructive/3652146)). On December 2, 2025, he sat with Putin and Dmitriev across from Witkoff and [Jared Kushner](/people/jared-kushner/) for about five hours and described the meeting as "extremely useful, constructive and highly informative," confirming that Ukraine's NATO accession had been discussed while agreeing with the Americans not to disclose the essence ([China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202512/04/WS6930dd3fa310d6866eb2cc94.html)).

The pattern held into 2026. On January 22, Putin met Witkoff and Kushner — joined this time by White House staffer Josh Gruenbaum — for 3 hours and 10 minutes, and Ushakov told reporters afterward that without resolving the territorial question "according to the formula agreed upon in Anchorage" there was "no point in hoping" for a settlement; he also confirmed the envoys had shared their firsthand impressions from Davos and announced follow-up talks in the UAE ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/trumps-envoys-witkoff-kushner-arrive-in-moscow-to-meet-putin/)). The January meeting capped a sequence in which the same man who has advised Putin since 2012 has been present at — and publicly framed — every US encounter.

## The sanctions paradox

OpenSanctions lists three sanctions entries for Ushakov: Canada (under its Special Economic Measures Act), Ukraine's National Security and Defense Council (February 12, 2023), and Australia (February 24, 2025) ([OpenSanctions](https://www.opensanctions.org/entities/Q4479563/)). Notably absent: the United States, the EU and the UK. The contrast with his own negotiating table is sharp — Dmitriev, his frequent partner in the 2025–2026 US meetings, is under US sanctions, which RBC-Ukraine reported with the American description of him as "a known Putin ally" ([RBC-Ukraine](https://newsukraine.rbc.ua/news/bloomberg-learns-whom-putin-involves-in-us-1739555350.html)).
