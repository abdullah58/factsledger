## Overview

James R. Mountain is a Pueblo de San Ildefonso leader who governed his pueblo in 2006–2007 and again in 2015–2017, served as [New Mexico](/places/new-mexico/)'s Indian Affairs secretary-designate in 2023, and chaired the [All Pueblo Council of Governors](/organizations/all-pueblo-council-of-governors/) until resigning in late September 2025 ([New Mexico Governor's Office](https://www.governor.state.nm.us/2023/02/03/governor-lujan-grisham-announces-new-secretary-of-indian-affairs-department/); [Associated Press](https://srnnews.com/embattled-figure-in-native-politics-resigns-as-chairman-of-pueblo-governors-council-in-new-mexico/)). His public career runs from tribal government and water settlements through a contested state cabinet tenure to intertribal advocacy on [Chaco Canyon](/places/chaco-canyon/), and it ended at the council with a resignation days after an arrest on suspicion of driving while intoxicated ([Associated Press](https://srnnews.com/embattled-figure-in-native-politics-resigns-as-chairman-of-pueblo-governors-council-in-new-mexico/)).

## Governor of San Ildefonso Pueblo

Mountain served as governor of the Pueblo de San Ildefonso in 2006–2007 and again in 2015–2017, and he had previously served on the pueblo's Supreme Court and tribal council ([New Mexico Governor's Office](https://www.governor.state.nm.us/2023/02/03/governor-lujan-grisham-announces-new-secretary-of-indian-affairs-department/)). During his governorships he led the pueblo in completing the decades-old Aamodt Water Settlement, which the Governor's Office describes as ensuring the pueblo's water rights in perpetuity ([New Mexico Governor's Office](https://www.governor.state.nm.us/2023/02/03/governor-lujan-grisham-announces-new-secretary-of-indian-affairs-department/)). He also led the pueblo to the principles of agreement with Santa Fe County in 2017 and to the Indian Land Claims Settlement in 2006 ([New Mexico Governor's Office](https://www.governor.state.nm.us/2023/02/03/governor-lujan-grisham-announces-new-secretary-of-indian-affairs-department/)). Since 2018 he has owned and operated Mountain+Associates, LLC, a state-tribal affairs consulting firm ([New Mexico Governor's Office](https://www.governor.state.nm.us/2023/02/03/governor-lujan-grisham-announces-new-secretary-of-indian-affairs-department/)).

## New Mexico Indian Affairs secretary — and a contested appointment

Governor Michelle Lujan Grisham announced Mountain's appointment as secretary of the New Mexico Indian Affairs Department on 3 February 2023, praising his history as a leader in San Ildefonso and his expertise in state and tribal relations ([New Mexico Governor's Office](https://www.governor.state.nm.us/2023/02/03/governor-lujan-grisham-announces-new-secretary-of-indian-affairs-department/)). The appointment immediately drew opposition from members of the state's Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women and Relatives Task Force, which was housed in the department, because Mountain had been indicted in 2008 on charges of criminal sexual penetration, kidnapping, and aggravated battery against a household member ([New Mexico In Depth](https://www.iwmf.org/reporting/controversial-indian-affairs-secretary-leaving-agency/)). That case was dismissed in 2010 because prosecutors said they had insufficient evidence to proceed to trial, and the governor's office urged critics to "respect the judicial process and acknowledge the results" ([New Mexico In Depth](https://www.iwmf.org/reporting/controversial-indian-affairs-secretary-leaving-agency/); [Associated Press](https://srnnews.com/embattled-figure-in-native-politics-resigns-as-chairman-of-pueblo-governors-council-in-new-mexico/)).

Mountain's Senate confirmation process stalled, and after about ten months he left the department in December 2023 to become the governor's senior policy adviser for tribal affairs, with Deputy Secretary Josett Monette succeeding him as cabinet secretary ([Associated Press](https://srnnews.com/embattled-figure-in-native-politics-resigns-as-chairman-of-pueblo-governors-council-in-new-mexico/); [New Mexico In Depth](https://www.iwmf.org/reporting/controversial-indian-affairs-secretary-leaving-agency/)). Neither the governor's office nor the department gave a reason for the departure ([New Mexico In Depth](https://www.iwmf.org/reporting/controversial-indian-affairs-secretary-leaving-agency/)). During his tenure the administration disbanded the task force whose members had opposed his appointment, and Mountain told lawmakers he had "failed" in not putting together a "comprehensive plan" and a "measurable timeline of actions" on the missing and murdered Indigenous people crisis ([New Mexico In Depth](https://www.iwmf.org/reporting/controversial-indian-affairs-secretary-leaving-agency/)).

## Chairman of the All Pueblo Council of Governors

Mountain was chairman of the All Pueblo Council of Governors in April 2025, when the council applauded the reintroduction of legislation to permanently protect federal lands in the Greater Chaco Region from future oil and gas leasing ([All Pueblo Council of Governors](https://publicinterestnetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2025.04.10-APCG_Chaco-Press-Release_-UPDATED.pdf)). In that statement he credited the renewed push to "countless meetings, cultural resource studies, and tireless advocacy" and thanked Senator Ben Ray Luján, Representative Teresa Leger Fernández, and the wider New Mexico Congressional Delegation ([All Pueblo Council of Governors](https://publicinterestnetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2025.04.10-APCG_Chaco-Press-Release_-UPDATED.pdf)). The campaign concerned Chaco Canyon, a [UNESCO](/organizations/unesco/) World Heritage Site, and the broader ten-mile zone around it ([All Pueblo Council of Governors](https://publicinterestnetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2025.04.10-APCG_Chaco-Press-Release_-UPDATED.pdf)).

His chairmanship ended abruptly later that year. Days after Pojoaque Pueblo police arrested him on suspicion of driving while intoxicated at a tribal casino, Mountain submitted his resignation letter to the council, effective immediately, and on the same day terminated his work as a contract adviser to the state Indian Affairs Department ([Associated Press](https://srnnews.com/embattled-figure-in-native-politics-resigns-as-chairman-of-pueblo-governors-council-in-new-mexico/)). He had been held over a weekend at a Santa Fe County jail after declining a field sobriety test, according to an online booking log and the Pojoaque Pueblo Tribal Court cited by the Associated Press ([Associated Press](https://srnnews.com/embattled-figure-in-native-politics-resigns-as-chairman-of-pueblo-governors-council-in-new-mexico/)). By 20 May 2026, the council's own release named [Joey Sanchez](/people/joey-sanchez/) of the Pueblo of Santa Ana as its chairman, giving the outer bracket for the succession ([All Pueblo Council of Governors](https://apcg.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/APCG_Chaco_PR5.20.2026-.pdf)).
