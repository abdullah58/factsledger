## Overview

Candace Parker is an American former professional basketball player whose 16-season WNBA career produced three championships with three different teams, two league MVP awards and two Olympic gold medals — a combination no other player in league history matches ([Associated Press](https://gmg-wdiv-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/sports/2024/04/28/candace-parker-a-3-time-wnba-champion-and-2-time-league-mvp-announces-retirement/)). Drafted No. 1 overall by the Los Angeles Sparks in 2008, she won Rookie of the Year and MVP in the same season, the only WNBA player ever to do so, then added titles with the Chicago Sky in 2021 and the Las Vegas Aces in 2023 ([Associated Press](https://gmg-wdiv-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/sports/2024/04/28/candace-parker-a-3-time-wnba-champion-and-2-time-league-mvp-announces-retirement/)). After retiring in April 2024 she became president of adidas Women's Basketball, and she had already entered the investing world years earlier as a backer of the alternative-asset platform [Alt](/organizations/alt/) in its $75 million Series B round ([adidas](https://news.adidas.com/editors-picks/candace-parker-becomes-president-of-adidas-women-s-basketball/s/3c9e4178-c33c-470a-b262-b75b3566f812); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/11/collectible-card-trading-platform-alt-raises-75m-as-it-eyes-other-asset-categories-and-preps-its-mobile-launch/)).

This profile covers the basketball player and investor Candace Parker — the Tennessee Lady Vol, three-team WNBA champion and adidas executive — and follows her from Naperville, Illinois, through college, three franchises, the Alt investment and the dual jersey retirements of 2025.

## From Naperville to Tennessee

Parker was born in St. Louis, Missouri, but moved to Naperville, Illinois, at age 2, and she starred at Naperville Central High School, leading her team to Class AA state titles in 2003 and 2004 while scoring 2,786 points and earning two national player-of-the-year honours ([Sporting News](https://www.sportingnews.com/us/ncaa-football/news/where-candace-parker-gameday-guest-picker/7f88e7f1b916cd152d2abca0)). She then played at Tennessee, where she took a redshirt season as a freshman in 2004 before amassing 2,137 career points and 972 rebounds from 2005 to 2008 ([Sporting News](https://www.sportingnews.com/us/ncaa-football/news/where-candace-parker-gameday-guest-picker/7f88e7f1b916cd152d2abca0)). Under the late Pat Summitt she played on Tennessee's last two national championship teams, in 2007 and 2008, leaving with one year of eligibility remaining ([Associated Press](https://gmg-wdiv-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/sports/2024/04/28/candace-parker-a-3-time-wnba-champion-and-2-time-league-mvp-announces-retirement/)). In 2006 she became the first woman ever to dunk in an NCAA Tournament game, and she was a two-time Wooden Award winner ([Field Level Media](https://fieldlevelmedia.com/news/wnba-great-candace-parker-retiring-after-16-seasons/); [CBS Sports](https://sportsfly.cbsistatic.com/wnba/news/sky-to-retire-candace-parkers-no-3-jersey-wnba-legend-led-chicago-to-only-championship-in-franchise-history/)).

Her college dominance translated immediately to the professional game. As the Sparks' No. 1 pick she won Rookie of the Year and league MVP in the same 2008 season, averaging 18.5 points, 9.5 rebounds and 3.4 assists while helping the Sparks to a 10-win improvement ([Associated Press](https://gmg-wdiv-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/sports/2024/04/28/candace-parker-a-3-time-wnba-champion-and-2-time-league-mvp-announces-retirement/)). She earned her second MVP award in 2013, and her trophy case also includes the 2013 All-Star Game MVP, the 2016 Finals MVP and the 2020 Defensive Player of the Year award ([Associated Press](https://gmg-wdiv-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/sports/2024/04/28/candace-parker-a-3-time-wnba-champion-and-2-time-league-mvp-announces-retirement/); [Field Level Media](https://fieldlevelmedia.com/news/wnba-great-candace-parker-retiring-after-16-seasons/)). Alongside her club career she won Olympic gold medals with Team USA at Beijing in 2008 and London in 2012, and she began working in broadcasting during her playing career, as an analyst for the NCAA Tournament with CBS Sports and for NBA TV ([Associated Press](https://gmg-wdiv-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/sports/2024/04/28/candace-parker-a-3-time-wnba-champion-and-2-time-league-mvp-announces-retirement/)).

## Three titles with three teams

Parker spent her first 13 seasons with the Sparks, winning her first WNBA title with them in 2016 ([Associated Press](https://gmg-wdiv-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/sports/2024/04/28/candace-parker-a-3-time-wnba-champion-and-2-time-league-mvp-announces-retirement/)). In Los Angeles she averaged 16.9 points, 8.6 rebounds and 3.9 assists, set the franchise assists record at 1,331, and ranks second in franchise rebounds and third in points ([NBC Sports](https://www.nbcsports.com/wnba/news/sparks-to-retire-candace-parkers-no-3-jersey-when-team-faces-chicago-on-june-29)). In early 2021 she left for her hometown Chicago Sky, and by the end of that summer Chicago was a WNBA champion for the first time, beating Phoenix in the Finals ([CBS Sports](https://sportsfly.cbsistatic.com/wnba/news/sky-to-retire-candace-parkers-no-3-jersey-wnba-legend-led-chicago-to-only-championship-in-franchise-history/)). She played her final season, in 2023, with the Las Vegas Aces, winning a third championship and becoming the only player in league history to win titles with three different teams ([Associated Press](https://gmg-wdiv-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/sports/2024/04/28/candace-parker-a-3-time-wnba-champion-and-2-time-league-mvp-announces-retirement/); [CBS Sports](https://sportsfly.cbsistatic.com/wnba/news/sky-to-retire-candace-parkers-no-3-jersey-wnba-legend-led-chicago-to-only-championship-in-franchise-history/)).

Across 410 games she totalled 6,574 points (ninth in WNBA history), 3,467 rebounds (third), 1,634 assists (seventh) and 619 blocks (fifth), with seven All-Star selections ([Field Level Media](https://fieldlevelmedia.com/news/wnba-great-candace-parker-retiring-after-16-seasons/)). The career totals are the citation-magnet here: a player who ranks top-ten all-time in points, rebounds, assists and blocks while winning everywhere she went is a statistical profile few athletes in any sport can match, and it explains why two franchises retired her jersey within two months of each other in 2025. She announced her retirement on 28 April 2024 after 16 seasons, writing that a foot injury that had required surgery in July 2023 — one of 10 surgeries over her career — had left her unwilling to "cheat the game" by playing on ([Associated Press](https://gmg-wdiv-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/sports/2024/04/28/candace-parker-a-3-time-wnba-champion-and-2-time-league-mvp-announces-retirement/)).

## Investor and executive: Alt, adidas and the 2025 honours

Parker's investing career is the angle most sports recaps miss. In November 2021, the same year she delivered Chicago its first WNBA title, she backed Alt's $75 million Series B round, which valued the trading-card platform at over $325 million and was led by Spearhead ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/11/collectible-card-trading-platform-alt-raises-75m-as-it-eyes-other-asset-categories-and-preps-its-mobile-launch/)). Built In independently corroborated the round and Parker's participation, naming her among the sports legends in the round alongside Tom Brady and Kevin Durant ([Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/alt-raises-75m-series-b-hiring-alternative-assets)). Alt's model — research, trade and vault graded trading cards, taking a 1.5% cut per sale, with over $70 million of cards already in its vault at the time of the round — made it a bet on collectibles as an asset class, not on a team or league ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/11/collectible-card-trading-platform-alt-raises-75m-as-it-eyes-other-asset-categories-and-preps-its-mobile-launch/)). No individual investment amount for Parker was disclosed, so this profile claims participation only, not a sum.

Retirement opened a second executive chapter rather than an exit from the sport. After 16 years as a brand partner, adidas announced Parker as president of adidas Women's Basketball, a role framed around representation, access and innovation for women's sports ([adidas](https://news.adidas.com/editors-picks/candace-parker-becomes-president-of-adidas-women-s-basketball/s/3c9e4178-c33c-470a-b262-b75b3566f812)). The honours then followed in quick succession: the Sparks retired her No. 3 jersey on 29 June 2025 at Crypto.com Arena, making her only the third Spark so honoured after Lisa Leslie and Penny Toler, and Tennessee had already retired her No. 3 in 2014 ([NBC Sports](https://www.nbcsports.com/wnba/news/sparks-to-retire-candace-parkers-no-3-jersey-when-team-faces-chicago-on-june-29)). The Sky followed on 25 August 2025, retiring her jersey when hosting the Aces — her second jersey retirement in a single summer, and a homecoming honour for a player who said Chicago raised her ([CBS Sports](https://sportsfly.cbsistatic.com/wnba/news/sky-to-retire-candace-parkers-no-3-jersey-wnba-legend-led-chicago-to-only-championship-in-franchise-history/)).
