## Overview

Sundar Pichai is the chief executive of [Google](https://factsledger.com/organizations/google/) and its parent company Alphabet, a position he has held since 2019, and a member of Alphabet's Board of Directors since July 2017 ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/new-updates/google-and-alphabet-ceo-sundar-pichai-is-returning-to-stanford-university-after-31-years-says-excited-to-celebrate-this-milestone/articleshow/129998364.cms?from=mdr)). But the résumé version misses what made his 2025–2026 genuinely unprecedented: in April 2025 he spent 90 minutes on a witness stand telling a federal judge that the Justice Department's proposed antitrust remedies would amount to a "de facto divestiture" of Google Search, and in August 2026 he personally announced the restructuring of Google DeepMind that placed its new operational chief directly under him ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-google-doj-antitrust-trial-sundar-pichai/); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/08/05/demis-hassabis-deepmind/)).

## The courtroom warning: "de facto divestiture of search"

The testimony came in the remedies phase of the U.S. government's antitrust case against Google, following Judge Amit Mehta's August 2024 ruling that Google "is a monopolist, and it has acted as one to maintain its monopoly" in search ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-google-doj-antitrust-trial-sundar-pichai/)). The Department of Justice proposed forcing Google to license its search-behavior data and web index to rivals at "marginal cost," to license its search technology, and potentially to divest assets like the Chrome browser ([bestmediainfo](https://bestmediainfo.com/mediainfo/mediainfo-digital/google-ceo-sundar-pichai-warns-dojs-antitrust-plan-could-kill-google-search-9020697)).

Pichai, testifying on April 30, 2025, described the data-sharing proposal as "so far-reaching, so extraordinary" that it would fundamentally reshape Google's entire search operation, warning it would make it "unviable" to continue investing in search research as Google had for two decades ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-google-doj-antitrust-trial-sundar-pichai/)). "It's not clear to me how to fund all the innovation we do if we were to give all of it away at marginal cost," he said, noting that Google had invested $49 billion in research and development the previous year, much of it in search and AI ([bestmediainfo](https://bestmediainfo.com/mediainfo/mediainfo-digital/google-ceo-sundar-pichai-warns-dojs-antitrust-plan-could-kill-google-search-9020697)). He also warned of privacy consequences: "People search on Google in their most vulnerable moments, and there seems to be no privacy protections" ([bestmediainfo](https://bestmediainfo.com/mediainfo/mediainfo-digital/google-ceo-sundar-pichai-warns-dojs-antitrust-plan-could-kill-google-search-9020697)).

The numbers behind the dispute were stark. The government argued Google's dominance was fueled by exclusive default-search deals with companies like Apple and Samsung that cost Google $26 billion in 2021 alone ([bestmediainfo](https://bestmediainfo.com/mediainfo/mediainfo-digital/google-ceo-sundar-pichai-warns-dojs-antitrust-plan-could-kill-google-search-9020697)). Pichai defended Google's stewardship of Chrome, which the DOJ sought to spin off, arguing that the browser's $1 billion annual investment had driven unmatched innovation ([bestmediainfo](https://bestmediainfo.com/mediainfo/mediainfo-digital/google-ceo-sundar-pichai-warns-dojs-antitrust-plan-could-kill-google-search-9020697)). During his 90 minutes on the stand he also pushed back on the DOJ's portrayal of Google as unstoppable, citing the ill-fated Google Plus as proof the company doesn't always win — and confirmed Google was close to a non-exclusive deal to offer Gemini on Apple devices as an alternative to ChatGPT ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-google-doj-antitrust-trial-sundar-pichai/)).

## Reorganizing AI leadership: the August 2026 handover

On August 5, 2026, Pichai announced the most significant leadership change at Google DeepMind since its 2023 formation: [Demis Hassabis](/people/demis-hassabis/) would hand over "day-to-day operational responsibilities" to become Chair of Google DeepMind and Chief Scientist of Alphabet, freeing him to focus on AGI strategy ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/08/05/demis-hassabis-deepmind/)). Day-to-day leadership went to Koray Kavukcuoglu, the division's former chief technology officer, who became senior vice president **reporting directly to Pichai** — overseeing "Gemini model development, Frontier AI research, and the Gemini app and developer teams" ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/08/05/demis-hassabis-deepmind/)).

Pichai's statement on Kavukcuoglu read as a deliberate endorsement: "Koray has been at DeepMind since its early days, and over his 13 years there, he has started our deep learning team and led the way on breakthroughs like WaveNet and DQN. I look forward to seeing him lead GDM into this next chapter" ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/08/05/demis-hassabis-deepmind/)). The same day, Pichai also announced that Jeff Dean was leaving Google to launch an independent public benefit corporation called Discovery Loop, with Gemini co-lead Oriol Vinyals joining him ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/08/05/demis-hassabis-deepmind/)).

## Chennai to Stanford to Google

Pichai grew up in Chennai, India, and studied engineering at the Indian Institute of Technology Kharagpur, where he received an Institute Silver Medal ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/new-updates/google-and-alphabet-ceo-sundar-pichai-is-returning-to-stanford-university-after-31-years-says-excited-to-celebrate-this-milestone/articleshow/129998364.cms?from=mdr)). He earned a master's in materials science and engineering from Stanford in 1995, followed by an MBA from the Wharton School, where he was named a Siebel Scholar and a Palmer Scholar ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/new-updates/google-and-alphabet-ceo-sundar-pichai-is-returning-to-stanford-university-after-31-years-says-excited-to-celebrate-this-milestone/articleshow/129998364.cms?from=mdr)).

He joined Google in 2004 and helped lead the development of Google Toolbar and then Google Chrome, which grew to become the world's most popular internet browser ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/new-updates/google-and-alphabet-ceo-sundar-pichai-is-returning-to-stanford-university-after-31-years-says-excited-to-celebrate-this-milestone/articleshow/129998364.cms?from=mdr)). On June 14, 2026, he returned to Stanford to deliver the university's 135th Commencement address in Stanford Stadium — 31 years after his graduation — his first in-person commencement speech (his only previous one had been the virtual "Dear Class of 2020" during the pandemic) ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/new-updates/google-and-alphabet-ceo-sundar-pichai-is-returning-to-stanford-university-after-31-years-says-excited-to-celebrate-this-milestone/articleshow/129998364.cms?from=mdr)). Stanford President Jonathan Levin praised him as "a deeply thoughtful leader who has a vivid sense for how technology can improve society and transform individual lives" ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/new-updates/google-and-alphabet-ceo-sundar-pichai-is-returning-to-stanford-university-after-31-years-says-excited-to-celebrate-this-milestone/articleshow/129998364.cms?from=mdr)).
