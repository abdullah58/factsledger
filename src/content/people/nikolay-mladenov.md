## The man warning that Gaza's window is closing

In September 2026, Nikolay Mladenov stood before the Hili Forum in Abu Dhabi and delivered the bluntest assessment of his tenure running the Board of Peace's Gaza work: if Israel launched another offensive, it would shut the Gulf states out of financing Gaza's reconstruction and leave Israel to reoccupy the territory. "It would certainly exclude anyone in the Gulf, be that the UAE, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, anyone else, [from] financing any reconstruction in Gaza," he said, adding that Israel "would have to reoccupy Gaza and take full responsibility for everything that follows from that." His verdict on the alternative was starker still: "If Gaza does not move forward, the two-state solution will become practically impossible." ([The Media Line](https://themedialine.org/top-stories/mladenov-says-impasse-in-gaza-reconstruction-could-end-2-state-solution/))

Mladenov is a Bulgarian diplomat whose career moved from Sofia's cabinet rooms to the United Nations' hardest Middle East posting and, in 2026, to the center of the American-led effort to govern postwar Gaza. *The Jerusalem Post* described him in August 2026 as the Board of Peace's director-general; *The Media Line* the following month called him its high representative for Gaza. (Some outlets render his first name "Nickolay.") The title varies by outlet; the job does not: turning a 15-point Gaza roadmap into something both [Hamas](/organizations/hamas/) and Israel will actually implement. ([The Jerusalem Post](http://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-905611); [The Media Line](https://themedialine.org/top-stories/mladenov-says-impasse-in-gaza-reconstruction-could-end-2-state-solution/))

## From Sofia to the United Nations

Mladenov founded the European Institute in Sofia and directed it until 2001, when he was elected to Bulgaria's National Assembly on the ticket of the centre-right Union of Democratic Forces. In 2007 he was elected a Member of the European Parliament, where he met his wife Gergana, the mother of their three children. ([DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/world/trump-pick-for-gaza-board-nickolay-mladenov-frequently-worked-to-ease-west-asia-tensions))

He entered government in 2009 as Bulgaria's defence minister, serving about a year, then became foreign minister from 2010 to 2013 — a tenure that coincided with the Arab Spring uprisings and Syria's descent into civil war. In 2012 he hosted in Bulgaria the first-ever meeting of the Syrian opposition, bringing together representatives of factions opposed to Bashar Assad; the forum ended with a joint declaration that marked the start of structured dialogue between the groups. ([DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/world/trump-pick-for-gaza-board-nickolay-mladenov-frequently-worked-to-ease-west-asia-tensions); [Al Jazeera Forum](https://archive-forum.aljazeera.net/speakers/nikolay-mladenov))

He joined the United Nations in 2013 as Special Representative for Iraq and head of the UN Assistance Mission for Iraq, serving until March 2015, when then-Secretary-General Ban Ki-moon appointed him the organization's top Middle East envoy. As UN Special Coordinator for the Middle East Peace Process from 2015 to 2020, he helped defuse cross-border violence between Israel and Hamas and kept alive the idea of a negotiated Israeli-Palestinian settlement. His master's degrees are in War Studies from King's College London and International Relations from the University of National and World Economy in Sofia. ([DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/world/trump-pick-for-gaza-board-nickolay-mladenov-frequently-worked-to-ease-west-asia-tensions); [Al Jazeera Forum](https://archive-forum.aljazeera.net/speakers/nikolay-mladenov))

What distinguished him, colleagues say, was access to both sides. Milen Keremedchiev, a former diplomat and Middle East expert, said Mladenov's later appointment reflected "significant contributions to peace," adding that he had earned the trust of both Israel and the Palestinians. Retired Israeli diplomat Alon Bar, who worked with him during his UN tenure, said serving as a UN envoy was difficult given Israel's long history of rocky relations with the world body, but that Mladenov "was able to create a relationship of trust with the political echelon in Israel, including Prime Minister Netanyahu" — while building confidence on the Palestinian side as well. Bar described his diplomacy as focused "not only on declarations and statements, but on trying to connect and trying to find bridges and trying to see where are the places where the positions of Israel, the Palestinians could meet." In February 2021, the Palestinian president awarded him the Grand Star of the Order of Jerusalem for his peace efforts. ([DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/world/trump-pick-for-gaza-board-nickolay-mladenov-frequently-worked-to-ease-west-asia-tensions))

## The Gaza mandate

In January 2026, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu announced that Mladenov had been designated director-general of the proposed US-led Board of Peace for Gaza, an appointment a US official confirmed. The unproven international body was tasked with supervising a new technocratic Palestinian government, the disarmament of Hamas, the deployment of an international security force, further Israeli troop pullbacks, and reconstruction. By then Mladenov was based in the UAE, where he had been appointed to run the Anwar Gargash Diplomatic Academy. ([DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/world/trump-pick-for-gaza-board-nickolay-mladenov-frequently-worked-to-ease-west-asia-tensions); [The Media Line](https://themedialine.org/top-stories/mladenov-says-impasse-in-gaza-reconstruction-could-end-2-state-solution/))

Mladenov presented the roadmap's 15 principles to the UN Security Council on May 21. On July 30, President Trump and the Board said Hamas and other Palestinian factions had accepted the roadmap after seven months of talks mediated by Egypt, Turkey, and Qatar — though Hamas described its acceptance as conditional and linked to phased disarmament, and Netanyahu rejected the plan in August. Under the plan, civilian and security authority would transfer to the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza, a technocratic body led by Ali Shaath, while an International Stabilization Force would support Palestinian police, collect weapons, and separate the two sides. ([The Media Line](https://themedialine.org/top-stories/mladenov-says-impasse-in-gaza-reconstruction-could-end-2-state-solution/))

The pivotal encounter came on August 16, 2026, in Cairo. US envoy [Jared Kushner](/people/jared-kushner/), former British prime minister [Tony Blair](/people/tony-blair/), and Mladenov met with a Hamas delegation to discuss implementing the Board's 15-point roadmap. The objective, a source told *The Jerusalem Post*, was to translate the steps in Mladenov's roadmap into concrete, verifiable actions aimed at maintaining the ceasefire and removing Hamas from power in Gaza. The meeting also covered transferring all governing responsibilities to the technocratic committee, deploying the International Stabilization Force, decommissioning Hamas weapons and infrastructure, Israeli withdrawal, and reconstruction. The *Post* reported the session "very productive," with Hamas asking Kushner to tell Trump the group remained committed to disarming — and Kushner answering that Washington wanted real efforts, "not just words." ([The Jerusalem Post](http://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-905611))

The next day, Kushner flew to Israel with Mladenov, Blair, and Board of Peace adviser Aryeh Lightstone to meet Netanyahu. What emerged reversed Mladenov's negotiated sequence: Israeli officials said the military would not redeploy until Hamas was fully disarmed and that no reconstruction would begin before then. Netanyahu's office did not publicly comment on Mladenov's description of the talks. ([The Media Line](https://themedialine.org/top-stories/mladenov-says-impasse-in-gaza-reconstruction-could-end-2-state-solution/))

At the Board's inaugural Washington meeting on February 19, the UAE had pledged $1.2 billion toward Gaza reconstruction, with Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and Kuwait each pledging $1 billion; Trump said nine countries had promised $7 billion and announced a separate US pledge of $10 billion. By September, Mladenov said, little funding had moved — and the pledges would cover less than a quarter of the $71.4 billion the EU, UN, and World Bank estimated Gaza would need over the next decade. The technocratic committee remained in Cairo, no international force had deployed, and Israel controlled more than half the territory. "It is extremely difficult to create a sequencing between two sides who have every reason in the world not to trust each other," Mladenov said. ([The Media Line](https://themedialine.org/top-stories/mladenov-says-impasse-in-gaza-reconstruction-could-end-2-state-solution/))

The work connects to the [Board of Peace](/organizations/board-of-peace/) itself, the body whose Gaza roadmap he was hired to implement.
