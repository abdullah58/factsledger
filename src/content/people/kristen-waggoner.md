## Overview

Kristen K. Waggoner is an American attorney who has served as CEO, president and chief counsel of [Alliance Defending Freedom](/organizations/alliance-defending-freedom/) (ADF) since October 1, 2022 ([The Christian Post](http://assets.christianpost.com/news/adf-leader-lists-three-big-threats-to-religious-liberty-in-the-united-states.html)). Before taking the organization's top job she was its general counsel and the lawyer who argued two of its most prominent Supreme Court cases, *Masterpiece Cakeshop* and *303 Creative*, winning both ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/press-release/adf-names-new-president-ceo/?sourcecode=10024064)). ADF describes itself as the world's largest legal organization dedicated to advancing religious liberty, free speech, parental rights and the sanctity of life, with more than 450 team members across 11 offices ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/profile/kristen-waggoner/)).

Waggoner grew up in a religious home and says her faith guides every aspect of her life, including the belief that government has no right to coerce people to say things they do not believe — a belief she extends to the rights of those who are not religious ([The Christian Post](http://assets.christianpost.com/news/adf-leader-lists-three-big-threats-to-religious-liberty-in-the-united-states.html)). She attended Northwest University, an Assemblies of God school in Kirkland, Washington, and Regent University School of Law in Virginia Beach, where she is listed as class of 1997 ([AG News](https://news.ag.org/en/News/Called-Before-the-Court); [Regent University](https://www.regent.edu/about-regent/impact/carrying-the-torch/)). After law school she clerked at the Washington Supreme Court and practiced for more than 15 years at a Seattle law firm, where she also worked as an ADF allied attorney on Washington and Ninth Circuit litigation before joining ADF full time in 2013 ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/profile/kristen-waggoner/); [The Christian Post](http://assets.christianpost.com/news/adf-leader-lists-three-big-threats-to-religious-liberty-in-the-united-states.html)).

## Three Supreme Court arguments

Waggoner's courtroom reputation was built on a sequence of First Amendment cases about artists and compelled speech. In *Masterpiece Cakeshop v. Colorado Civil Rights Commission*, she argued the appeal for baker Jack Phillips, who had declined to create a custom cake celebrating a same-sex wedding ([AG News](https://news.ag.org/en/News/Called-Before-the-Court)). The Court ruled 7-2 for Phillips on June 4, 2018, on the narrow ground that the Colorado Civil Rights Commission had shown hostility toward his religious beliefs — Justice Anthony Kennedy wrote that the Commission's consideration of the case "was neither tolerant nor respectful of his religious beliefs" ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/article/create-freely-story-behind-artists-who-fought-free-speech/)). The decision deliberately left open the broader free-speech question of whether public-accommodation laws could compel artists to express messages against their consciences, and that unanswered question is what drove the next case ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/article/create-freely-story-behind-artists-who-fought-free-speech/)).

ADF's 2022 announcement of Waggoner's promotion records that she had personally argued and won two Supreme Court victories by then, *Masterpiece Cakeshop* and *Uzuegbunam v. Preczewski* (2021), and was slated to argue her third, *303 Creative v. Elenis* ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/press-release/adf-names-new-president-ceo/?sourcecode=10024064)). In *303 Creative*, ADF filed a pre-enforcement challenge in September 2016 for Colorado web designer Lorie Smith, who wanted to offer wedding websites only for marriages between one man and one woman; the Tenth Circuit ruled for Colorado 2-1 in 2021, the Supreme Court agreed to hear the case in February 2022, and Waggoner represented Smith at oral arguments in December 2022 ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/article/create-freely-story-behind-artists-who-fought-free-speech/)). On June 30, 2023, the Court ruled 6-3 for Smith, with Justice Neil Gorsuch writing that the First Amendment prevents Colorado from forcing a web designer to create designs conveying messages the designer disagrees with ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/303_Creative_LLC_v._Elenis)). The opinion turned on stipulated facts: Smith serves customers regardless of sexual orientation but will not create expressions contradicting her beliefs for anyone, whether that means encouraging violence, demeaning another person, or promoting views inconsistent with her faith ([U.S. Supreme Court](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/600/21-476/)). The contrast between the two wins is the point: *Masterpiece* was a ruling about how a regulator behaved, while *303 Creative* was a ruling about what a government may compel an artist to say.

## Leading ADF: succession, scale, and the Chiles distinction

Waggoner became president and CEO on October 1, 2022, following Michael Farris, who had joined ADF in January 2017, and Alan Sears, who served 23 years as founding CEO; the board voted unanimously on August 11, 2022, and Sears, who had hired Waggoner in 2013, supported the decision ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/press-release/adf-names-new-president-ceo/?sourcecode=10024064)). The Christian Post independently confirmed the appointment and Farris's approval of his successor ([The Christian Post](http://assets.christianpost.com/news/adf-leader-lists-three-big-threats-to-religious-liberty-in-the-united-states.html)). She continued as general counsel, a dual role the organization now styles chief counsel, and told the Christian Post her role would expand from U.S. litigation to the international work and other programs outside litigation and advocacy ([The Christian Post](http://assets.christianpost.com/news/adf-leader-lists-three-big-threats-to-religious-liberty-in-the-united-states.html)). At the time of her promotion she described ADF's posture as proactive rather than defensive, and pointed to state-level expansion and parental rights as growth areas ([The Christian Post](http://assets.christianpost.com/news/adf-leader-lists-three-big-threats-to-religious-liberty-in-the-united-states.html)).

Under her leadership ADF continued its Supreme Court run, but the division of labor at the lectern changed, and the profile records it precisely. In *Chiles v. Salazar*, the challenge to Colorado's counseling restriction, ADF's timeline shows the suit filed in September 2022 by allied attorney Barry Arrington, a Tenth Circuit loss in September 2024, certiorari granted in March 2025, and an 8-1 Supreme Court win on March 31, 2026 — with ADF chief legal counsel Jim Campbell, not Waggoner, presenting oral arguments in October 2025 ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/article/supreme-court-upholds-free-speech-in-counseling/)). ADF also served on Mississippi's legal team in *Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization*, the decision that overturned *Roe v. Wade*; Waggoner did not argue *Dobbs* herself, and this profile does not credit her with doing so ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/press-release/adf-names-new-president-ceo/?sourcecode=10024064)). ADF's own current bio puts its Supreme Court wins at 20, with 19 under Waggoner's leadership, while its international arm's bio says 15 of its own cases plus a role in nearly 80 victories — the two counts measure different things, and the profile reports both attributions rather than merging them into one number ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/profile/kristen-waggoner/); [ADF International](https://ADFInternational.org/our-team/kristen-waggoner)).

Beyond litigation, ADF credits Waggoner with leading ADF International, which operates in more than 80 countries and litigates before international tribunals and national courts ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/profile/kristen-waggoner/)). In 2025 the Russell Kirk Center for Cultural Renewal awarded her the Richard D. McLellan Prize for Advancing Free Speech and Expression, and President Donald Trump appointed her to the advisory board of legal experts to the Religious Liberty Commission ([Alliance Defending Freedom](https://adflegal.org/profile/kristen-waggoner/)). Regent University's 2026 magazine profile notes that ADF lawyers under her leadership were then contending cases on six continents, including four pending before the U.S. Supreme Court and others before high courts in Nigeria, Finland, Turkey and Algeria ([Regent University](https://www.regent.edu/about-regent/impact/carrying-the-torch/)).

This profile does not claim that Waggoner argued *Chiles v. Salazar* (Jim Campbell did) or *Dobbs* (ADF served on Mississippi's team; she did not argue it). It makes no claim about Waggoner's private life beyond her own public statements of faith and calling.
