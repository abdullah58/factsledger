## Overview

Cade Carradine is an American actor billed as "Boxer Ray" opposite his father
Keith Carradine as "Ray" in Alan Rudolph's *Ray Meets Helen* (2017) — a son
playing the younger version of his father's character
([Box Office Mojo](https://www.boxofficemojo.com/title/tt5813010/credits/)).
Born July 19, 1982
([Asian World Film Festival](https://www.asianworldfilmfest.org/2017-jury-members)),
he is the son of Keith Carradine, the grandson of John Carradine, and the
half-brother of Martha Plimpton
([FilmReference](http://www.filmreference.com/film/77/Keith-Carradine.html)),
with a supporting role in Turkey's Oscar-nominated *Ayla: The Daughter of War*
([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)).

The Carradines are one of American film's most tangled family trees, and the
tangle is exactly why this profile starts with disambiguation. Cade is
**Keith Carradine's son** — not David Carradine's. Keith, born August 8, 1949,
in San Mateo, California, married Sandra Will on February 6, 1982 (they later
divorced); the children of that marriage are Cade Richmond and Sorel Johannah
([FilmReference](http://www.filmreference.com/film/77/Keith-Carradine.html)).
Martha Plimpton is Cade's half-sister, Keith's daughter with actress Shelley
Plimpton ([FilmReference](http://www.filmreference.com/film/77/Keith-Carradine.html)).
Keith is himself the son of actor John Richmond Reed — born Richmond Reed
Carradine, known professionally as John Carradine — and Sonia Sorel
([FilmReference](http://www.filmreference.com/film/77/Keith-Carradine.html);
[FilmReference](http://www.filmreference.com/film/50/John-Carradine.html)),
and the brother of actors David and Robert Carradine
([FilmReference](http://www.filmreference.com/film/77/Keith-Carradine.html)),
which makes David and Robert Cade's uncles and John Carradine and Sonia Sorel
his grandparents. Any biography that treats "Carradine" as one interchangeable
brand is selling the wrong family member.

## "Boxer Ray" — the father-son casting nobody talks about

Here is the gap in the record this profile exists to close. *Ray Meets
Helen*, Alan Rudolph's return to filmmaking after about fifteen years away
from the cinema, centers on Ray — played by Keith Carradine — a washed-up
former boxer who now does insurance legwork and mourns his ruined boxing
career ([Slant Magazine](https://www.slantmagazine.com/film/ray-meets-helen/);
[Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/movie/ray-meets-helen/)).
Box Office Mojo's full credits list Keith Carradine as "Ray" and, directly
below him, **Cade Carradine as "Boxer Ray"**
([Box Office Mojo](https://www.boxofficemojo.com/title/tt5813010/credits/)).
FilmReference's Keith Carradine biography confirms Cade Richmond Carradine is
Keith's son ([FilmReference](http://www.filmreference.com/film/77/Keith-Carradine.html)).
Put those two independently verified facts together and the casting becomes
remarkable: father and son on the same call sheet, the son embodying the
father's character in his boxing prime. Yet the reviews surfaced in this
pass — Slant's full review, and the Metacritic pull-quotes from the
*New York Times* (90), *The Film Stage* (83), the *Hollywood Reporter*, and
the *Los Angeles Times* (40 each)
([Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/movie/ray-meets-helen/)) — do not
mention it.

The film itself divided critics cleanly in half. Metacritic records a
Metascore of 64 — "Generally Favorable" — built on just five reviews: three
positive, two mixed, none negative
([Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/movie/ray-meets-helen/)). The
stat, the source, and the implication: a 64 from five critics means the film's
entire critical afterlife rests on a handful of sympathetic write-ups, not on
any broad consensus. It opened theatrically on May 4, 2018, through Moonstone
Entertainment as a 113-minute R-rated drama ([Slant Magazine](https://www.slantmagazine.com/film/ray-meets-helen/);
[Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/movie/ray-meets-helen/)), with Sondra
Locke as Helen, Keith David as the insurance executive Harve, and Jennifer
Tilly as Ray's ex-wife Ginger ([Slant Magazine](https://www.slantmagazine.com/film/ray-meets-helen/)).

## Ayla — the American face in Turkey's Oscar campaign

Cade Carradine's other anchor credit sits inside Turkey's state-backed Oscar
campaign. *Ayla: The Daughter of War*, directed by Can Ülkay from a screenplay
by Yiğit Güralp, dramatizes the true story of Turkish sergeant Süleyman
Dilbirliği and the Korean War orphan he named Ayla — with [İsmail Hacıoğlu](/people/ismail-hacioglu/) and
Çetin Tekindor as the young and elderly Dilbirliği, and Korean actresses Kim
Seol and Lee Kyung-jin as the young and adult Ayla
([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)).
Hürriyet Daily News names **"Cade Carradine and Eric Roberts" among the
supporting roles**
([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)),
in a Turkish–Korean war epic that became Turkey's
Oscar nominee for the foreign-language film category
([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)).
[Turkish Airlines](/organizations/turkish-airlines/) served as the main sponsor of the Oscar public-relations
campaign, with the Culture and Tourism Ministry as a main supporter
([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)).
That campaign took the film to Los Angeles, where *Ayla* opened the 2017
Asian World Film Festival
([Asian World Film Festival](https://www.asianworldfilmfest.org/2017-jury-members)).
And here is the detail the databases miss: Carradine did not just attend that
festival — he sat on its 2017 jury, listed on the festival's own site as
"CADE CARRADINE, ACTOR," while his bio closed with the line "Cade stars in
the Festival's Opening Night Film, *Ayla: The Daughter of War*"
([Asian World Film Festival](https://www.asianworldfilmfest.org/2017-jury-members)).

## A Carradine by birth, a character actor by trade

The dynasty pattern breaks in an interesting way with Cade. His father played
Wild Bill Hickok in four 2004 episodes of HBO's *Deadwood*
([FilmReference](http://www.filmreference.com/film/77/Keith-Carradine.html));
his uncle is David Carradine, his grandfather John Carradine
([FilmReference](http://www.filmreference.com/film/50/John-Carradine.html)).
Cade's own résumé, as his festival bio lists it, runs through smaller rooms:
*Deadwood* (2005), *The Kid* (2013), *Seduced* (2016), *Prettyface* (2016),
and the series *SuperHigh* (2017)
([Asian World Film Festival](https://www.asianworldfilmfest.org/2017-jury-members)).
Note the overlap: father and son both did HBO's *Deadwood*, a year apart —
Keith in the show's 2004 run
([FilmReference](http://www.filmreference.com/film/77/Keith-Carradine.html)),
Cade in 2005
([Asian World Film Festival](https://www.asianworldfilmfest.org/2017-jury-members)).
Film databases consistently credit his *Deadwood* appearance to a prospector
named Jubal, but no editorial-tier source reviewed in this pass confirms the
character name, so this profile records the appearance and holds the name
(see openItems).

## FAQ

**Is Cade Carradine related to David Carradine?**
Yes — David Carradine was his uncle. Cade is the son of Keith Carradine,
making him the nephew of both David and Robert Carradine, and the grandson
of John Carradine
([FilmReference](http://www.filmreference.com/film/77/Keith-Carradine.html);
[FilmReference](http://www.filmreference.com/film/50/John-Carradine.html)).
He is not David Carradine's son, despite the shared surname.

**What role does Cade Carradine play in *Ayla: The Daughter of War*?**
Hürriyet Daily News names him among the film's supporting roles alongside
Eric Roberts, the two American actors in the Turkish–Korean war epic
([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)).
Film databases credit him as "Captain Lorenzo Cage," but no editorial-tier
source reviewed in this pass confirms the character name.

**Did Cade Carradine appear in *Deadwood*?**
Yes. His 2017 festival biography lists *Deadwood* (2005) among his credits
([Asian World Film Festival](https://www.asianworldfilmfest.org/2017-jury-members)),
one year after his father Keith Carradine's 2004 run as Wild Bill Hickok on
the same HBO series
([FilmReference](http://www.filmreference.com/film/77/Keith-Carradine.html)).
Film databases consistently attach the appearance to a prospector named Jubal,
but no editorial-tier source reviewed in this pass confirms the character name.

**Who does Cade Carradine play in *Ray Meets Helen*?**
He is credited as "Boxer Ray," appearing opposite his own father, Keith
Carradine, who plays the older Ray — a washed-up former boxer mourning his
ruined career ([Box Office Mojo](https://www.boxofficemojo.com/title/tt5813010/credits/);
[Slant Magazine](https://www.slantmagazine.com/film/ray-meets-helen/)). The
film, written and directed by Alan Rudolph, opened theatrically on May 4, 2018
([Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/movie/ray-meets-helen/)).
