## Overview

Joey Sanchez is a Santa Ana Pueblo leader who, in 2026, served as chairman of the [All Pueblo Council of Governors](/organizations/all-pueblo-council-of-governors/), the body representing [New Mexico](/places/new-mexico/) and Texas Pueblo nations, and became its public voice in three linked fights over sacred landscapes and tribal consultation: the defence of [Chaco Canyon](/places/chaco-canyon/), the proposed protection of the Caja del Rio, and opposition to a Senate permitting bill ([The Paper](https://abq.news/2026/07/chaco-is-not-for-sale-nm-lawmakers-and-pueblo-leaders-fight-to-save-chaco-canyon/)). He had previously served as governor of Santa Ana Pueblo, and a Santa Ana Pueblo newsletter account says he was elected council chairman on December 4, 2025, after a competitive election ([Santa Ana Pueblo Department of Education](https://www.psaedu.org/files/0d90292ea/January+Newsletter+2026+Updated.pdf)). His public record in the sources read for this profile is almost entirely that of an advocate speaking for the council, not a personal biography — and this profile stays within that record.

## From Santa Ana governor to council chairman

Sanchez's earlier office is documented, though its dates are not. In July 2022 he attended New Mexico's semi-annual Government-to-Government Summit listed as "Gov. Joey Sanchez, Santa Ana Pueblo," alongside governors from Zia, Laguna, Jemez, Zuni, Nambe, Picuris, Cochiti, Tesuque, and Acoma pueblos ([Los Alamos Reporter](http://losalamosreporter.com/2022/07/23/governor-tribal-leaders-hold-semi-annual-government-to-government-summit/)). By 2026, independent reporting consistently described him instead as a former governor of Santa Ana Pueblo, including a Source NM piece that also described him as a tribal liaison, in a passage whose organisation name varies within the same article and is therefore flagged rather than asserted precisely here ([Source NM](https://www.tricityrecordnm.com/articles/high-school-sports/new-mexico-tribal-leaders-push-to-protect-chaco/)).

The Santa Ana Pueblo newsletter gives the fullest account of how he reached the council's top post, reporting that on December 4, 2025, the All Pueblo Council of Governors concluded its final meeting of the year with the election of a new chairman, vice chairman, and secretary, and that Joseph "Joey" Sanchez, described as a tribal member and former governor, faced several prominent leaders from across the pueblos before being elected chairman ([Santa Ana Pueblo Department of Education](https://www.psaedu.org/files/0d90292ea/January+Newsletter+2026+Updated.pdf)). That newsletter is a community source, not an independent one, so this profile treats the election account as supporting material, corroborated by the fact that independent 2026 reporting then repeatedly identified Sanchez as the council's chairman ([The Paper](https://abq.news/2026/07/chaco-is-not-for-sale-nm-lawmakers-and-pueblo-leaders-fight-to-save-chaco-canyon/)).

## The 2026 Chaco Canyon campaign

Sanchez's most sustained public role in 2026 was defending the federal protections around Chaco Culture National Historical Park, a [UNESCO](/organizations/unesco/) World Heritage Site landscape that Pueblo people describe as living and sacred rather than merely archaeological ([The Paper](https://abq.news/2026/07/chaco-is-not-for-sale-nm-lawmakers-and-pueblo-leaders-fight-to-save-chaco-canyon/)). The protections at stake dated to a 2023 order, Public Land Order 7923, which withdrew about 336,000 acres of federal land surrounding the park from new oil and gas leasing for 20 years ([The Paper](https://abq.news/2026/07/chaco-is-not-for-sale-nm-lawmakers-and-pueblo-leaders-fight-to-save-chaco-canyon/)). In 2026 the Interior Department moved to revoke that withdrawal, opening a short public comment period that tribal leaders and New Mexico's congressional delegation criticised as inadequate ([Source NM](https://www.heinrich.senate.gov/newsroom/in-the-news/source-new-mexico-new-mexico-officials-tribes-accuse-feds-of-rushing-to-reverse-chaco-canyon-drilling-ban)).

In April 2026, as the department opened a seven-day scoping comment period ending April 7, the council said it was "dismayed" to learn of the effort to unwind the protections, with Sanchez stating, "Chaco Canyon will always be revered and respected as a sacred place by Pueblo people," and adding, "We will use our collective voice to continue the fight" ([Source NM](https://www.heinrich.senate.gov/newsroom/in-the-news/source-new-mexico-new-mexico-officials-tribes-accuse-feds-of-rushing-to-reverse-chaco-canyon-drilling-ban)). By July, Sanchez had joined New Mexico's entire congressional delegation outside the U.S. Capitol, where Pueblo governors pressed Interior Secretary Doug Burgum to withdraw the revocation proposal, extend the comment period from 14 days to 90 days, and conduct meaningful government-to-government consultation with tribes before deciding ([The Paper](https://abq.news/2026/07/chaco-is-not-for-sale-nm-lawmakers-and-pueblo-leaders-fight-to-save-chaco-canyon/)). His framing at that rally was cultural as much as legal: "Chaco is not a museum," he said, describing a living cultural land that Pueblo people continue to travel to, pray in, and connect with their ancestors through ([The Paper](https://abq.news/2026/07/chaco-is-not-for-sale-nm-lawmakers-and-pueblo-leaders-fight-to-save-chaco-canyon/)). A later Source NM account from the same advocacy season put the same idea more personally, quoting Sanchez as saying that protecting Chaco meant keeping "a living museum" alive, and that touching its walls brought its spirituality back to life ([Source NM](https://www.tricityrecordnm.com/articles/high-school-sports/new-mexico-tribal-leaders-push-to-protect-chaco/)).

## Beyond Chaco: Caja del Rio and the permitting bill

Sanchez's council chairmanship in 2026 extended beyond Chaco to two further fronts, both again framed as protection of culturally significant land and of tribal consultation rights. In March 2026, Pueblo governors unanimously voted to endorse draft federal legislation to protect the 107,000-acre Caja del Rio plateau south of Santa Fe, a landscape described as spiritually, historically, and environmentally significant to nearby Pueblos and Spanish land grant communities ([Source NM](https://www.tricityrecordnm.com/articles/news/new-mexico-pueblo-governors-endorse-draft-federal-legislation-to-protect-caja-del-rio-landscape/)). The bill, sponsored by Senator Martin Heinrich and expected to be introduced in April 2026, would have designated the plateau as a national monument, adding limits on new industrial development ([Source NM](https://www.tricityrecordnm.com/articles/news/new-mexico-pueblo-governors-endorse-draft-federal-legislation-to-protect-caja-del-rio-landscape/)). Sanchez, identified as chair, said the unanimous vote showed "we all agree the Caja needs to be protected," and called the proposal a significant step toward long-term protection honouring the area's deep cultural and historical significance to Indigenous and local communities ([Source NM](https://www.tricityrecordnm.com/articles/news/new-mexico-pueblo-governors-endorse-draft-federal-legislation-to-protect-caja-del-rio-landscape/)).

In October 2026, Sanchez attended a meeting with Heinrich as the council's chairman to campaign against the Senate permitting bill, which the council opposed for overhauling Section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act — the provision requiring federal agencies to consider effects on historic and sacred sites and consult tribes before approving projects ([Center for Western Priorities](https://westernpriorities.org/2026/10/tribal-groups-break-with-heinrich-over-permitting-bill/)). According to that account, Sanchez said Heinrich told participants that "only technical" changes would be made to the bill, and that while Heinrich did not respond to requests for substantive amendments such as striking the Section 106 provisions, it was clear he was unwilling to do so — an account Heinrich's office disputed, saying the senator had told tribal governors he was open to bipartisan amendments ([Center for Western Priorities](https://westernpriorities.org/2026/10/tribal-groups-break-with-heinrich-over-permitting-bill/)). This last source is an advocacy publication and is used here only as corroboration of Sanchez's role and stated position, not as the sole support for any contested claim.
