## Overview

Ryan Caldbeck is an American founder and investor best known as the co-founder and former chief executive of [CircleUp](/organizations/circleup/), the [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) platform that used machine learning to fund emerging consumer brands, and as the founder and chief executive of Waystation AI, which applies artificial intelligence to consumer-goods procurement. He led CircleUp from its founding in 2012 until he stepped down as CEO in 2020, and he has since spoken publicly — unusually candidly for a founder — about the personal cost of that run ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/ryan-caldbeck-what-matters-integrity); [Reboot](https://www.reboot.io/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Edited-Transcript_Ryan_Caldbeck.docx.pdf)).

Caldbeck's career has stayed inside one industry: consumer packaged goods. Before founding CircleUp he spent nearly seven years as a growth investor in consumer private equity, and both of his companies sell the same idea in different forms — that data, not gut feel, should decide which consumer brands get capital and attention ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/events/bevnetlivesummer17/bevnet-live-summer-2017-go-inside-helio-circleups-ceo-machine-learning-building-winning-beverage-company); [SupplySide Food & Beverage Journal](https://www.supplysidefbj.com/author/ryan-caldbeck)).

## Early life, education and basketball

Caldbeck grew up in Shelburne, Vermont, and moved to [California](/places/california/) in 2003 for graduate school at Stanford ([Altoira](https://www.altoira.com/insights/episode-15-ryan-caldbeck); [Ryan Caldbeck](https://ryancaldbeck.co/)). His first paying job, he told Stanford's Graduate School of Business, was as a park ranger in Vermont — driving a tractor around a beach, keeping people safe and the bathrooms clean — an early lesson, he said, in noticing the small ways people make a difference ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/ryan-caldbeck-what-matters-integrity)).

He graduated from Duke University in 2001 with a dual BA in Public Policy and Psychology, and was a member of Duke's 2001 NCAA championship men's basketball team under Coach Mike Krzyzewski ([Altoira](https://www.altoira.com/insights/episode-15-ryan-caldbeck)). He went on to Stanford Graduate School of Business, graduating with an MBA in 2005 ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/ryan-caldbeck-what-matters-integrity)).

## Private equity and the gap that became CircleUp

After business school, Caldbeck went into growth equity with a mission, spending nearly seven years investing in consumer product and retail-focused private equity at TSG Consumer Partners and Encore Consumer Capital ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/events/bevnetlivesummer17/bevnet-live-summer-2017-go-inside-helio-circleups-ceo-machine-learning-building-winning-beverage-company)). The work paid well and he liked his colleagues, but he told Stanford GSB he was not passionate about it — and the more successful he became, the harder it was to leave ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/ryan-caldbeck-what-matters-integrity)).

What he saw in private equity was the gap. Again and again he met strong consumer and retail businesses that were too small to obtain funding through the customary private-equity channels: a $5 million business simply did not justify a large fund's time ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/events/bevnetlivesummer17/bevnet-live-summer-2017-go-inside-helio-circleups-ceo-machine-learning-building-winning-beverage-company)). The spark came in early 2011, while he sat on a panel and no one could answer the question "How do you raise money for a $5MM business?" — at that moment, he decided to start a company that would make an impact on exactly that problem ([Altoira](https://www.altoira.com/insights/episode-15-ryan-caldbeck)).

In 2012 he founded CircleUp with [Rory Eakin](/people/rory-eakin/), a friend and classmate from Stanford GSB, as an online marketplace connecting individual investors with entrepreneurs looking to raise capital for consumer startups ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/ryan-caldbeck-what-matters-integrity)). His own site dates the co-founding to 2011 — the year the idea took shape — while Stanford and the other independent sources read date the company's founding and launch to 2012; this profile uses 2012 and flags the difference in its open items. He married his wife in July 2012, a few months after CircleUp launched, and has said the relationship is his greatest achievement ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/ryan-caldbeck-what-matters-integrity)).

## Helio and the 2017 pivot

CircleUp's distinguishing asset was Helio, its proprietary machine-learning and natural-language-processing platform. Helio ingested data from thousands of sources — retail point-of-sale, e-commerce, social media, distribution and more — and used machine-learning models to algorithmically find, classify and evaluate private consumer companies at scale, bringing public-market style data rigour to private markets ([Ryan Caldbeck](https://ryancaldbeck.co/)). [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/40527386/this-investment-platform-funds-more-diverse-companies-by-focusing-on-data-not-founders) reported in 2018 that Helio tracked billions of data points on 1.3 million consumer and retail companies, and BevNET reported that it evaluated each deal on an average of 90,000 individual data points drawn from daily analysis of more than 1.2 million companies ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/events/bevnetlivesummer17/bevnet-live-summer-2017-go-inside-helio-circleups-ceo-machine-learning-building-winning-beverage-company)).

The data changed who got funded. Because Helio scored companies on brand strength, product quality, distribution, team and financials rather than on a founder's network or polish, 35% of the companies on the platform were women-led — roughly 17 times the venture industry average at the time — based, [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/40527386/this-investment-platform-funds-more-diverse-companies-by-focusing-on-data-not-founders) reported, solely on the merit of the startups. The platform's early picks illustrated the point: [Nutpods](/organizations/nutpods/), a dairy-free creamer startup doing less than $50,000 in annual sales when Helio flagged it, raised $1.2 million on CircleUp's marketplace in 2016 and received a direct CircleUp investment in 2017; an early version of Helio had likewise flagged [Halo Top](/organizations/halo-top/) before it became a top-selling ice-cream brand ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/40527386/this-investment-platform-funds-more-diverse-companies-by-focusing-on-data-not-founders)).

In 2017 CircleUp remade itself around that engine, pivoting from an equity marketplace into a data-intelligence company, with an equity investing business, a credit business and a software business licensed to institutional investors all built on top of Helio ([Ryan Caldbeck](https://ryancaldbeck.co/)). Caldbeck later described the pivot, in 2016–17, as a brutal stretch: laying people off while simultaneously raising a $125 million first-time venture fund ([Reboot](https://www.reboot.io/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Edited-Transcript_Ryan_Caldbeck.docx.pdf)). By 2017 the platform had helped fund more than 200 consumer product companies, and at its peak CircleUp had facilitated investments in over 600 businesses, according to Caldbeck's site ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/events/bevnetlivesummer17/bevnet-live-summer-2017-go-inside-helio-circleups-ceo-machine-learning-building-winning-beverage-company); [Ryan Caldbeck](https://ryancaldbeck.co/)).

| How companies were picked | Investors browsed and chose brands on the marketplace | Helio scored 1.2–1.3 million companies on billions of data points |

Recognition followed the model. CircleUp was named one of [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/40527386/this-investment-platform-funds-more-diverse-companies-by-focusing-on-data-not-founders)'s Top 10 Most Innovative Companies in Data Science, made the [CNBC](/organizations/cnbc/) Disruptor 50 and the [Forbes](/organizations/forbes/) FinTech 50, and was backed by [Union Square Ventures](/organizations/union-square-ventures/), Rose Park Advisors, [GV](/organizations/gv/), [Canaan Partners](/organizations/canaan-partners/), QED Advisors, TPG and [Temasek](/organizations/temasek/) ([Ryan Caldbeck](https://ryancaldbeck.co/); [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2023/06/01/2680901/0/en/Brightflow-AI-Acquires-CircleUp-to-Deepen-Customer-Insights-and-Help-Small-Businesses-Grow.html)). Caldbeck himself was named a "Titan of Retail" by Bloomberg and "40 Under 40" by both M&A Advisors and the San Francisco Business Times ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/events/bevnetlivesummer17/bevnet-live-summer-2017-go-inside-helio-circleups-ceo-machine-learning-building-winning-beverage-company)).

## Stepping down as CEO

Caldbeck served as CircleUp's CEO from its founding until 2020, when he transitioned to chairman and Nick Talwar — who had joined as president — succeeded him as chief executive ([Ryan Caldbeck](https://ryancaldbeck.co/); [Reboot](https://www.reboot.io/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Edited-Transcript_Ryan_Caldbeck.docx.pdf)). He had taken the decision to the board in October 2019, saying he needed to step down and someone else should take the reins ([Reboot](https://www.reboot.io/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Edited-Transcript_Ryan_Caldbeck.docx.pdf)).

He has written and spoken about the reasons with unusual openness. In his own account, the causes were the strain of pivoting a Series C company, fertility difficulties he and his wife went through beginning in 2016, a cancer diagnosis and operation, and burnout — a combination that left him exhausted and, in his words, feeling that he was not being himself with anyone ([Reboot](https://www.reboot.io/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Edited-Transcript_Ryan_Caldbeck.docx.pdf)). He has said he hopes sharing the experience helps other founders feel less lonely, and he credits working with a management coach, Ed Batista, with helping him through the transition ([Reboot](https://www.reboot.io/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Edited-Transcript_Ryan_Caldbeck.docx.pdf)).

## The sale of CircleUp and Waystation AI

On June 1, 2023, Brightflow AI announced it had acquired CircleUp, including the Helio insights engine, for undisclosed terms. Caldbeck was quoted in the announcement as CircleUp's chairman and founder, saying the deal would let customers get "the data, insights, and tools they need to grow their businesses faster and more efficiently," and Eakin, quoted as a founder, called it "a positive step forward for CircleUp and the industry" ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2023/06/01/2680901/0/en/Brightflow-AI-Acquires-CircleUp-to-Deepen-Customer-Insights-and-Help-Small-Businesses-Grow.html)). The release put Helio's scale at 1.6 million-plus brands and 16 million attributes across more than 200 data sources — company-stated figures, not independently audited ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2023/06/01/2680901/0/en/Brightflow-AI-Acquires-CircleUp-to-Deepen-Customer-Insights-and-Help-Small-Businesses-Grow.html)).

Caldbeck's next company stays in the same industry but attacks a different bottleneck. He is the founder and CEO of Waystation AI, based in Redwood City, which builds what he calls the intelligence layer for CPG procurement: it sits on a food or beverage company's supplier email, automatically structures every quote, specification and certificate, and gives procurement, quality and R&D teams shared visibility over sourcing — without asking suppliers to change how they work ([Ryan Caldbeck](https://ryancaldbeck.co/); [SupplySide Food & Beverage Journal](https://www.supplysidefbj.com/author/ryan-caldbeck)).

## FAQ

**Who co-founded CircleUp with Ryan Caldbeck?**
Rory Eakin, a friend and classmate of Caldbeck's at Stanford Graduate School of Business. The two founded CircleUp in 2012 as an online marketplace connecting investors with entrepreneurs raising capital for consumer startups ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/ryan-caldbeck-what-matters-integrity)).

**Why did Caldbeck step down as CircleUp's CEO?**
In his own public account, the reasons were the strain of pivoting the company in 2016–17, fertility difficulties he and his wife went through, a cancer diagnosis and operation, and burnout. He took the decision to the board in October 2019, and Nick Talwar succeeded him as CEO in 2020 ([Reboot](https://www.reboot.io/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Edited-Transcript_Ryan_Caldbeck.docx.pdf)).

**What is Helio?**
Helio was CircleUp's proprietary machine-learning and natural-language-processing platform. It ingested data from thousands of sources — retail sales, e-commerce, social media and distribution — to find, classify and evaluate private consumer companies, tracking billions of data points on about 1.3 million companies ([Ryan Caldbeck](https://ryancaldbeck.co/); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/40527386/this-investment-platform-funds-more-diverse-companies-by-focusing-on-data-not-founders)).

**What is Waystation AI?**
Waystation AI is Caldbeck's current company, where he is founder and CEO. It builds an intelligence layer for consumer-goods procurement, extracting quotes, specifications and certificates from supplier email so procurement, R&D and quality teams can work from one shared system ([SupplySide Food & Beverage Journal](https://www.supplysidefbj.com/author/ryan-caldbeck)).

**Was CircleUp founded in 2011 or 2012?**
Sources differ. Caldbeck's own site says he co-founded it in 2011, the year the idea took shape after a panel in early 2011; Stanford GSB and the other independent sources read date the founding and launch to 2012. This profile uses 2012 and flags the discrepancy openly ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/ryan-caldbeck-what-matters-integrity); [Ryan Caldbeck](https://ryancaldbeck.co/)).
