## Senate career

Thom Tillis is the Republican U.S. senator from North Carolina, first elected in 2014 and reelected to a second term in 2020, sitting on the Senate Finance, Veterans' Affairs, Banking, Housing and Urban Affairs, and Judiciary committees ([U.S. Senate](https://www.tillis.senate.gov/official-bio)). His path to the [Senate](/organizations/us-senate/) ran through a working-class upbringing — five siblings, frequent family moves, a warehouse records-clerk job after high school — then a degree earned at 36 and a 29-year private-sector career in technology and management consulting that took him to top-level executive roles at PricewaterhouseCoopers and IBM ([tillis.senate.gov](https://www.tillis.senate.gov/about-thom)). He entered North Carolina politics as a PTA president and a Town of Cornelius commissioner, won a North Carolina House seat in 2006, and was chosen by his peers as House speaker from 2011 to 2014 ([tillis.senate.gov](https://www.tillis.senate.gov/about-thom)). He lives with his wife Susan in Huntersville, North Carolina; they have two grown children ([U.S. Senate](https://www.tillis.senate.gov/official-bio)).

## The Hegseth break

Tillis voted to confirm [Pete Hegseth](/people/pete-hegseth/) as defense secretary, then became one of his most vocal Republican critics in the Senate. Earlier in 2026 he told Politico Magazine that Trump firing Hegseth was on "a Christmas wish list," and he believed Hegseth had misled Trump about the dynamics of a war in Iran ([The Hill](https://www.kcau9.com/2026/09/18/gop-senator-says-hegseth-needs-go-whether-its-impeachment-or-president-just-firing-him/)).

His first public call for Hegseth's removal came on September 2, 2026, right after Army Secretary [Dan Driscoll](/people/dan-driscoll/) resigned. Posting on X, Tillis wrote: "I have never witnessed more inept management of the brave men and women who serve our country." He said Hegseth was "intimidated by competence" and accused him of creating a "leadership void" at the Pentagon ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/4/senator-thom-tillis-urges-trump-to-fire-hegseth-over-inept-management)). In his statement he urged Trump to find new Pentagon leadership that would retain and strengthen military talent rather than diminish it ([The Hill](https://www.kcau9.com/2026/09/18/gop-senator-says-hegseth-needs-go-whether-its-impeachment-or-president-just-firing-him/)).

He renewed the call on September 17, after Driscoll — who had reportedly clashed with Hegseth over the firings of Army senior leadership — left at the end of August: "He needs to go, whether it's impeachment or the most expedient route — just the president to replace him — would be great." The renewal came two days after Rep. Thomas Massie introduced eight articles of impeachment against Hegseth over his handling of the Iran war and alleged oath violations. On impeachment, Tillis said, "I'll let the House do what the House is gonna do... I'm pretty well staked out on my assessment of Mr. Hegseth." He is not seeking reelection in November 2026 ([The Hill](https://www.kcau9.com/2026/09/18/gop-senator-says-hegseth-needs-go-whether-its-impeachment-or-president-just-firing-him/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim Tillis opposed Hegseth's confirmation — he voted for it before becoming his critic. It does not treat the September 17 "He needs to go" statement as his first call; the first came on September 2. And it does not reconcile Driscoll's resignation date: The Hill places it at the end of August, while Al Jazeera ties it to the days before the September 2 call — each is presented as its source reports it.
