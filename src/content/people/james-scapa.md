## Overview

James R. Scapa is the founder, chairman and former CEO of [Altair Engineering](/organizations/altair-engineering/), the simulation-software company he started in 1985 with two fellow engineering consultants and led for forty years — through 32 years as a private company, a celebrated 2017 IPO, and a sale to [Siemens](/organizations/siemens/) that valued the company at about $10.6 billion ([Columbia Engineering](https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/about/columbia-engineering-leadership/board-visitors/james-r-scapa); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/11/01/altair-engineering-soars-41-in-public-debut/1024)). He is not connected to Scapa Group plc, a separate company that manufactures bonding solutions and adhesive components ([SBWire](http://www.sbwire.com/press-releases/pdf/106497)).

Scapa grew up in New York, the child of Greek immigrants, and was the first American citizen in his family ([SBN](https://sbnonline.com/article/james-r-scapa-reorganizes-altair-engineering-inc-on-a-yearly-basis-to-keep-it-moving-forward/)). He earned a bachelor's degree in mechanical engineering from Columbia University and an MBA from the University of Michigan ([Columbia Engineering](https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/about/columbia-engineering-leadership/board-visitors/james-r-scapa)). He began his career at Ford Motor Company in 1978 and worked as an engineering consultant to the automotive industry before founding Altair ([SBN](https://sbnonline.com/article/james-r-scapa-reorganizes-altair-engineering-inc-on-a-yearly-basis-to-keep-it-moving-forward/)).

## From a Detroit consultancy to a software company

Altair began in 1985 as the result of a chance meeting of three engineering consultants on a project at a General Motors facility: Scapa, George Christ and Mark Kistner became its founders ([IndustryWeek](https://www.industryweek.com/innovation/product-development/article/21933075/altair-computing-inctroy-mich)). The early business was consulting in the then-new field of computer-aided engineering, done for carmakers, and the company stayed close to Detroit's auto industry for years ([SBN](https://sbnonline.com/article/james-r-scapa-reorganizes-altair-engineering-inc-on-a-yearly-basis-to-keep-it-moving-forward/)). Three years after founding, a sister company, Altair Computing, was set up to market software, and its early products — HyperMesh, introduced in 1990, and the OptiStruct topology-optimization tool — turned work first developed for consulting clients into commercial products ([IndustryWeek](https://www.industryweek.com/innovation/product-development/article/21933075/altair-computing-inctroy-mich)).

Scapa ran the company with a habit he described openly: reorganizing it roughly every year, on the theory that a growing business has to be reformulated to stay adaptable ([SBN](https://sbnonline.com/article/james-r-scapa-reorganizes-altair-engineering-inc-on-a-yearly-basis-to-keep-it-moving-forward/)). By the early 2010s the approach had produced a company with more than 1,800 employees and offices in 19 countries, revenue topping $237 million in 2012, and more than 3,000 clients across automotive, aerospace, government and defense, and consumer products ([SBN](https://sbnonline.com/article/james-r-scapa-reorganizes-altair-engineering-inc-on-a-yearly-basis-to-keep-it-moving-forward/)).

## The IPO, the acquisition spree, and the Siemens sale

Altair spent 32 years as a private company before going public on November 1, 2017 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/11/01/altair-engineering-soars-41-in-public-debut/1024)). The offering priced at $13 per share, raised $156 million, and closed its first day at $18.31, up more than 40 percent ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/11/01/altair-engineering-soars-41-in-public-debut/1024)). The company was unusual among software debutants: it had never been venture-backed, though it had once taken private-equity funding from [General Atlantic](/organizations/general-atlantic/), which Scapa bought out in 2013, and it was already profitable, with $313.2 million in prior-year revenue and $10.2 million in net income ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/11/01/altair-engineering-soars-41-in-public-debut/1024)). Its software, built on technology spun out of [NASA](/organizations/nasa/), counted [Blue Origin](/organizations/blue-origin/), SpaceX and [Facebook](/organizations/facebook/) among its customers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/11/01/altair-engineering-soars-41-in-public-debut/1024)).

The IPO gave Scapa capital for an acquisition strategy that became his signature — about thirty companies bought since the founding, aimed at extending Altair from simulation into data science and artificial intelligence ([ZDNet](https://www.zdnet.com/article/computer-simulation-enters-the-ai-age-the-altair-story/)). Not every deal pleased investors: the November 2018 purchase of DataWatch for $176 million in cash, then Altair's largest deal, sent the stock down as shareholders questioned the move away from predictable simulation "solvers" ([ZDNet](https://www.zdnet.com/article/computer-simulation-enters-the-ai-age-the-altair-story/)). Scapa's answer was that algorithms were starting to drive decisions across engineering, and that simulation data plus machine learning — "simulation-driven digital twins, and data-driven digital twins" — would let designs refine themselves ([ZDNet](https://www.zdnet.com/article/computer-simulation-enters-the-ai-age-the-altair-story/)). The strategy continued with RapidMiner, a low-code data analytics platform used by more than one million people, which Altair agreed to acquire in September 2022 and completed buying that October ([HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/altair-to-acquire-rapidminer/); [Microwave Journal](https://www.microwavejournal.com/articles/38981-altair-completes-acquisition-of-rapidminer)).

The end of the independent run came on October 30, 2024, when Altair agreed to be acquired by Siemens at $113.00 per share in cash, an equity value of about $10.6 billion and a 19 percent premium to the October 21 closing price ([Altair Engineering Inc. via GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2024/10/30/2972140/0/en/Altair-Signs-Definitive-Agreement-with-Siemens-to-be-Acquired-for-10-6-Billion.html)). Siemens described the same price as an enterprise value of about $10 billion — the two figures differ because equity value and enterprise value measure the deal differently, not because either side misreported it ([Siemens AG](https://press.siemens.com/jp/en/pressrelease/pr-20241030)). Scapa called the deal "the culmination of nearly 40 years in which Altair has grown from a startup in Detroit to a world-class software and technology company" ([Altair Engineering Inc. via GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2024/10/30/2972140/0/en/Altair-Signs-Definitive-Agreement-with-Siemens-to-be-Acquired-for-10-6-Billion.html)). Siemens completed the acquisition on March 26, 2025, and Scapa subsequently co-founded Escape Investment Management, a family office focused on investing in technology innovation ([Siemens AG](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/siemens-acquires-altair-create-most-complete-ai-powered-portfolio-industrial-software); [Columbia Engineering](https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/about/columbia-engineering-leadership/board-visitors/james-r-scapa)).
