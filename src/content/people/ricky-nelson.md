## Overview

Ricky Nelson — born Eric Hilliard Nelson — was an American singer and actor who grew up in public on his family's radio and television show and became one of rock and roll's first teen idols. His "Poor Little Fool" was the first song ever to top the Billboard Hot 100; he later pioneered country rock, scored a 1972 comeback with "Garden Party", and entered the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame two years after dying in a plane crash at 45.

Nelson was born on 8 May 1940 in Teaneck, New Jersey, in the [United States](/places/united-states/), the younger son of bandleader Ozzie Nelson and singer Harriet Nelson ([AllMusic](http://www.allmusic.com/artist/rick-nelson-mn0000364912)). He entered professional entertainment before his tenth birthday, appearing with his parents and his brother David on a radio comedy series built around the family, which moved to television in the early 1950s — so he grew into a teenager in front of a national audience ([AllMusic](http://www.allmusic.com/artist/rick-nelson-mn0000364912)). That public childhood became his launchpad: when rock and roll arrived in 1956, the 16-year-old used the family show to debut as a singer, and within a year he was one of the best-selling recording artists in the country ([AllMusic](http://www.allmusic.com/artist/rick-nelson-mn0000364912); [HISTORY](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/december-31/rick-nelson-dies-in-a-plane-crash?catId=13)).

## Television child star to teen idol

Nelson's fame began before he could choose it. He was still a child when he joined his parents' radio comedy, and by the early 1950s the family series was on television, with Ricky growing into a teenager in public ([AllMusic](http://www.allmusic.com/artist/rick-nelson-mn0000364912)). Rock and roll reached him at exactly the right age: in 1956 the new music turned his life around, and he started recording almost accidentally the following year, supposedly to impress a girlfriend who was an Elvis Presley fan ([AllMusic](http://www.allmusic.com/artist/rick-nelson-mn0000364912)). His debut single, a cover of Fats Domino's "I'm Walkin'", went to No. 4, helped enormously by plugs on the family show — as all his early singles would be ([AllMusic](http://www.allmusic.com/artist/rick-nelson-mn0000364912)). The flip side did even better, reaching No. 2 ([ClassicRockHistory.com](https://www.classicrockhistory.com/top-10-ricky-nelson-songs/)). HISTORY calls the template he established — a TV star picking up a guitar at the end of a sitcom episode — one that inspired hundreds of imitators, while noting that what set Nelson apart was genuine musical talent ([HISTORY](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/december-31/rick-nelson-dies-in-a-plane-crash?catId=13)).

## The Hot 100 record: first No. 1 and the hit years

Nelson's chart record is anchored by a first that can never be repeated. When Billboard launched the Hot 100 on 4 August 1958, his "Poor Little Fool" — written by Sharon Sheeley — was its first No. 1, the first of 1,131 No. 1s Billboard counted by November 2021 ([Billboard](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/11/01/the-first-billboard-all-that-was-new-bright-and-interesting-on-the-boards/); [ClassicRockHistory.com](https://www.classicrockhistory.com/top-10-ricky-nelson-songs/)). The hits then came fast: from 1957 to 1962 he landed in the Top 40 about 30 times, making him about the highest-selling singer in the United States except for Elvis Presley; HISTORY notes only Elvis and Pat Boone had more Top 40 hits in that span ([AllMusic](http://www.allmusic.com/artist/rick-nelson-mn0000364912); [HISTORY](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/december-31/rick-nelson-dies-in-a-plane-crash?catId=13)). He preferred the rockabilly of Carl Perkins and Elvis to teen-idol pop, recruited the great guitarist James Burton, and cut ballads like "Lonesome Town" alongside rockers like "Stood Up" ([AllMusic](http://www.allmusic.com/artist/rick-nelson-mn0000364912)). His second No. 1, "Travelin' Man", arrived in 1961 with "Hello Mary Lou" on the flip side ([ClassicRockHistory.com](https://www.classicrockhistory.com/top-10-ricky-nelson-songs/)).

## Reinvention: country rock and "Garden Party"

Nelson spent the second half of his career fighting the teen-idol label. Lured from Imperial by a 20-year Decca contract in 1963, he kept scoring hits for about a year, but "For You" (No. 6, early 1964) proved his last big smash of the decade, and the Beatles' arrival that year dented his commercial appeal ([AllMusic](http://www.allmusic.com/artist/rick-nelson-mn0000364912); [HISTORY](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/december-31/rick-nelson-dies-in-a-plane-crash?catId=13)). Country had always run through his work, and by the late 1960s it dominated: he formed the Stone Canyon Band, one of the earliest country-rock groups, with musicians linked to Poco, Buck Owens, Little Feat and Roger McGuinn ([AllMusic](http://www.allmusic.com/artist/rick-nelson-mn0000364912)). HISTORY credits that late-1960s work with helping inspire the California sound of Linda Ronstadt and the Eagles ([HISTORY](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/december-31/rick-nelson-dies-in-a-plane-crash?catId=13)). A Bob Dylan cover, "She Belongs to Me", returned him to the Top 40 in 1970, and his self-written "Garden Party" — his answer to a frosty oldies-show reception — reached No. 6 in 1972, his last Top Ten hit ([AllMusic](http://www.allmusic.com/artist/rick-nelson-mn0000364912)).

## Death in the De Kalb crash

Nelson died on 31 December 1985, aged 45, when the private DC-3 carrying him to a New Year's Eve show in Dallas crashed near De Kalb, Texas ([AllMusic](http://www.allmusic.com/artist/rick-nelson-mn0000364912); [HISTORY](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/december-31/rick-nelson-dies-in-a-plane-crash?catId=13)). He had boarded the plane in Guntersville, Alabama; shortly before Dallas the cabin filled with smoke, and the two pilots survived an emergency landing in a field, while all seven passengers were killed ([HISTORY](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/december-31/rick-nelson-dies-in-a-plane-crash?catId=13)). The National Transportation Safety Board's final report on the DC-3, registered N711Y, records that the crew could not start the cabin heater despite repeated attempts, that smoke then entered the cabin, and that the fire originated in the aft cabin area, on the right-hand side near the floor line — but the ignition and fuel sources were not determined ([National Transportation Safety Board](https://data.ntsb.gov/carol-repgen/api/Aviation/ReportMain/GenerateNewestReport/15850/pdf)). The report also found the pilot in command did not follow the in-flight fire checklist and did not use the oxygen system or hand-held extinguishers ([National Transportation Safety Board](https://data.ntsb.gov/carol-repgen/api/Aviation/ReportMain/GenerateNewestReport/15850/pdf)).

## Legacy: Rock Hall and a shared Grammy

Recognition came fast after his death. In 1987 he was posthumously inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a Performer, inducted by John Fogerty of Creedence Clearwater Revival; the Hall's own account stresses that the teen heartthrob "blew skeptical rock and rollers away with his own rock chops" ([Rock & Roll Hall of Fame](https://rockhall.com/inductees/ricky-nelson/)). HISTORY notes the induction as an honour no other former child actor had yet achieved ([HISTORY](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/december-31/rick-nelson-dies-in-a-plane-crash?catId=13)). The same year brought his Grammy: at the 29th Annual Grammy Awards, Best Spoken Word Or Non-Musical Recording went to *Interviews From The Class Of '55 Recording Sessions*, shared by Johnny Cash, Carl Perkins, Jerry Lee Lewis, Roy Orbison, Sam Phillips, Rick Nelson and Chips Moman ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/awards/29th-annual-grammy-awards/)). The pairing is fitting: the session reunited Nelson with the Sun Records generation he had idolised as a teenager, sealing a career that began as a television gimmick and ended with a respected place in rock history.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who was Ricky Nelson?**
Eric Hilliard "Ricky" Nelson (1940–1985) was an American singer and actor who grew up on his family's radio and television show, *The Adventures of Ozzie and Harriet*, and became one of the first rock-and-roll teen idols, later pioneering country rock with the Stone Canyon Band.

**What was Ricky Nelson's first No. 1 song?**
"Poor Little Fool", written by Sharon Sheeley, topped the Billboard Hot 100 when the chart premiered on 4 August 1958 — making it the first No. 1 in the chart's history. His second No. 1 on the Hot 100 was "Travelin' Man" in 1961.

**Was Ricky Nelson in the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame?**
Yes. He was posthumously inducted in 1987 as a Performer, two years after his death in the 1985 plane crash, with John Fogerty of Creedence Clearwater Revival as his inductor. HISTORY describes it as an honour no other former child actor had yet achieved.

**How did Ricky Nelson die?**
On 31 December 1985, aged 45, Nelson died when the chartered DC-3 carrying him to a New Year's Eve show in Dallas caught fire in flight and crash-landed near De Kalb, Texas. Both pilots survived; all seven passengers were killed.

**Did Ricky Nelson win a Grammy?**
Yes — one, shared. At the 1987 Grammy Awards, he shared Best Spoken Word Or Non-Musical Recording with Johnny Cash, Carl Perkins, Jerry Lee Lewis, Roy Orbison, Sam Phillips and Chips Moman for *Interviews From The Class Of '55 Recording Sessions*.
