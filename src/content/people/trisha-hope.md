## Overview

Trisha Hope is a realtor from League City, Texas, who became one of Donald Trump's most recognizable grassroots supporters — and then one of his most pointed defectors. In 2018 she published *Just the Tweets*, a book collecting Trump's Twitter posts verbatim, joined the roving band of die-hard rally-goers known as the Front Row Joes, and traveled the country in an RV to claim front-row seats at Trump events ([Primetimer](https://www.primetimer.com/news/who-is-trisha-hope-former-trump-supporter-says-epstein-comment-eroded-faith-in-the-president); [WNYC Studios](https://www.wnycstudios.org/podcasts/10-things-scare-me/articles/trisha-hope-10-things-that-scare-me)). She later served as a delegate to the 2024 Republican National Convention and donated January 6-themed memorabilia to the Smithsonian Institution ([Galveston County Republican Party](https://galvestoncntygop.com/trisha-hope-donates-to-smithsonian/)). In September 2026 she publicly broke with Trump in a Politico Playbook interview, calling him "a fraud and a liar" and saying she would support a third impeachment if Republicans lost the midterms ([Newswav](https://newswav.com/article/even-trump-s-once-loyal-front-row-joes-have-had-enough-of-him-fraud-and-lia-A2609_IbHJFD); [DNYUZ](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/28/trumps-most-obsessed-groupies-turn-after-dagger-in-heart-betrayal-a-fraud-and-liar/)).

## A funeral-home childhood and a painful start to adulthood

The fullest biographical account Hope has given comes from her own words on WNYC Studios' *10 Things That Scare Me*, published February 20, 2019. She grew up in a family that owned a funeral home, "so death was all around me," and recalled walking into her parents' room as a child to stand over their bed and make sure they were breathing ([WNYC Studios](https://www.wnycstudios.org/podcasts/10-things-scare-me/articles/trisha-hope-10-things-that-scare-me)).

Her childhood memories include a school trip to AstroWorld, the Houston amusement park, where peer pressure coaxed the thirteen-year-old onto a roller coaster: she "screamed so loud that I locked my jaw, and I walked around the rest of that day... with my mouth wide open," and says she has never ridden a roller coaster since ([WNYC Studios](https://www.wnycstudios.org/podcasts/10-things-scare-me/articles/trisha-hope-10-things-that-scare-me)).

Adulthood began with trauma. At eighteen she became pregnant and gave birth naturally when labor came on too quickly — "it was just excruciating pain, and my baby was born stillborn" ([WNYC Studios](https://www.wnycstudios.org/podcasts/10-things-scare-me/articles/trisha-hope-10-things-that-scare-me)). She described herself on the program as "a realtor, and I'm a mother, and a wife, and a sister," and spoke of a youngest daughter and a grandson whose future she feared for ([WNYC Studios](https://www.wnycstudios.org/podcasts/10-things-scare-me/articles/trisha-hope-10-things-that-scare-me)).

Working life brought another confrontation. Employed by a home builder, she said a supervisor "spoke to me in a sexual manner" until she left; when another woman in a similar situation asked her to join a lawsuit, she agreed, looked the man "directly in the eye" at his deposition to tell him what he did "wasn't okay," and he was fired ([WNYC Studios](https://www.wnycstudios.org/podcasts/10-things-scare-me/articles/trisha-hope-10-things-that-scare-me)). In the same appearance she defended Brett Kavanaugh, saying she thought it "shameful that a man was attacked in the way that he was with zero proof and found guilty by the left immediately" ([WNYC Studios](https://www.wnycstudios.org/podcasts/10-things-scare-me/articles/trisha-hope-10-things-that-scare-me)).

## *Just the Tweets* (2018)

Hope said she was inspired to publish her book after many friends and family members who did not have accounts on then-Twitter kept asking her about Trump's posts. She collected his tweets from his first year in office and published them as *Just the Tweets: President Donald J. Trump's Historical Collection of Tweets, Vol. 1* ([Primetimer](https://www.primetimer.com/news/who-is-trisha-hope-former-trump-supporter-says-epstein-comment-eroded-faith-in-the-president)).

"The reason it's named that is because it's just the tweets. It's not my commentary, it's not my opinion. It's just his tweets," she told Houston's Click2Houston in a 2018 interview, quoted by Primetimer. "I hope to get people to read it and understand and watch and reflect what he said when he said it and then how things evolve. It's printed as he wrote it, so if there's a typo in his tweet, there's a typo in the book. It's exactly the way he wrote it" ([Primetimer](https://www.primetimer.com/news/who-is-trisha-hope-former-trump-supporter-says-epstein-comment-eroded-faith-in-the-president)).

The book covered the tweets from January 20, 2017 — Trump's first inauguration — through January 19, 2018. The Week reported that it sold for $35.95 ([Primetimer](https://www.primetimer.com/news/who-is-trisha-hope-former-trump-supporter-says-epstein-comment-eroded-faith-in-the-president)).

## Front Row Joe

In 2018, Hope joined the Front Row Joes, the group of Trump's most ardent supporters who dedicate large portions of their lives to traveling to rally venues to secure front-row seats, as USA Today and CBS News have reported ([Primetimer](https://www.primetimer.com/news/who-is-trisha-hope-former-trump-supporter-says-epstein-comment-eroded-faith-in-the-president)). Proceeds from *Just the Tweets* helped pay for her travel to rallies across the country in an RV she bought after giving up her Texas apartment ([MeidasPlus](https://www.meidasplus.com/p/today-in-politics-bulletin-469-92826)). On the 2019 WNYC program she said plainly: "I have traveled the country attending Trump rallies and it's taken a whole lot of my time. I put my real estate business on the back burner" ([WNYC Studios](https://www.wnycstudios.org/podcasts/10-things-scare-me/articles/trisha-hope-10-things-that-scare-me)).

By 2019 she was a fixture of the rally circuit's media life. On October 10, 2019, she appeared with Fox News contributor Lawrence Jones on *The Sean Hannity Show* podcast, live from the Minnesota Trump rally, where she had teamed up with Bikers for Trump: $5 of each book's proceeds would go to the group when buyers used the coupon code "bikers" ([Everand](https://www.everand.com/podcast/448196262/Just-The-Tweets)). Primetimer also reports that she hosts the Front Row Joe Show on YouTube ([Primetimer](https://www.primetimer.com/news/who-is-trisha-hope-former-trump-supporter-says-epstein-comment-eroded-faith-in-the-president)).

Her devotion extended beyond rallies. During Trump's first impeachment, she spent two weeks in Washington walking the halls of Congress to defend him: "I went to every single senator's office as well as Republican congressmen," she told Playbook. "I did everything I could for him" ([MeidasPlus](https://www.meidasplus.com/p/today-in-politics-bulletin-469-92826)).

## 2024 RNC delegate and the Smithsonian donation

Hope's loyalty was formalized at the party level in 2024, when she served as a delegate to the Republican National Convention. While there, a Smithsonian Institution representative approached her about the hat and earrings she was wearing, which had been designed to highlight and support January 6 defendants — "J6ers" — and asked whether she would consider donating them for a future exhibit on the convention ([Galveston County Republican Party](https://galvestoncntygop.com/trisha-hope-donates-to-smithsonian/)).

She began working with Claire Jerry, the curator for the Division of Political History at the National Museum of American History, and on May 20, 2025, traveled to the Smithsonian to permanently place the hat and earrings in the museum's collection ([Galveston County Republican Party](https://galvestoncntygop.com/trisha-hope-donates-to-smithsonian/)). The items honored Master Sergeant and Green Beret Jeremy Brown, former NYPD 9/11 first responder Sara Carpenter, and Texans Mark and Jalise Middleton, who ran a nonprofit assisting January 6 defendants. Suzzanne Monk, founder of the J6 Pardon Project, and her book *How to Pardon the J6ers: A Comprehensive Strategy Guide for President Donald J. Trump* had inspired the hat's design ([Galveston County Republican Party](https://galvestoncntygop.com/trisha-hope-donates-to-smithsonian/)).

## The break: "a fraud and a liar" (September 2026)

Hope's public break with Trump arrived in a Politico Playbook interview published September 28, 2026. She called the president "a fraud and a liar," and said the one scenario that would bring her back to Washington for Trump's sake had inverted: "If we do lose the midterms and he is impeached again, I will be walking the halls of Congress in support of that impeachment" ([Newswav](https://newswav.com/article/even-trump-s-once-loyal-front-row-joes-have-had-enough-of-him-fraud-and-lia-A2609_IbHJFD); [DNYUZ](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/28/trumps-most-obsessed-groupies-turn-after-dagger-in-heart-betrayal-a-fraud-and-liar/)).

She traced her disillusionment to a July 8, 2025 White House cabinet meeting. When a reporter attempted to ask Attorney General Pam Bondi about the Justice Department's handling of the Jeffrey Epstein files, Trump cut the question off: "Are you still talking about Jeffrey Epstein? This guy's been talked about for years" — a moment verified by an Associated Press report on the meeting ([SRN News](https://srnnews.com/trump-comes-to-bondis-defense-amid-uproar-from-his-base-over-jeffrey-epstein-files-flop/); [MeidasPlus](https://www.meidasplus.com/p/today-in-politics-bulletin-469-92826)).

"It was like a dagger in my heart," Hope said. "It was like a light switch went on and a veil lifted, and I saw him for who he was, and I have not been able to unsee it since" ([MeidasPlus](https://www.meidasplus.com/p/today-in-politics-bulletin-469-92826)).

The war with Iran confirmed her turn, she said: "He's said the war is over 600 different times. He tells us they don't have a military, they don't have a navy. They're still blowing up things. It's just all lies" ([DNYUZ](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/28/trumps-most-obsessed-groupies-turn-after-dagger-in-heart-betrayal-a-fraud-and-liar/)). She also questioned Trump's own description of himself as "very rich," and said she could no longer trust a word from him ([Newswav](https://newswav.com/article/even-trump-s-once-loyal-front-row-joes-have-had-enough-of-him-fraud-and-lia-A2609_IbHJFD)).

Hope was not alone. Founding Joe Rick Frazier, who has attended more than 100 Trump rallies, said the president "can't be trusted," citing the Epstein files and what he called Trump's abandoned no-war promise, and said he was "to the point of not going to the polls in November" ([DNYUZ](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/28/trumps-most-obsessed-groupies-turn-after-dagger-in-heart-betrayal-a-fraud-and-liar/)). Former White House press secretary [Sean Spicer](/people/sean-spicer/), who produced a documentary about the Front Row Joes, told Playbook the defections made him more concerned about the softer voters who helped put Trump over the top: "Can another candidate even really pull off a rally? It's like asking where Deadheads go after the Grateful Dead broke up" ([DNYUZ](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/28/trumps-most-obsessed-groupies-turn-after-dagger-in-heart-betrayal-a-fraud-and-liar/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not assert that Hope attended the January 6, 2021 rally or entered the Capitol; Primetimer's "self-declared January 6 activist" label refers to her advocacy for January 6 defendants, not to her own presence that day. It does not claim any connection between Hope and the television show *The Biggest Loser* — a search for such a link surfaced only an unrelated participant named Hope Wright, and the claim is omitted entirely. The Tom Hoefling-reposted social-media quote in which Hope is said to have called Trump "a sick pedophile" was seen only as a snippet of a repost of her X post and is not treated as verified here; the verified 2026 on-record statements are the ones quoted above.
