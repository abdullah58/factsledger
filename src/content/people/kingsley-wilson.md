## Overview

Kingsley Wilson is the press secretary for the Department of War, the Pentagon — the public face of an institution that, in her official biography and in her own briefings, now goes by the "Department of War" name ([U.S. Department of War](https://www.war.gov/About/Biographies/Biography/Article/4261363/kingsley-wilson/)). She arrived at the podium through a promotion that was itself news: in late May 2025, chief Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell announced on X that Wilson — then the deputy press secretary — would take the top communications job, writing that her "leadership has been integral to the DoD's success" ([Jewish Review](https://jewishreview.co.il/official-who-posted-antisemitic-rhetoric-becomes-pentagon-press-secretary-37203/)). She reposted the announcement, saying she was "honored to serve President Trump and our warfighters" ([Jewish Review](https://jewishreview.co.il/official-who-posted-antisemitic-rhetoric-becomes-pentagon-press-secretary-37203/)).

What makes her tenure unusual is the gap between the controversy that preceded it and the authority she now exercises from the briefing room. In September 2026, as Representative Thomas Massie pushed a privileged impeachment resolution against Defense Secretary [Pete Hegseth](/people/pete-hegseth/) over the Iran war, Wilson defended him in a statement to NPR, calling him a "transformative leader" ([NPR via WEKU](https://www.weku.org/2026-09-16/rep-thomas-massie-moves-to-impeach-defense-secretary-pete-hegseth-over-the-iran-war?_amp=true)). That is the job as she holds it now: the department's chief defender in public. The road to it ran through one of the most bruising vetting controversies of the second Trump administration's early personnel picks.

## The March 2025 reporting

Wilson swore in as a presidential appointee on January 20, 2025, and was appointed deputy press secretary at the Pentagon the next day ([U.S. Department of War](https://www.war.gov/About/Biographies/Biography/Article/4261363/kingsley-wilson/); [Military.com](https://www.military.com/daily-news/2025/03/06/pentagons-no-2-spokesperson-has-long-history-of-antisemitic-bigoted-social-media-posts.html)). Within weeks, her social-media history became the subject of detailed reporting. On March 6, 2025, NPR's Tom Dreisbach documented years of posts and podcast commentary from her time at the right-wing think tank the Center for Renewing America ([NPR](https://www.wcbe.org/npr-news/2025-03-06/a-pentagon-press-secretary-has-history-of-pushing-antisemitic-extremist-theories?_amp=true)).

The posts that drew the sharpest criticism concerned Leo Frank, the Jewish man lynched by an antisemitic mob in Georgia in 1915 after being falsely accused of raping and murdering a 13-year-old girl — a case historians widely agree was a miscarriage of justice, and which spurred the creation of the Anti-Defamation League ([NPR](https://www.wcbe.org/npr-news/2025-03-06/a-pentagon-press-secretary-has-history-of-pushing-antisemitic-extremist-theories?_amp=true)). Wilson had written in 2023 that "Leo Frank raped & murdered a 13-year-old girl. He also tried to frame a black man for his crime," and repeated the claim the following year ([NPR](https://www.wcbe.org/npr-news/2025-03-06/a-pentagon-press-secretary-has-history-of-pushing-antisemitic-extremist-theories?_amp=true)). The ADL called on her to retract the remarks; its spokesperson said "white supremacists and other antisemites have long used conspiracy theories about the Leo Frank case to cast doubt on the circumstances of [his] antisemitic lynching" ([NPR](https://www.wcbe.org/npr-news/2025-03-06/a-pentagon-press-secretary-has-history-of-pushing-antisemitic-extremist-theories?_amp=true)). The American Jewish Committee called her "clearly unfit for her role," writing that "anyone who posts antisemitic conspiracy theories lifted right out of the neo-Nazi playbook should not be in public office" ([NPR](https://www.wcbe.org/npr-news/2025-03-06/a-pentagon-press-secretary-has-history-of-pushing-antisemitic-extremist-theories?_amp=true)).

NPR also reported that Wilson had stated a belief in the "great replacement theory," which the ADL describes as a racist conspiracy theory amplified by white nationalist groups; that she had praised Vladimir Putin's "encyclopedic knowledge of his people's history" as "beyond impressive"; that she posted in 2022 that "the American intelligence apparatus is more evil than Vladimir Putin"; and that she said on One America News in 2023 that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy "should be arrested on sight for what he has done to the American taxpayer" ([NPR](https://www.wcbe.org/npr-news/2025-03-06/a-pentagon-press-secretary-has-history-of-pushing-antisemitic-extremist-theories?_amp=true)). Her targets included Republicans who backed Ukraine aid: she called House Speaker [Mike Johnson](/people/mike-johnson/) "weak-willed" and lacking "testicular fortitude," dubbed him "Mini-Mike Johnson" and "an embarrassment," and posted "DEPORT Lindsey Graham & ship him to Ukraine!" ([NPR](https://www.wcbe.org/npr-news/2025-03-06/a-pentagon-press-secretary-has-history-of-pushing-antisemitic-extremist-theories?_amp=true)).

Military.com's parallel investigation found the same pattern stretching back to 2021: she had repeatedly called colonialism a "humanitarian venture," argued American lands were "conquered" rather than stolen from Native Americans — a slogan used in propaganda by the white supremacist group Patriot Front — called Confederate General Robert E. Lee "one of the greatest Americans to ever live," and praised Germany's far-right AfD party while repeatedly posting the slogan "Deutschland den Deutschen. Ausländer raus" ("Germany for Germans, foreigners out") ([Military.com](https://www.military.com/daily-news/2025/03/06/pentagons-no-2-spokesperson-has-long-history-of-antisemitic-bigoted-social-media-posts.html)). A researcher at George Washington University's Program on Extremism told the outlet: "If you look at those tweets in totality, what it comes down to is that she is an unabashed white nationalist" ([Military.com](https://www.military.com/daily-news/2025/03/06/pentagons-no-2-spokesperson-has-long-history-of-antisemitic-bigoted-social-media-posts.html)). Politico reported that the posts had angered lawmakers on both sides of the aisle and raised questions about whether appropriate vetting had been done before her appointment; Republican Senator Deb Fischer called the comments "horrible" and "just not appropriate" ([NPR](https://www.wcbe.org/npr-news/2025-03-06/a-pentagon-press-secretary-has-history-of-pushing-antisemitic-extremist-theories?_amp=true); [Military.com](https://www.military.com/daily-news/2025/03/06/pentagons-no-2-spokesperson-has-long-history-of-antisemitic-bigoted-social-media-posts.html)).

## Career before the Pentagon

Before joining the department, Wilson built a career as a conservative communications operative. Her official biography describes her as a "seasoned spokesperson and communications expert with nearly a decade of experience in television, radio, strategic communications, and digital media" ([U.S. Department of War](https://www.war.gov/About/Biographies/Biography/Article/4261363/kingsley-wilson/)). She served as associate director of strategic communications and digital media at the Center for Renewing America, where she frequently represented the organization on television and radio, and before that as director of operations for the social-media platform GETTR — the bio credits her with taking the start-up "from day-one launch to one million users in three days and number one in the App Store" ([U.S. Department of War](https://www.war.gov/About/Biographies/Biography/Article/4261363/kingsley-wilson/)). She is a Trump campaign alum, having been deputy to the Senior Advisor for Strategy on Donald J. Trump's 2020 re-election campaign ([U.S. Department of War](https://www.war.gov/About/Biographies/Biography/Article/4261363/kingsley-wilson/)). She earned a bachelor's degree in political science with a minor in classical studies from the University of California, Los Angeles ([U.S. Department of War](https://www.war.gov/About/Biographies/Biography/Article/4261363/kingsley-wilson/)). NPR identified her as the daughter of Steve Cortes, a former Trump campaign adviser ([NPR](https://www.wcbe.org/npr-news/2025-03-06/a-pentagon-press-secretary-has-history-of-pushing-antisemitic-extremist-theories?_amp=true)).

## In the press secretary's chair

Wilson's record in the top job is the part of her story the early coverage never got to. On December 2, 2025, she held an on-camera, on-the-record Pentagon briefing — the full transcript shows a press secretary fully in command of the administration's messaging ([GlobalSecurity.org transcript](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2025/12/mil-251202-dod01.htm)). She opened by honoring Specialist Sarah Beckstrom, the National Guardsman murdered in Washington that weekend, then welcomed "official new members of the Pentagon press corps," declaring that "legacy media chose to self-deport from this building" and that the department would not "beg these old gatekeepers to come back" ([GlobalSecurity.org transcript](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2025/12/mil-251202-dod01.htm)).

Substantively, she announced that 21 kinetic strikes had been conducted against drug boats in the Western Hemisphere with 82 "narcoterrorists" killed, defended the strikes as lawful and presidentially directed, and said the department's review of the Afghanistan withdrawal would be complete by summer 2026 under chief spokesman Sean Parnell ([GlobalSecurity.org transcript](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2025/12/mil-251202-dod01.htm)). On Iran, she declared that "Operation Midnight Hammer was an absolute resounding success. Iran's nuclear capabilities were totally obliterated" ([GlobalSecurity.org transcript](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2025/12/mil-251202-dod01.htm)). She also announced a new War Department division for strategic marketing, to be led by Abbey Lower as Deputy Assistant to the Secretary of War for Strategic Marketing, tasked with recruitment campaigns ([GlobalSecurity.org transcript](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/library/news/2025/12/mil-251202-dod01.htm)).

Nine months later, her September 2026 statement to NPR showed how completely the controversy had been absorbed into her role: asked about an active congressional effort to impeach her boss, the Pentagon's press secretary answered with a single quoted phrase — "transformative leader" ([NPR via WEKU](https://www.weku.org/2026-09-16/rep-thomas-massie-moves-to-impeach-defense-secretary-pete-hegseth-over-the-iran-war?_amp=true)).
