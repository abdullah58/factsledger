## Overview

Shantanu Narayen (born May 27, 1963, in Hyderabad, India) is the Indian-American business executive who has led [Adobe](/organizations/adobe/) as president and chief executive since December 1, 2007, later also serving as chairman ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/adobe-names-anil-chakravarthy-ceo-2026-09-03/)). He is the architect of Adobe's defining business-model change — abandoning perpetual software licenses for subscription-based Creative Cloud — and of its most expensive failed deal, the $20 billion agreement to buy [Figma](/organizations/figma/) that regulators blocked in 2023 ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1410670/backlash-begins-against-adobe-s-subscription-only-plan.html); [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/23/12/18/adobes-figma-deal-isnt-going-to-happen-and-will-cost-adobe-1-billion)). As of October 25, 2026, he remains CEO, but his tenure has a confirmed end date: Adobe announced in March 2026 that he would step down once a successor was appointed, and on September 3, 2026, Reuters reported that Anil Chakravarthy will succeed him effective December 1, 2026, with Narayen becoming executive chair ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/adobe-names-anil-chakravarthy-ceo-2026-09-03/)).

Narayen grew up in Hyderabad in a Telugu family: his father ran a plastics business and his mother taught American literature, and he attended Hyderabad Public School ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/who-is-shantanu-narayen-the-hyderabad-born-engineer-who-led-adobes-cloud-shift-92123.htm)). He earned a bachelor's degree in electronics and communication engineering from the University College of Engineering at Osmania University in Hyderabad, moved to the United States, and completed a master's in computer science at Bowling Green State University in Ohio in 1986 ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/who-is-shantanu-narayen-the-hyderabad-born-engineer-who-led-adobes-cloud-shift-92123.htm)). While working full-time, he completed an MBA at the Haas School of Business at the University of California, Berkeley, in 1993, attending classes at night ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/who-is-shantanu-narayen-the-hyderabad-born-engineer-who-led-adobes-cloud-shift-92123.htm)).

## The long climb inside Adobe

Narayen's career before Adobe was a tour of Silicon Valley's hardware and imaging world: he began in 1986 at Measurex Automation Systems, a Cupertino company building computer control systems, joined [Apple](/organizations/apple/) in product development roles in 1989 and stayed until 1995, then served as director of desktop and collaboration products at Silicon Graphics ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/who-is-shantanu-narayen-the-hyderabad-born-engineer-who-led-adobes-cloud-shift-92123.htm)). In 1996 he co-founded Pictra Inc., an early startup for sharing digital photos over the internet — the venture that introduced him to Adobe ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/who-is-shantanu-narayen-the-hyderabad-born-engineer-who-led-adobes-cloud-shift-92123.htm)).

He joined Adobe in January 1998 as vice president and general manager of the engineering technology group, was promoted to senior vice president of worldwide products in January 1999 and to executive vice president of worldwide product marketing and development in March 2001, and became president and chief operating officer in January 2005 — in time, as Computerworld noted, to help engineer the [Macromedia](/organizations/macromedia/) purchase later that year ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/188115/adobeceo.html); [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1570046/update-chizen-out-at-month-s-end-as-adobe-ceo.html)). When [Bruce Chizen](/people/bruce-chizen/) stepped down, Narayen took over as CEO on December 1, 2007, with the founders [Charles Geschke](/people/charles-geschke/) and [John Warnock](/people/john-warnock/) still co-chairing the board ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/188115/adobeceo.html)). Storyboard18, citing Macrotrends, puts the scale of what followed in one line: Adobe's annual revenue grew from about $3 billion in 2007 to $23.7 billion by the end of 2025 ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/who-is-shantanu-narayen-the-hyderabad-born-engineer-who-led-adobes-cloud-shift-92123.htm)).

## Creative Cloud: the bet customers hated first

Narayen's defining decision came in 2013, when Adobe halted development of the perpetual-license Creative Suite — there would be no CS7 — and moved all upgrades for [Photoshop](/creative-works/photoshop/), [Illustrator](/creative-works/illustrator/), and the rest of the suite exclusively to subscription-based Creative Cloud ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1410670/backlash-begins-against-adobe-s-subscription-only-plan.html)). Customers revolted immediately: a Change.org petition demanding Adobe back away from subscription-only pricing collected over 4,400 signatures within days, with signers calling the model "paying Adobe rent for the rest of my life" ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1410670/backlash-begins-against-adobe-s-subscription-only-plan.html)). Adobe's defense, given to Wall Street analysts, was the revenue logic — subscriptions would "drive a bigger and bigger and bigger recurring revenue stream," in CFO Mark Garrett's words, smoothing the peaks and valleys of upgrade cycles — and Garrett cited 500,000 subscribers at the time, targeting 1.25 million by the end of 2013 ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1410670/backlash-begins-against-adobe-s-subscription-only-plan.html)). The bet held: the subscription model became the template the rest of the software industry copied, and it underwrote Adobe's growth for the next decade.

## The Figma deal, the regulators, and the handover

A decade later, Narayen tried to buy the threat instead of outgrowing it. In September 2022 Adobe agreed to acquire Figma, the browser-based collaborative design platform, for about $20 billion in cash and stock ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/23/12/18/adobes-figma-deal-isnt-going-to-happen-and-will-cost-adobe-1-billion)). Regulators in the European Union and the United Kingdom pushed back, with Britain's [Competition and Markets Authority](/organizations/competition-and-markets-authority/) and the [European Union](/organizations/european-union/)'s Commission scrutinizing whether the deal would harm innovation in design software ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/23/12/18/adobes-figma-deal-isnt-going-to-happen-and-will-cost-adobe-1-billion)). On December 18, 2023, the companies mutually terminated the agreement, saying there was no clear path to approval, and Adobe paid Figma a $1 billion termination fee ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/23/12/18/adobes-figma-deal-isnt-going-to-happen-and-will-cost-adobe-1-billion)). Narayen said Adobe "strongly disagree[d] with the recent regulatory findings, but we believe it is in our respective best interests to move forward independently" ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/23/12/18/adobes-figma-deal-isnt-going-to-happen-and-will-cost-adobe-1-billion)). Adobe's own rival product in the space, [Adobe XD](/creative-works/adobe-xd/), never matched Figma's collaborative model — the gap the acquisition was meant to close.

The handover now closes the tenure. Adobe announced in March 2026 that Narayen would step down as CEO once a successor was appointed and remain as chairman; Reuters reported on September 3, 2026, that Anil Chakravarthy, who leads Adobe's customer-experience business, will become president and CEO effective December 1, 2026, with Narayen transitioning to executive chair and working closely with him on the transition ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/adobe-names-anil-chakravarthy-ceo-2026-09-03/)). Outside Adobe, Narayen sits on the board of Pfizer and serves as vice chairman of the US-India Strategic Partnership Forum, and in 2019 he received the Padma Shri, India's fourth-highest civilian honour ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-makers/who-is-shantanu-narayen-the-hyderabad-born-engineer-who-led-adobes-cloud-shift-92123.htm)).
