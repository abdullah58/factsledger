## Overview

Glenn Close has one of the most decorated careers in American acting — three Tony Awards, three Emmy Awards, three Golden Globes — and one of its most lopsided statistics: eight Academy Award nominations without a win ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/entertainment/hollywood/glenn-close-on-eight-oscar-nods-with-no-wins-i-am-not-a-loser-7306652/)). But the part of her life she has chosen to make count the most is the one she did not grow up discussing at all: mental illness. In 2010 she co-founded Bring Change to Mind, a national nonprofit devoted to ending the stigma around mental illness, after her sister Jessie was diagnosed with bipolar disorder and her nephew Calen Pick with schizoaffective disorder ([The Commonwealth Club of California](https://www.commonwealthclub.org/events/archive/podcast/glenn-close-bring-change-mind)). It was the family story the Closes had never told — and its telling is the work Close says matters most.

## The silence she grew up in

When Close became famous as Alex Forrest, the "bunny-boiling" stalker in 1987's *Fatal Attraction*, she had no idea her younger sister Jessie had been living with an actual mental illness ([WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/healthheroes/features/2015-peoples-choice-glenn-close)). Jessie had struggled with bipolar disorder all her life, but was not diagnosed until 2004, when she was 51 and confessed to Close that she was constantly thinking of killing herself ([WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/healthheroes/features/2015-peoples-choice-glenn-close)). Close guided her sister into a top psychiatric hospital in Boston ([WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/healthheroes/features/2015-peoples-choice-glenn-close)). The Closes — a wealthy, old Greenwich, Connecticut family — had never discussed such things: not the uncle who had committed suicide, not the great-uncle who was institutionalized ([WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/healthheroes/features/2015-peoples-choice-glenn-close)). "We treat mental illness the way we talked about cancer decades ago," Close said. "It's whispered, it isn't talked about, it's shameful" ([WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/healthheroes/features/2015-peoples-choice-glenn-close)).

In 2010 she founded Bring Change 2 Mind (BC2M), a national organization that works to change public attitudes toward mental illness through public service announcements and school programs ([WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/healthheroes/features/2015-peoples-choice-glenn-close)). Its seven PSAs — some made with the help of director Ron Howard — delivered stigma-reducing messaging to more than four billion individuals, and its peer-led high-school clubs operate in 16 states ([The Commonwealth Club of California](https://www.commonwealthclub.org/events/archive/podcast/glenn-close-bring-change-mind); [WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/healthheroes/features/2015-peoples-choice-glenn-close)). In 2014 the foundation partnered with Indiana University on the College Toolbox Project and launched a similar program for high schools, LETSBringChange2Mind ([WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/healthheroes/features/2015-peoples-choice-glenn-close)). Jessie and her adult son Calen, who has schizoaffective disorder, now have their conditions under control and often travel with Close to speak about stigma ([WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/healthheroes/features/2015-peoples-choice-glenn-close)). In 2015 she was named one of WebMD's Health Heroes, winning the People's Choice award ([WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/healthheroes/features/2015-peoples-choice-glenn-close)).

## The stage foundation

Close was born Glenda Veronica Close on March 19, 1947, in Greenwich, Connecticut ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/facts/Glenn-Close)). Her professional theatre debut came in Harold Prince's 1974 Broadway revival of *Love for Love* ([West End Theatre](https://www.westendtheatre.com/42192/star-watch/glenn-close/)). The stage made her first: an Obie Award for *The Singular Life of Albert Nobbs*, then three Tony Awards — Best Actress in a Play for *The Real Thing* (1984) and *Death and the Maiden* (1992), both directed by Mike Nichols, and Best Actress in a Musical for *Sunset Boulevard* (1995), in which she originated Norma Desmond for Los Angeles and Broadway ([West End Theatre](https://www.westendtheatre.com/42192/star-watch/glenn-close/); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/facts/Glenn-Close)). After two decades away from Broadway, she returned in November 2014 to lead Edward Albee's *A Delicate Balance* ([West End Theatre](https://www.westendtheatre.com/42192/star-watch/glenn-close/)), and in April 2016 she made her West End debut reprising Norma Desmond at the London Coliseum, a 43-performance run ([West End Theatre](https://www.westendtheatre.com/42192/star-watch/glenn-close/)).

## Eight Oscar nominations, no wins — and one extraordinary year

The Academy has nominated Close eight times without once giving her the statue: four times for Best Actress and four times for Best Supporting Actress, across *The World According to Garp*, *The Big Chill*, *The Natural*, *Fatal Attraction*, *Dangerous Liaisons*, *Albert Nobbs*, *The Wife* and *Hillbilly Elegy* ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/entertainment/hollywood/glenn-close-on-eight-oscar-nods-with-no-wins-i-am-not-a-loser-7306652/)). After the eighth loss — to *Minari*'s Youn Yuh-jung in 2021 — the Los Angeles Times noted she was tied as the most-nominated actor never to win, which prompted Emmy winner Sarah Paulson to tweet in her defense that the "click bait" conversation should cease ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/entertainment/hollywood/glenn-close-on-eight-oscar-nods-with-no-wins-i-am-not-a-loser-7306652/)). Close told the Associated Press she hadn't seen the article or the tweet, but had an answer anyway: "First of all, I don't think I'm a loser." Then, looking into the camera: "Who in that category is a loser? You're there, you're five people honored for the work that you've done by your peers. What's better than that?" ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/entertainment/hollywood/glenn-close-on-eight-oscar-nods-with-no-wins-i-am-not-a-loser-7306652/))

That 2021 nomination came for playing Bonnie "Mamaw" Vance — [JD Vance](/people/jd-vance/)'s grandmother — in Ron Howard's *Hillbilly Elegy* ([Cinema Online](https://www.cinema.com.my/Articles/news_details.aspx?search=2021.n_glenngetsbest_59229)). In the same year, she was also nominated for Worst Supporting Actress at the Golden Raspberry Awards for that exact role — the only actor ever to receive both an Oscar and a Razzie nomination for the same film; other dual-nominees, like Melissa McCarthy in 2019 and Sylvester Stallone in 2004, were nominated for two different films ([Cinema Online](https://www.cinema.com.my/Articles/news_details.aspx?search=2021.n_glenngetsbest_59229)). She had also received Golden Globe and SAG nominations for the role, losing the Globe to Jodie Foster for *The Mauritanian* ([Cinema Online](https://www.cinema.com.my/Articles/news_details.aspx?search=2021.n_glenngetsbest_59229)).

## Television and the other trophies

Whatever the Academy withheld, the rest of the industry granted: three Emmys (1995, 2008, 2009), three Golden Globes (2005, 2008, 2019 — including Best Actress in a Motion Picture Drama) and three Grammy nominations ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/facts/Glenn-Close)). Her Emmy wins came for *Serving in Silence: The Margarethe Cammermeyer Story* and for the FX series *Damages*, which also earned her a Golden Globe ([West End Theatre](https://www.westendtheatre.com/42192/star-watch/glenn-close/)). She produced and starred in nine television movies, including the *Sarah, Plain and Tall* series, and won a SAG Award for playing Eleanor in *The Lion in Winter* ([West End Theatre](https://www.westendtheatre.com/42192/star-watch/glenn-close/)). Just before the pandemic, she recorded a spoken-word jazz album with Grammy-winning musician Ted Nash at Jazz at Lincoln Center ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/entertainment/hollywood/glenn-close-on-eight-oscar-nods-with-no-wins-i-am-not-a-loser-7306652/)).

## A childhood inside a closed world

On Apple TV+'s *The Me You Can't See*, the mental-health series by Oprah Winfrey and Prince Harry, Close said her family had joined Moral Re-Armament — MRA, a religious movement — when she was 7, and she did not leave until she was 22 ([Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/entertainment/glenn-close-the-me-you-cant-see-interview-cult)). The group exercised "a lot of control" over its members; "everybody spouted the same things," and anything done for oneself was considered selfish ([Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/entertainment/glenn-close-the-me-you-cant-see-interview-cult)). Her father's involvement was so deep that he moved the whole family to Switzerland, and Close partly attributed her three divorces to the severity of that childhood ([Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/entertainment/glenn-close-the-me-you-cant-see-interview-cult)). She said she has lived with low-grade depression for much of her life — "a mist or a veil over you" — and in 2019 she moved to Bozeman, Montana, to be near her siblings, a closeness she says has psychologically grounded her ([Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/entertainment/glenn-close-the-me-you-cant-see-interview-cult); [The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/entertainment/hollywood/glenn-close-on-eight-oscar-nods-with-no-wins-i-am-not-a-loser-7306652/)).
