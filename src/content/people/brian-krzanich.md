## Overview

Brian Krzanich is an American engineer and technology executive who spent 36 years at [Intel](/organizations/intel/), including five as its chief executive from May 2013 to June 2018, before resigning after an investigation found he had violated the company's policy against relationships between managers and employees ([Associated Press](https://phys.org/news/2018-06-intel-ceo-consensual-relationship-employee.html)). He then led CDK Global, a supplier of software to car dealerships, from November 2018 to July 2022 — ending in an $8.3 billion take-private sale to Brookfield Business Partners — and has been President and Chief Executive Officer of [Cerence](/organizations/cerence/) Inc., the automotive voice-AI company, since October 7, 2024 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1768267/000119312524233891/d869370dex991.htm)).

## Thirty-six years at Intel

Krzanich joined Intel in 1982 as an engineer and rose through the ranks, becoming chief operating officer in 2012, where he oversaw Intel's China strategy, before being named chief executive in 2013 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1768267/000119312524233891/d869370dex991.htm)). He holds a bachelor's degree in chemistry from San Jose State University ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1768267/000119312524233891/d869370dex991.htm)). As CEO he pushed Intel beyond the personal computer into cloud computing, high-speed memory, the Internet of Things, artificial intelligence and self-driving cars ([Associated Press](https://phys.org/news/2018-06-intel-ceo-consensual-relationship-employee.html)).

That push was bought, in large part. Intel acquired programmable-chip maker Altera for $16.7 billion in cash and stock in June 2015, and announced the $15.3 billion acquisition of Mobileye, the Israeli driver-assistance and autonomous-driving company, in March 2017 — the two largest deals of his tenure ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/business/intels-15-3b-mobileye-deal-isnt-largest-acquisition-date/)). Cerence's appointment release credits his Intel years with scaling the business from $52 billion to more than $70 billion in revenue ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1768267/000119312524233891/d869370dex991.htm)).

Krzanich was also a public champion of workplace diversity. In 2015 he challenged the technology industry to increase the hiring of women and minorities, set a goal of full representation in Intel's workforce by 2020, and said Intel would invest $300 million to improve diversity ([Associated Press](https://phys.org/news/2018-06-intel-ceo-consensual-relationship-employee.html)). The same tenure, however, is remembered for Intel's manufacturing stumbles: the company's 10nm process delays, which handed competitors a window, are described by [The Register](/organizations/the-register/) as the biggest fumble of his time at Intel ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2024/10/15/cerence-appoints-ex-intel-boss-brian-krzanich-as-its-new-ceo/871904?td=readmore)).

## The 2018 resignation

On June 21, 2018, Intel announced that Krzanich had resigned as chief executive and as a member of the board. The company said it had recently been informed that he had a past consensual relationship with an Intel employee, and that an investigation by internal and external counsel had confirmed a violation of Intel's non-fraternization policy, which applies to all managers; the board accepted his resignation, and chief financial officer Robert Swan became interim chief executive immediately ([Associated Press](https://phys.org/news/2018-06-intel-ceo-consensual-relationship-employee.html)). Intel said the policy had been in place since 2011, applied to direct and indirect reports, and had been enforced against managers on several occasions ([Associated Press](https://phys.org/news/2018-06-intel-ceo-consensual-relationship-employee.html)).

The resignation came in a difficult year for Intel. The company had begun 2018 in a firestorm over the Meltdown and Spectre processor security flaws, and Krzanich had been heavily criticized for selling Intel stock after the company learned of the flaws but before they were disclosed publicly ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2018/intel-roiled-ceo-brian-krzanichs-resignation-latest-challenge-big-chipmaker/)). The Associated Press reported that he sold about $39 million in Intel stock and options in late November 2017, after Intel was notified of the vulnerabilities in June but before they were publicly known; Intel said at the time that the sale was unrelated to the security flaws ([Associated Press](https://phys.org/news/2018-06-intel-ceo-consensual-relationship-employee.html)). Intel's shares closed down 2.4% on the day of the resignation announcement ([Associated Press](https://phys.org/news/2018-06-intel-ceo-consensual-relationship-employee.html)).

## CDK Global and the return to the CEO's chair

Less than five months after leaving Intel, Krzanich was appointed president and chief executive of CDK Global in November 2018, succeeding Brian MacDonald ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001109116/8fcae918-3786-47de-91f4-a3739080e264.pdf)). CDK supplies software to the retail automotive industry, and Cerence's release says that after stabilizing the business, Krzanich "delivered ten consecutive quarters of growth and ultimately achieved a 2022 take-private exit to Brookfield Business Partners for $8.3 billion" ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1768267/000119312524233891/d869370dex991.htm)). He stepped down in July 2022, when MacDonald returned as acting president and chief executive ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001109116/8fcae918-3786-47de-91f4-a3739080e264.pdf)).

## Cerence — and a contested homecoming

On October 7, 2024, Cerence Inc. announced that Krzanich had been appointed chief executive officer and a member of its board of directors, effective immediately, succeeding Stefan Ortmanns, who departed as CEO and resigned from the board ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1768267/000119312524233891/d869370dex991.htm)). Cerence chairman Arun Sarin said the company believed "a leadership change is necessary to execute the next phase of the Company's transformation" and cited Krzanich's record of large-scale business transformations and his expertise in AI and cloud computing ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1768267/000119312524233891/d869370dex991.htm)). Krzanich said he looked forward to advancing the company's generative AI and voice interaction capabilities ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1768267/000119312524233891/d869370dex991.htm)).

The appointment was not welcomed quietly. The Register reported that the announcement triggered a wave of public criticism on LinkedIn recalling the Intel resignation and the 10nm delays, and that Cerence disabled comments on its LinkedIn posts about the appointment ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2024/10/15/cerence-appoints-ex-intel-boss-brian-krzanich-as-its-new-ceo/871904?td=readmore)). Cerence, which builds AI-powered voice assistants for vehicles, is headquartered in [Burlington, Massachusetts](/places/burlington-massachusetts/) — the same town where [Nuance Communications](/organizations/nuance-communications/), the company it was spun off from in 2019, was based ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1768267/000119312524233891/d869370dex991.htm)).

Beyond Cerence, Krzanich has served on several public-company boards, including SES AI from January 2024 to November 2024 and Electric Last Mile Solutions from June 2021 to July 2022 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001109116/8fcae918-3786-47de-91f4-a3739080e264.pdf)). On October 14, 2026, he joined the board of directors of Entravision Communications Corporation, with the board expanding from seven to nine directors; the filing describes him as 66, with more than 40 years in the technology industry and chief executive roles at three publicly traded technology companies ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001109116/8fcae918-3786-47de-91f4-a3739080e264.pdf)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Brian Krzanich doing now?** He has been President and Chief Executive Officer of Cerence Inc., the automotive voice-AI company, since October 7, 2024, and he joined the board of Entravision Communications Corporation on October 14, 2026 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1768267/000119312524233891/d869370dex991.htm); [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001109116/8fcae918-3786-47de-91f4-a3739080e264.pdf)).

**What did he do at CDK Global?** As president and CEO from November 2018 to July 2022, he stabilized the dealership-software company, delivered ten consecutive quarters of growth, and completed a take-private sale to Brookfield Business Partners for $8.3 billion ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1768267/000119312524233891/d869370dex991.htm)).

**How long did he work at Intel?** Thirty-six years, from 1982 to 2018, including chief operating officer from 2012 and chief executive from May 2013 to June 2018 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1768267/000119312524233891/d869370dex991.htm)).

**What did Brian Krzanich study?** He holds a bachelor's degree in chemistry from San Jose State University ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001109116/8fcae918-3786-47de-91f4-a3739080e264.pdf)).
