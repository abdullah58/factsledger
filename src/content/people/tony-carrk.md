## Overview

Tony Carrk is the executive director of [Accountable.US](/organizations/accountable-us/), the Washington-based nonprofit watchdog he led as of 2023 and still headed through at least March 2025 ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/2024-06-15/money-laundering-charges-raise-questions-about-the-direction-of-the-epoch-times); [Accountable.US](https://accountable.us/trump-accountability-phase-ii-exposing-the-power-grabs-and-backroom-deals-costing-you/)). A Democratic opposition researcher by training, Carrk spent two decades in public affairs before taking the top job, most prominently as research director for [Hillary Clinton](/people/hillary-clinton/)'s 2015–2016 presidential campaign ([Accountable.US](https://accountable.us/about/our-board-and-leadership/tony-carrk/)). He became a nationally quoted figure in June 2024 when he told [NPR](/organizations/npr/) his team had spotted discrepancies in The [Epoch Times](/organizations/the-epoch-times/)' nonprofit tax filings before the outlet's chief financial officer was charged with money laundering ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/2024-06-15/money-laundering-charges-raise-questions-about-the-direction-of-the-epoch-times)).

NPR has described Accountable.US as "a progressive nonprofit watchdog organization that monitors financial flows of right-wing organizations" ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/2024-06-15/money-laundering-charges-raise-questions-about-the-direction-of-the-epoch-times)). The group's own site summarizes Carrk's background as "two decades of experience in public affairs as a skilled researcher and director for issue advocacy and political campaigns" ([Accountable.US](https://accountable.us/about/our-board-and-leadership/tony-carrk/)). In a December 2024 interview, Carrk framed the watchdog's mission as showing voters "whose side" officeholders are actually fighting for ([Center for American Progress Action Fund](https://www.americanprogressaction.org/article/tony-carrk-on-holding-trump-accountable-in-2025/)).

## Career in Democratic research and campaigns

Carrk's first role at Accountable.US was vice president of issue campaigns. Before that he was vice president of policy and research at the Center for American Progress Action Fund, and earlier director of the Health Care War Room, with prior senior research and policy roles for Friends of Terry McAuliffe and the Democratic Senate Campaign Committee ([Accountable.US](https://accountable.us/about/our-board-and-leadership/tony-carrk/)). The Fund's own podcast introduction independently restates much of that history and calls him "a former CAP Action alum" ([Center for American Progress Action Fund](https://www.americanprogressaction.org/article/tony-carrk-on-holding-trump-accountable-in-2025/)). He graduated from Siena College and holds a Master of Public Policy from Georgetown University ([Accountable.US](https://accountable.us/about/our-board-and-leadership/tony-carrk/)).

In 2015 and 2016 he was research director for Hillary Clinton's presidential campaign ([Accountable.US](https://accountable.us/about/our-board-and-leadership/tony-carrk/); [NPR](https://www.kedm.org/politics-government/2016-10-15/wikileaks-claims-to-release-hillary-clintons-goldman-sachs-transcripts?_amp=true)). Weeks before Clinton formally announced her candidacy, he and campaign spokesman Brian Fallon began holding regular war-room meetings with a team of eight volunteers to fight back against Peter Schweizer's forthcoming book "Clinton Cash," according to reporting by Politico's Annie Karni — read here via a full-text republish, since the original page was inaccessible ([attributed republish](https://standupamericaus.org/?p=18420)). The campaign questioned Schweizer's objectivity and selectively leaked chapters to undercut exclusive arrangements he had made with The New York Times and The Washington Post ([attributed republish](https://standupamericaus.org/?p=18420)).

In January 2016, Carrk emailed Clinton campaign chairman John Podesta and other top advisers excerpts from Clinton's paid speeches to financial firms, under the subject line "Goldman Sachs Paid Speeches," flagging policy positions he said needed "an extra scrub" ([NPR](https://www.kedm.org/politics-government/2016-10-15/wikileaks-claims-to-release-hillary-clintons-goldman-sachs-transcripts?_amp=true); [Deadspin](https://Deadspin.com/leaked-emails-hillary-clinton-gave-paid-speeches-to-wa-1787553311/)). WikiLeaks published the email that October; the campaign refused to verify the authenticity of the documents, which it described as stolen ([NPR](https://www.kedm.org/politics-government/2016-10-15/wikileaks-claims-to-release-hillary-clintons-goldman-sachs-transcripts?_amp=true)).

InfluenceWatch reports that Carrk also founded Cherchio Research, Communications & Strategy, a Democratic-aligned firm offering opposition research, vetting reports, and strategic communications; that claim appears in no other read source and is flagged as unverified below ([InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/person/tony-carrk/)).

## Leading Accountable.US

Carrk was in the executive-director role "as of 2023" per InfluenceWatch ([InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/person/tony-carrk/)), and was publicly serving when NPR quoted him in June 2024 ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/2024-06-15/money-laundering-charges-raise-questions-about-the-direction-of-the-epoch-times)), when he appeared on CAP Action's "The Tent" in December 2024 ([Center for American Progress Action Fund](https://www.americanprogressaction.org/article/tony-carrk-on-holding-trump-accountable-in-2025/)), and when he signed a March 2025 memo as executive director ([Accountable.US](https://accountable.us/trump-accountability-phase-ii-exposing-the-power-grabs-and-backroom-deals-costing-you/)).

His most nationally visible episode came in June 2024. Federal prosecutors charged Epoch Times chief financial officer Weidong "Bill" Guan with laundering at least $67 million in allegedly criminal proceeds, and NPR reported that Accountable.US had alerted it to irregularities in the federal tax filings of the outlet's nonprofits — then filed an IRS complaint against the Epoch Public Foundation and the Epoch Times Association, seeking a civil investigation of their 2021 and 2022 returns ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/2024-06-15/money-laundering-charges-raise-questions-about-the-direction-of-the-epoch-times)). Carrk told NPR his team had already been examining the nonprofits' tax returns before the charges were made public: "We noticed that there was some financial discrepancies. This is something that is worth investigation" ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/2024-06-15/money-laundering-charges-raise-questions-about-the-direction-of-the-epoch-times)). Nonprofit-tax experts NPR consulted called the returns "a hot mess" (Brian Mittendorf of Ohio State), "fishy" (Georgetown's Brian Galle), and pointed to the "baffling" $20.5 million in liabilities the foundation listed for 2021 (Philip Hackney of the University of Pittsburgh) ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/2024-06-15/money-laundering-charges-raise-questions-about-the-direction-of-the-epoch-times)).

Under Carrk, the group kept its focus on the second [Donald Trump](/people/donald-trump/) administration into 2025. On February 13, End Citizens United and Accountable.US launched TrumpsCorruptCabinet.com, a database of Cabinet members' financial entanglements and conflicts of interest, with Carrk calling exposure of the "Wealthy-First, Workers-Last agenda" critical ([Accountable.US](https://accountable.us/end-citizens-united-and-accountable-us-launch-corrupt-cabinet-website/)). On March 18, Carrk signed a memo launching "Phase Two" of the group's Trump Accountability War Room: Phase One had scrutinized nominees' conflicts of interest, while Phase Two would track abuses of power by connecting officials' self-enrichment to higher costs for families — a framing the memo said polling showed broke through best with the public ([Accountable.US](https://accountable.us/trump-accountability-phase-ii-exposing-the-power-grabs-and-backroom-deals-costing-you/)).

## Carrk's stated approach to accountability

In the December 2024 interview, Carrk laid out a strategy shaped by the first Trump term, when, he said, progressives' approach of elevating every issue and controversy "did not work" ([Center for American Progress Action Fund](https://www.americanprogressaction.org/article/tony-carrk-on-holding-trump-accountable-in-2025/)). Instead, he argued, advocates must "pick their battles" and use confirmation fights to show the public "whose side" nominees are on — citing, as examples, Dr. Mehmet Oz's investments in health-insurance companies against his oversight of Medicare and Medicaid, an oil-and-gas-billionaire nominee for the Interior Department, an IRS-commissioner nominee who had called for abolishing the income tax, and the influx of corporate lobbyists ([Center for American Progress Action Fund](https://www.americanprogressaction.org/article/tony-carrk-on-holding-trump-accountable-in-2025/)).

He also discussed the group's 2024 work with the Center for American Progress raising alarms about Project 2025, which he called "deeply unpopular" and designed to gut checks and balances while consolidating power in the presidency — much of it, he warned, achievable by executive action without Congress ([Center for American Progress Action Fund](https://www.americanprogressaction.org/article/tony-carrk-on-holding-trump-accountable-in-2025/)). For optimism, he cited two precedents: Democrats' rebound after the 2004 election, retaking the House and Senate within two years and then the presidency in 2008, and the 2017 defeat of the Affordable Care Act repeal, when public organizing stretched an expected February vote into the summer until the Senate rejected repeal in July ([Center for American Progress Action Fund](https://www.americanprogressaction.org/article/tony-carrk-on-holding-trump-accountable-in-2025/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is Tony Carrk?**
Tony Carrk is the executive director of Accountable.US, a progressive nonprofit watchdog that monitors financial flows of right-wing organizations ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/2024-06-15/money-laundering-charges-raise-questions-about-the-direction-of-the-epoch-times)). A Democratic researcher with two decades in public affairs, he previously directed research for Hillary Clinton's 2016 presidential campaign and held senior roles at the Center for American Progress Action Fund and Protect Our Care ([Accountable.US](https://accountable.us/about/our-board-and-leadership/tony-carrk/)).

**What did Carrk do in Hillary Clinton's 2016 campaign?**
As research director in 2015–2016, Carrk ran opposition research, including early war-room meetings with spokesman Brian Fallon to counter the book "Clinton Cash" ([attributed republish](https://standupamericaus.org/?p=18420)). In January 2016 he circulated excerpts of Clinton's paid speeches to advisers, flagging positions needing "an extra scrub" — an email WikiLeaks later published, which the campaign never authenticated ([NPR](https://www.kedm.org/politics-government/2016-10-15/wikileaks-claims-to-release-hillary-clintons-goldman-sachs-transcripts?_amp=true)).

**Why was Carrk quoted about The Epoch Times in 2024?**
His team reviewed the nonprofit tax filings of Epoch Times-linked organizations and flagged discrepancies to NPR. Accountable.US then filed an IRS complaint seeking a civil investigation of the Epoch Public Foundation's and Epoch Times Association's 2021–2022 returns, after the outlet's CFO was charged with laundering at least $67 million ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/2024-06-15/money-laundering-charges-raise-questions-about-the-direction-of-the-epoch-times)).

**What is Accountable.US?**
Accountable.US is a Washington-based nonprofit watchdog that investigates conflicts of interest and special-interest influence. NPR describes it as a progressive organization monitoring financial flows of right-wing groups ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/2024-06-15/money-laundering-charges-raise-questions-about-the-direction-of-the-epoch-times)). Under Carrk it filed the 2024 IRS complaint against Epoch Times nonprofits and ran a Trump Accountability War Room ([Accountable.US](https://accountable.us/trump-accountability-phase-ii-exposing-the-power-grabs-and-backroom-deals-costing-you/)).
