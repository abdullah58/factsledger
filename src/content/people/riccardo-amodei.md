## Overview

Riccardo Amodei was an Italian-American leathersmith, the father of [Anthropic](/organizations/anthropic/) co-founders [Dario Amodei](/people/dario-amodei/) and [Daniela Amodei](/people/daniela-amodei/) ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Dario-Amodei)). No interview with Riccardo himself surfaced in the research for this profile; the fullest published portrait of him comes through his son's account in a July 2025 profile of Dario Amodei built on more than two dozen interviews with him, his friends, colleagues, and competitors ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)).

In that profile, Dario describes his parents as "a loving couple who he says sought to improve the world": his mother Elena Engel ran renovation and construction projects for libraries in Berkeley and San Francisco, while "his father Riccardo Amodei was a trained leathersmith" ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). Dario was born in San Francisco in 1983, the oldest child of Elena Engel and Riccardo Amodei, and his sister Daniela is four years younger ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Dario-Amodei)).

## A craftsman's household

The household the sources sketch is one where a father who worked leather by hand and a mother who built public repositories of knowledge raised two children who would go on to co-found Anthropic ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Dario-Amodei)). Dario says his parents "gave me a sense of right and wrong and what was important in the world," imbuing a "strong sense of responsibility" ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). Britannica similarly describes the family as close-knit, with parents who taught him "what was important in the world" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Dario-Amodei)).

That instilled responsibility showed up early: as a Caltech undergraduate, Dario publicly rebuked his fellow students in the campus newspaper over their passivity toward the coming Iraq war, writing in March 2003 that opposition in principle meant nothing without action ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). The detail matters because it is the earliest public trace of the moral seriousness his parents are credited with forming — the same seriousness that later defined his break with OpenAI and the founding of Anthropic ([MundoAmérica](https://www.mundoamerica.com/news/2026/09/15/6aa90640e85ece901e8b4589.html)).

## Death and its aftermath

In 2006, while Dario was a graduate student of theoretical physics at Princeton, Riccardo died ([MundoAmérica](https://www.mundoamerica.com/news/2026/09/15/6aa90640e85ece901e8b4589.html)). Britannica names the cause as hepatitis C and says that within four years the disease was likely curable ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Dario-Amodei)). MundoAmérica calls it "a variant of Hepatitis C" and writes that "sadness turned to anger just four years later when a new drug — called sofosbuvir — was approved, curing over 95% of patients" ([MundoAmérica](https://www.mundoamerica.com/news/2026/09/15/6aa90640e85ece901e8b4589.html)). Kantrowitz, notably, never names the illness at all, calling it only "a rare illness" that Riccardo had "long fought," and frames the gap as a breakthrough that turned the illness "from something that was 50% fatal to 95% curable" ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)).

Riccardo's death redirected Dario's studies from theoretical physics to biology ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). As a doctoral student he joined a team at Stanford researching metastasis detection through protein analysis; finding the lab work cumbersome, he sought a shortcut in machine learning, then an emerging branch of artificial intelligence ([MundoAmérica](https://www.mundoamerica.com/news/2026/09/15/6aa90640e85ece901e8b4589.html)).

The loss became the emotional core of Dario's public case for accelerating science. Kantrowitz quotes him: "There was someone who worked on the cure to this disease, that managed to cure it, and save a bunch of people's lives, but could have saved even more" ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). Jade Wang, who dated Dario in the early 2010s, described the wound as "the difference between his father most likely dying and most likely living" ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). In a 2025 interview quoted by Britannica, Dario said: "my father died because of cures that could have happened a few years [earlier]. I understand the benefit of this technology" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Dario-Amodei)). And in September 2026, opening his essay "We Must Pace the Frontier," he wrote in his own words: "My own father died of a disease that was cured just a few years after his death" ([Dario Amodei](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier)). MundoAmérica adds that Dario told CBS, "I feel a personal responsibility in the development of AI," citing his father as the driving force behind his eagerness to press every advantage of new technologies ([MundoAmérica](https://www.mundoamerica.com/news/2026/09/15/6aa90640e85ece901e8b4589.html)).
