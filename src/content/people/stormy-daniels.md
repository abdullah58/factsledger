## The life behind the hush-money case

Every profile of Stormy Daniels opens with the Trump hush-money case, and this one does not skip it — but the part of her story that nobody covers is what happened after 2018, when the cameras stayed on her and the costs kept compounding. The 2024 Peacock documentary Stormy chronicles her life between 2018 and 2023, and it shows a woman who went from friend to foe of a celebrity businessman turned president, then had to live with what that enmity cost ([Montana Public Radio](https://www.mtpr.org/2024-03-19/threats-debt-and-trumps-advances-stormy-doc-examines-the-life-of-stormy-daniels?_amp=true)).

The film begins in 2023, around the time Trump was indicted over the hush-money payments ([Montana Public Radio](https://www.mtpr.org/2024-03-19/threats-debt-and-trumps-advances-stormy-doc-examines-the-life-of-stormy-daniels?_amp=true)). Daniels watches the news unfold on television and says, "Let's go," before walking off screen ([Montana Public Radio](https://www.mtpr.org/2024-03-19/threats-debt-and-trumps-advances-stormy-doc-examines-the-life-of-stormy-daniels?_amp=true)). What follows is the part the news cycle never lingered on: the threats grew from insults into direct death threats. "Back in 2018, there was stuff like 'liar, s***, gold digger,'" she says in the film. "This time around, it is very different. It is direct threats. It is 'I'm going to come to your house and slit your throat'" ([Montana Public Radio](https://www.mtpr.org/2024-03-19/threats-debt-and-trumps-advances-stormy-doc-examines-the-life-of-stormy-daniels?_amp=true)). She says she did not feel protected by the justice system and accused it of ignoring her concerns about her safety ([Montana Public Radio](https://www.mtpr.org/2024-03-19/threats-debt-and-trumps-advances-stormy-doc-examines-the-life-of-stormy-daniels?_amp=true)).

The film also documents the personal wreckage: amid the six-year conflict with Trump, her marriage ended, her relationship with her daughter became strained, and she felt her safety was constantly in jeopardy ([Montana Public Radio](https://www.mtpr.org/2024-03-19/threats-debt-and-trumps-advances-stormy-doc-examines-the-life-of-stormy-daniels?_amp=true)). And yet, with Trump about to go on trial, she said she was willing to testify against him: "I'm more prepared with my legal knowledge but I'm also tired. Like, my soul is so tired," she said. "I won't give up because I'm telling the truth" ([Montana Public Radio](https://www.mtpr.org/2024-03-19/threats-debt-and-trumps-advances-stormy-doc-examines-the-life-of-stormy-daniels?_amp=true)).

Financially, the fight nearly broke her. In 2018 she had sued Trump for defamation over a tweet suggesting she had lied about being threatened in 2011 to stay silent; a federal judge dismissed the suit and ordered her to pay Trump's legal fees, and she lost the appeal ([Montana Public Radio](https://www.mtpr.org/2024-03-19/threats-debt-and-trumps-advances-stormy-doc-examines-the-life-of-stormy-daniels?_amp=true)). She ended up owing Trump more than $600,000 in attorney fees, and the film says she feared losing her home ([Montana Public Radio](https://www.mtpr.org/2024-03-19/threats-debt-and-trumps-advances-stormy-doc-examines-the-life-of-stormy-daniels?_amp=true)).

The documentary's witnesses included actor Seth Rogen, who had worked with Daniels on the 2007 film Knocked Up, and late-night host [Jimmy Kimmel](https://factsledger.com/people/jimmy-kimmel/), who had interviewed her in 2018 while her nondisclosure agreement was still in effect and had puppets reenact her account of Trump ([Montana Public Radio](https://www.mtpr.org/2024-03-19/threats-debt-and-trumps-advances-stormy-doc-examines-the-life-of-stormy-daniels?_amp=true)). "She told the truth and she paid a price for that," Kimmel said in the film. "It's not something that just goes away" ([Montana Public Radio](https://www.mtpr.org/2024-03-19/threats-debt-and-trumps-advances-stormy-doc-examines-the-life-of-stormy-daniels?_amp=true)).

## Before she was a headline

Daniels was born Stephanie A. Gregory in Baton Rouge, Louisiana; her legal name is Stephanie Clifford ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/); [Associated Press](https://www.wcia.com/news/politics/ap-politics/ap-key-takeaways-from-stormy-daniels-testimony-in-trump-hush-money-trial/amp/)). Her parents separated when she was a child, and she wrote in her 2018 memoir Full Disclosure that she was sexually abused by an older man when she was nine ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)).

She entered the adult entertainment world young, performing a "guest set" at a strip club when she was 17, and went on to become a star and director of pornographic films over more than two decades ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)). At trial she told jurors she started exotic dancing in high school, began appearing in adult films at 23, and eventually directed more than 150 films, winning a roster of industry awards ([Associated Press](https://www.wcia.com/news/politics/ap-politics/ap-key-takeaways-from-stormy-daniels-testimony-in-trump-hush-money-trial/amp/)). She chose the stage name Stormy, which a 2018 New York Times profile called "more tempestuous" than the other favorite names of the trade; [The Guardian](https://factsledger.com/organizations/the-guardian/) traced the full pseudonym to Mötley Crüe bassist Nikki Sixx naming his daughter Storm, combined with her fondness for Jack Daniel's whiskey ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)).

## Lake Tahoe, July 2006

Daniels and Trump met at a charity golf tournament in Lake Tahoe in July 2006, when she was 27 and he was 60 ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)). She was greeting golfers between tees; he was the host of The Apprentice ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)). At trial she testified that her studio was a sponsor of the outing, that Trump called her "the smart one," and that she accepted his dinner invitation partly because it might help her career ([Associated Press](https://www.wcia.com/news/politics/ap-politics/ap-key-takeaways-from-stormy-daniels-testimony-in-trump-hush-money-trial/amp/)).

What followed is the most contested part of the story. According to Daniels, one of Trump's bodyguards invited her to have dinner with his boss, which turned out to be an invitation to Trump's suite; he greeted her in silk pajamas and showed her a golf magazine with his picture on the cover ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)). She told CBS's 60 Minutes in 2018 that she joked someone should spank him with the magazine — "so he turned around and pulled his pants down a little... and I just gave him a couple swats" ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)). They went on to have what Daniels called in her book "the least impressive sex I'd ever had"; Trump has always denied having sex with Daniels and has called her claims a "total con job" and accused her of extortion ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)).

At trial she gave the jury a longer account: she left to use the bathroom and was startled to find Trump in his underwear when she returned; she did not feel physically or verbally threatened but realized he was "bigger and blocking the way"; "the next thing I know was: I was on the bed," and they were having sex ([Associated Press](https://www.wcia.com/news/politics/ap-politics/ap-key-takeaways-from-stormy-daniels-testimony-in-trump-hush-money-trial/amp/)). The encounter was brief and left her shaking; "I just wanted to leave," she testified ([Associated Press](https://www.wcia.com/news/politics/ap-politics/ap-key-takeaways-from-stormy-daniels-testimony-in-trump-hush-money-trial/amp/)).

## The contact that followed

The two stayed in occasional touch. According to Daniels, Trump called her after that night and nicknamed her "Honeybunch"; they saw each other at least twice more in 2007 but did not sleep together again ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)). She met him again at his request in July 2007 at the Beverly Hills Hotel to discuss a possible appearance on Celebrity Apprentice; she said he wanted to have sex again and she declined, and that he later called to say he had not been able to get her booked on the show ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)).

## From a killed magazine story to a $130,000 NDA

Things were quiet until 2011, when Daniels, apparently still bitter over the broken promise to cast her, considered going public ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)). Her agent negotiated a $15,000 agreement with Life & Style, but the magazine backed off after [Michael Cohen](/people/michael-cohen/), then Trump's close aide, threatened to sue ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)).

In the spring of 2016, as Trump ran for the White House, her agent approached outlets including the National Enquirer and found no takers — partly because Daniels had denied the story back in 2011 ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)). Then, two days before the second presidential debate, The [Washington Post](https://factsledger.com/organizations/washington-post/) published the "Access Hollywood" tape, and Daniels and her team realized the story of Trump cheating on his then-pregnant wife could be valuable ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)). The Enquirer's editor, Dylan Howard, connected Daniels's side to Cohen, and they negotiated a $130,000 nondisclosure agreement ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)).

The payment itself was not illegal — paying for an NDA is lawful — but because it was made just before the election to bury information that could damage Trump's prospects, prosecutors argued it violated campaign rules ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)). Cohen subsequently said Trump had approved the deal; after Trump became president, Trump and his company reimbursed Cohen and falsely recorded those payments as legal fees ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)). In August 2018 Cohen pleaded guilty to campaign-finance violations in making the payment, among other charges; he was sentenced to three years, and his sentence ended in November 2021 ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)).

## The trial and the verdict

Daniels testified at Trump's criminal trial in Manhattan on May 7 and May 9, 2024, in the first criminal trial of a former American president ([Associated Press](https://www.wcia.com/news/politics/ap-politics/ap-key-takeaways-from-stormy-daniels-testimony-in-trump-hush-money-trial/amp/)). Trump's lawyers moved for a mistrial midway through her testimony, arguing her account had nothing to do with the case and would prejudice the jury; the judge rejected the request and faulted the defense for not raising more objections during the testimony ([Associated Press](https://www.wcia.com/news/politics/ap-politics/ap-key-takeaways-from-stormy-daniels-testimony-in-trump-hush-money-trial/amp/)). On cross-examination, the defense suggested she was motivated by money; when asked "Am I correct that you hate President Trump?", Daniels said, "Yes" — and said she wanted him held accountable, "if he's convicted" ([Associated Press](https://www.wcia.com/news/politics/ap-politics/ap-key-takeaways-from-stormy-daniels-testimony-in-trump-hush-money-trial/amp/)). At one point the judge told defense lawyers he could hear Trump "cursing audibly" during her testimony ([Associated Press](https://www.wcia.com/news/politics/ap-politics/ap-key-takeaways-from-stormy-daniels-testimony-in-trump-hush-money-trial/amp/)).

On May 30, 2024, a Manhattan jury found Trump guilty on all 34 felony counts of falsifying business records to conceal the payment, making him the first former U.S. president convicted of felony crimes ([Ace News Today](https://acenewstoday.com/2025/01/donald-trump-sentenced-in-hush-money-case-to-unconditional-discharge-with-no-penalties/), quoting the Associated Press). On January 10, 2025, Judge Juan Merchan sentenced him to an unconditional discharge — the convictions stand, but Trump received no jail time, fines, or probation — with Trump appearing remotely from Mar-a-Lago ([Ace News Today](https://acenewstoday.com/2025/01/donald-trump-sentenced-in-hush-money-case-to-unconditional-discharge-with-no-penalties/)).
