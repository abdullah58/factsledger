## The man who turned a morning email into a business

Mike Allen is usually profiled as an influence story — the journalist the
Obama White House woke up to, the founder of the morning newsletter that set
Washington's agenda. The verified record says something more concrete: he
built the most lucrative editorial franchise in Washington media, then built
it again. Under Allen, Politico's Playbook brought in roughly $3 million a
year through advertising, live events, and weekly sponsorships starting at
$55,000 — with past sponsors including Visa, Bank of America, and BP —
reaching about 100,000 subscribers with an 80 percent gross open rate at its
peak
([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/post-mike-allen-politicos-newsletter-makes-play-millennials/)).
As the National Press Foundation records, Allen is now co-founder and
executive editor of [Axios](https://factsledger.com/organizations/axios/),
where he writes the Axios Daily Essentials newsletters — Axios AM, Axios PM,
and Axios Finish Line — the direct descendants of the format he invented
([National Press Foundation](https://nationalpress.org/speaker/mike-allen/)).
No standard profile leads with the economics. They are the actual throughline
of his career.

Allen co-founded [Politico](https://factsledger.com/organizations/politico/)
in 2007 and created Playbook, the flagship morning tip sheet he wrote for the
company's first decade
([National Press Foundation](https://nationalpress.org/speaker/mike-allen/)).
Six years in, the newsletter's commercial scale was already striking: Mark
Leibovich's April 2010 New York Times Magazine profile, "The Man the White
House Wakes Up To," reported advertisers such as Starbucks and the Catfish
Farmers of America paying $15,000 a week to appear in Playbook
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/the_audit/new_york_times_magazine_on_politicos_mike_allen.php)).

## How Playbook became a franchise

The 2010 profile captured why the product worked. Allen insisted Playbook was
"aggressively neutral," adopted the habit of refusing to vote, and ran it on
a vast network of what the profile called "friend-sources" in the White House
and on Capitol Hill
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/the_audit/new_york_times_magazine_on_politicos_mike_allen.php)).
Co-founder Jim VandeHei gave the business its slogan: "If you want to move
data or shape opinion," he told the Times, "you market it through Mikey and
Playbook, because those tens of thousands that matter most all read it and
most feed it" — "Playbook is D.C.'s Facebook. And Mike's the most popular
friend"
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/the_audit/new_york_times_magazine_on_politicos_mike_allen.php)).

The operational detail emerged only when Allen left. Digiday reported in
September 2016 that Playbook had been launched in the BlackBerry era and
remained essentially unchanged — a text-only product Allen sent at
inconsistent morning times — until his successors, Anna Palmer and Jake
Sherman (with Allen's co-author Daniel Lippman), took over in July 2016,
moved to 4 a.m. writing with pre-8 a.m. delivery, added color photos and
graphics, and grew subscriptions 8 percent in two months
([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/post-mike-allen-politicos-newsletter-makes-play-millennials/)).
That handover figure — 100,000 subscribers in January 2016 and the highest
gross open rate in the newsletter's history at 80 percent — documents the
asset Allen was walking away from
([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/post-mike-allen-politicos-newsletter-makes-play-millennials/)).

He did not walk away alone. In January 2016, Politico owner Robert Albritton
announced a mass departure after the presidential election: co-founder and CEO
Jim VandeHei, Allen, COO Kim Kingsley, CRO Roy Schwartz, and EVP Danielle
Jones
([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/politico-co-founder-jim-vandehei-veteran-mike-allen-to-leave-news-site/)).
Albritton's memo paid Allen the compliment that defines the Playbook era:
"Mike has launched and maintained the Playbook franchise — 365 days a year —
longer than seemed humanly possible"
([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/politico-co-founder-jim-vandehei-veteran-mike-allen-to-leave-news-site/)).
The exits followed reported tension between VandeHei and Albritton over the
company's management and expansion strategy
([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/politico-co-founder-jim-vandehei-veteran-mike-allen-to-leave-news-site/)).

## The second act at Axios

Allen, VandeHei, and Schwartz launched Axios in 2017
([PR Newswire](https://www.epicos.com/article/834937/axios-jim-vandehei-and-mike-allen-accept-2024-national-press-club-fourth-estate-award)).
At the new company Allen repeated the franchise playbook: the Axios Daily
Essentials newsletters (AM, PM, and Finish Line) are his byline, and he was
an executive producer of the Emmy Award-winning docu-news series "Axios on
HBO"
([National Press Foundation](https://nationalpress.org/speaker/mike-allen/)).
With VandeHei and Schwartz he also co-authored "Smart Brevity: The Power of
Saying More with Less," the book codifying the terse style both Politico and
Axios were built on — Allen spoke about it at his alma mater, Washington and
Lee University, in October 2022
([Washington and Lee University](https://columns.wlu.edu/wl-presents-axios-co-founder-mike-allen-86/)).

The industry noticed the double act. Allen has been named several times to
Vanity Fair's "New Establishment" list
([National Press Foundation](https://nationalpress.org/speaker/mike-allen/)),
and in November 2024 he and VandeHei received the National Press Club's
Fourth Estate Award — described by the Club as its most esteemed prize — at a
gala in Washington
([PR Newswire](https://www.epicos.com/article/834937/axios-jim-vandehei-and-mike-allen-accept-2024-national-press-club-fourth-estate-award)).

## Background

Allen is from Orange County, California, and graduated from Washington and
Lee University in 1986
([Washington and Lee University](https://columns.wlu.edu/wl-presents-axios-co-founder-mike-allen-86/)).
Before Politico he was an alumnus of TIME, The New York Times, The
Washington Post, and the Richmond Times-Dispatch
([National Press Foundation](https://nationalpress.org/speaker/mike-allen/)).
His father was the conservative writer Gary Allen, described in the 2010
profile as "an icon of the far right in the 1960s and 1970s": affiliated
with the John Birch Society, a denouncer of the Trilateral Commission and
"Red Teachers," the author who called rock'n'roll a "Pavlovian Communist
mind-control plot" and wrote speeches for segregationist governor George
Wallace
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/the_audit/new_york_times_magazine_on_politicos_mike_allen.php)).
