## Overview

Eight days before this profile was written, Marco Rubio became the most institutionally powerful Secretary of State in half a century. In September 2026, President Trump signed a commission formally making Rubio his permanent national security adviser, ending more than a year in an acting role — and making him the first person since Henry Kissinger (1973–1975) to hold both jobs at the same time ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/22/marco-rubio-national-security-adviser-trump/91891708007/)). Kissinger's dual tenure is the only precedent: he served as Nixon's national security adviser from 1969 and secretary of state from 1973, holding both until November 1975; other prominent officials such as Colin Powell and Condoleezza Rice held both positions during their careers, but never simultaneously ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/white-house/4737811/marco-rubio-named-national-security-adviser-16-months-acting/)).

Rubio's rise to this position was unusual even by the standards of this administration. At one point in 2025 he held four government titles at once — secretary of state, acting national security adviser, acting administrator of the U.S. Agency for International Development, and acting archivist of the United States — a portfolio that spawned viral memes of Rubio in doctored photos as everything from a Spirit Airlines pilot to a White House beekeeper ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/white-house/4737811/marco-rubio-named-national-security-adviser-16-months-acting/); [Fox News Digital](https://foxnews.com/politics/rubios-meme-worthy-job-collection-takes-new-turn-permanent-trump-upgrade)). Trump himself has framed it as simple trust: "When I have a problem, I call up Marco. He gets it solved" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/22/marco-rubio-national-security-adviser-trump/91891708007/)).

Before any of that, Rubio was the son of Cuban immigrants in Miami who climbed from a West Miami city commission seat to the Speaker's chair in Florida's House, then to the U.S. Senate as the Tea Party's "crown prince," and then — after a bruising presidential run against the man who would twice make him the country's chief diplomat — to the 72nd Secretary of State, confirmed by a historic unanimous vote ([Council on Foreign Relations](https://www.cfr.org/articles/campaign-2016-senator-marco-rubio-gop-presidential-candidate); [Agence France-Presse](https://globalnation.inquirer.net/261885/marco-rubio-wins-unanimous-nod-to-be-top-us-diplomat)).

## The dual role: from acting to permanent

Rubio took over the national security adviser's portfolio on May 1, 2025, when Trump tapped then-adviser Mike Waltz to become U.S. ambassador to the United Nations. Waltz's departure came weeks after he was involved in the incident that became known as "Signalgate": on March 24, 2025, the Atlantic's editor in chief, Jeffrey Goldberg, reported he had been accidentally added to a Signal group chat — started by Waltz and including Rubio, Vice President JD Vance, and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth — in which senior officials discussed operational plans and other likely highly classified details of U.S. military strikes on Yemen's Houthis ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/politics/marco-rubio-fast-facts); [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/white-house/4737811/marco-rubio-named-national-security-adviser-16-months-acting/)).

What was announced as an interim fix became something larger. For sixteen months Rubio served in the role in an acting capacity while simultaneously running the [State Department](https://factsledger.com/organizations/us-department-of-state/), until Trump signed a commission making the appointment permanent in September 2026, first reported by MS NOW during Trump's trip to New York for the UN General Assembly ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/22/marco-rubio-national-security-adviser-trump/91891708007/); [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/white-house/4737811/marco-rubio-named-national-security-adviser-16-months-acting/)). A White House spokesperson confirmed the signed commission to the Washington Examiner, though the White House did not clarify exactly when Trump signed it ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/white-house/4737811/marco-rubio-named-national-security-adviser-16-months-acting/)). As national security adviser, Rubio oversees the National Security Council staff and serves as Trump's top adviser on foreign policy, intelligence, and defense while coordinating the administration's national security policy — on top of leading the State Department ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/white-house/4737811/marco-rubio-named-national-security-adviser-16-months-acting/)).

The other two titles peeled off along the way. Rubio handed his acting-USAID-administrator responsibilities to Office of Management and Budget director Russ Vought in August 2025, and he delegated the acting-archivist role to James Byron in February 2026 ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/white-house/4737811/marco-rubio-named-national-security-adviser-16-months-acting/)). [Fox News](/organizations/fox-news/) Digital, which had chronicled the whole portfolio, reported that the archivist post had been held from February to December 2025 ([Fox News Digital](https://foxnews.com/politics/rubios-meme-worthy-job-collection-takes-new-turn-permanent-trump-upgrade)).

With great portfolio comes great speculation. Rubio has been floated repeatedly as a contender for the 2028 Republican presidential nomination, with polling showing him trailing Vice President JD Vance; Trump has described the two as a presidential "dream team" while stopping short of endorsing either ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/news/white-house/4737811/marco-rubio-named-national-security-adviser-16-months-acting/)). Rubio himself has publicly denied any such plans. "I don't know what the future holds, but I have no plans at this time," he told Colombian news station RCN. "I'm not a candidate for anything right now. And I'm 100% focused on my job, and I have to be because I have two jobs" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/22/marco-rubio-national-security-adviser-trump/91891708007/)).

## Secretary of State: the tenure so far

Trump nominated Rubio for the State Department in November 2024, and the Senate confirmed him on January 20, 2025 — Inauguration Day itself — by a vote of 99 to 0, the first cabinet member confirmed in the second Trump administration ([House Committee on Foreign Affairs](http://docs.house.gov/meetings/FA/FA00/20260603/119336/HHRG-119-FA00-Bio-RubioM-20260603.pdf)). He was sworn in as the 72nd Secretary of State on January 21, 2025 ([House Committee on Foreign Affairs](http://docs.house.gov/meetings/FA/FA00/20260603/119336/HHRG-119-FA00-Bio-RubioM-20260603.pdf)). The vote made him the first Hispanic and first fluent Spanish speaker to hold the top U.S. diplomatic post — a milestone noted by Agence France-Presse as the Republican-led Senate approved him only hours after the inauguration ([Agence France-Presse](https://globalnation.inquirer.net/261885/marco-rubio-wins-unanimous-nod-to-be-top-us-diplomat)). Democrats joined in the unanimity: the top Democrat on the Foreign Relations Committee, Senator Jeanne Shaheen, told the Senate floor that "while we may not always agree, I believe he has the skills, knowledge and qualifications to be secretary of state," and the panel cleared his nomination barely an hour before the full vote ([Agence France-Presse](https://globalnation.inquirer.net/261885/marco-rubio-wins-unanimous-nod-to-be-top-us-diplomat)).

His confirmation hearing set the tone of his tenure. Rubio accused China of cheating its way to superpower status and called it "the most potent and dangerous near-peer adversary this nation has ever faced" ([Agence France-Presse](https://globalnation.inquirer.net/261885/marco-rubio-wins-unanimous-nod-to-be-top-us-diplomat)). On his first day he met with the foreign ministers of the Quad countries — Australia, India, and Japan — signaling where the new administration's early attention would go ([Agence France-Presse](https://globalnation.inquirer.net/261885/marco-rubio-wins-unanimous-nod-to-be-top-us-diplomat)).

On [Ukraine](/places/ukraine/), Rubio has served as the administration's chief reality-checker. On June 25, 2026, during a visit to Bahrain, he flatly contradicted Moscow's framing of the August 2025 Trump–Putin summit in Anchorage: "There was no agreement in Alaska. There was a proposal, but there was no agreement," he told reporters. "If there had been an agreement, we would have had an end to the war" ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/no-ukraine-agreement-reached-at-trump-putin-alaska-summit-rubio-says/)). Russian officials had repeatedly described the summit as a turning point that laid the groundwork for negotiations, and the [Kremlin](/organizations/kremlin/) had accused Washington of failing to follow through on "agreements" it claimed were reached there ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/no-ukraine-agreement-reached-at-trump-putin-alaska-summit-rubio-says/)).

India has been one of his busiest portfolios. Rubio made his first official visit to India in May 2026, a four-day trip aimed at recalibrating a bilateral relationship that had faced headwinds, and Trump publicly called him "the greatest secretary of state in the history of the US" while asking him to pass along greetings to Prime Minister Modi ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/about/who-is-marco-rubio)). By September 2026, the dossier had grown thornier: India's foreign minister was raising concerns over U.S. sanctions that could impose tariffs on countries buying Russian crude, even as Washington highlighted its energy-security support ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/about/who-is-marco-rubio)).

## The Senate years and the hawkish record

Rubio served in the U.S. Senate from 2011 to 2025, building the foreign-policy record that made him a plausible secretary of state. He was a Senior Member of the Senate Foreign Relations Committee, Vice Chair of the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence, a member of the powerful Appropriations Committee, and a member of the Small Business and Entrepreneurship Committee ([House Committee on Foreign Affairs](http://docs.house.gov/meetings/FA/FA00/20260603/119336/HHRG-119-FA00-Bio-RubioM-20260603.pdf)). His signature legislative achievement was writing and passing the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act — described in his official biography as the greatest turning point in U.S.–China relations in decades — and he also co-led the Hong Kong Relations Act and the VERDAD Act ([House Committee on Foreign Affairs](http://docs.house.gov/meetings/FA/FA00/20260603/119336/HHRG-119-FA00-Bio-RubioM-20260603.pdf)). During the first Trump administration he worked with the president to hold members of the Cuban regime accountable, targeting the dictatorship's military-run holding company ([House Committee on Foreign Affairs](http://docs.house.gov/meetings/FA/FA00/20260603/119336/HHRG-119-FA00-Bio-RubioM-20260603.pdf)). And he created the Paycheck Protection Program, the pandemic-era small-business lending program ([House Committee on Foreign Affairs](http://docs.house.gov/meetings/FA/FA00/20260603/119336/HHRG-119-FA00-Bio-RubioM-20260603.pdf)).

## The rise: from Miami to the Tea Party's "crown prince"

Rubio was born Marco Antonio Rubio on May 28, 1971, in Miami, Florida ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/politics/marco-rubio-fast-facts)). He is the son of Cuban immigrants who came to the United States in 1956 — his father Mario Rubio worked as a banquet bartender, his mother Oriales García Rubio split time as a stay-at-home mom and hotel maid — and he has said he was drawn to public service in large part through conversations with his grandfather, who had witnessed how communism destroyed his homeland ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/politics/marco-rubio-fast-facts); [House Committee on Foreign Affairs](http://docs.house.gov/meetings/FA/FA00/20260603/119336/HHRG-119-FA00-Bio-RubioM-20260603.pdf)). His education ran through Tarkio College in Missouri, Santa Fe Community College, a political science degree from the University of Florida in 1993, and a law degree from the University of Miami, earned cum laude in 1996; he later taught politics at Florida International University ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/politics/marco-rubio-fast-facts); [The Straits Times](https://www.straitstimes.com/singapore/us-republican-presidential-nomination-race-10-things-about-marco-rubio)). Along the way there were distinctly human details: baptized Mormon at age eight before his family returned to the Roman Catholic Church; a die-hard Miami Dolphins fan whose wife Jeanette was once a Dolphins cheerleader; and a fan of hip-hop who counted Tupac among his musical guilty pleasures ([The Straits Times](https://www.straitstimes.com/singapore/us-republican-presidential-nomination-race-10-things-about-marco-rubio)).

His political career moved fast. He won a seat on the West Miami City Commission in 1998, then a seat in the Florida House of Representatives in 2000; within three years he was majority leader, and within seven he was Speaker of the House — the first Cuban-American to hold that position ([Council on Foreign Relations](https://www.cfr.org/articles/campaign-2016-senator-marco-rubio-gop-presidential-candidate); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/politics/marco-rubio-fast-facts)). In 2010 he leapt to the national stage, winning Florida's open Senate seat as an underdog against former governor Charlie Crist on the strength of the Tea Party wave — a victory that earned him the moniker the Tea Party's "crown prince" ([Council on Foreign Relations](https://www.cfr.org/articles/campaign-2016-senator-marco-rubio-gop-presidential-candidate); [The Straits Times](https://www.straitstimes.com/singapore/us-republican-presidential-nomination-race-10-things-about-marco-rubio)).

In April 2015 he launched a bid for the Republican presidential nomination, announcing at Miami's Freedom Tower — the building where hundreds of thousands of Cuban exiles had been processed in the 1960s and '70s — with a message about his generation leading a "new American century" ([Council on Foreign Relations](https://www.cfr.org/articles/campaign-2016-senator-marco-rubio-gop-presidential-candidate); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/politics/marco-rubio-fast-facts)). He suspended the campaign on March 15, 2016, after losing the Florida primary to Donald Trump, who had famously belittled him as "Little Marco" during the race; Rubio then endorsed Trump before the general election and was largely supportive of his presidency ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/politics/marco-rubio-fast-facts); [Agence France-Presse](https://globalnation.inquirer.net/261885/marco-rubio-wins-unanimous-nod-to-be-top-us-diplomat)). A decade later, the man who mocked him was handing him the most powerful dual portfolio in American diplomacy — and calling him the person he phones when there is a problem to solve.
