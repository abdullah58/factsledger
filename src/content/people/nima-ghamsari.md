## Overview

Nima Ghamsari is the co-founder and chief executive of [Blend](/organizations/blend/), the mortgage-technology company he founded in 2012 and took public in 2021, and he also chairs its board of directors ([Blend](https://blend.com/bio/nima-ghamsari/)). An Iranian-born immigrant who grew up in [Ohio](/places/ohio/) after his family moved to the [United States](/places/united-states/) in 1987, he studied computer science at [Stanford University](/universities/stanford-university/), joined [Palantir](/organizations/palantir/) on graduation, and left at 26 to rebuild mortgage lending as cloud software ([Forbes](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/how-nima-ghamsaris-blend-labs-prevented-the-us-mortgage-market-meltdown/72291/1)).

Ghamsari leads Blend's corporate and product strategy toward what the company calls a simple, transparent and accessible financial services ecosystem ([Blend](https://blend.com/bio/nima-ghamsari/)). His defining choice was to retrofit banks rather than disrupt them: Blend sells cloud software to lenders, so a borrower applies through their own bank's website while Blend runs the workflow underneath ([Forbes](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/how-nima-ghamsaris-blend-labs-prevented-the-us-mortgage-market-meltdown/72291/1)).

## An immigrant childhood built on a mortgage

Ghamsari was born in [Iran](/places/iran/) and moved to the United States in 1987, when he was a year old, as his parents pursued graduate studies at the University of Michigan; the family then settled in Cincinnati, where his father taught mathematics at the University of Cincinnati and his mother taught chemistry at Xavier University ([Forbes](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/how-nima-ghamsaris-blend-labs-prevented-the-us-mortgage-market-meltdown/72291/1)). After years of renting, his parents were approved in 1998 for a low-down-payment mortgage and bought a plain two-story home in Cincinnati for about $100,000 — the rock, in Forbes' telling, on which the family prospered ([Forbes](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/how-nima-ghamsaris-blend-labs-prevented-the-us-mortgage-market-meltdown/72291/1)). The episode is the personal origin of Blend: a founder who had watched a mortgage change his own family's fortunes, and who later set out to make the process feel as easy as buying something online ([Forbes](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/how-nima-ghamsaris-blend-labs-prevented-the-us-mortgage-market-meltdown/72291/1)).

## Stanford, poker and Palantir

At Stanford, a need-based scholarship helped cover Ghamsari's tuition, but he also played online poker to pay for living expenses, with winnings extending well into six figures ([Forbes](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/how-nima-ghamsaris-blend-labs-prevented-the-us-mortgage-market-meltdown/72291/1)). He graduated in 2008 with a B.S. in computer science, by which point he had bought an Aston Martin with his poker winnings, and was recruited by Palantir Technologies ([Forbes](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/how-nima-ghamsaris-blend-labs-prevented-the-us-mortgage-market-meltdown/72291/1); [Blend](https://blend.com/bio/nima-ghamsari/)). At Palantir he was one of the first employees of its commercial group — a group he co-founded — working on major data challenges for the financial sector and advising Palantir's chief executive ([Blend](https://blend.com/bio/nima-ghamsari/); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/author/nima-ghamsari/)). The timing was fateful: he joined in 2008, just as the financial system began to collapse, and Palantir's post-crisis work put him inside America's teetering big banks, whose decades-old, paper-based systems gave them a poor grasp of their own mortgage exposures ([Forbes](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/how-nima-ghamsaris-blend-labs-prevented-the-us-mortgage-market-meltdown/72291/1)). After the crisis he worked directly with major banks and agencies on software to help underwater borrowers avoid foreclosure, an experience HousingWire credits with introducing him to mortgages ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/author/nima-ghamsari/)). He was named to Fortune's 40 Under 40 list in 2020 ([Blend](https://blend.com/bio/nima-ghamsari/)).

## Founding Blend: retrofit, don't disrupt

In 2012, aged 26, Ghamsari quit Palantir — walking away from stock options that would have paid out when Palantir went public in 2020 — and founded Blend with former quant trader Rosco Hill, engineer Eugene Marinelli and Erin Collard, head trader of billionaire [Peter Thiel](/people/peter-thiel/)'s Clarium Capital hedge fund ([Forbes](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/how-nima-ghamsaris-blend-labs-prevented-the-us-mortgage-market-meltdown/72291/1)). Early backing came from Thiel and Max Levchin, whose instalment-payments company [Affirm](/organizations/affirm/) later went public ([Forbes](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/how-nima-ghamsaris-blend-labs-prevented-the-us-mortgage-market-meltdown/72291/1)). The pitch was unfashionable: rather than disrupting banks, Blend would retrofit them, charging for software that made their lending faster and cheaper ([Forbes](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/how-nima-ghamsaris-blend-labs-prevented-the-us-mortgage-market-meltdown/72291/1)). Venture capitalists were sceptical, and through the first half of 2017 Blend had received just $67 million in outside funding ([Forbes](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/how-nima-ghamsaris-blend-labs-prevented-the-us-mortgage-market-meltdown/72291/1)).

The catalyst was a competitor. In 2015 Dan Gilbert's Quicken Loans launched Rocket Mortgage, cutting closing times from more than 40 days to about a month, and every bank board suddenly wanted a digital answer ([Forbes](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/how-nima-ghamsaris-blend-labs-prevented-the-us-mortgage-market-meltdown/72291/1)). Movement Mortgage had already taken the leap in 2014, becoming the first major lender to digitise origination and underwriting with Blend's technology ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/inside-blends-high-stakes-transformation/)). By 2020 Blend's software was used to process $1.4 trillion in mortgage and consumer loans, up nearly threefold on the prior year, saving an average of 7.3 days and $520 in operational costs per loan (MarketWise Advisors, via Forbes) ([Forbes](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/how-nima-ghamsaris-blend-labs-prevented-the-us-mortgage-market-meltdown/72291/1)).

## The IPO — and total control

Blend filed confidentially for an initial public offering in April 2021, and on 16 July 2021 Ghamsari rang the opening bell on Wall Street as Blend listed on the [New York Stock Exchange](/organizations/new-york-stock-exchange/) under the ticker BLND ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/inside-blends-high-stakes-transformation/); [Finovate](https://finovate.com/blend-raises-360-million-in-ipo-achieves-4-billion-valuation/)). The offering sold 20 million Class A shares at $18 apiece, raising $360 million, and the shares closed the first day at $20.90 ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-is-now-worth-4-6b-now-what/)). The valuation most often quoted for the listing is about $4 billion, but that is the pricing valuation: the first-day close implied about $4.6 billion — the numbers differ by timing, not by fact, and this profile flags both rather than picking one ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-is-now-worth-4-6b-now-what/); [Finovate](https://finovate.com/blend-raises-360-million-in-ipo-achieves-4-billion-valuation/)). The listing came despite Blend never having turned a profit: it lost $75 million on $96 million of revenue in 2020, according to its S-1 filing ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-is-now-worth-4-6b-now-what/)). What the IPO did give Ghamsari was control: he controls 100% of Blend's Class B shares, giving him complete control of the firm ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-is-now-worth-4-6b-now-what/)).

## The crash and the platform pivot

The boom did not last. Nearly two years after going public, with its mortgage-heavy client base reeling from the Federal Reserve's unprecedented series of interest rate hikes, Blend's losses had ballooned to over $1 billion, including a $796 million loss in 2022 alone ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/inside-blends-high-stakes-transformation/)). Operating expenses in 2022 jumped to $835.8 million from $313.2 million in 2021, with $450 million related to the [Title365](/organizations/title365/) acquisition — the title company Blend had agreed to buy for $422 million in March 2021 — and the company carried a $225 million term loan due in 2025 ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/inside-blends-high-stakes-transformation/); [Forbes](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/how-nima-ghamsaris-blend-labs-prevented-the-us-mortgage-market-meltdown/72291/1)). By 3 April 2023 the stock traded at 99 cents a share and the market capitalisation had fallen to about $239 million ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/inside-blends-high-stakes-transformation/)). Ghamsari's answer was to rebuild Blend as a platform company: in March 2023 it launched Blend Builder, a composable-tech platform letting lenders assemble their own origination products from modular blocks — pricing, income verification, closing — in a low-code, drag-and-drop environment ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/inside-blends-high-stakes-transformation/)).

## The 2026 AI reset: Autopilot

Ghamsari has been unusually candid about what went wrong. "It probably gave me an inflated sense of how well I was executing," he told Fortune of the boom era, adding "I had overestimated my operating ability" and "I made the company take on too many things" — prompting a reset toward being "really, really great at one thing" ([Fortune](https://thefifthskill.com/blends-post-ipo-reset-ceo-nima-ghamsari-bets-that-ai-can-turn-it-all-around/)). That one thing for 2026 is Autopilot, Blend's AI agent announced in early March 2026, which reads borrower documents, checks them and updates the file, turning work that took days into seconds ([Fortune](https://thefifthskill.com/blends-post-ipo-reset-ceo-nima-ghamsari-bets-that-ai-can-turn-it-all-around/)). Ghamsari frames it as an attack on the roughly $11,000 in human cost and hundreds of hours lenders spend per mortgage, and about 20% of Blend's customers adopted it within the first month ([Fortune](https://thefifthskill.com/blends-post-ipo-reset-ceo-nima-ghamsari-bets-that-ai-can-turn-it-all-around/)). By August 2026 Autopilot had processed more than 50,000 loans, with about 19% higher conversion for consumers going through it and cycle times three days shorter after three months ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/videos/nima-ghamsari-on-reimagining-mortgage-with-ai-everything-can-be-the-future/)). Ghamsari's stated ambition is to rebuild the process from the ground up rather than automate it incrementally — "the biggest mistake people make when going into AI is trying to be too incremental" — and he imagines borrowers, within five years, being fully approved and clear to close within five to ten minutes of starting with a lender ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/videos/nima-ghamsari-on-reimagining-mortgage-with-ai-everything-can-be-the-future/)). Fortune put the April 2026 market capitalisation at about $437 million and reported five consecutive profitable quarters ([Fortune](https://thefifthskill.com/blends-post-ipo-reset-ceo-nima-ghamsari-bets-that-ai-can-turn-it-all-around/)).
