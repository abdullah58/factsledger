Kim Seol (김설, born March 18, 2011) is a South Korean former child actress best known for two roles she played before turning seven: Jin-ju, the scene-stealing younger sister in tvN's *Reply 1988* (2015–2016), and the young Ayla in the Turkish-Korean co-production *Ayla: The Daughter of War* (2017) ([HanCinema](https://www.hanCinema.net/korean_Kim_Seol.php); [The Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10614537)). After *Ayla* she stepped back from acting and moved into gifted STEM education — a second act that Korea's major newspapers have documented year by year ([JoongAng Ilbo](https://www.joongang.co.kr/article/25381430)).

## Overview

Kim Seol's birthdate is listed as March 18, 2011 by the Korean-cinema database HanCinema, which also notes the alias Kim Sul ([HanCinema](https://www.hanCinema.net/korean_Kim_Seol.php)). The date is independently corroborated by Maeil Business Newspaper, which marked her 12th birthday on March 18, 2022 ([Maeil Business Newspaper](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/hot-issues/10258156)). She debuted at age three in the 2014 film *Ode to My Father* as the young Soon-yi ([The Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10614537)), and at four was cast as Seong Jin-ju in tvN's *Reply 1988* — the younger sister of Sun-woo, played by Go Kyung-pyo — a performance Korean press still cites for its natural delivery at that age ([JoongAng Ilbo](https://www.joongang.co.kr/article/25381430); [The Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10614537)). She is not Seolhyun: that is Kim Seol-hyun, the singer-actress and former member of the girl group AOA, an older performer of a different generation ([Apple TV](https://tv.apple.com/us/person/kim-seol-hyun/umc.cpc.3xbqe1qe26qr0vhukpkofolgj)). Nor is she the real-life Ayla — that is Kim Eun-ja, the woman whose wartime childhood the film dramatizes; Kim Seol played her as a child ([Maeil Business Newspaper](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/hot-issues/8355260)).

## From Jin-ju to young Ayla

*Reply 1988* made Kim Seol a national figure: the affectionate "선우 오빠~" ("Sun-woo oppa~") she delivered as the toddler Jin-ju is still quoted in Korean media, and the role earned her the nicknames "국민 아가" (national baby) and "국민 아역배우" (national child actor) ([Maeil Business Newspaper](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/culture/11465761)). At five she was cast as the young Ayla — the orphaned Korean girl of the Korean War — in *Ayla: The Daughter of War*, with Lee Kyung-jin as the adult Ayla ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633); [Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/cinema/2017/11/17/ayla-movie-that-binds-2-nations-hearts/amp)). A Maeil review singled out her performance, writing that she rendered the traumatized child's gradual opening to Süleyman — played by İsmail Hacıoğlu — with truthful, layered acting ([Maeil Business Newspaper](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/hot-issues/8355260)). The casting was not without controversy: Maeil's Star Today reported in January 2017 that a Korean father living in Turkey alleged his own five-year-old had first been promised the lead role, while the producer Dijital Sanatlar denied any contract had ever existed — a dispute neither side resolved publicly ([Maeil Business Newspaper](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/movies/7683094)).

## Meeting the real Ayla

Kim Seol's own words about the film survive in the Korean press: she said she wished all fathers would watch *Ayla*, and wanted to tell "Süleyman grandpa in heaven" — the real-life protagonist, who died in 2017 — that the film was opening in Korea ([Maeil Business Newspaper](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/hot-issues/8312762)). The film itself traveled well: it took the audience award at the 2018 Sedona International Film Festival and the editing award at the 2017 Cape Town International Film Festival ([Maeil Business Newspaper](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/hot-issues/8312762)). Then came the image the Korean press loved: in the winter of 2017–18, at a Turkish film festival, Kim Seol was photographed with Kim Eun-ja — the real-life Ayla — both wearing hanbok ([topstarnews](https://www.topstarnews.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=434184)). She posted the photo to her mother-operated Instagram captioned "두 아일라" (two Aylas), noting that the hanbok had been dressed on them by the designer Park Sul-nyeo ([topstarnews](https://www.topstarnews.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=434184)).

## The academics pivot

Since *Ayla*, Kim Seol has largely stayed out of acting, focusing on academics after enrolling in a local gifted education center's invention program in 2021 ([The Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10614537)). She completed it that December, was elected class president within two weeks of starting middle school, and earned a model-student commendation ([Maeil Business Newspaper](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/culture/11465761); [kbizoom](https://kbizoom.com/reply-1988s-jin-joo-child-actress-kim-seol-elected-as-class-president/)), then completed another gifted ceremony in November 2025 ([The Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10614537)). The aptitude runs in the family: her older brother Kim Gyeom was introduced on SBS's *Gifted Discovery Team* as a math prodigy who grasped high-school-level mathematics in fifth grade ([Maeil Business Newspaper](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/culture/11465761)). Korean gifted centers admit students only from fourth grade through second grade of middle school ([The Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10614537)) — so her 2021 enrollment, at age ten in fourth grade, hit the earliest possible entry point. The performance door may not be closed: in November 2024 she posted a musical-rehearsal photo ([kbizoom](https://kbizoom.com/kim-seol-reply-1988-middle-school-transformation-instagram-video/)).

## FAQ

**Is Kim Seol the same person as Seolhyun?**
No. Seolhyun — born Kim Seol-hyun (김설현) — is the singer-actress and former member of the K-pop girl group AOA, an older performer of a different generation ([Apple TV](https://tv.apple.com/us/person/kim-seol-hyun/umc.cpc.3xbqe1qe26qr0vhukpkofolgj)). Kim Seol (김설), born March 18, 2011, is the former child actress of *Reply 1988* and *Ayla* ([HanCinema](https://www.hanCinema.net/korean_Kim_Seol.php)).

**Is Kim Seol still acting?**
Effectively no, for now. Since *Ayla* (2017) she has focused on academics, enrolling in a gifted education center's invention program in 2021 and completing further gifted ceremonies through 2025 ([The Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10614537)). JoongAng Ilbo reported her future acting plans as unknown ([JoongAng Ilbo](https://www.joongang.co.kr/article/25381430)).

**Who is the real Ayla?**
The real Ayla is Kim Eun-ja (김은자), the Korean woman whose wartime childhood — rescued by Turkish soldier Süleyman Dilbirliği during the Korean War — the film dramatizes ([Maeil Business Newspaper](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/hot-issues/8355260)). Kim Seol played her as a five-year-old child; the two were photographed together in hanbok at a Turkish film festival in winter 2017–18 ([topstarnews](https://www.topstarnews.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=434184)).

**What was the casting controversy around Ayla?**
A January 2017 Maeil Star Today report said a Korean father alleged his five-year-old daughter had been promised the lead role, then displaced when another child actress was cast ([Maeil Business Newspaper](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/movies/7683094)). Producer Dijital Sanatlar denied any contract had existed ([Maeil Business Newspaper](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/movies/7683094)). The article never named the actress, and the dispute was never resolved publicly ([Maeil Business Newspaper](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/movies/7683094)).
