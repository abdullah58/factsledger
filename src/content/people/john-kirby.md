John F. Kirby is a retired United States Navy rear admiral whose career is unusual for one reason that quick biographies tend to bury: he served as the Pentagon's chief spokesman under two presidents a decade apart — the first uniformed officer to hold that job under [Secretary of Defense Chuck Hagel](https://www.hstoday.us/subject-matter-areas/intelligence/kirby-moving-from-dod-to-serve-as-national-security-council-coordinator-for-strategic-communications/) in the Obama administration, and then again as Assistant to the Secretary of Defense for Public Affairs under Lloyd Austin in the Biden administration ([University of Nebraska Omaha biography](http://unomaha.edu/special-events/chuck-hagel-forum-in-global-leadership/speaker-biographies/kirby-bio.pdf); [Homeland Security Today](https://www.hstoday.us/subject-matter-areas/intelligence/kirby-moving-from-dod-to-serve-as-national-security-council-coordinator-for-strategic-communications/)). Between those tours he was the State Department's chief spokesman, a CNN analyst, and ultimately the White House's National Security Communications Advisor — and since November 2025 he has led the University of Chicago's Institute of Politics ([University of Chicago News](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/john-f-kirby-appointed-director-uchicagos-institute-politics)).

## A Navy public-affairs career

Kirby is a St. Petersburg, Florida native who graduated from the University of South Florida in 1985 with a bachelor's degree in history, then commissioned in the Navy in September 1986 after completing Officer Candidate School in Newport, Rhode Island ([U.S. Navy biography](https://www.navy.mil/Leadership/Bios/Display-Bio/Article/2227276/rear-admiral-john-kirby/)). He later earned a Master of Science in international relations from Troy State University and a Master of Arts in national security and strategic studies from the Naval War College ([University of Chicago News](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/john-f-kirby-appointed-director-uchicagos-institute-politics)).

His 28-plus years of uniformed service were built almost entirely around military public affairs, but he started as a surface warfare officer aboard the frigate USS Aubrey Fitch before shifting to communications billets that put him on some of the Navy's most visible stages: public affairs officer for the USS Forrestal, a staff tour with Commander, 2nd Fleet aboard USS Mount Whitney, an instructor assignment at the Naval Academy, public affairs officer for the Blue Angels, and editor-in-chief of *All Hands* magazine ([U.S. Navy biography](https://www.navy.mil/Leadership/Bios/Display-Bio/Article/2227276/rear-admiral-john-kirby/)). He rose to lead the whole community as Chief of Naval Information, directing a public-affairs enterprise of more than 2,700 people, and his final Navy posting was deputy assistant secretary of defense for media operations ([U.S. Navy biography](https://www.navy.mil/Leadership/Bios/Display-Bio/Article/2227276/rear-admiral-john-kirby/)).

Along the way he became the personal spokesman for the men at the very top of the chain: in 2011 he served as chief spokesman for Adm. Mike Mullen, then Chairman of the [Joint Chiefs of Staff](/organizations/joint-chiefs-of-staff/), and later as the military spokesman for Secretary of Defense Leon Panetta ([University of Nebraska Omaha biography](http://unomaha.edu/special-events/chuck-hagel-forum-in-global-leadership/speaker-biographies/kirby-bio.pdf)). He retired in 2015 at the rank of rear admiral (lower half) ([Homeland Security Today](https://www.hstoday.us/subject-matter-areas/intelligence/kirby-moving-from-dod-to-serve-as-national-security-council-coordinator-for-strategic-communications/)).

## Two tours as the Pentagon's voice

Kirby's first turn as Pentagon Press Secretary came under Secretary of Defense Chuck Hagel in the Obama administration, from 2013 to 2015 ([Homeland Security Today](https://www.hstoday.us/subject-matter-areas/intelligence/kirby-moving-from-dod-to-serve-as-national-security-council-coordinator-for-strategic-communications/)). The University of Nebraska Omaha biography states explicitly that he was "the first uniformed officer to hold the position of chief spokesman for the Department of Defense" — a distinction that captures how unusual his trajectory was: career public-affairs officers normally serve the flag officers; Kirby became the flag officer ([University of Nebraska Omaha biography](http://unomaha.edu/special-events/chuck-hagel-forum-in-global-leadership/speaker-biographies/kirby-bio.pdf)).

In May 2015 he moved to the State Department, serving as the department's spokesperson through January 2017 and as Assistant Secretary for the Bureau of Public Affairs from December 2015 to January 2017 ([Homeland Security Today](https://www.hstoday.us/subject-matter-areas/intelligence/kirby-moving-from-dod-to-serve-as-national-security-council-coordinator-for-strategic-communications/)). After the Obama administration ended, he spent 2017 to 2021 as a military and diplomatic analyst for [CNN](/organizations/cnn/) and as an adjunct lecturer at Georgetown University's School of Foreign Service ([University of Nebraska Omaha biography](http://unomaha.edu/special-events/chuck-hagel-forum-in-global-leadership/speaker-biographies/kirby-bio.pdf)).

The Biden administration brought him back to the Pentagon in 2021 as Assistant to the Secretary of Defense for Public Affairs, advising Lloyd Austin and serving as the department's chief spokesperson ([Homeland Security Today](https://www.hstoday.us/subject-matter-areas/intelligence/kirby-moving-from-dod-to-serve-as-national-security-council-coordinator-for-strategic-communications/)). In May 2022, President [Joe Biden](/people/joe-biden/) announced that Kirby would move to the White House as National Security Council Coordinator for Strategic Communications, a role housed at the NSC and reporting to the National Security Advisor ([Homeland Security Today](https://www.hstoday.us/subject-matter-areas/intelligence/kirby-moving-from-dod-to-serve-as-national-security-council-coordinator-for-strategic-communications/)). He served as the White House National Security Communications Advisor on foreign and defense policy issues from 2022 to 2025, coordinating interagency national security messaging and speaking for the administration on those issues ([University of Chicago News](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/john-f-kirby-appointed-director-uchicagos-institute-politics)).

## The blunt briefing style

Kirby became known for plain-spoken, sometimes combative briefings — a style on full display in February 2024, when [Tucker Carlson](/people/tucker-carlson/)'s interview with [Vladimir Putin](/people/vladimir-putin/) prompted a White House pre-buttal. "I don't think we need another interview with Vladimir Putin to understand his brutality," Kirby said, adding ahead of the interview's release: "Remember, you're listening to Vladimir Putin. And you shouldn't take at face value anything he has to say." ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/2/8/what-is-next-for-ukraine-after-tucker-carlsons-interview-with-vladimir-putin); [Associated Press](https://www.khaosodenglish.com/news/2024/02/09/putin-uses-tucker-carlson-interview-to-press-his-ukraine-narrative/)) The episode captured how Kirby operated at the podium: short sentences, direct judgment, no hedging.

## The University of Chicago

On October 20, 2025, the University of Chicago announced that Kirby would become director of its Institute of Politics, effective November 15 ([University of Chicago News](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/john-f-kirby-appointed-director-uchicagos-institute-politics)). The nonpartisan institute, founded in 2013 by David Axelrod, describes its mission as promoting civic engagement, civil discourse, and free expression among students ([University of Chicago News](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/john-f-kirby-appointed-director-uchicagos-institute-politics)). Kirby succeeded Heidi Heitkamp, the former U.S. senator from North Dakota who led the institute from 2023 to 2025, and Axelrod — the founding director and chair of the board of advisors — praised him as "a thoughtful, proven leader, whose lifelong devotion to service, extraordinary experience, and demonstrated commitment to constructive dialogue completely align with our values and mission" ([University of Chicago News](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/john-f-kirby-appointed-director-uchicagos-institute-politics)).
