**Antoine Fuqua** is an American film director who began in 1990s music videos — directing promos for Prince, Toni Braxton and Stevie Wonder — and broke through with the 2001 crime thriller *Training Day*, for which Denzel Washington won the Academy Award for Best Actor ([BET](https://www.bet.com/article/j683qd/antoine-fuqua-i-m-not-a-black-filmmaker-i-m-a-filmmaker); [The Fader](https://www.thefader.com/2023/01/19/michael-jackson-biopic-antoine-fuqua)). In January 2023 he was announced as the director of *Michael*, the estate-backed [Michael Jackson](/people/michael-jackson/) biopic written by three-time Oscar nominee John Logan ([The Fader](https://www.thefader.com/2023/01/19/michael-jackson-biopic-antoine-fuqua)). Released in April 2026, the film became the first biopic ever to cross $1 billion at the global box office — the highest-grossing film of his career, and of studio Lionsgate's history ([Mint](https://www.livemint.com/entertainment/michael-crosses-1-billion-worldwide-becomes-highest-grossing-biopic-in-hollywood-history-11783888904398.html)).

The Pittsburgh native's path to that record runs through two long partnerships — with music, and with Washington. Fuqua has said his "first films" were music videos, and that Jackson's own MTV-era work — "the first Black artist to play in heavy rotation on MTV" — influenced him to make music videos ([The Fader](https://www.thefader.com/2023/01/19/michael-jackson-biopic-antoine-fuqua)). With Washington he has made five features, from *Training Day* to the *Equalizer* trilogy and the *Magnificent Seven* remake ([BET](https://www.bet.com/article/j683qd/antoine-fuqua-i-m-not-a-black-filmmaker-i-m-a-filmmaker); [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/entertainment/2025/11/09/who-is-antoine-fuqua-director-of-michael-jackson-biopic-michael.html)).

## Directing Michael: from the 2023 announcement to the billion-dollar record

The *Michael* job was reported in January 2023 by *The Hollywood Reporter* and *Deadline*: Fuqua would direct, Graham King would produce alongside the co-executors of Jackson's estate, and Lionsgate would distribute ([The Fader](https://www.thefader.com/2023/01/19/michael-jackson-biopic-antoine-fuqua)). The appointment closed a loop the director had drawn himself: the man who learned filmmaking through music videos would now direct the artist whose videos had pulled him into the form ([The Fader](https://www.thefader.com/2023/01/19/michael-jackson-biopic-antoine-fuqua)).

Mid-production, the film became a legal case study. After attorneys for the estate discovered a settlement clause barring the depiction or mention of accuser Jordan Chandler in any film, the original third act — built around the 1993 allegations — was scrapped entirely. A 22-day reshoot in [Los Angeles](/places/los-angeles/) in June 2025 added $10–15 million to the $155 million budget; the estate covered the cost as its own error and took an equity stake ([The Playlist](https://theplaylist.net/michael-lionsgates-antoine-fuqua-directed-biopic-underwent-15m-reshoots-after-child-abuse-allegation-scenes-in-third-act-were-cut-report-20260407/)). The rebuilt film ends with Jackson's 1988 *Bad* era, its dramatic weight shifted to his father [Joe Jackson](/people/joe-jackson/), played by Colman Domingo ([The Playlist](https://theplaylist.net/michael-lionsgates-antoine-fuqua-directed-biopic-underwent-15m-reshoots-after-child-abuse-allegation-scenes-in-third-act-were-cut-report-20260407/)).

Ahead of release, Fuqua addressed the allegations in a *New Yorker* interview, saying he was not convinced Jackson did what he was accused of, voicing skepticism of some accusers' parents while conceding he did not know the truth and that "sometimes people do nasty things for money" ([NME](https://www.nme.com/news/film/michael-director-antoine-fuqua-questions-jackson-accusers-integrity-sometimes-people-do-nasty-things-for-money-3941640)). Reviews were mixed, but audiences made *Michael* the biggest opening ever for a musical biopic and then the first biopic in history past $1 billion, overtaking *Bohemian Rhapsody* (~$911 million) and *Oppenheimer* ($975 million) ([Mint](https://www.livemint.com/entertainment/michael-crosses-1-billion-worldwide-becomes-highest-grossing-biopic-in-hollywood-history-11783888904398.html)).

## From music videos to Training Day

Before features, Fuqua was a music-video director, helming 1990s promos for Prince, Toni Braxton and Stevie Wonder ([The Fader](https://www.thefader.com/2023/01/19/michael-jackson-biopic-antoine-fuqua)). An electrical engineering graduate, he moved into features with the Chow Yun-fat action vehicle *The Replacement Killers* (1998), then proved himself in both drama and action ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/entertainment/2025/11/09/who-is-antoine-fuqua-director-of-michael-jackson-biopic-michael.html)). His breakthrough, the police procedural *Training Day* (2001), followed a rookie cop (Ethan Hawke) and his corrupt senior partner (Denzel Washington) through a single day with the LAPD; Washington's performance won him the Academy Award for Best Actor, with Hawke nominated for Best Supporting Actor ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/entertainment/2025/11/09/who-is-antoine-fuqua-director-of-michael-jackson-biopic-michael.html)).

Fuqua's range extends beyond narrative action: he directed and executive-produced HBO's multi-award-winning documentary *What's My Name: Muhammad Ali*, and he has worked with recording artists across his feature career — Eminem, 50 Cent and Lil Wayne among them ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/entertainment/2025/11/09/who-is-antoine-fuqua-director-of-michael-jackson-biopic-michael.html); [BET](https://www.bet.com/article/j683qd/antoine-fuqua-i-m-not-a-black-filmmaker-i-m-a-filmmaker)).

## The Denzel Washington partnership and the Equalizer era

The defining collaboration of Fuqua's career is with Denzel Washington — who called Fuqua to ask him to direct *The Equalizer* ([BET](https://www.bet.com/article/j683qd/antoine-fuqua-i-m-not-a-black-filmmaker-i-m-a-filmmaker)). Asked in 2014 about casting actors of color in roles that default to white men, Fuqua said it is a conscious choice — he tells casting directors to bring him "the best actors that fit this role" — and recalled pitching Washington for the Yul Brynner part in the *Magnificent Seven* remake: "the room kind of went silent for a minute and then it was like, 'great idea'" ([BET](https://www.bet.com/article/j683qd/antoine-fuqua-i-m-not-a-black-filmmaker-i-m-a-filmmaker)). His broader position is stated in that interview's title: "I'm not a Black filmmaker, I'm a filmmaker" ([BET](https://www.bet.com/article/j683qd/antoine-fuqua-i-m-not-a-black-filmmaker-i-m-a-filmmaker)).

The *Equalizer* trilogy's combined worldwide gross of nearly $600 million is the relevant benchmark ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/entertainment/2025/11/09/who-is-antoine-fuqua-director-of-michael-jackson-biopic-michael.html)) — because *Michael*'s $1 billion means a single Fuqua biopic out-earned the entire nine-year franchise that had defined his career ([Mint](https://www.livemint.com/entertainment/michael-crosses-1-billion-worldwide-becomes-highest-grossing-biopic-in-hollywood-history-11783888904398.html)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is Antoine Fuqua?**
Antoine Fuqua is the American director of *Training Day* (2001) and the *Equalizer* trilogy. Announced as director of the Michael Jackson biopic *Michael* in January 2023, he helmed the Lionsgate film that became the first biopic to cross $1 billion at the global box office.

**What is Antoine Fuqua best known for?**
For *Training Day*, which won Denzel Washington the Best Actor Oscar; for the three-film *Equalizer* franchise with Washington; and for starting in 1990s music videos. His 2026 *Michael* is now his highest-grossing film.

**Did Denzel Washington win an Oscar for *Training Day*?**
Yes — the Academy Award for Best Actor for playing corrupt LAPD detective Alonzo Harris, with co-star Ethan Hawke nominated for Best Supporting Actor. The win anchored Fuqua's breakthrough and his long collaboration with Washington.

**Why did *Michael* need expensive reshoots?**
The Jackson estate's settlement with accuser Jordan Chandler barred his depiction or mention in any film. The original third act was scrapped, and a 22-day Los Angeles reshoot added $10–15 million to the budget, paid by the estate.
