## Overview

Lauryn Hill is an American singer, rapper, songwriter and record producer born on 26 May 1975 in South Orange, New Jersey, in the [United States](/places/united-states/), whose single solo studio album, *The Miseducation of Lauryn Hill* (1998), won five Grammy Awards in one night, became the first hip-hop album to win Album of the Year, and was later certified diamond and ranked the greatest album of all time by Apple Music ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Lauryn-Hill); [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/lauryn-hill/9621/)). Before going solo she was the frontwoman of the Fugees, whose 1996 album *The Score* sold more than 18 million copies ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Lauryn-Hill)).

Hill's career is unusual in popular music: almost everything that defines her reputation rests on two records made within two years of each other, and on a solo career that effectively stopped after one album ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/05/apple-music-reveals-top-10-albums-of-all-time-on-100-best-list/)). She retreated from the spotlight after 1999, recorded no follow-up studio album, and spent the following decades touring sporadically, appearing on soundtracks and guest spots, and reuniting with the Fugees in fits and starts ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Lauryn-Hill); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/music/lauryn-hill)).

## From South Orange to the Fugees

Hill grew up in a musical household: her father Mal was a computer programmer who sang at weddings, her mother Valerie was an English teacher who loved the piano, and the family listened to Motown records ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/music/lauryn-hill)). At thirteen she sang Smokey Robinson's "Who's Lovin' You" at an amateur showcase at the Apollo Theater in Harlem, winning over a famously blunt audience that had first booed her ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/music/lauryn-hill)). As a teenager she acted, with a recurring role in the soap opera *As the World Turns*, a part in *Sister Act 2: Back in the Habit* (1993) alongside Whoopi Goldberg, and a small role in Steven Soderbergh's *King of the Hill* (1993) ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/music/lauryn-hill)).

At school she met Prakazrel "Pras" Michel, and with his cousin Wyclef Jean the trio formed a group first called the Tranzlator Crew, renamed the Fugees in 1993, which Hill helped finance with her acting earnings ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Lauryn-Hill)). Their debut, *Blunted on Reality* (1994), drew poor reviews, but *The Score* (1996) — built around a remake of Roberta Flack's 1973 hit "Killing Me Softly with His Song" — sold more than 18 million copies and won two Grammys, including Best Rap Album ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Lauryn-Hill)). Hill abandoned a history degree at Columbia University as the group took off, and in 1996 she founded the Refugee Project to aid underprivileged youths ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Lauryn-Hill)).

## The Miseducation of Lauryn Hill

Hill recorded her solo debut in early 1998 in Jamaica, at the Bob Marley Museum Studio, and *The Miseducation of Lauryn Hill* was released on 25 August 1998 by Ruffhouse and Columbia Records ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Lauryn-Hill); [Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/649379-first-female-rapper-to-reach-riaa-diamond-status)). It debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200, selling 422,624 copies in its first week — then a record for a female artist — driven by the No. 1 hit "Doo Wop (That Thing)" ([Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/649379-first-female-rapper-to-reach-riaa-diamond-status); [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/lauryn-hill/9621/)). The album's neo-soul sound bridged hip-hop, rhythm and blues and mainstream pop, with deeply personal lyrics about self, family and community ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Lauryn-Hill)).

At the 1999 Grammy Awards she was nominated ten times and won five: Album of the Year, Best New Artist, Best R&B Album, Best R&B Song and Best Female R&B Vocal Performance ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/lauryn-hill/9621/)). She became the first woman to win five or more awards in a single night, and the album is credited as the first hip-hop album ever to earn Album of the Year ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/lauryn-hill/9621/)). The record's afterlife was complicated by a lawsuit from musicians and producers who said they had not been properly credited for their work; the case was settled out of court ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/music/lauryn-hill)). By then Hill had a son, Zion, born in 1997 with Rohan Marley, son of reggae icon Bob Marley ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Lauryn-Hill)).

## Retreat, return and reunion

Success brought pressure Hill did not want. She followed her debut with the two-disc live album *MTV Unplugged No. 2.0* in 2002 — an unpolished acoustic performance that sold poorly — and co-headlined the Smokin' Grooves tour with OutKast later that year, but released no second studio album ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Lauryn-Hill)). A Fugees reunion attempt between 2004 and 2006 produced concerts and a few songs but no album, and for the rest of the decade her recorded output was limited to guest appearances and film-soundtrack songs ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/music/lauryn-hill); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Lauryn-Hill)).

In 2012 Hill pleaded guilty to failing to file tax returns for 2005–07 and was sentenced to three months in prison and three months in home confinement; after her release in 2013 she issued the single "Neurotic Society (Compulsory Mix)" to fulfil a court requirement ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Lauryn-Hill); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/music/lauryn-hill)). Later work included six tracks on *Nina Revisited… A Tribute to Nina Simone* (2015) and "Guarding the Gates" for the film *Queen & Slim* (2019) ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Lauryn-Hill)). The Fugees finally reunited in 2023 at the Global Citizen Festival in Central Park, [New York City](/places/new-york-city/), and Hill announced a tour marking the album's 25th anniversary — but the tour was cancelled the following year ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/music/lauryn-hill)).

## Records, rankings and canonisation

Hill's chart and institutional record is what keeps a one-album solo career at the centre of hip-hop history. The RIAA certified *The Miseducation of Lauryn Hill* diamond in February 2021 for 10 million units in the United States ([Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/649379-first-female-rapper-to-reach-riaa-diamond-status)), making Hill the first female rapper to reach the milestone — a commercial fact that underpins the album's later canonisation by the Library of Congress and Apple Music. The Library of Congress had already selected the album for the 2014 National Recording Registry, describing it as the breakthrough recording that fuses soul, rhythm and blues, rap and reggae ([Library Journal infoDOCKET](http://www.infodocket.com/2015/03/25/library-of-congress-recordings-by-the-doors-steve-martin-radiohead-and-others-added-to-national-recording-registry/)).

Billboard has ranked her twice, and the two lists tell a story about how her standing shifted. In 2015 its 10 Greatest Rappers of All Time list placed Hill seventh — the sole woman on it — with [Lil Wayne](/people/lil-wayne/) tenth ([SA Hip Hop Mag](https://sahiphopmag.co.za/2015/11/check-out-the-10-greatest-rappers-of-all-time-by-billboard/)). A decade later, Billboard's staff list of the 25 greatest female rappers put [Nicki Minaj](/people/nicki-minaj/) first, Missy Elliott second and Hill third, with Minaj's case resting on 149 Hot 100 entries and the highest-grossing tour by a female rapper ([Black Enterprise](https://www.blackenterprise.com/nicki-minaj-best-female-rapper-of-all-time/)). Apple Music then ranked *Miseducation* No. 1 on its 100 Best Albums list in May 2024, calling it Hill's debut — and only — solo studio album ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/05/apple-music-reveals-top-10-albums-of-all-time-on-100-best-list/)). In February 2026 she became a first-time nominee for the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame, one of 17 performer nominees for that year's class ([Rock & Roll Hall of Fame](https://rockhall.com/press-release/rock-roll-hall-of-fame-foundation-reveals-2026-performer-nominees-list/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**How many Grammy Awards has Lauryn Hill won?**

Hill has won eight Grammy Awards from 19 nominations ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/lauryn-hill/9621/)). Five of those wins came in a single night at the 1999 ceremony, for *The Miseducation of Lauryn Hill*, including Album of the Year and Best New Artist. Her other wins include two as a member of the Fugees for *The Score* in 1997.

**What is Lauryn Hill's most famous album?**

*The Miseducation of Lauryn Hill* (1998), her debut and only solo studio album ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/05/apple-music-reveals-top-10-albums-of-all-time-on-100-best-list/)). It debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200, won five Grammys, was certified diamond by the RIAA in 2021 for 10 million US units, and was ranked No. 1 on Apple Music's 100 Best Albums list in 2024 ([Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/649379-first-female-rapper-to-reach-riaa-diamond-status)).

**Was Lauryn Hill in the Fugees?**

Yes. Hill formed the group as a teenager with Prakazrel "Pras" Michel and Wyclef Jean, first as the Tranzlator Crew and from 1993 as the Fugees ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Lauryn-Hill)). Their second album, *The Score* (1996), sold more than 18 million copies and won two Grammys. The group broke up in the late 1990s and reunited on stage in 2023.

**Is Lauryn Hill in the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame?**

Not yet. Hill was named a first-time nominee for the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame class of 2026, announced on 25 February 2026, as one of 17 performer nominees ([Rock & Roll Hall of Fame](https://rockhall.com/press-release/rock-roll-hall-of-fame-foundation-reveals-2026-performer-nominees-list/)). The inductees were to be revealed in April 2026, with the ceremony in the fall.
