## Overview

Robert William "Bob" Fioretti (born 8 March 1953) is a [Chicago](/places/chicago/) civil-rights attorney and former alderman who represented the city's 2nd Ward from 2007 to 2015, then ran unsuccessfully for higher office seven times, switching from the Democratic Party to the Republican Party in 2022 and becoming the Republican nominee for Illinois attorney general in 2026 ([Lake Forest Journal](https://lakeforestjournal.com/articles/fioretti-seeks-attorney-general-post-against-raoul-mva061ft)). A litigator involved in more than 500 civil-rights cases, he also represented the family of Baby Tamia in a custody case that changed Illinois adoption law, and he acted for the [Copernicus Foundation](/organizations/copernicus-foundation/) in its 2020 lawsuit against the [City of Chicago](/organizations/city-of-chicago/) over a [Jefferson Park](/places/jefferson-park/) firehouse sale ([Forest Park Review](https://www.forestparkreview.com/2024/10/14/who-is-running-for-cook-county-states-attorney/); [Chicago Sun-Times](https://chicago.suntimes.com/2020/11/11/21561394/chicago-firehouse-sale-copernicus-foundation-lawsuit-jefferson-park)).

## A civil-rights lawyer

Fioretti built his public reputation in the courtroom before he ever held office. As senior supervising attorney in the General Litigation Division of Chicago's Corporation Counsel under Mayor Harold Washington, he was involved in more than 500 civil-rights cases and personally tried more than 100, and he was repeatedly appointed a special assistant attorney general of Illinois and a special assistant state's attorney in Cook, DuPage and Will counties ([Forest Park Review](https://www.forestparkreview.com/2024/10/14/who-is-running-for-cook-county-states-attorney/)). Before winning elected office he represented the family of Baby Tamia in a 2005 interstate custody case that led to changes in Illinois adoption law ([Lake Forest Journal](https://lakeforestjournal.com/articles/fioretti-seeks-attorney-general-post-against-raoul-mva061ft)). In 2022 he was one of the lawyers for Dolton Mayor Tiffany Henyard in her challenge to a recall referendum, a case of first impression in which the Illinois Appellate Court ruled both referenda invalid and permanently barred certification of the results ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/illinois/court-of-appeals-first-appellate-district/2022/1-22-0898-0.html)).

## Alderman of the 2nd Ward

Fioretti won his council seat in 2007 by unseating 14-year incumbent Madeline Haithcock in a run-off, and he was re-elected in 2011 with 55% of the vote against five opponents, his closest rival taking about 31% of 14,494 votes cast ([ChicagoTalks](https://chicagotalks.colum.edu/despite-throat-cancer-fioretti-will-serve-the-2nd-ward-again/)). He also served two terms as the 2nd Ward's Democratic committeeman, and he voted against six of the eight city budgets presented during his tenure, citing cuts to mental-health services and police staffing ([Forest Park Review](https://www.forestparkreview.com/2024/10/14/who-is-running-for-cook-county-states-attorney/); [The Chicagoland Journal](https://thechicagolandjournal.com/bob-fioretti-makes-case-for-experience-independence-in-illinois-attorney-general-race/)). Diagnosed with throat cancer in late 2010, he set aside a possible mayoral run to seek re-election instead, and he has since said he survived stage-four cancer and has fully recovered ([ChicagoTalks](https://chicagotalks.colum.edu/despite-throat-cancer-fioretti-will-serve-the-2nd-ward-again/); [The Chicagoland Journal](https://thechicagolandjournal.com/bob-fioretti-makes-case-for-experience-independence-in-illinois-attorney-general-race/)). One measure of his independence: he voted with Mayor Richard M. Daley only 36% of the time, according to University of Illinois at Chicago professor Dick Simpson ([ChicagoTalks](https://chicagotalks.colum.edu/despite-throat-cancer-fioretti-will-serve-the-2nd-ward-again/)). He says he helped bring about 8,000 jobs to the ward and organised job fairs for residents and veterans ([The Chicagoland Journal](https://thechicagolandjournal.com/bob-fioretti-makes-case-for-experience-independence-in-illinois-attorney-general-race/)).

## Seven campaigns and a party switch

After leaving the council in 2015, Fioretti ran for Chicago mayor twice (2015 and 2019), for the Illinois State Senate in 2016, for Cook County Board president in 2018 and 2022, and for Cook County State's Attorney in 2020 and 2024 — losing all seven races ([Lake Forest Journal](https://lakeforestjournal.com/articles/fioretti-seeks-attorney-general-post-against-raoul-mva061ft)). In 2022 he left the Democratic Party for the Republican Party, saying "the Democratic Party left me. They left family values, fiscal responsibility and freedom behind" ([Lake Forest Journal](https://lakeforestjournal.com/articles/fioretti-seeks-attorney-general-post-against-raoul-mva061ft)). He announced his run for Illinois attorney general on 14 October 2025 and won the March 2026 Republican primary with 495,600 votes — 99.41% of the Republican primary vote — a near-unanimous result for a candidate who had been a Democrat four years earlier ([Illinois State Board of Elections](https://www.elections.il.gov/ElectionOperations/ElectionVoteTotals.aspx?ElectionID=116)). In the 3 November 2026 general election, the [Associated Press](/organizations/associated-press/) called the race for incumbent Kwame Raoul at 10:55 p.m., with Raoul at 61.5% with 85% of ballots counted; Fioretti had not conceded that night ([The Daily Northwestern](https://dailynorthwestern.com/2026/11/04/city/longtime-democratic-illinois-attorney-general-kwame-raoul-projected-to-win-reelection/)).

## FAQ

### Who is Robert Fioretti?

Robert "Bob" Fioretti is a Chicago civil-rights attorney and former alderman who represented the city's 2nd Ward from 2007 to 2015. He later ran unsuccessfully for mayor, State Senate, County Board president and State's Attorney, switched to the Republican Party in 2022, and was the Republican nominee for Illinois attorney general in 2026.

### What did Fioretti do as a lawyer?

He was a senior supervising attorney in Chicago's Corporation Counsel under Mayor Harold Washington, involved in more than 500 civil-rights cases and personally trying more than 100. He later practised governmental law and complex litigation, represented the Baby Tamia family in an adoption case, and defended Dolton's mayor against a recall referendum.

### What was Fioretti's role in the Copernicus Foundation firehouse case?

He was the Foundation's attorney when it sued the City of Chicago in November 2020 after the city agreed to sell a former Jefferson Park firehouse to an [Ambrosia Homes](/organizations/ambrosia-homes-development/) affiliate effectively for $10, rejecting the Foundation's $300,000 offer. Fioretti called for "an open, transparent and fair process."

### Why did Fioretti switch parties in 2022?

He said "the Democratic Party left me. They left family values, fiscal responsibility and freedom behind." The switch came after two decades as a Democrat, including two terms as 2nd Ward Democratic committeeman, and preceded his runs for County Board president and State's Attorney as a Republican.

### Has Fioretti ever won an election?

Yes, three times: the 2nd Ward aldermanic elections of 2007 and 2011, and the 2026 Republican primary for Illinois attorney general, which he won with 99.41% of the vote. He lost all seven general-election campaigns he contested after leaving the City Council, including the 2026 attorney-general race.
