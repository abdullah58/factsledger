## Overview

Aman Sanger is the co-founder and chief operating officer of [Anysphere](/organizations/anysphere/), the company behind Cursor, the AI code editor that became one of the defining developer tools of the AI era ([Gold House](https://goldhouse.org/people/aman-sanger-sualeh-asif/)). Born in New York to Indian-origin parents, he began coding at 14, studied Computer Science at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](/universities/massachusetts-institute-of-technology/), and left MIT in 2022 with three classmates to build Anysphere ([Gold House](https://goldhouse.org/people/aman-sanger-sualeh-asif/); [OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/four-cursor-co-founders-turn-billionaires-in-their-mid-20s-after-spacex-acquisition/)). As chief operating officer he owned the commercial side of the company — product strategy, distribution, and community building — while his co-founders carried the core modelling work ([Gold House](https://goldhouse.org/people/aman-sanger-sualeh-asif/)).

That division of labour is the part of the Cursor story most coverage skips. The headlines track valuations: a $29.3 billion Series D in November 2025 that made all four founders billionaires on paper, then a $60 billion SpaceX acquisition in 2026 ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/this-ai-coding-startup-just-minted-4-new-billionaires/91265014); [Latent Space](https://latent.space/wiki/anysphere/)). Sanger's own record, in his own words from 2023, is about something narrower and more durable: building an editor that does useful work after autocomplete, and selling it first to individual developers and teams before enterprises ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/11/anysphere-raises-8m-from-openai-to-build-an-ai-powered-ide/)).

## From MIT to a failed CAD tool — and the pivot to code

Sanger met [Michael Truell](/people/michael-truell/), [Sualeh Asif](/people/sualeh-asif/), and [Arvid Lunnemark](/people/arvid-lunnemark/) at MIT, where the four became close friends around a shared goal: an integrated development environment that could speed up common programming work such as debugging ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/11/anysphere-raises-8m-from-openai-to-build-an-ai-powered-ide/)). Before Anysphere, Sanger had built a deliberately broad commercial background, with internships at Google, Bridgewater Associates, and You.com, plus his own AI consultancy ([Gold House](https://goldhouse.org/people/aman-sanger-sualeh-asif/); [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/cursor)). That breadth — medical AI and enterprise machine learning alongside consumer search — later mapped directly onto his operating role, where the job was turning a research demo into a product developers would pay for.

The team's first product was not a code editor at all. With Truell, Sanger built an earlier project also called Cursor, for computer-aided design, training models from scratch to predict CAD operations ([Latent Space](https://latent.space/wiki/anysphere/)). Limited training data made larger models overfit, spatial reasoning about how drawing and extrusion operations changed a three-dimensional structure proved hard, and building plugins for SolidWorks and Onshape was difficult, followed by a brief text-to-image experiment ([Latent Space](https://latent.space/wiki/anysphere/)). Around December 2022, Sanger and Truell obtained early GPT-4 access through OpenAI's Converge program — before taking any OpenAI investment — and testing the model on generation, refactoring, editing, and debugging convinced Sanger that programming needed a broader interface than autocomplete ([Latent Space](https://latent.space/wiki/anysphere/)). Meanwhile Lunnemark and Asif, whom Sanger and Truell knew from MIT, had independently begun pursuing an AI programming environment, and after several months the two pairs joined forces ([Latent Space](https://latent.space/wiki/anysphere/)). The combined team raised a $400,000 pre-seed round in April 2022 and, by mid-2022, pivoted fully to software development after concluding that GitHub Copilot as a plugin was improving only incrementally ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/cursor)). Cursor launched in March 2023 as a fork of VS Code ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/cursor)).

## The operating brief: beyond autocomplete, teams before enterprises

Sanger's clearest public statement of the product came in October 2023, when Anysphere announced an $8 million seed round led by OpenAI's Startup Fund, bringing its total raised to $11 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/11/anysphere-raises-8m-from-openai-to-build-an-ai-powered-ide/)). "When people think 'AI plus coding,' their mind usually goes to AI-powered autocomplete," Sanger said by email. "We think this has been done particularly well by GitHub Copilot and others, so we're focused on features that come *after* autocomplete, like finding and fixing bugs and codebase Q&A" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/11/anysphere-raises-8m-from-openai-to-build-an-ai-powered-ide/)). On go-to-market he was equally specific: "For now, we're focused on the individual and teams experience over Enterprise," adding that in the long term Cursor would be "a no-brainer for enterprises, given the massive boost in developer productivity" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/11/anysphere-raises-8m-from-openai-to-build-an-ai-powered-ide/)). At that point the company was a five-person team with tens of thousands of users and annual recurring revenue already over $1 million, about a year after founding ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/11/anysphere-raises-8m-from-openai-to-build-an-ai-powered-ide/)).

The technical work behind that pitch included two pieces Sanger described in later founder interviews. The team's early prompting tool, Priompt, managed variable amounts of code, documentation, and conversation history within a fixed token budget, with rules that first removed older chat messages and then truncated file contents as space became scarce ([Latent Space](https://latent.space/wiki/anysphere/)). And the indexing system cached embedding vectors by code-chunk hash, so colleagues working on near-identical repositories did not repeatedly embed the same code ([Latent Space](https://latent.space/wiki/anysphere/)). Both are cost and latency work — the unglamorous infrastructure that lets an AI editor feel fast — and both fit the operating brief Gold House attributes to Sanger: removing the "bottlenecks" of software-engineering bandwidth in order to accelerate general AI progress ([Gold House](https://goldhouse.org/people/aman-sanger-sualeh-asif/)). He was also a Neo Scholar, part of the mentorship network for technical undergraduates that connected the founders with Silicon Valley investors and mentors early ([Forbes](https://www.europesays.com/ie/179193/); [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/cursor)).

## The billionaire round and the SpaceX chapter

The commercial bet paid off with unusual speed. Annual recurring revenue grew from $1 million in 2023 to $100 million in about 12 months, and by November 2025 the company announced a $2.3 billion Series D at a $29.3 billion valuation, with total funding reaching $3.38 billion ([Forbes](https://www.europesays.com/ie/179193/)). Inc. corroborates the round and its investor list — existing backers Accel, Thrive Capital, Andreessen Horowitz, and DST, joined by Coatue, NVIDIA, and Google — and reports that, according to Forbes, the valuation made all four founders, all in their mid-20s, into billionaires ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/this-ai-coding-startup-just-minted-4-new-billionaires/91265014)). Forbes estimated each founder held a 4.5% stake worth at least $1.3 billion, and put Cursor's reach at millions of developers on some 50,000 teams at enterprises including Nvidia, Adobe, Uber, Shopify, and PayPal, with more than $1 billion in annualised revenue ([Forbes](https://www.europesays.com/ie/179193/)). In October 2025 Cursor had also launched Composer, its own model trained to generate code faster and carry out file and codebase edits, reducing reliance on costly third-party models ([Forbes](https://www.europesays.com/ie/179193/); [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/this-ai-coding-startup-just-minted-4-new-billionaires/91265014)).

In 2026 the story moved from venture rounds to acquisition. The two companies had struck an agreement in April giving [SpaceX](/organizations/spacex/) the option to acquire Cursor for $60 billion or pay $10 billion for continued access to its models ([OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/four-cursor-co-founders-turn-billionaires-in-their-mid-20s-after-spacex-acquisition/)). Latent Space records an April 2026 SpaceXAI training partnership, a joint Grok 4.6 release on August 12, and an August 14 announcement that SpaceX had completed its acquisition of Cursor ([Latent Space](https://latent.space/wiki/anysphere/)). Later wealth estimates for the founders diverge sharply depending on the stake figure used — about $2.7 billion each on Forbes' 4.5% stakes at a $60 billion valuation, versus about $5.5 billion each on the Bloomberg Billionaires Index's roughly 9% stakes reported by OfficeChai — so this profile does not assert a single post-acquisition net worth for Sanger ([OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/four-cursor-co-founders-turn-billionaires-in-their-mid-20s-after-spacex-acquisition/)).
