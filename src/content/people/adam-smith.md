## Overview

First, the disambiguation, because the name demands it: this is **Adam Smith the congressman** — David Adam Smith, the Democrat who has represented Washington's 9th district in the U.S. House since 1997 — not the 18th-century Scottish economist Adam Smith who wrote *The Wealth of Nations* ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/member/adam-smith/S000510)). The economist has been dead for over two centuries; the congressman is, as of 2026, one of the most consequential Democrats on defense policy in the country.

Smith is the top Democrat on the House Armed Services Committee, a position he has held since 2010, and he has served as both the committee's chair and its ranking member across different Congresses ([Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/top-defense-democrat-vows-more-trump-scrutiny-if-house-flips); [Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/event/representative-adam-smith-on-how-congress-will-shape-us-defense-policy/)). What makes him worth profiling now is not the biography — though it's an unusually working-class one for a defense-policy heavyweight — but what he spent 2026 doing: building the legal and oversight infrastructure for a confrontation with the Trump administration's military moves, from the war-powers fight over the Iran strikes to a bill written specifically to stop a Qatari-gifted 747 from becoming a presidential library trophy ([House Armed Services Committee Democrats](https://democrats-armedservices.house.gov/2026/9/courtney-smith-introduce-bill-to-prohibit-qatari-gifted-air-force-one-from-becoming-president-trump-s-private-jet-or-library-trophy)).

## The 2026 oversight build-up

Smith's 2026 unfolded as a three-part campaign, and the least-covered part is also the most specific. On September 14, 2026, Smith joined Congressman Joe Courtney (D-Conn.), the ranking member of the House Seapower and Projection Forces Subcommittee, to introduce the **Safeguarding Taxpayer Investment for Executive Airlift Act** ([House Armed Services Committee Democrats](https://democrats-armedservices.house.gov/2026/9/courtney-smith-introduce-bill-to-prohibit-qatari-gifted-air-force-one-from-becoming-president-trump-s-private-jet-or-library-trophy)). The bill would prohibit the use of congressionally authorized or appropriated fiscal year 2027 funding to transfer the Qatari-gifted Boeing 747 — now being used as Air Force One, designated the VC-25B Bridge Aircraft — out of the possession of the [U.S. Air Force](/organizations/air-force/).

The backstory, as the committee's release tells it: the luxury jet arrived from Qatar with only about 1,000 flight hours, which President Trump himself described as practically "brand new," and then absorbed substantial taxpayer investment to retrofit it to presidential-transport standards ([House Armed Services Committee Democrats](https://democrats-armedservices.house.gov/2026/9/courtney-smith-introduce-bill-to-prohibit-qatari-gifted-air-force-one-from-becoming-president-trump-s-private-jet-or-library-trophy)). Trump has publicly claimed the plane will be transferred to his presidential library when it retires in 2028 — a prospect Courtney called "an unacceptable misuse of taxpayer dollars." Smith, as an original co-sponsor, framed the problem more broadly: "Everything about the president's acceptance of the Qatari-gifted Boeing 747 now being used as Air Force One is deeply troubling. From the ethical and constitutional concerns of accepting such a gift from a foreign government, to his belief that he can take personal possession of an aircraft that has cost millions of dollars of taxpayers' money to bring it up to the standards required for transportation of the president" ([House Armed Services Committee Democrats](https://democrats-armedservices.house.gov/2026/9/courtney-smith-introduce-bill-to-prohibit-qatari-gifted-air-force-one-from-becoming-president-trump-s-private-jet-or-library-trophy)). Executive airlift programs, including Air Force One, fall under the jurisdiction of the Seapower and Projection Forces Subcommittee — Courtney's turf, with Smith lending the full committee's ranking Democrat as co-sponsor.

The second part of the campaign is constitutional. In a March 2, 2026 interview with [NPR](/organizations/npr/)'s Leila Fadel, Smith addressed the U.S. strikes on Iran directly, saying there was no evidence of the "imminent threat" the administration claimed — "he hasn't even offered any facts to back that up" — and that the administration still had not told Congress what the war's objectives were, which he called "incredibly irresponsible given the level of destruction that is currently going on in the Middle East and the number of American lives that have been placed at risk" ([NPR](https://www.kcsm.org/npr-news/2026-03-02/rep-adam-smith-on-the-u-s-strikes-on-iran-and-the-debate-over-trumps-war-powers)). On the legality of bypassing Congress, he was blunt: "No president has ever gone to war... launched a full-scale war in the Middle East, and the president has not made his case to the American people. He's not made his case to Congress. He's not asked for congressional support. That's illegal. But it is also directly contrary to how a constitutional republic is supposed to function. This is more of an autocratic, monarchical decision" ([NPR](https://www.kcsm.org/npr-news/2026-03-02/rep-adam-smith-on-the-u-s-strikes-on-iran-and-the-debate-over-trumps-war-powers)).

He also laid out what Congress could theoretically do about it: "require the president to stop the war until he gets congressional approval" or "cut off the money" through appropriations law ([NPR](https://www.kcsm.org/npr-news/2026-03-02/rep-adam-smith-on-the-u-s-strikes-on-iran-and-the-debate-over-trumps-war-powers)). The catch, as he put it, is the second half of the equation: the Republican Congress under House Speaker Mike Johnson and Senate Majority Leader John Thune has "simply abdicated all responsibility as the legislative branch and as an Article 1 part of our Constitution," deciding instead that "Congress will simply do whatever Trump wants without questioning them" — agreeing with the president, he added dryly, "if he wants one thing one moment," and agreeing "if he wants the exact opposite thing the next moment" too ([NPR](https://www.kcsm.org/npr-news/2026-03-02/rep-adam-smith-on-the-u-s-strikes-on-iran-and-the-debate-over-trumps-war-powers)).

The third part is conditional on November. In a 2026 interview with Bloomberg Government, Smith pledged that if Democrats flip the House in the November elections and he retakes the committee gavel, the remainder of Trump's term would look very different: "We do have to do more effective oversight of the most corrupt administration in the history of the country," he said, promising "more transparency on what the [Pentagon](https://factsledger.com/organizations/pentagon/) is up to, more transparency on what the war plans are in Iran, Venezuela, Cuba, and with the boat strikes" ([Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/top-defense-democrat-vows-more-trump-scrutiny-if-house-flips)). The context, per the piece: an ongoing conflict with Iran after late-February strikes, Smith pressing Pentagon chief Pete Hegseth on the reasons for starting the war, stepped-up pressure on Cuba's regime, Caribbean strikes against alleged drug-trafficking vessels, and the capture of Venezuelan leader Nicolas Maduro — actions widely criticized by Democrats ([Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/top-defense-democrat-vows-more-trump-scrutiny-if-house-flips)).

## Defense-policy priorities

Oversight is only half of Smith's 2026 agenda. The other half is what the Pentagon should actually be doing, and here his priorities are more technocratic than the war-powers rhetoric might suggest. His top substantive priority, he told Bloomberg Government, is **acquisition reform**, which he sums up as "innovate faster at scale": competitive munitions programs, mandated low-cost alternatives, quicker decisions, and a move away from programs of record and excessive requirements toward solutions ([Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/top-defense-democrat-vows-more-trump-scrutiny-if-house-flips)). In November 2025 he took the same message to the Atlantic Council's #ACFrontPage series, laying out his priorities for the fiscal year 2026 National Defense Authorization Act and reflecting on "lessons from his tenure as committee chair and ranking member" — Congress's role, in his telling, being to make sure the Pentagon can handle strategic competition and modernization while keeping bipartisan support for defense sustainable ([Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/event/representative-adam-smith-on-how-congress-will-shape-us-defense-policy/)).

He is equally direct about money. Smith pushed back on the White House's $1.5 trillion Pentagon budget request as "unrealistic": "In a world where we're coming up on a $40 trillion debt and we're over a hundred percent of debt to GDP ratio and we are completely unwilling to raise taxes, I think it is disastrous," he said. "I think it contributes to a fiscal catastrophe that will weaken us far more than having one too few battleships" ([Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/top-defense-democrat-vows-more-trump-scrutiny-if-house-flips)). The underlying strategy, in his view, is "confused and overly ambitious" — too focused on trying to "dominate" different parts of the world, when the realistic alternative is a strategy "based on a more realistic way that we can be strong enough to deter our adversaries and meet our national security objectives" ([Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/top-defense-democrat-vows-more-trump-scrutiny-if-house-flips)).

His self-description is one of the more quotable passages in recent defense-politics coverage, and it explains his positioning inside his own party: "I come at it from a fairly progressive perspective," he said. "I support Medicare for all. I support more taxes on the wealthy and corporations, but I don't support the idea that we need to smash capitalism and we need to hate all businesses and all rich people. I just think we need to effectively tax and regulate them" ([Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/top-defense-democrat-vows-more-trump-scrutiny-if-house-flips)). On countering Trump on national security, he argued Democrats need "a reasonably cohesive coalition of forces that want to do something different than Trump" — but admitted that "right now, you don't even have a desire to build that coalition," with some in the party preferring to "smash the existing Democratic Party so that they can take it over and run it the way they want to run it" ([Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/top-defense-democrat-vows-more-trump-scrutiny-if-house-flips)).

## The Vance episode and the career path

The moment that introduced Smith to many outside defense circles came in February 2025, after Vice President JD Vance's confrontational speech at the [Munich Security Conference](/organizations/munich-security-conference/), where Vance told European leaders their gravest threat came from within — censorship and retreat from shared values — while touching only briefly on Ukraine ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/europe/2025/02/14/munich-security-conference-jd-vance-speech/)). Smith, as the top Democrat on Armed Services, responded with some of the sharpest language of his career: Vance's "hollow reiteration of the 'might makes right' rhetoric weakens our standing in the world and our national security and defence," he said, calling it "his ham-fisted attempt to gaslight those fighting for democracy in the face of blatant Russian aggression and Putin's bloodthirsty desire to break [Nato] and fracture the 50-plus nation coalition that has come to Ukraine's defence as it fights for survival" — "an appalling display of cynicism and cowardice" ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/europe/2025/02/14/munich-security-conference-jd-vance-speech/)). The speech landed days after Trump held direct talks with Vladimir Putin about ending the war in Ukraine, amid European fears of a deal cut over Kyiv's head ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/europe/2025/02/14/munich-security-conference-jd-vance-speech/)).

The biography behind the rhetoric is unusually blue-collar for someone who now shapes the defense budget. Smith was born June 15, 1965, in Washington, D.C., and raised in SeaTac, Washington, where his father Ben worked as a ramp serviceman at SeaTac Airport and was active in the local Machinists' Union; his mother Leila stayed home raising Adam and his two brothers ([adamsmith.house.gov](http://adamsmith.house.gov/)). He went through Bow Lake Elementary, Chinook Middle School, and Tyee High School, graduating in 1983, then spent a year at Western Washington University before transferring to Fordham University — working his way through college loading trucks for UPS — and graduating in 1987 with a degree in Political Science ([adamsmith.house.gov](http://adamsmith.house.gov/)). He earned his law degree from the University of Washington School of Law in 1990, practiced at the firm Cromwell, Mendoza and Belur in 1992, then served as a prosecutor for the City of Seattle from 1993 to 1995, focusing on drunk driving and domestic violence cases, before working as a judge pro tempore in 1996 ([adamsmith.house.gov](http://adamsmith.house.gov/)).

Congress.gov records him simply: "Representative Adam Smith (1965 - ). In Congress 1997 - Present," representing Washington's 9th district from the 105th through the 119th Congresses as a Democrat ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/member/adam-smith/S000510)). Nearly three decades in the House, more than a decade and a half as his party's lead voice on the Armed Services Committee — and, in 2026, positioning himself as the man who would run the Pentagon's oversight if the gavel changes hands in November.
