## Overview

George Herbert Walker Bush was the 41st President of the [United States](/places/united-states/) (1989–1993) and the 43rd Vice President (1981–1989), a World War II naval aviator who held almost every senior appointed office in American government — congressman, ambassador to the United Nations, Republican Party chairman, envoy to China and CIA director — before reaching the White House ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/George-H-W-Bush)). Born on 12 June 1924 in Milton, Massachusetts, he was the son of Senator Prescott Bush and Dorothy Walker Bush, married Barbara Pierce in January 1945, and died on 30 November 2018 in Houston, [Texas](/places/texas/), aged 94 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/George-H-W-Bush)). He also hosted an event at Chicago's [Copernicus Center](/organizations/copernicus-center/) theater ([Block Club Chicago](https://blockclubchicago.org/2021/07/26/jefferson-parks-copernicus-foundation-marks-50-years-with-history-book-this-place-is-a-legacy/)). This is George H.W. Bush, the 41st President — not his eldest son, George W. Bush, the 43rd President.

## From Navy pilot to diplomat, CIA director and vice-president

Bush enlisted in the US Naval Reserve on leaving Phillips Academy, becoming the Navy's youngest pilot and flying 58 combat missions in the Pacific during [World War II](/events/world-war-ii/); shot down on 2 September 1944 over Chi Chi Jima, he won the Distinguished Flying Cross ([Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/looking-back-george-hw-bushs-lifelong-career-public-service-180970936/)). After graduating from Yale in economics in 1948, he moved to Texas, built oil companies — Bush-Overbey in 1951, Zapata Petroleum in 1953 — won a House seat in 1966 and voted for the Civil Rights Act of 1968 despite resistance at home ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/George-H-W-Bush)). After two Senate defeats he served as ambassador to the [United Nations](/organizations/united-nations/) (1971–1973), chairman of the [Republican Party](/organizations/republican-party/) (1973–1974), envoy to [China](/places/china/) (1974–1976) and CIA director (1976–1977), then as vice-president under Ronald Reagan, including an eight-hour stint as the first-ever Acting President during Reagan's cancer surgery in 1985 ([Biographical Directory of the United States Congress](https://bioguideretro.congress.gov/member/b001166)).

## A one-term presidency defined by foreign policy

Bush won the 1988 election with Dan Quayle, taking 53 percent of the popular vote against Michael Dukakis on pledges of "a kinder, and gentler, nation" and "Read my lips, no new taxes!" — a pledge he broke in 1990 by raising taxes to cut the deficit ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/George-H-W-Bush)). At home he signed two landmark laws in 1990, the Americans with Disabilities Act and a Clean Air Act amendment, and negotiated the North American Free Trade Agreement, later signed under Clinton ([Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/looking-back-george-hw-bushs-lifelong-career-public-service-180970936/)). Abroad he ordered the 1989 Panama invasion that toppled Manuel Noriega, managed the end of the Cold War — the Berlin Wall fell, Germany reunified within [NATO](/organizations/nato/), and he signed arms treaties with Gorbachev — and, after Iraq invaded Kuwait in August 1990, built a coalition and authorised the January 1991 air offensive that restored Kuwait's independence ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/George-H-W-Bush)). Approval soared to about 90 percent after the Gulf victory, yet recession persisted into 1992 and he lost to Clinton, 37 to 43 percent, Perot taking 19 percent ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/George-H-W-Bush)).

## Service after the White House

Bush's post-presidency is the part of his story biographies compress most, yet it was active. As president he launched the "Points of Light" initiative, naming 1,020 Daily Points of Light from all 50 states — a figure showing how he made service a presidential programme, not a slogan ([Bush Library](https://www.bush41library.gov/bushes/president-george-bush)). After office he helped raise hundreds of millions of dollars for charity, chaired the M. D. Anderson Cancer Center Board of Visitors (2001–2003) and the National Constitution Center (2006–2008), and became honorary chairman of the Points of Light Institute ([Bush Library](https://www.bush41library.gov/bushes/president-george-bush)). He partnered with successor Bill Clinton on relief for the 2004 tsunami, Katrina and Rita in 2005 and Ike in 2008, and in 2005 became UN Special Envoy after the South Asian earthquake in Pakistan claimed nearly 75,000 lives ([Bush Library](https://www.bush41library.gov/bushes/president-george-bush)). In 2017 he joined Carter, Clinton, George W. Bush and Obama to launch the One America Appeal, and in 2011 accepted the Presidential Medal of Freedom from Obama ([Bush Library](https://www.bush41library.gov/bushes/president-george-bush)).

## FAQ

### Who was George H.W. Bush?

George Herbert Walker Bush was the 41st President of the United States (1989–1993) and the 43rd Vice President (1981–1989) under Ronald Reagan. A World War II naval aviator and Texas oilman, he previously served as a congressman, UN ambassador, Republican Party chairman, envoy to China and CIA director.

### What did George H.W. Bush achieve as president?

He oversaw the peaceful end of the Cold War, German reunification within NATO and arms-reduction treaties with Mikhail Gorbachev; led the coalition that freed Kuwait in the Gulf War; signed the Americans with Disabilities Act and the Clean Air Act amendment in 1990; and negotiated NAFTA.

### Why did George H.W. Bush lose re-election in 1992?

A recession that began in late 1990 persisted into the election year, and his 1990 decision to raise taxes broke his famous "Read my lips, no new taxes!" pledge. He lost to Bill Clinton, 37 percent to 43 percent, with Ross Perot taking 19 percent.

### What was the "Points of Light" initiative?

It was Bush's programme to promote volunteerism and community service, launched as president. He named 1,020 Daily Points of Light from all 50 states during his term, and after leaving office he served as honorary chairman of the Points of Light Institute.

### What did George H.W. Bush do after leaving the White House?

He raised hundreds of millions of dollars for charity, chaired the M. D. Anderson Cancer Center Board of Visitors and the National Constitution Center, partnered with Bill Clinton on disaster relief, served as UN Special Envoy after the 2005 South Asian earthquake, and joined four other presidents in the 2017 One America Appeal.
