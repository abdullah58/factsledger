## Overview

John Sun is an American fintech entrepreneur who co-founded [Avant](/organizations/avant/) in 2012 — serving as its chief credit officer in its early years — and now leads Spring Labs as founder and CEO, building AI tools that help banks and fintechs run compliance work ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/spring-labs-raises-5m-to-help-compliance-teams-scale-like-modern-ai-powered-engineering-teams-302762679.html)). Before Avant, he and [Paul Zhang](/people/paul-zhang/) built Debteye, a startup that tried to automate credit counselling, in [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/)'s Summer 2011 batch ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2011/07/26/yc-funded-debteye-wants-to-be-your-much-cheaper-credit-counselor/)). His career runs in a straight line through one problem: financial institutions hold data they cannot safely share, and borrowers hold debts they cannot easily manage — and each of his three companies attacked a different side of it.

This is not Justin Sun, the crypto entrepreneur behind TRON. The two men share a surname and a fintech label and nothing else; this profile is about the Avant co-founder, and the other John — Justin — Sun has a separate profile ([Justin Sun](/people/justin-sun/)). Every fact here is verified from Avant, Debteye, Y Combinator or Spring Labs sources, which is the only way to keep the two apart.

## Debteye: automating credit counselling

Sun's partnership with Paul Zhang predates both of their companies: the two have been friends since they were nine years old ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)). Both interned at [Al Goldstein](/people/al-goldstein/)'s previous company Enova, a subprime online lender, and Goldstein stayed in touch after their internships, impressed by their technical skill ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)). From Enova, Sun and Zhang launched Debteye in 2011 as part of the Y Combinator incubator program, alongside a third co-founder, Kevin Yu ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html); [Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/debteye)).

Debteye's idea was to make the multi-billion-dollar debt-consulting industry largely irrelevant. The service analysed a user's bank data — using Yodlee, the same backend that powered Mint — then recommended how much to save each month and which bills to pay first, and helped users fill out pre-generated debt-settlement and debt-management forms, even faxing them to the bank, plus a script to read to the banker ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2011/07/26/yc-funded-debteye-wants-to-be-your-much-cheaper-credit-counselor/)). Sun told TechCrunch that studies showed it was better to pay off smaller debts first regardless of interest rate, because the sense of accomplishment kept people going — and that large banks had already set the terms they would offer, leaving little room for professional consultants to negotiate, which was why a standardised script could work ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2011/07/26/yc-funded-debteye-wants-to-be-your-much-cheaper-credit-counselor/)). The three co-founders were all certified credit counsellors, the service was free at launch with a monthly fee planned, and Y Combinator's directory now lists Debteye as "Acquired" — without naming an acquirer or a date ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2011/07/26/yc-funded-debteye-wants-to-be-your-much-cheaper-credit-counselor/); [Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/debteye)).

Debteye "didn't get much traction", Zhang later said, but it provided some of the underpinnings for what became Avant ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)). The through-line is visible in retrospect: the same instinct — use software to replace an expensive, opaque human intermediary in consumer finance — that Debteye applied to credit counselling, Avant would apply to lending itself.

## Avant: chief credit officer of a near-prime lender

Sun and Zhang started AvantCredit with CEO Al Goldstein in 2012 ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/avantcredit-secures-20m-to-make-financial-loans-more-accessible-with-big-data)). By April 2015 Sun was 30 and Avant's chief credit officer, and the company had been named to Inc.'s 2015 30 Under 30 list ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)). The near-prime thesis was that subprime lenders were not lending to near-prime customers and the big banks had pulled back since the 2008 financial crisis, leaving borrowers with FICO scores between 580 and 700 badly served ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)).

Sun's job was the credit model. He and Zhang built a proprietary scoring model that crunched more than 500 variables — repayment history on credit cards, mortgages and car loans, plus nontraditional data such as apartment rental information and cell-phone bill history, and even nonstructured data such as whether applicants filled out forms in upper- or lowercase letters and the time of day they applied ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)). In 2013 Sun told VentureBeat that AvantCredit's immediacy — approving and funding a loan of up to $10,000 in near real time — was something "not even banks offer", and that the goal was a product "clear, transparent, and easy to use" with more competitive rates than subprime lenders, because better credit assessment meant better pricing ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/avantcredit-secures-20m-to-make-financial-loans-more-accessible-with-big-data)). By April 2015 Avant had originated $800 million in loans to 200,000 customers ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)).

The stat-plus-source-plus-implication is the model's breadth: a 500-variable model that weighed keystroke patterns alongside repayment history let Avant price risk where a single FICO score could not, which is the technical reason a near-prime lender could exist at all ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)). When Sun left Avant is not verified in any source read, and this profile does not assert a departure date.

## Spring Labs: from blockchain data-sharing to AI compliance

In 2017 Sun co-founded Spring Labs in Marina del Rey, California, with Adam Jiwan and Anna Fridman — a company built, in CoinDesk's words, by "the team and board of the lending platform Avant" ([TransUnion](https://newsroom.transunion.com/spring-labs-and-transunion-bring-credit-data-to-public-blockchain-networks-for-the-first-time/); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2019/02/12/gm-financial-partners-with-blockchain-startup-to-fight-identity-fraud)). The original project was the Spring Protocol, a blockchain-based network that let financial institutions exchange information without sharing the underlying source data, using advanced cryptography to decouple data ownership from data value ([Built In LA](https://www.builtinla.com/articles/spring-labs-raises-23-million-series-a)). The first products were Spring Verify for identity verification, Spring Protect against loan stacking — borrowers taking multiple loans from different lenders — and Spring Defense for fraud monitoring ([Built In LA](https://www.builtinla.com/articles/spring-labs-raises-23-million-series-a)).

Funding followed the idea quickly. Spring Labs raised a $14.75 million seed round in March 2018 led by August Capital, then a $23 million Series A in June 2019 led by GreatPoint Ventures, with August Capital, General Motors Ventures, RRE Ventures, Galaxy Digital, Multicoin Capital, The Pritzker Group and CardWorks participating ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2019/02/12/gm-financial-partners-with-blockchain-startup-to-fight-identity-fraud); [Built In LA](https://www.builtinla.com/articles/spring-labs-raises-23-million-series-a)). In February 2019 GM Financial partnered with the company on identity-verification solutions and explored taking a stake, while the Spring Founding Industry Partners program signed up SoFi, OnDeck Capital, Avant, GreenSky, Funding Circle, BlueVine, Fundation, Upgrade, Fundbox and Better Mortgage ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2019/02/12/gm-financial-partners-with-blockchain-startup-to-fight-identity-fraud)). Adam Jiwan was CEO and co-founder at that point; by January 2022 Sun was CEO, and the company was a Series B business with about 50 employees and more than $63 million raised ([TransUnion](https://newsroom.transunion.com/spring-labs-and-transunion-bring-credit-data-to-public-blockchain-networks-for-the-first-time/)). The date Sun took over as CEO was not verified, and this profile does not assert one.

The TransUnion partnership of January 2022 was the high-water mark of the blockchain chapter: for the first time, off-chain credit data from TransUnion would be made available on public blockchain networks via Spring Labs' ky0x Digital Passport, letting DeFi and Web3 applications check a user's reputation — KYC/AML and credit data — without the user revealing identity or personal information ([TransUnion](https://newsroom.transunion.com/spring-labs-and-transunion-bring-credit-data-to-public-blockchain-networks-for-the-first-time/)). Sun's framing was that the tool would "foster greater trust (and adoption) in DeFi products and services" by putting off-chain reputation on-chain while preserving privacy and anonymity ([TransUnion](https://newsroom.transunion.com/spring-labs-and-transunion-bring-credit-data-to-public-blockchain-networks-for-the-first-time/)).

## The 2026 pivot: compliance agents

By May 2026 Spring Labs had pivoted from blockchain identity to agentic AI for compliance. It raised $5 million led by BankTech Ventures and Haymaker Ventures to expand from AI complaint and dispute agents into a broader compliance workflow and agent-orchestration platform for banks, sponsor banks and fintechs ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/spring-labs-raises-5m-to-help-compliance-teams-scale-like-modern-ai-powered-engineering-teams-302762679.html)). Sun, now CEO and co-founder, described the problem as execution rather than data: "Every customer conversation can create downstream work: complaints to identify, disputes to route, cases to document, reports to file, and escalations to manage" — and said Spring Labs was building AI agents to execute that work within customer-defined policies, "reliably, consistently, and under the control of compliance teams" ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/spring-labs-raises-5m-to-help-compliance-teams-scale-like-modern-ai-powered-engineering-teams-302762679.html)).

The first product, Zanko, focuses on complaints, QA/QC and disputes, helping banks identify potential complaints and standardise handling; its Complaints Agent achieved up to 97% complaint-identification accuracy in the company's own internal testing and customer pilots — a company-reported benchmark, not an independent test ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/spring-labs-raises-5m-to-help-compliance-teams-scale-like-modern-ai-powered-engineering-teams-302762679.html)). The company says it works with dozens of sponsor banks and fintechs, including WebBank and First Electronic Bank, and was already piloting a compliance-focused agent-orchestration layer extending beyond complaints and disputes ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/spring-labs-raises-5m-to-help-compliance-teams-scale-like-modern-ai-powered-engineering-teams-302762679.html)). The arc is consistent with Sun's whole career: the 2011 problem was consumers who could not manage debt; the 2026 problem is banks that cannot scale compliance — and in both cases the answer offered is software that does the routine work inside strict rules.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is John Sun?**
John Sun is the co-founder of Avant (2012) and Debteye (Y Combinator 2011), and the founder and CEO of Spring Labs, an AI compliance platform for banks and fintechs ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/spring-labs-raises-5m-to-help-compliance-teams-scale-like-modern-ai-powered-engineering-teams-302762679.html)).

**Is John Sun the same person as Justin Sun?**
No. John Sun is the Avant and Spring Labs co-founder profiled here; Justin Sun is the crypto entrepreneur behind TRON, a different person with a separate profile (Justin Sun). The two share only a surname and a fintech label.

**What was Debteye?**
Debteye was a Y Combinator Summer 2011 startup co-founded by Sun, Paul Zhang and Kevin Yu that automated credit counselling — analysing bank data, recommending which debts to pay first, and generating the forms and scripts for negotiating with banks ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2011/07/26/yc-funded-debteye-wants-to-be-your-much-cheaper-credit-counselor/)). Y Combinator lists it as "Acquired", with no acquirer named.

**What does Spring Labs do now?**
As of May 2026 it builds an AI-native compliance and operations platform for financial institutions; its first product, Zanko, handles complaints, QA/QC and disputes, and it is piloting a broader agent-orchestration layer for compliance workflows ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/spring-labs-raises-5m-to-help-compliance-teams-scale-like-modern-ai-powered-engineering-teams-302762679.html)).

**What was John Sun's role at Avant?**
He was a co-founder and, by 2015, chief credit officer, co-developing the 500-plus-variable scoring model that underwrote Avant's near-prime loans ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)). When he left Avant is not verified.
