## Overview

Eyal Lifshitz is an Israeli-American entrepreneur who co-founded [BlueVine](/organizations/bluevine/) in 2013 and led it as chief executive from online invoice factoring into a digital small-business banking platform serving about 175,000 active customers ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)). On September 28, 2026, [Valley National Bancorp](/organizations/valley-national-bancorp/) announced it would acquire BlueVine for approximately $340 million, with Lifshitz joining Valley as Head of Small Business Banking when the transaction closes ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)).

Lifshitz was born in New York City to Israeli parents, spent his elementary and middle-school years in the United States — living in New Jersey for a time — and moved back to Israel with his family when he was about 13 ([StartupFundraising](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/eyal-lifshitz)). He served for over three years in a combat unit of the Israel Defense Forces, completing his service around the year 2000 ([StartupFundraising](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/eyal-lifshitz)). His first job had been delivering pizzas, and he did not own a computer before he was about 21 or 22; he began an electrical engineering degree in 2000 after pizza deliveries to technology companies convinced him that engineering was the job he wanted ([Calcalistech](https://m.calcalistech.com/Article.aspx?guid=3899255)).

## A third-generation small-business family

Lifshitz describes himself as a third-generation small-business entrepreneur, and the family history is the through-line of his career ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/author/eyal-lifshitz)). His father ran a physical therapy clinic on Manhattan's Upper East Side for 30 years, and his grandfather owned a lighting and electricity store for 40 years ([Calcalistech](https://m.calcalistech.com/Article.aspx?guid=3899255)). Watching both men run small businesses gave him an early view of their central problem: not a lack of customers, but cash flow — money owed and not yet paid while rent and wages came due ([Chicago Booth](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/why-booth/stories/eyal-lifshitz-bluevine)). "It's part of why I'm passionate about helping small businesses, which is why I started BlueVine," he told the University of Chicago Booth School of Business ([Chicago Booth](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/why-booth/stories/eyal-lifshitz-bluevine)).

That background did not make him an entrepreneur early. By his own account he followed "a pretty safe journey" through large, strong-brand employers and took little risk before BlueVine, not wanting to become a founder until he was in his 30s ([Calcalistech](https://m.calcalistech.com/Article.aspx?guid=3899255)). He earned his MBA from Chicago Booth in 2009 ([Chicago Booth](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/why-booth/stories/eyal-lifshitz-bluevine)), and after business school he worked as a consultant at McKinsey & Company before moving into venture capital ([StartupFundraising](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/eyal-lifshitz)).

## From investor to founder

Lifshitz's venture career was at Greylock IL, Greylock Partners' dedicated fund for Israel and Europe, where he worked as a principal assessing potential investments ([Chicago Booth](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/why-booth/stories/eyal-lifshitz-bluevine)). His author biography at *Entrepreneur* likewise records that he was a principal at Greylock before BlueVine ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/author/eyal-lifshitz)). In a podcast interview he said he had been involved in investing more than $100 million in startups while at the firm ([StartupFundraising](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/eyal-lifshitz)). Before Greylock he had begun his career as an engineer at Texas Instruments, according to the same interview account ([StartupFundraising](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/eyal-lifshitz)) — the Texas Instruments and McKinsey roles rest on his own telling, with no independent corroboration found.

Working in venture moved him toward founding rather than away from it: assessing fintech investments, he saw that the innovations transforming traditional lending could be applied to factoring — advancing cash against small businesses' unpaid invoices — and that the opportunity was personal as well as commercial ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/company-values-should-be-timeless-not-just-something-we-will-do-for-x-months-years-says-eyal-9f5a1a0c6424)). At first he thought of the idea as an investor and imagined hiring a chief executive to run it; he then concluded that nobody else could execute his vision the way he could, and at 35 he decided that if he did not try, he would regret it in 20 years ([Calcalistech](https://m.calcalistech.com/Article.aspx?guid=3899255)). BlueVine's first capital came from Greylock Partners Israel — by then known as 83North — and Lightspeed Venture Partners ([StartupFundraising](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/eyal-lifshitz)).

## Building BlueVine: factoring to a digital bank

Lifshitz founded BlueVine in 2013 with [Nir Klar](/people/nir-klar/), and the company launched in 2014 with its first product, a modernised, tech-forward approach to invoice factoring ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)) ([Chicago Booth](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/why-booth/stories/eyal-lifshitz-bluevine)). The model was simple: BlueVine issued cash to small businesses in exchange for their unpaid invoices at a discount, with up to $5 million in working capital available in days ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/bluevine-brings-in-60-million/)). By the $60 million Series E in June 2018 — led by [Menlo Ventures](/organizations/menlo-ventures/), with [SVB Capital](/organizations/svb-capital/) participating, bringing total financing to $578 million — BlueVine had funded more than $900 million in loans to over 10,000 customers, 80% of them repeat borrowers ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/bluevine-brings-in-60-million/)).

The product set widened deliberately rather than by pivot: lines of credit and term loans followed factoring, and then, in Lifshitz's telling, the team realised the real opportunity was to fix small-business banking as a whole, since customers were patching together five or six vendors for services their bank did not offer ([Calcalistech](https://m.calcalistech.com/Article.aspx?guid=3899255)) ([Chicago Booth](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/why-booth/stories/eyal-lifshitz-bluevine)). A $102.5 million Series F in late 2019 valued the company at approximately $700-800 million and was paired with the launch of a business checking account; checking arrived in early 2020 and a bill-pay product that summer ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)) ([Chicago Booth](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/why-booth/stories/eyal-lifshitz-bluevine)).

The pandemic tested that platform within months. From its Silicon Valley headquarters at the time, BlueVine and its banking partners ultimately provided more than 155,000 small businesses with access to $4.5 billion in [Paycheck Protection Program](/events/paycheck-protection-program/) loans in a matter of months — more funding than most regional banks and some of the top 10 banks managed ([Chicago Booth](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/why-booth/stories/eyal-lifshitz-bluevine)). The company stood its loan process up in under 10 days, hired interns in Tel Aviv to work through North American nighttime hours, and at one point had everybody in the company processing loans, from engineers to human-resources staff ([Chicago Booth](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/why-booth/stories/eyal-lifshitz-bluevine)). In late June and early July 2020 it was funding about $50 million in PPP loans per day — about 5% of entire US banking-industry production at the time ([Chicago Booth](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/why-booth/stories/eyal-lifshitz-bluevine)). By 2020 the company had funded or served over 180,000 small businesses and provided $7.5 billion in financing since its founding, with almost 300 employees ([Chicago Booth](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/why-booth/stories/eyal-lifshitz-bluevine)).

## Leading a scaling company

As BlueVine grew — 185 people in early 2018, with plans to reach 230 that year — Lifshitz's role shifted from doing everything himself to building structure, hiring people better than himself at their functions, and holding his direct reports accountable for living the company's values ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/company-values-should-be-timeless-not-just-something-we-will-do-for-x-months-years-says-eyal-9f5a1a0c6424)). The five values the company settled on were Move Fast, Make it Simple, Push Boundaries, Constantly Learn, and Win Together, each chosen to be universal and timeless rather than a description of a passing phase ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/company-values-should-be-timeless-not-just-something-we-will-do-for-x-months-years-says-eyal-9f5a1a0c6424)). He kept himself close to the ground with monthly coffee chats with different employees and by reading the company's Glassdoor reviews, noting that they tend toward extremes ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/company-values-should-be-timeless-not-just-something-we-will-do-for-x-months-years-says-eyal-9f5a1a0c6424)). "I think I'm becoming more and more of a CEO as the company becomes larger," he said in 2021, describing the transition as moving from driver to coach ([Calcalistech](https://m.calcalistech.com/Article.aspx?guid=3899255)). He has also written publicly about small-business finance as a columnist for *Entrepreneur*, including pieces on cash flow, co-founder matching, and the credit cycle ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/author/eyal-lifshitz)).

## The Valley National acquisition

By 2026 BlueVine was headquartered in [Jersey City](/places/jersey-city/), New Jersey, with approximately $2.1 billion in digitally sourced deposits growing at roughly 35% a year since 2023 ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)). The sale to Valley National Bancorp grew out of a chance meeting between Lifshitz and Valley chief operating officer Russ Barrett at the Money20/20 conference the previous year, when neither firm was looking for a deal ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/bluevine-valley-bank-execs-talk-integration-approach/873084/)). In the spring of 2026, BlueVine was exploring becoming a bank through a merger or acquisition, as acquirer or acquired, and Lifshitz recalled the meeting, reached out, and was in Barrett's office two days later ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/bluevine-valley-bank-execs-talk-integration-approach/873084/)).

The deal announced on September 28, 2026, pays approximately $340 million in cash and stock for a company that brings Valley $2.1 billion in low-cost digital deposits, about 175,000 active small-business customers, and roughly 180 research-and-development employees and engineers ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)) ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/bluevine-valley-bank-execs-talk-integration-approach/873084/)). It is expected to close in early 2027, after which Lifshitz will oversee Valley's small-business arm, including the bank's legacy small-business clients — a combination Valley's chief executive said would grow its small-business customer base 20-fold ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/bluevine-valley-bank-execs-talk-integration-approach/873084/)). Lifshitz said his confidence in the match came down to three tests: whether the team gets to do what it is passionate about, whether it likes the day-to-day work, and whether it likes the people it will work with ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/bluevine-valley-bank-execs-talk-integration-approach/873084/)). He described what BlueVine brings as the combination built at scale — a nationwide digital small-business banking platform, a proven customer-acquisition engine, and a full-stack banking and lending platform with risk systems and small-business expertise ([Tearsheet](https://tearsheet.co/5-questions/how-bluevine-will-change-under-valley-bank-without-losing-its-core/)).

The price is a comedown from BlueVine's last private valuation: $340 million against the approximately $700-800 million at which the late-2019 Series F valued the company ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)). For Valley — a bank founded in 1927 with more than $66 billion in assets and over 220 branches — the prize is a digital deposit franchise and a national customer-acquisition channel to complement its relationship-banking model ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)).
