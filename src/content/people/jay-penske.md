## Overview

Jay Penske is an American media executive who founded [Penske Media Corporation](/organizations/penske-media-corporation/) (PMC) and, as its chairman and chief executive, built it from a small internet-services company into one of the largest media owners in the [United States](/places/united-states/), with a portfolio spanning [Variety](/organizations/variety/), [Rolling Stone](/organizations/rolling-stone/), Billboard, [The Hollywood Reporter](/organizations/the-hollywood-reporter/) and Deadline, plus the Golden Globe Awards and the South by Southwest festival ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/leadership/jay)).

The son of automobile magnate Roger Penske, he graduated from the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania, specialising in finance, management and information systems, and co-founded Firefly Mobile, which made the first mobile phone for children, before turning to publishing ([Business of Fashion](https://www.businessoffashion.com/people/jay-penske/); [Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/leadership/jay)). PMC's brands and live events now reach more than 340 million people each month, according to the company — a scale that makes Penske one of the most powerful, and most scrutinised, figures in Hollywood media ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/leadership/jay)). He is not the same Penske as the automotive business: PMC is his own company, while Penske Corporation is his father's automotive and transport group ([Business of Fashion](https://www.businessoffashion.com/people/jay-penske/)).

## From Mail.com to a media roll-up

Penske founded his media company in 2003, according to Business of Fashion, although PMC's own website dates its founding to 2004 — a one-year discrepancy the sources do not explain ([Business of Fashion](https://www.businessoffashion.com/people/jay-penske/); [Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/leadership/jay)). Before publishing, he co-founded Firefly Mobile, which created the first mobile phone for children, and his company began as an internet-services business built around the Mail.com email portal ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/leadership/jay)). His first major publishing move came in June 2009, when Mail.com Media acquired Nikki Finke's Deadline Hollywood Daily, a must-read Hollywood blog; Finke kept full editorial and design control, and the price was never disclosed ([AllThingsD](https://allthingsd.com/20090623/sold-hollywood-blog-queen-nikki-finke-goes-to-mailcom/)). In September 2010 he sold the Mail.com email business itself to Germany's United Internet Group for a reported $50–100 million, keeping the content sites and refocusing the company on publishing ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2010/09/20/mail-com-media/)).

## Buying the trades: Variety, Rolling Stone and Billboard

The pattern of the next decade was buying distressed legacy titles cheap and rebuilding them digitally. In October 2012 PMC bought Variety, the entertainment trade paper founded in 1905, from Reed Elsevier for about $25 million, with backing from hedge fund Third Point ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2012/10/10/variety-sold-to-finkes-boss/)). Penske told the Los Angeles Times that Variety had "stumbled on the digital landscape" and that he intended to make it "absolutely fundamental and indispensable", adding: "We are not buying Variety to gut the newsroom. We are buying the business to build it" ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2012/10/10/variety-sold-to-finkes-boss/)). In 2014 he bought Fairchild Fashion Media, including Women's Wear Daily, from Condé Nast; the division had lost a reported $50 million in five years, and Penske cut daily print editions, made WWD a glossy weekly and pushed its content online ([Business of Fashion](https://www.businessoffashion.com/people/jay-penske/)).

## The Rolling Stone and Billboard deals

In December 2017 PMC acquired a controlling stake in Wenner Media, publisher of Rolling Stone, in a deal a source told the New York Times was believed to be valued at close to $100 million; Jann Wenner stayed on as editorial director and his son Gus remained president and chief operating officer ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2017/12/21/variety-publisher-penske-media-acquires-majority-of-rolling-stone-wenner-media/)). PMC took over the remaining 49 percent in January 2019, becoming Rolling Stone's full owner ([Business of Fashion](https://www.businessoffashion.com/people/jay-penske/)). The buying spree was partly funded by outside money: in February 2018 PMC took a $200 million investment from a Saudi government-backed company, later identified as the Saudi Research and Marketing Group, while Penske kept "well in excess of 51 percent" control ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/jay-penske-saudi-stake-media-company-200-million-khashoggi-murder/)). Then, on 23 September 2020, PMC and [MRC](/organizations/mrc/) announced PMRC, a joint venture operating Billboard, The Hollywood Reporter and Vibe alongside Variety, Rolling Stone and [Music Business Worldwide](/organizations/music-business-worldwide/), putting the two great Hollywood trades — rivals for some 90 years — under one roof ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)).

## Live events and the Golden Globes

Penske's second empire is live events. In April 2021 his joint venture with MRC bought a 50 percent stake in South by Southwest after the pandemic forced the festival to cancel its 2020 edition and hold the 2021 event online; co-founder Roland Swenson called Penske's approach "a true lifeline for us" ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/business/sxsw-penske-media-full-ownership/)). PMC raised its stake to 51 percent in 2023 and took full ownership in August 2026, with SXSW remaining headquartered in Austin ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/business/sxsw-penske-media-full-ownership/)). In January 2023 Penske Media Eldridge, PMC's joint venture with Eldridge Industries, acquired Dick Clark Productions, producer of the Golden Globe Awards, the American Music Awards, the Academy of Country Music Awards, the [Billboard Music Awards](/events/billboard-music-awards/), Dick Clark's New Year's Rockin' Eve and the Streamy Awards ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/penske-media-eldridge-acquires-dick-clark-productions-producer-of-the-golden-globes-and-billboard-music-awards/?ref=pennyfractions.ghost.io)). DCP and Eldridge then acquired the Golden Globes' assets from the Hollywood Foreign Press Association in June 2023 ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/movies/2026/07/28/hfpa-lawsuit-over-control-of-golden-globe-awards-seeks-150m/91081363007/)).

## Vox Media and the PMX era

In February 2023 PMC paid $100 million for a 20 percent stake in Vox Media, publisher of Vox, New York Magazine, Thrillist, Popsugar and SB Nation, becoming its largest shareholder, and Jay Penske joined Vox Media's board ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/vox-media-majority-stake-penske-media-corporation/)). Vox had last been valued at $1 billion in 2015, when NBCUniversal invested $200 million, so the 2023 deal priced the company far below its peak ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/vox-media-majority-stake-penske-media-corporation/)). On 18 June 2026 PMC acquired Vox Media's remaining assets, after James Murdoch's Lupa Systems bought Vox.com, the Vox Media podcast network and New York Magazine for more than $300 million in May; the acquired brands include The Verge, Eater, SB Nation, Popsugar, The Dodo, Punch and Thrillist ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/rolling-stone-magazine-parent-buys-vox-media-digital-media-expansion-2026-06-18/); [AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/publishers/what-remains-of-vox-media-is-getting-bought-by-penske-media-corp/)). PMC folded the combined portfolio of more than 25 brands into a new subsidiary, PMX, which it says reaches hundreds of millions of people monthly and produces over 300 live events a year ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/rolling-stone-magazine-parent-buys-vox-media-digital-media-expansion-2026-06-18/)).

## Scrutiny: the Saudi stake and the HFPA lawsuit

Scale brought scrutiny. The $200 million Saudi investment drew criticism after the October 2018 murder of Washington Post columnist Jamal Khashoggi, because the investor, SRMG, is majority-owned by the Saudi government through al-Ahli funds; PMC never publicly addressed the tie, and rival Endeavor returned its own $400 million Saudi investment in March 2019 ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/jay-penske-saudi-stake-media-company-200-million-khashoggi-murder/)). The deeper challenge came on 28 July 2026, when the reactivated Hollywood Foreign Press Association sued Jay Penske, PMC, the Golden Globe Foundation and its chief executive Gregory Goeckner in federal court, seeking more than $150 million in damages and the unwinding of the Golden Globes deal ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/movies/2026/07/28/hfpa-lawsuit-over-control-of-golden-globe-awards-seeks-150m/91081363007/)). ## Frequently asked questions

### Who is Jay Penske?

Jay Penske is an American media executive, the founder, chairman and chief executive of Penske Media Corporation. He is the son of automobile magnate Roger Penske, graduated from the University of Pennsylvania's Wharton School, and built PMC from an internet-services company into the owner of Variety, Rolling Stone, Billboard and The Hollywood Reporter.

### What does Penske Media Corporation own?

PMC owns Variety, Rolling Stone, Billboard, The Hollywood Reporter, Deadline, Women's Wear Daily, Robb Report, IndieWire and other titles, plus Dick Clark Productions, which produces the Golden Globes, the American Music Awards and the Billboard Music Awards, and the South by Southwest festival, which it fully owns.

### How did Penske Media get Billboard?

PMC gained operating control of Billboard through PMRC, a joint venture with MRC announced on 23 September 2020 that also covers The Hollywood Reporter and Vibe. TheWrap reported Penske secured 51 percent of the THR-Billboard business for $100 million, though the official announcement disclosed no financial terms.

### Is Jay Penske related to Roger Penske?

Yes. Jay Penske is the son of Roger Penske, the automobile magnate and former racing driver who founded Penske Corporation. The two companies are separate: Roger Penske runs the automotive and transport group, while Jay Penske founded and runs Penske Media Corporation.

### Why is the Hollywood Foreign Press Association suing Jay Penske?

On 28 July 2026 the HFPA sued Penske, PMC and others in federal court, seeking more than $150 million and the unwinding of the Golden Globes acquisition. It alleges Penske orchestrated the 2021 boycott of the Globes to devalue them and acquire them cheaply — claims the Golden Globes spokesperson called absurd.
