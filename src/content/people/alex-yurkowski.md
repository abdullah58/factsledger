## Overview

Alex Yurkowski is the co-founder and chief technology officer of [ClickUp](/organizations/clickup/), the all-in-one workplace productivity platform he built with [Zeb Evans](/people/zeb-evans/) in 2017 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/04/clickup-layoffs/)). A former IBM software engineer, he wrote the original version of ClickUp as an internal tool for the pair's own startup team, after deciding that none of the productivity apps they were using — [Asana](/organizations/asana/), Trello, Slack — actually helped them get work done ([Contrary Research](https://research2.contrary.com/company/clickup)). That internal tool became a standalone product that reached a $4 billion valuation in 2021 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2224795)).

Yurkowski is the quieter of ClickUp's two founders. Evans is the public face — the founder-CEO quoted in every funding announcement — while Yurkowski has given few interviews and built his reputation inside the engineering organization. The fullest public account of his background and thinking is a single long-form podcast interview from January 2021, which this profile draws on heavily and attributes as his own account ([Modern CTO](https://moderncto.io/270-alex-yurkowski-cto-co-founder-at-clickup/)).

## From IBM to ClickUp

Yurkowski describes himself as "a math and science kid" who played a lot of video games and always knew he wanted to study computer science and build things ([Modern CTO](https://moderncto.io/270-alex-yurkowski-cto-co-founder-at-clickup/)). Out of school he went to IBM, where he had previously interned, rotating through several teams and working on Bluemix and cloud integrations. He says he never really wanted the IBM job — he had applied to startups and small businesses, and they all told him he was not experienced enough — but in hindsight the large-company grounding mattered: at a startup of two to four people, he notes, "you aren't going to have anyone to guide you."

While at IBM in Raleigh, North Carolina, he spent his weekends trying to find someone to build a startup with, posting on CoFoundersLab.com. Raleigh, he says, had few people willing to put in another forty hours on top of a forty-hour week, and he had nearly given up on the site when Evans — the CEO and co-founder — emailed him through it. They met, "it just worked out," and Evans offered to fund the work so Yurkowski could leave IBM and build full-time. Yurkowski took the leap just after getting engaged and moved across the country to [Los Angeles](/places/los-angeles/), [California](/places/california/), despite people around him warning that quitting his job, moving, and getting engaged at once was too much change ([Modern CTO](https://moderncto.io/270-alex-yurkowski-cto-co-founder-at-clickup/)).

The pair were not starting from scratch. Evans had run Fast Followerz, a social media automation business with 25 employees, and had moved on to Mimri, a social media platform; Yurkowski joined that effort as co-founder and CTO, and the team moved to Silicon Valley ([Contrary Research](https://research2.contrary.com/company/clickup)). ClickUp itself began as a side project inside that work: the team was juggling multiple productivity apps and Yurkowski "decided to build a custom, bundled app that better served their productivity needs." When the team realized the productivity tool had more potential than Mimri, it became their focus ([Contrary Research](https://research2.contrary.com/company/clickup)).

## Building ClickUp and the funding arc

The product's founding idea was refusal to specialize. Investors who looked at the plan told the founders it would never work — they needed to pick a market and target it — but Yurkowski says they deliberately went the other way, building a vertical-agnostic, fully customizable platform: "no matter what vertical or what kind of team you are, your workflow should fit." The first paying customer was a jiu-jitsu gym scheduling its team tasks in the product ([Modern CTO](https://moderncto.io/270-alex-yurkowski-cto-co-founder-at-clickup/)). Growth for the first three years was entirely organic and word-of-mouth, with no marketing budget and no sales team until late 2019 or early 2020; by the spring of 2020, about three years after founding, ClickUp had roughly 100,000 customers ([Contrary Research](https://research2.contrary.com/company/clickup); [Modern CTO](https://moderncto.io/270-alex-yurkowski-cto-co-founder-at-clickup/)).

The Series C figures come from [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2224795)'s funding coverage: the round funded a 600-job European expansion, and by then 800,000 teams were on the platform — up from 200,000 a year earlier — including enterprise customers such as [Google](/organizations/google/), [McDonald's](/organizations/mcdonalds/), [Booking.com](/organizations/booking-com/), and [Netflix](/organizations/netflix/), with 40% of the business already outside the [United States](/places/united-states/) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2224795)). The Series B numbers — $100 million, $1 billion valuation, 2 million users — come from the same outlet's December 2020 report ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/12/15/clickup-hits-1-billion-valuation-in-100m-series-b-raise/)).

What followed the peak is company context rather than Yurkowski's story: ClickUp cut 7% of staff in May 2022, 10% (about 90 people) in July 2023, and 22% in May 2026, with Evans framing the last cut as a rebuild around roughly 3,000 internal AI agents and a "100x org" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/04/clickup-layoffs/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/25/what-clickups-mass-layoff-tells-us-about-the-future-of-work/)). No fully-read source describes Yurkowski's role in those decisions, and this profile does not assert one.

## How he runs engineering

Yurkowski's account of the early engineering culture is practical rather than visionary. Hiring, he says, was "a bit of both" luck and learning: Evans brought people over from his previous company, including developers in Europe, and the team kept early hires like Sergei, "one of our developers, [who] is amazing. And he's still with us today." The quality he screened for above all in the early days was work ethic — people willing to put in the time — plus shared values, and above all making sure engineers understood "what are we making and why," which was easier because the team used its own tool every day ([Modern CTO](https://moderncto.io/270-alex-yurkowski-cto-co-founder-at-clickup/)).

The company's core values, in his telling, were written down only after a few years of working together, distilled from what the team already did: customer service (Evans came from hospitality), hard work, and getting things done rather than letting them fall through the cracks. One value shifted as the company grew, from an earlier "get it done now" to "progress over perfection" — ship what delivers value to customers now and keep improving it, rather than holding work back until it is perfect ([Modern CTO](https://moderncto.io/270-alex-yurkowski-cto-co-founder-at-clickup/)). The shipping rhythm behind that value was weekly Friday releases, with product, marketing, and sales all coordinating in the same platform so release notes, tagging, and questions lived in one place ([Modern CTO](https://moderncto.io/270-alex-yurkowski-cto-co-founder-at-clickup/)).

On security, his position is that it must be "baked into your development process": rules in the codebase, pull-request reviews before anything reaches production, automated scans and penetration tests, and occasional external audits — which, he notes, mostly find version updates rather than real vulnerabilities. SOC 2 compliance, he says, forced the team to document processes and produce evidence for them, but he is blunt that "SOC 2 compliance does not make your application secure," and he describes compliance regimes such as GDPR and California's rules as vague and getting "weirder every year" ([Modern CTO](https://moderncto.io/270-alex-yurkowski-cto-co-founder-at-clickup/)).

**Who is Alex Yurkowski?** He is the co-founder and chief technology officer of ClickUp, the workplace productivity platform. **Did Alex Yurkowski really co-found ClickUp?** Yes. TechCrunch states in its own voice that ClickUp was "Founded by Zeb Evans and Alex Yurkowski in 2017," and Yurkowski says in a 2021 interview that he met Evans through CoFoundersLab and "we founded ClickUp" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/04/clickup-layoffs/); [Modern CTO](https://moderncto.io/270-alex-yurkowski-cto-co-founder-at-clickup/)).

**What is ClickUp?** An all-in-one workplace platform combining tasks, docs, chat, whiteboards, goals, and time tracking, founded in 2017 in [San Diego](/places/san-diego/).
