Mark Thompson spent his first nine months running [CNN](/organizations/cnn/) making almost no visible moves. Then, in July 2024, he sent his staff a memo that did three big things at once: it merged all three of CNN's newsrooms into one, cut around 100 roles, and promised CNN's first subscription products before the end of the year. That memo — not the résumé that got him hired — is the real story of the Thompson era at CNN. Three months later the paywall arrived on schedule. ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/cnn-subscription-product-restructuring-layoffs/))

## The appointment and the pedigree

Warner Bros. Discovery named Thompson Chairman and CEO of CNN Worldwide, effective October 9, 2023. He reports to WBD chief executive David Zaslav and acts as editor-in-chief, ultimately responsible for all CNN content. The remit covers CNN's strategy, operations and business units — its global portfolio of networks, channels and programming across broadcast, streaming and digital — and roughly 4,000 employees worldwide. Zaslav's case for him was simple: Thompson had, in his words, "transformed for the digital age two of the world's most respected news organizations." ([Warner Bros. Discovery](https://www.wbd.com:443/mark-thompson-named-chairman-and-ceo-of-cnn-worldwide))

Thompson arrived with the CV Zaslav described. As BBC Director-General — the job combines CEO and editor-in-chief — from 2004 to 2012, he presided over one of the world's biggest newsrooms and dozens of TV and radio services, led the development of BBC iPlayer (the first streaming service from a major broadcaster), expanded web and smartphone services from news into education and entertainment, and oversaw coverage from the 2008–09 financial crisis to the 2012 London Olympics. He had not arrived at the BBC through the front office: he had worked his way up as a researcher, director, field producer and showrunner in the news division. ([Warner Bros. Discovery](https://www.wbd.com:443/mark-thompson-named-chairman-and-ceo-of-cnn-worldwide))

At The New York Times from 2012 to 2020, his tenure was the digital-turnaround template everyone cites: paid digital subscriptions rose tenfold, total digital revenues more than doubled, and the period produced The Daily podcast alongside NYT Cooking, NYT Games and Wirecutter. He left at the end of 2020, later chairing Ancestry, and was knighted for services to media in the King's Birthday Honours in June 2023. ([Warner Bros. Discovery](https://www.wbd.com:443/mark-thompson-named-chairman-and-ceo-of-cnn-worldwide))

## The July 2024 memo: three newsrooms become one

The memo Thompson sent staff was his long-awaited digital vision, and it was blunt. "Wherever possible, we've closed open positions rather than target currently occupied roles," he wrote, "However, some of our colleagues will learn today that their jobs are being eliminated or are at risk." Around 100 roles were cut from a workforce of more than 3,500 — though new digital posts were opened at the same time. ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/cnn-subscription-product-restructuring-layoffs/))

The structural change was bigger than the headcount. Thompson formally merged CNN's three newsrooms — US Newsgathering (TV), International Newsgathering (TV), and Global Digital News — into one, with longtime executive Virginia Moseley put in charge of all reporting across CNN's platforms. It was the end of a newsroom architecture that dated back decades. ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/cnn-subscription-product-restructuring-layoffs/))

The memo's forward half was all subscription and video. Thompson committed to subscriber-first products, with the first launch before the end of 2024 — "best-in-class, subscription-ready products that will provide need-to-know news, analysis and context in compelling new formats and experiences." Video, he wrote, would be "at the heart of our future," with "a re-imagined video experience on every platform — from pure digital to streaming, FAST channels and more." He had brought in Alex MacCallum earlier in the year to lead digital product development, and announced Ben Monnie — arriving from Google, with a decade at the NYT alongside Thompson and MacCallum — as SVP of product growth strategy reporting to her. ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/cnn-subscription-product-restructuring-layoffs/))

A new "TV Future Lab" was created to develop and manage streaming and VOD programming for the Max platform: Kate Bolduan's "5 Things" was relaunched as a streaming test case, with CNN Originals and CNN en Español FAST channels planned. And Charlie Moore, appointed to oversee primetime, was asked with top executive producers to improve audience competitiveness while watching production costs — a nod to CNN's poor primetime ratings even after hosting the first presidential debate in June. ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/cnn-subscription-product-restructuring-layoffs/))

## The paywall arrives on schedule

On October 1, 2024 — three months after the memo — CNN rolled out a paywall asking some users to pay $3.99 a month for access to CNN.com, triggered after readers consumed a certain number of free articles. "Starting today, we are asking users in the United States to pay a small recurring fee for unlimited access to CNN.com's world-class articles," said MacCallum, by then EVP of digital products and services. Subscribers got unlimited articles plus original documentaries, election features, a curated daily news selection and fewer ads; the company said the product was in preliminary form and would expand. ([Reuters](https://jack1065.com/2024/10/01/cnn-launches-paywall-to-charge-3-99-a-month-for-some-users/))

Reuters drew the connection explicitly: "The subscription push is also in tandem with CNN CEO Mark Thompson's plans to shift the company away from being a television-focused organization to a multi-platform news company by bolstering its digital business." The memo had promised a subscription product before year-end; the paywall was the delivery. ([Reuters](https://jack1065.com/2024/10/01/cnn-launches-paywall-to-charge-3-99-a-month-for-some-users/))

## The Collins double-duty

As the second Trump administration took shape, Thompson made his signature talent move. CNN named [Kaitlan Collins](/people/kaitlan-collins/) anchor and chief White House correspondent in what it called "an unprecedented and expanded new role" — she kept her weekday 9 p.m. program, "The Source with Kaitlan Collins," while leading coverage of the new Trump White House across digital, television and streaming. Thompson's line: "Kaitlan Collins is the perfect person to lead coverage of the new Trump White House, even as she continues to anchor her key primetime show 'The Source.'" Collins had joined CNN in 2017, broken stories across both administrations, and moderated the network's May 2023 live town hall with Trump. ([CNN](http://EditorandPublisher.com/stories/cnn-announces-broad-new-role-for-anchor-kaitlan-collins,253162))

One correction is worth making, since it has caused confusion: there was no new "dual role" for Thompson himself in 2024. His Chairman-and-CEO title dates from the 2023 appointment. The double-duty role that year was Collins's — an anchor running a White House beat — and it was Thompson's design.
