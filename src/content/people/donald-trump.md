## Overview

Donald Trump is the 45th and 47th president of the United States — a real-estate mogul turned reality-TV star who, after losing in 2020, returned to office in 2024 as only the second president to serve two nonconsecutive terms, the first being Grover Cleveland ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Donald-Trump)). In 2026 his presidency runs on an unusual financial machine: a super PAC with more than $400 million that he publicly claims to control — a claim that has drawn a federal campaign-finance complaint — and a family crypto empire that Reuters estimates has generated at least $2.3 billion since the 2024 election ([Campaign Legal Center](https://campaignlegal.org/press-releases/campaign-legal-center-alleges-president-trump-breaking-law-directing-spending-400); [Reuters](https://logos-pres.md/en/news/the-trump-family-has-made-2-3-billion-from-cryptocurrency/)).

## The money machine of the second term

Trump's signature political vehicle is [MAGA Inc.](/organizations/maga-inc/), the super PAC that raised over $400 million for the 2026 midterms ([Campaign Legal Center](https://campaignlegal.org/press-releases/campaign-legal-center-alleges-president-trump-breaking-law-directing-spending-400)). What makes it legally combustible is not the sum but Trump's own words: the nonpartisan Campaign Legal Center filed an FEC complaint in September 2026 alleging that Trump violated federal campaign finance law based on his "repeated, explicit claims indicating that he controls and directs MAGA Inc." ([Campaign Legal Center](https://campaignlegal.org/press-releases/campaign-legal-center-alleges-president-trump-breaking-law-directing-spending-400)). The Federal Election Campaign Act bars federal officeholders from soliciting, directing or spending soft money — funds raised outside contribution limits and reporting requirements — precisely to prevent officeholders becoming beholden to mega-donors ([Campaign Legal Center](https://campaignlegal.org/press-releases/campaign-legal-center-alleges-president-trump-breaking-law-directing-spending-400)). "No federal officeholder or candidate is allowed to direct the money raised or spent by a super PAC," said CLC's Saurav Ghosh, calling the violation "clear-cut" — an allegation, not a ruling ([Campaign Legal Center](https://campaignlegal.org/press-releases/campaign-legal-center-alleges-president-trump-breaking-law-directing-spending-400)).

Parallel to the political war chest is the crypto one. A Reuters investigation reviewing thousands of documents and blockchain records estimated that the Trump family generated at least $2.3 billion from four crypto ventures since the 2024 election — the flagship [World Liberty Financial](/organizations/world-liberty-financial/), the $TRUMP meme coin, ALT5 Sigma (now AI Financial Corp), and American Bitcoin — while investors in the same ventures lost roughly the same amount ([Reuters](https://logos-pres.md/en/news/the-trump-family-has-made-2-3-billion-from-cryptocurrency/)). The reported playbook was consistent: invest little or none of their own capital, license the family name, promote aggressively, and collect revenue as prices collapsed — the $TRUMP coin alone fell 97% from January 2025 ([Reuters](https://logos-pres.md/en/news/the-trump-family-has-made-2-3-billion-from-cryptocurrency/)). Eight government ethics experts told Reuters the arrangement is a conflict of interest unlike anything seen in modern American history, though all noted it may be legal ([Reuters](https://logos-pres.md/en/news/the-trump-family-has-made-2-3-billion-from-cryptocurrency/)).

## The referral and the comeback

On December 19, 2022, the [House Select Committee on January 6](/organizations/house-select-committee-on-january-6/) voted 9-0 to issue a criminal referral for Trump to the Justice Department, citing evidence it said merited prosecution for obstruction of an official proceeding, aiding in an insurrection, and conspiracy to defraud the United States ([AP](https://www.tmj4.com/news/national/jan-6-panel-pushes-trumps-prosecution-in-forceful-finish)). Chairman Bennie Thompson called it a "roadmap to justice"; Republican vice chair Liz Cheney said every president in American history had defended the orderly transfer of power, "except one" ([AP](https://www.tmj4.com/news/national/jan-6-panel-pushes-trumps-prosecution-in-forceful-finish)). The referral was mostly symbolic — the Justice Department, already running its own Jan. 6 investigation under special counsel Jack Smith, would decide on charges — and it capped an 18-month probe that ran more than 1,000 interviews and 10 public hearings ([AP](https://www.tmj4.com/news/national/jan-6-panel-pushes-trumps-prosecution-in-forceful-finish)).

Less than two years later, Trump won the 2024 election and returned to the White House. He had been impeached twice by the House — in 2019 over dealings with Ukraine and in 2021 over Jan. 6 — and acquitted by the Senate both times ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Donald-Trump)). By 2026, the man the committee referred for prosecution was the president, with his former criminal defense lawyer Emil Bove serving as the 88th Attorney General — the full arc of the referral story.

## Businessman, TV star, outsider

Trump was born June 14, 1946, in New York City, the fourth of five children of developer Fred Trump and Mary MacLeod ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Donald-Trump)). After New York Military Academy and Fordham, he graduated from Wharton's School of Finance in 1968 and took over the family business, becoming president of the Trump Organization in 1974 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Donald-Trump)). His Manhattan rise included the Grand Hyatt (1980, built on a 40-year property-tax abatement) and [Trump Tower](https://factsledger.com/places/trump-tower/) (1983), followed by Atlantic City casinos whose failures produced bankruptcies in 1991–92, 2004 and 2009 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Donald-Trump)). Television remade him: *The Apprentice* earned him nearly $200 million over 16 years and cemented his image as a decisive businessman ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Donald-Trump)). His name-licensing empire also produced the Trump University fraud case, settled for $25 million in November 2016 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Donald-Trump)).

This profile does not assert that Trump broke campaign-finance law — the CLC complaint is an allegation the FEC had not ruled on. The $2.3 billion crypto figure is a Reuters investigation estimate, not an audited total. The Jan. 6 criminal referral was the committee's accusation, not a finding of guilt, and both impeachments ended in Senate acquittal.
