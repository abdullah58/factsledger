## Overview

Timothy J. Mayopoulos is an American financial-services executive and lawyer who was president and chief executive officer of [Fannie Mae](/organizations/fannie-mae/) from 2012 to 2018, president of [Blend](/organizations/blend/) from 2019 to 2023, and the chief executive the FDIC appointed to run Silicon Valley Bridge Bank after Silicon Valley Bank failed in March 2023 ([Happen Bank](https://www.happen.com/company/leadership/timothy-j-mayopoulos?b=lc); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/former-fannie-mae-chief-named-as-ceo-of-silicon-valley-bank-n-a/)). His career is a series of crisis postings: he joined Fannie Mae months after the government seized it in 2008, left a Wall Street legal career for a fintech startup, and was handed a failed bank on a Monday morning ([Cornell University](https://as.cornell.edu/news/fannie-mae-ceo-sees-his-career-interests-come-full-circle); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/former-fannie-mae-chief-named-as-ceo-of-silicon-valley-bank-n-a/)). In April 2026 he became independent chairman of LendingClub, the online lender whose board he joined in 2016 ([LendingClub](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001409970/5c7e007f-a17c-40bd-98dd-fccc6a84731e.pdf)).

The through-line is his own description of himself to Cornell University: he is "one of those people who runs toward fires" ([Cornell University](https://as.cornell.edu/news/fannie-mae-ceo-sees-his-career-interests-come-full-circle)). At Fannie Mae he delivered more than $167 billion in dividends to the [U.S. Treasury](/organizations/us-department-of-the-treasury/) ([Happen Bank](https://www.happen.com/company/leadership/timothy-j-mayopoulos?b=lc)). At Blend he helped take a mortgage-software startup public at a targeted valuation of nearly $4 billion ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-hopes-to-be-valued-at-4b-following-ipo/?shared=email&msg=fail)). At Silicon Valley Bridge Bank he kept a failed lender open until a buyer was found ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/26/first-citizens-to-acquire-silicon-valley-bank/)).

## A Wall Street lawyer who ran toward fires

Mayopoulos grew up in modest circumstances: his father was a union organizer who never finished college, and his mother worked part-time at a Sears store to send him to Cornell, where he majored in English and graduated in 1980 with only $2,000 of debt ([Cornell University](https://as.cornell.edu/news/fannie-mae-ceo-sees-his-career-interests-come-full-circle)). After graduation he worked as a VISTA volunteer and at Broome County Legal Services, then earned his J.D. cum laude at NYU ([Cornell University](https://as.cornell.edu/news/fannie-mae-ceo-sees-his-career-interests-come-full-circle); [Happen Bank](https://www.happen.com/company/leadership/timothy-j-mayopoulos?b=lc)). He clerked for a federal judge, practised at Davis Polk in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/), spent nearly two years from 1994 on the Whitewater investigation, and held senior roles at Donaldson, Lufkin & Jenrette, [Credit Suisse First Boston](/organizations/credit-suisse/) and Deutsche Bank ([Cornell University](https://as.cornell.edu/news/fannie-mae-ceo-sees-his-career-interests-come-full-circle); [MortgageOrb](https://mortgageorb.com/tim-mayopoulos-joins-blend-as-president-among-other-executive-hires)). He then became general counsel of Bank of America ([Finance-Commerce](https://finance-commerce.com/2012/06/fannie-mae-names-mayopoulos-as-ceo/)). On 10 December 2008, as the bank prepared to acquire Merrill Lynch, he was told to leave immediately and testified he was given no reason ([ABA Journal](https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/how_b_of_as_gc_got_fired_leave_now_and_leave_your_blackberry_behind)). The [SEC](/organizations/us-securities-and-exchange-commission/) later said he was fired to make room for Brian Moynihan ([ABA Journal](https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/sec_says_bofa_gc_didnt_get_fired_over_legal_advice/)).

## Turning around Fannie Mae

Mayopoulos joined Fannie Mae in 2009 as executive vice president, general counsel and corporate secretary, shortly after the government placed it in conservatorship, later becoming chief administrative officer ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/former-fannie-mae-chief-named-as-ceo-of-silicon-valley-bank-n-a/); [MortgageOrb](https://mortgageorb.com/tim-mayopoulos-joins-blend-as-president-among-other-executive-hires)). He was named president and chief executive officer effective 18 June 2012, succeeding Michael J. Williams ([Finance-Commerce](https://finance-commerce.com/2012/06/fannie-mae-names-mayopoulos-as-ceo/)). The government had put $116 billion into Fannie Mae; by January 2018 taxpayers had received almost $50 billion more in dividends than was invested, and total dividends passed $167 billion under his leadership ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2018/01/24/fannie-mae-ceo-reflects-housing-10-years-after); [Happen Bank](https://www.happen.com/company/leadership/timothy-j-mayopoulos?b=lc)). He pushed Day 1 Certainty, which verifies borrower assets, income and employment upfront, and shifted much of the company's credit risk to private capital ([Fannie Mae](https://www.fanniemae.com/newsroom/fannie-mae-news/fannie-mae-first-quarter-2017-earnings-media-call-remarks-timothy-j-mayopoulos); [Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2018/01/24/fannie-mae-ceo-reflects-housing-10-years-after)). Fannie Mae financed roughly 30 percent of all mortgages in the [United States](/places/united-states/), on a roughly $3 trillion balance sheet ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2018/01/24/fannie-mae-ceo-reflects-housing-10-years-after)). Glassdoor ranked him 59th among U.S. chief executives in 2017, with 92 percent employee approval ([CEOWORLD Magazine](https://ceoworld.biz/2017/06/23/the-great-fall-of-tim-cook-americas-top-100-highest-rated-ceos-for-2017/)). He left on 15 October 2018, succeeded as interim chief executive by Hugh R. Frater, a former Berkadia chief executive ([Fannie Mae](http://www.fanniemae.com/portal/media/corporate-news/2018/interim-fannie-mae-ceo-frater-6773.html)).

## The conflict-of-interest investigation

In 2017 the Federal Housing Finance Agency's Inspector General investigated whether Mayopoulos had promptly disclosed a "significant and ongoing" dating relationship with the former chief legal officer of Fifth Third Bancorp ([House Oversight Committee](https://oversight.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/2017-05-10-JEC-to-Watt-FHFA-Fannie-CEO-Conflict-of-Interest-due-5-24-R...1.pdf)). The Inspector General found a prudent chief executive would have disclosed the relationship in August 2015, but Mayopoulos decided that November not to disclose it, answering "no" on his annual conflict-of-interest questionnaire ([House letter](https://oversight.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/2017-05-10-JEC-to-Watt-FHFA-Fannie-CEO-Conflict-of-Interest-due-5-24-R...1.pdf)). In March 2016 his subordinate, the chief compliance and ethics officer, verbally told him to seek guidance if any Fifth Third business arose — an informal recusal never formally documented. Fifth Third fired its chief legal officer in July 2016 over the relationship; that month Mayopoulos also sponsored an out-of-cycle $985,000-to-$1,050,000 pay rise for the compliance chief. Fifth Third sold $944 million in loans to Fannie Mae and serviced $13.2–14.1 billion of its loans while the relationship was undisclosed ([House letter](https://oversight.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/2017-05-10-JEC-to-Watt-FHFA-Fannie-CEO-Conflict-of-Interest-due-5-24-R...1.pdf)). The Inspector General recommended discipline up to removal for "inappropriate tone at the top"; the FHFA director promised action by 21 June 2017, with no outcome on record ([House letter](https://oversight.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/2017-05-10-JEC-to-Watt-FHFA-Fannie-CEO-Conflict-of-Interest-due-5-24-R...1.pdf)).
## Blend and the move into fintech

In January 2019 Mayopoulos joined [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/)-based Blend as its first president and a board member, leading go-to-market and corporate support functions ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/47971-former-fannie-mae-ceo-tim-mayopoulos-named-president-of-blend/)). Blend's chief executive, [Nima Ghamsari](/people/nima-ghamsari/), called him "an innovative and transformational leader", and Mayopoulos said he had an "abiding passion" for affordable housing and technology ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/47971-former-fannie-mae-ceo-tim-mayopoulos-named-president-of-blend/)). Blend's platform had processed more than $230 billion in loan applications in 2018, with 350 employees and more than 130 customers ([MortgageOrb](https://mortgageorb.com/tim-mayopoulos-joins-blend-as-president-among-other-executive-hires)). In July 2021 Blend set terms for its initial public offering, aiming to raise up to $360 million at a valuation of nearly $4 billion — 20 million shares at $16 to $18, listed on the [New York Stock Exchange](/organizations/new-york-stock-exchange/) as BLND, with [Goldman Sachs](/organizations/goldman-sachs/), [Allen & Company](/organizations/allen-and-company/) and [Wells Fargo](/organizations/wells-fargo/) as lead underwriters ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-hopes-to-be-valued-at-4b-following-ipo/?shared=email&msg=fail)). Blend had last been valued at $3.3 billion after a January 2021 Series G round ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-hopes-to-be-valued-at-4b-following-ipo/?shared=email&msg=fail)). In January 2023 he notified Blend he would step down as president in the first quarter, as the company cut about 340 jobs — 28 percent of its U.S. workforce ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-cuts-workforce-shakes-up-leadership/)).

## The Silicon Valley Bank rescue

Two months after leaving Blend came his third crisis posting. Silicon Valley Bank was closed on 10 March 2023 by California regulators after a run on its deposits, and the FDIC was appointed receiver ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/former-fannie-mae-chief-named-as-ceo-of-silicon-valley-bank-n-a/)). On 13 March the FDIC transferred all deposits, insured and uninsured, and substantially all assets to a newly created bridge bank, Silicon Valley Bridge Bank, N.A., and named Mayopoulos its chief executive ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/former-fannie-mae-chief-named-as-ceo-of-silicon-valley-bank-n-a/)). He was known to the FDIC, having sat on its Systemic Resolution Advisory Committee for more than two years ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/former-fannie-mae-chief-named-as-ceo-of-silicon-valley-bank-n-a/)). He emailed clients that evening that the bank was "open and conducting business as usual" ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/former-fannie-mae-chief-named-as-ceo-of-silicon-valley-bank-n-a/)). On 27 March 2023 First Citizens Bank agreed to buy about $72 billion of the bridge bank's deposits and loans at a $16.5 billion discount, with the FDIC estimating the failure would cost its Deposit Insurance Fund about $20 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/26/first-citizens-to-acquire-silicon-valley-bank/)). Seventeen branches reopened as First Citizens and Mayopoulos stepped down, telling staff the deal "keeps the bank together in a way that provides the most value for our customers" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/26/first-citizens-to-acquire-silicon-valley-bank/)).

## Boards, advisory work and the LendingClub chairmanship

Mayopoulos joined the LendingClub board as an independent director in August 2016, during the upheaval that followed the sudden departure of founder Renaud Laplanche, when the online lender had just posted an $81.4 million quarterly loss ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/37736-fannie-mae-ceo-timothy-mayopoulos-joining-lending-club-board-of-directors/)). He said he was joining because LendingClub had "an opportunity to change banking for the better" and made affordable credit more broadly accessible ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/37736-fannie-mae-ceo-timothy-mayopoulos-joining-lending-club-board-of-directors/)). He has also served on the boards of SAIC and Blend, and on the FDIC's Systemic Resolution Advisory Committee since 2020 ([Happen Bank](https://www.happen.com/company/leadership/timothy-j-mayopoulos?b=lc)). On 22 January 2026 LendingClub disclosed that John C. (Hans) Morris would resign as independent chairman effective 31 March 2026, and that the board had appointed Mayopoulos to succeed him effective 1 April 2026, citing his "decades of experience in financial services leadership and governance" ([LendingClub](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001409970/5c7e007f-a17c-40bd-98dd-fccc6a84731e.pdf)). ## Frequently asked questions

**What is Timothy Mayopoulos best known for?** He is best known as Fannie Mae's president and CEO from 2012 to 2018, returning the government-controlled mortgage giant to profit and delivering more than $167 billion in dividends to the U.S. Treasury ([Happen Bank](https://www.happen.com/company/leadership/timothy-j-mayopoulos?b=lc)). He later led Blend as president through its 2021 IPO and ran Silicon Valley Bridge Bank after Silicon Valley Bank failed in March 2023 ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/former-fannie-mae-chief-named-as-ceo-of-silicon-valley-bank-n-a/)).

**Why was Timothy Mayopoulos fired from Bank of America?** He was dismissed as general counsel on 10 December 2008, as Bank of America prepared to acquire Merrill Lynch, and was told to leave immediately with no reason given ([ABA Journal](https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/how_b_of_as_gc_got_fired_leave_now_and_leave_your_blackberry_behind)). The SEC later said he was fired to create a position for Brian Moynihan, while bank officials called it a general downsizing; the reason remains disputed ([ABA Journal](https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/sec_says_bofa_gc_didnt_get_fired_over_legal_advice/)).

**What happened in the Fannie Mae conflict-of-interest investigation?** The FHFA Inspector General found Mayopoulos disclosed a relationship with a Fifth Third Bancorp executive months late, in March 2016 rather than August 2015, and recommended discipline up to removal as CEO ([House Oversight Committee](https://oversight.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/2017-05-10-JEC-to-Watt-FHFA-Fannie-CEO-Conflict-of-Interest-due-5-24-R...1.pdf)). The FHFA director promised action by June 2017, but no source read states what followed; Mayopoulos stayed CEO until October 2018 ([House Oversight Committee](https://oversight.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/2017-05-10-JEC-to-Watt-FHFA-Fannie-CEO-Conflict-of-Interest-due-5-24-R...1.pdf)).

**What is Timothy Mayopoulos doing now?** He became independent chairman of the LendingClub board on 1 April 2026, succeeding Hans Morris, and remains a director of Blend and SAIC ([LendingClub](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001409970/5c7e007f-a17c-40bd-98dd-fccc6a84731e.pdf); [Happen Bank](https://www.happen.com/company/leadership/timothy-j-mayopoulos?b=lc)). LendingClub has rebranded as Happen Bank, which lists him as its chairman ([Happen Bank](https://www.happen.com/company/leadership/timothy-j-mayopoulos?b=lc)).
