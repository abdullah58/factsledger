## Overview

Before Jensen Huang ran the world's most valuable chip company, he washed dishes at a Denny's. He was 15, working at a Denny's in Portland — dishwasher first, then busboy, then waiter. He still talked about it with pride decades later: "I was a dishwasher, I was a busboy, I waited tables," he said. "No one can carry more coffee cups than I can." ([NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-dennys-trillion/))

In 1993 he co-founded NVIDIA with Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem — in a Denny's booth in Silicon Valley, a spot Huang picked, he said, because "it had all the coffee you could drink and no one could chase you out" ([NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-dennys-trillion/)). Thirty years later, in September 2023, he returned to that Denny's with the chain's CEO Kelli Valade to unveil a plaque reading "the booth that launched a $1 trillion company," while Denny's announced a $25,000 Trillion-Dollar Incubator Contest for the next idea born in one of its booths ([The FPS Review](https://www.thefpsreview.com/2023/09/27/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-returns-to-the-birthplace-of-the-gpu-dennys/); [NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-dennys-trillion/)). Of his restaurant jobs he said simply: "It teaches you humility, it teaches you hard work, it teaches you hospitality." ([NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-dennys-trillion/))

Huang — full name Jen-Hsun Huang — has been the co-founder, president and CEO of NVIDIA since he started the company in 1993, one of the longest continuous runs as a tech chief executive anywhere ([NVIDIA](http://nvidianews.nvidia.com/bios/jensen-huang)). Born in Taiwan in 1963, he came to the United States as a boy, studied electrical engineering at Oregon State and Stanford, and spent his early career at chipmakers LSI Logic and AMD before betting on 3D graphics ([Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/nvidia-founder-and-ceo-jensen-huang-to-give-caltechs-130th-commencement-address)).

That bet became the company behind the graphics processing unit — NVIDIA says it invented the GPU in 1999, a claim IEEE repeated when it gave Huang its highest honor — and, decades later, the hardware the AI boom runs on ([NVIDIA](http://nvidianews.nvidia.com/bios/jensen-huang); [IEEE](https://www.centralcharts.com/en/news/5414565-eqs-news-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-awarded-2026-ieee-medal-of-honor-technology-s-most-prestigious-recognition)). On January 6, 2026, IEEE announced Huang as the recipient of its 2026 Medal of Honor, with a $2 million prize, for visionary leadership and pioneering work in accelerated computing ([IEEE](https://www.centralcharts.com/en/news/5414565-eqs-news-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-awarded-2026-ieee-medal-of-honor-technology-s-most-prestigious-recognition)). In March 2026 he was appointed to the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/trump-names-jensen-huang-mark-zuckerberg-to-13-member-science-and-tech-council/)).

## Early life and education

Huang was born in Taiwan in 1963 and spent part of his childhood in Thailand before the family sent him and his brother to the United States ([Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/nvidia-founder-and-ceo-jensen-huang-to-give-caltechs-130th-commencement-address)). He was nine when the two boys arrived in Tacoma, Washington; the family later reunited in Oregon, where he attended Aloha High School and graduated at 16 ([Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/nvidia-founder-and-ceo-jensen-huang-to-give-caltechs-130th-commencement-address)).

He earned his bachelor's degree in electrical engineering from Oregon State University and a master's from Stanford, finishing the Stanford degree in 1992 ([NVIDIA](http://nvidianews.nvidia.com/bios/jensen-huang); [Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/nvidia-founder-and-ceo-jensen-huang-to-give-caltechs-130th-commencement-address)). Before starting his own company he worked at LSI Logic and then AMD, two stops that gave him a ground-level education in the chip business ([NVIDIA](http://nvidianews.nvidia.com/bios/jensen-huang)).

On stage at Stripe Sessions in 2024, Huang filled in the years the institutional biographies skim: he arrived in the United States in 1973 at nine, his older brother almost eleven, and attended the Oneida Baptist Institute in Kentucky. He was the youngest kid in the school — the older kids worked the tobacco farm, and after they left for the day he cleaned the bathrooms. "I was definitely the best bathroom cleaner the world's ever seen, I'm sure of it," he joked. The Denny's jobs came later: "I started out as a dishwasher and became a busboy and became a waiter. And I loved every one of them." ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/gb/sessions/2024/a-conversation-with-nvidias-jensen-huang))

## Founding NVIDIA and the GPU era

In 1993 Huang founded NVIDIA with Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem, aiming at 3D graphics and visualization — a niche market at the time ([NVIDIA](http://nvidianews.nvidia.com/bios/jensen-huang); [Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/nvidia-founder-and-ceo-jensen-huang-to-give-caltechs-130th-commencement-address)). The early years were rough, but the RIVA 128 chip's success in 1997 steadied the company ([Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/nvidia-founder-and-ceo-jensen-huang-to-give-caltechs-130th-commencement-address)).

Two years later came the claim that defines the company's identity: NVIDIA says it invented the GPU in 1999 ([NVIDIA](http://nvidianews.nvidia.com/bios/jensen-huang)). IEEE's 2026 Medal of Honor announcement repeats that statement — that the company invented the first GPU in 1999 — which is worth phrasing carefully: it credits NVIDIA the company, a team effort, not Huang alone ([IEEE](https://www.centralcharts.com/en/news/5414565-eqs-news-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-awarded-2026-ieee-medal-of-honor-technology-s-most-prestigious-recognition)). From graphics, the company built the CUDA platform and an accelerated-computing architecture that Caltech describes as the bridge from graphics to general-purpose parallel computing — the technical road that led to the AI surge, including the AlexNet moment ([Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/nvidia-founder-and-ceo-jensen-huang-to-give-caltechs-130th-commencement-address)).

## How he runs NVIDIA

Huang laid out his management system in a 2024 fireside chat at Stripe Sessions, and it is deliberately unconventional. He has about 60 direct reports — "sixty direct reports, yup" — and he holds no one-on-one meetings: "Almost everything that I say, I say to everybody all at the same time." His stated reason: "I love that everybody's working off of the same song sheet. I love that there is no privileged access to information." With a staff that large, he said, the company sheds roughly seven layers of hierarchy; his staff meetings run every other week and are organized around issues, not reports ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/gb/sessions/2024/a-conversation-with-nvidias-jensen-huang)).

Feedback happens the same way — in public. "I give you feedback right there in front of everybody," he said. "Feedback is learning. For what reason are you the only person who should learn this?... Learning from mistakes, other people's mistakes, is the best way to learn." He added: "I really discourage one-on-ones." ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/gb/sessions/2024/a-conversation-with-nvidias-jensen-huang))

His strategy doctrine has a name: "zero-billion-dollar markets." "I'd rather be a market maker, market creator than a market taker," he said — if something has never been done before and is genuinely hard, the market for it is zero, which keeps less committed rivals away. He distrusts spreadsheets for such bets: "I hate spreadsheets because you can make spreadsheets do whatever you want... Words are reasoning." CUDA was the doctrine's hardest test: he called it "an incredible disaster overnight" — it added cost to the chips, crushed gross margins, and sank the company's market cap to about $1 billion — yet he kept funding it for roughly a decade because he believed the reasoning. "Could NVIDIA be as successful in AI without CUDA? No. Impossible. It is potentially one of the most important inventions in modern computing." ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/gb/sessions/2024/a-conversation-with-nvidias-jensen-huang))

He is blunt about what it costs. "I don't love every day of my job... Every year I'm not happy about the company, but I love the company every single second," he said — and the line he has used with Stanford students: "I wish upon you plenty of pain and suffering," meaning greatness requires struggle. He dislikes firing people — "I'd rather improve you than give up on you" — and put it this way: "I'd rather torture you into greatness because I believe in you." His own routine: awake at 6 (it used to be 5; the dogs changed it), working from the moment he wakes to the moment he sleeps, seven days a week, with no reporting meetings — only meetings about problems, ideas, brainstorming, or creation. "CEOs are pinch hitters," he said. And on why a 28,000-person company out-punches giants several times its size: "You can achieve operational excellence through process but craft can only be achieved with tenure." ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/gb/sessions/2024/a-conversation-with-nvidias-jensen-huang))

## The AI boom and the $5 trillion milestone

Huang has a habit of showing up in person at the moments that matter. In 2016 he boxed up the first DGX-1 supercomputer unit, drove it to San Francisco, and delivered it to OpenAI himself — a $300,000 machine, by his own account, handed to [Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk/) in a cramped second-floor office that, as Huang put it, "turned out to be OpenAI" ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/jensen-huangs-secret-soft-spot-for-elon-musk-heres-the-story-you-didnt-know/articleshow/125766939.cms)).

Under his leadership, NVIDIA became the first company to top $5 trillion in market capitalization in October 2025, per IEEE ([IEEE](https://www.centralcharts.com/en/news/5414565-eqs-news-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-awarded-2026-ieee-medal-of-honor-technology-s-most-prestigious-recognition)). On December 11, 2025, TIME named the "Architects of AI" its Person of the Year, with Huang among the illustrated figures alongside Sam Altman, Elon Musk, [Lisa Su](/people/lisa-su/), [Dario Amodei](/people/dario-amodei/), Demis Hassabis, [Fei-Fei Li](/people/fei-fei-li/) and Mark Zuckerberg; he used the moment to talk about AI's economic significance ([Agence France-Presse](https://cebudailynews.inquirer.net/677990/time-magazine-names-architects-of-ai-as-person-of-the-year)).

The wealth followed the stock. Forbes put his fortune at $198.8 billion (No. 8) and Bloomberg at $191 billion on September 5, 2026, tied largely to an approximately 3% NVIDIA stake — figures that move daily and should be read as date-stamped estimates, not a fixed number ([Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/world-news/us-news/inside-jensen-huangs-net-worth-surge-how-a-12-9b-deal-made-the-nvidia-ceo-even-richer/4332490/?ref=articlehyperlinking)).

## Awards, public role, and philanthropy

The honors have stacked up. In 2025 Huang was one of seven recipients of the Queen Elizabeth Prize for Engineering, presented by King Charles III at St James's Palace for the GPU architectures behind modern AI; NVIDIA's chief scientist Bill Dally shared the prize, and Huang also received the Professor Stephen Hawking Fellowship at the Cambridge Union ([NVIDIA Blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-founder-and-ceo-jensen-huang-and-chief-scientist-bill-dally-awarded-prestigious-queen-elizabeth-prize-for-engineering/)). The 2026 IEEE Medal of Honor — the organization's highest accolade, established in 1917, whose past recipients include Vinton Cerf, Robert Kahn, Bradford Parkinson and Morris Chang — put him in rare company ([IEEE](https://www.centralcharts.com/en/news/5414565-eqs-news-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-awarded-2026-ieee-medal-of-honor-technology-s-most-prestigious-recognition)).

In March 2026 President Trump named Huang to the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology, in an initial 13-member group whose work included AI policy amid U.S.–China competition; the council was co-chaired by David Sacks and Michael Kratsios ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/trump-names-jensen-huang-mark-zuckerberg-to-13-member-science-and-tech-council/)). The initial 13-member group also included Mark Zuckerberg and Larry Ellison ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/trump-names-jensen-huang-mark-zuckerberg-to-13-member-science-and-tech-council/)).

The philanthropy runs through the family.
