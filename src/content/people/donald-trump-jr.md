## Overview

Donald Trump Jr. is the eldest son of U.S. President Donald Trump, an executive vice president of the family’s Trump Organization, and — since November 2024 — a partner at the venture firm 1789 Capital, which has grown from under $1 billion to about $3.5 billion in assets under management during his father’s second term ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/20/politics/trump-jr-investments-federal-funding-invs?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). Alongside his brother Eric, he has built a portfolio of defense-sector investments — drone-component maker Unusual Machines, Israeli drone-maker XTEND and Powerus — that have drawn conflict-of-interest scrutiny as those companies compete for government contracts ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/trump-jr-backed-unusual-machines-us-asset-manager-invest-10-million-canadas-2026-09-28/)). In September 2026 he was at the center of two controversies: a Reuters exclusive revealing a $10 million investment in Canadian drone maker Draganfly, and ProPublica’s investigation showing that Russian businessman Umar Kremlev bankrolled much of his Bahamas wedding weekend — prompting a House Oversight investigation and a pledge to repay ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/trump-jr-backed-unusual-machines-us-asset-manager-invest-10-million-canadas-2026-09-28/); [ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/article/donald-trump-jr-wedding-umar-kremlev-reporting)).

## From Trump Organization to political firebrand

Donald John Trump Jr. was born on December 31, 1977, in New York City, the eldest child of Donald Trump Sr. and his first wife, Czech-born model Ivana Trump; his younger siblings from that marriage are Ivanka and Eric Trump, with half-siblings Tiffany and Barron from their father’s later marriages ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Donald-Trump-Jr)). He graduated from the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania in 2000 with a degree in economics and joined the Trump Organization in 2001, rising to executive vice president with responsibility for real estate, hotel, commercial and golf projects — including the Trump International Hotel and Tower Chicago (2009) and Trump SoHo (2010) ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Donald-Trump-Jr)).

From 2005 to 2015 he appeared on his father’s reality show *The Apprentice* as a boardroom judge ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Donald-Trump-Jr)). When Donald Trump Sr. launched his 2015 presidential campaign, Trump Jr. became one of its most visible surrogates, campaigning aggressively on social media. That role produced his most scrutinized episode: in June 2016 he met privately at Trump Tower with a Russian attorney who had claimed to have damaging information about Hillary Clinton ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Donald-Trump-Jr)). After the election he worked on his father’s transition team; when Trump took office in 2017, Don Jr. and Eric assumed control of the Trump Organization to avoid conflicts of interest ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Donald-Trump-Jr)).

He remained his father’s most combative public defender. On January 6, 2021, he spoke at the rally in Washington, D.C., warning Republicans who would not challenge certification of the election that “we’re coming for you,” and reportedly asked his father to condemn the Capitol attack as it unfolded ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Donald-Trump-Jr)). In 2022 he testified before the congressional committee investigating the attack, and later that year he testified in New York Attorney General Letitia James’s inquiry into the Trump Organization; in September 2022 James sued the organization, Trump Sr. and Trump Jr. among others, alleging “persistent and repeated business fraud” ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Donald-Trump-Jr)). He also published two books, *Triggered: How the Left Thrives on Hate and Wants to Silence Us* (2019) and *Liberal Privilege: Joe Biden and the Democrats’ Defense of the Indefensible* (2020) ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Donald-Trump-Jr)).

## 1789 Capital: the post-election investment vehicle

Days after his father’s 2024 election victory, Trump Jr. announced at a GOP donor retreat that he would not take a job in the administration but would instead join a little-known Florida investment firm: 1789 Capital, founded in 2023 by Omeed Malik and co-founders as an anti-ESG vehicle pitched as “patriotic capitalism,” named for the year the Bill of Rights was drafted ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/20/politics/trump-jr-investments-federal-funding-invs?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). The timing coincided with explosive growth. 1789 Capital disclosed managing nearly $1 billion in assets less than a year after he joined; by May 2026 a partner told the Financial Times it managed about $3.5 billion, with roughly 40 percent of the money coming from foreign investors, according to SEC filings ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/20/politics/trump-jr-investments-federal-funding-invs?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)).

A CNN analysis of federal contracting data found that ten defense, space and software companies backed by 1789 Capital were awarded more than $1.6 billion in federal contracts and grants in the first 500 days of Trump’s second term — 79 percent more than they received in the same period at the end of Joe Biden’s term — with about 18 percent tied to no-bid contracts, versus 7.5 percent under Biden ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/20/politics/trump-jr-investments-federal-funding-invs?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). Among the beneficiaries: rare-earth magnet maker Vulcan Elements, selected for a $620 million loan from the Trump administration within months of 1789’s investment; Anduril, awarded about $1.25 billion in Trump’s first 500 days versus roughly $760 million in Biden’s last 500; and Axiom Space, which received more than $300 million under Trump against about $153 million before ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/20/politics/trump-jr-investments-federal-funding-invs?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)).

CNN stressed there was no evidence that 1789 Capital or Trump Jr. pushed for any of these awards: the firm’s attorney said “the answer is unequivocally no” when asked whether anyone at the firm had discussed federal contracts with the administration, and Trump Jr.’s spokesman said he plays no role in the contracting process and “does not interface with the Federal Government as part of his role with any company that he invests in or advises” ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/20/politics/trump-jr-investments-federal-funding-invs?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). At the same time, Trump Jr. publicly pitched investors on his access: “We understand what the administration wants to do, because we helped craft some of that messaging,” he told a Saudi business conference, “so we can be on the outside but still have that understanding of what they plan to do” ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/20/politics/trump-jr-investments-federal-funding-invs?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). Government watchdogs said the pattern could undermine public trust, and the [White House](https://factsledger.com/organizations/the-white-house) rejected the criticism, with spokeswoman Anna Kelly stating, “There are no conflicts of interest” ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/20/politics/trump-jr-investments-federal-funding-invs?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)).

## The drone portfolio: Unusual Machines, Draganfly and Pakistan

On November 27, 2024, Unusual Machines — a Florida drone-component company — announced that Trump Jr. was joining its advisory board as both an investor and an advisor. The company described him as the “second-largest shareholder,” holding over 330,000 shares, and CEO [Allan Evans](/people/allan-evans/) said the appointment would bring “unique expertise we need as we bring drone component manufacturing back to America” ([DroneLife](https://dronelife.com/2024/11/27/donald-trump-jr-joins-unusual-machines-advisory-board-stock-soars/)). Trump Jr. framed it in trade terms: “The need for drones is obvious. It is also obvious that we must stop buying Chinese drones and Chinese drone parts” ([DroneLife](https://dronelife.com/2024/11/27/donald-trump-jr-joins-unusual-machines-advisory-board-stock-soars/)). The stock soared more than 100 percent in early trading, closing at $9.77 — an 82.28 percent gain on the day, on volume of over 56 million shares ([DroneLife](https://dronelife.com/2024/11/27/donald-trump-jr-joins-unusual-machines-advisory-board-stock-soars/)).

Less than a year later the once-obscure company landed its biggest Pentagon deal yet: the U.S. Army contracted it to produce 3,500 drone motors and related components, signaling it would order another 20,000 parts the following year ([LiveMint](https://www.livemint.com/news/us-news/trump-jr-backed-drone-firm-unusual-machines-lands-pentagon-biggest-contract-set-to-supply-thousands-of-drone-parts-11761322957592.html)). CEO Evans said Trump Jr. did not help secure the deal, and his spokesperson told the Financial Times: “Don has never communicated with anyone in the Administration on behalf of Unusual Machines or about the contract in question. His advisory role with them has nothing to do with interfacing with the government” ([LiveMint](https://www.livemint.com/news/us-news/trump-jr-backed-drone-firm-unusual-machines-lands-pentagon-biggest-contract-set-to-supply-thousands-of-drone-parts-11761322957592.html)). The award followed a June 2025 executive order signed by President Trump to accelerate domestic drone production ([LiveMint](https://www.livemint.com/news/us-news/trump-jr-backed-drone-firm-unusual-machines-lands-pentagon-biggest-contract-set-to-supply-thousands-of-drone-parts-11761322957592.html)).

The next chapter broke on September 28, 2026, when Reuters exclusively reported that Trump Jr.-backed Unusual Machines and an unnamed U.S. asset management company were investing $10 million in Draganfly, a Canadian drone-services firm whose systems have been supplied to Ukraine for mapping and humanitarian missions ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/trump-jr-backed-unusual-machines-us-asset-manager-invest-10-million-canadas-2026-09-28/)). Each investor would put in $5 million, based on Draganfly’s $5.35 closing price on September 25, with the deal expected to close around September 30 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/trump-jr-backed-unusual-machines-us-asset-manager-invest-10-million-canadas-2026-09-28/)). Reuters noted the Trump family’s defense-sector investments “have drawn scrutiny amid concerns about potential conflicts of interest, particularly its stakes in defense firms that regularly compete for government contracts,” and that Trump Jr. did not respond to a request for comment ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/trump-jr-backed-unusual-machines-us-asset-manager-invest-10-million-canadas-2026-09-28/)).

A second drone thread ran through Pakistan. On September 16, 2026, a delegation from Powerus — a year-old Florida drone company backed by Don Jr. and Eric Trump through an investment vehicle called American Ventures — was received by Pakistan’s military chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir, in Rawalpindi; the next day Powerus announced a “limited procurement order” from Pakistan’s Ministry of Defence plus a memorandum of understanding with senior Pakistani defense officials ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/23/pakistans-new-drone-deal-is-with-trump-backed-firm-also-selling-to-india)). U.S. regulatory filings show American Ventures is expected to hold a 9.9 percent beneficial stake in the combined company after Powerus merges with Nasdaq-listed Aureus Greenway Holdings ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/23/pakistans-new-drone-deal-is-with-trump-backed-firm-also-selling-to-india)). A spokesperson for Trump Jr. told media outlets he is a passive investor with no role in the company’s operations or contracts and had no prior knowledge of the Pakistan deals; Powerus said the brothers “have no involvement in how the company runs its business or structures its defence relationships, domestically or internationally” and hold no corporate-governance role; the White House said there are no conflicts of interest ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/23/pakistans-new-drone-deal-is-with-trump-backed-firm-also-selling-to-india)). The deal also raised geopolitical eyebrows: weeks earlier Powerus had given India’s Paras Defence an exclusive license to make and sell its Guardian interceptor technology in India, and one of Powerus’s cofounders is a veteran of the Israeli military — a country Pakistan does not recognize ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/23/pakistans-new-drone-deal-is-with-trump-backed-firm-also-selling-to-india)). Reuters also reported the brothers have backed Israeli drone-maker XTEND and Powerus ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/trump-jr-backed-unusual-machines-us-asset-manager-invest-10-million-canadas-2026-09-28/)).

## The Kremlev wedding controversy

In May 2026, Trump Jr. married socialite Bettina Anderson over a wedding weekend spread across two ultra-exclusive private islands in the Bahamas’ Exumas chain; a Friday ceremony with an even smaller group was followed by Saturday and Sunday parties ([ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/article/donald-trump-jr-wedding-umar-kremlev-reporting)). ProPublica’s September investigation revealed that much of the weekend was secretly bankrolled by Umar Kremlev, a wealthy Russian businessman who heads the International Boxing Association (IBA) and has ties to Vladimir Putin and the Russian security services — ties that prompted the Ukrainian government to sanction him personally ([ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/article/donald-trump-jr-wedding-umar-kremlev-reporting)). ProPublica found, from financial records and interviews with three people familiar with the events, that Kremlev paid to rent out a private island for multiple nights at roughly $100,000 per night, picked up the tab for the Saturday and Sunday parties, and paid for the fireworks display — typically priced around $70,000 ([ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/article/donald-trump-jr-wedding-umar-kremlev-reporting)). The payments came from a Dubai entity affiliated with the IBA, which ProPublica noted has been financed by the Russian state-owned energy giant Gazprom ([ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/article/donald-trump-jr-wedding-umar-kremlev-reporting)).

Kremlev’s attendance had been scrubbed from the couple’s curated photos, but ProPublica confirmed it through a group shot posted on Instagram by a friend of the bride, showing Kremlev standing just behind the newlyweds among about 35 guests ([ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/article/donald-trump-jr-wedding-umar-kremlev-reporting)). Within hours of publication, the top Democrat on the House Oversight Committee opened an investigation, requesting communications between Trump Jr. and Kremlev and records of “all purchases made for your wedding by foreign nationals” ([ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/article/donald-trump-jr-wedding-umar-kremlev-reporting)). The couple responded on Instagram: “Our dear friend Umar very generously hosted two incredible nights of celebrations for us AFTER our wedding. It was an extraordinarily generous wedding gift from a friend, and something for which we were and remain incredibly grateful,” while drawing a distinction between the ceremony itself and the parties ([ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/article/donald-trump-jr-wedding-umar-kremlev-reporting)). A spokesperson for Trump Jr. did not dispute the payments, saying “Umar is a personal friend of Don” who is “not someone he has a business relationship with”; Kremlev’s press office said the two first met “a couple of years ago” and that Kremlev “has never discussed political matters with any of his American friends and acquaintances” ([ProPublica](https://www.propublica.org/article/donald-trump-jr-wedding-umar-kremlev-reporting)).

On September 18, 2026, President Trump told reporters his son would pay the money back: “When I heard that story, I asked about it. He said, ‘No, Dad, I’m paying it back.’ And that was a while ago. So I heard he’s paying” ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/world/trump-son-repay-russian-oligarch-wedding-parties-10884797/)). A person close to the situation confirmed to Reuters that Don Jr. intends to reimburse Kremlev ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/world/trump-son-repay-russian-oligarch-wedding-parties-10884797/)). Trump added that he did not personally know Kremlev and called the controversy “not a big deal,” noting he himself has thrown wedding parties for people at Mar-a-Lago ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/world/trump-son-repay-russian-oligarch-wedding-parties-10884797/)). Trump did not attend his son’s wedding ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/world/trump-son-repay-russian-oligarch-wedding-parties-10884797/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not state Don Jr.’s net worth: no source read verified a figure, and none is given. It does not assert that any federal contract or award was obtained through his influence — CNN, the companies involved, and the White House all deny any swaying, and no evidence of lobbying was found in the sources read.
