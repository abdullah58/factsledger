## Overview

Jared Kushner is an American real estate investor, former senior [White House](https://factsledger.com/organizations/the-white-house)
adviser and, since February 2026, Donald Trump's Special Envoy for Peace —
the diplomat Trump has deployed to Gaza, Iran and the Russia-Ukraine war
alongside special envoy Steve Witkoff
([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/9/6/us-envoys-hold-substantive-talks-with-zelenskyy-in-first-visit-to-kyiv);
[Associated Press](https://mb.com.ph/2026/09/07/us-envoys-witkoff-and-kushner-hold-talks-in-ukraine-after-meeting-with-putin)).
Born January 10, 1981, in Livingston, New Jersey, he built his early career at
[Kushner Companies](/organizations/kushner-companies/) — buying the *[New York Observer](/organizations/new-york-observer/)* in 2006 for about $10
million, acquiring 666 Fifth Avenue for $1.8 billion in 2007, and becoming
company CEO in 2008 — before marrying Ivanka Trump in 2009 and entering her
father's political orbit
([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner)).

Kushner ran Trump's 2016 campaign digital strategy, served as Senior Adviser
from January 2017 with a sprawling portfolio — the Office of American
Innovation, Middle East diplomacy, criminal-justice reform, trade and the
COVID-19 response — and was a central figure in the September 2020
Israel–UAE normalization known as the Abraham Accords
([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner);
[ThePrint](https://theprint.in/diplomacy/trump-didnt-want-to-visit-india-over-covid-fears-but-jared-kushner-convinced-him-ex-aide/746597/)).
After Trump's first term he founded the private equity firm [Affinity Partners](/organizations/affinity-partners/)
in 2021, anchored by a $2 billion commitment from Saudi Arabia's Public
Investment Fund — a fund whose growth past $6 billion has made Kushner's
overlap of private fundraising and public diplomacy one of the most
scrutinized features of his second-act career
([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/worldbiz/middle-east/kushners-affinity-partners-raises-5b-amid-diplomatic-role-middle-east-1543866)).

## From Livingston to Kushner Companies

EBSCO's reference biography records that Kushner was born January 10, 1981,
and raised in Livingston, New Jersey. He studied at Harvard and then earned
joint law and business degrees from New York University in 2007
([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner)).
In 2006 he bought the *New York Observer* for about $10 million and ran the
paper. The defining deal of his real estate years came in 2007, when Kushner
Companies acquired the Manhattan office tower at 666 Fifth Avenue for $1.8
billion, and he became the family firm's chief executive in 2008
([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner)).
He married Ivanka Trump in 2009, a union that would later make him both a
Trump family member and a Trump administration official
([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner)).

## The 2016 campaign and the first [White House](/places/white-house/) term

Kushner joined Donald Trump's presidential campaign in 2015 and ran its
digital strategy, guiding the creation of "Project Alamo" and working with
the data firm Cambridge Analytica
([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner)).
After the election he became Senior Adviser to the President in January 2017
and headed the Office of American Innovation, with a portfolio that spanned
Middle East diplomacy, criminal-justice reform, government modernization,
trade and the COVID-19 response
([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner)).
His influence inside the building was unusual for a staffer: he held walk-in
privileges at the Oval Office, and [Stephanie Grisham](/people/stephanie-grisham/)'s memoir, as reported by
ThePrint, portrayed him as effectively "in charge of Trump's re-election
campaign" — the same memoir account says Trump initially did not want to make
the February 2020 India visit over COVID fears, and that Kushner convinced him
to go
([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/diplomacy/trump-didnt-want-to-visit-india-over-covid-fears-but-jared-kushner-convinced-him-ex-aide/746597/)).

His record includes the First Step Act criminal-justice reform, work on the
USMCA trade agreement, the border wall effort, China trade policy and the
pandemic response; in 2018 he received Mexico's Aztec Eagle award and was
granted permanent top-security clearance
([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner)).
The investigations touched him too: his past meetings and business dealings
came under scrutiny in the Russia-related probes, and EBSCO's biography
states that in 2019 he was referred to federal prosecutors for inconsistencies
in his testimony
([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner)).
On January 6, 2021, he was traveling in the Middle East on diplomatic
business and did not take part in the day's events; he later spoke voluntarily
to the House Select Committee, and in 2023 he and Ivanka Trump were subpoenaed
by the special counsel to testify about the Capitol attack
([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner)).

The diplomatic achievement most associated with his first term is the
September 2020 Israel–UAE normalization — the Abraham Accords — in which he
played a key brokering role
([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/diplomacy/trump-didnt-want-to-visit-india-over-covid-fears-but-jared-kushner-convinced-him-ex-aide/746597/);
[EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner)).
He published the memoir *Breaking History* in 2022 and moved with his family
to Miami
([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner)).

## Affinity Partners and the conflict-of-interest fight

In 2021, after Trump's first term ended, Kushner founded the private equity
firm Affinity Partners
([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner)).
Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund committed $2 billion to the new firm —
approved, according to Dexter Filkins's *New Yorker* investigation as
summarized by UA.NEWS, over the objections of the fund's own investment
committee, which had cited Kushner's lack of investment experience, high risk
and significant fees; the deal was also noted in U.S. congressional documents
([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/worldbiz/middle-east/kushners-affinity-partners-raises-5b-amid-diplomatic-role-middle-east-1543866);
[UA.NEWS](https://ua.news/en/world/kushner-poiednuie-peregovori-shchodo-ukrayini-z-miliardnim-biznesom-the-new-yorker)).
Entities from the United Arab Emirates and Qatar followed, and in 2024 the
[Qatar Investment Authority](/organizations/qatar-investment-authority/) and an Abu Dhabi entity added another $1.5
billion, taking reported assets under management to about $4.8 billion by
year's end
([UA.NEWS](https://ua.news/en/world/kushner-poiednuie-peregovori-shchodo-ukrayini-z-miliardnim-biznesom-the-new-yorker)).

The fee economics drew sustained attention. The [Senate Finance Committee](/organizations/senate-finance-committee/)
examined the firm in 2024, and data from that inquiry — again via the
*New Yorker* summary — showed Affinity managing roughly $3 billion after
nearly three years while having deployed only about one-third of it, yet
already collecting about $157 million in fees
([UA.NEWS](https://ua.news/en/world/kushner-poiednuie-peregovori-shchodo-ukrayini-z-miliardnim-biznesom-the-new-yorker)).
EBSCO's biography, summarizing *New York Times* reporting from September
2024, gives the parallel snapshot for that date: nearly $3 billion raised,
about $112 million collected in fees, and no profits yet returned to
investors
([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner)).

Filkins's September 2026 reporting, as carried by TBS News, added that
Kushner raised a further $5 billion from Middle Eastern investors while
taking part in U.S. diplomatic negotiations with Iran, lifting Affinity's
total capital above $6 billion after roughly 30 percent growth in 2025; the
firm, the report said, collects tens of millions of dollars a year in
management fees though only part of the capital is deployed, and has used
Saudi-backed capital for Israeli investments including about $110 million in
the car-leasing company [Shlomo Group](/organizations/shlomo-group/) and roughly $300 million in Phoenix
Financial, presented as building an economic link between Saudi Arabia and
Israel
([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/worldbiz/middle-east/kushners-affinity-partners-raises-5b-amid-diplomatic-role-middle-east-1543866)).
Ethics experts quoted in the reporting argued that working on relations with
countries that are also major investors in his private business exposes him
to legal and ethical scrutiny; the Trump administration rejected suggestions
that his business interests improperly influence his diplomacy
([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/worldbiz/middle-east/kushners-affinity-partners-raises-5b-amid-diplomatic-role-middle-east-1543866)).
Kushner himself has defended the arrangement — "What people call conflicts
of interest, Steve and I call experience and trusted relationships that we
have throughout the world," he told CBS's *60 Minutes* — and UA.NEWS's
summary notes that the White House points to his formally unsalaried role as
an answer to the conflict claims, while ethics specialists say the absence of
a salary does not resolve the underlying issue
([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/worldbiz/middle-east/kushners-affinity-partners-raises-5b-amid-diplomatic-role-middle-east-1543866);
[UA.NEWS](https://ua.news/en/world/kushner-poiednuie-peregovori-shchodo-ukrayini-z-miliardnim-biznesom-the-new-yorker)).
The same summary stresses there is no direct evidence that the Saudi
investment rewarded any specific political decision
([UA.NEWS](https://ua.news/en/world/kushner-poiednuie-peregovori-shchodo-ukrayini-z-miliardnim-biznesom-the-new-yorker)).
In December 2025, Kushner withdrew from an effort to acquire Warner Bros.
Discovery and shelved plans for a luxury hotel in Belgrade
([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner)).

## Second term: Special Envoy for Peace

On February 19, 2026, Trump announced Kushner's appointment as Special Envoy
for Peace during the inaugural meeting of the Peace Council (the Board of
Peace) at the U.S. Institute of Peace in Washington, D.C., with Politico
reporting the move and Trump quoted as saying, "We're making Jared a peace
envoy"
([Mezha](https://mezha.net/eng/bukvy/jared-kushner-named-us-special-envoy-for-peace-initiatives/)).
The government's official transcript of that meeting identifies him as "U.S.
Special Envoy for Peace Jared C. Kushner" and records Trump thanking him and
Witkoff for their work concerning Gaza; the transcript's index also carries
the variant "U.S. Special Envoy for Peace Missions," the title Trump himself
used again aboard Air Force One on September 13, 2026
([U.S. Government Publishing Office](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/DCPD-202600120/pdf/DCPD-202600120.pdf);
[Akron Jewish News](https://www.akronjewishnews.com/news/nation_world/trump-hamas-willing-to-give-up-its-weapons/article_e50b2140-d18a-5632-8551-003ac0062bd1.html)).

Kushner had already been working Gaza in the background during 2025:
EBSCO's biography credits him with a behind-the-scenes role in Trump's Gaza
plan, which produced an exchange of Palestinian prisoners and Israeli
hostages, a ceasefire, and a partial Israeli withdrawal
([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner)).
In 2026 the diplomacy moved into the open. On August 16, 2026, Kushner met a
[Hamas](/organizations/hamas/) delegation in Cairo together with [Tony Blair](/people/tony-blair/) and [Nikolay Mladenov](/people/nikolay-mladenov/)
to discuss a [Board of Peace](/organizations/board-of-peace/) roadmap for Gaza — covering
ceasefire implementation, governance transfer, Hamas disarmament, Israeli
withdrawal, reconstruction and relief — with Axios describing the talks as
"very productive"; the same report noted bodies of deceased hostages being
returned and quoted Axios on Kushner pressing Hamas for concrete
disarmament steps, while also recording Trump's July Board of Peace agreement
and Netanyahu's rejection of it
([The Jerusalem Post](http://www.jpost.com/middle-east/article-905611)).

One account from that period remains openly disputed. At his September 6
press conference in Kyiv, Kushner said he had used the diplomatic fallout
from Israel's September 2025 strike in Doha as leverage to push Israel toward
a deal — "a terrible thing," in his words, that ultimately proved
unsuccessful; the Likud party dismissed his version as "nonsense," insisting
the agreement came from Israeli military pressure and Trump's own demands
rather than the Doha fallout
([Akron Jewish News](https://www.akronjewishnews.com/news/nation_world/trump-hamas-willing-to-give-up-its-weapons/article_e50b2140-d18a-5632-8551-003ac0062bd1.html)).
On September 13, aboard Air Force One, Trump gave his own verdict on the Gaza
file: "U.S. Special Envoy to the Middle East Steve Witkoff and U.S. Special
Envoy for Peace Missions Jared Kushner... Nobody [else] could have done the
job," adding, "We got the hostages back"
([Akron Jewish News](https://www.akronjewishnews.com/news/nation_world/trump-hamas-willing-to-give-up-its-weapons/article_e50b2140-d18a-5632-8551-003ac0062bd1.html)).

## The September 2026 Moscow–Kyiv shuttle

Kushner was brought into the Russia-Ukraine file in late 2025 alongside
Witkoff, and the *New Yorker* investigation's account — via UA.NEWS — says
both men started with limited knowledge of the war, viewing it through a
business-deal lens; one former U.S. official quoted in the piece said, "They
think this is a real estate deal. It seems they don't understand that this is
a war"
([UA.NEWS](https://ua.news/en/world/kushner-poiednuie-peregovori-shchodo-ukrayini-z-miliardnim-biznesom-the-new-yorker)).
That same summary reports that after Florida meetings with Ukrainian and
Russian representatives, the two envoys presented a draft peace plan calling
for significant Ukrainian concessions — including territorial ones, a
renunciation of NATO membership and a substantial reduction of Ukraine's
military — which drew heavy criticism once details emerged, though White
House press secretary Caroline Levitt had initially described it as a good
plan for both sides
([UA.NEWS](https://ua.news/en/world/kushner-poiednuie-peregovori-shchodo-ukrayini-z-miliardnim-biznesom-the-new-yorker)).

The most visible episode came on September 5–6, 2026. Kushner and Witkoff met
Vladimir Putin in Moscow for more than three hours behind closed doors on
September 5, then traveled by train from Poland to Kyiv on September 6 —
their first visit to the Ukrainian capital since Russia's February 2022
invasion, after eight previous trips to Moscow — for three rounds of talks
with [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](/people/volodymyr-zelenskyy/) that the AP described as "encouraging" and Al
Jazeera as "substantive," with no breakthrough announced
([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/9/6/us-envoys-hold-substantive-talks-with-zelenskyy-in-first-visit-to-kyiv);
[Associated Press](https://mb.com.ph/2026/09/07/us-envoys-witkoff-and-kushner-hold-talks-in-ukraine-after-meeting-with-putin)).
The national-security advisers of Britain, France and Germany joined one
session; the agenda covered territory, security guarantees, air defence
(including Patriots), a winter support package and reconstruction; and the
parties discussed a follow-up meeting, possible trilateral talks and a
durable-peace framework, with the envoys in near-daily contact with both
sides
([Associated Press](https://mb.com.ph/2026/09/07/us-envoys-witkoff-and-kushner-hold-talks-in-ukraine-after-meeting-with-putin)).
Zelenskyy took the envoys to Saint Sophia Cathedral in Kyiv; both sides
agreed not to strike each other's capitals for three days even as fighting
continued elsewhere
([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/9/6/us-envoys-hold-substantive-talks-with-zelenskyy-in-first-visit-to-kyiv)).

Kushner's own framing stayed in deal language. In Kyiv he called the
conflict "a very, very hard problem" and said the goal was a durable,
"win-win" settlement rather than merely stopping the shooting
([Associated Press](https://mb.com.ph/2026/09/07/us-envoys-witkoff-and-kushner-hold-talks-in-ukraine-after-meeting-with-putin);
[Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2026/9/6/us-envoys-hold-substantive-talks-with-zelenskyy-in-first-visit-to-kyiv)).
Addressing the YES forum by video on September 11, he named territory the
core obstacle — Putin had stated the line Moscow wanted, Ukraine refused to
withdraw to it, and the mediators were hunting for a "creative solution"
that could fit Ukraine's goals while still extracting an end to the war from
Russia; he said he hoped to announce next steps within weeks, prompting
reported laughter from the audience when he added that Putin was committed to
finding a way forward
([Censor.NET](https://censor.net/en/news/4023152/kushner-identified-the-most-difficult-issue-in-the-negotiations-between-ukraine-and-russia);
[Türkiye Today](https://www.turkiyetoday.com/world/us-may-unveil-next-phase-of-ukraine-russia-talks-within-weeks-kushner-3228050)).
He praised Ukraine's resilience, called the war economically "negative sum,"
insisted "These are terribly complex problems," and pledged the team would
"return when there is something to discuss" rather than practice show
diplomacy
([Türkiye Today](https://www.turkiyetoday.com/world/us-may-unveil-next-phase-of-ukraine-russia-talks-within-weeks-kushner-3228050);
[Censor.NET](https://censor.net/en/news/4023152/kushner-identified-the-most-difficult-issue-in-the-negotiations-between-ukraine-and-russia)).
Days later, actor and director [Sean Penn](/people/sean-penn/) used a *Vanity Fair* interview to
ask Kushner and Ivanka Trump to watch his documentary *Black Sunflowers*
about young Ukrainians — not, Penn said, to attack a president, but to create
human connection and reflection
([New Voice of Ukraine](https://english.nv.ua/life/sean-penn-wants-jared-kushner-and-ivanka-trump-to-watch-his-new-ukraine-documentary-50641243.html)).

The Iran file shadows the same period. Filkins's reporting, via TBS News,
says Kushner raised the extra $5 billion for Affinity while taking part in
the U.S. diplomatic negotiations with Iran; UA.NEWS's summary adds that the
February 2026 Geneva talks with Tehran — where Washington demanded Iran hand
over enriched-uranium stockpiles and halt enrichment — proceeded with no
technical adviser in the room with Kushner and Witkoff, a claim the White
House denied, and that hours after the two men briefed Trump on the outcome,
the U.S. and Israel launched strikes against Iran
([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/worldbiz/middle-east/kushners-affinity-partners-raises-5b-amid-diplomatic-role-middle-east-1543866);
[UA.NEWS](https://ua.news/en/world/kushner-poiednuie-peregovori-shchodo-ukrayini-z-miliardnim-biznesom-the-new-yorker)).
The TBS summary also notes that after regional conflict and dissatisfaction
among some Gulf investors over the lack of stability, Kushner took a
secondary role in parts of the peace diplomacy, with Vice President JD Vance
assuming a larger one
([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/worldbiz/middle-east/kushners-affinity-partners-raises-5b-amid-diplomatic-role-middle-east-1543866)).
