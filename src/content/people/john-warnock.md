## Overview

John Edward Warnock (1940–2023) was the American computer scientist who co-founded [Adobe](/organizations/adobe/) with [Charles Geschke](/people/charles-geschke/) in 1982 and invented the technologies underneath modern publishing: [PostScript](/creative-works/postscript/), the first full programming language used to control a printer, and the Portable Document Format (PDF), which became the standard way documents are exchanged electronically ([Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/profile/john-warnock/); [University of Utah](https://www.price.utah.edu/2023/08/21/remembering-john-warnock)). He led Adobe as president and then as chairman and CEO until 2000, remained co-chairman with Geschke until 2017, and died on August 19, 2023, in Los Altos, California, aged 82, after a nearly two-year battle with pancreatic cancer ([New York Times](https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/nytimes/name/john-warnock-obituary?id=53006048)).

Warnock was born on October 6, 1940, in Salt Lake City, Utah, and earned four degrees from the University of Utah: bachelor's degrees in mathematics and philosophy in 1961, a master's in mathematics in 1964, and a doctorate in electrical engineering in 1969 ([University of Utah](https://www.price.utah.edu/2023/08/21/remembering-john-warnock)). His doctoral thesis, on a hidden-surface algorithm for computer-generated halftone pictures, showed how three-dimensional images could be broken into visible polygons simple enough for the computers of the time to render — the "Warnock algorithm" behind the first realistic software-generated images ([University of Utah](https://www.price.utah.edu/2023/08/21/remembering-john-warnock)).

## From the Warnock algorithm to Interpress

After a stint at Evans & Sutherland, the computer-graphics firm founded by his adviser David Evans and Ivan Sutherland, Warnock joined [Xerox PARC](/organizations/xerox-parc/) in 1978 as a principal scientist working on interactive graphics ([Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/profile/john-warnock/)). There he re-implemented an interpretive graphics language he had developed at Evans & Sutherland — first called the Design System, then "JaM" for "John and Martin" (with Martin Newell) — and proposed it as the framework for a Xerox printer protocol ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/adobe-co-founder-john-warnock-on-the-competitive-advantages-of-aesthetics-and-the-right-technology/)). Over two years, five designers including Warnock and Geschke built that protocol, Interpress ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/adobe-co-founder-john-warnock-on-the-competitive-advantages-of-aesthetics-and-the-right-technology/)).

Xerox's answer killed the project inside the company: it loved Interpress, would make it a standard, and would lock it up until all of its printers could support it — a wait Warnock and Geschke judged would never end in their lifetimes ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/adobe-co-founder-john-warnock-on-the-competitive-advantages-of-aesthetics-and-the-right-technology/)). Warnock flew to consult his old thesis adviser Dave Evans, who introduced the pair to investment banker Bill Hambrecht, and on December 2, 1982, they founded Adobe ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/adobe-co-founder-john-warnock-on-the-competitive-advantages-of-aesthetics-and-the-right-technology/)). Their founding bet was the opposite of Xerox's: publish the language, make it open, and let application and operating-system developers adopt it, because without documentation adoption could never happen ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/adobe-co-founder-john-warnock-on-the-competitive-advantages-of-aesthetics-and-the-right-technology/)).

## PostScript, Illustrator, and the PDF that almost died

PostScript's two technical breakthroughs, in Warnock's own account, were using a full programming language to drive a printer — so programs like Apple's QuickDraw could be interpreted inside the printer itself — and solving the font problem at 300 dots per inch ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/adobe-co-founder-john-warnock-on-the-competitive-advantages-of-aesthetics-and-the-right-technology/)). His trick was to stretch characters so their strokes aligned with the printer's raster grid, then cache the result; the idea was so simple that Adobe kept it as a trade secret rather than patenting it, reasoning that a patent would teach everyone the workaround ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/adobe-co-founder-john-warnock-on-the-competitive-advantages-of-aesthetics-and-the-right-technology/)). Combined in 1985 with [Apple](/organizations/apple/) hardware, PostScript formed the first desktop-publishing system, letting nearly anyone compose documents and print them as they appeared on screen ([Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/profile/john-warnock/)).

At PostScript's peak, Warnock pushed diversification precisely to avoid becoming a one-product company, starting the [Illustrator](/creative-works/illustrator/) project; Illustrator was introduced in 1987 ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/adobe-co-founder-john-warnock-on-the-competitive-advantages-of-aesthetics-and-the-right-technology/)). [Photoshop](/creative-works/photoshop/) was licensed from brothers [Thomas Knoll](/people/thomas-knoll/) and [John Knoll](/people/john-knoll/) — Geschke's account dates the licensing to 1988, Warnock's recollection to the Knolls' approach in 1989, a discrepancy flagged in the open items — and Adobe later bought Photoshop outright when the Knolls thought it would "run out of gas" ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/adobe-co-founder-john-warnock-on-the-competitive-advantages-of-aesthetics-and-the-right-technology/)). The PDF grew from a related insight: because every PostScript operator could be redefined, a PostScript program could be flattened into a static file of delimited pages — the "Distiller" idea Warnock had first used to cut a [Steve Jobs](/people/steve-jobs/) tax-form demo's print time from two minutes to twenty seconds ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/adobe-co-founder-john-warnock-on-the-competitive-advantages-of-aesthetics-and-the-right-technology/)).

[Acrobat](/creative-works/acrobat/) did not take off at first. The world did not understand electronic document exchange, one analyst called it "the dumbest idea I've ever heard," and Adobe's own board sought to kill the product — but Warnock persisted, and once the Reader was free, Acrobat became one of Adobe's most profitable products ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/adobe-co-founder-john-warnock-on-the-competitive-advantages-of-aesthetics-and-the-right-technology/)). His aesthetic streak — a love of typography visible in the stone alphabet carving in his entryway — also blinded Adobe at times: he admitted the company sat out the early web partly because, from a design point of view, early HTML was "awful" ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/adobe-co-founder-john-warnock-on-the-competitive-advantages-of-aesthetics-and-the-right-technology/)). He retired as CEO in 2000 and handed the company to [Bruce Chizen](/people/bruce-chizen/), who had joined Adobe in 1994 ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1570046/update-chizen-out-at-month-s-end-as-adobe-ceo.html)).

Beyond Adobe, Warnock collected rare books from 1986, starting with a 1570 edition of Euclid's Elements, founded Octavo in 1995 to sell high-resolution scans of great books, and later published them freely at rarebookroom.org, which holds more than 400 volumes ([University of Utah](https://humanities.utah.edu/news/remembering-john-warnock.php)). With his wife Marva, he gave the naming gift for the John E. and Marva M. Warnock Engineering Building at the University of Utah ([University of Utah](https://www.price.utah.edu/2023/08/21/remembering-john-warnock)). His awards included the National Medal of Technology and Innovation from President Barack Obama, the Marconi Prize in 2010, and the IEEE Computer Entrepreneur Award ([University of Utah](https://www.price.utah.edu/2023/08/21/remembering-john-warnock)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not settle the 1988-versus-1989 Photoshop licensing year or the 2000-versus-2001 CEO retirement year; both are flagged above. It does not claim Warnock invented PostScript or PDF alone — both grew from Interpress and from colleagues' work, including Geschke's, and the profile credits that lineage. It makes no claim about Warnock's private life beyond what the University of Utah and obituary sources document, and it does not assert that Octavo was commercially successful; Warnock himself said it was not, which is why the scans were later given away free.
