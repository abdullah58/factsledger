## Overview

Hiroshi Ichikura (一倉 宏, born 1955 in Gunma Prefecture) is a Japanese advertising
copywriter and creative director — and, in a second career that English-language sources
almost never mention, a lyricist whose words have been sung by pop stars and schoolchildren
alike ([TCC Hall of Fame](https://www.tcc.gr.jp/hall-of-fame/)). The Tokyo Copywriters
Club's Hall of Fame profile, which inducted him in 2023, lists among his principal works
the lyrics to Kazuyoshi Saito's 「ウエディング・ソング」 ("Wedding Song") and to the NHK
*Minna no Uta* song 「うちゅうひこうしのうた」 ("Uchuu Hikoushi no Uta", music by Yoko
Kanno) ([TCC Hall of Fame](https://www.tcc.gr.jp/hall-of-fame/)). In advertising, his
career runs from [Suntory](/organizations/suntory/)'s in-house production room in 1978, through Nakahata Advertising
Production, to the independent Ichikura Advertising Production he founded in 1990 — five
decades of campaigns for Sony, TOTO, ANA, FamilyMart, Recruit and many others
([TCC Hall of Fame](https://www.tcc.gr.jp/hall-of-fame/),
[64th Sendenkaigi Award](https://awardg.sendenkaigi.com/senden)).

## From Suntory's production room to independence

Ichikura graduated from the University of Tsukuba in 1978 as a member of its very first
graduating class, and joined Suntory's advertising department production room as a
copywriter ([TCC Hall of Fame](https://www.tcc.gr.jp/hall-of-fame/)). His early work there
included newspaper advertising for Toris whisky and the launches of new whiskies 21 and Q
and the beer brand Malts ([TCC Hall of Fame](https://www.tcc.gr.jp/hall-of-fame/)). In
1987 he moved to Nakahata Advertising Production, where for the first time he handled
clients other than Suntory — Sony and TOTO ([TCC Hall of Fame](https://www.tcc.gr.jp/hall-of-fame/)).
He went independent in 1990, founding Ichikura Advertising Production
([TCC Hall of Fame](https://www.tcc.gr.jp/hall-of-fame/)).

The official biography on the 64th Sendenkaigi Award site adds a telling detail: Suntory
hired him in an era when it recruited copywriters as specialist technical staff
(専門の技術職) ([64th Sendenkaigi Award](https://awardg.sendenkaigi.com/senden)). That
same biography distills his career into six signature lines: 「うまいんだな、これがっ」
for Malts, 「きれいなおねえさんは、好きですか。」 for Matsushita Electric Works,
「あなたと、コンビに、」 for FamilyMart, 「いい空は青い。」 for ANA,
「まだ、ここにない、出会い。」 for Recruit, and 「ポカリ、のまなきゃ。」 for Otsuka
Pharmaceutical ([64th Sendenkaigi Award](https://awardg.sendenkaigi.com/senden)). The TCC
Hall of Fame page illustrates his output with representative works including NTT Data
newspaper advertising from 1990 and an ANA newspaper campaign from 2002
([TCC Hall of Fame](https://www.tcc.gr.jp/hall-of-fame/)).

## The lyricist's second career

What sets Ichikura apart from his copywriting peers — and what no English-language source
appears to cover — is his parallel life as a lyricist. The TCC Hall of Fame entry gives
pride of place, alongside his books, to two lyric works: 「ウエディング・ソング」
("Wedding Song") for singer-songwriter Kazuyoshi Saito, and 「うちゅうひこうしのうた」
("Uchuu Hikoushi no Uta", "The Astronaut's Song") for NHK's long-running *Minna no Uta*
("Songs for Everyone") program, with music by Yoko Kanno
([TCC Hall of Fame](https://www.tcc.gr.jp/hall-of-fame/)). That a copywriter's words
could travel from newspaper ads into pop songs and a national children's song program is
the least-documented facet of his career, and the natural starting point for any profile
that wants to say something new about him.

## Books, honors, and his own canon

Ichikura is also the author of two books on words and copywriting:
『人生を３つの単語で表すとしたら』 ("If You Had to Describe Life in Three Words",
Kodansha) and 『ことばになりたい』 ("I Want to Become Words", Mainichi Shimbun-sha)
([TCC Hall of Fame](https://www.tcc.gr.jp/hall-of-fame/)). The TCC profile notes the TCC
Grand Prix among his many advertising awards
([TCC Hall of Fame](https://www.tcc.gr.jp/hall-of-fame/)), and the LoveTheWork awards
directory credits him with 2 Gold, 4 Silver and 2 Bronze wins
([LoveTheWork](https://www.lovethework.com/en/directory/individuals/hiroshi-ichikura-4830)).

In 2023 he was inducted into the TCC Hall of Fame, the Tokyo Copywriters Club's hall of
honor for copywriters of outstanding achievement
([TCC Hall of Fame](https://www.tcc.gr.jp/hall-of-fame/)). Following his induction he took
part in a public recording for the advertising press's "copy 100TEN" series, personally
explaining his ten best-known lines; the program's own page ranks entries #10 through #4
as 「家に帰れば、積水ハウス」 (Sekisui House, 2010), 「いえなかった言葉たちは、どうして
いますか。」 (NTT, 1998), 「憲法第22条の歌 職業選択の自由 ahahan」 (Gakusei Engokai
Sarida, 1990), 「まだ、ここにない、出会い。」 (Recruit, 2010),
「音が、進化した。人は、どうですか。」 (Sony Walkman, 1987), 「あなたと、コンビニ、」
(FamilyMart, 1989), and 「愛に雪、恋を白。」 (JR East "JR Ski Ski", 1999)
([100TEN program](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rLAtJBRxOGQ)).

In 2026 he was named chief judge (審査員長) of the 64th Sendenkaigi Award
([64th Sendenkaigi Award](https://awardg.sendenkaigi.com/senden)). The organizer describes
the award as Japan's largest public advertising competition, with the 64th edition
accepting entries from 2026-07-01 through 2026-09-30
([Sendenkaigi announcement](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000828.000002888.html)).

## Ties to the University of Tsukuba

Ichikura's alma mater threads through his story: he graduated from the University of
Tsukuba in 1978 as part of its first graduating class
([TCC Hall of Fame](https://www.tcc.gr.jp/hall-of-fame/)). The university has its own
message song, "IMAGINE THE FUTURE～未来を想え"; its composer, fellow Tsukuba alumnus
Yoichiro Yoshikawa, died on 2026-01-16, as the university announced on 2026-01-26
([University of Tsukuba](https://www.tsukuba.ac.jp/news/20260126131406.html)). An
alumna's message on the university's 50th-anniversary site describes "IMAGINE THE FUTURE"
as the slogan that symbolizes the institution
([University of Tsukuba](https://www.tsukuba.ac.jp/50th-anniv/message-from-alumni.html)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim that Ichikura devised the University of Tsukuba's "IMAGINE
THE FUTURE." branding slogan or wrote the lyrics of its message song. Japanese Wikipedia
credits him with both, citing two official University of Tsukuba pages, but those pages
could not be retrieved during this research, so the attribution is unverified here and is
recorded as an open item above rather than stated as fact.

It does not give his birth day or month: the sources read in full give only the year
1955. It does not repeat the further lyric-work claims found only in secondary summaries
(commercial-song associations or works for other artists), which were not read in full.
It does not rely on the AdverTimes 100TEN article or the 2020 Yomiuri interview, whose
article-specific URLs could not be located; the 100TEN facts used here come from the
program's own video page.
