## Overview

**Thavarchand Gehlot** (born 18 May 1948; also spelled Thaawarchand or Thawar Chand Gehlot) is an Indian politician whose career runs from trade-union activism in [Madhya Pradesh](/places/madhya-pradesh/) to the Union cabinet and then to Raj Bhavan: four terms in the Lok Sabha, Union Minister of Social Justice and Empowerment from 2014 to July 2021, Leader of the House in the Rajya Sabha, and 19th Governor of Karnataka from July 2021 ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/thaawarchand-gehlot-takes-oath-as-19th-governor-of-karnataka-2483988); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/politics/thawar-chand-gehlot-114052601134_1.html)). He was the BJP's most recognisable Scheduled Caste face in the Modi governments, and his governorship became one of the most friction-filled in recent Karnataka history ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/politics/thawar-chand-gehlot-114052601134_1.html)).

In September 2020, as Leader of the House, Gehlot seconded the nomination that returned Harivansh Narayan Singh as Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha — the parliamentary moment that ties him to this site's [2020 Deputy Chairman election](/events/2020-deputy-chairman-rajya-sabha-election/) profile ([Zee News](https://zeenews.india.com/india/harivansh-narayan-singh-re-elected-as-rajya-sabha-deputy-chairman-2309634.html)). As Governor, he twice collided with the Congress government of Chief Minister Siddaramaiah: sanctioning the Chief Minister's prosecution in the MUDA land case in 2024, and walking out of the legislature in January 2026 after delivering a two-line address ([Times Now](https://www.timesnownews.com/india/in-major-setback-to-siddaramaiah-karnataka-hc-upholds-governors-sanction-to-prosecute-cm-in-land-case-article-113623759); [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/karnataka/karnataka-joint-legislature-session-high-drama-after-governor-gehlot-two-lines-address-siddaramaiah-says-unconstitutional/article70536700.ece)). In October 2026, President Droupadi Murmu appointed Sudha Yadav to succeed him ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/sudha-yadav-appointed-new-governor-of-karnataka-4180262)).

## From Rupeta to Parliament

Gehlot was born on 18 May 1948 in Rupeta village, Nagda tehsil, Ujjain district, Madhya Pradesh, to Ramlal Gehlot and Suman Bai, and took his BA from Vikram University in Ujjain ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/thaawarchand-gehlot-takes-oath-as-19th-governor-of-karnataka-2483988)). He entered politics in 1962 through the Jan Sangh, having been associated with the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS), and was jailed several times between 1968 and 1971 for raising workers' issues, and again during the Emergency in the 1970s ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/thaawarchand-gehlot-takes-oath-as-19th-governor-of-karnataka-2483988)).

His electoral career began in 1980, and he became a three-term MLA before reaching Parliament ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/thaawarchand-gehlot-takes-oath-as-19th-governor-of-karnataka-2483988); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/politics/thawar-chand-gehlot-114052601134_1.html)). He was first elected to the Lok Sabha from Shajapur in 1996, and won again in 1998, 1999, and 2004, serving four consecutive terms until 2009 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/politics/thawar-chand-gehlot-114052601134_1.html); [NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/thaawarchand-gehlot-takes-oath-as-19th-governor-of-karnataka-2483988)). Within the BJP organisation he was a general secretary, a member of the parliamentary board, and in charge of the party's Scheduled Caste Morcha ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/politics/thawar-chand-gehlot-114052601134_1.html)). He also served as the party's Karnataka state in-charge for years between 2006 and 2014, while general secretary — an assignment that made him familiar with the state he would later govern ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/thaawarchand-gehlot-takes-oath-as-19th-governor-of-karnataka-2483988)). He later served in the Rajya Sabha, where he rose to Leader of the House ([Zee News](https://zeenews.india.com/india/harivansh-narayan-singh-re-elected-as-rajya-sabha-deputy-chairman-2309634.html)).

## Minister for Social Justice and Empowerment

In 2014, Gehlot was appointed Union Minister of Social Justice and Empowerment — his first stint as a minister, after decades as an organiser and parliamentarian ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/politics/thawar-chand-gehlot-114052601134_1.html)). He held the portfolio through the Modi government's first term and into its second, until July 2021 ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/thaawarchand-gehlot-takes-oath-as-19th-governor-of-karnataka-2483988)). The ministry's most visible accessibility work came under the Accessible India Campaign, built on three pillars: the built environment, the transportation sector, and the information and communication technology ecosystem ([Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1701915&ui=2&tf=1&pli=1&reg=3&lang=2)).

Near the end of his tenure, on 2 March 2021, Gehlot launched the Sugamya Bharat App, a crowdsourcing mobile application for reporting accessibility problems across those three pillars, alongside a handbook of accessibility good and bad practice ([Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1701915&ui=2&tf=1&pli=1&reg=3&lang=2)). The app let registered users raise complaints with geo-tagged photographs, receive status updates through resolution, and use features including font-size adjustment, colour contrast, text-to-speech, and an integrated screen reader in Hindi and English ([Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1701915&ui=2&tf=1&pli=1&reg=3&lang=2)). The app was framed as a step toward accessibility as mandated by Sections 40–46 of the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 ([Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1701915&ui=2&tf=1&pli=1&reg=3&lang=2)). His ministry years also made him the BJP's most prominent Scheduled Caste face at the Centre — a role grounded in his own Dalit background and his long trade-union and Morcha work ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/politics/thawar-chand-gehlot-114052601134_1.html)).

## Governor of Karnataka: friction and succession

Gehlot was appointed Governor of Karnataka on 6 July 2021 and sworn in on 11 July as the state's 19th Governor, succeeding Vajubhai Vala; the oath was administered by Karnataka High Court Chief Justice Abhay Shreeniwas Oka ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/thaawarchand-gehlot-takes-oath-as-19th-governor-of-karnataka-2483988)). The governorship's defining episode came in August 2024, when he granted sanction for the prosecution of Chief Minister Siddaramaiah in connection with alleged irregularities in land allocation by the Mysuru Urban Development Authority (MUDA) ([Times Now](https://www.timesnownews.com/india/in-major-setback-to-siddaramaiah-karnataka-hc-upholds-governors-sanction-to-prosecute-cm-in-land-case-article-113623759)). Activists had alleged that 14 premium sites in Mysuru were allocated to Siddaramaiah's wife, Parvathi, as compensation for acquired land, causing a claimed Rs 45 crore loss to the exchequer — allegations, not findings ([Times Now](https://www.timesnownews.com/india/in-major-setback-to-siddaramaiah-karnataka-hc-upholds-governors-sanction-to-prosecute-cm-in-land-case-article-113623759)).

Siddaramaiah challenged the sanction, arguing the Governor could not proceed without the state Cabinet's approval. On 24 September 2024 the Karnataka High Court dismissed the petition, holding that the Governor could take an "independent decision" in exceptional circumstances and need not fall back on ministers' advice, and finding that Gehlot had "applied his mind in abundance" ([Times Now](https://www.timesnownews.com/india/in-major-setback-to-siddaramaiah-karnataka-hc-upholds-governors-sanction-to-prosecute-cm-in-land-case-article-113623759)).

The second collision came on 22 January 2026, on the first day of the joint legislature session. The government's address ran to 122 paragraphs across 43 pages, but Gehlot objected to 10 paragraphs accusing the Centre of "injustice" to Karnataka in tax devolution and centrally sponsored schemes ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/karnataka/karnataka-joint-legislature-session-high-drama-after-governor-gehlot-two-lines-address-siddaramaiah-says-unconstitutional/article70536700.ece)). That 122-paragraph address — with the confrontation turning on just 10 of its paragraphs — shows how a small slice of a ceremonial text can become a constitutional flashpoint. The speech also sought repeal of the Viksit Bharat Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission–Gramin Act and restoration of the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/karnataka/karnataka-joint-legislature-session-high-drama-after-governor-gehlot-two-lines-address-siddaramaiah-says-unconstitutional/article70536700.ece)). Gehlot delivered a two-line address of his own, left without waiting for the national anthem, and was escorted out by marshals amid pandemonium as Congress members raised slogans and attempted to gherao him ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/karnataka/karnataka-joint-legislature-session-high-drama-after-governor-gehlot-two-lines-address-siddaramaiah-says-unconstitutional/article70536700.ece)). Siddaramaiah called the act unconstitutional, accused Gehlot of functioning as a "puppet of the Central government," and said the government was examining approaching the Supreme Court ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/karnataka/karnataka-joint-legislature-session-high-drama-after-governor-gehlot-two-lines-address-siddaramaiah-says-unconstitutional/article70536700.ece)). The walkout followed similar confrontations days earlier in Tamil Nadu and Kerala, and echoed a 2011 Karnataka precedent when Governor H. R. Bhardwaj walked out during BJP rule ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/karnataka/karnataka-joint-legislature-session-high-drama-after-governor-gehlot-two-lines-address-siddaramaiah-says-unconstitutional/article70536700.ece)).

The term ended with a routine succession rather than a further confrontation. On 12 October 2026, President Droupadi Murmu appointed former MP Sudha Yadav to succeed Gehlot as Governor of Karnataka, effective from the date she assumes charge ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/sudha-yadav-appointed-new-governor-of-karnataka-4180262)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is Thavarchand Gehlot?**
Thavarchand Gehlot is an Indian politician who served as Union Minister of Social Justice and Empowerment from 2014 to 2021 and as the 19th Governor of Karnataka from July 2021. A BJP organiser from Madhya Pradesh, he spent four terms in the Lok Sabha from Shajapur and later became Leader of the House in the Rajya Sabha.

**Why is Thavarchand Gehlot known as the BJP's Dalit face?**
Gehlot, who was born into a Dalit family in Madhya Pradesh, headed the BJP's Scheduled Caste Morcha and served as the party's most prominent Scheduled Caste minister at the Centre as Social Justice Minister from 2014 to 2021. His background in trade-union work reinforced that role.

**What was the MUDA sanction by Governor Gehlot?**
On 17 August 2024, Gehlot granted sanction to prosecute Chief Minister Siddaramaiah over alleged irregularities in Mysuru Urban Development Authority land allocations to the Chief Minister's wife. The Karnataka High Court upheld the sanction in September 2024, holding the Governor could take an independent decision in exceptional circumstances.

**Why did Governor Gehlot walk out of the Karnataka Assembly in January 2026?**
On 22 January 2026, Gehlot objected to 10 paragraphs of the government's 122-paragraph address that accused the Centre of injustice to Karnataka and sought repeal of the VB-G RAM G Act. He delivered a two-line address of his own and left, prompting the Chief Minister to call the act unconstitutional.

**Who succeeded Thavarchand Gehlot as Karnataka Governor?**
On 12 October 2026, President Droupadi Murmu appointed former MP Sudha Yadav to succeed Gehlot as Governor of Karnataka. The appointment takes effect from the date Yadav assumes charge of the office. Yadav previously represented Mahendragarh in the Lok Sabha from 1999 to 2004.
