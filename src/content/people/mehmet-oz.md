## Before he was "America's doctor," he was a vice-chair of surgery

Long before Mehmet Oz became a daytime-TV fixture, his résumé was the kind surgeons frame. His institutional biography lists a Harvard undergraduate degree in 1982 and a joint M.D. from the University of Pennsylvania School of Medicine and MBA from the Wharton School in 1986 ([World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/people/mehmet-c-oz/)). At New York Presbyterian and Columbia University he rose to Vice-Chair of Surgery and Professor of Cardiac Surgery, founded the Complementary Medicine Program, and directed the Cardiovascular Institute ([World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/people/mehmet-c-oz/)). The record behind those titles is substantial: more than 400 publications, six patents, six New York Times bestselling books, and research interests spanning minimally invasive cardiac surgery and healthcare policy ([World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/people/mehmet-c-oz/)).

That academic spine is the part of Oz's story his television fame mostly drowned out. He was a cardiothoracic surgeon first — a fact that matters for the job he holds now, running the federal agency that pays for much of the country's heart surgery ([Akron Beacon Journal (USA Today network)](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/politics/2025/04/03/senate-mehmet-oz-medicare-medicaid/82791406007/)).

## The television doctor

Oz's public career began in the 1990s as a health expert on *The Oprah Winfrey Show*, where he made dozens of appearances and was eventually dubbed "America's doctor" by Winfrey herself ([Akron Beacon Journal (USA Today network)](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/politics/2025/04/03/senate-mehmet-oz-medicare-medicaid/82791406007/)). In 2009 he launched *The Dr. Oz Show* with Winfrey's Harpo Productions and Sony Pictures Television; it ran for 13 seasons and won multiple Daytime Emmy Awards, and Oz earned a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame ([Medical Economics](https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/trump-nominee-dr-oz-to-take-helm-of-cms-prompting-congratulations-and-criticism)).

The show also made him a magnet for criticism from within his own profession. In 2014 he testified before the U.S. Senate after being accused of false advertising for weight-loss supplements he had promoted on air ([NPR](https://www.wuot.org/2024-11-19/trump-picks-dr-oz-to-lead-medicare-and-medicaid)). The following year, ten doctors wrote to Columbia University's medical school urging it to dismiss him, arguing that much of his show's advice was unsupported by scientific evidence and sometimes contradicted it ([NPR](https://www.wuot.org/2024-11-19/trump-picks-dr-oz-to-lead-medicare-and-medicaid)). The Beacon Journal's account of his career notes his promotion of malaria drugs for COVID-19 as "a theory that was debunked," and his marketing of treatments "not backed by experts," such as combining pregnancy hormones with a 500-calorie diet for weight loss ([Akron Beacon Journal (USA Today network)](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/politics/2025/04/03/senate-mehmet-oz-medicare-medicaid/82791406007/))([NPR](https://www.wuot.org/2024-11-19/trump-picks-dr-oz-to-lead-medicare-and-medicaid)).

## The Senate run and the CMS nomination

In 2022 Oz ran as the Republican nominee for the U.S. Senate in Pennsylvania, endorsed by Donald Trump in the primary; he lost to Democrat John Fetterman ([NPR](https://www.wuot.org/2024-11-19/trump-picks-dr-oz-to-lead-medicare-and-medicaid))([Akron Beacon Journal (USA Today network)](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/politics/2025/04/03/senate-mehmet-oz-medicare-medicaid/82791406007/)). On the campaign trail he argued the government had "patronized and misled" the public during the COVID pandemic ([NPR](https://www.wuot.org/2024-11-19/trump-picks-dr-oz-to-lead-medicare-and-medicaid)).

On November 19, 2024, President-elect Trump announced on Truth Social and in a press release that he would nominate Oz — then 64 — to head the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services, saying "Dr. Oz will work closely with Robert F. Kennedy Jr. to take on the illness industrial complex, and all the horrible chronic diseases left in its wake" ([NPR](https://www.wuot.org/2024-11-19/trump-picks-dr-oz-to-lead-medicare-and-medicaid)). CMS sits inside the Department of Health and Human Services and covers Medicare, Medicaid, the Children's Health Insurance Program, and the Affordable Care Act marketplaces — health coverage for more than 160 million Americans, run by roughly 6,500 employees spending about $1.5 trillion a year ([Akron Beacon Journal (USA Today network)](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/politics/2025/04/03/senate-mehmet-oz-medicare-medicaid/82791406007/))([NPR](https://www.wuot.org/2024-11-19/trump-picks-dr-oz-to-lead-medicare-and-medicaid)).

The nomination divided opinion along lines his career had already drawn. At his confirmation hearing he vowed to give patients more say in their care and crack down on fraud, waste, and abuse in federal health programs, pledging: "I commit to doing whatever I can, working tirelessly, to ensure that CMS provides Americans with access to superb care, especially Americans who are our most vulnerable — our young, our disabled and our elderly" ([Akron Beacon Journal (USA Today network)](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/politics/2025/04/03/senate-mehmet-oz-medicare-medicaid/82791406007/)). Supporters in Congress welcomed him: Rep. Brett Guthrie and Rep. Buddy Carter called him "an accomplished physician, an innovator, and a world-class communicator, who will help modernize the agency" ([Medical Economics](https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/trump-nominee-dr-oz-to-take-helm-of-cms-prompting-congratulations-and-criticism)). Critics answered in kind. Sen. Ron Wyden called him "yet another slick salesman peddling unproven cures and treatments" ([Medical Economics](https://www.medicaleconomics.com/view/trump-nominee-dr-oz-to-take-helm-of-cms-prompting-congratulations-and-criticism)), the watchdog [Accountable.US](/organizations/accountable-us/) warned about his support for privatizing Medicare through Medicare Advantage plans ([NPR](https://www.wuot.org/2024-11-19/trump-picks-dr-oz-to-lead-medicare-and-medicaid)), and Georgetown health-law scholar Lawrence Gostin called him "unfit" to run CMS ([NPR](https://www.wuot.org/2024-11-19/trump-picks-dr-oz-to-lead-medicare-and-medicaid)).

On April 3, 2025, the Senate confirmed Oz 53-45, strictly along party lines, succeeding Chiquita Brooks-LaSure ([Akron Beacon Journal (USA Today network)](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/politics/2025/04/03/senate-mehmet-oz-medicare-medicaid/82791406007/)).

## America.gov, on stage

Oz's most visible public act as administrator came a day before this profile was written. On September 29, 2026, the Trump administration launched America.gov, an AI-powered federal portal meant to be a single front door to government services ([WUTV (TNND/Fox affiliate network)](https://wutv29.com/news/nation-world/america-gov-launches-as-president-donald-trumps-new-ai-shortcut-to-federal-services-government-agencies-chief-design-officer-joe-gebbia)). Onstage at the launch event, Oz demonstrated how Americans could use the site to sign up for Medicare ([WUTV (TNND/Fox affiliate network)](https://wutv29.com/news/nation-world/america-gov-launches-as-president-donald-trumps-new-ai-shortcut-to-federal-services-government-agencies-chief-design-officer-joe-gebbia)). He brought a woman named Lisa onstage, who revealed her newly issued Medicare beneficiary card after spending more than a year trying to enroll ([WUTV (TNND/Fox affiliate network)](https://wutv29.com/news/nation-world/america-gov-launches-as-president-donald-trumps-new-ai-shortcut-to-federal-services-government-agencies-chief-design-officer-joe-gebbia)). America.gov "explains what applies to Lisa's situation and guides her through the decisions," Oz said — the TV doctor's stagecraft, now deployed for a government enrollment drive ([WUTV (TNND/Fox affiliate network)](https://wutv29.com/news/nation-world/america-gov-launches-as-president-donald-trumps-new-ai-shortcut-to-federal-services-government-agencies-chief-design-officer-joe-gebbia)). At the same event, Marco Rubio demonstrated online passport applications, and Trump signed an executive order directing agencies to integrate with the portal ([WUTV (TNND/Fox affiliate network)](https://wutv29.com/news/nation-world/america-gov-launches-as-president-donald-trumps-new-ai-shortcut-to-federal-services-government-agencies-chief-design-officer-joe-gebbia)).
