## Overview

Zeb Evans is the founder and chief executive officer of [ClickUp](/organizations/clickup/), the San Diego productivity-software company behind the all-in-one workplace platform of the same name ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2224795)). He founded the company in 2017 with [Alex Yurkowski](/people/alex-yurkowski/), bootstrapped it to profitability before taking outside capital, and raised it to a $4 billion valuation on a $400 million Series C in October 2021 ([Foundr](https://foundr.com/articles/leadership/personal-growth/zeb-evans); [Contrary Research](https://research2.contrary.com/company/clickup)). In May 2026 he cut 22% of ClickUp's workforce and rebuilt the company around some 3,000 internal AI agents, a move he calls building a "100x org" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/25/what-clickups-mass-layoff-tells-us-about-the-future-of-work/)).

Evans's public story is inseparable from three near-death experiences — a childhood accident, a gunpoint robbery in college, and a seizure years later — which he credits with his fixation on time as a finite resource and with ClickUp's mission, in his words, to save people time ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/202210/paul-kix/clickup-zeb-evans-dying-to-succeed-2022.html); [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/zeb-evans-founder-ceo-of-clickup-interview-series/)). He also serves as ClickUp's de facto Chief Product Officer, overseeing its product release schedule ([ClickUp](https://clickup.com/about/zeb-evans)).

## Early life and three near-death experiences

Evans was entrepreneurial from childhood. His first "business" was selling candy out of his lunch box in elementary school — for which he was suspended — followed by selling toys he asked for as wholesale novelty gifts ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/zeb-evans-founder-ceo-of-clickup-interview-series/)). At 10, a jet-ski accident in North Carolina sliced open his obliques; in hospital, doctors discovered a ruptured appendix draining into his body, and he spent about two months recovering with tubes in his midsection ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/202210/paul-kix/clickup-zeb-evans-dying-to-succeed-2022.html)). Bedridden, he used the hospital's one public laptop to discover Alibaba and began buying and selling Disney Vault DVDs — an episode he calls the moment he married his love of entrepreneurship with technology ([Foundr](https://foundr.com/articles/leadership/personal-growth/zeb-evans)). Before leaving hospital he wrote in a journal that he wanted "a successful, big company that's as big as McDonald's," a note his father Chuck saw ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/202210/paul-kix/clickup-zeb-evans-dying-to-succeed-2022.html)).

He enrolled at Virginia Tech in 2008, running a music production and artist-management business, and was a sophomore when he was robbed at gunpoint during a home invasion ([Contrary Research](https://research2.contrary.com/company/clickup)). "I had that realization when I had the gun pointed to my head — that this isn't what I want to do with my life," he told Foundr, adding that he did not want to work for someone else and that "you learn business by doing" ([Foundr](https://foundr.com/articles/leadership/personal-growth/zeb-evans)). The next day he dropped out of school to work on his business full time. Inc. described him as 33 years old in October 2022, when ClickUp employed nearly 1,000 people ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/202210/paul-kix/clickup-zeb-evans-dying-to-succeed-2022.html)).

## Fast Followerz and Mimri

Evans's first real company was Fast Followerz, a social media marketing firm built from the custom social tools he had made for his entertainment business. He started it with $100 and himself, and within four years it had millions in yearly revenue and around 25 employees; by 2015 it had 25 employees, over 1,000 clients and $2.5 million in annual revenue, and was one of the largest firms of its type in the [United States](/places/united-states/) ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/zeb-evans-founder-ceo-of-clickup-interview-series/); [Contrary Research](https://research2.contrary.com/company/clickup)). Four years after founding it, Evans had a seizure in a movie theater while watching "The Martian" and spent about a week fearing he had a brain tumor, which his father had suffered; tests showed an anomaly, not a tumor ([Foundr](https://foundr.com/articles/leadership/personal-growth/zeb-evans)). The scare reframed the business for him: social media was "more about ego than community," and Fast Followerz was fueling that ego. He left the firm and drove across the country to Palo Alto to build something with a larger impact ([Foundr](https://foundr.com/articles/leadership/personal-growth/zeb-evans); [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/zeb-evans-founder-ceo-of-clickup-interview-series/)).

His first Silicon Valley startup was Mimri, a first-person video social network and his first from-scratch tech startup. It folded within a year — but Evans credits it with the lessons that made ClickUp possible: "If I hadn't done that one, I don't think ClickUp would really exist today in the same way," he told Foundr ([Foundr](https://foundr.com/articles/leadership/personal-growth/zeb-evans)).

## Founding ClickUp

Evans's next idea was a safer Craigslist competitor, but before building it he challenged his team to spend a month building their own productivity tool. The frustration was personal: across his companies he had been juggling 14 or 15 different productivity tools at once, and the stack was supposed to make the team more productive while doing the opposite ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/zeb-evans-founder-ceo-of-clickup-interview-series/); [Foundr](https://foundr.com/articles/leadership/personal-growth/zeb-evans)). Within a month the team was obsessed with the tool instead of the marketplace app, and it became the first version of ClickUp ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/zeb-evans-founder-ceo-of-clickup-interview-series/)). In 2017 Evans stopped battling Craigslist and started building the all-in-one productivity solution, setting up in a two-story Silicon Valley house where the small team worked on the first floor and slept on the second ([Foundr](https://foundr.com/articles/leadership/personal-growth/zeb-evans)). The first version was built in six months using $2.5 million from the sale of Evans's previous business, and growth came through an organic SEO strategy targeting searches for Trello, [Asana](/organizations/asana/) and productivity software ([Foundr](https://foundr.com/articles/leadership/personal-growth/zeb-evans)).

ClickUp grew with no marketing budget for three years, mainly by word of mouth, reaching about 100,000 customers by the spring of 2020 — and it was profitable before Evans approached investors for the first time, which he says shocked them ([Contrary Research](https://research2.contrary.com/company/clickup); [Foundr](https://foundr.com/articles/leadership/personal-growth/zeb-evans)). In 2020 it was named the best project management product by the Proddys ([Foundr](https://foundr.com/articles/leadership/personal-growth/zeb-evans)). The founding year matters beyond biography: this site's own ClickUp profile had carried it as an unverified open item, and it is now confirmed by two independent tier2 sources plus the company itself, which says Evans "has led ClickUp's growth from the company's inception in 2017" ([ClickUp](https://clickup.com/about/zeb-evans)). Some aggregator pages give 2016 or January 2018 instead; the profile uses 2017 and flags the disagreement.

## The funding ladder

ClickUp's rise from bootstrapped tool to unicorn took sixteen months of fundraising. The ladder below rests on the sources cited in the surrounding text.

| Round | Date | Amount | Valuation | Lead investor(s) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Series A | June 2020 | $35 million | Not disclosed | Craft Ventures |
| Series B | December 2020 | $100 million | $1 billion | Georgian |
| Series C | October 2021 | $400 million | $4 billion | [Andreessen Horowitz](/organizations/andreessen-horowitz/) and [Tiger Global](/organizations/tiger-global/) |

The $35 million Series A in June 2020 was ClickUp's first outside capital ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/12/15/clickup-hits-1-billion-valuation-in-100m-series-b-raise/)). Six months later the $100 million Series B, led by Canadian firm Georgian with Craft Ventures participating, made ClickUp a unicorn — a valuation first reported by Bloomberg. Users had doubled to 2 million and revenue had grown ninefold since the start of the year, and the company, then about 200 people, planned to double headcount again ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/12/15/clickup-hits-1-billion-valuation-in-100m-series-b-raise/)). The October 2021 Series C — $400 million at a $4 billion post-money valuation, co-led by Andreessen Horowitz and Tiger Global with [Lightspeed Venture Partners](/organizations/lightspeed-venture-partners/) and Meritech Capital Partners participating — was described as the largest Series C to date in workplace productivity, bringing total funding past $535 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2224795); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2021/10/27/productivity-platform-provider-clickup-valued-4-billion-raising-400-million-series-c/)). By then ClickUp had 800,000 teams, up from 200,000 a year earlier, more than 85,000 paying businesses, and revenue that had tripled on top of 900% growth during the first nine months of the [COVID-19 pandemic](/events/covid-19-pandemic/); 40% of its business was already outside the US, and enterprise customers included [Google](/organizations/google/), McDonald's, Booking.com and [Netflix](/organizations/netflix/) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2224795); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2021/10/27/productivity-platform-provider-clickup-valued-4-billion-raising-400-million-series-c/)). "ClickUp's sole purpose has always been to make the world more productive. Time is our most valuable resource," Evans said in the announcement ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2224795)). The round funded a 600-job European expansion with a new headquarters in Ireland, and the company went on to open a Dublin office and a Salt Lake City office, air its first Super Bowl ad in February 2022, and acquire unified-search tool Slapdash that April ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2224795); [Contrary Research](https://research2.contrary.com/company/clickup)). Contrary Research, citing Inc., reported 2021 revenue of $85 million and a trajectory toward $200 million in 2022, and by February 2023 the platform had more than 8 million users across 1.6 million teams ([Contrary Research](https://research2.contrary.com/company/clickup)).

## Scale, cuts, and the AI turn

The comedown from the 2021 peak came in stages, and Evans framed each stage as a move toward efficiency rather than retreat. In May 2022 ClickUp laid off 7% of its workforce, about 60 employees, as a move toward profitability; in July 2023 it cut another 10%, about 90 employees, concentrated in customer support and service with additional engineering cuts ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2023/07/04/productivity-software-startup-clickup-lets-go-10-workforce/)). Evans told TechCrunch the 2023 "realignment enables us to be a best-in-class IPO-ready company in efficiency while continuing to overachieve in growth," and said the company would keep hiring for product and revenue roles; affected workers received 12 weeks' severance, six months' insurance, visa support, and kept their laptops ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2023/07/04/productivity-software-startup-clickup-lets-go-10-workforce/)).

The AI turn followed. In September 2025 ClickUp announced it had surpassed $300 million in annual recurring revenue and served over 20 million users, with AI sales up 400% year over year and AI usage up 800% — figures the company reported itself — and Evans positioned ClickUp as the "first Converged AI Workspace" ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250909757692/en/ClickUp-accelerating-to-%24300-Million-Annual-Recurring-Revenue-400-Growth-in-AI-Sales)). In February 2026 the company announced six executive hires, including an Audit Committee Chair, aimed at scaling toward $1 billion-plus in ARR ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260204725256/en/ClickUp-Ushers-in-New-Phase-of-Growth-with-Strategic-Executive-Hires-to-Power-Path-to-$1B-ARR)). ClickUp had also been featured on the [Forbes](/organizations/forbes/) Cloud 100 list, Forbes' America's Best Startup Employers 2024 list, and Fast Company's Most Innovative Companies list ([ClickUp](https://clickup.com/about/zeb-evans)).

Then, on May 21, 2026, Evans announced on [X](/organizations/x/) that ClickUp had cut 22% of its workforce — and insisted the move "wasn't about cutting costs" but about restructuring around what he called a "100x org," with a goal of 100x output ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/25/what-clickups-mass-layoff-tells-us-about-the-future-of-work/)). The company had introduced roughly 3,000 internal AI agents to handle complex tasks on employees' behalf, according to Fortune's reporting, with staff now expected to direct the agents and review their output ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/25/what-clickups-mass-layoff-tells-us-about-the-future-of-work/)). "Most savings from this change will flow directly back into the people who stay. We'll be introducing million-dollar salary bands. If you create outsized impact using AI, you'll be paid outside of traditional bands," Evans wrote, adding that "the people that automate their jobs with AI will always have a job" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/25/what-clickups-mass-layoff-tells-us-about-the-future-of-work/)). He told TechCrunch the company is measuring productivity gains internally and plans to productize them: "Instead of gamifying token cost, we gamify value created and time saved" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/25/what-clickups-mass-layoff-tells-us-about-the-future-of-work/)).

The claim deserves the skepticism it received. A [Gartner](/organizations/gartner/) survey cited by TechCrunch found that about 80% of companies using autonomous technology had cut jobs, but that those reductions were not necessarily translating into meaningful financial returns — suggesting some companies use unproven AI as cover for downsizing ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/25/what-clickups-mass-layoff-tells-us-about-the-future-of-work/)). Whether the "100x org" is a genuine operating-model breakthrough or a 2021-vintage valuation story in AI clothing remains an open question, and this profile reports Evans's claims alongside that counterweight rather than settling it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is Zeb Evans?** He is the founder and CEO of ClickUp, the San Diego productivity-software company, and its de facto Chief Product Officer. He founded the company in 2017 with Alex Yurkowski and has led it from a bootstrapped internal tool to a $4 billion valuation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2224795); [ClickUp](https://clickup.com/about/zeb-evans)).

**How did Zeb Evans start ClickUp?** After leaving his social media marketing firm Fast Followerz and a failed startup, Mimri, he set his team a one-month challenge to build their own productivity tool to replace the 14-plus apps they were juggling. The team became obsessed with the tool instead, and it became ClickUp's first version, built in six months with $2.5 million from the sale of his previous business ([Foundr](https://foundr.com/articles/leadership/personal-growth/zeb-evans); [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/zeb-evans-founder-ceo-of-clickup-interview-series/)).

**How much funding has ClickUp raised?** Over $535 million as of the October 2021 Series C: a $35 million Series A in June 2020, a $100 million Series B at a $1 billion valuation in December 2020, and a $400 million Series C at a $4 billion valuation in October 2021 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/12/15/clickup-hits-1-billion-valuation-in-100m-series-b-raise/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2224795)).

**What happened at ClickUp in 2026?** In May 2026 Evans cut 22% of the workforce and restructured the company around roughly 3,000 internal AI agents, calling the goal a "100x org" and promising million-dollar salary bands for top AI-leveraged performers — while denying the move was about cost-cutting ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/25/what-clickups-mass-layoff-tells-us-about-the-future-of-work/)).
