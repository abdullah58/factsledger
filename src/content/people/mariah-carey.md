## Overview

Few artists have ever gotten to close a career-defining chapter three decades after opening it — and Mariah Carey has done it twice in the same two months. In December 2025, "All I Want for Christmas Is You" logged its 20th total week at No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100, making it the longest-running No. 1 in chart history ([Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/music/2025/12/15/mariah-carey-all-i-want-for-christmas-is-you-billboard-record/87780997007/)). Days later, Reuters announced her as the first major music act in the opening ceremony of the Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics, performing at Milan's San Siro Stadium on February 6 under the theme "Harmony" ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/mariah-carey-feature-milano-cortina-games-opening-ceremony-2025-12-15/)). Between those two bookends came a MusiCares Person of the Year honor on January 30, 2026 — and all of it arrived on the back of *Here for It All*, her first studio album in seven years ([Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/music/2025/12/15/mariah-carey-all-i-want-for-christmas-is-you-billboard-record/87780997007/)).

The record's poetry is in who it beat. The two songs tied at 19 weeks were Lil Nas X's "Old Town Road" and Shaboozey's "A Bar Song (Tipsy)" — and "Old Town Road" had itself taken the longevity record from Carey's own "One Sweet Day," her 1995 collaboration with Boyz II Men, which held 16 weeks ([Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/music/2025/12/15/mariah-carey-all-i-want-for-christmas-is-you-billboard-record/87780997007/)). Carey lost the crown to "Old Town Road" in 2019, watched Shaboozey tie that mark in 2024, and took it back in December 2025 with a song released when she was in her twenties. Her Instagram post about it was, by the account of the coverage, a humbly delivered victory lap ([Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/music/2025/12/15/mariah-carey-all-i-want-for-christmas-is-you-billboard-record/87780997007/)).

## The debut that wouldn't leave

[The Recording Academy](/organizations/recording-academy/)'s artist bio begins with the fundamentals: born Mariah Carey on March 27, 1970, in Huntington, New York ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/mariah-carey/856/)). Her self-titled debut album, released in 1990, hit No. 1 on the Billboard 200 and stayed there for eleven weeks, producing the No. 1 hits "Vision of Love," "Love Takes Time," and "Someday" ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/mariah-carey/856/)). At the 33rd Grammy Awards in 1991 she took home her first two Grammys, including Best New Artist ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/mariah-carey/856/)). Across her career, the Academy credits her with 5 wins from 34 nominations ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/mariah-carey/856/)).

The Academy's retrospective adds texture: she was nominated in five categories at the 1991 Grammys; the debut's lead single, "Vision of Love," showed off the whistle register that became her signature; and her path to the record contract ran through a four-song demo tape made during her high-school years with songwriter Ben Marguiles, a move from Long Island in 1988, and a crossing of paths with Latin Grammy winner Brenda K. Starr ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/mariah-carey/856/)). Her godmother, the bio notes, is Grammy-winning R&B artist Patti LaBelle ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/mariah-carey/856/)).

## The record she keeps trading with herself

The Christmas song's numbers are worth laying out plainly. "All I Want for Christmas Is You" has sold more than 50 million copies worldwide and is certified 18-times platinum by the RIAA ([Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/music/2025/12/15/mariah-carey-all-i-want-for-christmas-is-you-billboard-record/87780997007/)). Remarkably, it did not top the Hot 100 until December 2019 — 25 years after its 1994 release — after a chart-rules update let recurrent songs compete again ([Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/music/2025/12/15/mariah-carey-all-i-want-for-christmas-is-you-billboard-record/87780997007/)). The parent album, *Merry Christmas*, reached Diamond status, making it her third Diamond record after *Music Box* (1993) and *Daydream* (1995) ([Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/music/2025/12/15/mariah-carey-all-i-want-for-christmas-is-you-billboard-record/87780997007/)).

The longevity record she now holds is a strange loop. "One Sweet Day" held the mark at 16 weeks; "Old Town Road" supplanted it in 2019; Shaboozey tied that 19-week record in 2024; and Carey reclaimed it at 20 weeks in December 2025 with a holiday song that has effectively become a permanent American institution ([Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/music/2025/12/15/mariah-carey-all-i-want-for-christmas-is-you-billboard-record/87780997007/)).

## The 2012 American Idol panel

Carey's stint as a television judge is one of the most-discussed chapters of her career, and its cast is a matter of record. Her hire for season 12 of *American Idol* was confirmed in July 2012; on September 16, 2012, the panel was finalized with Carey, [Nicki Minaj](/people/nicki-minaj/), Keith Urban, and Randy Jackson as the four judges ([SPIN](https://www.spinmagazine.com/2012/09/american-idol-mariah-carey-nicki-minaj-keith-urban-randy-jackson-judges/)). The season debuted in January 2013 ([SPIN](https://www.spinmagazine.com/2012/09/american-idol-mariah-carey-nicki-minaj-keith-urban-randy-jackson-judges/)). The Carey–Minaj dynamic became the season's defining storyline in the entertainment press, but the panel's composition itself — two pop superstars, a country star, and the show's veteran judge — was the network's bet on reinvention.

## Here for It All and the 2025–26 crescendo

The comeback infrastructure was real before the chart record made it official. Carey wrapped her Christmas Time residency in Las Vegas and in September 2025 released *Here for It All*, her 16th studio album — her first in seven years ([Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/music/2025/12/15/mariah-carey-all-i-want-for-christmas-is-you-billboard-record/87780997007/)). That same September, MTV presented her with the MTV Video Vanguard Award, and she celebrated the 20th anniversary of *The Emancipation of Mimi* — the comeback album built around "We Belong Together" and "It's Like That" ([Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/music/2025/12/15/mariah-carey-all-i-want-for-christmas-is-you-billboard-record/87780997007/)).

Then came the honors sequence: MusiCares Person of the Year on January 30, 2026, and the Milano Cortina opening ceremony on February 6, where she was announced as the first major music act, performing at San Siro under the "Harmony" theme in a ceremony produced by Balich Wonder Studio ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/mariah-carey-feature-milano-cortina-games-opening-ceremony-2025-12-15/); [Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/music/2025/12/15/mariah-carey-all-i-want-for-christmas-is-you-billboard-record/87780997007/)).

The philanthropy runs alongside the career, not after it. Carey has donated more than $1 million to the Fresh Air Fund, which provides free summer experiences for children from low-income communities and named its Camp Mariah after her ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/mariah-carey/856/)). She founded Camp Mariah in 1994 in partnership with the Fund to support underserved youth, and her philanthropic work extended to communities affected by Hurricane Katrina and the COVID-19 pandemic ([Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/music/2025/12/15/mariah-carey-all-i-want-for-christmas-is-you-billboard-record/87780997007/)).
