## Overview

Lauren Kolodny is an American venture capitalist and the co-founder and Managing Partner of [Acrew Capital](/organizations/acrew-capital/), where she leads the firm's fintech practice. Her name is attached to one of venture capital's most retold bets: in early 2016, when [Chime](/organizations/chime/) had been turned down by about 100 investors and was nearly out of money, Kolodny — then a partner at Aspect Ventures — was the only investor who said yes, leading the neobank's $9 million Series A extension ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)). Nine years later, Chime invited her to the podium to ring the opening bell at [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/) on its IPO day ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)).

That single decision made her career, but the thesis behind it predates Chime by a decade. Kolodny began her working life building technology partnerships for the Clinton Foundation in India, where she watched early mobile money take hold, and then worked in product marketing at Google, helping build Google Workspace and leading the launch of Google Drive ([SALT](https://www.salt.org/speakers/lauren-kolodny); [Money20/20](https://us.money2020.com/agenda/speakers/lauren-kolodny-s103-117134)). The through-line in her investing since is technology that democratizes financial services for everyday people — from a free mobile bank for the working class to, most recently, an AI startup that automates estate settlement ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/24/chime-backer-lauren-kolodny-bets-on-ai-to-revolutionize-estate-processing/)).

## The Chime bet: one yes against a hundred no's

Chime was founded in 2012, and its first five or six years were, in co-founder [Ryan King](/people/ryan-king/)'s words, "very difficult in terms of convincing investors to invest in the idea and the business." In early 2016 the company was trying to raise an extension to its Series A and pitched about 100 investors — "maybe more" — and got about 100 rejections, leaving it almost out of money ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)). The founders still believed in the mission: an online bank experience aimed at the working class, free for users, making its money on interchange fees, with no overdraft fees and tools to help customers build credit ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)). Investor after investor looked at the heavily regulated industry, the company's meager growth at the time, and passed.

Kolodny was the exception. She led the $9 million extension — the only term sheet Chime had — and, according to the company's disclosure of its private-round share prices, bought in at 26 cents per share ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)). King has credited her ever since: "She really took a bet on Chris and I, and believed in our passion and zeal and sort of attitude" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)). That check brought Chime's total raise to $21 million at the time, and the company would go on to raise about $2.65 billion as a private company, according to PitchBook estimates ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)). The payoff arrived on June 12, 2025, when Chime went public, pricing its shares at $27 — above its announced $24–$26 range — and raising $864 million; the shares opened at $42, putting the company at about a $14.5 billion market cap by midday, and closed at $37, about $12 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)). Kolodny stood on the podium with the founders to ring the opening bell — a rare honor for an investor, and a measure of how much the company credited her with its survival ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)).

## From clean tech and Google to venture capital

Kolodny's path to that bet began far from Silicon Valley deal-making. She launched a nonprofit that brought an alternative to firewood cookstoves to homes in Tanzania, then went to New Delhi to work on clean technology — until the global financial crisis scuttled the project ([Barron's](https://www.barrons.com/articles/lauren-kolodny-90e5110b)). She began her career proper building technology partnerships for the Clinton Foundation in India, where she witnessed some of the earliest entrepreneurial activity around mobile money and developed a strong belief that fintech could drive financial inclusion ([SALT](https://www.salt.org/speakers/lauren-kolodny)). That early glimpse of technology democratizing financial services for the masses became the fodder for the thesis-driven investor she would become ([Barron's](https://www.barrons.com/articles/lauren-kolodny-90e5110b)).

She returned to the United States to work in product marketing at Google, where she led a number of launches for Google Workspace, including leading the launch of Google Drive for businesses ([SALT](https://www.salt.org/speakers/lauren-kolodny); [Acrew Capital](https://www.acrewcapital.com/team-members/lauren-kolodny)). Two early experiences shaped the rest of her career: the Google product work, and joining the board of trustees of her alma mater, Brown University, at 23 — the university's youngest board member — which fueled her excitement for strategic roles and introduced her to venture capitalist Theresia Gouw, her future co-founder at Acrew ([Barron's](https://www.barrons.com/articles/lauren-kolodny-90e5110b); [SALT](https://www.salt.org/speakers/lauren-kolodny)). She is now a Trustee Emerita of Brown University and serves on the Stanford Graduate School of Business's Trust ([Money20/20](https://us.money2020.com/agenda/speakers/lauren-kolodny-s103-117134)).

After Google she followed Gouw to Aspect Ventures, where she became a partner and led or co-led early investments including Chime and Gusto. When Gouw and Aspect co-founder Jennifer Fonstad went separate ways in 2019, Kolodny left with Gouw to build the new firm.

## Building Acrew Capital

Acrew Capital was deliberately built as a team venture. In December 2019, four months after the Aspect split became public, Gouw announced that Acrew had closed its $250 million debut fund, having originally targeted $175–$200 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/12/18/theresia-gouws-multigenerational-new-firm-acrew-just-closed-a-250-million-debut-fund/)). Gouw launched Acrew with Kolodny, Mark Kraynak, Vishal Lugani and Asad Khaliq — all previously at Aspect — and described the five as co-founders who operate as a team with equal say on investment decisions, a deliberately "multigenerational founding team" meant to position the firm through many funds and many cycles ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/12/18/theresia-gouws-multigenerational-new-firm-acrew-just-closed-a-250-million-debut-fund/)). Kolodny built and leads the firm's fintech practice ([SALT](https://www.salt.org/speakers/lauren-kolodny)).

By the time the debut fund closed, Acrew had already made six investments. The largest was Chime itself, which had just closed a $500 million round at a $5.8 billion valuation — with Gouw crediting Kolodny for leading the company's Series A ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/12/18/theresia-gouws-multigenerational-new-firm-acrew-just-closed-a-250-million-debut-fund/)). The rest of the early book showed the same fintech tilt: Klar, a challenger bank in Mexico City that had closed on $57.5 million in debt and equity funding that September, and Augtera Networks, a network-automation startup ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/12/18/theresia-gouws-multigenerational-new-firm-acrew-just-closed-a-250-million-debut-fund/)). The firm's stated plan was to lead or co-lead seed and Series A rounds — writing initial checks of $4–$7 million — across financial services, cybersecurity, the future of work, and interconnected data ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/12/18/theresia-gouws-multigenerational-new-firm-acrew-just-closed-a-250-million-debut-fund/)).

## The thesis today: Alix and the democratization of everything

Kolodny's recent investing shows the Chime thesis applied to new corners of consumer finance. In 2025 she led a $20 million Series A in Alix, a startup that uses AI to automate estate settlement ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/24/chime-backer-lauren-kolodny-bets-on-ai-to-revolutionize-estate-processing/)). Alix's founder, Alexandra Mysoor, started the company after spending 900 hours over 18 months settling her best friend's late mother's estate — calling banks, locating 401(k)s, canceling accounts, distributing assets — a paper-driven process she described as archaic and shockingly hard ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/24/chime-backer-lauren-kolodny-bets-on-ai-to-revolutionize-estate-processing/)). Alix charges 1% of an estate's value, or $9,000–$12,000 for inheritances under $1 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/24/chime-backer-lauren-kolodny-bets-on-ai-to-revolutionize-estate-processing/)).

What drew Kolodny in was the scale of the mismatch: economists estimate trillions of dollars will transfer to millennial and Gen Z generations over the next two decades, yet the paperwork of settling an estate remains a burden on grieving families, with no company offering comprehensive start-to-finish settlement services ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/24/chime-backer-lauren-kolodny-bets-on-ai-to-revolutionize-estate-processing/)). "How is it possible that there's this messy problem that involves so much project management that there aren't even meaningful services around?" she told TechCrunch. "This is exactly the kind of problem that AI should be solving" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/24/chime-backer-lauren-kolodny-bets-on-ai-to-revolutionize-estate-processing/)). She sees Alix as among the first of many AI-powered startups that will democratize financial services and administrative processes historically available only to the ultra-wealthy ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/24/chime-backer-lauren-kolodny-bets-on-ai-to-revolutionize-estate-processing/)).

Her wider portfolio reflects the same instinct: alongside Chime and Alix, she has backed Bilt, Catena, Crux, Divvy (acquired by Bill.com), Gusto, Invisible Technologies, Plaid and Wispr Flow, plus Clara, Klar, Papaya and Pie Insurance ([Money20/20](https://us.money2020.com/agenda/speakers/lauren-kolodny-s103-117134); [Acrew Capital](https://www.acrewcapital.com/team-members/lauren-kolodny)).

## Recognition

The Chime outcome put Kolodny on the industry's scoreboards. She was named to the Forbes Midas List — the annual ranking of the world's top venture capitalists — three years in a row, and in 2026 to Barron's list of the 100 Most Influential Women in U.S. Finance ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/24/chime-backer-lauren-kolodny-bets-on-ai-to-revolutionize-estate-processing/); [Money20/20](https://us.money2020.com/agenda/speakers/lauren-kolodny-s103-117134)). Earlier honors include Forbes 30 Under 30 for Venture Capital in 2016, the 2021 Forbes Midas Brink List, the Wall Street Journal's 10 Women to Watch list, and Business Insider's Rising Stars of Venture Capital ([SALT](https://www.salt.org/speakers/lauren-kolodny)). Barron's credits her with a knack for identifying categories that could produce big winners early and a disciplined approach to gaining expertise before investing across an ecosystem ([Barron's](https://www.barrons.com/articles/lauren-kolodny-90e5110b)).
