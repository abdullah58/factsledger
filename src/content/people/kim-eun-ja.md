Kim Eun-ja was the real-life Ayla: a Korean War orphan whom Turkish Army Sergeant [Süleyman Dilbirliği](/people/suleyman-dilbirligi/) found alone in 1950, named Ayla, and raised at his garrison for over a year. Reunited with him in Seoul in 2010 after sixty years apart, she lived on in Incheon under veterans-ministry care until her death at 80, announced by the Turkish Embassy in Seoul ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/ayla-kim-eunja-passes-away-at-80-209134)).

## Overview

Every retelling of Kim Eun-ja's story stops at the embrace in a Seoul park — but her life ran another chapter that almost no English-language page covers. She was found as a roughly five-year-old orphan in the wreckage of the Korean War, given a Turkish name, and raised for over a year by soldiers thousands of miles from their own homes ([Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=145773)). Then she vanished into an ordinary Korean life for six decades, telling no one she had ever been called Ayla, until a government phone call in 2010 asked if the name meant anything to her ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)). She became, in her final years, a living symbol of the Turkish–Korean bond — meeting President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan in 2018 and receiving regular embassy visits — before dying in Incheon at 80 after pneumonia, with the Turkish ambassador to Seoul offering her family condolences ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/ayla-kim-eunja-passes-away-at-80-209134)).

## The garrison year

Turkey's 5,090-strong brigade arrived in Korea on 17 September 1950 at the United Nations' behest, under General Tahsin Yazıcı, and among the thousands of soldiers who went voluntarily was 25-year-old Sergeant Süleyman Dilbirliği ([Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=145773)). That figure explains why one of the Korean War's most retold humanitarian stories belongs to a Turkish sergeant rather than to a larger UN contingent: Turkish troops, in the words of Turkey's ambassador to Korea, "did not only fight against the enemies but also provided humanitarian assistance to the civilians and sheltered orphaned Korean children" at the battlefield ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).

### Found in the fighting

One night, after heavy fighting, Dilbirliği and his squadron spotted movement behind some bushes on their way back to base: a five-year-old girl, glaring at them through extreme fear, her hands nearly frozen ([Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=145773)). She carried no identity papers and could not speak, so the soldiers gave her the Turkish name Ayla ([Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=145773)). Sources gloss the name three ways — the halo of light around the moon, a face round and bright like the moon, and the moonlight under which she was found ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350); [Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=145773); [Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/ayla-kim-eunja-passes-away-at-80-209134)). Where exactly she was found also varies by account: Korea.net describes the movement behind the bushes, while Turkish soldiers later recalled a crying girl in the ruins of a pillaged village in North Korean territory ([Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=145773); [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).

### Life at the Turkish camp

The camp became her home and playground. She learned Turkish, even acted as an interpreter, and called Dilbirliği "Baba" — father — while the other soldiers became her uncles and brothers ([Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=145773)). Decades later she still recalled those days as the happiest of her life: the soldiers stitched clothes for her out of their own battledress, staged snowball fights, and on rest days took her for rides on their tanks ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)). Dilbirliği kept a wartime photograph of the two of them, taken by a fellow Turkish soldier, until the 2010 reunion — and it was those same photographs that would later identify her ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).

### Three accounts of the parting

What all three accounts agree on is the aftermath: she was placed in the Turkish-run care system in Suwon — the school the soldiers had set up for the orphans they sheltered ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).

## Sixty years of silence

For sixty years, Kim told no one about her Turkish father or the name he had given her — nobody else had ever called her Ayla ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)). She would later say she realized, too late, that she knew nothing about him — not his name, not his face in a photograph — only that he was somewhere in Turkey ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)). The search began at an Istanbul event marking the war's 60th anniversary, where Dilbirliği spoke publicly about Ayla and South Korean officials started looking for the girl ([Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/turkey/2017/11/22/real-life-ayla-by-bedside-of-turkish-soldier-who-helped-her-1511298345)).

### The search

One day in 2010, a Suwon city government official called Kim at home in Incheon to ask whether she had once been called by that name — and days later a producer for a Chuncheon MBC documentary appeared at her door carrying Dilbirliği's wartime photographs ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)). The sergeant had been contacting Korean tourism offices in Turkey trying to find her, and word of it reached the producer, who pieced together his photos, the Ankara School, and Kim's close contacts there ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)). She said she could not believe what she was hearing — her father had been looking for her all along ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).

### The reunion

The reunion, filmed for the documentary, took place in a park in Yeouido, western Seoul: Dilbirliği walked toward her asking if she was Ayla, quickening his pace, and she embraced him through her tears, repeating "Baba, why did you come so late?" ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)). Dilbirliği's wife Nimet was there too, and Kim's family was photographed together with the couple at Ankara Park in Seoul ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)). "I saw him walk toward me, and his gait, his face — I knew it was my father," she said ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).

### The Ankara School and the years between

After the parting, Kim was taken to the Ankara School in Suwon — a school the Turkish forces set up for war orphans, deliberately not called an orphanage, where some 60 to 70 children lived and were taught Turkish ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)). Her story was not unique: Turkish troops sheltered many orphaned Korean children at the battlefield, and as their numbers grew the fighting units sent them to Suwon to be cared for "under a common roof," in the ambassador's account ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)). She left the school as a teenager to work — waiting tables, sewing in a clothing factory, assisting in a hospital's gynecology department — and at 24 married through a matchmaker and had a son ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).

## The film years and the final chapters

The last decade of Kim's life was her most public: the story that had been a private memory for sixty years became a national film, and the Turkish state embraced her as a living symbol of the two countries' bond ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/ayla-kim-eunja-passes-away-at-80-209134)).

### The 2017 film and the farewell

In 2017 the story became [Ayla: The Daughter of War](/creative-works/ayla-the-daughter-of-war/), directed by Can Ülkay — a box-office success that Türkiye selected as its official submission for the foreign-language-film Oscar ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/ayla-kim-eunja-passes-away-at-80-209134)). Kim toured Turkey with the cast, traveling to Gaziantep for a premiere, and praised the film as a story of love, peace and wartimes based on her own life; she said all Turks had become her family ([Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/cinema/2017/11/22/turkish-people-are-my-family-real-life-ayla-kim-eunja-says)). That November she flew to Istanbul to visit the 91-year-old Dilbirliği, then in intensive care at Haydarpaşa Numune Hospital with respiratory failure: he could only gesture with his hands while she wept at his bedside, and the Istanbul official who accompanied her told Anadolu Agency that a doctor said it was the first time the veteran had responded in days ([Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/turkey/2017/11/22/real-life-ayla-by-bedside-of-turkish-soldier-who-helped-her-1511298345)). Dilbirliği died later in 2017 ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/ayla-kim-eunja-passes-away-at-80-209134)).

### The last years in Incheon

Kim's remaining years kept her at the center of the bilateral friendship. She met President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan during his 2018 state visit to Seoul, was regularly visited by Turkish embassy officials, and never forgot what she called her Turkish father ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/ayla-kim-eunja-passes-away-at-80-209134)). After contracting COVID-19 in 2022 she received care at Songdo Hana Hospital under the support of South Korea's Patriots and Veterans Affairs Ministry, and she battled dementia in her final years ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/ayla-kim-eunja-passes-away-at-80-209134)). She died in Incheon on May 12 at 80 after a bout of pneumonia, surrounded by her children and grandchildren; the Turkish Embassy in Seoul announced her death and Ambassador Murat Tamer visited the family to offer condolences ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/ayla-kim-eunja-passes-away-at-80-209134)).

## FAQ

**Who was Kim Eun-ja?**
She was the real-life Ayla: a Korean War orphan found around age five by Turkish Sergeant Süleyman Dilbirliği in 1950, named Ayla, and raised at the Turkish garrison for over a year before they were separated ([Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=145773)). After a 2010 reunion in Seoul, her story became the 2017 Turkish film *Ayla: The Daughter of War* ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/ayla-kim-eunja-passes-away-at-80-209134)).

**Why did the sergeant name her Ayla?**
She had no identity papers and could not speak, so the soldiers chose a Turkish name — but sources gloss it three ways: the halo of light around the moon, a face round and bright like the moon, and the moonlight under which she was found ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350); [Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=145773); [Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/ayla-kim-eunja-passes-away-at-80-209134)).

**How were they reunited after sixty years?**
Dilbirliği spoke about Ayla at an Istanbul event marking the war's 60th anniversary, prompting South Korean officials to search ([Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/turkey/2017/11/22/real-life-ayla-by-bedside-of-turkish-soldier-who-helped-her-1511298345)). A Suwon official's 2010 phone call located Kim, a Chuncheon MBC producer confirmed her with wartime photographs, and the two embraced in a Yeouido park ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).

**What happened to her after the war?**
She grew up at the Turkish-run Ankara School in Suwon, left as a teenager for jobs including waitressing and factory work, married at 24 through a matchmaker, and had a son ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)). She later lived in Incheon, met President Erdoğan in 2018, and died there at 80 after pneumonia, with the Turkish Embassy announcing her death ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/ayla-kim-eunja-passes-away-at-80-209134)).

**Was the 2017 film faithful to her story?**
*Ayla: The Daughter of War* was Türkiye's official foreign-language Oscar submission and a Turkish box-office success ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/ayla-kim-eunja-passes-away-at-80-209134)). Kim toured with its cast and endorsed it as a story of love, peace and wartimes drawn from her own life, saying Turkish soldiers had been kind to all the war's orphans ([Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/cinema/2017/11/22/turkish-people-are-my-family-real-life-ayla-kim-eunja-says)).
