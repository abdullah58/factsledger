## Overview

Zach Witkoff is the son who built the crypto track of the family business while his father flew Trump's diplomacy. Until 2025, he was, in the words of The New York Times, "virtually unknown in the crypto world" — a real-estate scion who ran the family office. Then, as his father jetting around the globe on White House duty, the younger Witkoff "transformed into a crypto superstar — the face of World Liberty" ([The New York Times](https://web.archive.org/web/20250915185635/https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/15/us/politics/trump-uae-chips-witkoff-world-liberty.html)). He is the co-founder and CEO of World Liberty Financial, the Trump-family cryptocurrency venture; the chairman of the Nasdaq-listed ALT5 Sigma; and, since August 2026, the president and chairman of the proposed World Liberty Trust national bank ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/us-regulator-approves-bank-charter-for-trump-backed-crypto-company-world-liberty-financial/)). His defining moment came on a Dubai stage in May 2025, when he announced that an Abu Dhabi sovereign fund would use his company's stablecoin to complete a $2 billion investment in Binance — at the time, the largest investment in a crypto company ever made ([The New York Times](https://web.archive.org/web/20250915185635/https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/15/us/politics/trump-uae-chips-witkoff-world-liberty.html)). What no standard biography ties together is the parallel structure behind it: father and son, diplomacy and crypto, running on the same Gulf capital at the same time.

## From construction sites to the family office

Witkoff's entry into business started in the family trade. He had been working on construction sites alongside his father from the age of 15, and a few years after graduating top of his class from the University of Miami's Business School, he started Witkoff Capital in 2019 — the family office, built to expand the group's hard-asset holdings into technology ([Luxury Travel Magazine](https://www.luxurytravelmagazine.com/news-articles/zach-witkoff-and-the-impact-of-technology-on-real-estate)). He was explicit about where he was looking: "We are very long in hard assets and want to diversify into tech while maintaining a strong real estate presence," he said in the 2019 interview, naming AI and blockchain as the themes the family office was watching ([Luxury Travel Magazine](https://www.luxurytravelmagazine.com/news-articles/zach-witkoff-and-the-impact-of-technology-on-real-estate)). The crypto star's origin story was a family office, not a trading desk — a detail the crypto press rarely mentions. The interview also recorded [the Witkoff Group](/organizations/witkoff-group/)'s hospitality footprint — the West Hollywood EDITION, the Times Square EDITION, the Public Hotel in New York — the luxury pipeline he grew up inside ([Luxury Travel Magazine](https://www.luxurytravelmagazine.com/news-articles/zach-witkoff-and-the-impact-of-technology-on-real-estate)).

## The Dubai announcement

World Liberty Financial launched in September 2024 with [Donald Trump](https://factsledger.com/people/donald-trump/) and his sons alongside the Witkoffs, and Zach took the chief executive role ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/us-regulator-approves-bank-charter-for-trump-backed-crypto-company-world-liberty-financial/)). In March 2025 he joined a group of the industry's most powerful executives for a White House crypto gathering headlined by Trump and the administration's AI and crypto czar, [David Sacks](https://factsledger.com/people/david-sacks/) ([The New York Times](https://web.archive.org/web/20250915185635/https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/15/us/politics/trump-uae-chips-witkoff-world-liberty.html)). The same month he became a father, naming his son "Don," after the president ([The New York Times](https://web.archive.org/web/20250915185635/https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/15/us/politics/trump-uae-chips-witkoff-world-liberty.html)).

On March 25, 2025, Witkoff announced on X that World Liberty would begin selling a dollar-pegged stablecoin, USD1, tailored to "sovereign investors and major institutions" — the announcement also revealed that BitGo, a custodian backed by Sacks's Craft Ventures, would store the reserves underlying the coin ([The New York Times](https://web.archive.org/web/20250915185635/https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/15/us/politics/trump-uae-chips-witkoff-world-liberty.html)). Then came Dubai. On stage at Token2049 in May, flanked by crypto investor Justin Sun and Eric Trump, Witkoff praised the Emirates as "just one incredible country," "the most innovative country on planet Earth today," and revealed the deal: MGX — the Abu Dhabi investment vehicle chaired by Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed Al Nahyan — would use USD1 to complete a $2 billion investment in Binance, the giant crypto exchange ([The New York Times](https://web.archive.org/web/20250915185635/https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/15/us/politics/trump-uae-chips-witkoff-world-liberty.html)). The transaction, the largest ever in a crypto company according to Binance, effectively handed World Liberty a $2 billion bank deposit — funds the company could invest to generate returns in the tens of millions of dollars annually ([The New York Times](https://web.archive.org/web/20250915185635/https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/15/us/politics/trump-uae-chips-witkoff-world-liberty.html)). "We thank MGX and Binance for their trust in us," Witkoff said from the stage ([The New York Times](https://web.archive.org/web/20250915185635/https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/15/us/politics/trump-uae-chips-witkoff-world-liberty.html)).

## The parallel track

The striking part of the Times investigation was how closely the crypto operation ran alongside the diplomacy. While Steve Witkoff was Trump's envoy, his son "kept tabs on the Middle East discussions" and, in private conversations, alluded to high-level meetings in the region — with Sheikh Tahnoon having become "a good friend of the family," as Zach told an associate ([The New York Times](https://web.archive.org/web/20250915185635/https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/15/us/politics/trump-uae-chips-witkoff-world-liberty.html)). Beginning in January 2025, Fiacc Larkin — a computer-science expert who served as G42's head of crypto — joined World Liberty with the title of "chief strategic adviser," creating what the Times described plainly: "while the U.A.E. was negotiating with the White House to secure chips for G42, a G42 employee was helping the Witkoffs and the Trumps make money" ([The New York Times](https://web.archive.org/web/20250915185635/https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/15/us/politics/trump-uae-chips-witkoff-world-liberty.html)). On the Dubai stage, Witkoff's praise went further: "We really need to take a page out of His Highness's and the Emirates' book. They are just an amazing example of how you can lead with innovation while also maintaining your family values" ([The New York Times](https://web.archive.org/web/20250915185635/https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/15/us/politics/trump-uae-chips-witkoff-world-liberty.html)). The White House and World Liberty both denied any connection between the chips deal and the crypto deal, with an administration spokeswoman calling the crypto transaction "totally unrelated to any government business" ([The New York Times](https://web.archive.org/web/20250915185635/https://www.nytimes.com/2025/09/15/us/politics/trump-uae-chips-witkoff-world-liberty.html)).

## A second chairman's seat and a bank charter

In August 2025, Witkoff added a second corporate leadership seat: when ALT5 Sigma Corporation, a Nasdaq-listed fintech, closed a $1.5 billion offering led by World Liberty, he became chairman of its board — with Eric Trump joining as a director and World Liberty co-founder Zak Folkman as a board observer ([ALT5 Sigma Corporation](https://marketwirenews.com/stock/alts/news/alt5-sigma-corporation-announces-closing-of-1-5-bill-7299950035645665.html)). The company said it would implement a treasury strategy holding about 7.5% of the total $WLFI token supply ([ALT5 Sigma Corporation](https://marketwirenews.com/stock/alts/news/alt5-sigma-corporation-announces-closing-of-1-5-bill-7299950035645665.html)).

Then came the bank. On August 14, 2026, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency granted preliminary conditional approval for World Liberty Trust Company, N.A., to organize as a national trust bank — an application filed in January 2026 ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/us-regulator-approves-bank-charter-for-trump-backed-crypto-company-world-liberty-financial/)). If finalized, the charter would let World Liberty issue, redeem and custody USD1 in-house — functions then handled by BitGo — under federal supervision, while not permitting deposit-taking or lending like a traditional bank ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/us-regulator-approves-bank-charter-for-trump-backed-crypto-company-world-liberty-financial/)). Zach Witkoff is the trust company's president and chairman; his statement on the approval was: "We welcome continuous scrutiny from Federal regulators for many years to come" ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/us-regulator-approves-bank-charter-for-trump-backed-crypto-company-world-liberty-financial/)). The OCC attached conditions including at least $20 million in capital and a qualified internal-audit manager, and noted it had received "passivity agreements" from investors — including Eric Trump — vowing not to seek control of the bank ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/us-regulator-approves-bank-charter-for-trump-backed-crypto-company-world-liberty-financial/)). By August 2026, USD1 had grown to about $4 billion in market capitalization, the fourth-largest stablecoin, and [Reuters](https://factsledger.com/organizations/reuters/) estimated the Trump family had earned about $50 million from USD1 through June 2026 — with World Liberty having "funneled more than $1.6 billion to the U.S. president and his family as of April," by Reuters's calculations ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/us-regulator-approves-bank-charter-for-trump-backed-crypto-company-world-liberty-financial/)).

## The family's cut and the 2022 arrest

Financial disclosures reported in September 2026 show the scale of the family's stake: Steve Witkoff made nearly $107 million in 2025 from a holding company affiliated with World Liberty, according to documents obtained by a government watchdog ([ABC News](https://www.kbnwnews.com/2026/09/09/steve-witkoff-earned-nearly-107m-from-trump-affiliated-crypto-venture-disclosures-show/)). At one point he was listed as "co-founder emeritus" on World Liberty's website, a title he shared with Trump; the firm is operated by Zach Witkoff and Trump's three sons ([ABC News](https://www.kbnwnews.com/2026/09/09/steve-witkoff-earned-nearly-107m-from-trump-affiliated-crypto-venture-disclosures-show/)). Trump himself reported $526 million from World Liberty token sales and another $65 million from sales of equity in the venture's holding company in 2025 ([ABC News](https://www.kbnwnews.com/2026/09/09/steve-witkoff-earned-nearly-107m-from-trump-affiliated-crypto-venture-disclosures-show/)). In May 2025 World Liberty had said Steve Witkoff was divesting and had "no operational role, no financial interest in WLFI deals, and no influence on day-to-day decisions"; by September 2026 a White House spokeswoman said he had "fully divested from World Liberty Financial" ([ABC News](https://www.kbnwnews.com/2026/09/09/steve-witkoff-earned-nearly-107m-from-trump-affiliated-crypto-venture-disclosures-show/)).

One earlier episode resurfaced in April 2026, when body-camera footage of Witkoff's New Year's Day 2022 arrest outside Miami's E11EVEN nightclub was published ([The Newsground](https://thenewsground.com/bodycam-footage-shows-2022-arrest-of-zach-witkoff-outside-miami-nightclub/)). The then-28-year-old was arrested after a fight, and officers searching his pockets found a bag of cocaine; he was charged with disorderly conduct, resisting arrest, and felony possession of cocaine ([The Newsground](https://thenewsground.com/bodycam-footage-shows-2022-arrest-of-zach-witkoff-outside-miami-nightclub/)). The footage shows him repeatedly insisting he had "done nothing" and at one point saying, "I'm friends with Marc Roberts, I swear to God" — referring to the club's owner — to which a security guard replied, "Nobody cares. Stop dropping names" ([The Newsground](https://thenewsground.com/bodycam-footage-shows-2022-arrest-of-zach-witkoff-outside-miami-nightclub/)). Court records show he posted bond within days and entered a written plea of not guilty on January 3, 2022; prosecutors filed formal charges in February but the matter never went to trial — the felony cocaine charge and one count of resisting arrest were dropped by the prosecutor ([The Newsground](https://thenewsground.com/bodycam-footage-shows-2022-arrest-of-zach-witkoff-outside-miami-nightclub/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not credit Zach Witkoff with the Middle East envoy role — that is his father Steve's position, and any source assigning it to Zach is corrected here. It does not assert a "$1.4B UAE-DMG deal" involving Zach; no such deal was found in a full-read source, and the closest verified transactions are the MGX $2 billion Binance investment and the snippet-level 49% stake report, which is not asserted. It does not take a position on whether the chips and crypto deals were linked — the Times found no evidence of an explicit quid pro quo, and the White House and World Liberty deny any connection. It does not state his exact birth date or marriage details, which did not appear in a full-read source. USD1 figures are dated datapoints, not current readings, and the OCC charter approval is preliminary and conditional — the bank cannot open until pre-opening conditions are met.
