Gavin Newsom is the rare American politician whose business résumé matters as much as his electoral one. Before he was California's 40th governor, he was a wine-shop owner who built the PlumpJack Group into a small hospitality empire — bankrolled, in no small part, by one of America's richest families. That fortune-and-friendship backstory, more than any single policy, is what the quick online bios tend to skip. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom))

## The Getty connection

Newsom was born on October 10, 1967, in San Francisco, a fourth-generation San Franciscan, to William and Tessa Newsom. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom)) His parents divorced while he and his sister Hillary were young, and the children lived with their mother, who supported them by working three jobs: waitress, secretary, and bookkeeper. His father was a judge in the San Francisco courts. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom)) As a child he struggled with dyslexia, a difficulty he has spoken about publicly for years. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom); [The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/gavin-newsom-california-governor-politics-trump-10058921/lite/))

The consequential relationship of his youth was not political but social: the Newsom family was close to the Gettys, the billion-dollar oil family. William Newsom had attended high school with Gordon Getty, son of J. Paul Getty, and William's father had acted as something of a father figure to Gordon, whose own father was often away in Europe. When J. Paul Getty's grandson John Paul Getty III was kidnapped in Italy in 1973 and the elder Getty refused to pay ransom, William Newsom helped negotiate the teenager's release. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom)) The two families vacationed together — African safaris, trips to Alaska — and the Gettys became strong supporters of Gavin Newsom's business ventures and, later, his political ambitions. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom))

After graduating from Santa Clara University in 1989 with a bachelor's degree in political science, Newsom opened a wine shop and created the PlumpJack Management Group, which ran his wine distribution business and restaurants. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom)) He also invested in a ski resort in Squaw Valley and a 53-acre vineyard in Napa, and the Getty family helped finance many of these businesses. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom)) The governor's own official biography describes the same arc in plainer terms: a retail wine shop that grew into the PlumpJack Group, managing restaurants, hotels, and wineries across California. ([Office of the Governor of California](https://www.gov.ca.gov/about/))

## San Francisco: the board, the mayor's office, the marriage licenses

Newsom's path into politics ran through family connections. His father was friends with John Burton, the leader of the California State Senate, who recommended the younger Newsom to San Francisco mayor Willie Brown. Brown first appointed him to the city commission overseeing traffic and parking, then to an empty seat on the Board of Supervisors. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom)) Newsom was elected to the board three times, serving from 1997 to 2004. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom))

When Brown's term ended, Newsom ran for mayor in 2003 against Green Party candidate Matt Gonzalez, campaigning on homelessness, schools, and ending corruption and special interests. With Getty-family help he raised $4 million — nearly ten times Gonzalez's total — and won endorsements from former president Bill Clinton and former vice president Al Gore. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom)) He won the December 9 runoff with 53 percent of the vote and was sworn in on January 9, 2004, by his own father. At thirty-seven he was the city's youngest mayor in more than a century. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom)) In his first days he cut his own pay in light of a $330 million budget deficit, appointed women as chief of police and chief of the fire department, and hired an openly gay chief of staff. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom))

Then came the decision that made him nationally famous. After hearing President George W. Bush's State of the Union address denounce gay marriage, Newsom directed the city to issue marriage licenses to same-sex couples. More than four thousand gay and lesbian couples from California and around the country traveled to San Francisco to marry, and Newsom personally presided over the marriage of his chief of staff and his longtime partner. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom)) His official biography still frames it as bringing same-sex marriage "to the forefront of the national conversation." ([Office of the Governor of California](https://www.gov.ca.gov/about/)) Within weeks, California courts declared the licenses illegally distributed and voided the marriages, ruling that Newsom lacked the authority to defy existing state law. Governor Arnold Schwarzenegger said Newsom had acted above the law, and President Bush called the marriages troubling to America. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom)) San Francisco was nonetheless the first American city to sanction gay and lesbian weddings — Massachusetts followed that summer, California voters approved Proposition 8 in 2008, courts dismantled it by 2013, same-sex marriage became legal nationwide in 2015, and in 2024 California voters removed Prop 8's remaining language from the state constitution. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom))

## Governor: pandemic, recall, and a second term

After withdrawing from the 2009 gubernatorial race, Newsom served as California's 49th lieutenant governor from 2011 through 2018, then ran for governor on a progressive platform, a promise of budget prudence, and open opposition to President Donald Trump's policies. He won in a landslide — 61.9 percent to Republican John Cox's 38.1 percent — and was sworn in on January 7, 2019. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom))

His first term was dominated by COVID-19. He mandated the nation's first statewide stay-at-home order, which health experts said likely prevented a higher death toll in California. But the pandemic also produced his worst political wound: in November 2020, just as he was announcing that rising cases would delay easing restaurant restrictions, a Los Angeles TV station published photos of him at a lobbyist's fiftieth birthday party at a high-end Napa Valley restaurant — seated close together with other guests, no face coverings. Critics called it hypocrisy; Newsom apologized. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom))

The fallout fed a recall effort. In September 2021, voters defeated the recall 62.7 to 37.3 percent, making Newsom only the second governor in American history to survive one, and he was reelected in 2022 with just over 59 percent of the vote. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom)) His second-term signings included a ban on local governments requiring voter ID, a prohibition on book bans in public libraries, entertainment zones permitting outdoor alcohol sales, limits on medical bills' effect on credit, eviction protections, tiny-home shelters for the homeless — and a formal state apology to Black residents for enslavement paired with a veto of a bill that would have required financial reparations. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom))

## The Trump-era antagonist and 2028

With Trump's return to the presidency in November 2024, Newsom called a special legislative session to shield California's climate, immigration, and reproductive-rights policies from federal rollback. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom)) By 2025 the relationship had turned openly combative: Newsom attacked the administration over withheld federal recovery funding after January's Southern California wildfires, over immigration enforcement tactics, and over mid-decade gerrymandering in Republican states. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom)) When Trump ordered thousands of National Guard troops and hundreds of Marines into Los Angeles over Newsom's objections, Newsom said the Guard had been called without his procedurally required consent; Trump mused that arresting the governor "would be a great thing," and California's attorney general sued over the deployment. ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/gavin-newsom-california-governor-politics-trump-10058921/lite/))

Newsom answered with a media strategy of his own. In March 2025 he launched the podcast *This Is Gavin Newsom*, deliberately booking Republicans — including former Trump strategist Steve Bannon — in a bid for audiences beyond the Democratic base. ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/gavin-newsom-california-governor-politics-trump-10058921/lite/)) And his signature 2025 move was Proposition 50, the Election Rigging Response Act: a constitutional amendment letting California's Democratic legislature temporarily redraw congressional maps to offset Republican redistricting in Texas. On November 4, 2025, more than eight million Californians turned out and over 63 percent voted yes. ([USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/politics/2025/11/05/did-prop-50-pass-california-redistricting/87101470007/)) The new maps apply to congressional elections starting in 2026 and remain in effect through 2030, after which the nonpartisan citizens' commission takes over again. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom)) Trump denounced the vote as a "GIANT SCAM" on Truth Social; Newsom called it "a night for the Democratic party." ([USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/politics/2025/11/05/did-prop-50-pass-california-redistricting/87101470007/))

Through 2024 he had been a loyal [Joe Biden](/people/joe-biden/) surrogate, dismissing concerns about Biden's age, and he denied maneuvering to replace Biden on the ballot. ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/gavin-newsom-california-governor-politics-trump-10058921/lite/)) By the end of 2025, political polls and betting odds showed him as an early frontrunner for the 2028 Democratic presidential nomination. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom))

Newsom married lawyer and television commentator Kimberly Guilfoyle in 2001; they divorced in 2006. In 2008 he married actor and filmmaker Jennifer Siebel Newsom, and they have four children: Montana, Hunter, Brooklynn, and Dutch. ([EBSCO](http://ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/gavin-newsom))
