## Overview

Leon Black is the billionaire co-founder of Apollo Global Management, the private equity firm he built from a 1990 startup into one of the world's largest investment houses — and the most prominent Wall Street figure entangled in the Jeffrey Epstein affair. In January 2021 he stepped down as Apollo's CEO after an Apollo-commissioned investigation found he had paid Epstein $158 million in fees from 2012 to 2017. Five years later, his refusal to cooperate with Congress's Epstein investigation made him the rare private citizen held in contempt: on September 16, 2026, the full House of Representatives voted unanimously to hold him in contempt of Congress ([Forbes](https://www.thewealthadvisor.com/article/billionaire-leon-black-step-down-private-equity-giant-apollo-global-after-release-jeffrey); [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/16/leon-black-epstein-james-comer/91792494007/)).

This profile states only documented, attributed public facts about a living person. Allegations are sourced to those who made them; Black's denials, stated through his counsel, are given with them.

## September 2026: held in contempt

The contempt saga unfolded over two weeks in September 2026. Black had appeared voluntarily before the [House Oversight Committee](/organizations/house-oversight-committee/) in June, but lawmakers said he refused to answer their questions about nondisclosure agreements ([AP](https://srnnews.com/house-panel-recommends-contempt-charge-for-billionaire-leon-black-in-epstein-probe/)). The committee then issued two subpoenas: one compelling him to produce the NDAs — those to which he was a party and any mentioning Epstein or Epstein's convicted accomplice Ghislaine Maxwell — and one compelling him to appear for a deposition ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/16/leon-black-epstein-james-comer/91792494007/)). The deposition was originally set for July 16; at Black's request the committee moved it to September 3. He refused to appear — and sued the panel, asking a federal court to block the subpoenas ([AP](https://srnnews.com/house-panel-recommends-contempt-charge-for-billionaire-leon-black-in-epstein-probe/)).

On September 15, the Oversight Committee voted unanimously, with Republicans and Democrats together, to recommend that Black be held in contempt of Congress for failing to comply with the testimony subpoena ([AP](https://srnnews.com/house-panel-recommends-contempt-charge-for-billionaire-leon-black-in-epstein-probe/)). Chairman James Comer, a Kentucky Republican, said Black was "hiding behind litigation to delay having to provide answers to the American people": "No one is above the law… We have no choice but to hold him in contempt" ([AP](https://srnnews.com/house-panel-recommends-contempt-charge-for-billionaire-leon-black-in-epstein-probe/)). The panel's ranking Democrat, Robert Garcia of California, called Black's behavior "unacceptable" ([AP](https://srnnews.com/house-panel-recommends-contempt-charge-for-billionaire-leon-black-in-epstein-probe/)).

The next day, the full House voted unanimously to hold Black in contempt — a day after the committee's bipartisan vote ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/16/leon-black-epstein-james-comer/91792494007/)). "Subpoenas are not suggestions," Comer said. "They carry the force of law and require full compliance" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/16/leon-black-epstein-james-comer/91792494007/)). A group of more than a dozen Epstein survivors, including Annie Farmer and Dani Bensky, praised Comer for "showing real resolve" and "sending a clear message that wealth and influence do not put anyone beyond the reach of congressional oversight" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/16/leon-black-epstein-james-comer/91792494007/)). The contempt finding would next go to the Justice Department, with prosecution possible — though the House was scheduled to break until after the November midterm elections, leaving next steps uncertain ([AP](https://srnnews.com/house-panel-recommends-contempt-charge-for-billionaire-leon-black-in-epstein-probe/)).

Black's attorneys, Susan Estrich and Aaron Cutler, issued a scathing response, calling the committee's action a "complete abomination" ([AP](https://srnnews.com/house-panel-recommends-contempt-charge-for-billionaire-leon-black-in-epstein-probe/)). "Mr. Black has repeatedly said that he feels terrible for Epstein's victims and that he regrets ever doing business with Epstein," they stated. "He never abused a woman. He never was with an underage woman. He never engaged in sex trafficking. He never paid Epstein for access to women. He was never blackmailed by Epstein. Mr. Black had no knowledge of any of Epstein's heinous conduct" ([AP](https://srnnews.com/house-panel-recommends-contempt-charge-for-billionaire-leon-black-in-epstein-probe/)). They said they had filed a complaint with the Office of Congressional Conduct seeking an investigation into what they called Comer's unethical tactics ([AP](https://srnnews.com/house-panel-recommends-contempt-charge-for-billionaire-leon-black-in-epstein-probe/)).

Comer argued Black's testimony was crucial because he had "maintained a close personal relationship with Epstein for years during the time when Epstein was engaged in sex trafficking"; Black has said the relationship was strictly professional ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/16/leon-black-epstein-james-comer/91792494007/)). Black was one of several prominent figures asked to appear in the probe, including Bill Clinton and Bill Gates ([AP](https://srnnews.com/house-panel-recommends-contempt-charge-for-billionaire-leon-black-in-epstein-probe/)).

## The money: $158 million, and the competing figures

The financial relationship with [Jeffrey Epstein](https://factsledger.com/people/jeffrey-epstein/) is documented in three attributed accounts that do not agree on the total. The Apollo board's 2021 investigation, conducted by the law firm Dechert, found that Black paid Epstein $158 million in fees from 2012 to 2017 — after Epstein's 2008 guilty plea to soliciting prostitution from a minor — for "bona fide tax, estate planning and other related services," plus more than $30 million in loans and a $10 million donation to Epstein's charity ([AP](https://srnnews.com/house-panel-recommends-contempt-charge-for-billionaire-leon-black-in-epstein-probe/); [Forbes](https://www.thewealthadvisor.com/article/billionaire-leon-black-step-down-private-equity-giant-apollo-global-after-release-jeffrey)). Dechert said the work "conferred more than $1 billion and as much as $2 billion or more in value" on Black, and found "no evidence that Mr. Black was involved in any way with Mr. Epstein's criminal activities at any time" ([Forbes](https://www.thewealthadvisor.com/article/billionaire-leon-black-step-down-private-equity-giant-apollo-global-after-release-jeffrey)).

The 2026 lawmakers alleged the figure was $180 million ([AP](https://srnnews.com/house-panel-recommends-contempt-charge-for-billionaire-leon-black-in-epstein-probe/)). And emails obtained by the *New York Times* showed Epstein pressuring Black between 2015 and 2016 — demanding $25 million upfront and $40 million a year — with the total paid put at $170 million in that account ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/world/2025/10/19/who-is-leon-black-billionaire-was-pressured-by-jeffrey-epstein-for-millions-in-cash-via-emails.html)). Black's lawyer, Susan Estrich, said he paid only "for tax and estate planning advice that saved him and his family billions of dollars," and that Black ultimately fired Epstein because he was disruptive and his fees excessive ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/world/2025/10/19/who-is-leon-black-billionaire-was-pressured-by-jeffrey-epstein-for-millions-in-cash-via-emails.html)).

## The career: Dartmouth to Drexel to Apollo

Black, born in 1951, studied philosophy and history at Dartmouth and earned a Harvard MBA in the 1970s ([Forbes](https://www.thewealthadvisor.com/article/billionaire-leon-black-step-down-private-equity-giant-apollo-global-after-release-jeffrey); [The Observer](https://observer.co.uk/culture/books/article/the-confessions-of-leon-black-the-billionaire-who-bankrolled-jeffrey-epstein)). He joined the investment bank Drexel Burnham Lambert and rose through the ranks to become head of mergers and acquisitions ([Forbes](https://www.thewealthadvisor.com/article/billionaire-leon-black-step-down-private-equity-giant-apollo-global-after-release-jeffrey)). After Drexel's 1989–90 collapse, he struck out on his own, co-founding Apollo Advisors in 1990 with colleagues Marc Rowan and Josh Harris ([Forbes](https://www.thewealthadvisor.com/article/billionaire-leon-black-step-down-private-equity-giant-apollo-global-after-release-jeffrey)).

In January 2021, as Apollo released the Dechert findings, Black announced he would step down as CEO — the firm then managed $433 billion in assets — with co-founder Rowan replacing him by mid-year while Black remained chairman ([Forbes](https://www.thewealthadvisor.com/article/billionaire-leon-black-step-down-private-equity-giant-apollo-global-after-release-jeffrey)). *Forbes* estimated his net worth at $8.2 billion in 2021 and $13 billion in October 2025 (219th richest in the world), with Black retaining about 7 percent of Apollo's shares ([Forbes](https://www.thewealthadvisor.com/article/billionaire-leon-black-step-down-private-equity-giant-apollo-global-after-release-jeffrey); [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/world/2025/10/19/who-is-leon-black-billionaire-was-pressured-by-jeffrey-epstein-for-millions-in-cash-via-emails.html)).

Beyond finance, Black is a prominent art collector: in 2012 he paid $120 million for a pastel version of Edvard Munch's *The Scream*, and in 2018 he was installed as chairman of MoMA ([The Observer](https://observer.co.uk/culture/books/article/the-confessions-of-leon-black-the-billionaire-who-bankrolled-jeffrey-epstein)).
