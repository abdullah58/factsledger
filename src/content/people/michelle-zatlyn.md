## Overview

Michelle Zatlyn is the co-founder, president and co-chair of [Cloudflare](/organizations/cloudflare/), the internet-infrastructure company she started in 2009 with [Matthew Prince](/people/matthew-prince/) and [Lee Holloway](/people/lee-holloway/) ([Aspen Institute](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/people/michelle-zatlyn/)). Born in Prince Albert, Saskatchewan, she trained as a chemist, planned to become a doctor, and reached the American technology industry only after a failed work visa forced a detour through [Harvard Business School](/universities/harvard-business-school/) ([Carnegie Corporation of New York](https://carnegie.org/great-immigrants/michelle-zatlyn/)). Inside Cloudflare she has been the operator of the founding trio — running the business day to day while Prince led strategy — and she now shares the board chair with him ([Atlassian Corporation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037221000033/ex991.htm)).

## From chemistry to a visa detour

Zatlyn was born in Prince Albert, Saskatchewan, Canada, and first intended to become a doctor, earning a Bachelor of Science in chemistry from McGill University ([Carnegie Corporation of New York](https://carnegie.org/great-immigrants/michelle-zatlyn/)). Instead she fell in love with business — and then hit a wall: after securing a job she was unable to earn a work visa and did not move to the United States until she was later accepted to pursue an MBA at Harvard Business School ([Carnegie Corporation of New York](https://carnegie.org/great-immigrants/michelle-zatlyn/)). She graduated from McGill with distinction and from Harvard Business School in 2009, where she was awarded the Dubilier Prize for Entrepreneurship ([Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/profile/michelle-zatlyn/)).

Before Cloudflare she held positions at Google and Toshiba and launched two successful startups ([Aspen Institute](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/people/michelle-zatlyn/)). The detour shaped her public views on immigration and hiring: discussing the visa challenges she overcame, she told Forbes that "diversity is ideal when solving hard problems because people who are different look at problems in different ways… Combine that with trust and a shared vision and you're likely to have success" ([Carnegie Corporation of New York](https://carnegie.org/great-immigrants/michelle-zatlyn/)).

## Building Cloudflare

At Harvard Business School in 2009, Prince described [Project Honey Pot](/organizations/project-honey-pot/) to Zatlyn on a class trip to [Silicon Valley](/places/silicon-valley/); she immediately recognised the opportunity to stop internet threats rather than only track them, and that evening the two sketched Cloudflare's business model on a napkin, recruited professor Eisenmann as an advisor, and enlisted Holloway to build the prototype ([Harvard Business School Club of New York](https://www.hbscny.org/events/register-virtual-how-cloudflareas-founders-matthew-prince-hbs-a09-and-michelle-zatlyn-hbs-a09-are-helping-to-build-a-better-internet/)). They won the Harvard Business School Business Plan Competition that year, and Cloudflare launched publicly on 27 September 2010 at TechCrunch Disrupt in [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) ([Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com/our-story/)).

Zatlyn has led Cloudflare since its inception, overseeing operations as the company grew from a startup into a public company that listed on the [New York Stock Exchange](/organizations/new-york-stock-exchange/) in September 2019 under the ticker NET ([Atlassian Corporation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037221000033/ex991.htm)). Prince credits her with bringing discipline to the founders: "Had it just been me and Lee, we'd still be in the small office over the nail salon in Palo Alto, California, talking about all the amazing things we could do" ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90587133/exclusive-cloudflare-promotes-michelle-zatlyn-to-president-a-gain-for-women-in-tech)). At the IPO she owned 6.8% of Cloudflare's Class B shares and 5.6% of all shares outstanding ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/08/15/cloudflare-has-a-third-cofounder-lee-holloway-whos-credited-with-making-the-company-what-is-today/)).

Her titles trace the company's growth:

| When | Title |
| --- | --- |
| 2009 | Co-founder |
| December 2020 | President — added to co-founder and chief operating officer, with sales, customer support, special projects, communications and human resources under her remit |
| September 2021 | Director, Atlassian |
| February 2025 | Co-chair of the board, alongside Prince; title now president and co-chair |

The December 2020 promotion, disclosed in an SEC 8-K, put her in a small group of women serving as president at a publicly traded technology company ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90587133/exclusive-cloudflare-promotes-michelle-zatlyn-to-president-a-gain-for-women-in-tech)). She also led the company's coronavirus response that year ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90587133/exclusive-cloudflare-promotes-michelle-zatlyn-to-president-a-gain-for-women-in-tech)). In February 2025 she was appointed co-chair of the board alongside Prince; Cloudflare's Form S-8 filed that month is signed by her as "Co-Chair" and by Prince as "Chief Executive Officer and Co-Chair" ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1477333/000147733325000045/cloudflares-82025.htm)). Her title is now president and co-chair — the chief operating officer title she carried for years no longer appears ([Aspen Institute](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/people/michelle-zatlyn/)).

## Boards and recognition

Beyond Cloudflare, Zatlyn was appointed to the board of directors of Atlassian on 9 September 2021 ([Atlassian Corporation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037221000033/ex991.htm)). She also serves on the board of the World Economic Forum Young Global Leaders Foundation, and previously served on the Computer History Museum's NextGen Advisory Board and the Federal Communications Commission's Open Internet Advisory Committee ([Atlassian Corporation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037221000033/ex991.htm)).

Her leadership has been recognised repeatedly: she has been named to Fortune's 40 Under 40, named a CNBC Changemaker, recognised as a Top Female Founder by Inc. magazine, and awarded C100's Icon of Canadian Entrepreneurship ([Aspen Institute](https://www.aspeninstitute.org/people/michelle-zatlyn/), [Carnegie Corporation of New York](https://carnegie.org/great-immigrants/michelle-zatlyn/)). The Computer History Museum also lists her among Inc.'s Top 15 Women to Watch in Tech and the San Francisco Business Times' 40 Under 40 ([Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/profile/michelle-zatlyn/)). By the figures in Atlassian's 2021 announcement, the company she helped build then blocked an average of 87 billion cyber threats a day for customers including about 19% of the Fortune 1,000; Carnegie put its reach more plainly in 2026, saying Cloudflare helps power and secure roughly 20 percent of the web ([Atlassian Corporation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037221000033/ex991.htm), [Carnegie Corporation of New York](https://carnegie.org/great-immigrants/michelle-zatlyn/)).

## FAQ

### Who is Michelle Zatlyn?

Michelle Zatlyn is the Canadian co-founder, president and co-chair of Cloudflare, the internet-infrastructure company she founded in 2009 with Matthew Prince and Lee Holloway. Born in Prince Albert, Saskatchewan, she studied chemistry at McGill University and earned an MBA from Harvard Business School.

### What does Michelle Zatlyn do at Cloudflare?

She is the company's president and co-chair, and has overseen its operations since its founding. She was chief operating officer for years, was named president in December 2020, and became co-chair of the board in February 2025, when the chief operating officer title was dropped.

### Is Michelle Zatlyn on Atlassian's board?

Yes. Atlassian appointed her to its board of directors on 9 September 2021. She also serves on the board of the World Economic Forum Young Global Leaders Foundation.

### Why did Michelle Zatlyn switch from medicine to business?

She planned to become a doctor and studied chemistry at McGill for that path, but fell in love with business instead. A failed US work visa then delayed her move to America until Harvard Business School admitted her for an MBA — where she met her Cloudflare co-founder.
