Sherrod Brown's political life has a shape most biographies skip: it began with an astronaut handing him a badge in Mansfield, Ohio, and ended, for the moment, with the cryptocurrency industry paying tens of millions to unseat him. He spent 18 years in the U.S. Senate as the face of Midwestern progressive populism, lost his seat in one of the most expensive races in American history, and then came back for another one.

## The Glenn connection

On July 18, 2011, with John Glenn turning 90, Brown took the Senate floor and told a story that barely appears in any profile of him. "I was 16 years old when John Glenn presented to me and another couple dozen Eagle Scouts in Mansfield, OH, our Eagle Scout Award, teaching us yet again about community service and community pride," he said ([Congressional Record](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-157/issue-107/senate-section/article/S4634-2)).

That was only the middle of the arc. Brown recalled that when he decided to run for the Senate in the fall of 2005, "the first people we called were Annie and John Glenn." And then: "When I was 54, in one of the most memorable moments of my professional life — with John's wife Annie and my wife Connie in the gallery — John Glenn escorted me into this Senate Chamber to be sworn in as a Senator from Ohio" ([Congressional Record](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-157/issue-107/senate-section/article/S4634-2)). The same man who pinned the badge on a teenager in Mansfield walked him into the Senate four decades later.

## Mansfield roots

Brown was born November 9, 1952, in Mansfield, Richland County, Ohio ([Biographical Directory of the U.S. Congress](https://bioguideretro.congress.gov/Home/MemberDetails?memIndex=b000944)). His father Charles was a physician and his mother Emily a civic activist who led the Mansfield YWCA and helped found the Mansfield-area Habitat for Humanity and the Ohio Hunger Task Force ([Akron Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/2019/01/31/sherrod-brown-classmates-remember-activist-even-high-school/2655800002/)).

The activism started early. As a senior at Mansfield Senior in 1970, Brown and two friends organized a rally for the first Earth Day; about 1,000 students marched down Park Avenue West. He was student council president, and classmate Dave Busler recalled that Brown used the post to protest the Vietnam War and push for black studies — "He pissed some people off," Busler said ([Akron Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/2019/01/31/sherrod-brown-classmates-remember-activist-even-high-school/2655800002/)).

After earning a degree in Russian studies from Yale University in 1974, Brown upset an incumbent to become the youngest state representative in Ohio history — he was 22 ([Akron Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/2019/01/31/sherrod-brown-classmates-remember-activist-even-high-school/2655800002/)). He also picked up a master's degree from Ohio State University in 1981 and taught at OSU's Mansfield branch from 1979 to 1981 ([Biographical Directory of the U.S. Congress](https://bioguideretro.congress.gov/Home/MemberDetails?memIndex=b000944)).

## Three decades of office-holding

Brown served in the Ohio House from 1975 to 1982 and as Ohio Secretary of State from 1983 to 1991. He was elected to the U.S. House for Ohio's 13th district in 1992, serving from January 1993 to January 2007 — seven terms. In 2006 he defeated two-term Republican incumbent Mike DeWine for the Senate, was reelected in 2012 and 2018, and served from January 3, 2007, to January 3, 2025 ([Biographical Directory of the U.S. Congress](https://bioguideretro.congress.gov/Home/MemberDetails?memIndex=b000944)).

In the Senate he became a fixture of the "workers first" wing: a critic of free-trade deals and, in the 117th and 118th Congresses, chairman of the Committee on Banking, Housing and Urban Affairs ([Biographical Directory of the U.S. Congress](https://bioguideretro.congress.gov/Home/MemberDetails?memIndex=b000944)). He is also an author — *Congress from the Inside* (1999), *Myths of Free Trade* (2004), and *Desk 88: Eight Progressive Senators Who Changed America* (2019) ([Biographical Directory of the U.S. Congress](https://bioguideretro.congress.gov/Home/MemberDetails?memIndex=b000944)).

## The 2024 loss — and crypto's role

Brown's reelection bid in 2024 ran headlong into the industry he had frustrated as Banking chairman. In one of the hardest-fought and most expensive Senate races ever waged, he lost to Republican businessman Bernie Moreno by nearly four points ([Roll Call](https://d33gy59ovltp76.cloudfront.net/news/sherrod-brown-launches-senate-comeback-bid-in-ohio-roll-call)). Crypto-industry coverage documented the scale: Defend American Jobs, the Republican-aligned affiliate of the Fairshake super PAC, spent about $40.1 million supporting Moreno in Ohio ([AltcoinBuzz](https://www.altcoinbuzz.io/sherrod-brown-returns-to-a-senate-seat-crypto-spent-40m-to-flip)).

The industry did not hide its satisfaction. Senate Banking chair Tim Scott said at the Wyoming Blockchain Symposium: "Thank you to all of y'all for getting rid of Sherrod Brown. Literally, the industry put Bernie Moreno in the Senate" — a quote crypto press reported bluntly ([AltcoinBuzz](https://www.altcoinbuzz.io/sherrod-brown-returns-to-a-senate-seat-crypto-spent-40m-to-flip)). Stand With Crypto, a Coinbase-backed advocacy group, labels Brown "strongly against crypto" ([AltcoinBuzz](https://www.altcoinbuzz.io/sherrod-brown-returns-to-a-senate-seat-crypto-spent-40m-to-flip)).

## The 2026 comeback

Brown had said he had no plans to return to office. Then, in an August 18, 2025 video announcement, he reversed course: "Standing up for workers. Treating everyone with dignity and respect. Working as hard as possible for the people of Ohio. I've tried to live my life by those principles. But these days that's not what's happening in Washington" ([Roll Call](https://d33gy59ovltp76.cloudfront.net/news/sherrod-brown-launches-senate-comeback-bid-in-ohio-roll-call)).

His target is the seat [J.D. Vance](/people/jd-vance/) vacated to become vice president. Gov. Mike DeWine appointed Lt. Gov. Jon Husted to it in January 2025, and the November 3, 2026 special election will decide who serves the remainder of Vance's term — meaning the winner runs again in 2028 ([WGHN](https://wghn.com/2025/08/18/sherrod-brown-launches-senate-comeback-bid-in-ohio-for-2026/)). Brown won the Democratic primary in March 2026 and now faces the appointed incumbent.

Forecasters took notice: Inside Elections shifted its rating from Solid Republican to Lean Republican after Brown entered ([Roll Call](https://d33gy59ovltp76.cloudfront.net/news/sherrod-brown-launches-senate-comeback-bid-in-ohio-roll-call)). On the trail, Brown has leaned on pocketbook attacks — at a September 29, 2026 Fremont town hall he framed Husted as the culprit behind tariffs and inflation, claiming fertilizer prices have doubled and blaming Husted's support for the war in Iran for diesel spikes, before turning to rural health care and Medicaid cuts before about 160 attendees ([Fremont News-Messenger](https://www.thenews-messenger.com/story/news/local/2026/09/29/sherrod-brown-attacks-husted-tariffs-inflation/91993857007/)).

The [U.S. Senate](/organizations/us-senate/) he hopes to rejoin has 53 Republicans. Democrats would need to flip four seats to retake the chamber — an uphill climb, but Brown's candidacy is one of the party's brightest spots ([Roll Call](https://d33gy59ovltp76.cloudfront.net/news/sherrod-brown-launches-senate-comeback-bid-in-ohio-roll-call)).
