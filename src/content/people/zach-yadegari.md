## Overview

Zach Yadegari is a technology entrepreneur who co-founded Cal AI, an AI-powered calorie-tracking app, while still a high-school senior. The app lets users photograph a meal and uses image recognition plus the phone's depth sensor to estimate calories, protein, carbohydrates and fat — no manual logging required ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/24/techcrunch-disrupt-2026-cal-ais-zach-yadegari-on-how-to-create-viral-growth-and-capitalize-on-it/)). In under two years from its May 2024 launch, Cal AI passed 15 million downloads and was generating tens of millions of dollars in annual revenue, before MyFitnessPal acquired it in a deal that closed in December 2025 — all before Yadegari's 20th birthday ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/24/techcrunch-disrupt-2026-cal-ais-zach-yadegari-on-how-to-create-viral-growth-and-capitalize-on-it/)). It was his second exit: at 16 he had already sold Totally Science, a gaming website popular with students, for a reported $100,000 ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/he-built-an-ai-app-in-high-school-made-40m-and-sold-to-myfitnesspal-now-hes-aiming-even-bigger/91307748?ref=arwriter.ai)). At 19, he is building Flow, a health-and-productivity brand whose first product is a hardware alarm clock ([Inc.](http://www.inc.com/brian-contreras/teenage-founder-startup-exit-cal-ai-flow-alarm-clock/91358150)).

## Coding at seven, selling at sixteen

Yadegari's start in technology came almost by accident: at seven, a coding camp his mother sent him to sparked his interest, and he taught himself the rest by binge-watching YouTube tutorials ([VnExpress](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/tech/personalities/how-18-year-old-ceo-rejected-by-ivy-league-schools-built-30m-ai-health-app-4951664.html)). By ten he was charging $30 an hour to teach coding to other kids, scaling from one tutee to three via an ad in a local Facebook group — "for a kid at 10 years old, I felt pretty rich," he told *Forbes* ([VnExpress](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/tech/personalities/how-18-year-old-ceo-rejected-by-ivy-league-schools-built-30m-ai-health-app-4951664.html)). He built his first app at twelve, and watching *The Social Network*, the film about Mark Zuckerberg's rise, gave him his ambition: "I watched that movie and thought, I want to do that" ([Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/life/technology-he-coded-at-7-drove-a-lamborghini-at-18-sold-his-ai-startup-at-19-now-building-an-alarm-clock-4265912/)).

His first real business arrived with the COVID-19 pandemic. Noticing schools handing out Chromebooks for remote learning, he built Totally Science, a website collecting popular games in one place that students could play unblocked at school, without downloads or registration ([VnExpress](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/tech/personalities/how-18-year-old-ceo-rejected-by-ivy-league-schools-built-30m-ai-health-app-4951664.html)). The site drew more than five million users and earned him thousands of dollars a year through Google AdSense for two years ([Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/life/technology-he-coded-at-7-drove-a-lamborghini-at-18-sold-his-ai-startup-at-19-now-building-an-alarm-clock-4265912/)). In January 2024 he sold it to FreezeNova for $100,000 ([Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/life/technology-he-coded-at-7-drove-a-lamborghini-at-18-sold-his-ai-startup-at-19-now-building-an-alarm-clock-4265912/)).

## Cal AI: a photo instead of a food diary

After Totally Science, Yadegari teamed up with Henry Langmack, a fellow coder he had met at a camp, to try consumer apps. Their first project, Grind Clock — a motivational alarm clock inspired by David Goggins — hit 20,000 downloads in its first two weeks but soon lost momentum ([VnExpress](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/tech/personalities/how-18-year-old-ceo-rejected-by-ivy-league-schools-built-30m-ai-health-app-4951664.html)). Determined to improve, Yadegari recruited Blake Anderson, a developer with two hit apps already behind him, who brought funding and experience and joined as a co-founder of what became Cal AI ([VnExpress](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/tech/personalities/how-18-year-old-ceo-rejected-by-ivy-league-schools-built-30m-ai-health-app-4951664.html)).

The idea came from Yadegari's own frustration. As a skinny teenager trying to gain weight, he realized most of his progress came from diet rather than exercise — but the leading calorie-tracking app he tried was unreliable, and he ended up avoiding restaurants and the school cafeteria because he couldn't estimate calories accurately ([VnExpress](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/tech/personalities/how-18-year-old-ceo-rejected-by-ivy-league-schools-built-30m-ai-health-app-4951664.html)). "I had struggled with calorie tracking in my past," he told *Inc.* "I wanted to build a better calorie tracking app, something that's at least better for my life" ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/he-built-an-ai-app-in-high-school-made-40m-and-sold-to-myfitnesspal-now-hes-aiming-even-bigger/91307748?ref=arwriter.ai)). He and his co-founder built the app whenever they could — even during high-school classes ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/he-built-an-ai-app-in-high-school-made-40m-and-sold-to-myfitnesspal-now-hes-aiming-even-bigger/91307748?ref=arwriter.ai)).

Cal AI launched in May 2024 ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/education/news/who-is-zach-yadegari-an-18-year-old-rejected-by-ivy-leagues-now-the-founder-of-a-million-dollar-ai-empire/articleshow/124515308.cms)). Its growth, Yadegari says, came largely from paid acquisition rather than word of mouth: campaigns with nutrition and fitness influencers plus heavy performance advertising on Facebook, TikTok and [Instagram](/organizations/instagram/), with marketing spend rising in lockstep with revenue ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/he-built-an-ai-app-in-high-school-made-40m-and-sold-to-myfitnesspal-now-hes-aiming-even-bigger/91307748?ref=arwriter.ai)). The numbers followed: more than 15 million downloads and over $30 million in annual revenue in under two years, according to MyFitnessPal ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/myfitnesspal-has-acquired-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app-built-by-teens/)). Yadegari himself put the trailing figure higher, telling *Inc.* the company earned $40 million over the twelve months ending in March 2026 and was on track for $50 million through 2026 ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/he-built-an-ai-app-in-high-school-made-40m-and-sold-to-myfitnesspal-now-hes-aiming-even-bigger/91307748?ref=arwriter.ai)). *Fortune* reviewed financial records showing monthly revenue of several million dollars when Yadegari was 18 ([VnExpress](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/tech/personalities/how-18-year-old-ceo-rejected-by-ivy-league-schools-built-30m-ai-health-app-4951664.html)).

## The MyFitnessPal acquisition

MyFitnessPal had Cal AI on its radar for a long time. CEO Mike Fisher said the company watches roughly 70 competitors large and small, and first noticed Cal AI climbing the app-store rankings via tools like Sensor Tower; deal talks then ran on and off for almost a year ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/myfitnesspal-has-acquired-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app-built-by-teens/)). What sealed it, Fisher said, was meeting the founders: "You have a conversation with them, like I did late spring last year, and you walk away saying this is an impressive young man" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/myfitnesspal-has-acquired-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app-built-by-teens/)). He was struck by small details — like the team holding its weekly standup on Sunday nights, because the founders were still in school ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/myfitnesspal-acquires-cal-ai-teen-founders/)).

The deal closed in December 2025 and was announced on March 2, 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/myfitnesspal-has-acquired-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app-built-by-teens/)). MyFitnessPal retained the whole Cal AI team and kept the app independent rather than folding it into its flagship product: Cal AI serves users who want speed over precision, while MyFitnessPal serves those who want to fine-tune every input ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/myfitnesspal-has-acquired-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app-built-by-teens/)). Cal AI did gain one upgrade — integration with MyFitnessPal's nutrition database of 20 million foods, 68,500 brands and meals at more than 380 restaurant chains ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/myfitnesspal-acquires-cal-ai-teen-founders/)). The price was never disclosed; Fisher said only that the Cal AI team didn't have to sell and was happy with the offer ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/02/myfitnesspal-has-acquired-cal-ai-the-viral-calorie-app-built-by-teens/)). Yadegari stayed on for what his PR representative called "a transitional period to ensure a seamless handoff" ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/he-built-an-ai-app-in-high-school-made-40m-and-sold-to-myfitnesspal-now-hes-aiming-even-bigger/91307748?ref=arwriter.ai)).

## Rejected by fifteen colleges, then a new act

In March 2025, Yadegari posted on X that he had applied to 18 universities and been accepted to only three — rejected by 15, including Stanford, MIT, Harvard, Yale, Columbia and Princeton — despite a 4.0 GPA and a 34 ACT score ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/he-built-an-ai-app-in-high-school-made-40m-and-sold-to-myfitnesspal-now-hes-aiming-even-bigger/91307748?ref=arwriter.ai); [Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/education/news/who-is-zach-yadegari-an-18-year-old-rejected-by-ivy-leagues-now-the-founder-of-a-million-dollar-ai-empire/articleshow/124515308.cms)). "I think that entrepreneurial accomplishments may not be fully appreciated," he told the *New York Post* ([VnExpress](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/tech/personalities/how-18-year-old-ceo-rejected-by-ivy-league-schools-built-30m-ai-health-app-4951664.html)). Georgia Tech, the University of Miami and the University of Texas offered him places; he chose Miami for its social environment over prestige ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/education/news/who-is-zach-yadegari-an-18-year-old-rejected-by-ivy-leagues-now-the-founder-of-a-million-dollar-ai-empire/articleshow/124515308.cms)). He had once said he didn't want to be "the archetypal dropout founder," but by early 2026 his view had shifted: "I'm more ambitious, I'm more f**king hungry than I've ever been. I need to drop out, and I need to build a team in person, and build a billion-dollar company" ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/he-built-an-ai-app-in-high-school-made-40m-and-sold-to-myfitnesspal-now-hes-aiming-even-bigger/91307748?ref=arwriter.ai)). After half a semester he took a gap term and was weighing leaving college entirely ([Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/life/technology-he-coded-at-7-drove-a-lamborghini-at-18-sold-his-ai-startup-at-19-now-building-an-alarm-clock-4265912/)).

His third venture is Flow, a brand for health-and-productivity products. Its first release, the Flow Alarm Clock (March 2026), pairs a physical dock — the sleeper must get up and tap the phone against it to silence the alarm — with a companion app that blocks selected apps until the alarm is disengaged, plus sleep-pattern tracking ([Inc.](http://www.inc.com/brian-contreras/teenage-founder-startup-exit-cal-ai-flow-alarm-clock/91358150)). The motivation was personal: "I sometimes wake up, and I scroll on my phone for 30 minutes" ([Inc.](http://www.inc.com/brian-contreras/teenage-founder-startup-exit-cal-ai-flow-alarm-clock/91358150)). On September 24, 2026, TechCrunch named him to its Disrupt 2026 Builders Stage lineup, where he is scheduled to give a fireside chat titled "How to Create Viral Growth and Capitalize On It" at the October 13–15 event in San Francisco ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/24/techcrunch-disrupt-2026-cal-ais-zach-yadegari-on-how-to-create-viral-growth-and-capitalize-on-it/)).
