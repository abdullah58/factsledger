## Overview

Fawn Johnson is an American journalist who works as a News Director at [Bloomberg Industry Group](/organizations/bloomberg/), where she oversees newsroom training and development and helps formulate tools and policies around the use of artificial intelligence ([Bloomberg Industry Group](https://news.bloombergtax.com/author/fawn-johnson-20100356)). Her career traces a path through Washington's major policy newsrooms: she first covered Congress at BNA Inc., then reported for CongressDaily, Dow Jones Newswires and [The Wall Street Journal](/organizations/wall-street-journal/) before becoming a National Journal correspondent, and later served as chief policy editor at Morning Consult ([National Education Policy Center](https://nepc.colorado.edu/author/johnson-fawn) [Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2010/national-journal-takes-two-reporters-from-wsj/) [Kappan Online](https://kappanonline.org/fawn-johnsons-inside-view-of-education-politics/)). She won First Prize in the Education Writers Association's 2011 National Awards for Education Reporting for a National Journal piece titled "Report Card" ([Education Writers Association](https://ewa.org/members-news/awards/2011-winners-of-the-national-awards-for-education-reporting)). She holds a B.A. from Bates College and an M.A. from the Annenberg School for Communication at the [University of Pennsylvania](/universities/university-of-pennsylvania/) ([National Education Policy Center](https://nepc.colorado.edu/author/johnson-fawn)).

## From BNA to the Wall Street Journal

Johnson first covered Congress at BNA Inc., where her beats included labor, welfare, immigration and asbestos liability ([National Education Policy Center](https://nepc.colorado.edu/author/johnson-fawn)). She moved to CongressDaily, a National Journal Group publication, where she covered health care, labor and immigration ([National Education Policy Center](https://nepc.colorado.edu/author/johnson-fawn)). She then reported for Dow Jones Newswires and The Wall Street Journal, covering financial regulation and telecommunications ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2010/national-journal-takes-two-reporters-from-wsj/)).

## National Journal and education reporting

On August 12, 2010, the National Journal Group announced that Johnson would join as a general assignment correspondent covering transportation and education, as part of a newsroom relaunch ahead of a new website ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2010/national-journal-takes-two-reporters-from-wsj/)). Editor-in-chief Ron Fournier described her and fellow hire Susan Davis as "wicked smart and deeply sourced journalists" ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2010/national-journal-takes-two-reporters-from-wsj/)). At National Journal she took over the education beat from Eliza Krigman in 2010, though it was never her main assignment. "Basically, I was a general assignment reporter," she said in an interview; colleagues held the "money" beats such as defense, and "I got what was left" ([Kappan Online](https://kappanonline.org/fawn-johnsons-inside-view-of-education-politics/)).

She made education coverage work by anchoring stories in a striking local example with national resonance, such as a 2014 Common Core story reported from Alabama ([Kappan Online](https://kappanonline.org/fawn-johnsons-inside-view-of-education-politics/)). She also ran an education blog at National Journal, where she wrote candidly about developments such as the Atlanta cheating convictions, and she moderated a 2012 Education Writers Association panel titled "What's In Store for Common Core?" ([Kappan Online](https://kappanonline.org/fawn-johnsons-inside-view-of-education-politics/)). Her work was picked up "pretty frequently" by sister publication The Atlantic, according to the profile's author ([Kappan Online](https://kappanonline.org/fawn-johnsons-inside-view-of-education-politics/)).

The piece she called her proudest was a retrospective in which she went back to the authors of the No Child Left Behind Act and asked them to reflect on what did and didn't work. "You can really see how difficult it is to legislate this stuff," she said. "They really tried and they got only some of it" ([Kappan Online](https://kappanonline.org/fawn-johnsons-inside-view-of-education-politics/)). The Education Writers Association record shows her 2011 First Prize — in the Print, Small Market, Single-Day News Coverage or Feature category — went to a National Journal entry titled "Report Card" ([Education Writers Association](https://ewa.org/members-news/awards/2011-winners-of-the-national-awards-for-education-reporting)). That year's contest drew 340 submissions with 61 winners, announced at the association's Philadelphia conference ([Education Writers Association](https://ewa.org/members-news/awards/2011-winners-of-the-national-awards-for-education-reporting)). Whether the No Child Left Behind retrospective and "Report Card" are the same piece could not be confirmed from the sources read.

In July 2015 she left National Journal to become chief policy editor at Morning Consult, while continuing to write about education from the new position ([Kappan Online](https://kappanonline.org/fawn-johnsons-inside-view-of-education-politics/)). A Cision media note dated July 28, 2015 reported she would join Morning Consult on August 3 to cover policy on Capitol Hill, noting she had covered Congress for over a decade, including at the Wall Street Journal, Dow Jones Newswires and Bloomberg BNA ([Cision](https://stage.cision.com/us/2015/07/fawn-johnson-in-at-morning-consult/)).

## Newsroom leadership at Bloomberg

Johnson is now a News Director at Bloomberg Industry Group, overseeing newsroom training and development and formulating tools and policies around artificial intelligence ([Bloomberg Industry Group](https://news.bloombergtax.com/author/fawn-johnson-20100356)). Before becoming an editor, she spent more than a decade as a reporter covering Congress and federal agencies for CongressDaily, the Wall Street Journal and National Journal, and Bloomberg describes her as "an expert in the inner workings of government" ([Bloomberg Industry Group](https://news.bloombergtax.com/author/fawn-johnson-20100356)). Her past beats, according to Bloomberg, include immigration, employment law, health policy, telecommunications and financial services ([Bloomberg Industry Group](https://news.bloombergtax.com/author/fawn-johnson-20100356)).
