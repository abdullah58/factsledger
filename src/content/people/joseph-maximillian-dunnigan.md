## Overview

Joseph Maximillian Dunnigan is a [Scottish](/places/scotland/) social entrepreneur based in [Tallinn](/places/tallinn/), [Estonia](/places/estonia/), who founded the [Banned Books Museum](/organizations/banned-books-museum/) — a small museum of censored, banned and burned books — and opened it on 5 December 2020 after studying social entrepreneurship at [Tallinn University](/universities/tallinn-university/) ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/banned-books-museum/); [Clumsy Girl Travels](https://clumsygirltravels.com/museums-in-tallinn/)). He is the museum's founder, spokesperson and leader, and the registered non-profit behind it, Banned Books MTÜ, lists him as a management board member since its registration on 28 August 2020 ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/banned-books-museum/); [Estonian Business Register](https://ariregister.rik.ee/eng/company/80584386/BANNED-BOOKS-MT%C3%9C)).

The museum sits in Tallinn's Old Town, is free to visit, and preserves banned, censored and burned books from around the world, with visitors able to pick up and read the physical copies ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/banned-books-museum/); [Clumsy Girl Travels](https://clumsygirltravels.com/museums-in-tallinn/); [The Voyages of Tim Vetter](https://www.listennotes.com/podcasts/the-voyages-of-tim/episode-297-banned-books-RaCY_JIW8BO/)). Dunnigan runs it as a social enterprise without government funding or large grants, a model he has described publicly in Tallinn University's Law and Society Blog ([Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/yti/mediahub/blogs/realities-running-social-enterprise-joe-dunnigan-founder-banned-books-museum)). The registry record ties the personal story to a verifiable legal fact: the association's beneficial-owner entry records the country of his personal identification code as the United Kingdom and his country of residence as Estonia ([Estonian Business Register](https://ariregister.rik.ee/eng/company/80584386/BANNED-BOOKS-MT%C3%9C)).

## From Scotland to China to Tallinn

Dunnigan is originally from Scotland and had been living in Estonia for several years by 2025, but the decisive part of his story happened in between, in [China](/places/china/) ([Clumsy Girl Travels](https://clumsygirltravels.com/museums-in-tallinn/)). His interest in censorship developed while he was living in China, and it was during his master's studies in social entrepreneurship that he decided to create the museum ([Clumsy Girl Travels](https://clumsygirltravels.com/museums-in-tallinn/)). Tallinn University confirms the academic half of that account, describing him as a student of its Social Entrepreneurship Master's Programme (SEMA) ([Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/yti/mediahub/blogs/realities-running-social-enterprise-joe-dunnigan-founder-banned-books-museum)).

He chose Estonia deliberately. As he puts it, "here we can present books that are still banned in so many big countries" ([Clumsy Girl Travels](https://clumsygirltravels.com/museums-in-tallinn/)). The choice of a small, free-speech-conscious Baltic state as the home for a collection of books banned elsewhere is the thread that connects his time in China to the museum in Tallinn: he had seen censorship at first hand, studied how to build a mission-driven venture, and then placed the result where the banned books themselves could be shown ([Clumsy Girl Travels](https://clumsygirltravels.com/museums-in-tallinn/); [Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/yti/mediahub/blogs/realities-running-social-enterprise-joe-dunnigan-founder-banned-books-museum)). No source read for this profile states where in Scotland he is from, when he lived in China, or what he did there, so none of that is claimed here.

## Founding the Banned Books Museum

Dunnigan opened the museum on 5 December 2020 in Tallinn's Old Town, a few months after registering Banned Books MTÜ as a non-profit association on 28 August 2020 ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/banned-books-museum/); [Estonian Business Register](https://ariregister.rik.ee/eng/company/80584386/BANNED-BOOKS-MT%C3%9C)). The [Estonian Business Register](/organizations/estonian-business-register/) lists him as a management board member from the registration date, alongside a second board member, Urve Leemets, who joined on the same day, and every board member may represent the association in all transactions ([Estonian Business Register](https://ariregister.rik.ee/eng/company/80584386/BANNED-BOOKS-MT%C3%9C)). The museum is part museum and part bookstore, for books that have been banned, censored, restricted or burned, and it also runs a book club, a podcast and discussion groups ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/banned-books-museum/)).

Dunnigan has described the museum's purpose in his own words: "We're trying to make people think about the concept of free speech and about the free exchange of ideas. This is a place that you can come and learn about these kinds of things and you can contemplate these kinds of questions. I really feel very strongly that there should be a place in the world like, some small corner of the world, some tiny little place where these ideas can be preserved" ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/banned-books-museum/)). The museum's stated aim is to preserve "ideas, conversations, discussions, and thoughts", with books as "a direct connection to the mind of the author, and the crystalized ideas they deemed worthy of sharing" ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/banned-books-museum/)). Its programme puts that into practice: the Estonian Museum Card describes the museum's aim as equipping people "with the ability to recognize literary censorship and thereby elevate public discourse on censorship, propaganda, and freedom of speech", and notes that it has received book donations from persecuted writers ([Estonian Museum Card](https://muuseumikaart.ee/en/museums/the-banned-books-museum/)).

At opening, the collection held over 100 books organised by country — including sections for Estonia, Russia, the UK, China and the USA — plus sections for children's books, books about censorship and books that were burned; titles on the shelves included *Lord of the Flies*, *The Handmaid's Tale* and *The Hate U Give* ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/banned-books-museum/)). By 2024 the Estonian Museum Card put the collection at 350 books from 100 different countries, each displayed with explanatory text about the context of its banning, with a further "secret section" visible only to Museum Card holders ([Estonian Museum Card](https://muuseumikaart.ee/en/museums/the-banned-books-museum/)). Visitors are explicitly encouraged to engage with material they may personally disagree with or find offensive, and the most controversial titles, such as *The Anarchist Cookbook*, are kept under the counter and shown on request ([Estonian Museum Card](https://muuseumikaart.ee/en/museums/the-banned-books-museum/); [Clumsy Girl Travels](https://clumsygirltravels.com/museums-in-tallinn/)).

## Running it as a social enterprise

Dunnigan is unusually open about the business reality behind the museum. He describes it as a mission-driven business that does not rely on government funding or large grants, and his advice to aspiring social entrepreneurs is blunt: "Nobody is going to help you. You're not going to get a phone call telling you, 'Hey, we love what you're doing, here's money for your salaries.' It's just not going to happen" ([Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/yti/mediahub/blogs/realities-running-social-enterprise-joe-dunnigan-founder-banned-books-museum)). Social entrepreneurs, in his view, "must find their own path to financial sustainability", thinking through the business model from the start rather than assuming grants or EU funding will fill the gaps ([Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/yti/mediahub/blogs/realities-running-social-enterprise-joe-dunnigan-founder-banned-books-museum)).

The registry's financial record shows that path working, slowly. Banned Books MTÜ filed valid annual reports for every year from 2021 to 2025, with revenue rising from €2,543 in 2021 to €19,836 in 2025, and the association turning its first profit, €986, in 2024 after three loss-making years, rising to €2,149 in 2025 ([Estonian Business Register](https://ariregister.rik.ee/eng/company/80584386/BANNED-BOOKS-MT%C3%9C)). The stat matters because it corroborates Dunnigan's own account: a small, self-funded venture growing its earned income year by year rather than living on grants ([Estonian Business Register](https://ariregister.rik.ee/eng/company/80584386/BANNED-BOOKS-MT%C3%9C); [Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/yti/mediahub/blogs/realities-running-social-enterprise-joe-dunnigan-founder-banned-books-museum)). His operating advice follows the same listener-first logic: "All you have to do is listen to people. They will tell you what they're going to pay for", and for anyone starting out in Estonia, "ask for what you want" — "People respond well to honesty and frankness. It's a little trick I found in Estonia" ([Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/yti/mediahub/blogs/realities-running-social-enterprise-joe-dunnigan-founder-banned-books-museum)). He is equally direct that social entrepreneurship is not for everyone, and that founders should be honest about whether their venture is a social enterprise, a grant-dependent non-profit or a corporate social responsibility project: "that's fine — just don't try to force it to be something else" ([Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/yti/mediahub/blogs/realities-running-social-enterprise-joe-dunnigan-founder-banned-books-museum)).

His public profile beyond the museum is small but consistent. In April 2023 he recorded an 82-minute interview at the museum for The Voyages of Tim Vetter podcast, covering the museum's history, his life and relationship to books, and censorship around the world ([The Voyages of Tim Vetter](https://www.listennotes.com/podcasts/the-voyages-of-tim/episode-297-banned-books-RaCY_JIW8BO/)). Nearly six years after opening, the museum is still operating — its 2025 annual report was submitted in July 2026 — and Dunnigan is still presented as its founder in Tallinn University's coverage ([Estonian Business Register](https://ariregister.rik.ee/eng/company/80584386/BANNED-BOOKS-MT%C3%9C); [Tallinn University](https://www.tlu.ee/en/yti/mediahub/blogs/realities-running-social-enterprise-joe-dunnigan-founder-banned-books-museum)).
