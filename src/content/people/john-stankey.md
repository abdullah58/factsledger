## Overview

John T. Stankey is the chairman and chief executive officer of [AT&T](/organizations/att/), the U.S. telecommunications giant he has served since 1985. Elected CEO effective July 1, 2020, and board chair in February 2025, Stankey rose through technology and operations roles — including CEO of WarnerMedia — and has defined his tenure by reversing AT&T's media strategy: spinning WarnerMedia into Warner Bros. Discovery and selling off DirecTV to refocus the company on 5G and fiber connectivity ([UCLA Anderson](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/news-and-events/position-success); [AT&T announcement](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200424005352/en/Randall-Stephenson-to-Retire-as-ATT-CEO-Serve-as-Executive-Chairman-of-the-Board-until-January-2021-ATT-Board-Elects-John-Stankey-as-CEO-and-Board-Member); [Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/at-t-names-ceo-john-stankey-as-board-chair-succeeding-william-kennard/)).

A first-generation college student raised in a Southern California suburb, Stankey earned a B.B.A. in finance from Loyola Marymount University and an M.B.A. from UCLA Anderson's class of 1991 ([UCLA Anderson](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/news-and-events/position-success)). His prior roles span chief technology officer, chief strategy officer, CEO of AT&T Operations and AT&T Business Solutions, and president and chief operating officer ([Business Roundtable](https://opportunity.businessroundtable.org/about-us/members/john-stankey)). Under his leadership AT&T reports more than $125 billion in 2025 revenues and more than 100 million U.S. wireless and broadband subscribers, and invested over $150 billion in its networks between 2021 and 2025 ([Business Roundtable](https://opportunity.businessroundtable.org/about-us/members/john-stankey)).

## Unwinding the media bets: WarnerMedia and DirecTV

Stankey inherited two large media bets — DirecTV, bought in 2015, and the WarnerMedia entertainment arm — and unwound both to concentrate on connectivity ([Mobile World Live](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/att/att-closes-directv-stake-sale-to-tpg-capital/); [Media Play News](https://www.mediaplaynews.com/discovery-warnermedia-complete-merger/)). In 2021 he spun DirecTV out with AT&T TV and U-verse into a venture with TPG Capital, valued at $16.3 billion ([Mobile World Live](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/att/att-closes-directv-stake-sale-to-tpg-capital/)). Then, in a deal struck in September 2024 and completed in July 2025, AT&T sold its remaining 70% DirecTV stake to TPG for $7.6 billion in expected cash payments — ending a holding bought for $49 billion ten years earlier ([Mobile World Live](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/att/att-closes-directv-stake-sale-to-tpg-capital/)).

The larger reversal was WarnerMedia. On April 8, 2022, AT&T completed a $43 billion Reverse Morris Trust merger combining WarnerMedia with Discovery Inc. to form Warner Bros. Discovery, led by Discovery chief David Zaslav and trading on Nasdaq from April 11 as "WBD" ([Media Play News](https://www.mediaplaynews.com/discovery-warnermedia-complete-merger/)). AT&T received $40.4 billion in cash, while AT&T shareholders got 71% of the new company — 1.7 billion shares, or 0.241917 WBD shares per AT&T share ([Media Play News](https://www.mediaplaynews.com/discovery-warnermedia-complete-merger/)). Stankey said the deals would cut debt and let AT&T focus on wireless, including 5G: "We are at the dawn of a new era in connectivity" ([Media Play News](https://www.mediaplaynews.com/discovery-warnermedia-complete-merger/)).

## The democracy-scorecard episode

In September 2022 the watchdog [Accountable.US](/organizations/accountable-us/) unveiled its American Democracy Scorecard, grading the top 100 U.S. corporations on 14 criteria — their pro-democracy statements, political-spending transparency, and PAC donations tied to voting-rights votes, 2020 election objections, and the January 6 commission ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression)). Two-thirds of the companies received failing marks, and AT&T was the lowest scorer, followed by Verizon, with Charter Communications and Comcast also in the bottom ten — making telecommunications the worst-ranked industry ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression)).

The scorecard's aggregates included $6.2 million to state legislators backing restrictive voting laws and $5.7 million to members of Congress who voted to overturn the 2020 election ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression)). Months earlier, Stankey had said after Georgia's voting law that "the right to vote is sacred" — yet AT&T's Texas PAC gave $100,000 to Governor Greg Abbott on the day he called a special session on voting limits ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression)). The group's then-president Kyle Herrig then wrote directly to Stankey urging him to "get your priorities straight" and align the company's actions with its stated values, while Accountable.US planned a six-figure digital campaign targeting executives, elected officials, and business-school students ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression)). None of the telecom companies responded to the outlet's request for comment ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression)).

## A 40-year company man — and how he says he runs it

Stankey is an AT&T lifer: he joined in 1985 — technically starting at Pacific Bell — and worked the Los Angeles cellular market during the late-1980s boom, before running technology, strategy, operations, and business units through to president and COO in October 2019 ([UCLA Anderson](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/news-and-events/position-success); [AT&T announcement](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200424005352/en/Randall-Stephenson-to-Retire-as-ATT-CEO-Serve-as-Executive-Chairman-of-the-Board-until-January-2021-ATT-Board-Elects-John-Stankey-as-CEO-and-Board-Member)). In February 2025 he was elected board chair, succeeding William Kennard, the former FCC chairman who became lead independent director ([Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/at-t-names-ceo-john-stankey-as-board-chair-succeeding-william-kennard/)).

His strategy is connectivity: AT&T reported 28.9 million fiber passings at the end of 2024 and plans 45 million by the end of 2029, plus 5 million more through Gigapower, its joint venture with BlackRock ([Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/at-t-names-ceo-john-stankey-as-board-chair-succeeding-william-kennard/)). Asked how he leads, Stankey calls "CEO" the vanity title — "the truth of the matter is, I'm the chief human resource officer" — comparing the job to "being the general manager of a sports team" and to a "provocateur or disruptor from within" ([UCLA Anderson](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/news-and-events/position-success)). He says the roughly $24 billion AT&T invests each year is allocated through lessons from UCLA professor Richard Rumelt's strategy class — second-order thinking and capital-markets theory he still uses daily ([UCLA Anderson](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/news-and-events/position-success)). In 2025 UCLA Anderson gave him its John E. Anderson Distinguished Alumni Award ([UCLA Anderson](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/news-and-events/position-success)).

## FAQ

**Who is John Stankey?**
John Stankey is the chairman and CEO of AT&T, one of the world's largest telecom companies. He joined AT&T in 1985, rose through technology and operations roles including CEO of WarnerMedia, became CEO on July 1, 2020, and was elected board chair in February 2025. He holds a finance degree from Loyola Marymount and an MBA from UCLA ([Business Roundtable](https://opportunity.businessroundtable.org/about-us/members/john-stankey); [UCLA Anderson](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/news-and-events/position-success)).

**When did John Stankey become CEO of AT&T?**
AT&T's board elected Stankey CEO effective July 1, 2020, succeeding Randall Stephenson after a succession search begun in 2017; he joined the board June 1, 2020. Stephenson stayed on as executive chairman until January 2021 to ensure a smooth transition, according to AT&T's announcement ([AT&T announcement](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200424005352/en/Randall-Stephenson-to-Retire-as-ATT-CEO-Serve-as-Executive-Chairman-of-the-Board-until-January-2021-ATT-Board-Elects-John-Stankey-as-CEO-and-Board-Member)).

**What did Stankey do with WarnerMedia and DirecTV?**
He reversed AT&T's media strategy. WarnerMedia was spun off and merged with Discovery Inc. on April 8, 2022, forming Warner Bros. Discovery in a $43 billion deal ([Media Play News](https://www.mediaplaynews.com/discovery-warnermedia-complete-merger/)). DirecTV was spun out with TPG Capital in 2021, and AT&T's remaining 70% stake was sold to TPG in a deal struck in September 2024 and closed in July 2025 ([Mobile World Live](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/att/att-closes-directv-stake-sale-to-tpg-capital/)).

**Why did AT&T score lowest on the 2022 American Democracy Scorecard?**
Accountable.US graded the top 100 U.S. corporations on 14 democracy criteria — statements, spending transparency, and PAC donations tied to voting-rights and election-certification votes — and AT&T ranked lowest, with two-thirds of companies failing. These are the watchdog's claims as reported by Capital & Main; the telecom companies did not respond to comment requests ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression)).

**What is AT&T's fiber target under Stankey?**
AT&T reported 28.9 million fiber passings at the end of 2024 and plans to reach 45 million by the end of 2029 — roughly 3 million new passings per year — plus another 5 million through Gigapower, its fiber joint venture with BlackRock. Fiber is the centerpiece of Stankey's connectivity-focused strategy ([Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/at-t-names-ceo-john-stankey-as-board-chair-succeeding-william-kennard/)).
