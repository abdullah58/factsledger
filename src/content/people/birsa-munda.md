**Birsa Munda** (15 November 1875 – 9 June 1900) was a Munda tribal leader from present-day [Jharkhand](/places/jharkhand/) who led the Ulgulan ("Great Tumult") of 1899–1900 against British colonial rule, demanding land rights and tribal self-rule ([DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/who-was-birsa-munda-whose-ulgulan-declared-the-end-of-british-rule-in-jharkhand/)). Arrested on 3 February 1900, he died in Ranchi jail on 9 June 1900 at about 25; colonial records attribute his death to cholera, while suspicions of foul play persist in popular memory ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-history/birsa-munda-legacy-ulgulan-adivasi-identity-explained-10733209/)).

His rebellion changed the law: the Chotanagpur Tenancy Act of 1908 restricted the transfer of Adivasi land to outsiders ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-history/birsa-munda-legacy-ulgulan-adivasi-identity-explained-10733209/)). He is revered as "Dharti Aba" (Father of the Earth), his birth anniversary is observed nationally as Janjatiya Gaurav Divas, and his story reaches children in Season 2 of [Krish, Trish and Baltiboy](/creative-works/krish-trish-and-baltiboy/) ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2073379&reg=3&lang=2); [The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/entertainment/second-season-of-animated-series-krish-trish-and-baltiboy-bharat-hain-hum-launched-at-iffi/)).

## Early life and the road to rebellion

Birsa Munda was born on 15 November 1875 in Ulihatu, a village in Jharkhand's Khunti district, into the Munda tribal community of the Chotanagpur plateau ([DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/who-was-birsa-munda-whose-ulgulan-declared-the-end-of-british-rule-in-jharkhand/)). His father Sugna Munda is believed to have been associated with the Sardari movement, an earlier Adivasi mobilisation against the erosion of traditional land rights ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-history/birsa-munda-legacy-ulgulan-adivasi-identity-explained-10733209/)). Family accounts say he studied at missionary schools in Chaibasa as "Daud" — recalled by some as "David" — until a disagreement with church authorities ended that association; he then came under Vaishnava influence through a local guru ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-history/birsa-munda-legacy-ulgulan-adivasi-identity-explained-10733209/)). A Ranchi Tribal Research Institute researcher says he grasped the erosion of tribal rights as a school student and was expelled in 1890 ([DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/who-was-birsa-munda-whose-ulgulan-declared-the-end-of-british-rule-in-jharkhand/)). The backdrop was the collapse of the Khuntkatti system of collective land stewardship as colonial revenue policy empowered outsider landlords, moneylenders and forced labour ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-history/birsa-munda-legacy-ulgulan-adivasi-identity-explained-10733209/)). He founded the Birsait faith, a reform movement distinct from Sarnaism, Christianity and Hinduism, and his followers revered him as "Bhagwan" and "Dharti Aba" — Father of the Earth ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-history/birsa-munda-legacy-ulgulan-adivasi-identity-explained-10733209/)).

## The Ulgulan: a tribal war for land and self-rule

By 1895 Birsa had become enough of a threat for the colonial state to act: arrested on 24 August 1895 at Chalkad, he was sentenced on 19 November 1895 to two years' rigorous imprisonment under Section 505 of the Indian Penal Code ([DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/who-was-birsa-munda-whose-ulgulan-declared-the-end-of-british-rule-in-jharkhand/)). Released in 1897, he declared the Ulgulan — "The Great Rebellion" — on 24 December 1899, asserting tribal rights over water, forests and land ([DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/who-was-birsa-munda-whose-ulgulan-declared-the-end-of-british-rule-in-jharkhand/)). Thousands of followers proclaimed "Diku Raj Tuntu Jana – Abua Raj Ete Jana": the rule of outsiders is over, our own rule has begun ([DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/who-was-birsa-munda-whose-ulgulan-declared-the-end-of-british-rule-in-jharkhand/)). The climax came on 9 January 1900 at Dombari Buru, a hill in a Khunti village, where thousands of Mundas gathered with bows and arrows; the British surrounded the hill with guns and cannon ([DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/who-was-birsa-munda-whose-ulgulan-declared-the-end-of-british-rule-in-jharkhand/)). The Statesman reported on 25 January 1900 that around 400 tribals died, the Tajna river running red ([DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/who-was-birsa-munda-whose-ulgulan-declared-the-end-of-british-rule-in-jharkhand/)). The Indian Express notes the gap between that record and Adivasi memory, in which the toll was far higher and Dombari Buru became a lasting symbol of Adivasi resistance ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-history/birsa-munda-legacy-ulgulan-adivasi-identity-explained-10733209/)).

## Arrest, trial and death at Ranchi

On the night of 3 February 1900 Birsa was arrested while sleeping in the forest and taken secretly to Ranchi ([DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/who-was-birsa-munda-whose-ulgulan-declared-the-end-of-british-rule-in-jharkhand/)). On 1 June 1900 the British authorities announced that he had contracted cholera; he died on 9 June, and the date is not in dispute — every full-read account agrees on it. The cause is. The Indian Express, working from the colonial record, reports death attributed to complications of cholera and chronic dysentery, with poisoning suspicions persisting in popular memory; the IANS account says he died "under mysterious circumstances", adding torture, a mock trial before Magistrate W.S. Kutus and a discarded body ([DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/who-was-birsa-munda-whose-ulgulan-declared-the-end-of-british-rule-in-jharkhand/); [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-history/birsa-munda-legacy-ulgulan-adivasi-identity-explained-10733209/)). This profile presents both rather than choosing.

## Aftermath: from rebellion to law

The rebellion was crushed, but the law changed. The Chotanagpur Tenancy (CNT) Act, 1908, was enacted after decades of Adivasi resistance to protect customary land rights in the Chotanagpur region; the ethnographer-missionary Father John-Baptist Hoffmann is widely regarded as one of the principal architects of the legal framework behind it ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-history/birsa-munda-legacy-ulgulan-adivasi-identity-explained-10733209/)). Its central objective was preventing the transfer of Adivasi land to non-Adivasis while recognising traditional tenure systems such as Khuntkatti and Bhuinhari; despite misuse, it remains one of the strongest legal safeguards against tribal land alienation in Jharkhand ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-history/birsa-munda-legacy-ulgulan-adivasi-identity-explained-10733209/)). The self-governance aspiration of the Ulgulan was later channelled into the movement for a separate Jharkhand under Jaipal Singh Munda in the mid-1900s ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-history/birsa-munda-legacy-ulgulan-adivasi-identity-explained-10733209/)). Around 400 tribals were killed at Dombari Buru on 9 January 1900, according to the Statesman report of 25 January 1900 cited by DD News — a toll that explains why the hill, rather than the courtroom where Birsa was tried, became the central symbol of his memory ([DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/who-was-birsa-munda-whose-ulgulan-declared-the-end-of-british-rule-in-jharkhand/); [Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-history/birsa-munda-legacy-ulgulan-adivasi-identity-explained-10733209/)).

## Commemoration: museum, national day, and the screen

National recognition came long after the rebellion: the Government of India declared 15 November, Birsa's birth anniversary, as Janjatiya Gaurav Divas in 2021, and that day Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](/people/narendra-modi/) inaugurated the Bhagwan Birsa Munda Memorial Garden cum Freedom Fighter Museum at Ranchi by video conference, with Culture Minister G. Kishan Reddy present alongside Jharkhand's Governor, Chief Minister and Tribal Affairs Minister ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1772110&reg=3&lang=2)). The museum stands at the Old Central Jail where Birsa died — the jail itself has since been converted into the Birsa Munda Smriti Sangrahalaya ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1772110&reg=3&lang=2); [DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/who-was-birsa-munda-whose-ulgulan-declared-the-end-of-british-rule-in-jharkhand/)). In November 2024 the government launched the 150th birth-anniversary year of "Dharti Aaba Bhagwan Birsa Munda", noting 11 tribal freedom-fighter museums sanctioned nationwide ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2073379&reg=3&lang=2)). His story has also reached children on screen: Season 2 of Krish, Trish and Baltiboy, subtitled "Bharat Hain Hum", was launched at the [International Film Festival of India](/events/international-film-festival-of-india/) in Goa and features "Birsa Munda from Jharkhand" among its unsung freedom fighters ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/entertainment/second-season-of-animated-series-krish-trish-and-baltiboy-bharat-hain-hum-launched-at-iffi/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is Birsa Munda called "Dharti Aba"?**
"Dharti Aba" means "Father of the Earth" in Mundari, and his followers revered him as "Bhagwan" (Lord), attributing miracles to him. The title reflects his spiritual charisma as founder of the Birsait faith and his defence of tribal land as a collective inheritance ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-history/birsa-munda-legacy-ulgulan-adivasi-identity-explained-10733209/)).

**What was the Ulgulan fighting for?**
Tribal self-rule ("Abua Raj") and control over water, forests and land, against British revenue policy, outsider landlords and moneylenders (the "dikus"), and forced-labour regimes. Declared on 24 December 1899, it mobilised thousands across the Chotanagpur plateau and was crushed at Dombari Buru in January 1900, after which the colonial state eventually changed land law ([DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/who-was-birsa-munda-whose-ulgulan-declared-the-end-of-british-rule-in-jharkhand/)).

**How did Birsa Munda die?**
He died on 9 June 1900 in Ranchi jail, arrested a few months earlier. Colonial records attribute his death to complications of cholera and chronic dysentery; poisoning suspicions persist among followers and in popular memory. A more dramatic IANS account adds torture and a mock trial, unconfirmed in the record-based version ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-history/birsa-munda-legacy-ulgulan-adivasi-identity-explained-10733209/); [DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/who-was-birsa-munda-whose-ulgulan-declared-the-end-of-british-rule-in-jharkhand/)).
