## Overview

James Clayton Dobson Jr. (April 21, 1936 – August 21, 2025) was an American child psychologist who founded [Focus on the Family](/organizations/focus-on-the-family/) in 1977 and turned a parenting radio program into one of the largest evangelical ministries in the [United States](/places/united-states/), then used that platform to become a central organizer of the American religious right ([Associated Press](https://www.wvlt.tv/2025/08/21/james-dobson-founder-focus-family-has-died/)). He co-founded the Family Research Council and the legal organization now known as [Alliance Defending Freedom](/organizations/alliance-defending-freedom/), wrote more than 70 books, and died on August 21, 2025, aged 89 ([Religion News Service](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/james-dobson-focus-on-the-family-founder-and-family-values-advocate-dies-at-89/)).

Dobson was born in Shreveport, Louisiana, the only child of Myrtle and James Dobson Sr., traveling evangelists with the Church of the Nazarene ([Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/08/died-james-dobson/)). He studied psychology at Pasadena College, now Point Loma Nazarene University, and went on to earn a doctorate at the University of Southern California medical school, where his Ph.D. in child psychology was awarded in 1967 ([Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/08/died-james-dobson/); [Encyclopedia of Religion](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/legal-and-political-magazines/dobson-james-c-jr-1936-evangelical-leader)). He taught on the pediatric faculty of the USC School of Medicine for 14 years and did academic research at Children's Hospital Los Angeles, including a $5 million study of dietary treatment for children with a rare genetic disorder ([Religion News Service](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/james-dobson-focus-on-the-family-founder-and-family-values-advocate-dies-at-89/); [Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/08/died-james-dobson/)). He remained a professor of pediatrics at USC until 1980 and an attending psychologist at Children's Hospital of Los Angeles until 1983 ([Encyclopedia of Religion](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/legal-and-political-magazines/dobson-james-c-jr-1936-evangelical-leader)).

## From Dare to Discipline to a broadcasting empire

Dobson's public career began with alarm at the cultural changes of the 1960s: rising divorce, children born outside marriage, and what he saw as collapsing respect for authority ([Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/08/died-james-dobson/)). He started teaching a Sunday school class answering young parents' questions, then wrote *Dare to Discipline* in six months ([Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/08/died-james-dobson/)). The 1970 book, which defended strict-but-loving discipline including spanking against the permissive parenting then in fashion, sold 2 million copies over 22 years, with a 1992 updated edition selling another 1.5 million ([Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/08/died-james-dobson/)). The book's success made him, in the Encyclopedia of Religion's phrase, the evangelical Dr. Spock ([Encyclopedia of Religion](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/legal-and-political-magazines/dobson-james-c-jr-1936-evangelical-leader)).

Dobson left academia in 1976 and, the next year, opened a small two-room office in Arcadia, California, that he called Focus on the Family ([Religion News Service](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/james-dobson-focus-on-the-family-founder-and-family-values-advocate-dies-at-89/)). *Focus on the Family* began in 1977 as a 15-minute weekly radio program, later revamped in 1981 into a daily half-hour conversation, and it was on 800 Christian radio stations every day by 1982 ([Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/08/died-james-dobson/)). The ministry moved to Colorado Springs in 1991 ([NPR](https://www.publicradioeast.org/2025-08-22/focus-on-the-family-founder-james-dobson-has-died-at-age-89)), where it grew into what Christianity Today calls a $140 million multimedia enterprise reaching an estimated 220 million people in more than 150 countries, while the Associated Press puts its peak staff at more than 1,000 employees and Religion News Service at more than 1,300 ([Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/08/died-james-dobson/); [Associated Press](https://www.wvlt.tv/2025/08/21/james-dobson-founder-focus-family-has-died/); [Religion News Service](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/james-dobson-focus-on-the-family-founder-and-family-values-advocate-dies-at-89/)). Its best-known program after the flagship show was *Adventures in Odyssey*, a radio drama about youngsters learning life lessons from the inventor John Avery Whittaker, which became one of the most-listened-to programs on Christian radio ([Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/08/died-james-dobson/)).

## Political organizer: the Family Research Council and ADF

Dobson kept Focus on the Family formally nonpolitical while building separate vehicles for political work. In the early 1980s he started the Family Research Council in Washington to advocate pro-family policies — Religion News Service dates it to 1983, with Gary Bauer, while the Encyclopedia of Religion gives 1982 ([Religion News Service](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/james-dobson-focus-on-the-family-founder-and-family-values-advocate-dies-at-89/); [Encyclopedia of Religion](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/legal-and-political-magazines/dobson-james-c-jr-1936-evangelical-leader)). In the 1980s he was regularly invited to the White House to consult with President Ronald Reagan and his staff, and in 1985 he was appointed to Attorney General Edwin Meese's Commission on Pornography ([Religion News Service](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/james-dobson-focus-on-the-family-founder-and-family-values-advocate-dies-at-89/)). Liberty University's obituary also records appointments by Presidents Reagan and George H. W. Bush and describes him as an adviser to five U.S. presidents ([Liberty University News](https://www.liberty.edu/news/2025/08/21/liberty-mourns-the-loss-of-christian-leader-author-and-family-expert-dr-james-c-dobson/)).

In Colorado, Dobson backed Amendment 2, a state campaign to prevent state or local governments from passing laws protecting people from discrimination based on sexual orientation; voters passed it, but the U.S. Supreme Court eventually struck it down ([NPR](https://www.publicradioeast.org/2025-08-22/focus-on-the-family-founder-james-dobson-has-died-at-age-89)). In 1994 he was one of the co-founders, along with Bill Bright and D. James Kennedy, of the Alliance Defense Fund, a legal organization now known as Alliance Defending Freedom ([Religion News Service](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/james-dobson-focus-on-the-family-founder-and-family-values-advocate-dies-at-89/)). Baptist News Global's account of the founding names Dobson, Bright, Kennedy, Don Wildmon and two dozen other conservative Christian leaders who formed the group to battle the [American Civil Liberties Union](/organizations/aclu/) ([Baptist News Global](https://baptistnews.com/article/legal-group-adf-seeks-to-reshape-america-into-a-christian-nation/)). The founding year matters because it conflicts with the 1993 date carried elsewhere, including the existing FactsLedger profile of the organization; this profile follows the 1994 dating of the two fully-read obituary and news accounts and flags the conflict rather than resolving it silently.

Dobson's politics also produced episodes that drew criticism from outside his movement. His 1989 videotaped interview with serial murderer Ted Bundy, recorded the day before Bundy's execution, presented pornography as an influence on Bundy's crimes, a claim disputed by experts on violent behavior ([Encyclopedia of Religion](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/legal-and-political-magazines/dobson-james-c-jr-1936-evangelical-leader)). Critics also accused him of replacing the gospel with "family values," while even some critics praised the practical effect of his parenting advice ([Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/08/died-james-dobson/)).

## Later years, departure, and death

Dobson endorsed a presidential candidate for the first time in 2004, backing George W. Bush's reelection bid, and turned leadership of Focus on the Family over to Jim Daly the following year ([Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/08/died-james-dobson/)). He left the organization in 2010, according to the Associated Press, and founded the Dr. James Dobson Family Institute, continuing through the nationally syndicated *Family Talk* broadcast, which the institute says is carried by 1,500 radio outlets with more than half a million weekly listeners ([Associated Press](https://www.wvlt.tv/2025/08/21/james-dobson-founder-focus-family-has-died/)). Religion News Service dates the departure to 2009 and notes that some reports at the time said he was pushed out ([Religion News Service](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/james-dobson-focus-on-the-family-founder-and-family-values-advocate-dies-at-89/)). In 2016 he partnered with Liberty University to launch the James C. Dobson Center for Child Development, Marriage, and Family Studies ([Liberty University News](https://www.liberty.edu/news/2025/08/21/liberty-mourns-the-loss-of-christian-leader-author-and-family-expert-dr-james-c-dobson/)). He served on President [Donald Trump](/people/donald-trump/)'s Evangelical Executive Advisory Board and praised Trump's Supreme Court appointments after the 2022 *Dobbs* decision overturned *Roe v. Wade* ([Associated Press](https://www.wvlt.tv/2025/08/21/james-dobson-founder-focus-family-has-died/); [Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/08/died-james-dobson/)). He died on August 21, 2025, at 89, survived by his wife of 64 years, Shirley, their children Danae and Ryan, a daughter-in-law and two grandchildren ([Associated Press](https://www.wvlt.tv/2025/08/21/james-dobson-founder-focus-family-has-died/)).
