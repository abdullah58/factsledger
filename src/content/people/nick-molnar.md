## Overview

Nick Molnar is the Australian entrepreneur who co-founded [Afterpay](/organizations/afterpay/), the buy now, pay later company, with [Anthony Eisen](/people/anthony-eisen/) in [Sydney](/places/sydney/) in 2014. He ran it as co-CEO through its 2016 stock market listing and its $29 billion acquisition by [Block](/organizations/block/) in 2022, and he now leads sales across Block's Square, [Cash App](/organizations/cash-app/) and Afterpay businesses ([Reuters](https://d2233.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/08/02/dorsey-led-29-billion-deal-delivers-prompt-payday-for-afterpay-founders/)).

The idea came from watching a generation turn away from credit. Molnar sold jewellery from his bedroom while studying commerce at the University of Sydney, and he traced Afterpay's origin to a post-2008 shift among millennials: "It all started from seeing a shift in 2008 of millennials toward debit cards over credit cards" ([Reuters](https://d2233.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/08/02/dorsey-led-29-billion-deal-delivers-prompt-payday-for-afterpay-founders/)). Shoppers paid in four interest-free instalments; merchants paid Afterpay 4–6% of the shelf price, the retailer was paid immediately, and Afterpay bore the job of recovering money from customers ([Reuters](https://d2233.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/08/02/dorsey-led-29-billion-deal-delivers-prompt-payday-for-afterpay-founders/)) ([University of Sydney](http://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2018/03/26/a-breakthrough-business-thats-changing-how-we-shop.html)).

## From a bedroom jewellery shop to Afterpay

At school he worked in the family jewellery business at Sydney's Wynyard station and imported and sold headphones from Japan, describing himself as "always entrepreneurial, trying to come up with a way to trade my next product and make a dollar" ([University of Sydney](http://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2018/03/26/a-breakthrough-business-thats-changing-how-we-shop.html)). A rugby scholarship took him to the University of Sydney to study commerce (finance and international business); at 19, working from his bedroom, he was Australia's biggest eBay jewellery seller ([University of Sydney](http://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2018/03/26/a-breakthrough-business-thats-changing-how-we-shop.html)).

After university he took an investment-banking job despite making about five times more selling jewellery, until the man who hired him said he would hold the job open for 12 months — but that Molnar should run the jewellery business full time ([University of Sydney](http://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2018/03/26/a-breakthrough-business-thats-changing-how-we-shop.html)). That business became Ice Online, described by PMI as Australia's largest online-only jewellery retailer ([PMI](https://www.pmi.org/future-50/2022-honorees/nick-molnar)). The decisive meeting came on errands: on regular trips to the Bellevue Hill post office, Molnar was approached by neighbour Anthony Eisen, then chief investment officer at Guinness Peat Group, curious about the bedroom light burning late at night ([University of Sydney](http://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2018/03/26/a-breakthrough-business-thats-changing-how-we-shop.html)).

Molnar brought retail experience and Eisen financial expertise, but the pair lacked payments technology, so they partnered with Touchcorp, experienced in large-scale payments processing, including for Medicare HICAPS ([University of Sydney](http://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2018/03/26/a-breakthrough-business-thats-changing-how-we-shop.html)). That partnership became a full merger in 2017, after which the combined group relisted; by early 2018 Afterpay counted 10,000 retailers and more than 1.3 million consumers ([University of Sydney](http://www.sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2018/03/26/a-breakthrough-business-thats-changing-how-we-shop.html)).

## The Block years — and a changed view of regulation

In August 2021 Square announced it would buy Afterpay for $29 billion (A$39 billion) — a deal Reuters called Australia's biggest ever ([Reuters](https://d2233.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/08/02/dorsey-led-29-billion-deal-delivers-prompt-payday-for-afterpay-founders/)) ([PMI](https://www.pmi.org/future-50/2022-honorees/nick-molnar)). Reuters reported each founder stood to receive about A$2.46 billion ($1.8 billion) and would stay on; when the deal completed on 31 January 2022, Molnar and Eisen joined Block to lead Afterpay's seller and consumer businesses within its Square and Cash App ecosystems ([Reuters](https://d2233.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/08/02/dorsey-led-29-billion-deal-delivers-prompt-payday-for-afterpay-founders/)) ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220131005811/en)).

There had been regulatory friction: a 2019 Australian anti-money-laundering audit that ended in an all-clear, and a 2020 payment of A$1.5 million to Californian regulators who said Afterpay had operated there before holding a licence — a claim the company denied ([Reuters](https://d2233.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/08/02/dorsey-led-29-billion-deal-delivers-prompt-payday-for-afterpay-founders/)). By 2024 his role had widened beyond Afterpay: on Block's second-quarter earnings call, chief executive Jack Dorsey announced Molnar would head a sales group across Square, Cash App and Afterpay, arguing Afterpay had brought a "high-performing sales culture" into Block that would "put Square back in the lead for sellers around the world" ([Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/dorsey-puts-afterpays-molnar-in-charge-of-blocks-cross-company-sales-to-kickstart-growth/)). His brief included lifting Square's sales team, hiring representatives, building out field sales, and connecting Square's systems with Cash App's consumer scale ([Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/dorsey-puts-afterpays-molnar-in-charge-of-blocks-cross-company-sales-to-kickstart-growth/)).

Then came a reversal Molnar welcomed: from 10 June 2025, Australian buy now, pay later providers fell under credit regulation, needing a credit licence and income-and-expense checks on new customers ([Colitco](https://colitco.com/afterpay-new-laws-credit-checks-groceries/)). Molnar's response was enthusiastic rather than defensive: "I believe it provides the legitimacy [that] the buy now, pay later market needs and the ability for us to scale well into the future," adding that some of Australia's biggest industries had held off offering Afterpay until it was regulated, and that the company was "now starting to scale into more everyday spend" ([Azzet](https://azzet.com/news/stricter-bnpl-rules-prompt-consumer-rethink)).

## Wealth and recognition

Australian media widely reported Molnar as the country's youngest self-made billionaire (Reuters), and PMI named him to its 2022 Future 50 list for "pulling in younger shoppers by creating a modern twist on layaway" ([Reuters](https://d2233.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/08/02/dorsey-led-29-billion-deal-delivers-prompt-payday-for-afterpay-founders/)) ([PMI](https://www.pmi.org/future-50/2022-honorees/nick-molnar)). The wealth estimates that followed him show how closely a founder's fortune tracks a single stock: the Financial Review's Young Rich List put him 6th in 2025 at an estimated $1.1 billion, now listed under Block rather than Afterpay ([Nine for Brands](https://www.nineforbrands.com.au/media-release/wealth-surges-for-under-40s-on-the-financial-review-young-rich-list/)). That figure is an estimate at a point in time, not a bank balance — and it sits well below the pandemic peak, when Afterpay shares broke A$100 in October 2020 and hit A$160 by February 2021 ([Reuters](https://d2233.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/08/02/dorsey-led-29-billion-deal-delivers-prompt-payday-for-afterpay-founders/)).
