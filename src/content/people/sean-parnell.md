## Overview

Sean Parnell is a decorated Afghanistan veteran who went from leading an infantry platoon nicknamed "the Outlaws" to serving as the Pentagon's chief spokesman — and, in September 2026, became Defense Secretary [Pete Hegseth](/people/pete-hegseth/)'s pick for secretary of the Army ([[The Hill](/organizations/the-hill/)](https://www.alabamas42.com/hill-politics/senators-spurn-hegseths-pick-amid-jockeying-for-army-secretary-nomination/)). His memoir *Outlaw Platoon* made him a known figure in veteran circles; his political runs in Pennsylvania made him a Trump-backed candidate; and a custody battle that ended his 2022 Senate campaign is now the obstacle senators cite against his confirmation ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/local-news/2021-11-22/trump-backed-pa-senate-candidate-suspends-his-campaign-after-losing-custody-battle?_amp=true)).

## The Outlaws: sixteen months in Afghanistan

Parnell arrived in Afghanistan in February 2006 at 24 years old, an infantry officer with the 2nd Battalion, 87th Infantry Regiment, 3rd Brigade Combat Team — and his first experience was searing ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/74841/outlaw_former_infantry_officer_shares_experiences_pledges_to_help_others)). He had flown ahead of his platoon to Forward Operating Base Bermel in the country's east to scout the area; minutes after stepping off a CH-47 Chinook, insurgent fire overshot the base and wounded and killed local children playing nearby ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/74841/outlaw_former_infantry_officer_shares_experiences_pledges_to_help_others)). He raced to the aid station carrying a bleeding child — and watched the little girl die in his arms ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/74841/outlaw_former_infantry_officer_shares_experiences_pledges_to_help_others)). "I see her face every day of my life," he said years later ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/74841/outlaw_former_infantry_officer_shares_experiences_pledges_to_help_others)).

He had gotten there by an unlikely route. Two years earlier he had been a college student about to graduate from Duquesne University in Pittsburgh, having transferred from Clarion University after 9/11 to join the school's ROTC program ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/74841/outlaw_former_infantry_officer_shares_experiences_pledges_to_help_others)). "My whole life I was surrounded by people telling me I can't do something or I wasn't good enough," he said. "I joined the Army to be in the infantry — the light infantry — and I wanted to be a Ranger" ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/74841/outlaw_former_infantry_officer_shares_experiences_pledges_to_help_others)).

He led B Company's 3rd Platoon — nicknamed "the Outlaws" — through a 16-month deployment that he later said tested them to the edge ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/74841/outlaw_former_infantry_officer_shares_experiences_pledges_to_help_others)). The platoon had trained for two years expecting an ill-equipped enemy and instead found war-hardened fighters who had been fighting or training since the 1980s ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/74841/outlaw_former_infantry_officer_shares_experiences_pledges_to_help_others)). Parnell earned a Bronze Star for valor and a Purple Heart; he came home with shrapnel in his leg and a traumatic brain injury — vision problems, migraines, and cerebrospinal fluid leaking from his ears and nose through the last half of the deployment — and refused treatment until the unit returned ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/74841/outlaw_former_infantry_officer_shares_experiences_pledges_to_help_others)).

The Pritzker Military Museum & Library records the broader tally: six years in the Army as an Airborne Ranger with the 10th Mountain Division, 485 days of fighting on the Afghan-Pakistan border, retired as a captain, two Bronze Stars (one for valor) and a Purple Heart, wounded on June 10, 2006 ([Pritzker Military Museum & Library](https://www.pritzkermilitary.org/whats_on/pritzker-military-presents/sean-parnell-outlaw-platoon)). His platoon killed more than 350 enemy fighters, and 85% of its members received Purple Hearts ([Pritzker Military Museum & Library](https://www.pritzkermilitary.org/whats_on/pritzker-military-presents/sean-parnell-outlaw-platoon)).

## Outlaw Platoon

Parnell wrote his memoir, *Outlaw Platoon: Heroes, Renegades, Infidels, and the Brotherhood of War in Afghanistan*, published by HarperCollins, to make sure his men were not forgotten ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/74841/outlaw_former_infantry_officer_shares_experiences_pledges_to_help_others)). "The main reason I wrote the book … was I promised (my men) that I would figure out a way to tell their story," he said, explaining that he felt they "were overlooked" and that "nobody in America knew the extent of the war in Afghanistan" at the time of the deployment ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/74841/outlaw_former_infantry_officer_shares_experiences_pledges_to_help_others)). Writing, he said, was "hugely therapeutic": "taking the war out of myself and putting it on a page" let the story exist outside of him ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/74841/outlaw_former_infantry_officer_shares_experiences_pledges_to_help_others)). He was medically retired from the Army after the deployment ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/74841/outlaw_former_infantry_officer_shares_experiences_pledges_to_help_others)).

## Politics: the House bid and the 2022 Senate run

Parnell's first run for office was in 2020, for Pennsylvania's 17th Congressional District; he lost to Conor Lamb, 51.1% to 48.9%, according to IBTimes SG ([IBTimes SG](https://www.ibtimes.sg/who-sean-parnell-pete-hegseths-pick-become-next-army-secretary-93412)). In 2021 he entered the Republican primary for the U.S. Senate seat being vacated by Pat Toomey, with [Donald Trump](https://factsledger.com/people/donald-trump/)'s endorsement ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/local-news/2021-11-22/trump-backed-pa-senate-candidate-suspends-his-campaign-after-losing-custody-battle?_amp=true)). On November 22, 2021, he suspended that campaign ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/local-news/2021-11-22/trump-backed-pa-senate-candidate-suspends-his-campaign-after-losing-custody-battle?_amp=true)).

## The custody battle — stated carefully

The reason was a custody ruling. A court order made public that day granted Parnell's estranged wife, Laurie Snell, sole legal custody and primary physical custody of the couple's three children; both parents had sought sole custody ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/local-news/2021-11-22/trump-backed-pa-senate-candidate-suspends-his-campaign-after-losing-custody-battle?_amp=true)). Snell had accused Parnell of abuses ranging from choking her and striking one of their children to trying to convince her to have an abortion when she was six months pregnant, according to media reports ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/local-news/2021-11-22/trump-backed-pa-senate-candidate-suspends-his-campaign-after-losing-custody-battle?_amp=true)). Parnell emphatically denied the allegations during the custody hearing; when shown a photograph that purportedly showed a handprint-like welt on his son's back, he responded: "That is not a picture of my son" ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/local-news/2021-11-22/trump-backed-pa-senate-candidate-suspends-his-campaign-after-losing-custody-battle?_amp=true)). The judge found Parnell the "less credible" witness, according to *The Washington Post*, and awarded Parnell custody three weekends a month ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/local-news/2021-11-22/trump-backed-pa-senate-candidate-suspends-his-campaign-after-losing-custody-battle?_amp=true)).

In his suspension statement, Parnell said he was "devastated" by the ruling and strongly disagreed with it, adding that he planned to ask the court to reconsider — but that with a Senate campaign he could not give his children his full focus: "My focus right now is 100% on my children, and I want them to know I do not have any other priorities and will never stop fighting for them" ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/local-news/2021-11-22/trump-backed-pa-senate-candidate-suspends-his-campaign-after-losing-custody-battle?_amp=true)). [The Hill](https://factsledger.com/organizations/the-hill/) later reported that in 2024 Parnell wrote he had been "vindicated" and now shares custody of all five of his children — Parnell's own claim, not an independently verified finding ([The Hill](https://www.alabamas42.com/hill-politics/senators-spurn-hegseths-pick-amid-jockeying-for-army-secretary-nomination/)).

## The Pentagon and the Army-secretary fight

In February 2025, Trump appointed Parnell Assistant to the Secretary of Defense for Public Affairs — the Pentagon's chief spokesman — and credited him with helping advance the MISSION Act ([IBTimes SG](https://www.ibtimes.sg/who-sean-parnell-pete-hegseths-pick-become-next-army-secretary-93412)). Hegseth tapped him in May 2025 to lead a review of the Afghanistan withdrawal ([IBTimes SG](https://www.ibtimes.sg/who-sean-parnell-pete-hegseths-pick-become-next-army-secretary-93412)). The Hill describes him in that role as Hegseth's senior adviser, "a culture warrior in Hegseth's mode" with boosters inside the Pentagon and allies in MAGA circles ([The Hill](https://www.alabamas42.com/hill-politics/senators-spurn-hegseths-pick-amid-jockeying-for-army-secretary-nomination/)).

The Army's top civilian job opened when [Dan Driscoll](/people/dan-driscoll/) — an ally of Vice President [J.D. Vance](/people/jd-vance/) — resigned in August 2026 after months of internal clashes with Hegseth ([The Hill](https://www.alabamas42.com/hill-politics/senators-spurn-hegseths-pick-amid-jockeying-for-army-secretary-nomination/)). Hegseth spoke with Trump about Parnell at the start of September, and Parnell became the secretary's choice ([The Hill](https://www.alabamas42.com/hill-politics/senators-spurn-hegseths-pick-amid-jockeying-for-army-secretary-nomination/)). The reception on Capitol Hill has been icy "in part over abuse allegations from his estranged wife": a congressional aide and former committee staffer said Parnell currently would not have enough support to advance out of the Senate Armed Services Committee if Trump nominated him ([The Hill](https://www.alabamas42.com/hill-politics/senators-spurn-hegseths-pick-amid-jockeying-for-army-secretary-nomination/)). Sen. Richard Blumenthal said, "Right now, I'd be very doubtful that he could be confirmed"; Sen. Kevin Cramer called Parnell's record, "particularly the spousal abuse stuff … just a bridge too far for the vast majority of people"; committee chair Roger Wicker declined to comment until someone is nominated ([The Hill](https://www.alabamas42.com/hill-politics/senators-spurn-hegseths-pick-amid-jockeying-for-army-secretary-nomination/)).

Parnell does have defenders: Sen. Dave McCormick called him "a good friend, a decorated combat veteran, a true patriot, and a fellow Pennsylvanian," and Sen. Tim Sheehy, a former Navy SEAL on the committee, said Parnell has been "doing a tremendous job alongside" Hegseth ([The Hill](https://www.alabamas42.com/hill-politics/senators-spurn-hegseths-pick-amid-jockeying-for-army-secretary-nomination/)). Sen. Mark Kelly — who admitted he does not know much about Parnell — called the job "a promotion" and added: "I've never seen somebody that I can think of that went from communications/spokesperson to running the whole thing" ([The Hill](https://www.alabamas42.com/hill-politics/senators-spurn-hegseths-pick-amid-jockeying-for-army-secretary-nomination/)).

He is not the only contender. The White House is also considering Marc Andersen, the assistant secretary of the Army for financial management and comptroller, and Derrick Anderson, the assistant secretary of Defense for special operations and low-intensity conflict ([The Hill](https://www.alabamas42.com/hill-politics/senators-spurn-hegseths-pick-amid-jockeying-for-army-secretary-nomination/)). Three Medal of Honor recipients backed Andersen — though one, Earl Plumlee, sided with Parnell, citing his "vast amount of experience … in interacting with the industry" ([The Hill](https://www.alabamas42.com/hill-politics/senators-spurn-hegseths-pick-amid-jockeying-for-army-secretary-nomination/)). Adam Telle, the assistant secretary for civil works, became acting secretary four days after Driscoll's exit; White House chief of staff Susie Wiles is reportedly leading the selection process, and Trump "will announce a nominee … at a time and place of his choosing," with the Senate unlikely to take up a confirmation until after the midterms ([The Hill](https://www.alabamas42.com/hill-politics/senators-spurn-hegseths-pick-amid-jockeying-for-army-secretary-nomination/)).

The 2024 "vindicated" statement is Parnell's own claim, reported by The Hill, not an independently confirmed outcome. The February 2025 appointment date, the MISSION Act credit, the May 2025 withdrawal-review role, and the 2020 election margin come from IBTimes SG and are attributed as such. It does not claim Trump has formally nominated him: as of September 2026, no nomination has been announced. The alleged judge's wording that Parnell "did commit some acts of abuse," a possible MISSION Act résumé dispute, and the paywalled Wall Street Journal reporting about Dane Hughes are all held back as unverifiable this session.
