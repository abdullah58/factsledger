## Overview

Zak Penn is the American screenwriter behind *X2*, *The Incredible Hulk*, *Ready Player One* and *Free Guy*, who in 2026 co-created *Stuart Fails to Save the Universe* — the 10-part HBO Max sequel to *The Big Bang Theory* — with [Chuck Lorre](/people/chuck-lorre/) and [Bill Prady](/people/bill-prady/) ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2026-08-03/stuart-fails-to-save-the-universe-is-a-fun-and-familiar-big-bang-spin-off)). What the fan-wiki filmography lists never mention is the most distinctive thing about him: he is Hollywood's most publicly self-critical blockbuster writer. He calls *The Avengers* "probably the most overrated movie I've worked on," *X-Men: The Last Stand* "the most underrated," and owns the failure of *Elektra* as his own misreading of the rating ([Cole Haddon](https://colehaddon.substack.com/p/q-and-a-screenwriter-zak-penn-on)). His career also has a visible through-line that NPR's David Bianculli draws explicitly: like *Free Guy* — "about a background player in a videogame who steps into the narrative spotlight" — the new series takes *Big Bang*'s peripheral characters and makes them the leads ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2026-08-03/stuart-fails-to-save-the-universe-is-a-fun-and-familiar-big-bang-spin-off)).

## First scripts, early failures

Penn wrote his first script, *Last Action Hero*, at age 22 and 23 — "with the confidence of a 22- and 23-year-old who was pretty sure that he knew better than other people about what should be on screen" ([Cole Haddon](https://colehaddon.substack.com/p/q-and-a-screenwriter-zak-penn-on)). He originated the story but was reduced to a "Story By" credit by the time it reached the screen, and the 1993 film bombed ([Cole Haddon](https://colehaddon.substack.com/p/q-and-a-screenwriter-zak-penn-on)). "It knocked all the stuffing right out of me," he said — and he argues the failure was useful: "if that movie had been a success, who knows? I probably would have thought I was right about everything" ([Cole Haddon](https://colehaddon.substack.com/p/q-and-a-screenwriter-zak-penn-on)).

His next film, the college satire *PCU* (1994), which he co-wrote, also failed at the box office — and Penn treated it as a kind of fuel: "good, then I have not yet had my moment" ([Cole Haddon](https://colehaddon.substack.com/p/q-and-a-screenwriter-zak-penn-on)). The early-1990s failures set the template for the career that followed: a writer who assumes the audience knows when something doesn't work, and says so himself.

## The Marvel years — rated by their author

By the early 2000s, Penn's name was appearing on Marvel films, first at Fox and then in the MCU: *X2* (2003), *Elektra* (2005), *X-Men: The Last Stand* (2006), the script for *The Incredible Hulk*, and the story credit on *The Avengers* (2012) ([Cole Haddon](https://colehaddon.substack.com/p/q-and-a-screenwriter-zak-penn-on)). In a long-form interview he ranked his own work with unusual candor. On *Elektra*: "I have no problem admitting like *Elektra* is a movie that gets a lot of criticism that's probably deserved. And it's my fault because I thought I was writing an R-rated movie, and I should have known at that point in my career that of course it wasn't going to be an R-rated movie" — it was released PG-13 ([Cole Haddon](https://colehaddon.substack.com/p/q-and-a-screenwriter-zak-penn-on)). On *X-Men: The Last Stand*: "the most underrated action movie I ever wrote... it's actually got some terrific action sequences, and I think the plot is pretty well constructed" ([Cole Haddon](https://colehaddon.substack.com/p/q-and-a-screenwriter-zak-penn-on)). And on *The Avengers*: "obviously there're sequences that we always knew we were going to do, and they're really cool in the movie. I feel like *Avengers* is probably the most overrated movie I've worked on in that people think it's great, but I'm not sure that it is great" ([Cole Haddon](https://colehaddon.substack.com/p/q-and-a-screenwriter-zak-penn-on)).

## Behind the camera

Penn has also directed, with a taste for meta and improv formats. In 2004 he made the mockumentary *Incident at Loch Ness*, starring himself and Werner Herzog as themselves; in 2007 he directed the improv comedy *The Grand*; and in 2014 he helmed a real documentary, *Atari: Game Over*, about the excavation of a site where hundreds of thousands of the video game *E.T.*'s cartridges were buried ([Cole Haddon](https://colehaddon.substack.com/p/q-and-a-screenwriter-zak-penn-on)). The *E.T.* documentary became the bridge to his biggest job: he invited Ernest Cline, the author of *Ready Player One*, to appear in it before they ever worked on a script together ([ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/ready-player-one-ernest-cline-interview/)).

## *Ready Player One*: the collaboration model

Penn was hired to rewrite Cline's earlier drafts of *Ready Player One*, and the two had already become friends over Atari and movies — Cline was "a huge fan of his filmography" and called *PCU* "hysterical" ([ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/ready-player-one-ernest-cline-interview/)). Crucially, Penn "made me a collaborator from the beginning," Cline said — "whenever he needed to talk about changing something, we would talk it through" ([ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/ready-player-one-ernest-cline-interview/)). When Spielberg came aboard, it became "this great three-way collaboration," with the three of them spitballing changes to things from the book that wouldn't be cinematic — including The Shining sequence, which Cline called "just team effort" ([ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/ready-player-one-ernest-cline-interview/)).

There is a twist that only Penn's career makes possible: Cline said that *Last Action Hero* — the film Penn disowns — had, "along with *The Purple Rose of Cairo*, helped inspire the flicksyncs in my novel," the idea of stepping inside one of your favorite movies that became one of the film's set pieces ([ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/ready-player-one-ernest-cline-interview/)). Penn himself calls *Ready Player One* "the most complicated movie I've worked on" and says even Spielberg called it his most complicated — and, after the back-to-back failures of his twenties, he said: "I got to work with Steven Spielberg and I didn't get fired. And I got credit, and he took me under his wing, and I have zero to complain about" ([Cole Haddon](https://colehaddon.substack.com/p/q-and-a-screenwriter-zak-penn-on)).

## Television: *Alphas*, *Beacon 23*, and *Stuart Fails to Save the Universe*

Penn created the Syfy series *Alphas*, and then, for MGM+, created and showran *Beacon 23*, adapted from Hugh Howey's 2015 fix-up novel of five short stories about deep-space lighthouse keepers ([Cole Haddon](https://colehaddon.substack.com/p/q-and-a-screenwriter-zak-penn-on); [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/beacon-23-underrated-hugh-howey-sci-fi-adaptation/)). The eight-episode first season, starring Lena Headey and Stephan James and executive-produced alongside Glen Mazzara, premiered on November 12, 2023 ([The Futon Critic](http://www.thefutoncritic.com/news/2023/09/20/mgmplus-picks-up-science-fiction-mystery-thriller-beacon-23-257010/20230920mgm01/)). CBR's August 2026 retrospective reported the series was canceled after two seasons, holding only a 55% Rotten Tomatoes approval rating — "the underrated hard sci-fi show failed to garner widespread attention" ([CBR](https://www.cbr.com/beacon-23-underrated-hugh-howey-sci-fi-adaptation/)).

Then came the project that completes the through-line. *Stuart Fails to Save the Universe*, a 10-part HBO Max sequel to *The Big Bang Theory*, follows a quartet of that show's supporting characters — Stuart the comic-book-store owner, his girlfriend Denise, the lunky geologist Bert, and Barry Kripke — thrown into a multiverse they must fail to save ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2026-08-03/stuart-fails-to-save-the-universe-is-a-fun-and-familiar-big-bang-spin-off)). Concocted by Chuck Lorre and Bill Prady, creators of *The Big Bang Theory*, "along with Zak Penn," it has no laugh track and "plays like an action-adventure series" — but, as Bianculli writes, "it's relentlessly, inventively funny" ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2026-08-03/stuart-fails-to-save-the-universe-is-a-fun-and-familiar-big-bang-spin-off)). Every episode visits a new universe, every opening-credits sequence is different (even with its own spoilers), and the theme is by Danny Elfman ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2026-08-03/stuart-fails-to-save-the-universe-is-a-fun-and-familiar-big-bang-spin-off)). Bianculli's comparison is the one Penn's whole career earns: it is "a lot like" *Free Guy* — "and just as playful" ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2026-08-03/stuart-fails-to-save-the-universe-is-a-fun-and-familiar-big-bang-spin-off)).
