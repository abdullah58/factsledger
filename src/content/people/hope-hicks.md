## The witness everyone subpoenaed

No standard profile of Hope Hicks puts the right fact first. They recount the
résumé — model, PR operative, Trump aide — as though the jobs were the story.
They are not. The story is the oath. Hicks is, by the available record, the
one Trump aide who was called back under oath at every stage of the Trump
era: before the House Intelligence Committee in 2018, before House Democrats
in 2019, before the January 6 select committee in 2022, and under criminal
subpoena on the witness stand in Manhattan in 2024
([Akron Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/nation-world/2018/02/28/white-house-communications-director-hope/10548166007/);
[Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/5/3/takeaways-from-day-11-of-trumps-new-york-hush-money-trial-with-hope-hicks)).
The same loyal aide who wrote "deny, deny, deny" on the Access Hollywood tape
in October 2016 became, eight years later, the prosecution witness who broke
down crying on the stand. That arc — from gatekeeper to evidence — is the
actual subject of any honest profile.

It began with no politics at all. Hicks was a former model and an associate
at a New York PR firm who went to work for Ivanka Trump's fashion line and
then the Trump Organization in 2014. Trump picked her as his 2016 campaign
press secretary when she was 26; she later testified she thought he "might be
joking."
([NPR](https://radio.wcmu.org/2018-10-09/former-wh-communications-director-hope-hicks-lands-job-at-fox)).
In a January 2017 Forbes video she said: "I knew very
little about politics. Obviously I wasn't expecting to take part in this and
certainly not to play the role that I had"
([NPR](https://radio.wcmu.org/2018-10-09/former-wh-communications-director-hope-hicks-lands-job-at-fox)).

## Nine hours, nine years, nine questions of privilege

In the White House she was the director of strategic communications, a title
the Akron Beacon Journal noted "only partly captured her more expansive role
as the president's gatekeeper to the press," occupying the desk closest to the
Oval Office and serving as Trump's smiling validator — the fourth person to
hold the communications director job after she was promoted to it in 2017
([Akron Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/nation-world/2018/02/28/white-house-communications-director-hope/10548166007/)).
On February 27, 2018, she sat for nine hours with the House Intelligence
Committee on Russia. The next day she announced her resignation. In between,
she told investigators she had occasionally told "white lies" for Trump —
but not about the Russia investigation.
She had also been interviewed by Robert Mueller's team about her role in
crafting the statement about Donald Trump Jr.'s 2016 meeting with Russians
([Akron Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/nation-world/2018/02/28/white-house-communications-director-hope/10548166007/)).
Her resignation announcement came while she was under unusual personal
strain: her relationship with White House staff secretary Rob Porter had just
become public after he resigned over domestic-abuse allegations by two
ex-wives, and Hicks had helped craft the White House's response to the
scandal
([Akron Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/nation-world/2018/02/28/white-house-communications-director-hope/10548166007/)).

She landed at the Murdochs'. On October 9, 2018, she was named executive vice
president and chief communications officer of the new, slimmed-down Fox Corp —
the entity formed from what remained after Disney bought most of 21st Century
Fox — a move NPR's David Folkenflik called a marker of "the close ties
between the Trump White House, the Murdochs and [Fox News](/organizations/fox-news/)." The same report
carries the detail that locks in her successor:
[Bill Shine](/people/bill-shine/), the former Fox News co-president, was by
then "communications director for the White House"
Reporters who covered her considered her "an honest broker who works hard
and remains totally loyal to Trump while recognizing his flaws," in
Folkenflik's account
([NPR](https://radio.wcmu.org/2018-10-09/former-wh-communications-director-hope-hicks-lands-job-at-fox)).

Then the boomerang. In early 2020 she returned to the White House as counselor
and senior adviser, officially working under Jared Kushner, and was still
there on January 6, 2021 — a fact that would put her at the center of the
next sworn chapter
([anews](https://www.anews.com.tr/world/2020/10/02/hope-hicks-the-white-house-counselor-who-lets-trump-be-trump)).

## The texts and the tears

Hicks gave a formal interview to the
[House select committee investigating the January 6 attack](/organizations/house-select-committee-on-january-6/)
on October 25, 2022, but her most quoted testimony from that day was also on the record in her phone. During the riot she texted Ivanka
Trump's chief of staff Julie Radford: "We all look like domestic terrorists
now." That evening, after Trump attacked Mike Pence in a tweet as rioters
chanted for the vice president, she texted a colleague: "Attacking the VP?
Wtf is wrong with him." The committee's final report also records that she
had suggested several times before January 6 that Trump publicly state the
day must remain peaceful — and that he refused
([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/international/us-house-jan-6-panel-report-describes-trump-s-engagement-in-conspiracy-122122300132_1.html)).

The final oath came in a criminal courtroom. Subpoenaed by Manhattan
prosecutors, Hicks testified on May 3, 2024 as the highest-profile witness in
the hush-money trial. She described learning about the Access Hollywood tape
from a Washington Post reporter's email, being "concerned, very concerned,"
and forwarding the request to campaign leadership with the recommendation to
"deny, deny, deny." She testified that Trump "knew what he wanted to say and
how he wanted to say it, and we were all just following his lead," and that
his chief worry was his wife: "I don't think he wanted anyone in his family
to be hurt or embarrassed," and he asked her to make sure the morning's
newspapers weren't delivered to the residence.
Under cross-examination by defense lawyer Emil Bove she broke down in tears
and had to excuse herself, forcing a brief pause in the proceedings
([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/5/3/takeaways-from-day-11-of-trumps-new-york-hush-money-trial-with-hope-hicks)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim Hicks leaked to the press or wrote a tell-all —
she never gave interviews and remained unstained by the administration's
controversies by her own design. It does not assert the details of her June
2019 closed-door House Judiciary testimony, which appeared only in search
snippets. It does not claim a specific last day in the White House in 2018 or
assert anything about her activities after January 2021, which were not
verified in fully read sources. It does not repeat the claim that her
resignation was connected to the Russia investigation — the White House
denied it, and the timing, while suggestive, is not evidence.
