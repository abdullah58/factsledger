The most consequential thing Jon Stewart ever did may not have aired on Comedy Central. His own network's press office credits him with championing the James Zadroga 9/11 Health and Compensation Act of 2010 to support 9/11 first responders ([Paramount](https://www.paramountpressexpress.com/comedy-central/releases/?view=109214-jon-stewart-returns-as-host-of-the-daily-show-every-monday-starting-february-12th)), and nearly a decade later he was still the cause's most visible face in Washington — this time in tears, shouting at a nearly empty congressional hearing room.

## From satirist to advocate: the 9/11 first-responders fight

Stewart's advocacy began while he was still at the Daily Show desk. CBS News reported that he first devoted an entire episode of the program to the political debate over the Zadroga Act back in 2010, and that he has since become one of the most vocal advocates for 9/11 responders, repeatedly defending their right to health care coverage ([CBS News](https://www.etonline.com/jon-stewart-breaks-down-at-hearing-on-911-responders-bill-you-should-be-ashamed-of-yourselves)). Congress passed the Zadroga Act in 2010 over opposition from some Republicans who balked at its $7 billion price tag ([CBS News](https://www.etonline.com/jon-stewart-breaks-down-at-hearing-on-911-responders-bill-you-should-be-ashamed-of-yourselves)).

The money did not last. A 2015 reauthorization extended the World Trade Center Health Program through 2090 but funded the Victim Compensation Fund portion for only five more years, and in February 2019 the fund announced a shortfall that would cut pending awards by 50 percent and future awards by 70 percent ([U.S. Senator Kirsten Gillibrand](https://www.gillibrand.senate.gov/news/press/release/gillibrand-statement-on-president-trump-signing-permanent-9-11-victim-compensation-fund-bill-into-law/)). That June, Stewart appeared before the House Judiciary Subcommittee on Civil Rights and Civil Liberties and broke down as he scolded the members who had not shown up ([CBS News](https://www.etonline.com/jon-stewart-breaks-down-at-hearing-on-911-responders-bill-you-should-be-ashamed-of-yourselves)). "I can't help but think what an incredible metaphor this room is... a filled room of 9/11 first responders and in front of me, a nearly empty Congress. Sick and dying, they brought themselves down here to speak to no one... shameful," he said, with a little over half of the subcommittee's 14 members present, mostly Democrats ([CBS News](https://www.etonline.com/jon-stewart-breaks-down-at-hearing-on-911-responders-bill-you-should-be-ashamed-of-yourselves)). He called the turnout "an embarrassment to this country" and "a stain on this institution," and the hearing room gave him a standing ovation after more than five minutes of testimony ([CBS News](https://www.etonline.com/jon-stewart-breaks-down-at-hearing-on-911-responders-bill-you-should-be-ashamed-of-yourselves)).

The bill on the table — the Never Forget the Heroes Act of 2019, introduced by Jerry Nadler, Carolyn Maloney, and Peter King with support from Chuck Schumer and Kirsten Gillibrand — became the vehicle for permanent authorization ([CBS News](https://www.etonline.com/jon-stewart-breaks-down-at-hearing-on-911-responders-bill-you-should-be-ashamed-of-yourselves)). Senator Gillibrand's office records that the measure, formally named for first responders James Zadroga, Ray Pfeifer, and Luis Alvarez, passed the House 402–12 on July 12, 2019 and the Senate 97–2 on July 23, 2019, and that President Trump signed it into law on July 29, 2019, keeping the fund open through 2090 ([U.S. Senator Kirsten Gillibrand](https://www.gillibrand.senate.gov/news/press/release/gillibrand-statement-on-president-trump-signing-permanent-9-11-victim-compensation-fund-bill-into-law/)).

## The Daily Show years

Stewart — born Jonathan Stuart Leibowitz — rose to prominence as a standup comic and hosted several short-lived talk shows before taking over Comedy Central's The Daily Show in 1999 ([Canvas Arts](https://artscanvas.org/film-tv/jon-stewart-to-receive-mark-twain-lifetime-achievement-award)); Poynter likewise dates his arrival as host to 1999, noting the show itself debuted in 1996 ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2011/as-daily-show-turns-15-years-old-jon-stewarts-best-media-criticism-moments/)). His 16-year run turned the program, in his network's words, into a "cultural juggernaut" — the place politicians visited to make their case and viewers visited to make sense of the world ([Paramount](https://www.paramountpressexpress.com/comedy-central/releases/?view=109214-jon-stewart-returns-as-host-of-the-daily-show-every-monday-starting-february-12th)).

The tenure also functioned as a star factory: the show launched Stephen Colbert, John Oliver, Trevor Noah, Michael Che, and Samantha Bee, among others including Steve Carell and Jessica Williams ([Paramount](https://www.paramountpressexpress.com/comedy-central/releases/?view=109214-jon-stewart-returns-as-host-of-the-daily-show-every-monday-starting-february-12th)). As host and executive producer, Stewart collected 56 Emmy nominations and 24 wins, with ten consecutive victories for Outstanding Variety, Music, or Comedy Series — the longest streak in that category's history — plus three Peabody Awards ([Paramount](https://www.paramountpressexpress.com/comedy-central/releases/?view=109214-jon-stewart-returns-as-host-of-the-daily-show-every-monday-starting-february-12th)). He stepped down in 2015 ([Canvas Arts](https://artscanvas.org/film-tv/jon-stewart-to-receive-mark-twain-lifetime-achievement-award)).

## The Crossfire night

On October 15, 2004, Stewart walked into the studio of [CNN's](/organizations/cnn/) Crossfire and used his guest slot to argue the show was damaging American political life ([Sage Publications](https://cmn-cdn-001.sagepub.com/books/titles/227621/att_sb1_17695.pdf)). "Stop, stop, stop, stop hurting America," he told co-hosts Tucker Carlson and Paul Begala, accusing them of "partisan hackery" and of doing "theater" when they should be doing debate ([Sage Publications](https://cmn-cdn-001.sagepub.com/books/titles/227621/att_sb1_17695.pdf)). "You have a responsibility to the public discourse, and you fail miserably," he told them ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2011/as-daily-show-turns-15-years-old-jon-stewarts-best-media-criticism-moments/)).

The exchange entered television lore, and three months later CNN canceled Crossfire ([Sage Publications](https://cmn-cdn-001.sagepub.com/books/titles/227621/att_sb1_17695.pdf)); Poynter likewise dates the cancellation to "a few months later" ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2011/as-daily-show-turns-15-years-old-jon-stewarts-best-media-criticism-moments/)). CNN chief executive Jonathan Klein publicly sided with Stewart: "I guess I come down more firmly in the Jon Stewart camp," he said, adding, "I doubt that when the president sits down with his advisers they scream at him to bring him up to date on all of the issues... I don't know why we don't treat the audience with the same respect" ([Sage Publications](https://cmn-cdn-001.sagepub.com/books/titles/227621/att_sb1_17695.pdf)). Stewart's own reaction was bewilderment: "I had no idea that if you wanted a show canceled, all you had to do was say it out loud" ([Sage Publications](https://cmn-cdn-001.sagepub.com/books/titles/227621/att_sb1_17695.pdf)). Contemporary accounts held that the appearance rocketed Stewart to new prominence and may have sealed Crossfire's fate ([Canvas Arts](https://artscanvas.org/film-tv/jon-stewart-to-receive-mark-twain-lifetime-achievement-award)).

## The soccer player from William & Mary

Long before late-night television, Stewart was a college soccer player. He graduated from the College of William & Mary in 1984 and spent three seasons (1981–83) on the Tribe men's soccer team, compiling 32 points on 10 goals and 12 assists while the squad went 40–15–9 ([William & Mary Athletics](https://tribeathletics.com/news/2006/1/19/205063638.aspx)). His signature moment came in the 1983 ECAC championship, when he scored the only goal in a 1–0 win over Connecticut that sent William & Mary to its second-ever NCAA Tournament appearance ([William & Mary Athletics](https://tribeathletics.com/news/2006/1/19/205063638.aspx)). In May 2004 he returned to campus to deliver the college's commencement address — the first Tribe soccer player ever chosen for that honor — and the National Soccer Coaches Association of America named him its Honorary All-American for 2005 ([William & Mary Athletics](https://tribeathletics.com/news/2006/1/19/205063638.aspx)).

## Mark Twain Prize and the 2024 return

In January 2022 Stewart was named the 23rd recipient of the Kennedy Center's Mark Twain Prize for American Humor, with the gala held April 24, 2022 ([Canvas Arts](https://artscanvas.org/film-tv/jon-stewart-to-receive-mark-twain-lifetime-achievement-award)); Paramount confirms the 2022 prize in its own release ([Paramount](https://www.paramountpressexpress.com/comedy-central/releases/?view=109214-jon-stewart-returns-as-host-of-the-daily-show-every-monday-starting-february-12th)). Kennedy Center president Deborah F. Rutter praised him not only for comedy but for advocacy, saying his work for first responders and veterans showed "that we all can make a difference in this world through humor, humanity, and patriotism" ([Canvas Arts](https://artscanvas.org/film-tv/jon-stewart-to-receive-mark-twain-lifetime-achievement-award)). Stewart had himself spoken at Dave Chappelle's 2019 Mark Twain ceremony ([Canvas Arts](https://artscanvas.org/film-tv/jon-stewart-to-receive-mark-twain-lifetime-achievement-award)).

On January 24, 2024, Comedy Central announced that Stewart would return to executive produce The Daily Show and host every Monday night starting February 12 — "the day after the Super Bowl" — through the 2024 election cycle, reuniting with showrunner and executive producer Jen Flanz and bringing his longtime manager James "Baby Doll" Dixon aboard as an executive producer ([Paramount](https://www.paramountpressexpress.com/comedy-central/releases/?view=109214-jon-stewart-returns-as-host-of-the-daily-show-every-monday-starting-february-12th)). "Jon Stewart is the voice of our generation," said network chief Chris McCarthy in the announcement, "and we are honored to have him return... to help us all make sense of the insanity and division roiling the country as we enter the election season" ([Paramount](https://www.paramountpressexpress.com/comedy-central/releases/?view=109214-jon-stewart-returns-as-host-of-the-daily-show-every-monday-starting-february-12th)).
