## Overview

**Alison Hastings** is a British media figure who was appointed a vice-president of the British Board of Film Classification in November 2008, after an open competition; she sat on the board's Presidential Team that jointly refused [*The Bunny Game*](/creative-works/the-bunny-game/) a certificate in October 2011, blocking its UK supply ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/hastings-lemos-named-vps-at-british-ratings-board/4039874.article); [JoBlo](https://www.joblo.com/adam-rehmeiers-the-bunny-game-banned-by-british-board-of-film-classification/amp/)). Before the BBFC she edited the Newcastle daily *The Chronicle* from 1996 to 2002 and was a founding trustee of the BBC Trust ([HoldTheFrontPage](https://www.holdthefrontpage.co.uk/2016/news/ex-regional-daily-editor-takes-up-director-role-with-publisher/)).

## From the Chronicle to the BBC Trust

Hastings edited the Newcastle daily *The Chronicle* for six years, from 1996 until 2002, having been made editor at 29 after more than 18 years as a journalist in the local press ([HoldTheFrontPage](https://www.holdthefrontpage.co.uk/2016/news/ex-regional-daily-editor-takes-up-director-role-with-publisher/)). She served as a Press Complaints Commissioner and became a founding trustee of the BBC Trust, the corporation's governing body ([HoldTheFrontPage](https://www.holdthefrontpage.co.uk/2016/news/ex-regional-daily-editor-takes-up-director-role-with-publisher/); [Prolific North](https://www.prolificnorth.co.uk/news/alison-hasting-joins-archant-board/)). As the Trust's member for England she chaired Audience Council England, its advisory body for English audiences ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/hastings-lemos-named-vps-at-british-ratings-board/4039874.article)). Based in Liverpool, she worked as a media consultant ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/hastings-lemos-named-vps-at-british-ratings-board/4039874.article); [Prolific North](https://www.prolificnorth.co.uk/news/alison-hasting-joins-archant-board/)), and in 2023 she joined the board of the regional publisher Archant as a non-executive director ([Prolific North](https://www.prolificnorth.co.uk/news/alison-hasting-joins-archant-board/)). Her career arc — regional press editor, press regulator, broadcast governor — made her the working journalist's voice in British media regulation, the practitioner among policy-makers.

## The BBFC vice-presidency

After an open competition, Hastings was appointed a vice-president of the BBFC in 2008, taking up the post that November alongside Gerard Lemos as Janet Lewis-Jones and Lord Taylor of Warwick stepped down after ten years ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/hastings-lemos-named-vps-at-british-ratings-board/4039874.article)). President Sir Quentin Thomas said the pair would bring "a depth of highly relevant experience and expertise to the Board" ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/hastings-lemos-named-vps-at-british-ratings-board/4039874.article)). In October 2011 she sat on the Presidential Team that, with Director David Cooke, formally refused *The Bunny Game* a certificate — a joint decision meaning the film could not be legally supplied anywhere in the UK ([JoBlo](https://www.joblo.com/adam-rehmeiers-the-bunny-game-banned-by-british-board-of-film-classification/amp/)). The refusal rested on the board's view that the film's unremitting sexual and physical abuse was so pervasive that cuts were impractical ([JoBlo](https://www.joblo.com/adam-rehmeiers-the-bunny-game-banned-by-british-board-of-film-classification/amp/)). Whether she remains a vice-president is not confirmed by the sources read for this profile.
