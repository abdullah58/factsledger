## Overview

Missy Elliott — born Melissa Arnette Elliott on July 1, 1971, in Portsmouth, Virginia — is an American rapper, singer, songwriter and record producer whose six platinum albums, four Grammy Awards and futuristic music videos made her one of the most influential figures in hip-hop ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/missy-elliott-mn0000502371); [Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2024/04/08/missy-elliott-goes-out-of-this-world-with-ciara-busta-rhymes-and-timbaland-on-first-arena-headling-tour/)). She was the first female rapper inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame in 2019 and the first female hip-hop artist inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2023, and in 2025 Billboard's staff ranked her the second-greatest female rapper of all time ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/queen-latifah-inducts-missy-elliott-songwriters-hall-fame-lizzo-performs/); [Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/article/missy-elliott-willie-nelson-and-george-michael-among-rock-hall-inductees/); [Black Enterprise](https://www.blackenterprise.com/nicki-minaj-best-female-rapper-of-all-time/)).

Born in the [United States](/places/united-states/) in Portsmouth, Virginia, Elliott sang in her church choir as a child and began writing songs while still young ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)). She first broke through as a member of the R&B group Sista, then built a second career writing and producing hits for other artists — most famously Aaliyah — before her solo debut made her a star in her own right ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)). Her albums are known for staccato dance rhythms, cartoon sound effects, comic profanity and spoken-word interludes, a sound she built with her longtime collaborator Timbaland ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)).

## From Sista to Supa Dupa Fly

Elliott created her own breakthrough in 1991, taking the members of her first group, Fayze — later renamed Sista — to a Portsmouth hotel where DeVante Swing of Jodeci was staying, and Swing signed the group to his Elektra-affiliated Swing Mob label ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)). Her first Billboard chart impression came in 1993 as co-writer, co-producer and featured vocalist on Raven-Symoné's "That's What Little Girls Are Made Of", which reached No. 68 on the pop chart ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/missy-elliott-mn0000502371)). Sista's 1994 album, 4 All the Sistas Around da World, was well reviewed but sold poorly and was soon pulled from shelves, though its single "Brand New" reached No. 84 on the R&B/hip-hop chart ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/missy-elliott-mn0000502371)). Undaunted, Elliott formed a production team with Timbaland and began writing for Jodeci and others, most notably Aaliyah, for whose 1996 album One in a Million she and Timbaland produced eight tracks, including four hit singles ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)). She also established her own Elektra imprint, The Goldmind, Inc., and her debut solo album, Supa Dupa Fly, arrived in 1997, debuting at No. 3 on the Billboard 200 and earning platinum certification after selling more than 1.2 million copies in the United States ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)). Its lead single, "The Rain (Supa Dupa Fly)", adapted Ann Peebles's 1973 hit "I Can't Stand the Rain", and the album brought Elliott her first Grammy nomination ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)).

## The platinum run, 1997–2005

Elliott's second album, Da Real World (1999), was darker and more experimental, featuring collaborations with Aaliyah, Beyoncé, Lil' Kim and Big Boi ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)). "Hot Boyz" spent eighteen consecutive weeks at the top of the Billboard rap charts and six weeks at number one on the R&B/hip-hop chart, a run that landed it in the Guinness Book of World Records, and it was Elliott's first headlining Top Ten pop hit and first solo platinum single ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/missy-elliott-mn0000502371)). Miss E... So Addictive (2001) became her third platinum album in a row, powered by "One Minute Man" and "Get Ur Freak On"; the latter earned her first Grammy Award and a Soul Train Award ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)). With Under Construction (2002) she became the best-selling female hip-hop artist in the US, and its single "Work It" won a Grammy and spent five weeks atop the Billboard R&B/hip-hop chart ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)). Between the late 1990s and early 2000s she sold more than 7.6 million albums in the United States ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)) — a commercial run that made her the best-selling female rapper in the country by album sales at the time, and the foundation for the hall-of-fame firsts that followed. The Cookbook (2005), her sixth album, was the first Timbaland did not produce most of, and its single "Lose Control" peaked at No. 3 on the Hot 100; the album was certified platinum by the [RIAA](/organizations/riaa/) in January 2022 ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)).

## Songwriter, producer and video pioneer

Elliott's influence runs well beyond her own records. As a producer and songwriter she worked with Mary J. Blige, Whitney Houston, Mariah Carey and Janet Jackson, and Grammy.com credits her with songs for Aaliyah, Beyoncé and Ariana Grande as well ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott); [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/queen-latifah-inducts-missy-elliott-songwriters-hall-fame-lizzo-performs/)). With Timbaland she popularized a new hip-hop sound built on variable syncopated phrasing, snare and hi-hat drums rather than bass, and comic sound effects and vocalizations ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)). Her videos were just as distinctive: she has won eight MTV Video Music Awards, and in 2019 she became the first female rapper to receive the MTV Michael Jackson Video Vanguard Award ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2024/04/08/missy-elliott-goes-out-of-this-world-with-ciara-busta-rhymes-and-timbaland-on-first-arena-headling-tour/); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)). A planned seventh album, at one point titled Block Party, was announced in 2008 but never released; in 2011 Elliott revealed the delay was partly due to Graves' disease, diagnosed three years earlier, which stabilized after treatment ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)). She kept working as a producer and featured artist, performed with Katy Perry at the Super Bowl XLIX halftime show in 2015, and returned with the five-song Iconology EP in 2019, her first release in fourteen years ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)).

## Honours and firsts

Elliott's honours are a run of firsts. On June 13, 2019, she was inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame, becoming the first female rapper to receive the honour and only the third rapper of any gender, after Jay-Z in 2017 and Jermaine Dupri in 2018; Queen Latifah presented the award, Lizzo performed "Sock It 2 Me", and Michelle Obama sent a video message ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/queen-latifah-inducts-missy-elliott-songwriters-hall-fame-lizzo-performs/)). She was the first female rapper ever nominated for the Hall, and inductees qualify only after at least twenty years of hit-writing ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/queen-latifah-inducts-missy-elliott-songwriters-hall-fame-lizzo-performs/)). In 2023 she became the first female hip-hop artist inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, in her first year of eligibility — artists qualify twenty-five years after their first commercial recording — and the Hall called her "a true pathbreaker in a male-dominated genre" ([Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/article/missy-elliott-willie-nelson-and-george-michael-among-rock-hall-inductees/)). The ceremony took place on November 3, 2023, at the Barclays Center in Brooklyn, [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) ([Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/article/missy-elliott-willie-nelson-and-george-michael-among-rock-hall-inductees/)). She received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame in late 2021, saw part of McLean Street in Portsmouth named "Missy Elliott Boulevard" in 2022, and was named a 2022 recipient of the National Medal of Arts, presented by President Joe Biden at the White House in 2024 ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)). In April 2025, Billboard's staff list of the 25 greatest female rappers of all time placed her at No. 2, behind [Nicki Minaj](/people/nicki-minaj/) and ahead of Lauryn Hill, Lil Kim and Queen Latifah ([Black Enterprise](https://www.blackenterprise.com/nicki-minaj-best-female-rapper-of-all-time/)).

## The first headline tour

Remarkably, Elliott waited until 2024 to headline a tour of her own: Out of This World — The Missy Elliott Experience, a 24-city North American arena run from July 4 in Vancouver to August 22 in Rosemont, Illinois, with Busta Rhymes, Ciara and special guest Timbaland, produced by Live Nation ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2024/04/08/missy-elliott-goes-out-of-this-world-with-ciara-busta-rhymes-and-timbaland-on-first-arena-headling-tour/)). "Being the first female hip-hop artist to be inducted into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame and now going out on my first headline tour," she said at the announcement, "this is an incredible time in my life" ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2024/04/08/missy-elliott-goes-out-of-this-world-with-ciara-busta-rhymes-and-timbaland-on-first-arena-headling-tour/)). The tour won the [BET](/organizations/bet/) Hip-Hop Award for Best Live Performer in 2024 and [Pollstar](/organizations/pollstar/)'s Hip-Hop Tour of the Year in 2025 ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is Missy Elliott?** Missy Elliott, born Melissa Arnette Elliott on July 1, 1971, in Portsmouth, Virginia, is an American rapper, singer, songwriter and record producer. She began in the R&B group Sista, wrote and produced hits for Aaliyah and others, then released six platinum solo albums between 1997 and 2005 ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/missy-elliott-mn0000502371); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/womens-studies-and-feminism/missy-elliott)).

**How many Grammy Awards has Missy Elliott won?** She has won four Grammy Awards from 22 nominations, according to the Recording Academy's own artist record. The wins were for "Get Ur Freak On" (2002), "Scream a.k.a. Itchin'" (2003), "Work It" (2004) and the "Lose Control" video (2006) ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/missy-misdemeanor-elliott/11451/)).

**What makes Missy Elliott historically significant?** She was the first female rapper inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame (2019) and the first female hip-hop artist inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame (2023), both in her first year of eligibility for the latter. Billboard also ranked her the second-greatest female rapper of all time in 2025 ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/queen-latifah-inducts-missy-elliott-songwriters-hall-fame-lizzo-performs/); [Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/article/missy-elliott-willie-nelson-and-george-michael-among-rock-hall-inductees/); [Black Enterprise](https://www.blackenterprise.com/nicki-minaj-best-female-rapper-of-all-time/)).

**When did Missy Elliott first headline a tour?** Her first headline tour was Out of This World — The Missy Elliott Experience in 2024, a 24-city North American arena run from July 4 in Vancouver to August 22 in Rosemont, Illinois, with Busta Rhymes, Ciara and Timbaland.
