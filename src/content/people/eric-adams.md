## The "future of the Democratic Party" who ended it in the wilderness

Four years ago, Eric Adams swept into office with swaggering confidence, pledging to lead a government unlike any other in history and declaring himself the "future of the Democratic Party." He delivered on the first promise, if not the way he intended. As his tumultuous term came to an end, Adams, 65, found himself in the political wilderness, his one-time aspirations as a party leader a distant memory ([The Associated Press](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5475888-nyc-mayor-eric-adams-once-called-himself-the-future-of-the-democratic-party-what-went-wrong)).

He will likely be remembered for one superlative: he is the only New York City mayor of the modern era to be indicted while in office. "That's a disappointment for voters, especially for Black voters, who had high expectations and aspirations," said Basil Smikle, a political strategist who once ran the state Democratic Party. "He entered with a lot of political capital, and that was squandered, in part because of his own hubris" ([The Associated Press](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5475888-nyc-mayor-eric-adams-once-called-himself-the-future-of-the-democratic-party-what-went-wrong)).

The strange subplots AP chose to memorialize — his hatred of rats and fear of ghosts, the mysteries about his home, his diet, and his childhood, the catchphrases and head-scratching stories that could turn a mundane bureaucratic event into a meme — are telling. Adams often declined to say who was footing the bills for his meals, his entry into private clubs, or his flights. When reporters staked out his nightlife, they found that Adams, who long professed to be a vegan, regularly ordered the branzino. Asked about it, he acknowledged eating fish and occasionally "nibbling" on chicken, describing himself as "perfectly imperfect" ([The Associated Press](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5475888-nyc-mayor-eric-adams-once-called-himself-the-future-of-the-democratic-party-what-went-wrong)).

But the fuller story — the one no reference bio traces as a single arc — is how a teenager beaten by cops became a cop-reformer, then a tough-on-crime mayor, then a defendant whose freedom was negotiated between a president's lawyer and a federal judge.

## Beaten at 14, a captain by retirement

Adams was born September 1, 1960, in Brooklyn, the fourth of six children, and raised in poverty — first in Brownsville, then in South Jamaica, Queens, where the family frequently faced eviction ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/education/news/eric-adams-educational-qualification-how-a-dyslexic-student-turned-nypd-captain-and-broke-ranks-for-an-independent-mayoral-run/articleshow/122107771.cms)). At 14 he joined a gang and was arrested for criminal trespassing. In police custody, he was allegedly beaten by NYPD officers — an experience that, as Adams has said in multiple interviews, left him with post-traumatic stress disorder and motivated him to join law enforcement to change it from within. Encouraged by community leaders like the Reverend Herbert Daughtry, he turned his life around, graduating from Bayside High School in Queens ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/education/news/eric-adams-educational-qualification-how-a-dyslexic-student-turned-nypd-captain-and-broke-ranks-for-an-independent-mayoral-run/articleshow/122107771.cms)).

He earned an associate's degree from New York City College of Technology, a bachelor's from John Jay College of Criminal Justice, and a Master of Public Administration from Marist College — crediting a dyslexia diagnosis in college with transforming his academic performance, and becoming an advocate for early dyslexia screening in public schools ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/education/news/eric-adams-educational-qualification-how-a-dyslexic-student-turned-nypd-captain-and-broke-ranks-for-an-independent-mayoral-run/articleshow/122107771.cms)).

In 1984 Adams joined the New York City Transit Police, which later merged with the NYPD. Over 22 years he rose to captain — and spent much of that time fighting the department from inside. In 1995 he co-founded 100 Blacks in Law Enforcement Who Care, an advocacy group for Black officers, and frequently criticized racial profiling. He was eventually investigated by Internal Affairs for speaking out in uniform, an episode that led to his retirement in 2006 ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/education/news/eric-adams-educational-qualification-how-a-dyslexic-student-turned-nypd-captain-and-broke-ranks-for-an-independent-mayoral-run/articleshow/122107771.cms)). He also courted early controversy by appearing with Nation of Islam leader Louis Farrakhan ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/education/news/eric-adams-educational-qualification-how-a-dyslexic-student-turned-nypd-captain-and-broke-ranks-for-an-independent-mayoral-run/articleshow/122107771.cms)).

## The decades-long run for City Hall

Adams entered politics in 2006, winning a State Senate seat in central Brooklyn, a place he has called home for more than 30 years. From 2007 to 2013 he supported marriage equality, gun control, and police accountability ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/education/news/eric-adams-educational-qualification-how-a-dyslexic-student-turned-nypd-captain-and-broke-ranks-for-an-independent-mayoral-run/articleshow/122107771.cms); [Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/management/2021/11/eric-adams-elected-nyc-mayor/186602/?oref=rf-next-story)).

In 2013 he was elected Brooklyn Borough President — the first Black American to hold the office — and was re-elected in 2017. His tenure mixed affordable-housing advocacy, public-health campaigns like plant-based diets, and education access through free community college with criticism over the nonprofit One Brooklyn Fund, which prompted investigations into donor influence ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/education/news/eric-adams-educational-qualification-how-a-dyslexic-student-turned-nypd-captain-and-broke-ranks-for-an-independent-mayoral-run/articleshow/122107771.cms)).

He had planned his mayoral run for decades. In June 2021 he emerged from a hotly contested, ranked-choice Democratic primary, and on November 2 he beat Republican [Curtis Sliwa](/people/curtis-sliwa/) 67% to 27%, becoming the city's second Black mayor. "After years of praying and hoping and struggling and working I did it: City Hall," he said that night ([Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/management/2021/11/eric-adams-elected-nyc-mayor/186602/?oref=rf-next-story)).

He inherited a city facing what he called a three-headed crisis: "We are fighting COVID, crime and economic devastation all at once." He increased street and subway patrols, brought back a controversial plainclothes anti-crime unit disbanded under his predecessor, appointed the department's first female police commissioner, and pitched himself as a blue-collar mayor who would work with big business ([Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/management/2021/11/eric-adams-elected-nyc-mayor/186602/?oref=rf-next-story); [The Associated Press](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5475888-nyc-mayor-eric-adams-once-called-himself-the-future-of-the-democratic-party-what-went-wrong)). In November 2021 he tweeted that he would take his first three paychecks as mayor in bitcoin and declared New York City would become "the center of the cryptocurrency industry" ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/education/news/eric-adams-educational-qualification-how-a-dyslexic-student-turned-nypd-captain-and-broke-ranks-for-an-independent-mayoral-run/articleshow/122107771.cms)).

## The indictment

The corruption investigation into Adams' campaign, launched quietly in the early stages of his mayoralty, first spilled into public view in the fall of 2023, when federal agents seized the mayor's phones as he was leaving an event ([The Associated Press](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5475888-nyc-mayor-eric-adams-once-called-himself-the-future-of-the-democratic-party-what-went-wrong)). On September 26, 2024, federal prosecutors unsealed a 57-page indictment: conspiracy to commit wire fraud, bribery, and receiving campaign contributions from a foreign national, describing a decade-long trail that began in his Brooklyn years ([SRN/AP](https://srnnews.com/a-look-inside-the-indictment-accusing-new-york-citys-mayor-of-taking-bribes/)).

The details were lurid. Prosecutors said Adams took trips to France, China, Sri Lanka, India, Hungary, Ghana, and Turkey that were partly or fully paid for by people looking to buy his influence — free hotel rooms, seat upgrades worth thousands, expensive meals, entertainment, "even a trip to a Turkish bath," totaling more than $100,000. He allegedly returned the favor in September 2021 by ensuring Turkey's newly built diplomatic tower in Manhattan would skip a fire inspection it was certain to fail, and agreed not to release a statement on Armenian Genocide Remembrance Day ([SRN/AP](https://srnnews.com/a-look-inside-the-indictment-accusing-new-york-citys-mayor-of-taking-bribes/)). A Turkish official called him a "true friend of Turkey"; Adams allegedly replied, "Yes even more a true friend of yours. You are my brother. I am hear (sic) to help" ([SRN/AP](https://srnnews.com/a-look-inside-the-indictment-accusing-new-york-citys-mayor-of-taking-bribes/)).

Prosecutors also accused him of conspiring to collect illegal donations through straw donors — including a plan, allegedly approved by Adams, to funnel up to $100,000 in banned contributions through U.S. employees of a Turkish university — that helped him qualify for more than $10 million in public campaign funds. The indictment said Adams produced two phones to the FBI but withheld his personal one, later turned it over locked, and claimed to have forgotten the password; a staffer deleted an encrypted messaging app in a bathroom during the investigation ([SRN/AP](https://srnnews.com/a-look-inside-the-indictment-accusing-new-york-citys-mayor-of-taking-bribes/)).

Adams pleaded not guilty on September 27, 2024 — "I am not guilty, your honor," he told the judge — and vowed to stay in office. His lawyer Alex Spiro dismissed the case as "the airline upgrade corruption case," arguing the consulate had asked for a "courtesy, not payback" ([SRN/AP](https://srnnews.com/a-look-inside-the-indictment-accusing-new-york-citys-mayor-of-taking-bribes/)). Adams insisted, without evidence, that the Biden administration had politically targeted him for criticizing its immigration policy ([The Associated Press](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5475888-nyc-mayor-eric-adams-once-called-himself-the-future-of-the-democratic-party-what-went-wrong)).

The scandal metastasized across City Hall: phones seized from the police commissioner, the schools chancellor, and multiple deputy mayors; a mass exodus of leadership; a chief adviser indicted by state prosecutors in a separate alleged bribery scheme involving a bike lane and a minor TV role; another longtime adviser forced to resign after handing a chip bag filled with cash to a reporter; and corruption allegations within the police department linked to longtime friends Adams had installed ([The Associated Press](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5475888-nyc-mayor-eric-adams-once-called-himself-the-future-of-the-democratic-party-what-went-wrong)). Supporters and critics agreed on one diagnosis: Adams was loyal to a fault, refusing to distance himself from long-serving allies even after they appeared to cross ethical lines. Former first deputy mayor Sheena Wright described "two City Halls" — one of competent leaders executing priorities, another of people "who knew the mayor for a long time, and who were allowed to operate outside the norms of government" ([The Associated Press](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5475888-nyc-mayor-eric-adams-once-called-himself-the-future-of-the-democratic-party-what-went-wrong)).

## The bargain and the dismissal

Facing years in prison and a plummeting approval rating, Adams began aligning himself with President Donald Trump, avoiding criticism of the Republican and even leaving open the possibility of switching parties. Weeks after Trump took office, the Justice Department dismissed the case, writing in a two-page memo that it had interfered with Adams' ability to help with the president's immigration agenda ([The Associated Press](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5475888-nyc-mayor-eric-adams-once-called-himself-the-future-of-the-democratic-party-what-went-wrong)).

The memo, as acting Deputy Attorney General [Emil Bove](/people/emil-bove/) framed it, ordered Manhattan prosecutors to drop the case. Then-acting U.S. Attorney Danielle Sassoon resisted, saying she couldn't defend a dismissal linked to political considerations — and quit, along with several other career prosecutors in New York and Washington. Bove then stood stone-faced and alone at the prosecution table in Manhattan to seek the dismissal himself, later telling the office: "There are no separate sovereigns in this executive branch" ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/); [SRN/AP](https://srnnews.com/judge-dismisses-corruption-case-against-new-york-city-mayor-eric-adams/)).

Judge Dale E. Ho's April 2, 2025 ruling gave Adams what he wanted and more. Ho agreed dismissal was the only practical outcome — courts can't force prosecutors to move forward — but dismissed the case "with prejudice," denying the Justice Department the option to refile after the election. "Everything here smacks of a bargain: dismissal of the indictment in exchange for immigration policy concessions," Ho wrote, calling it "disturbing" that public officials might get special treatment by complying with policy goals. He did not opine on the merits ([SRN/AP](https://srnnews.com/judge-dismisses-corruption-case-against-new-york-city-mayor-eric-adams/)).

Adams lauded the decision: "As I said all along, this case never should have been brought, and I did nothing wrong." Then, holding up a copy of FBI Director Kash Patel's 2023 book "Government Gangsters," he encouraged people to read it ([SRN/AP](https://srnnews.com/judge-dismisses-corruption-case-against-new-york-city-mayor-eric-adams/)).

But politically, the dismissal was the beginning of the end. In the view of Evan Thies, one of Adams' closest advisers, "the memo hit like a nuclear bomb." Days later Adams appeared on Fox & Friends alongside Trump's border czar Tom Homan, who threatened to "be up his butt" if the mayor didn't comply with Trump's agenda. "It seemed to confirm the belief that he had traded his duty to New Yorkers for his personal freedom," Thies recalled. "It wasn't true, but that was perception." Adams denied striking any deal, at times blaming bureaucrats in the "deep state" ([The Associated Press](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5475888-nyc-mayor-eric-adams-once-called-himself-the-future-of-the-democratic-party-what-went-wrong)).

## The exit: withdrawal, endorsement, succession

Adams skipped the 2025 Democratic primary and ran for re-election as an independent — the same race [Zohran Mamdani](/people/zohran-mamdani/) entered in 2024 and would win. With approval at a record low and fundraising dwindling, Adams abandoned the campaign in September 2025, blaming the shadow of the dismissed federal case ([The Associated Press](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5475888-nyc-mayor-eric-adams-once-called-himself-the-future-of-the-democratic-party-what-went-wrong); [Reuters](https://srnnews.com/outgoing-new-york-mayor-adams-endorses-cuomo-for-mayor-attacks-mamdani/)).

On October 23, 2025, he endorsed former Governor [Andrew Cuomo](/people/andrew-cuomo/) — a one-time rival he had recently called a "snake and a liar" — to stop Mamdani, whom he branded a "snake-oil salesman." Cuomo called Mamdani an "existential threat"; Adams pledged to campaign in "Black and brown communities" for him. Mamdani shot back: "Andrew Cuomo is running for Eric Adams' second term… two men who share an affinity for corruption and Trump capitulation" ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/outgoing-new-york-mayor-adams-endorses-cuomo-for-mayor-attacks-mamdani/); [The Associated Press](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5475888-nyc-mayor-eric-adams-once-called-himself-the-future-of-the-democratic-party-what-went-wrong)).

Adams closed his term burying a time capsule of his achievements beneath a Manhattan sidewalk, telling City Hall: "I did what I had to do, I left everything I had on the ice, and I'm looking forward to the next step of my journey." His plans after office remained uncertain ([The Associated Press](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/5475888-nyc-mayor-eric-adams-once-called-himself-the-future-of-the-democratic-party-what-went-wrong)).
