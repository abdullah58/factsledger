## Overview

Every biography of [Michael](/creative-works/michael-2026-film/) Jackson ends with his death on June 25, 2009. That is
exactly what makes the standard biographies incomplete. Jackson died with
enormous financial debts, and then something happened that no obituary
anticipated: the estate run by the two men named in his will — attorney John
Branca and music executive [John McClain](/people/john-mcclain/) — turned his music into the most valuable
posthumous entertainment franchise in history, with an estimated $3.5 billion in
earnings since 2009
([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson);
[Music Ally](https://musically.com/2025/11/04/michael-jackson-is-the-highest-paid-dead-celebrity-of-2025/)).
In 2024 Sony bought half of Jackson's publishing and recorded masters catalog for
at least $600 million — described as the largest-ever valuation of a single
musician's music assets — and in 2026 that estate money produced the biopic
about Jackson himself, the first biopic ever to cross $1 billion at the box
office
([Consequence](https://consequence.net/2024/02/michael-jackson-estate-sells-catalog-sony-music/);
[Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/john-mcclain-co-executor-of-the-michael-jackson-estate-and-veteran-music-executive-dies-at-71/)).
Months before that film's release, McClain — who had shared control of Jackson's
legacy for 17 years — died in Los Angeles at 71
([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/john-mcclain-co-executor-of-the-michael-jackson-estate-and-veteran-music-executive-dies-at-71/)).
The story of Jackson the performer is the most documented in pop history; the
story of Jackson the posthumous business empire is the one the reference pages
never wrote.

## The artist: from Gary, Indiana to the best-selling album ever

Michael Joseph Jackson was born August 29, 1958, in Gary, Indiana, one of ten
children born to Joseph "Joe" Jackson and Katherine (née Scruse) Jackson — one
child died shortly after birth ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)).
Joe, a former boxer who worked as a crane operator in a steel mill and played
guitar in a blues band, shaped five of his sons into the Jackson 5 and made the
five-year-old Michael its lead singer
([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)). Family
members later described Joe's discipline as including physical abuse — "if you
messed up, you got hit, sometimes with a belt, sometimes with a switch," as
Michael recounted — and the brothers rehearsed hours daily before playing clubs
on the Chitlin Circuit
([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)). Motown
Records president Berry Gordy Jr. signed the group in 1969, and they became an
immediate success, scoring four consecutive number-one pop hits in 1970 with "I
Want You Back," "ABC," "The Love You Save" and "I'll Be There," with Michael
singled out by critics as a "prodigy"
([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)).

Jackson's solo career, still as a Motown artist at first, broke through when the
group moved to Epic as the Jacksons and he paired with producer Quincy Jones.
*Off the Wall* (1979) was the best-selling album of the year and eventually sold
more than 20 million copies, yielding the hits "Don't Stop 'til You Get Enough"
and "Rock with You"
([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)). Three
years later came *Thriller* (1982): it stayed on the charts for more than two
years, sold more than 65 million copies globally — the best-selling album in
history — and captured seven Grammys, with Jackson winning an eighth that night
for his narration of the *E.T.: The Extra-Terrestrial* audiobook, the first
artist ever to win eight Grammys in one night
([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)). The
follow-up single "Billie Jean" gave him his vehicle for the trademark moonwalk,
which he debuted on the May 1983 television special *Motown 25: Yesterday, Today,
Forever* — a moment that made him a pop-culture phenomenon
([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)). By 1984 he
was known worldwide as the "King of Pop"; *Bad* (1987) produced five
chart-topping hits including the title song and "Man in the Mirror," and he went
on to release *Dangerous* (1991), *HIStory: Past, Present, and Future, Book I*
(1995) and *Invincible* (2001), his last studio album
([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)). Across his
career he won 13 Grammy Awards out of 38 nominations and was inducted into the
Rock and Roll Hall of Fame twice — in 1997 as a member of the Jackson 5 and in
2001 as a solo artist
([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)).

## Allegations, trials and a divided legacy

Jackson's artistic dominance ran alongside a reputation that was permanently
divided. In 1993 he was accused of child molestation by a 13-year-old boy he had
befriended; a civil suit was settled out of court
([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)). He married
twice in the years around the scandal — secretly to Lisa Marie Presley, Elvis
Presley's daughter, in 1994 (divorced in 1996), then to nurse Debbie Rowe, with
whom he had two children, Michael Joseph (called Prince) and Paris-Michael
Katherine (called Paris), before their divorce; a third child, Prince Michael
(nicknamed Blanket, later Bigi), was born via a surrogate mother in 2002
([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)). In
November 2003 Jackson was arrested and charged with child molestation involving a
different boy than the 1993 case; actor Macaulay Culkin, who had befriended
Jackson as a nine-year-old, testified in his defense and said he had never
experienced anything inappropriate with him — and after a 14-week trial that
became a media circus, Jackson was acquitted in 2005
([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)).

In the wake of those events Jackson suffered a financial collapse that forced
the sale of many of his considerable assets, including ultimately his Neverland
Ranch in Los Olivos, California
([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)). He was
preparing a 50-concert comeback engagement in London when he died of cardiac
arrest on June 25, 2009; in August the coroner ruled his death a homicide caused
by a lethal combination of sedatives and propofol, an anesthetic, and in November
2011 his personal physician was found guilty of involuntary manslaughter
([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)). A public
memorial at the Staples Center in Los Angeles on July 7 drew tributes from
Stevie Wonder, Berry Gordy Jr., Brooke Shields and others, and the rehearsal
footage film *This Is It* premiered that October
([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)). One of the
long-running tabloid controversies — that Jackson had bleached his skin to look
white — was answered by the medical record: he revealed a vitiligo diagnosis in
a 1993 televised interview, saying the change began in the early 1980s, and the
2009 autopsy report confirmed he had vitiligo
([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)).

## The posthumous empire

Jackson's will named Branca and McClain as co-executors, and together they built
what the music press describes as an unprecedented posthumous franchise: two
posthumous albums (*Michael* and *Xscape*), two Cirque du Soleil productions, the
four-time Tony-winning Broadway musical *MJ The Musical*, two documentaries
directed by Spike Lee, the concert film *This Is It* and the biopic *Michael*
([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/john-mcclain-co-executor-of-the-michael-jackson-estate-and-veteran-music-executive-dies-at-71/)).
The centerpiece was the catalog: Jackson had bought the ATV publishing catalog —
including the majority of the Beatles' publishing rights — and merged his stake
with Sony Music in 1995 to form Sony/ATV Music Publishing, which Sony bought
outright in 2016 for around $750 million
([Consequence](https://consequence.net/2024/02/michael-jackson-estate-sells-catalog-sony-music/)).
In February 2024 Sony Music agreed to buy half of Jackson's remaining publishing
and recorded masters for at least $600 million, valuing his music assets at over
$1.2 billion — expected to be the largest-ever valuation of a musician's music
assets; the deal excluded theatrical royalties but included non-Jackson songs in
his Mijac publishing catalog, such as works by Sly & the Family Stone, Curtis
Mayfield and Ray Charles, and a California appeals court later that year cleared
the estate to proceed
([Consequence](https://consequence.net/2024/02/michael-jackson-estate-sells-catalog-sony-music/);
[Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/john-mcclain-co-executor-of-the-michael-jackson-estate-and-veteran-music-executive-dies-at-71/)).

The result: Forbes named Jackson the highest-paid dead celebrity of 2025 with an
estimated $105 million — his 13th time at number one in the 16 full calendar
years since his death — and put the estate's total earnings since 2009 at an
estimated $3.5 billion, driven by streaming royalties, five productions of *MJ:
The Musical* and the long-running Las Vegas Cirque du Soleil show
([Music Ally](https://musically.com/2025/11/04/michael-jackson-is-the-highest-paid-dead-celebrity-of-2025/)).
The estate has also been the shield for his reputation: it sued HBO over the
2019 documentary *Leaving Neverland*, whose subjects were not part of the 1993
or 2005 cases, calling the film "a complete fiction" — and in 2024 that case was
dismissed after a settlement that removed the documentary from the channel's
programming
([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)). The 2026
biopic *Michael*, produced by Graham King alongside the two co-executors and
starring Jackson's nephew Jaafar Jackson, ends its narrative in the late 1980s —
the scenes that would have confronted the 1993 allegations were rewritten after
lawyers found a clause in the 1993 settlement barring depiction of the accuser —
and its release gave the catalog a fresh boost, with *Thriller* reentering the
*Billboard* 200 and topping the R&B/hip-hop albums chart again
([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/john-mcclain-co-executor-of-the-michael-jackson-estate-and-veteran-music-executive-dies-at-71/);
[Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/michael-jackson)). For the
full production history of that film, see this site's profile:
[Michael (2026 film)](https://factsledger.com/creative-works/michael-2026-film).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not take a position on the truth of the allegations against
Jackson — it reports only what the legal record shows (the 1993 civil suit
settled out of court; the 2005 acquittal; the estate's denial of the *Leaving
Neverland* claims) and does not repeat the claims themselves as facts. It does
not assert a single figure for the 2024 Sony deal: outlets report at least $600
million on an over-$1.2-billion valuation, with one trade outlet putting the
valuation as high as $1.5 billion, and both are recorded as reported in
openItems. It does not state a precise figure for the estate's debts at Jackson's
death — only that they were described as enormous. It does not repeat
Britannica's account of a 2026 lawsuit against the estate by a family of five
children, which rests on a single account read for this profile and is flagged
in openItems rather than asserted. It makes no claim about the 2026 biopic's
exact box-office totals, which are documented in this site's dedicated film
profile, and it does not cover Jackson's siblings, children or the wider Jackson
family beyond what the sources state. The Forbes earnings figures are the
publication's estimates, reported as such, not independently audited accounts.
