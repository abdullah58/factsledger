## Overview

Matthew Prince is the co-founder, chief executive officer and co-chair of [Cloudflare](/organizations/cloudflare/), the internet-infrastructure company he started in 2009 with [Lee Holloway](/people/lee-holloway/) and [Michelle Zatlyn](/people/michelle-zatlyn/) ([Harvard Business School Club of New York](https://www.hbscny.org/events/register-virtual-how-cloudflareas-founders-matthew-prince-hbs-a09-and-michelle-zatlyn-hbs-a09-are-helping-to-build-a-better-internet/)). A lawyer by training who wrote his first computer program at age seven, Prince built his career at the junction of internet law and spam-fighting, co-creating the open-source [Project Honey Pot](/organizations/project-honey-pot/) before turning it into a company that now sits in front of a large share of the web ([Black Hat](https://blackhat.com/us-13/speakers/Matthew-Prince.html)). He is also the public face of Cloudflare's hardest calls — terminating extremist customers — and, since 2025, a campaigner to buy the Utah ski resort where he grew up.

## From spam-hunting to Cloudflare

Prince was born in 1974 and grew up in Park City, Utah, where he later worked as a ski instructor at Park City Mountain Resort ([The Storm Skiing Journal](https://www.stormskiing.com/p/the-storm-10132026-cloudflare-ceo)). He studied English literature and computer science at Trinity College, earned a law degree from the University of Chicago, and is a member of the Illinois Bar ([Harvard Business School Club of New York](https://www.hbscny.org/events/register-virtual-how-cloudflareas-founders-matthew-prince-hbs-a09-and-michelle-zatlyn-hbs-a09-are-helping-to-build-a-better-internet/)). After law school he worked as an attorney for a single day before joining a startup, and he taught technology law as an adjunct professor at the John Marshall Law School in Chicago ([Black Hat](https://blackhat.com/us-13/speakers/Matthew-Prince.html)).

In 2004 Prince and Holloway set out to answer a narrow question — where does email spam come from? — and built Project Honey Pot, a system that let website owners track how spammers harvested email addresses ([Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com/our-story/)). The project grew into a community of thousands of websites in more than 185 countries, and Prince co-founded the anti-spam company Unspam Technologies around it, remaining its chairman as of 2019 ([Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com/our-story/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/08/15/cloudflare-has-a-third-cofounder-lee-holloway-whos-credited-with-making-the-company-what-is-today/)).

The turn into Cloudflare came at [Harvard Business School](/universities/harvard-business-school/), where Prince took a sabbatical to earn his MBA in 2009, graduating as a George F. Baker Scholar and winning the Dubilier Prize for Entrepreneurship ([Harvard Business School Club of New York](https://www.hbscny.org/events/register-virtual-how-cloudflareas-founders-matthew-prince-hbs-a09-and-michelle-zatlyn-hbs-a09-are-helping-to-build-a-better-internet/)). On a class trip to [Silicon Valley](/places/silicon-valley/) he described Project Honey Pot to classmate Michelle Zatlyn, who saw the chance to stop internet threats rather than only track them; that evening the two sketched the business model on a napkin, recruited HBS professor Eisenmann as an advisor, and enlisted Holloway to build the prototype ([Harvard Business School Club of New York](https://www.hbscny.org/events/register-virtual-how-cloudflareas-founders-matthew-prince-hbs-a09-and-michelle-zatlyn-hbs-a09-are-helping-to-build-a-better-internet/)). They won the Harvard Business School Business Plan Competition in April 2009, closed Series A funding that November with Venrock and Pelion Venture Partners, and launched publicly on 27 September 2010 at TechCrunch Disrupt in [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) ([Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com/our-story/)).

Cloudflare listed on the [New York Stock Exchange](/organizations/new-york-stock-exchange/) in September 2019 under the ticker NET ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/cloudflares-ipo-filing-at-a-glance-rising-revenue-falling-losses-and-risky-customers)). At the IPO Prince owned 20.2% of the company's Class B shares and 16.6% of all shares outstanding ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/08/15/cloudflare-has-a-third-cofounder-lee-holloway-whos-credited-with-making-the-company-what-is-today/)). He has led the company as chief executive since its founding, and by February 2025 he was signing SEC filings as chief executive officer and co-chair of the board, sharing the chair with Zatlyn ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1477333/000147733325000045/cloudflares-82025.htm)). Outside the company he is a World Economic Forum Technology Pioneer, a member of the Council on Foreign Relations, and winner of the 2011 Tech Fellow Award ([Harvard Business School Club of New York](https://www.hbscny.org/events/register-virtual-how-cloudflareas-founders-matthew-prince-hbs-a09-and-michelle-zatlyn-hbs-a09-are-helping-to-build-a-better-internet/)).

## The content-moderation decisions

Prince's most public leadership test came over customers Cloudflare would rather not have. In August 2017 the company terminated the neo-Nazi site the [Daily Stormer](/organizations/daily-stormer/), and on 5 August 2019 it terminated [8chan](/organizations/8chan/), the forum where the El Paso gunman appears to have posted his screed minutes before killing 20 people at a Walmart — the same pattern seen before the [Christchurch](/events/christchurch-mosque-shootings/) and Poway attacks ([Cloudflare Blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/terminating-service-for-8chan/)). Prince's own post put the rationale bluntly: 8chan had "proven themselves to be lawless and that lawlessness has caused multiple tragic deaths" ([Cloudflare Blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/terminating-service-for-8chan/)).

What makes the episode distinctive is how uncomfortable Prince said he was with the power he had just exercised. The post argues that Cloudflare, as a private company, is not bound by the First Amendment, and that the real question is the Rule of Law — policies must be transparent and consistent, and a network provider has no political legitimacy to decide what content is good or bad ([Cloudflare Blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/terminating-service-for-8chan/)). He also admitted the limits of the act: "Unfortunately the action we take today won't fix hate online… It will almost certainly not even remove 8chan from the Internet. But it is the right thing to do" ([Cloudflare Blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/terminating-service-for-8chan/)). The tension — a self-described near free-speech absolutist repeatedly forced to draw a line — has defined his public profile ever since, and Cloudflare's IPO filing treated the terminations as a formal risk factor ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/cloudflares-ipo-filing-at-a-glance-rising-revenue-falling-losses-and-risky-customers)).

## The Park City campaign

Since 2025 Prince has run a second, very local campaign: persuading Vail Resorts to sell him Park City Mountain Resort, the largest ski area in the United States, where he grew up skiing ([SnowBrains](https://snowbrains.com/activist-investor-reportedly-circling-vail-resorts-as-prince-pushes-for-change-at-park-city-mountain-resort-ut/)). He owns the town's Park Record newspaper, and in a June 2026 interview with the Colorado Sun he said he was ready to invest about $500 million in the resort, focused on snowmaking, lift upgrades and sharing profits with employees ([SnowBrains](https://snowbrains.com/activist-investor-reportedly-circling-vail-resorts-as-prince-pushes-for-change-at-park-city-mountain-resort-ut/)). He has pledged to reinvest every dollar the ski area earns back into the mountain and the people who work there, and envisions a car-free Main Street and a gondola connection over the peaks to Solitude and Alta ([The Storm Skiing Journal](https://www.stormskiing.com/p/the-storm-10132026-cloudflare-ceo)).

| | Prince's position | Vail Resorts' position |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Park City Mountain | Sell it to local ownership; ~$500 million for lifts, snowmaking and employee profit-sharing | Not for sale; no plans to sell any properties |
| The wider company | Move to an "asset-light" model: keep selling the Epic Pass, sell the underlying resorts | Keep the portfolio; Vail says it has no plans to sell any of its properties |
| The endgame | "Local ownership of PCMR is inevitable" (October 2026) | Vail maintains the resort is not for sale |

The standoff has spilled into Vail's boardroom: Semafor reported in June 2026 that activist investor Oasis Capital Management was pressing Vail to make changes and consider selling some of its mountains, and Vail hired a takeover-defense firm in response ([SnowBrains](https://snowbrains.com/activist-investor-reportedly-circling-vail-resorts-as-prince-pushes-for-change-at-park-city-mountain-resort-ut/)). As of late 2026 the resort had not been sold, and the campaign remained unresolved.

## FAQ

### Who is Matthew Prince?

Matthew Prince is the co-founder, chief executive officer and co-chair of Cloudflare, the internet-infrastructure and security company he founded in 2009 with Lee Holloway and Michelle Zatlyn. Trained as a lawyer, with degrees from Trinity College, the University of Chicago and Harvard Business School, he previously co-created the anti-spam system Project Honey Pot.

### How did Matthew Prince start Cloudflare?

Prince and Lee Holloway built Project Honey Pot in 2004 to trace email spam. At Harvard Business School in 2009, Prince told classmate Michelle Zatlyn about it; they sketched a business on a napkin, won the school's Business Plan Competition, raised Series A funding that November, and launched publicly in September 2010.

### Why did Cloudflare terminate 8chan?

After the August 2019 [El Paso shooting](/events/el-paso-shooting-2019/), whose gunman appears to have posted his manifesto on 8chan beforehand, Prince terminated the forum as a customer, saying it had "proven themselves to be lawless and that lawlessness has caused multiple tragic deaths". He stressed the decision would not fix online hate.

### Is Matthew Prince buying Park City Mountain Resort?

Not yet. Since 2025 he has publicly pressed Vail Resorts to sell him the resort and says he would invest about $500 million in lifts, snowmaking and employee profit-sharing. Vail says the resort is not for sale and has no plans to sell any of its properties.
