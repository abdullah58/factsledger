## Overview

Munish Varma is an investor who ran SoftBank Vision Fund's India business from 2017, leading its first big Indian bets — $200 million into PolicyBazaar and $400 million into FirstCry in 2018 — before leaving [SoftBank](/organizations/softbank-group/) in 2022 to join Rajeev Misra's One Investment Management in Abu Dhabi, where he is a licensed director. Before SoftBank he spent more than a decade in credit trading at Deutsche Bank and Nomura ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/leadership/cover-story-why-softbank-changed-gears-in-india/69585/1); [BusinessMirror](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2024/01/29/financier-entrusted-with-middle-east-billions-attempts-comeback/)).

Varma is a career credit man who crossed into venture capital late. He holds a Bachelor of Engineering in Chemical Engineering from Mangalore University and an MBA from Cornell University, and his pre-SoftBank career was built in the structured-credit businesses of Deutsche Bank and Nomura in [London](/places/london/) ([US Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1837238/000119312521030688/d16631ds1.htm)). At SoftBank he oversaw investment teams in Europe and India, led the fund's investments in OakNorth and PolicyBazaar, and chaired the group's $200 million SPAC, SVF Investment Corp. 2 ([US Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1837238/000119312521030688/d16631ds1.htm)). His second act, at One Investment Management, is a deliberate change of asset class: credit, not venture equity — the field he started in ([BusinessMirror](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2024/01/29/financier-entrusted-with-middle-east-billions-attempts-comeback/)).

## From credit trading to venture capital

Varma's route into investing ran through the credit markets, not the startup world. In October 2011 he joined Nomura as global head of structured lending and special situations, based in London and reporting to Georges Assi, global head of credit products, and Kieran Higgins, co-head of fixed income for Europe, the Middle East and Africa ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/people/2117710/nomura-hires-new-structured-lending-head)). He arrived after a 12-year stint at Deutsche Bank, according to Risk.net — though a later report put his time in Deutsche Bank's global markets division at 10 years, a discrepancy flagged in the open items below ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/people/2117710/nomura-hires-new-structured-lending-head); [IPE](http://www.ipe.com/news/people/wednesday-people-roundup/10004856.article)).

Three years later he was back at Deutsche Bank in a different guise. In November 2014, Deutsche Asset & Wealth Management hired him as head of structured solutions in its loans and deposits group, a newly created role in London reporting to Balaji Prasanna, head of lending, deposit products and wealth planning solutions ([IPE](http://www.ipe.com/news/people/wednesday-people-roundup/10004856.article)). He came from Nomura, where he had most recently been global head of structured credit ([IPE](http://www.ipe.com/news/people/wednesday-people-roundup/10004856.article)). The through-line of this period is illiquid credit: structured lending, special situations, and principal investments — the same judgement about downside and cash flow that would later shape his investing style at SoftBank, where he said the fund looked for a positive contribution margin rather than immediate profit ([Banking Gateway](https://www.banking-gateway.com/analysis/softbank-investment-advisers-vision-fund/)).

## SoftBank's India build-out

Varma joined SoftBank in March 2017, just two months before the $100 billion Vision Fund was officially launched in May 2017 ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/leadership/cover-story-why-softbank-changed-gears-in-india/69585/1)). As a managing partner at SoftBank Investment Advisers he was responsible for investments in [India](/places/india/), alongside fintech investments in the [United States](/places/united-states/) ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/leadership/cover-story-why-softbank-changed-gears-in-india/69585/1)). Towards the end of 2017 he was made responsible for India investments outright, at a time when SoftBank had no team on the ground and its India deals were handled by fund managers flying in and out ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/leadership/cover-story-why-softbank-changed-gears-in-india/69585/1)).

His first cheques set the pattern. With Rajeev Misra, the Vision Fund's chief executive, he made the fund's first investments in India in 2018: $200 million in online insurer PolicyBazaar and $400 million in baby-products retailer FirstCry ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/leadership/cover-story-why-softbank-changed-gears-in-india/69585/1)). The early deals, Varma said, "strengthened our conviction about India and the confidence to build a team on the ground," and the fund began hiring in [Mumbai](/places/mumbai/) from December 2018, bringing in Sumer Juneja to head investing in India and building a 12-member team in the Ceejay House offices in Worli ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/leadership/cover-story-why-softbank-changed-gears-in-india/69585/1)). Deployment followed: about $1 billion in 2018, $1.9 billion in 2019, a pandemic-slowed $221 million in 2020, then $2.46 billion in the first six months of 2021 alone ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/leadership/cover-story-why-softbank-changed-gears-in-india/69585/1)).

Varma's public account of the Vision Fund's method came in May 2019 at the Innovate Finance Global Summit, where he appeared with OakNorth chief executive Rishi Khosla ([Banking Gateway](https://www.banking-gateway.com/analysis/softbank-investment-advisers-vision-fund/)). He named three tests for any investment — the quality of the founding team, the size and potential of the market, and the timing — and said the fund, then holding 70 portfolio companies, looked for businesses whose product could scale at the right moment rather than for early-stage bets ([Banking Gateway](https://www.banking-gateway.com/analysis/softbank-investment-advisers-vision-fund/)). Profitability was not a prerequisite, he said: "I don't need you to show a profit. What you do need to show at some point is a contribution margin that is positive" ([Banking Gateway](https://www.banking-gateway.com/analysis/softbank-investment-advisers-vision-fund/)). The same long courtship applied to Meesho, whose founders the fund engaged for 15 to 18 months before its $210 million investment in 2021; Meesho was valued at $2.1 billion after a $300 million Series E that April, led by SoftBank ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/leadership/cover-story-why-softbank-changed-gears-in-india/69585/1)).

The fintech brief also produced the deal that ties Varma most directly to this site's other profiles. In February 2020, SoftBank Vision Fund 2 invested $100 million in [Behavox](/organizations/behavox/), the AI compliance-surveillance company ([AiThority](https://aithority.com/hot-startups/behavox-announces-100-million-investment-from-softbank-vision-fund-2/)). What made the round unusual was that SoftBank Investment Advisers was already a Behavox customer — it had bought the platform and used it before investing, performing its due diligence as a user first-hand ([AiThority](https://aithority.com/hot-startups/behavox-announces-100-million-investment-from-softbank-vision-fund-2/)). Varma's quoted verdict was emphatic: "We believe Behavox is at the forefront of the AI revolution… We've experienced the incredible insights and sophisticated analysis of the Behavox platform and are firm believers in their mission to organize all corporate communications data on earth to add value for successful companies" ([AiThority](https://aithority.com/hot-startups/behavox-announces-100-million-investment-from-softbank-vision-fund-2/)).

Alongside the investing, Varma held the governance roles that came with SoftBank's stakes. He sat on the board of Oyo, the hotel aggregator, alongside Gerry Lopez, head of SoftBank's operating group, and worked with Oyo's leadership and bankers on its $660 million bond issue, as well as helping Oyo list its properties on the Chinese platform Didi ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/leadership/cover-story-why-softbank-changed-gears-in-india/69585/1)). He also chaired SVF Investment Corp. 2, SoftBank's $200 million special-purpose acquisition company, as chairman and chief executive, with Navneet Govil as chief financial officer ([US Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1837238/000119312521030688/d16631ds1.htm)). And he served as a non-executive director of One97 Communications, the parent of Paytm, and of PB Fintech, the parent of PolicyBazaar — resigning from both on 14 March 2022, when SoftBank held about 17.5% of Paytm and about 13% of PolicyBazaar, in line with the group's global policy of stepping off the boards of portfolio companies once they had listed ([YourStory](http://yourstory.com/2022/03/softbank-executive-resigns-paytm-policybazaar-munish-varma)). The wider Vision Fund context in those years included the setbacks at [WeWork](/organizations/wework/) and [Uber](/organizations/uber/), both named in contemporaneous reporting as the fund's troubled marquee bets ([Banking Gateway](https://www.banking-gateway.com/analysis/softbank-investment-advisers-vision-fund/); [Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/leadership/cover-story-why-softbank-changed-gears-in-india/69585/1)).

## The second act: One Investment Management

Varma left SoftBank in 2022 with fellow managing partner Yanni Pipilis to join Rajeev Misra's new investment firm, One Investment Management (OneIM) ([Inshorts](https://inshorts.com/en/news/softbank-vision-fund-managers-to-join-rajeev-misras-startup-report-1658939693382); [BusinessMirror](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2024/01/29/financier-entrusted-with-middle-east-billions-attempts-comeback/)). The move reunited a team that went back to Deutsche Bank: Bloomberg described Varma and Pipilis as Misra's "trusted long-time lieutenants" who had "both worked with him at Deutsche Bank and Vision Fund," now leading a handpicked team of 25 investment professionals scouring for opportunities primarily in the US and Europe ([BusinessMirror](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2024/01/29/financier-entrusted-with-middle-east-billions-attempts-comeback/)). OneIM's pitch was a deliberate break with the Vision Fund's approach — a credit-focused asset manager deploying across asset classes rather than a technology-only equity fund, with Misra pledging to take fewer risks and deploy more slowly ([BusinessMirror](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2024/01/29/financier-entrusted-with-middle-east-billions-attempts-comeback/)).

The scale was still large. OneIM raised $6.8 billion from backers including Mubadala and Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed Al Nahyan's Royal Group, with Misra seeking to grow it past $10 billion ([BusinessMirror](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2024/01/29/financier-entrusted-with-middle-east-billions-attempts-comeback/)). By January 2024 it had invested only $1 billion of the $7 billion raised, including loans to WeWork and support for Apollo Global Management's leveraged buyout of The Restaurant Group, the owner of Wagamama ([BusinessMirror](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2024/01/29/financier-entrusted-with-middle-east-billions-attempts-comeback/)). Varma's formal standing in the new firm is on the public record in Abu Dhabi: he is a Licensed Director of One Investment Management Ltd, active from 30 March 2023, on the register of the Abu Dhabi Global Market's Financial Services Regulatory Authority ([Abu Dhabi Global Market](https://www.adgm.com/api/IndividualDetail/GenerateIndividualProfilePdf?IndividualId=173077)). By autumn 2024 the firm had grown to about 50 employees with about $8 billion in deployable funds, and its deal flow had moved into commercial real estate, including an early-2025 purchase with a partner of an 89.9% stake in a 6,788-unit German multifamily portfolio and a $500 million venture with RXR to finance multifamily projects ([Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/power-series/player/rajeev-misra/)). For Varma, the arc closes a circle: the credit investor who left banking for venture capital is back in credit, now deploying Gulf capital rather than a bank's balance sheet.
