## Overview

Charles Hearn is the co-founder and chief technology officer of [Alloy](/organizations/alloy/), the [New York City](/places/new-york-city/)-based company whose identity-decisioning platform helps banks and financial technology firms automate onboarding, transaction monitoring, and credit decisions ([UVA Today](https://archive.news.virginia.edu/content/meet-11-uva-alumni-new-forbes-30-under-30-lists/)). He built Alloy's product from its founding in 2015 alongside chief executive [Tommy Nicholas](/people/tommy-nicholas/) and co-founder [Laura Spiekerman](/people/laura-spiekerman/), and in 2020 he was named to the Forbes 30 Under 30 lists in enterprise technology ([UVA Today](https://archive.news.virginia.edu/content/meet-11-uva-alumni-new-forbes-30-under-30-lists/)). Where Nicholas supplied the company's commercial framing and Spiekerman its customer relationships, Hearn's public record is that of the builder: the engineer who turned a payments-industry frustration into a cloud platform that regulators and auditors could accept.

## From Microsoft to the Alloy build

Hearn earned a degree in computer science from the University of Virginia in 2013 and previously worked as a program manager at Microsoft ([UVA Today](https://archive.news.virginia.edu/content/meet-11-uva-alumni-new-forbes-30-under-30-lists/)). He then worked at a payments-processing startup — the ACH payments company Technical.ly identifies as Knox Payments, based in Richmond, Virginia — where he met Nicholas and Spiekerman ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/uncategorized/alloy-techstars-venture-capital/); [AWS Startups Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/startups/alloys-global-identity-decisioning-platform-built-on-aws/)). The group left to start Alloy in 2015 and moved to New York that summer after the company was accepted into the Techstars fintech accelerator ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/uncategorized/alloy-techstars-venture-capital/)). In the early company, the founders divided the work plainly: "Charles was building the product with a couple of engineers, I was having conversations with prospects and customers, and Tommy was implementing the solution and getting product feedback," Spiekerman later recalled ([AWS Startups Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/startups/alloys-global-identity-decisioning-platform-built-on-aws/)).

The technical bet was not obviously safe at the time. Hearn recalled getting many questions from initial clients in 2015 about whether financial infrastructure could even be built on the cloud, and credited the AWS Activate program, joined through Techstars, as essential to Alloy's first two years — removing server costs for a long stretch and providing an experienced solutions architect for the earliest infrastructure ([AWS Startups Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/startups/alloys-global-identity-decisioning-platform-built-on-aws/)). By September 2016 the company had raised about $1.5 million and had five customers ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/uncategorized/alloy-techstars-venture-capital/)); by the time UVA Today profiled Hearn in December 2020, Alloy had raised $56 million since its launch ([UVA Today](https://archive.news.virginia.edu/content/meet-11-uva-alumni-new-forbes-30-under-30-lists/)).

## The CTO's platform: one view of the customer

Hearn's account of the platform centres on unifying decisions that banks previously made in separate systems. In September 2021 Alloy raised a $100 million Series C at a $1.35 billion valuation, with a platform processing more than 455,000 identity decisions a day across more than 120 data sources ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/alloy-earns-1-35-billion-valuation-after-securing-series-c-investment/)). The same year it added transaction monitoring to its onboarding product ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2021/09/alloy-identity-verification-transaction-monitoring-platform-financial-services-kyc-laura-spiekerman/)), and Hearn described the next step as plugging the onboarding, transaction-monitoring, and credit-underwriting systems into each other so clients could make more intelligent identity and risk decisions from a unified view of the customer lifecycle, rather than analysing fragments of customer information in isolation ([AWS Startups Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/startups/alloys-global-identity-decisioning-platform-built-on-aws/)).

His approach to regulation was pragmatic rather than adversarial. "Being in financial services, we have a lot of requirements to do things a certain way," he said in a 2021 founder interview, but Alloy was always "second guessing, to some extent, what those requirements are for and if we can satisfy them another way and make our auditors happy without introducing any more red tape to the process" ([Eniac Ventures](https://eniacvc.medium.com/alloys-founders-explain-how-they-built-a-fintech-unicorn-b592e99fde1c)). The platform's transparency mattered commercially as well: clients could see which aspects of an identity matched across record sources and which did not, and could adjust their own risk tolerances without writing code ([AWS Startups Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/startups/alloys-global-identity-decisioning-platform-built-on-aws/)). In August 2022 Alloy expanded internationally, with clients in 40 countries able to access its services ([AWS Startups Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/startups/alloys-global-identity-decisioning-platform-built-on-aws/)), the same year it raised a further $52 million at a $1.55 billion valuation ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/regulation/2022/fraud-prevention-fintech-alloy-nets-52m-on-1-55b-valuation/)).
