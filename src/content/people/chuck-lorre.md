## Overview

Chuck Lorre — born Charles Michael Levine on October 18, 1952, in Manhattan, New York — is the American television writer and producer nicknamed the "King of Sitcoms" for a run of hit comedies including *Two and a Half Men* (2003–15), *The Big Bang Theory* (2007–19) and *Young Sheldon* (2017–24) ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre); [Parade](https://parade.com/news/chuck-lorre-blasts-trump-in-brutal-acceptance-speech)). He is also known for the personal vanity cards that close his episodes, for a famously combative relationship with network censors, and for a decade of public criticism of Donald Trump that peaked in September 2026, when he turned a charity award acceptance speech into a profanity-laced attack on the president ([SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/2008279/why-chuck-lorre-tv-shows-have-vanity-cards/); [Parade](https://parade.com/news/chuck-lorre-blasts-trump-in-brutal-acceptance-speech)).

## From Long Island to the recording studio to the writers' room

Lorre's family moved to Long Island when he was about five years old; his father, Robert Levine, ran a small luncheonette where the teenage Lorre often helped out, and his mother, Miriam Levine, was a homemaker who later worked in retail ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)). He developed a love of comedy watching stand-ups like Bob Hope and Don Rickles on *The Ed Sullivan Show* with his father ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)).

He attended the State University of New York at Potsdam for two years before dropping out to pursue music, then spent some 17 years as a touring guitarist and songwriter performing in bands at weddings, bars and on cruise ships ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)). The period produced genuine hits: he wrote the theme song for the animated series *Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles* and "French Kissin' in the USA," the first single released by Blondie's Debbie Harry as a solo artist — a Top 40 hit ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre); [TVNewsCheck](https://tvnewscheck.com/uncategorized/article/chuck-lorre-to-join-nab-hall-of-fame/)).

By the early 1980s, married to [Paula Smith](/people/paula-smith/) (1979; divorced 1992) with two daughters, Lorre sought a more stable living in television writing ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)). He started at DIC Enterprises writing for animated children's shows, with credits including *Pole Position* (1984) and *Heathcliff: The Movie* (1986) ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)). His first live-action job was a two-episode run on *Charles in Charge* in 1987, followed by a stint writing for *My Two Dads* (1987–90) ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)). His big break came in 1990–92 as a writer and later executive producer on the hit sitcom *Roseanne* ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre); [TVNewsCheck](https://tvnewscheck.com/uncategorized/article/chuck-lorre-to-join-nab-hall-of-fame/)).

## The sitcom empire

Lorre's first shot as executive producer, *Frannie's Turn* (1992), was canceled after five weeks; he had somewhat better luck with *Grace Under Fire* starring Brett Butler, though he was fired after the first season following clashes with Butler, and he lasted just 18 episodes in charge of *Cybill* ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)). His first sustained success was *Dharma & Greg* (1997–2002), the "opposites attract" comedy starring Jenna Elfman and Thomas Gibson, on which he stayed for the full 119-episode run ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)).

In 2003 he created *Two and a Half Men*, starring Charlie Sheen as jingle writer Charlie Harper, Jon Cryer as his brother Alan and Angus T. Jones as Alan's son Jake; it aired for 12 seasons and was among the most-watched prime-time comedies for much of its run ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)). In 2011 Sheen was fired after a series of bizarre public interviews in which he attacked Lorre; Sheen's character was killed offscreen in the season-nine opener, Ashton Kutcher was brought in as tech billionaire Walden Schmidt, and the show ran four more seasons before ending in 2015 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)).

In 2007 Lorre created *The Big Bang Theory*, starring Kaley Cuoco, Johnny Galecki, Jim Parsons, Simon Helberg and Kunal Nayyar (later joined by Mayim Bialik), built around a waitress befriending four brilliant but socially inept scientists; its 2017 prequel spinoff *Young Sheldon*, exploring Parsons's character's childhood, ran until 2024 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)). Around those tentpoles he built a deep bench: *Mike & Molly* (2010–16), about a couple who meet at a Chicago Overeaters Anonymous group; *Mom* (2013–21), with Allison Janney and Anna Faris as a mother and daughter, the latter recovering from substance use disorder; the single-camera *The Kominsky Method* (2018–21), about an aging acting coach played by Michael Douglas; *Bob Hearts Abishola* (2019–24); and the Netflix comedy *Disjointed* (2017–18) ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)). His hands-on style — overseeing nearly every writing and filming session — and his productivity have drawn comparisons to producer Norman Lear ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)).

The 2020s brought a streaming-era second act. *Bookie* (2023– ), starring Sebastian Maniscalco as a bookmaker, notably served as a vehicle to reconcile Lorre with Charlie Sheen, who appeared in two episodes ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)). And in July 2026, HBO Max premiered *Stuart Fails to Save the Universe*, a *Big Bang Theory* spinoff developed by Lorre and Bill Prady with screenwriter Zak Penn — all three serving as executive producers — following comic-book-store owner Stuart Bloom (Kevin Sussman) through a multiverse adventure ([Radio Times](https://www.radiotimes.com/tv/comedy/stuart-fails-to-save-the-universe-hbo-max-trailer-newsupdate/)).

## The vanity cards

Since *Dharma & Greg*, Lorre's network shows have ended each episode with a vanity card labeled "Chuck Lorre Productions" — a split-second message that began as a wink to the audience and a way to rant outside narrative constraints, and that fans came to expect ([SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/2008279/why-chuck-lorre-tv-shows-have-vanity-cards/)). Over three decades the cards have let him elaborate on plot points, call out network censorship and vent about his personal life; in 2012 he compiled a selection in the book *What Doesn't Kill Us Makes Us Bitter*, with his proceeds benefiting health-care charities and educational efforts ([SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/2008279/why-chuck-lorre-tv-shows-have-vanity-cards/); [TVNewsCheck](https://tvnewscheck.com/uncategorized/article/chuck-lorre-to-join-nab-hall-of-fame/)). Streaming has strained the tradition — season one of *Bookie* carried only two cards, and in the season-two premiere card Lorre joked that Max "actively dissuades viewers from reading end credits, let alone sticking around to read the mischievous word salad that is a classic Chuck Lorre vanity card" — but he keeps writing them: "[Vanity cards are] from another era. But you know, I'm trying to keep it alive," he told *Variety* in 2024 ([SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/2008279/why-chuck-lorre-tv-shows-have-vanity-cards/)). Lorre has also become known for an irascible temper and repeated fights with networks over what he characterizes as censorship of jokes in his scripts ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)).

## A decade of Trump criticism

Lorre's vanity cards became a running venue for Trump criticism starting with the 2016 campaign. One card read, "'Make America great again' is a bumper sticker for victimhood" (per *Deadline*); an earlier one offered "A Nonpartisan, Nondenominational Prayer for America. God, please make this election be over soon. Amen"; after Trump defeated Hillary Clinton, a card simply read "Uh-oh" (via *HuffPost*); and on the eve of Trump's first inauguration, the card for the *Big Bang* episode airing beforehand carried lyrics from George Harrison's "Beware of the Darkness," warning against "greedy leaders" ([TheList](https://www.thelist.com/1810887/big-bang-theory-shady-jab-at-donald-trump/)).

The sharpest card came in 2018, ahead of the midterm elections: vanity card #598, "My Prayer," asked God to encourage voter turnout and, "if you, in your divine wisdom, believe a fascist, hate-filled, fear-mongering, demagogic, truth-shattering, autocratic golf cheater is what we need right now, then, you know, thy will be done" — before adding, "remind those who collaborate with the darkness that thou art the light, and the light is not above whipping out a little Old Testament wrath" ([TheList](https://www.thelist.com/1810887/big-bang-theory-shady-jab-at-donald-trump/); [Parade](https://parade.com/news/chuck-lorre-blasts-trump-in-brutal-acceptance-speech)).

## September 2026: the Angel Awards speech

On Saturday, September 26, 2026, the 73-year-old Lorre received the Founders Angel Award at Project Angel Food's Angel Awards gala in Los Angeles — an honor first reported by the *Daily Beast* ([Parade](https://parade.com/news/chuck-lorre-blasts-trump-in-brutal-acceptance-speech); [Reality Tea](https://www.realitytea.com/2026/09/29/chuck-lorre-calls-out-donald-trump-los-angeles-speech/)). He abandoned a traditional thank-you speech and turned the occasion into a political roast. "But the burden of my profession demands that I take this opportunity to say a few words about that bloated orange piece of s–t in the White House," he said, adding: "because I'm a comedy writer, I have no choice but to explain how I wake up every morning to turn on CNN to see if the president of the United States had a heart attack while straining to take a dump" ([Parade](https://parade.com/news/chuck-lorre-blasts-trump-in-brutal-acceptance-speech); [Reality Tea](https://www.realitytea.com/2026/09/29/chuck-lorre-calls-out-donald-trump-los-angeles-speech/)).

He then turned to First Lady Melania Trump, calling her "cold-hearted" and claiming she "clearly hates her husband," and asked "how a country can embrace a smart, educated, warm, funny first lady like Michelle Obama and somehow be OK with [Melania]" ([Parade](https://parade.com/news/chuck-lorre-blasts-trump-in-brutal-acceptance-speech)). Lorre also urged Democrats toward the center — "I'd like to beg the [Democratic National Committee] to run from the suicidal insanity of the far-left woke culture in the coming election" — and mocked Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.'s vaccine skepticism: "I'll just point out those hard workers will likely die of measles because Bobby Kennedy told them not to get vaccinated," before imitating Kennedy's voice ("I actually practiced that") ([Parade](https://parade.com/news/chuck-lorre-blasts-trump-in-brutal-acceptance-speech); [Reality Tea](https://www.realitytea.com/2026/09/29/chuck-lorre-calls-out-donald-trump-los-angeles-speech/)). He closed with a tweaked Serenity Prayer — "God, grant me the serenity to no longer give a s–t about all the stupid people in this country" — and called Trump a "Russian asset" ([Parade](https://parade.com/news/chuck-lorre-blasts-trump-in-brutal-acceptance-speech)).

## Philanthropy and honors

Lorre's philanthropy runs through the Chuck Lorre Family Foundation. On February 5, 2026, the Foundation's Kitchen & Campus at Project Angel Food held its grand opening in Los Angeles, with Lorre, Jamie Lee Curtis, Lori Loughlin, Sharon Lawrence and Judith Light on hand; the new building expands the nonprofit's footprint from about 7,700 to 16,000 square feet, giving it capacity to produce more than 4.5 million meals a year for people facing critical and life-threatening illnesses ([Parade](https://parade.com/news/star-trek-actress-michele-specht-stunning-rare-public-appearance-project-angel-food)). In 2015 he spearheaded *The Big Bang Theory* Scholarship Endowment at UCLA for undergraduates in STEM fields who need financial aid; it has raised more than $4 million and supports a class of 20 scholars a year, with five more added each year in perpetuity ([TVNewsCheck](https://tvnewscheck.com/uncategorized/article/chuck-lorre-to-join-nab-hall-of-fame/)). Industry honors include induction into the NAB Broadcasting Hall of Fame at the 2016 NAB Show Television Luncheon in Las Vegas — where NAB's Marcellus Alexander praised his "biting wit and memorable characters" — plus a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, the NATPE Brandon Tartikoff Legacy Award, and induction into the Television Academy Hall of Fame in 2012 ([TVNewsCheck](https://tvnewscheck.com/uncategorized/article/chuck-lorre-to-join-nab-hall-of-fame/)).
