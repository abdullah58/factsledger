## Overview

Giannis Antetokounmpo is a Greek professional basketball player who spent 13 seasons with the Milwaukee Bucks, winning two Most Valuable Player awards and the 2021 NBA championship, before a 2026 trade sent him to the Miami Heat. He also backed alternative-asset platform [Alt](/organizations/alt/) in its 2021 $75 million Series B round. That arc — from selling goods on the streets of Athens to an NBA title and a seat on a collectibles startup's cap table — is what this profile documents, from sources read in full.

This profile covers Giannis Antetokounmpo himself, not his brothers: Thanasis, Kostas and Alex also became professional basketball players, but they are different people with separate careers ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Giannis-Antetokounmpo)). Giannis was born on December 6, 1994, in Athens, as Giannis Adetokunbo, the son of [Nigerian](/places/nigeria/) immigrants who had no legal status in Greece ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Giannis-Antetokounmpo)). He and three of his four brothers were considered stateless because Greece did not grant birthright citizenship, and the family faced financial difficulty, xenophobia and fear of deportation; to supplement their income, he occasionally sold items including sunglasses and watches on the streets of Athens ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Giannis-Antetokounmpo)). At age 13 he and his brothers caught the eye of a local coach and began playing basketball, and three years later Giannis joined Filathlitikos, a professional team in Greece ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Giannis-Antetokounmpo)). In 2013 the Bucks drafted him as the 15th overall pick, and around the same time he gained Greek citizenship, which enabled him to travel to the United States; the family subsequently changed their surname to Antetokounmpo ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Giannis-Antetokounmpo)).

## From Athens to Milwaukee's peak

Antetokounmpo made his NBA debut in the 2013–14 season, and although the Bucks struggled in his early years, he kept progressing until a breakthrough 2016–17 season earned him the league's Most Improved Player award ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Giannis-Antetokounmpo)). A versatile forward praised for both offensive and defensive play, he acquired the nickname "the Greek Freak" because of his outlandishly athletic feats ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Giannis-Antetokounmpo)). After a coaching change from Jason Kidd to Mike Budenholzer, Milwaukee finished the 2018–19 season with the best record in the league, and Antetokounmpo was named the NBA's MVP, averaging 27.7 points, 12.5 rebounds and 5.9 assists — though the team lost in the conference finals ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Giannis-Antetokounmpo)). The Bucks again posted the league's best record in 2019–20 but lost in the second round of the playoffs, while Antetokounmpo won his second MVP award and was named Defensive Player of the Year, becoming only the third person, after Michael Jordan in 1988 and Hakeem Olajuwon in 1994, to win both honours in the same season ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Giannis-Antetokounmpo)).

The title that followed made the individual awards look like a prelude. In the 2020–21 season the Bucks entered the playoffs as the third seed in the Eastern Conference, overcame multiple series deficits, and reached the title round against the Phoenix Suns ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Giannis-Antetokounmpo)). After losing the first two games, Milwaukee won four straight, and in the deciding Game 6 Antetokounmpo scored 50 points as the franchise claimed its second championship and its first since the team led by Kareem Abdul-Jabbar and Oscar Robertson won in 1971 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Giannis-Antetokounmpo)). ESPN's contemporaneous report fills in the night: a 105–98 victory in which he added 14 rebounds and five blocks, went 17-for-19 from the free-throw line, and scored 33 of his 50 points in the second half ([ESPN](https://www.espn.ph/nba/story/_/id/31851979/giannis-antetokounmpo-scores-50-points-game-6-nba-finals-milwaukee-bucks-win-club-1st-nba-title-1971)). He was named MVP of the Finals after averaging 35.2 points, 13.2 rebounds and 5.0 assists per game for the series ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Giannis-Antetokounmpo)). He had signed a five-year supermax contract extension before the season, and afterwards framed staying in Milwaukee as doing it "the hard way" rather than joining a super team ([ESPN](https://www.espn.ph/nba/story/_/id/31851979/giannis-antetokounmpo-scores-50-points-game-6-nba-finals-milwaukee-bucks-win-club-1st-nba-title-1971)).

The years after the title brought frustration rather than a repeat. In 2021–22 he led the Bucks to another postseason berth, but the team lost a seven-game second-round series to the Boston Celtics despite three games in which he scored 40 or more points ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Giannis-Antetokounmpo)). He continued to play at an MVP-calibre level in subsequent seasons, but the Bucks lost in the first round of each playoff from 2023 to 2025, partly because he missed games with injuries ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Giannis-Antetokounmpo)). Alongside his NBA career he also played for the Greek national team, debuting in 2013 on the under-20 team at the FIBA European Championships, starring on the senior team at multiple tournaments, and reaching his first Olympics at the [Paris](/places/paris/) 2024 Games, where he carried the Greek flag at the opening ceremony ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Giannis-Antetokounmpo)).

## Investor in Alt, then the 2026 move to Miami

Months after the championship, Antetokounmpo appeared on the cap table of a very different kind of asset business. In November 2021, trading-card platform Alt raised a $75 million Series B led by Spearhead at a valuation the company put at over $325 million, backed by Seven Seven Six, Breyer Capital, Shrug Capital, Apollo Projects and individual investors including Tom Brady, Giannis Antetokounmpo, Alex Morgan, Candace Parker and Marlon Humphrey ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/11/collectible-card-trading-platform-alt-raises-75m-as-it-eyes-other-asset-categories-and-preps-its-mobile-launch/)). Sports Business Journal independently reported the same round, the same Spearhead lead and the same athlete investors, and quoted Alt president [Nicole Colombo](/people/nicole-colombo/) defining alternative assets as any asset a traditional bank does not consider real collateral: "Where the banks stop, Alt starts" ([Sports Business Journal](https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Daily/Issues/2021/11/12/Technology/collectiblesexchange-alt-raises-75m-from-tom-brady-candace-parker-giannis-antetokounmpo-and-others.aspx)). The fit was legible: Alt had started as a marketplace for high-value sports cards, authenticated and stored in a vault it called the "Fort Knox of cards," taking a 1.5% cut on each sale, with over $70 million of cards stored by that point, and it was expanding into other trading cards and alternative assets ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/11/collectible-card-trading-platform-alt-raises-75m-as-it-eyes-other-asset-categories-and-preps-its-mobile-launch/)).

That Milwaukee chapter closed in 2026. The Bucks agreed to trade Antetokounmpo and Bobby Portis Jr. to Miami in late June, and the deal was official in early July, ending a 13-year run that included the 2021 title, a trip to the Eastern Conference finals, an NBA Cup title and the most wins in the NBA over a seven-year period ([Milwaukee Journal Sentinel](https://www.jsonline.com/story/sports/nba/bucks/2026/10/20/giannis-antetokounmpo-begins-pressure-filled-chapter-outside-milwaukee-miami-heat/92328064007/)). Individually, he left holding nearly every franchise record, with two MVPs, a Defensive Player of the Year award, a Finals MVP, an NBA Cup MVP and a place among the top 75 players in the history of the game ([Milwaukee Journal Sentinel](https://www.jsonline.com/story/sports/nba/bucks/2026/10/20/giannis-antetokounmpo-begins-pressure-filled-chapter-outside-milwaukee-miami-heat/92328064007/)). In Miami he entered a different kind of pressure under head coach Erik Spoelstra, having moved his family to South Florida and left Greece earlier than usual for a players-only minicamp ([Milwaukee Journal Sentinel](https://www.jsonline.com/story/sports/nba/bucks/2026/10/20/giannis-antetokounmpo-begins-pressure-filled-chapter-outside-milwaukee-miami-heat/92328064007/)). He arrived short of match fitness by his own history: he had not appeared in an NBA game since March 15, and had last played a game without any sort of restriction since November 15, 2025 ([Milwaukee Journal Sentinel](https://www.jsonline.com/story/sports/nba/bucks/2026/10/20/giannis-antetokounmpo-begins-pressure-filled-chapter-outside-milwaukee-miami-heat/92328064007/)). "I love pressure," he said in July. "I think I thrive [in] pressure. I think I need pressure at this time in my career" ([Milwaukee Journal Sentinel](https://www.jsonline.com/story/sports/nba/bucks/2026/10/20/giannis-antetokounmpo-begins-pressure-filled-chapter-outside-milwaukee-miami-heat/92328064007/)).
