## Overview

Ryan King is the co-founder of [Chime](/organizations/chime/), the American neobank, and its original chief technology officer — the engineer who built the company's technology organization from its founding in 2012, steered it through a near-collapse in 2016 when 100 investors in a row said no, and stayed with it to a [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/) listing in June 2025, where he now sits as a board member and principal shareholder ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)).

King's public record is thinner than that of his co-founder and chief executive, [Chris Britt](/people/chris-britt/), and much of it comes through Chime itself — but the company's IPO filing gives his career a precise spine: he was CTO from the founding until August 2022 and again from August 2023 to May 2024, has sat on the board since February 2019, and held about 31.3% of the company's post-IPO voting power ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1795586/000162828025028733/chimefinancialinc-sx1a.htm)). He is not the current CTO — a point many secondary biographies still get wrong — but the executive the CTOs report to.

## Before Chime: Plaxo, Comcast and a computer science education

King trained as a computer scientist, earning a B.S. in Computer Science and Engineering from the University of California, Los Angeles and an M.S. in Computer Science from Stanford University ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1795586/000162828025028733/chimefinancialinc-sx1a.htm)). Before Chime, he was vice president of engineering at Plaxo, an early professional social-networking company, and stayed through its acquisition by Comcast Interactive Media ([Engineering Leadership Community](https://sfelc.com/speaker/ryan-king)). He then served as chief technology officer and vice president at the Comcast Silicon Valley Innovation Center from 2010 to 2012 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1795586/000162828025028733/chimefinancialinc-sx1a.htm)). The same engineering-leadership biography credits him with senior engineering roles at Liberate Technologies and Microsoft, though those two roles are not corroborated by the IPO filing ([Engineering Leadership Community](https://sfelc.com/speaker/ryan-king)).

That background — a decade building consumer internet products inside a large media company — is what Britt was buying when the two founded Chime in 2012. The original concept was called 1debit, and the thesis, as seed investor Forerunner Ventures later recalled it, was that mobile would become the default channel for banking and that debit-card interchange fees could fund a no-fee model ([Forerunner Ventures](https://www.forerunnerventures.com/perspectives/celebrating-chimes-journey)). By the time of the 2018 Series C, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/05/31/no-fees-mobile-banking-service-chime-raises-70m-series-c-valuing-its-business-at-500m/) described King simply as the co-founder and CTO who "hails from Plaxo and Comcast" — the technical half of a founding pair in which Britt supplied the banking experience from Visa and Green Dot.

## A hundred no's, then an IPO

The defining episode of King's career at Chime came in early 2016. Trying to raise an extension to the Series A, he and Britt pitched 100 investors — "maybe more" — and got 100 rejections, with the company nearly out of money ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)). "We founded the company in 2012, and the first, really, five or six years was very difficult in terms of convincing investors to invest in the idea and the business. It was just way, way harder than I expected," King told [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/) on the morning of the IPO. The lifeline was a single investor: [Lauren Kolodny](/people/lauren-kolodny/), then a partner at Aspect Ventures, led a $9 million extension — the only term sheet the founders had — buying in at 26 cents a share ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)). Kolodny later co-founded [Acrew Capital](/organizations/acrew-capital/), and she stood on the podium with the founders to ring the Nasdaq opening bell ([Acrew Capital](https://perspectives.acrewcapital.com/p/the-early-bet-that-changed-chime-f1992ab6beb1)).

The rescue bought the time to prove the model. Chime passed a million accounts around the 2018 Series C, which raised $70 million at a roughly $500 million valuation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/05/31/no-fees-mobile-banking-service-chime-raises-70m-series-c-valuing-its-business-at-500m/)). By 2019, Iconiq had spent two years courting the founders before elbowing into the oversubscribed $200 million Series D at $5.22 a share ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/13/iconiq-vcs-spent-two-years-courting-chime-and-the-firm-isnt-selling-its-stake/)). In total the founders raised about $2.65 billion as a private company ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)). Revenue reached $1.3 billion in 2023 and $1.7 billion in 2024, losses shrank from $203 million to $25 million, and Chime turned profitable in the first quarter of 2025 with $13 million of net income on $519 million of revenue ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)).

On June 12, 2025, Chime raised $864 million in its IPO, pricing shares at $27 — above the $24 to $26 range — with the stock opening at $42 and closing the day at $37 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)). The offering was led by [Morgan Stanley](/organizations/morgan-stanley/), [Goldman Sachs](/organizations/goldman-sachs/) and [J.P. Morgan](/organizations/jpmorgan-chase/) ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1795586/000162828025028733/chimefinancialinc-sx1a.htm)). Minutes after the bell, King sat down with [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/) — and chose to talk about the hardest moment, not the win. During the IPO roadshow, a security guard in a marble-lined building spotted Britt's Chime card, winked, and said "Checking and savings, baby!" before giving the founders a high five ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)).

## Building the engineering organization — and handing the CTO title on

As CTO, King grew Chime's engineering organization from about 200 people to more than 1,200, navigating the phase changes that come with each order of magnitude — new team topologies, new vice presidents of engineering, new ways of setting goals ([Engineering Leadership Podcast](https://www.everand.com/podcast/689891981/The-Evolution-of-a-CTO-How-Your-Leadership-Can-Change-through-Hypergrowth-w-Ryan-King-Clarence-Chio-82-To-grow-your-engineering-team-from-200-to)). His stated operating principle was that teams should own their domains, services and data end to end, and that the organization has to be consciously aligned with the architecture as both evolve ([Engineering Leadership Podcast](https://www.everand.com/podcast/689891981/The-Evolution-of-a-CTO-How-Your-Leadership-Can-Change-through-Hypergrowth-w-Ryan-King-Clarence-Chio-82-To-grow-your-engineering-team-from-200-to)).

The most consequential technical decision of his tenure was to build rather than buy the company's core banking infrastructure. King led the development of ChimeCore, Chime's proprietary core banking platform, including a migration of more than 9 million members without any downtime; owning the full stack, he argued, is what lets Chime deploy AI and machine learning at scale ([FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/core-banking-technology/what-the-fintech-s-7-episode-14-building-chimecore-how-chime-developed-its-own-banking-infrastructure)).

The CTO title itself moved on twice. In August 2022, James Barrese was promoted to CTO — previously senior vice president of technology and engineering — and continued reporting to King ([Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2022/08/195058-us-fintech-chime-promotes-tenured-employees-to-senior-roles/)). King returned to the CTO role from August 2023 to May 2024 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1795586/000162828025028733/chimefinancialinc-sx1a.htm)), and in July 2024 Jeff Currier — formerly CTO of Galileo Financial Technologies, a SoFi company — joined as CTO, again reporting to King ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240723616961/en/Chime-Strengthens-C-Suite-with-Key-Leadership-Hires)). Announcing the hire, King framed it as "the next chapter of Chime's technical growth and maturity," focused on "strengthening our full technology stack and driving innovative data and AI programs" ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240723616961/en/Chime-Strengthens-C-Suite-with-Key-Leadership-Hires)).

## Board member, principal shareholder — and a second board seat

King's formal position today is co-founder and director, not operating executive. He has served on Chime's board since February 2019, and the IPO filing put his post-offering voting power at approximately 31.3% — 34.4% if all his outstanding equity awards had vested and converted — against Britt's 34.7% and 40.1% on the same bases ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1795586/000162828025028733/chimefinancialinc-sx1a.htm)). Together, the filing notes, the co-founders can "significantly influence or determine" any matter requiring a stockholder vote, including board elections and mergers ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1795586/000162828025028733/chimefinancialinc-sx1a.htm)). His confirmatory employment agreement provides a $475,000 annual base salary, at-will employment, and performance-based co-founder share awards tied to financial goals and stock-price hurdles ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1795586/000162828025028733/chimefinancialinc-sx1a.htm)).

Outside Chime, King has held one other documented board seat: in June 2020 he joined the board of ZayZoon Inc., a Calgary-based earned-wage-access company, alongside Marcos Lopez of Morgan Stanley's Shareworks business ([ZayZoon](https://www.zayzoon.com/press/releases/r/2020-06-17-board-members?hsLang=en)). ZayZoon described Chime at the time as a San Francisco challenger bank with more than 8 million accounts, fresh from a $500 million raise at a $5.8 billion valuation ([ZayZoon](https://www.zayzoon.com/press/releases/r/2020-06-17-board-members?hsLang=en)).
