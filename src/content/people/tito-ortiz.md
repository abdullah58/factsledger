## The contract fight that helped sell the UFC

Before [Dana White](/people/dana-white/) ran the UFC, he managed fighters — and Tito Ortiz was one of them, alongside [Chuck Liddell](/people/chuck-liddell/). White has told the story himself, on a 2023 podcast: he trained jiu-jitsu under John Lewis in Las Vegas alongside the Fertitta brothers, and it was through that orbit that he became the manager of Ortiz and Liddell ([EssentiallySports](https://www.essentiallysports.com/ufc-mma-news-i-got-into-a-huge-battle-dana-white-recalls-surprising-turn-of-events-involving-bj-penn-chuck-liddell-and-tito-ortiz-that-led-to-the-acquisition-of-the-ufc-twenty-two-years-ago/)). When Ortiz's contract came up for renegotiation, owner Bob Meyrowitz balked — reportedly snapping that there was "no more money left" and he didn't "even know if there's going to be another show." The blow-up is what made White call [Lorenzo Fertitta](/people/lorenzo-fertitta/) and pitch him on buying the company outright. The Fertittas did, installing White as president in January 2001 — so one of Ortiz's contract disputes sits among the direct triggers of the Zuffa acquisition ([EssentiallySports](https://www.essentiallysports.com/ufc-mma-news-i-got-into-a-huge-battle-dana-white-recalls-surprising-turn-of-events-involving-bj-penn-chuck-liddell-and-tito-ortiz-that-led-to-the-acquisition-of-the-ufc-twenty-two-years-ago/)).

## The Huntington Beach Bad Boy

Jacob Christopher "Tito" Ortiz, born January 23, 1975 in Huntington Beach, California and of Mexican descent, was one of the UFC's first genuine stars — "The Huntington Beach Bad Boy" ([Sherdog](https://www.sherdog.com/news/articles/By-The-Numbers-Tito-Ortiz-183958/amp)). He captured the UFC light heavyweight title from Wanderlei Silva at UFC 25 on April 14, 2000 and held it for 1,260 days through September 26, 2003, defending it five times before losing to Randy Couture at UFC 44 — the third-longest single reign at 205 pounds, behind only Jon Jones and Daniel Cormier ([Sherdog](https://www.sherdog.com/news/articles/By-The-Numbers-Tito-Ortiz-183958/amp)).

The numbers hold up: a 21-12-1 record across 34 fights, 15-11-1 in the UFC where he fought exclusively from 1999 to 2012, with 10 KO/TKO wins and five submissions. He scored three stoppages in under a minute — a 13-second TKO of Wes Albritton at UFC 13 (May 30, 1997), a 16-second knees submission of Jeremy Screeton (December 8, 1998), and a 32-second slam knockout of Evan Tanner at UFC 30 (February 23, 2001). His 33 takedowns rank sixth all-time among UFC light heavyweights, and his 1,241 total strikes landed at 205 pounds rank fourth ([Sherdog](https://www.sherdog.com/news/articles/By-The-Numbers-Tito-Ortiz-183958/amp)). His feuds with Ken Shamrock and Chuck Liddell — three fights apiece — were among the most heated and lucrative rivalries in MMA history ([Sherdog](https://www.sherdog.com/news/articles/By-The-Numbers-Tito-Ortiz-183958/amp)). He entered the pioneer wing of the UFC Hall of Fame in 2012 ([Sherdog](https://www.sherdog.com/news/articles/By-The-Numbers-Tito-Ortiz-183958/amp)).

His drawing power outlasted his title days. The main event of Bellator 131 — Ortiz versus Stephan Bonnar on November 15, 2014 — drew 1.8 million viewers, peaking at 2 million, making it the most-watched and highest-rated MMA fight on cable in 2014 and a Spike TV record ([Bellator](https://www.sherdog.com/news/pressreleases/A-RECORD-18-MILLION-VIEWERS-TUNE-IN-TO-SEE-TITO-ORTIZSTEPHAN-BONNAR-BOUT-AT-BELLATOR-131-ON-SPIKE-TV-77641)). His last professional fight came on December 7, 2019, under the Combate Global banner: a first-round rear-naked-choke submission of former [WWE](/organizations/wwe/) star Alberto Rodriguez ([Sherdog](https://www.sherdog.com/news/articles/By-The-Numbers-Tito-Ortiz-183958/amp)).

## The Apprentice and the council seat

Ortiz's turn toward politics began on television. In 2008 he was cast in Season 7 of NBC's *The Celebrity Apprentice* alongside Gene Simmons and Lennox Lewis; Donald Trump fired him in the ninth week. He later told *The Ringer* the experience had opened his eyes "like never before in my whole life" ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2020/11/03/politics/tito-ortiz-huntington-beach-donald-trump-election-city-council)).

The rest of his backstory reads like the inverse of a sports hagiography: childhood in Santa Ana before Huntington Beach, membership in a gang called F Troop, heroin-using parents, work at a moving company, a spell "hooked on meth" — until a college wrestling coach lured him back to the sport. Wrestling, he said, "saved my life" ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2020/11/03/politics/tito-ortiz-huntington-beach-donald-trump-election-city-council)). His record included a 2010 arrest on suspicion of domestic violence after an altercation with then-girlfriend Jenna Jameson — prosecutors declined to charge, citing insufficient corroboration to prove the case beyond a reasonable doubt — and a DUI in 2014 ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2020/11/03/politics/tito-ortiz-huntington-beach-donald-trump-election-city-council)).

In 2020 he ran for the Huntington Beach City Council — three seats, fifteen candidates — after briefly floating a run for mayor (impossible, since the council appoints the mayor) and a stint as a would-be police officer. He pitched himself as a Trump-style candidate, down to the "make Huntington Beach safe again" tagline and the Trump campaign's color scheme on his signs ([The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2020/11/03/politics/tito-ortiz-huntington-beach-donald-trump-election-city-council)). He won in November 2020 and became Mayor Pro Tem — then announced his resignation on June 1, 2021, about six months in, saying "This job isn't working for me," citing media "attacks" and his family's safety ([Associated Press](https://www.espn.ph/mma/story/_/id/31552778/former-mma-fighter-tito-ortiz-quits-california-city-council-citing-attacks)). The tenure had been rocky: he refused to wear masks at city events, called coronavirus safety measures a conspiracy, and in February 2021 filed an unemployment claim against the city with California's Employment Development Department even while collecting his council stipend and expense allowance — a claim he called politically motivated ([Associated Press](https://www.espn.ph/mma/story/_/id/31552778/former-mma-fighter-tito-ortiz-quits-california-city-council-citing-attacks)). The council voted 6-0 to install Councilwoman Barbara Delgleize as mayor pro tem ([Associated Press](https://www.espn.ph/mma/story/_/id/31552778/former-mma-fighter-tito-ortiz-quits-california-city-council-citing-attacks)).
