## Overview

**David Cooke** is the former Director of the British Board of Film Classification, serving from September 2004 to March 2016 — a twelve-year tenure during which he oversaw two public consultation reviews of the Classification Guidelines and moved the BBFC's work onto digital platforms; he took the 2011 decision to refuse [*The Bunny Game*](/creative-works/the-bunny-game/) a certificate jointly with the Presidential Team, meaning the film could not be legally supplied in the UK ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/news/bbfc-director-cooke-to-retire/5091816.article); [JoBlo](https://www.joblo.com/adam-rehmeiers-the-bunny-game-banned-by-british-board-of-film-classification/amp/)). A career civil servant since 1977, he came to the BBFC from the Home Office, Cabinet Office and Northern Ireland Office, where he worked on the Northern Ireland peace process ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/news/bbfc-director-cooke-to-retire/5091816.article); [BFI Screenonline](http://www.screenonline.org.uk/people/id/1051154/index.html)).

## From Whitehall to Soho Square

Cooke was a career civil servant from 1977, working across the Home Office, Cabinet Office and Northern Ireland Office; between 1987 and 1990 he headed a policy unit in the Home Office Broadcasting Department, where he was instrumental in setting up the Broadcasting Standards Council and the Radio Authority and in co-ordinating the controversial 1990 Broadcasting Act ([BFI Screenonline](http://www.screenonline.org.uk/people/id/1051154/index.html)). At the time of his BBFC appointment he was Associate Political Director at the Northern Ireland Office ([BFI Screenonline](http://www.screenonline.org.uk/people/id/1051154/index.html)), and Screen Daily records six director-level posts across government before the BBFC, including work on the Northern Ireland peace process ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/news/bbfc-director-cooke-to-retire/5091816.article)). He took over as BBFC Director in September 2004 after Robin Duval's retirement, declaring that classification must be "independent, fair and open" and command public confidence ([BFI Screenonline](http://www.screenonline.org.uk/people/id/1051154/index.html)). His first high-profile calls — an uncut 18 certificate for Michael Winterbottom's sexually explicit *Nine Songs* and an adults-only restriction for Tracey Emin's *Top Spot* over imitable suicide techniques — signalled continuity with Duval's openness policy ([BFI Screenonline](http://www.screenonline.org.uk/people/id/1051154/index.html)).

## Twelve years at the regulator

Cooke's twelve years at the BBFC covered the industry's digital transition. He oversaw two public consultation reviews of the Classification Guidelines and pushed the board's services onto new platforms: online regulation for video-on-demand, a classification framework for mobile operators, and age ratings for UK online music videos ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/news/bbfc-director-cooke-to-retire/5091816.article)). He worked with the presidential team and council of management to make the BBFC "more open and informative" as online outlets proliferated ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/news/bbfc-director-cooke-to-retire/5091816.article)). His most contentious call came in October 2011, when the board refused *The Bunny Game* a certificate; the decision was taken jointly by Cooke and the Presidential Team, and Cooke said issuing a certificate "would be inconsistent with the Board's Guidelines" and risked harm under the Video Recordings Act — banning its UK supply ([JoBlo](https://www.joblo.com/adam-rehmeiers-the-bunny-game-banned-by-british-board-of-film-classification/amp/)). He retired on March 12, 2016, at 60, with David Austin succeeding him; he later became a Visiting Professor at University College London and a Non-Executive Director for the Information Commissioner ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/news/bbfc-director-cooke-to-retire/5091816.article); [BHASVIC](https://www.bhasvic.ac.uk/news/david-cooke-former-director-of-the-bbfc-will-be-visiting-bhasvic-on-wednesday-2-march)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is David Cooke?**
David Cooke is the former Director of the British Board of Film Classification, leading the UK film regulator from September 2004 to March 2016. A career civil servant since 1977, he previously worked in the Home Office, Cabinet Office and Northern Ireland Office, including on the Northern Ireland peace process. This is the BBFC director, not any other namesake.

**What did David Cooke do before the BBFC?**
Cooke was a career civil servant from 1977. Between 1987 and 1990 he headed a policy unit in the Home Office Broadcasting Department, helping set up the Broadcasting Standards Council and the Radio Authority and co-ordinating the 1990 Broadcasting Act; at his BBFC appointment he was Associate Political Director at the Northern Ireland Office.

**What was Cooke's role in The Bunny Game ban?**
In October 2011 the BBFC refused *The Bunny Game* a certificate; the decision was taken jointly by Cooke as Director and the Presidential Team of Sir Quentin Thomas, Alison Hastings and Gerard Lemos. Cooke said issuing a certificate would have been inconsistent with the board's guidelines and risked harm under the Video Recordings Act.

**Who succeeded David Cooke at the BBFC?**
David Austin succeeded Cooke as BBFC director on March 12, 2016, following an open competition. Cooke, who had decided "some time ago" to retire when he turned 60, described the role as a "wonderful job" with "superb colleagues and an endlessly fascinating subject matter."
