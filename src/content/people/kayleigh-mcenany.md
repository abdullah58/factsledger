## Overview

Kayleigh McEnany's defining Washington story is not the podium — it is the phone call that put her behind it. In 2020, she was sitting in a car with her 3-month-old daughter when Donald Trump rang to ask her to become his next White House press secretary; she reached into the back seat to hand the baby a pink pacifier, hoping the child would not cry during the call ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/08/29/kayleigh-mcenany-white-house-press-secretary-motherhood/91408320007/)). She said yes, and then spent her tenure navigating an infant's schedule around the most visible communications job in American government. "I basically had to leave my baby in Florida and pray," she told USA Today in an exclusive August 2026 interview, describing how the COVID-19 lockdown forced her to commute between Washington and her daughter, while her husband and mother held the line ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/08/29/kayleigh-mcenany-white-house-press-secretary-motherhood/91408320007/)).

That motherhood-in-the-West-Wing thread runs through the whole profile, and it is the piece of McEnany's story that standard biographical outlines skip over. The outlines do the job of listing the titles: press secretary from April 2020 through January 2021, co-host of [Fox News](/organizations/fox-news/)'s *Outnumbered*, host of her own Saturday program ([Fox News Media](https://uk.advfn.com/stock-market/share-news/FOX-NEWS-CHANNEL-ANNOUNCES-NEW-WEEKEND-PROGRAMMING/96798073)). But the arc that matters starts earlier — at a producer's desk on Mike Huckabee's Fox News show — and the mechanics of how she climbed from cable pundit to the White House briefing room and back to a cable news desk are the part worth telling ([Metro US](https://www.metro.us/what-you-need-to-know-about-kayleigh-mcenany-rnc-spokeswoman/); [Fox News Media](https://uk.advfn.com/stock-market/share-news/FOX-NEWS-CHANNEL-ANNOUNCES-NEW-WEEKEND-PROGRAMMING/96798073)).

## Tampa to the briefing room pipeline

McEnany was born April 18, 1988, in Tampa, Florida ([Metro US](https://www.metro.us/what-you-need-to-know-about-kayleigh-mcenany-rnc-spokeswoman/)). Her schooling ran through three elite institutions: a J.D. from Harvard Law School, an undergraduate degree in international politics from Georgetown University's School of Foreign Service, and a stint studying politics and international relations at Oxford University's St Edmund Hall ([Metro US](https://www.metro.us/what-you-need-to-know-about-kayleigh-mcenany-rnc-spokeswoman/)). Fox News's own 2025 announcement repeats the same trio, which has become the standard credential block in her biography ([Fox News Media](https://uk.advfn.com/stock-market/share-news/FOX-NEWS-CHANNEL-ANNOUNCES-NEW-WEEKEND-PROGRAMMING/96798073)).

Before law school she interned in the Bush White House's Office of Media Affairs, for Congressman Adam Putnam, for Tom Gallagher's gubernatorial campaign, and on Bush-Cheney 2004 — an early sign of where her politics were pointed ([Metro US](https://www.metro.us/what-you-need-to-know-about-kayleigh-mcenany-rnc-spokeswoman/)). Her television career began on the production side, as a producer for former Arkansas governor Mike Huckabee's Fox News program ([Metro US](https://www.metro.us/what-you-need-to-know-about-kayleigh-mcenany-rnc-spokeswoman/)). She crossed to the on-air side in 2016 as a paid CNN contributor, with appearances on ABC's *The View* and Fox programs along the way ([Metro US](https://www.metro.us/what-you-need-to-know-about-kayleigh-mcenany-rnc-spokeswoman/)).

The pivot came in August 2017. She left CNN to join the Republican National Committee as its national spokeswoman — a move Metro US reported contemporaneously, with RNC chairwoman Ronna Romney McDaniel calling her "an integral part of our Party's ongoing commitment to promoting the Republican message" ([Metro US](https://www.metro.us/what-you-need-to-know-about-kayleigh-mcenany-rnc-spokeswoman/)). Just before that, she had been hosting a short pro-Trump video segment called "News of the Week" on Trump's official Facebook page — a hybrid of party work and cable performance that previewed the press-secretary role ([Metro US](https://www.metro.us/what-you-need-to-know-about-kayleigh-mcenany-rnc-spokeswoman/)). From the RNC she moved to the Trump 2020 reelection campaign as spokeswoman ([Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/589778/mcenany-takes-over-as-white-house-press-secretary)).

## White House press secretary

Trump appointed her White House press secretary on April 7, 2020, announcing the decision April 8; she took over the job on Wednesday, April 15, a week after her predecessor [Stephanie Grisham](/people/stephanie-grisham/) stepped down ([Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/589778/mcenany-takes-over-as-white-house-press-secretary)). At 31, she became Trump's fourth press secretary, following [Sean Spicer](/people/sean-spicer/), Sarah Huckabee Sanders and Grisham, and entered the role as the administration was locked in open conflict with several major news outlets over its COVID-19 response ([Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/589778/mcenany-takes-over-as-white-house-press-secretary)). She held the position through the end of Trump's first term in January 2021.

The job came with an unusual backstage arrangement. When she took it, her daughter was four months old, and the pandemic lockdown meant the baby stayed in Florida with her mother and husband while McEnany set up in Washington; they flew up to visit regularly, so the child ended up spending most of her time in D.C. ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/08/29/kayleigh-mcenany-white-house-press-secretary-motherhood/91408320007/)). She described making sure to have dinner with them when they were together and flying down to Florida on weekends, working remotely when the baby was there ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/08/29/kayleigh-mcenany-white-house-press-secretary-motherhood/91408320007/)). Chief of staff Mark Meadows became an unlikely ally of that schedule: she recalled watching the clock before a weekend flight home and, without saying a word, having Meadows tell her, "Kayleigh, don't you usually catch a flight around this time? Get out of here. Go see your family" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/08/29/kayleigh-mcenany-white-house-press-secretary-motherhood/91408320007/)). On one trip, Trump flew with her on a day trip, returned on Marine One on a Saturday night, and kept asking where her daughter was — then told her, "Kayleigh, next time send one of the younger (aides) on the trip. You go be with your family. Nothing is more important than that time" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/08/29/kayleigh-mcenany-white-house-press-secretary-motherhood/91408320007/)).

One detail of the pipeline connects her to the next generation of Trump communicators. Karoline Leavitt served as an assistant press secretary in the first Trump administration, and Leavitt's own campaign biography says she helped prepare Press Secretary McEnany for high-pressure briefings — a working relationship that foreshadowed Leavitt's own turn behind the briefing-room podium ([Karoline Leavitt](/people/karoline-leavitt/)).

## Fox News, books and family

McEnany joined Fox News as an on-air commentator on March 2, 2021, a few weeks after leaving the White House, and was later named co-host of the weekday ensemble program *Outnumbered* at 12 PM/ET — a slot the network says averages nearly 2 million viewers and occasionally outpaces ABC's *The View* ([Fox News Media](https://uk.advfn.com/stock-market/share-news/FOX-NEWS-CHANNEL-ANNOUNCES-NEW-WEEKEND-PROGRAMMING/96798073)). In September 2025, Fox News Media president Jay Wallace announced she would host her own two-hour program, *Saturday in America with Kayleigh McEnany*, premiering Saturday, September 20, 2025, from 10 AM to 12 PM ET on Saturdays, while she remained an *Outnumbered* co-host ([Fox News Media](https://uk.advfn.com/stock-market/share-news/FOX-NEWS-CHANNEL-ANNOUNCES-NEW-WEEKEND-PROGRAMMING/96798073)).

She is the author of two books: *The New American Revolution: The Making of a Populist Movement*, published by Threshold Editions in January 2018 while she was the RNC's national spokeswoman, and *For Such a Time as This: My Faith Journey through the White House and Beyond*, published by Post Hill Press in December 2021 ([Books-A-Million](https://www.booksamillion.com/p/New-American-Revolution/Kayleigh-McEnany/Q583632901); [Books-A-Million](https://www.booksamillion.com/p/Such-Time-This/Kayleigh-McEnany/Q645863512)).

McEnany married MLB pitcher Sean Gilmartin in November 2017 ([Metro US](https://www.metro.us/what-you-need-to-know-about-kayleigh-mcenany-rnc-spokeswoman/)). Her first child was born in 2019 while she was working on Trump's reelection campaign, and she and her husband have had two more children since leaving the White House — three in all ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/08/29/kayleigh-mcenany-white-house-press-secretary-motherhood/91408320007/)). Looking back, she keeps photographs of her daughter sitting on her White House desk and the baby's stroller in the White House, and remembers dancing with her to "Frozen" while the Marine Band spontaneously played "Let It Go" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/08/29/kayleigh-mcenany-white-house-press-secretary-motherhood/91408320007/)).
