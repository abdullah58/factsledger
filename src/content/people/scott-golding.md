## Overview

Scott Golding is the former office manager and bookkeeper of [Article 25](/organizations/article-25/), a humanitarian architecture charity in the [United Kingdom](/places/united-kingdom/), who was known at work as William Golding. In December 2015 he was sentenced to four years and eight months in prison for defrauding Article 25 of £260,479, after pleading guilty to fraud by abuse of position and false accounting ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)). He had joined the charity in 2013, after being released from a six-month prison sentence for stealing £9,500 from the Bristol children's hospital charity [Wallace and Gromit's Grand Appeal](/organizations/wallace-and-gromits-grand-appeal/) ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)).

Golding was 47 at the time of the Article 25 case, and was described at sentencing as being of no fixed address ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)). The two convictions, reported in the architecture and charity press in 2015, are the whole of his documented public record in the sources read for this profile: nothing about his life before the Grand Appeal case, or after the Article 25 sentence, appears in those sources, and this profile claims nothing about either period. The case matters beyond one man's record because it nearly closed the charity he worked for, leaving it with £611 in its account and dependent on an emergency appeal from the architecture and construction industry to survive ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)).

## The prior conviction: Wallace and Gromit's Grand Appeal

Before Article 25, Golding had served a six-month prison sentence for stealing £9,500 from Wallace and Gromit's Grand Appeal, the Bristol children's hospital charity ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)). Third Sector reports that he joined Article 25 in 2013 to look after its accounts after being released from that sentence ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)). That single-sentence account is the only detail of the earlier case found in the sources read in full for this profile: no second independent report of the Grand Appeal prosecution was located, and no source gives the date of that conviction, the court, or the circumstances of the theft beyond the amount. This profile therefore reports the prior conviction as Third Sector reported it, and no further.

What the later reporting does establish is that Article 25 did not know about that history when it hired him. A Charity Commission spokesman said after the 2015 fraud that the commission had met Article 25's trustees once they reported it, that the trustees had done the right thing in reporting the matter, and that the charity "was not aware of the previous conviction and was a victim of serious fraud" ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)). At Article 25, Golding worked under his middle name: Third Sector reports that he used the name William during the 18 months he worked for the charity ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)). The July 2015 reporting accordingly calls him William Golding, the charity's office manager and bookkeeper ([ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/769992/article-25-launch-appeal-after-uncovering-multiple-unapproved-payments-in-excess-of-200000-pounds); [Construction Management](https://constructionmanagement.co.uk/ai9d-char3ity-article-25-hit-2001000-fraud/)), while the charge and sentencing reports give his full name as Scott William Golding ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2015/07/15/article-25-architecture-charity-bookkeeper-charged-fraud/); [Architects' Journal](https://www.architectsjournal.co.uk/news/man-charged-over-article-25-missing-cash)).

## The Article 25 fraud, charge and sentence

The fraud came to light at the end of June 2015. Article 25's bank alerted its managing director to irregular account activity on 29 June, and the trustees then uncovered what they described as "a systematic falsification of financial statements that have hidden multiple unapproved payments, which are believed to be in excess of £200,000" ([ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/769992/article-25-launch-appeal-after-uncovering-multiple-unapproved-payments-in-excess-of-200000-pounds)). Construction Management reported the same sequence: the bank alert on 29 June, the falsified statements, and payments believed to exceed £200,000 ([Construction Management](https://constructionmanagement.co.uk/ai9d-char3ity-article-25-hit-2001000-fraud/)). Golding was absent on Monday 29 June and uncontactable from then on ([ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/769992/article-25-launch-appeal-after-uncovering-multiple-unapproved-payments-in-excess-of-200000-pounds)). Third Sector adds the detail that the fraud surfaced when the bank told the charity there was not enough money in the account to cover a credit card bill, and that Golding texted a colleague that day saying he would not be in work — and never returned ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)). The Metropolitan Police confirmed that police in Tower Hamlets were investigating an allegation of theft by an employee at a charity based in Canada Square, E14, reported on Wednesday 1 July ([ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/769992/article-25-launch-appeal-after-uncovering-multiple-unapproved-payments-in-excess-of-200000-pounds)).

The discovery left the charity facing insolvency. Its trustees appointed an insolvency expert and mounted a rescue campaign to raise £130,000, with £110,000 already pledged within the first week ([ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/769992/article-25-launch-appeal-after-uncovering-multiple-unapproved-payments-in-excess-of-200000-pounds)). By 15 July the £130,000 target had been reached, enabling the charity to continue its work, including projects in Myanmar, Haiti and Nepal ([ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/769992/article-25-launch-appeal-after-uncovering-multiple-unapproved-payments-in-excess-of-200000-pounds)). Chair [Sunand Prasad](/people/sunand-prasad/) called what had happened "a particularly bitter blow," noting that the charity was poised to work on some of the most important projects in its history ([Construction Management](https://constructionmanagement.co.uk/ai9d-char3ity-article-25-hit-2001000-fraud/)).

Golding was arrested and charged in mid-July 2015. Dezeen, citing a Metropolitan Police Service statement, reports that the 47-year-old former bookkeeper was arrested and charged with fraud and false accounting on 14 July and was due to appear in custody at Thames Magistrates' Court in London that day ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2015/07/15/article-25-architecture-charity-bookkeeper-charged-fraud/)). The Architects' Journal likewise reports that he was arrested and interviewed on 14 July, was set to appear at Thames Magistrates' Court the following morning, and was charged with false accounting and fraud by abuse of position ([Architects' Journal](https://www.architectsjournal.co.uk/news/man-charged-over-article-25-missing-cash)). Third Sector gives a slightly different arrest date, saying Golding went to a police station in Bournemouth on 13 July and was arrested and charged there; the discrepancy is flagged openly in this profile rather than resolved by guesswork ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)).

In November 2015, Golding pleaded guilty to charges of false accounting and fraud by abuse of position ([Architects' Journal](https://www.architectsjournal.co.uk/news/article-25-office-manager-gets-four-years-in-prison)). The following month he was sentenced at Snaresbrook Crown Court in north-east London to four years and eight months in prison after pleading guilty to using the charity's money to pay for designer goods and rent ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)). The final amount was £260,479 — well above the "in excess of £200,000" figure first reported when the fraud was discovered, a progression in the reporting as the investigation established the full sum rather than a contradiction between sources ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)).

The sentencing reports describe how the money was taken. Golding had used his position as office manager and bookkeeper to authorise payments from the charity to his own bank account for extra pay and bonuses and for goods and services that had nothing to do with Article 25 ([Architects' Journal](https://www.architectsjournal.co.uk/news/article-25-office-manager-gets-four-years-in-prison)). He paid himself an inflated salary and bonuses, paid his rent from the charity's bank account, and used the charity's bank cards to buy goods from stores including Alexander McQueen, Gucci, Prada and Harrods, according to the Crown Prosecution Service ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)). To conceal the theft he presented senior management with fictitious accounts portraying a healthy financial picture ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)), falsified bank statements to omit the fraudulent payments, and diverted calls from the bank to himself ([Architects' Journal](https://www.architectsjournal.co.uk/news/article-25-office-manager-gets-four-years-in-prison)).

The official reaction was unsparing. Crown Prosecution Service reviewing lawyer Nasreen Yadallee said Golding had "cruelly abused the trust of his employers at the charity purely to satisfy his own greed," and that the CPS would use the Proceeds of Crime Act to retrieve the money stolen ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)). Article 25 chair Sunand Prasad said that with Golding's imprisonment "it is good to see that justice has been done," but that the sentence could not itself repair the damage: "Golding worked systematically, forging documents, and defrauding Article 25 of vital funds," and had abused the personal trust of colleagues who worked alongside him daily ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)). Detective Sergeant Richard George, who investigated the fraud, described Golding's actions as a "despicable crime," noting that by pleading guilty he had spared his former employers a court trial, but that the fraud would have had a significant impact on the work the charity could have carried out ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)). That impact is the measurable consequence of the case: a charity left with £611 in its account, saved only by emergency funds pledged by the architecture and building industry ([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/charity-worker-jailed-260k-fraud/finance/article/1377751)).
