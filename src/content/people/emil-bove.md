## The prosecutor the Southern District couldn't keep — and the president couldn't stop promoting

In March 2018, David Patton, then head of Manhattan's federal public defender office, emailed the top officials of the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York to relay what eight defense lawyers were saying about one of their prosecutors, Emil Bove. One called him vindictive, someone who abused his power and was "impossible to deal with." Another called him "completely reckless and out of control." The full email, later published by Senate Democrats, carried the line that stuck: Bove was "the prosecutor version of a drunk driver — completely reckless and out of control" ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/); [Sen. Welch's office](https://www.welch.senate.gov/senators-demand-sdny-misconduct-records-ahead-of-emil-boves-nomination-hearing/)).

Patton asked for no specific discipline, writing only, "I fear there will be many future problems." The complaint did stall Bove's ascent, delaying his promotion to help run the office's terrorism and international narcotics unit ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/)). By October 2019 his standing had recovered enough that he was promoted to co-chief of that unit anyway — and within a year, the case that undid him there began to unravel ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/)).

That case, against Iranian banker Ali Sadr Hashemi Nejad on sanctions-evasion charges, ended with the conviction vacated in July 2020 after the government acknowledged problems in how evidence had been turned over. In September, the judge, Alison Nathan, issued an opinion excoriating the office: prosecutors had engaged in a "deliberate attempt to obscure" the truth, she wrote, though she stopped short of finding they had intentionally withheld documents or misled the court ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/)). Court-released communications showed Bove, as the unit's supervisor, acknowledging in a Sunday-night exchange that his team had told a "flat lie" to the judge, vowing to "smash" the defendant, making a lewd comment about one of the defendant's attorneys, and jokingly telling a colleague they would "get cocaine for you" so she could pull an all-nighter repairing the damage ([theanswerorlando.com's AP reprint](https://theanswerorlando.com/national/trumps-justice-department-enforcer-is-no-stranger-to-complaints-about-his-conduc/c17e00137d3362a74f0b432d0b3f6391)).

Around the same time, the Southern District's leaders decided to demote Bove after an internal investigation prompted by complaints about his management style that had cratered morale in his unit — but kept him in place until the Sadr matter was resolved, to avoid the appearance that anyone had done anything intentionally wrong ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/)). He departed the office in December 2021. And when the Jan. 6 attack came in the middle of all this, Bove — who would later pressure the FBI's interim leaders to hand over the names of personnel involved in Jan. 6 investigations — was, by colleagues' accounts, an unapologetic participant: watching from his Lower Manhattan office, working with the New York Joint Terrorism Task Force, and half-jokingly telling a team member to come back with an indictment of a rioter or not bother returning ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/)).

None of the standard biographies develop this stretch. It matters because it is the prologue to everything that followed: the same bulldog style, the same estranged-family feud with the office that made him, and the same willingness to treat disagreement as disloyalty.

## The making of a bulldog

Bove was born in 1981 in Seneca Falls, New York, and graduated from the State University of New York at Albany in 2003 with a B.A. in public policy and economics ([Alliance for Justice](https://afj.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Emil-Bove-SCOTUS-Shortlist-Report.pdf)). He applied for a paralegal job in the Southern District, hoping to be near the action, and was unexpectedly offered a slot in the securities unit. He left in 2005 for Georgetown's law school, earned his J.D. in 2008, clerked for Judge Richard J. Sullivan and then for Second Circuit Judge Richard C. Wesley, spent time at the white-shoe firm Sullivan & Cromwell, and in 2012 was hired as a prosecutor by Preet Bharara, the Obama-appointed U.S. attorney in Manhattan ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/); [Alliance for Justice](https://afj.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Emil-Bove-SCOTUS-Shortlist-Report.pdf)).

As a line attorney and supervisor he stacked up major cases: the indictment of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro on narco-terrorism charges, the conviction of Cesar Sayoc for mailing pipe bombs to prominent Democrats and Trump critics, a Hezbollah operative, an FBI employee accused of being a Chinese agent, and the trial of the man who detonated a pressure-cooker bomb in Chelsea, sentenced to two life terms ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/)). He kept a capped pipe bomb from one of his cases on his desk as a memento, and approached the job with the intensity he had shown as a standout college lacrosse player ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/)).

One episode colored his view of the office. In 2016, during a corruption investigation into Mayor Bill de Blasio's 2013 campaign fundraising, an FBI agent surprised Bove's wife — a policy adviser to the mayor — with a request to turn over her communications records. There was no allegation of wrongdoing by his wife, and de Blasio was never charged, but Bove saw the approach as needlessly traumatizing to his family, briefly considered quitting, and took several days off — which some colleagues thought was an overreaction ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/)). Years later, the 2018 public-defender complaint cut so deeply that he displayed a copy of it in his office as a corrective reminder of his past behavior ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/)).

## Trump's defense table

After leaving the Southern District in December 2021, Bove joined a small New Jersey firm. In the summer of 2023, when he began representing a woman charged by the Southern District with extensive fraud, prosecutors moved to disqualify him, arguing he might possess confidential information from his SDNY years; he pushed back on ethics grounds, but the client declined to waive the conflict and he withdrew, angered ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/)).

In September 2023 he joined Blanche Law, the firm founded by [Todd Blanche](/people/todd-blanche/), who like Bove had started at the Southern District as a paralegal and was then leading Trump's criminal defense ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/)). Bove was quickly added to the defense team as Blanche's second chair in the New York hush-money trial that ended in 34 felony convictions, and later represented Trump in the federal cases over classified documents and the effort to obstruct certification of the 2020 election ([Alliance for Justice](https://afj.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Emil-Bove-SCOTUS-Shortlist-Report.pdf)). He was the hard-driving counterpart to the more laid-back Blanche, willing to make overtly political arguments in court — at one hearing, Judge Aileen Cannon, the Trump appointee presiding over the documents case, offered a verbal eye-roll when Bove complained the case was interfering with Trump's campaign schedule: "Can we talk about the actual legal issues?" ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/)). During the Manhattan trial, Trump rarely belittled Bove the way he did his other lawyers in explosive holding-room sessions — perhaps because Bove was second in command to Blanche ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/)).

## The department's hatchet man

After Trump's 2024 win, Bove was named principal associate deputy attorney general, and on January 20, 2025, he became acting deputy attorney general, running the department's day-to-day operations until Blanche was confirmed in March ([Alliance for Justice](https://afj.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Emil-Bove-SCOTUS-Shortlist-Report.pdf); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/10/politics/emil-bove-swearing-in-todd-blanche-attorney-general?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). He quickly earned a reputation as Trump's enforcer, leading the first waves of mass oustings of career prosecutors and officials ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/10/politics/emil-bove-swearing-in-todd-blanche-attorney-general?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)).

The signature episode was the [Eric Adams](/people/eric-adams/) corruption case. Bove stood stone-faced and alone at the prosecution table in Manhattan to seek dismissal of the charges against the New York mayor — a task so dubious that two prosecutors resigned rather than carry it out, including interim U.S. Attorney Danielle Sassoon ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/)). Afterward he lashed out at his former office: "There are no separate sovereigns in this executive branch," he wrote, suggesting colleagues who disagreed should prepare to resign ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/)). CNN later reported he axed several prosecutors in New York and Washington who refused to sign the dismissal papers ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/10/politics/emil-bove-swearing-in-todd-blanche-attorney-general?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)).

He also became the face of the administration's retribution campaign against Jan. 6 investigators, pressuring the FBI's interim leaders for the names of personnel involved and firing prosecutors tied to those cases ([The New York Times](https://overpassesforamerica.com/emil-bove-justice-dept-s-no-2-targets-ny-office-where-he-rose-as-a-prosecutor/)). Then came the whistleblower: former DOJ lawyer Erez Reuveni alleged Bove had suggested government lawyers tell the courts "fuck you" and ignore orders blocking deportations of Venezuelans under the Alien Enemies Act. About 80 former federal and state judges signed a letter calling his record disqualifying; retired SDNY Judge Shira Scheindlin called him a "loyal soldier," noting Trump had posted that Bove would do anything necessary to make America great again ([TMJ4/AP](https://www.tmj4.com/politics/the-president/who-is-emil-bove-trumps-former-lawyer-confirmed-to-appeals-court)). At his hearing Bove said he did not recall the remarks and pushed back on the caricature: "I am not anybody's henchman. I'm not an enforcer. I'm a lawyer from a small town who never expected to be in an arena like this" ([TMJ4/AP](https://www.tmj4.com/politics/the-president/who-is-emil-bove-trumps-former-lawyer-confirmed-to-appeals-court)).

## A 50–49 confirmation and a lifetime seat

Trump nominated Bove to the Third Circuit on May 28, 2025, for the seat vacated by the retired Joseph A. Greenaway Jr. ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/nomination/119th-congress/346/2); [Alliance for Justice](https://afj.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Emil-Bove-SCOTUS-Shortlist-Report.pdf)). The [Senate Judiciary Committee](/organizations/senate-judiciary-committee/) advanced him 12–0 on July 17 after every Democrat walked out in protest of Chairman Chuck Grassley's refusal to let the whistleblower testify ([TMJ4/AP](https://www.tmj4.com/politics/the-president/who-is-emil-bove-trumps-former-lawyer-confirmed-to-appeals-court)). On July 29 the full Senate confirmed him 50–49, with only Republicans Susan Collins and Lisa Murkowski joining Democrats in opposition ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/nomination/119th-congress/346/2); [TMJ4/AP](https://www.tmj4.com/politics/the-president/who-is-emil-bove-trumps-former-lawyer-confirmed-to-appeals-court)). More than 900 former DOJ staff had urged rejection ([Alliance for Justice](https://afj.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Emil-Bove-SCOTUS-Shortlist-Report.pdf)).

## The oath that closed the loop

On August 10, 2026, Bove administered the oath of office to Todd Blanche as attorney general in an Oval Office ceremony held without press access, with Blanche's wife, daughter, and granddaughter present — and with FBI Director Kash Patel and White House deputy chief of staff Stephen Miller in attendance ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/10/politics/emil-bove-swearing-in-todd-blanche-attorney-general?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). Blanche had been confirmed early that Saturday morning, 50–49, with Collins and Murkowski again the Republican no votes; as part of his confirmation negotiations he signed a document rescinding the "anti-weaponization fund" created to compensate people who said they were improperly investigated ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/10/politics/emil-bove-swearing-in-todd-blanche-attorney-general?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)).

The symmetry was hard to miss. Two men who had started as paralegals in the Southern District, sat together at Trump's defense table, and run the Justice Department as its top two officials — now one a life-tenured appellate judge, swearing in the other as the nation's chief law enforcement officer.

## On the bench — and already a recusal fight

Bove serves on the Philadelphia-based Third Circuit, which hears appeals from Delaware, New Jersey, and Pennsylvania ([Alliance for Justice](https://afj.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Emil-Bove-SCOTUS-Shortlist-Report.pdf)). His first year on the bench has already revived the conflict questions from his confirmation. In April 2026, lawyers for Mahmoud Khalil — the former Columbia graduate student fighting deportation — asked Bove to step aside from en banc review of a January panel ruling that moved the administration closer to detaining and deporting the activist, arguing that as principal associate deputy attorney general Bove had "directed immigration enforcement investigations and decisions against student protesters on college campuses," including at Columbia ([SRN/AP](https://srnnews.com/activist-mahmoud-khalil-wants-ex-justice-department-official-off-panel-of-judges-weighing-his-appeal/)). The Justice Department said it saw "no basis for recusal but defers to Judge Bove"; Bove declined to comment ([SRN/AP](https://srnnews.com/activist-mahmoud-khalil-wants-ex-justice-department-official-off-panel-of-judges-weighing-his-appeal/)). During confirmation he had acknowledged his DOJ role "could give rise to actual or potential conflicts" and pledged to recuse from cases he was personally involved in ([SRN/AP](https://srnnews.com/activist-mahmoud-khalil-wants-ex-justice-department-official-off-panel-of-judges-weighing-his-appeal/)). The motion also noted that in February 2025, a month before Khalil's arrest, Bove had co-authored the memo creating a DOJ task force on "investigating and prosecuting acts of terrorism, antisemitic civil rights violations, and other federal crimes committed by Hamas supporters in the United States, including on college campuses" ([SRN/AP](https://srnnews.com/activist-mahmoud-khalil-wants-ex-justice-department-official-off-panel-of-judges-weighing-his-appeal/)).
