## Overview

Shibani Joshi's path into broadcast journalism ran in reverse of the usual order: she
started as a Morgan Stanley investment-banking analyst working on hundred-million-dollar
deals, then took a production-assistant job at [CNN](/organizations/cnn/)fn where she
worked 3 a.m. to noon making banners, creating graphics, and rolling teleprompter
([South Lakes Sentinel](https://www.southlakessentinel.com/uncategorized/2013/02/22/shibani-joshi-fox-business-reporter-and-anchor/)).
"I learned the business from the ground up," she said in a 2013 interview, describing how
she drew on her Wall Street experience to break into financial journalism
([South Lakes Sentinel](https://www.southlakessentinel.com/uncategorized/2013/02/22/shibani-joshi-fox-business-reporter-and-anchor/)).
That grounding shaped a career that moved from CNN's financial network through Disney/ABC's
strategy ranks to about six years as a technology reporter and anchor at the
[Fox Business Network](/organizations/fox-business-network/) and the
[Fox News Channel](/organizations/fox-news/), where she hosted her own technology segment,
"Joshi on Tech"
([Women Business Collaborative](https://wbcollaborative.org/a4i_summit_speakers/shibani-joshi/);
[CNBC Events](https://www.cnbcevents.com/speakers/shibani-joshi/)).
After leaving Fox, she built an independent practice as a communications advisor, keynote
speaker, and executive coach — including coaching Stanford Graduate School of Business
alumni — and teaches in the University of Oklahoma's Executive MBA program
([Shibani Joshi](https://shibanijoshi.com/about/);
[CNBC Events](https://www.cnbcevents.com/speakers/shibani-joshi/)).

## From hundred-million-dollar deals to the teleprompter

After graduating from the University of Oklahoma with a double major in finance and
accounting, Joshi began her career on Wall Street as an investment-banking analyst at
Morgan Stanley in New York, focused on corporate finance and mergers-and-acquisitions
projects
([Women Business Collaborative](https://wbcollaborative.org/a4i_summit_speakers/shibani-joshi/)).
After two years she decided banking would not be her career and aimed instead at financial
journalism, using her business background to land a production-assistant job at CNN
Financial Network
([South Lakes Sentinel](https://www.southlakessentinel.com/uncategorized/2013/02/22/shibani-joshi-fox-business-reporter-and-anchor/)).
The hours were grueling — 3 a.m. to noon — and the work unglamorous: banners, graphics,
teleprompter, deadline management
([South Lakes Sentinel](https://www.southlakessentinel.com/uncategorized/2013/02/22/shibani-joshi-fox-business-reporter-and-anchor/)).
Over time she learned to write, produced her own pieces, pitched ideas, and worked on the
Lou Dobbs weekend program
([South Lakes Sentinel](https://www.southlakessentinel.com/uncategorized/2013/02/22/shibani-joshi-fox-business-reporter-and-anchor/)).

She then earned an MBA at Harvard Business School, where she wrote for the school newspaper
and served as president of the Media and Entertainment Club to stay close to journalism
([South Lakes Sentinel](https://www.southlakessentinel.com/uncategorized/2013/02/22/shibani-joshi-fox-business-reporter-and-anchor/)).
Finding work after Harvard was difficult; to get the on-air experience she needed, she took
night classes at NYU and freelanced — producing for Reuters Television and Times Now,
reporting breaking news for News 12 Westchester, hosting ImagineAsian TV's *The Pulse*,
contributing technology and business stories to ABCNews.com and ABC News Now, and co-hosting
American Desi's *Point of View*
([South Lakes Sentinel](https://www.southlakessentinel.com/uncategorized/2013/02/22/shibani-joshi-fox-business-reporter-and-anchor/);
[Women Business Collaborative](https://wbcollaborative.org/a4i_summit_speakers/shibani-joshi/)).
She later stepped back into corporate strategy as Senior Manager of Digital Strategy at
Disney/ABC Media Networks, contributing to the firm's global strategy
([Women Business Collaborative](https://wbcollaborative.org/a4i_summit_speakers/shibani-joshi/)).

## Six years at Fox Business

In 2007 Joshi joined Fox Business Network as a reporter, beginning a tenure of almost six
years during which she became the dedicated on-air technology reporter for both Fox
Business and the Fox News Channel
([South Lakes Sentinel](https://www.southlakessentinel.com/uncategorized/2013/02/22/shibani-joshi-fox-business-reporter-and-anchor/);
[CNBC Events](https://www.cnbcevents.com/speakers/shibani-joshi/)).
She anchored a daily markets show and hosted her own technology segment, "Joshi on Tech,"
reporting extensively live from the floors of the New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq
([Women Business Collaborative](https://wbcollaborative.org/a4i_summit_speakers/shibani-joshi/)).
Her assignments covered the 2008 market crash, historic interest-rate cuts, record oil
prices, the Bernard Madoff scandal, the "Miracle on the Hudson," and the death of Steve
Jobs
([Women Business Collaborative](https://wbcollaborative.org/a4i_summit_speakers/shibani-joshi/)).
She landed exclusive interviews with large-cap technology chief executives from Apple,
Qualcomm, Google, Sony, and eBay, and interviewed executives from Uber, Dropbox, Rent the
Runway, and Angie's List in their early stages
([Shibani Joshi](https://shibanijoshi.com/about/)).

## The solar claim and its correction

In February 2013, appearing on *Fox & Friends*, Joshi was asked why Germany's solar-power
sector was doing so much better than America's. She answered that Germany is "a smaller
country, and they've got lots of sun... They've got a lot more sun than we do," adding
that on the East Coast solar was "just not going to work"
([RenewEconomy](https://reneweconomy.com.au/fox-news-solar-slam-dunk-germany-is-sunnier-than-us-53405/)).
*National Geographic*, in a piece on renewable-energy misinformation, cited the claim
directly — Germany, it pointed out, gets only about as much annual sun as Seattle or
Alaska, and its sunniest region gets less sun than almost anywhere in the lower 48
states — and reported that Joshi recanted the next day
([National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/environment/article/lovins-debunking-the-renewable-energy-disinformation-campaign)).

## Independent work after Fox

In 2014 Joshi created ShibanionTech.com, a blog on stories at the intersection of
lifestyle and technology, and later launched Boldly-Forward.com for personal essays on
work-life balance; her essays were featured on Elle.com
([Shibani Joshi](https://shibanijoshi.com/about/)).
She built a practice hosting events and leading conferences for corporate clients including
Comcast Business, KPMG, Google, Hewlett-Packard Enterprise, Mastercard, SAP, and T. Rowe
Price, and has moderated events for [CNBC](/organizations/cnbc/), the WSJ CIO Forum, and Fast Company
([Shibani Joshi](https://shibanijoshi.com/about/)).
In coaching, she leads career programs and provides executive career coaching to Stanford
Graduate School of Business alumni, focused on mid-career transitions and returns from
career breaks
([Shibani Joshi](https://shibanijoshi.com/about/);
[CNBC Events](https://www.cnbcevents.com/speakers/shibani-joshi/)).
She developed a course, "Strategic Leadership Communications," for the University of
Oklahoma's Executive MBA program at the Price College of Business, sits on the college's
Board of Advisors, and previously served as a trustee of The Children's School in La
Jolla, California
([Women Business Collaborative](https://wbcollaborative.org/a4i_summit_speakers/shibani-joshi/);
[CNBC Events](https://www.cnbcevents.com/speakers/shibani-joshi/)).
She lives in the Bay Area with her three children
([CNBC Events](https://www.cnbcevents.com/speakers/shibani-joshi/)).
