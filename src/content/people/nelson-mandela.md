## Overview

Nelson Rolihlahla Mandela (18 July 1918 – 5 December 2013) was a South African anti-apartheid leader who spent 27 years in prison, shared the 1993 Nobel Peace Prize, and became South Africa's first Black president, serving from 1994 to 1999 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela); [NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1993/mandela/facts/)). Born in Mvezo and known as Madiba, he led the resistance to apartheid, negotiated its peaceful end with President F. W. de Klerk, and presided over the transition to multiracial democracy ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)).

Mandela was born Rolihlahla Mandela on 18 July 1918 in Mvezo, South Africa; the name Nelson was later added by one of his teachers ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)). He was the son of Chief Henry Mandela of the Madiba clan of the Xhosa-speaking Tembu people, and after his father's death he was raised by Jongintaba, the regent of the Tembu ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)). Although he had a claim to the chieftainship, he renounced it to become a lawyer, attending South African Native College (later the University of Fort Hare) and studying law at the University of the Witwatersrand ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)).

## From lawyer to resistance leader

Mandela joined the African National Congress (ANC) in 1944 and became a leader of its Youth League, the same year he met and married his first wife, Evelyn Ntoko Mase ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)). In 1952, with fellow ANC leader Oliver Tambo, he established South Africa's first Black law practice in Johannesburg, specialising in cases arising from the post-1948 apartheid legislation ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)). That same year he played an important role in launching a campaign of defiance against South Africa's pass laws, which required nonwhites to carry documents authorising their presence in areas the government deemed "restricted", and he travelled throughout the country building support for nonviolent protest ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)). The campaign had a direct personal cost: on 2 December 1952 he was charged with 19 others for violating the Suppression of Communism Act and sentenced to nine months' hard labour, suspended for two years ([Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/trials-and-prison-chronology)).

In 1955 Mandela was involved in drafting the Freedom Charter, a document calling for nonracial social democracy in South Africa ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)). From 1952 he was intermittently banned, severely restricted in travel, association and speech, and in December 1956 he was arrested with more than 100 other people on treason charges ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)). After a four-and-a-half-year trial he and the last remaining accused were acquitted on 29 March 1961, and he went underground ([Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/trials-and-prison-chronology)).

The turning point came at Sharpeville. On 21 March 1960, 69 peaceful protesters were killed by police at Sharpeville, and on 30 March Mandela was one of thousands detained under a state of emergency ([Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/trials-and-prison-chronology)). On 8 April 1960 the apartheid regime banned the ANC and the Pan Africanist Congress ([Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/trials-and-prison-chronology)). After the massacre and the banning, Mandela abandoned his nonviolent stance and began advocating acts of sabotage against the regime ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)). NobelPrize.org records the same sequence: the Sharpeville massacre spurred the ANC to armed struggle, and Mandela was chosen to lead the covert guerrilla organisation, the "Spear of the Nation" ([NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1993/mandela/speedread/)). He went underground — known as the Black Pimpernel for his ability to evade capture — and was one of the founders of Umkhonto we Sizwe ("Spear of the Nation"), the ANC's military wing ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)).

## Twenty-seven years in prison

Mandela was arrested on 5 August 1962 at a road block in Natal, shortly after returning from guerrilla-warfare training in Algeria, and was sentenced to five years in prison ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)). The Mandela Foundation's chronology gives the precise dates: he was arrested near Howick in KwaZulu-Natal and sentenced on 7 November 1962 to five years for incitement and leaving the country illegally, beginning his sentence as prisoner 19476/62 ([Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/trials-and-prison-chronology)).

While he was imprisoned, he and several other men were tried for sabotage, treason and violent conspiracy in the Rivonia Trial, named after a Johannesburg suburb where police had discovered arms and equipment at the Umkhonto we Sizwe headquarters ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)). On 20 April 1964 he made his famous speech from the dock, in which he said he had cherished the ideal of a democratic and free society and was prepared to die for it, and the speech was published later that year as *I Am Prepared to Die* ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela); [Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/trials-and-prison-chronology)). On 12 June 1964 he and seven others were sentenced to life imprisonment, narrowly escaping the death penalty ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)).

From 1964 to 1982 Mandela was imprisoned on Robben Island, off Cape Town ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)). He was assigned the prisoner number 466/64, and on 31 March 1982 he was transferred to Pollsmoor Prison on the mainland ([Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/trials-and-prison-chronology)). In February 1985 he rejected President P. W. Botha's offer to release him and other political prisoners if they renounced violence ([Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/trials-and-prison-chronology)). He was transferred to Victor Verster Prison near Paarl on 7 December 1988 ([Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/trials-and-prison-chronology)). During his imprisonment he became a rallying point for South Africa's oppressed and the world's most famous political prisoner ([NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1993/mandela/facts/)). He was released from Victor Verster Prison on 11 February 1990, after 27 years in prison ([Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/trials-and-prison-chronology); [NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1993/mandela/speedread/)).

## Release, Nobel Prize and presidency

Mandela's negotiations in the early 1990s with President F. W. de Klerk helped end South Africa's apartheid system of racial segregation and ushered in a peaceful transition to majority rule ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)). The Nobel Peace Prize for 1993 was awarded jointly to Mandela and de Klerk "for their work for the peaceful termination of the apartheid regime, and for laying the foundations for a new democratic South Africa" ([NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1993/press-release/)). The prize was announced on 15 October 1993, and the medals were presented in Oslo on 10 December 1993 ([NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1993/mandela/speedread/)). The [Nobel Prize](/events/nobel-prize/) is the awarding body behind the honour, and its official record is the primary source for the prize's motivation and date.

The ANC won the 1994 parliamentary elections, and Mandela became president, presiding over the construction of democracy ([NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1993/mandela/speedread/)). He was South Africa's first Black president, serving from 1994 to 1999, and he did not cling to power but retired with honour in 1999 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela); [NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1993/mandela/speedread/)). The South African Government describes him as the country's first democratically elected president in 1994 and as a champion of reconciliation who was instrumental in the Truth and Reconciliation Commission, set up by the Government of National Unity to help deal with the atrocities of apartheid ([South African Government](https://www.gov.za/nelson-mandela-month-2014)). NobelPrize.org records that the Commission was formed in 1996 to promote reconciliation between ethnic groups and that, through public hearings, it exposed abuses committed by the apartheid regime ([NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1993/mandela/speedread/)). Under his leadership a democratisation process was launched, the country adopted a new constitution granting equal rights for all, and many poor people gained access to electricity and clean water — though serious problems remained, including high unemployment, crime, HIV infection and a persistent gap between rich and poor ([NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1993/mandela/speedread/)).

After retirement Mandela continued to campaign globally for peace, for children and in the fight against HIV and AIDS ([South African Government](https://www.gov.za/nelson-mandela-month-2014)). His autobiography, *Long Walk to Freedom*, which chronicles his early life and years in prison, was published in 1994 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)). He died on 5 December 2013 in Johannesburg, aged 95, and was laid to rest at Qunu in South Africa's Eastern Cape province on 15 December ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Nelson-Mandela)). His birthday, 18 July, has been declared Nelson Mandela International Day ([South African Government](https://www.gov.za/nelson-mandela-month-2014)).
