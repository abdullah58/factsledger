## Overview

For two and a half years, Karine Jean-Pierre spoke for Joe Biden's White House. Then, five months after the administration ended, she made an announcement that reframed her entire tenure: she had left the Democratic Party and registered as an independent. The news came with a book — *Independent: A Look Inside a Broken White House, Outside the Party Lines*, published October 21, 2025 by Legacy Lit, a Hachette imprint — that promised a close look at Biden's decision not to run for re-election and called for thinking beyond the two-party system ([AP](https://srnnews.com/former-white-house-press-secretary-karine-jean-pierre-has-a-book-out-this-fall-independent/)).

It was a striking break for someone whose career had been built inside Democratic institutions. Jean-Pierre had worked in two Democratic administrations and become, in 2022, the first Black person and the first openly LGBTQ person to serve as White House press secretary ([AP](https://srnnews.com/former-white-house-press-secretary-karine-jean-pierre-has-a-book-out-this-fall-independent/); [NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/world-news/biden-names-karine-jean-pierre-as-first-black-woman-white-house-press-secretary-2950390)).

## The break with the party

Jean-Pierre's own statement with the announcement framed the move as a response to the moment. "Until January 20, I was responsible for speaking on behalf of the President of the United States," she said. "At noon on that day, I became a private citizen who, like all Americans and many of our allies around the world, had to contend with what was to come next for our country. I determined that the danger we face as a country requires freeing ourselves of boxes. We need to be willing to exercise the ability to think creatively and plan strategically" ([AP](https://srnnews.com/former-white-house-press-secretary-karine-jean-pierre-has-a-book-out-this-fall-independent/)).

The publisher's description of the book went further, saying she would take readers "through the three weeks that led to Biden's abandoning his bid for a second term and the betrayal by the Democratic Party that led to his decision," and that she offered "clear arguments and provocative evidence as an insider about the importance of dismantling the torrent of disinformation and misinformation that has been rampant in recent elections" ([AP](https://srnnews.com/former-white-house-press-secretary-karine-jean-pierre-has-a-book-out-this-fall-independent/)). Her path into the role had included bouts of criticism — at times she was faulted for being evasive about Biden's physical condition ([AP](https://srnnews.com/former-white-house-press-secretary-karine-jean-pierre-has-a-book-out-this-fall-independent/)).

## The podium years

Biden announced her appointment on May 5, 2022, praising her "experience, talent and integrity," and she took over from Jen Psaki on May 13 ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/world-news/biden-names-karine-jean-pierre-as-first-black-woman-white-house-press-secretary-2950390)). Psaki marked the moment on Twitter: "She will be the first black woman and the first openly LGBTQ+ person to serve as the White House Press Secretary. Representation matters and she will give a voice to many, but also make many dream big about what is truly possible" ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/world-news/biden-names-karine-jean-pierre-as-first-black-woman-white-house-press-secretary-2950390)).

She had served as Psaki's deputy before stepping up, and earlier as a senior adviser during Biden's 2020 campaign ([AP](https://srnnews.com/former-white-house-press-secretary-karine-jean-pierre-has-a-book-out-this-fall-independent/)). She stayed in the job through the end of the administration — leaving, as she later put it, when her responsibility to speak for the president expired at noon on January 20, 2025 — and also carried a senior-adviser title under President [Joe Biden](/people/joe-biden/) ([AP](https://srnnews.com/former-white-house-press-secretary-karine-jean-pierre-has-a-book-out-this-fall-independent/); [Hachette](https://hachettespeakersbureau.com/karine-jean-pierre/)).

In 2023 she received the Phoenix Award, the Congressional Black Caucus Foundation's highest honor, recognizing what the foundation called her trailblazing leadership and dedication to uplifting marginalized communities ([Hachette](https://hachettespeakersbureau.com/karine-jean-pierre/)).

## A career through two administrations

Jean-Pierre's route to the briefing room ran through more than a decade of campaign and advocacy work. She started in New York City politics, handling legislative and budget affairs for one City Council member and serving as deputy chief of staff for another, then moved into national politics at the Center for Community and Corporate Ethics, where she advocated for greater corporate accountability ([Hachette](https://hachettespeakersbureau.com/karine-jean-pierre/)).

In 2008 she joined Barack Obama's presidential campaign; after the election she joined the Obama-Biden White House in the Office of Political Affairs, and in 2011 she moved to the 2012 re-election campaign as deputy battleground states director, working to mobilize key states for Obama's second term ([Hachette](https://hachettespeakersbureau.com/karine-jean-pierre/)). The AP notes she also served as a regional political director during Obama's first term ([AP](https://srnnews.com/former-white-house-press-secretary-karine-jean-pierre-has-a-book-out-this-fall-independent/)).

Her breakout as a campaign manager came when she ran Tish James's successful bid for New York City Public Advocate — making James the first woman to hold that office ([Hachette](https://hachettespeakersbureau.com/karine-jean-pierre/)). As a senior advisor to Biden's 2020 campaign, she then served as chief of staff to running mate Kamala Harris ([Hachette](https://hachettespeakersbureau.com/karine-jean-pierre/)).

Between campaigns, she built the public-facing career that made her a familiar face: a political analyst for NBC and MSNBC, an adjunct professor at Columbia University's School of International and Public Affairs, and chief public affairs officer for the liberal advocacy group MoveOn ([Hachette](https://hachettespeakersbureau.com/karine-jean-pierre/); [NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/world-news/biden-names-karine-jean-pierre-as-first-black-woman-white-house-press-secretary-2950390)). She holds a Master of Public Administration from Columbia's School of International and Public Affairs and received the MLK Day Visionary Award at the National Action Network's Martin Luther King Jr. Day breakfast ([Hachette](https://hachettespeakersbureau.com/karine-jean-pierre/)).

## Background and the story she told

Jean-Pierre was born in Martinique to Haitian parents who emigrated to the United States, where her father drove a taxi and her mother worked as a cleaner. Raised in New York and French-speaking, she has often described her family's background as an embodiment of the American dream that shaped her career ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/world-news/biden-names-karine-jean-pierre-as-first-black-woman-white-house-press-secretary-2950390)).

She has also spoken openly about painful chapters of her life. In her 2019 memoir *Moving Forward: A Story of Hope, Hard Work, and the Promise of America*, she wrote about the pressure of growing up in an immigrant household to succeed; in a post published on the MSNBC website she disclosed that she had been sexually abused as a child, suffered from depression, and at one point attempted suicide: "That pressure grew so big, and my sense of failure so strong, that I felt like my family would be better off without me" ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/world-news/biden-names-karine-jean-pierre-as-first-black-woman-white-house-press-secretary-2950390)). She has advocated for combatting mental-health stigma, and in a 2018 video for MoveOn she introduced herself in lines that became widely quoted: "I am everything that Donald Trump hates. I am a Black woman. I am gay. I am a mom" ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/world-news/biden-names-karine-jean-pierre-as-first-black-woman-white-house-press-secretary-2950390)). She has a daughter with her partner, a journalist with CNN ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/world-news/biden-names-karine-jean-pierre-as-first-black-woman-white-house-press-secretary-2950390)).
