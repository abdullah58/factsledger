## Overview

Süleyman Dilbirliği, a Turkish Army sergeant of the Korean War, is remembered for
one of the war's most documented acts of humanity: in the winter of 1950 he found
an orphaned Korean girl, Kim Eunja, in a ruined village, named her Ayla, and cared
for her for over a year at the Turkish garrison — going so far as to try to smuggle
her back to Turkey inside a large box rather than leave her behind
([Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=145773)). He was
caught, the two were separated, and they did not meet again for sixty years, until
South Korean officials traced Ayla and reunited them in Seoul in 2010
([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).
Their story became the 2017 Turkish film "[Ayla: The Daughter of War](/creative-works/ayla-the-daughter-of-war/)," Turkey's
foreign-language Oscar submission
([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)).
Dilbirliği died in Istanbul in December 2017 at age 91
([Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/turkey/2017/12/07/turkish-korean-war-veteran-who-inspired-film-ayla-passes-away-at-the-age-of-91/amp)).

## The rescue, and the box

Korea.net, the official website of the Republic of Korea, records that on 17
September 1950 Turkey dispatched a brigade of 5,090 soldiers to Korea at the [United Nations](/organizations/united-nations/)' behest, under the command of General Tahsin Yazıcı — and that among them
was Sergeant Süleyman Dilbirliği, aged 25, "one of the thousands of Turkish soldiers
who went to Korea voluntarily"
([Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=145773)).

One night, after heavy fighting, Dilbirliği and his squadron noticed movement in the
bushes on their way back to base: a five-year-old girl, frozen and terrified
([Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=145773)).
Other Turkish soldiers later recalled that she was found crying in the ruins of a
village in North Korea
([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).
She carried no identity papers and could not speak, so the soldiers gave her a
Turkish name, Ayla, for her round, bright, moon-like face — in Turkish, *ayla* is
the halo of light around the moon
([Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=145773);
[Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).

The camp became her home and playground. She learned Turkish, began acting as an
interpreter, and called Dilbirliği "Baba" — father
([Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=145773)).
Years later she remembered those days as the happiest of her life: the soldiers
stitched clothes for her from their own uniforms, staged snowball fights, and on
rest days took her for rides on their tanks
([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).

When the time came to leave, Dilbirliği, in Korea.net's account, refused to accept
the parting. He placed the girl inside a large box among his belongings in an
attempt to take her to Turkey — but was caught red-handed. His recorded farewell:
"You are like a daughter to me. Do not ever forget me. I will never forget you,
and I will patiently wait for the day when I can take you in my arms again!"
([Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=145773)).

Sources differ on the mechanics of the separation: Daily Sabah says the troops had
to return to Turkey and she was first entrusted to other troops, then sent to an
orphanage
([Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/turkey/2017/12/07/turkish-korean-war-veteran-who-inspired-film-ayla-passes-away-at-the-age-of-91/amp)),
while Korea JoongAng Daily reports that the relationship ended when Dilbirliği was
assigned to Japan
([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).
Sources also differ on how long they were together: 14 months
([Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/turkey/2017/12/07/turkish-korean-war-veteran-who-inspired-film-ayla-passes-away-at-the-age-of-91/amp)),
15 months
([Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=145773);
[Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)),
or a year and a half
([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).

## Sixty years of silence, then the search

Ayla was taken to the Ankara School in Suwon, a school the Turkish forces
established for war orphans — deliberately not called an orphanage — where some 60
to 70 children lived and were taught Turkish
([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).
She left the school as a teenager to find work — waiting tables, sewing in a
clothing factory, assisting in a hospital's gynecology department — and at 24 married
through a matchmaker and had a son
([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).

For sixty years she told no one about the Turkish soldier who had been her father.
"No one else had called me Ayla," she said. She did not recall his name and had no
photographs of him
([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).

The search began at an Istanbul event marking the 60th anniversary of the Korean
War, where Dilbirliği spoke publicly about Ayla; South Korean officials then began
looking for the girl
([Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/turkey/2017/12/07/turkish-korean-war-veteran-who-inspired-film-ayla-passes-away-at-the-age-of-91/amp)).
One day in 2010, a Suwon city official called Kim Eunja to ask whether she had once
been called by that name — and days later a producer for a Chuncheon MBC documentary
appeared at her Incheon home carrying Dilbirliği's wartime photographs of the two
of them; Dilbirliği had been contacting Korean tourism offices in Turkey trying to
find her
([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).
A documentary produced by TRT, Turkey's state broadcaster, about Turkish soldiers
in Korea helped bring the story into the open
([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)).

The reunion, filmed for the documentary, took place in a park in Yeouido, western
Seoul. "Is that you, Ayla?" Dilbirliği asked as he walked toward her; she embraced
him through her tears, repeating "Baba, why did you come so late?"
([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).
The pair — joined by Dilbirliği's wife Nimet — were also photographed together at
Ankara Park in Seoul
([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/korea/heroes-from-afar-turkish-soldiers-came-to-the-aid-of-wars-orphans/12141350)).
They wrote to each other regularly afterward, and Ayla visited Turkey again in 2012,
a claim preserved from this profile's original September 2026 verification of the
Hürriyet feature
([Hürriyet](https://www.hurriyet.com.tr/kelebek/televizyon/suleyman-astsubay-kimdir-suleyman-dilbirliginin-gercek-hikayesi-40967608)).

## The film and the final years

In 2016 it was announced that Dilbirliği's story would become a feature film; most
of it was shot in Turkey, the score was by Fahit Atakoğlu, the production company
was Dijital Sanatlar, and Turkish Airlines sponsored it
([Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/NewsFocus/Culture/view?articleId=145773)).
The film, "Ayla: The Daughter of War," directed by [Can Ülkay](/people/can-ulkay/), opened in Turkey on 8
September 2017 and was chosen as Turkey's submission for the foreign-language-film
Oscar; İsmail Hacıoğlu and Çetin Tekindor played the young and elderly Dilbirliği,
while [Kim Seol](/people/kim-seol/) and Lee Kyung-Jin played the young and adult Ayla
([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)).
The publicity campaign drew on support from Turkish Airlines and Turkey's Culture
and Tourism Ministry — though not without friction: screenwriter [Yiğit Güralp](/people/yigit-guralp/)
publicly accused the production team of freezing him out of the process
([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)).

Dilbirliği attended the film's premiere — but fell ill there, suffering respiratory
and renal failure, and was taken to the intensive care unit of Haydarpaşa Training
and Research Hospital in Istanbul, where his condition worsened and he died of
multiple organ failure on 7 December 2017, at 91
([Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/turkey/2017/12/07/turkish-korean-war-veteran-who-inspired-film-ayla-passes-away-at-the-age-of-91/amp)).
In late November, while he was still in hospital, Kim Eunja — then 71 — had flown in
to see him one last time
([Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/turkey/2017/12/07/turkish-korean-war-veteran-who-inspired-film-ayla-passes-away-at-the-age-of-91/amp)).
His funeral was held at the Selimiye Mosque in Istanbul, and he was buried at the
Karacaahmet cemetery
([Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/turkey/2017/12/07/turkish-korean-war-veteran-who-inspired-film-ayla-passes-away-at-the-age-of-91/amp)).
Years later, [Kim Eunja](/people/kim-eun-ja/) — the real-life Ayla — died in Incheon at the age of 80 after a bout of pneumonia, according to a written statement from the Turkish Embassy in Seoul reported by Hürriyet Daily News; the obituary gives the date as May 12 but carries no publication year, so no year is asserted here
([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/ayla-kim-eunja-passes-away-at-80-209134)).

On the first anniversary of his death, Turkey's Consulate General in Chicago and
the Gene Siskel Film Center held a commemorative screening of the film, sponsored
by the Yunus Emre Institute — a claim preserved from this profile's original
September 2026 direct read of the Anadolu Agency report, which could not be
re-fetched this pass
([Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/asia-pacific/turkish-foster-father-of-korean-war-orphan-remembered/1333413)).
