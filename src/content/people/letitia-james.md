Letitia James has been the attorney general of New York since 2019, winning the office in 2018 as the first woman of color elected to statewide office in New York and the first woman ever elected attorney general.[^1] Born and raised in Brooklyn, she is a graduate of Lehman College and Howard University School of Law.[^1] Before leading the state office, she was elected New York City's public advocate in 2013 and spent a decade on the New York City Council representing the 35th District in Brooklyn.[^1]

## The Trump fraud case

James built her national reputation pursuing powerful institutions, above all through the civil case against [Donald Trump](https://factsledger.com/people/donald-trump/). In 2022 she sued Trump, his business entities, and some of his family members, alleging they had fraudulently inflated the value of Trump's assets for years to secure better loan and insurance terms.[^1] New York State Judge Arthur Engoron agreed, finding after a months-long trial that Trump had overstated his wealth and property values, and ordered Trump to pay about 98% of a $464.6 million judgment — $454.2 million personally.[^1][^4] Engoron also barred Trump and [the Trump Organization](/organizations/trump-organization/) from applying for loans from state-registered banks for three years and effectively barred his sons from the business for two years, while keeping a court-appointed monitor in place.[^4]

In August 2025, a deeply divided New York appeals court threw out the half-billion-dollar penalty while preserving the fraud case. Four judges on the five-member panel voted to let the fraud finding stand, though all found problems in the judge's handling of the case; a fifth judge would have dismissed the case altogether. There was no majority opinion.[^4] With interest accruing, the sums had grown to $527 million and $515.2 million.[^4] James said she would ask the Court of Appeals — the state's highest court — to review: "It should not be lost to history: yet another court has ruled that the president violated the law, and that our case has merit."[^4]

## The NRA and Cuomo investigations

In August 2020, James sued the National Rifle Association, alleging it had diverted millions of dollars to fund luxuries for officials including chief executive Wayne LaPierre, failed to obtain board approval for conflicts of interest and insider transactions, and retaliated against whistleblowers who suspected financial misconduct.[^5] In December 2023, a five-judge appellate panel unanimously rejected the NRA's bid to end the probe, finding James had probable cause to investigate and sue and citing the "ample evidence of malfeasance" she claimed to uncover.[^5] A state judge had blocked her effort to dissolve the group in March 2022, finding a lack of evidence for what he called the "corporate death penalty," with a trial set for January 2024 to assess repayments and possible removals.[^5]

In August 2021, James announced the findings of a nearly five-month investigation into Governor [Andrew Cuomo](/people/andrew-cuomo/): Cuomo had sexually harassed multiple women and retaliated against at least one accuser, in what investigators called a "hostile work environment" rife with "fear and intimidation."[^6] The probe, conducted by two outside lawyers, involved interviews with 179 people, including Cuomo himself, and produced a 165-page public report; 11 accusers were found credible.[^6] Cuomo remained defiant, calling the facts "much different than what has been portrayed."[^6] The investigation concluded without referrals to criminal prosecutors, though local authorities could pursue their own cases.[^6]

## The reversal: James indicted, then the case collapses

In a striking reversal, James herself became a defendant. On October 9, 2025, a federal grand jury in the Eastern District of Virginia indicted her on two felony counts: bank fraud and making false statements to a financial institution.[^1][^2] The five-page indictment alleged that in 2020, James bought a three-bedroom house in Norfolk, Virginia, and obtained better mortgage terms by claiming it would be a secondary residence — instead renting it to a family of three, allegedly saving her nearly $19,000 in loan costs.[^2] Prosecutor Lindsey Halligan said James could face up to 30 years in prison on each count, plus fines of up to $1 million per charge.[^2] Her first court appearance was set for October 24.[^2]

The prosecution came after Halligan, installed by the Trump administration, replaced Erik Siebert, the veteran prosecutor who had resigned under pressure after telling the [Justice Department](https://factsledger.com/organizations/us-department-of-justice/) he had insufficient evidence to charge James.[^1] In a September social media post, Trump had called for James, former FBI Director [James Comey](/people/james-comey/), and Senator Adam Schiff to be prosecuted.[^1] James declared her innocence and called the prosecution a "desperate weaponisation of our justice system," saying: "He is forcing federal law enforcement agencies to do his bidding, all because I did my job as the New York State attorney general."[^2]

The case did not survive. Judge Cameron Currie dismissed the prosecutions of James and Comey on the grounds that Halligan had been unlawfully appointed — she was a former White House aide with no prior prosecutorial experience, and the court rejected the administration's retroactive "Special Attorney" designation as a fix.[^3] The dismissal was without prejudice, leaving prosecutors free to try again — but when they went back to a Virginia grand jury, it rejected a new mortgage fraud indictment against James.[^3]
