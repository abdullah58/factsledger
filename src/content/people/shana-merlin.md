## Overview

In November 2025, Shana Merlin announced that Merlin Works' classes and shows would stop at the end of the year — closing the classroom chapter of the improv school she founded in Austin, Texas, in 2003 after 22 years in business. The corporate-training arm, she wrote, was "headed strong into 2026." Merlin, an improv performer, teacher, and producer who has led classes since 1996 and calls 2025 her 30th year of improvising, tallied the school's run at thousands of students, dozens of teachers, hundreds of performances, $221,000 in rent paid to local arts and education organizations, and $617,000 paid to artists as contract labor. [A New Chapter For Merlin Works](https://www.merlin-works.com/2025/11/25/a-new-chapter-for-merlin-works/)

The reasons she gave were practical and personal: the loss of affordable spaces, customers tightening their belts, and — in her words — a year in which, after the unexpected death of her children's father in May, "those skills are needed most at home." She refused the failure frame: "Although we celebrate failure at Merlin Works, this isn't a failure. It's an ending." [A New Chapter For Merlin Works](https://www.merlin-works.com/2025/11/25/a-new-chapter-for-merlin-works/)

## From free passes to founder

Merlin's improv life began by accident. In 1992, when she was fourteen, she and her drama-club friends made the trek from suburban Atlanta to a new improv theater called Dad's Garage. One night the place was empty — the Atlanta Braves were playing in the World Series — and the volunteer at the box office handed the girls a fat stack of free passes and apologized for the cancelled show. They went to free show after free show for almost a year, "falling in love with improv," as she later wrote. She began performing at Dad's Garage in 1995. [Big Announcement: Merlin Works is Moving!](https://www.merlin-works.com/2012/11/06/big-announcement-merlin-works-is-moving/) [About Shana](https://www.merlin.works/about-shana/)

In 2000 she graduated college and helped open the Hideout Theatre in downtown Austin with Sean Hill. She started teaching the classes, helped run the troupe, and performed almost every weekend — "that's when I got my sea legs and went from passable amateur to budding professional comedian." She traveled to study with teachers including Keith Johnstone and Paul Sills and the teachers at BATS Improv in San Francisco. [Big Announcement: Merlin Works is Moving!](https://www.merlin-works.com/2012/11/06/big-announcement-merlin-works-is-moving/) At the Hideout she managed the theatre and served as dean of the school, devising curriculum and handling registration and teaching; before that she had performed with troupes including Los Paranoias, The Oxymorons, and Sean Hill's Austin Theatresports. In 2003 she went out on her own and opened Merlin Works to offer improv classes, corporate team building, and live comedy shows. [Austin Wide Open Magazine: Improv in Austin](https://www.merlin-works.com/2012/02/25/austin-wide-open-magazine-improv-in-austin/) [Big Announcement: Merlin Works is Moving!](https://www.merlin-works.com/2012/11/06/big-announcement-merlin-works-is-moving/)

## Three homes, two near-endings

In 2008 Merlin partnered with Gnap! Theater Projects and opened the Merlin Works Institute for Improvisation at the Salvage Vanguard Theater on Manor Road, an East Austin arts hub that became the base for classes, graduation shows, and private training events. [Big Announcement: Merlin Works is Moving!](https://www.merlin-works.com/2012/11/06/big-announcement-merlin-works-is-moving/) Then came the first near-ending: in January 2012, seven months pregnant, she learned Gnap! would get out of the weekly improv production business by year's end to focus on fewer, larger scripted works. After her son was born in March, she wrote, "I was convinced I would have to let it all go and shut Merlin Works down. I didn't see a way out and I had little time or energy left for my business." [Big Announcement: Merlin Works is Moving!](https://www.merlin-works.com/2012/11/06/big-announcement-merlin-works-is-moving/)

The rescue came on a summer walk through Hyde Park, where she ran into Karen LaShelle of Creative Action — her former boss and sometimes mentor — who refused to accept the shutdown and proposed a brainstorming meeting. That meeting produced the idea of moving to [ZACH Theatre](/organizations/zach-theatre/), Austin's oldest producing theater, which had a strong youth program but no adult classes; her improv friend and ZACH employee Nat Miller championed the deal. In January 2013 Merlin Works classes moved to ZACH — longer sessions, a central location on the banks of Lady Bird Lake near the Topfer Theater, and Second Sunday shows at 8pm on the 230-seat Kleberg Stage. [Big Announcement: Merlin Works is Moving!](https://www.merlin-works.com/2012/11/06/big-announcement-merlin-works-is-moving/) In a later interview she confirmed the shape of that crisis — a new baby, a tanking business, a producing partner winding down — and said the ZACH move led to the best year of business the school had seen. [Conversations Inspiring: Shana Merlin](https://voyagedallas.com/interview/conversations-inspiring-shana-merlin/)

The school's last decade was nomadic. March 2020 brought the final in-person class before pandemic shutdowns; classes later moved to Creative Action, which subsequently lost its space. A former dean of the school, writing a farewell tribute, recalled the early Salvage Vanguard days — Sunday-afternoon jams drawing 50 or more people, doubling as auditions for Gnap! — and the school's traditions: faculty rebranded as "The Known Wizards," and every student who graduated all six levels received a magic wand. [(Not-)Hideout Love List: Merlin Works](https://happywaffle.com/blog/not-hideout-love-list-merlin-works/) [Conversations Inspiring: Shana Merlin](https://voyagedallas.com/interview/conversations-inspiring-shana-merlin/) At the 2025 closing, the school could claim three home bases over the years — Salvage Vanguard Theater, ZACH Theater, and Creative Action — customer ratings of 4.7 stars on Google and 4.8 on Yelp ("The Top Rated Improv Company"), and a published book, *Instant Alchemy: The Science and Magic of Narrative Longform*. [A New Chapter For Merlin Works](https://www.merlin-works.com/2025/11/25/a-new-chapter-for-merlin-works/)

## Troupes, teaching, and the ephemeral stage

Merlin is a founding member of Girls Girls Girls, the all-female troupe her bio describes as B. Iden Payne Award-winning, and of Get Up, the trio she formed with Shannon McCormick — artistic director of Gnap! Theatre Projects and a teacher at Merlin Works — and Sara Farr. [About Shana](https://www.merlin.works/about-shana/) Get Up's long-form narrative style is driven by music: Farr sits behind a computer cueing thousands of sound clips, and Merlin and McCormick build characters and scenes to match, never knowing the genre until they arrive. Notably, Merlin was McCormick's first improv instructor; their first show as a team was a 25-minute send-up of the TV series *24* at FronteraFest, performed with a boombox playing the show's ticking-clock sound, and they debuted the sound-cue form at the 2006 Out of Bounds festival. Get Up is Austin Critics Table Award-winning. [Out of Bounds Preview: Get out and see Get Up](https://austin.culturemap.com/news/arts/08-29-11-17-44-out-of-bounds-preview-improv-lesson-1-get-up) [About Shana](https://www.merlin.works/about-shana/)

Merlin has been involved in the Out of Bounds Comedy Festival since its inception, serving on its selection committee. Her performing philosophy is simple: "The goal for me as an improviser is to perform on as many stages as I can. Improv is ephemeral. After the performance is done, that performance is gone." She has called Austin "Improv Rehab" — a place experienced improvisers move to after attending the festival. [Out of Bounds Preview: Get out and see Get Up](https://austin.culturemap.com/news/arts/08-29-11-17-44-out-of-bounds-preview-improv-lesson-1-get-up)

Her other credits include *Dusk*, her *Twilight* parody, which toured nationally in 2010, and a touring stint with *The Intergalactic Nemesis*. Corporate clients have included Dell, Goodwill, The Driskill Hotel, The University of Texas, and HP, and her bio also names HomeAway, T-Mobile, and Deloitte. She has been honored by the Austin Improv Collective as "Best Improv Teacher" — both sources agree on that title. Trained with the Alan Alda Center for Communicating Science, she lectures at the Texas A&M College of Medicine and is an associate at the UT Dell Medical School/Moody College Center for Health Communication. She is the mother of two.
