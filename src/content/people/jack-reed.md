## Overview

John Francis "Jack" Reed is the Democratic senior U.S. Senator from [Rhode Island](/places/united-states/), serving in the [U.S. Senate](/organizations/us-senate/) since 1997 — a West Point graduate and Army Ranger-qualified paratrooper who moved from the 82nd Airborne Division to the top of the Senate Armed Services Committee, which he chaired from 2021 to 2025 and now leads for the minority as ranking member ([House History](https://history.house.gov/People/Detail/20232?current_search_qs=%3FTerm%3DSearch%26SearchIn%26PreviousSearch%3DSearch%252cLastName%252c%252c%252c%252c%252cFalse%252cFalse%252cFalse%252c%252cCongress%26CurrentPage%3D860%26SortOrder%3DCongress%26ResultType%3DList%26Command%3D864), [Banking Committee](https://www.banking.senate.gov/newsroom/minority/as-congress-considers-potential-market-structure-legislation-warren-and-reed-press-doj-and-treasury-on-national-security-risks-posed-by-trump-affiliated-crypto-ventures-sale-of-tokens-to-illicit-actors)). **He is not Reed Hastings, the Netflix co-founder** — a confusion this profile states plainly.

Reed's route to the Senate ran through uniform and courtroom: nominated to West Point at 17 by Rhode Island's Senator John O. Pastore, he graduated in 1971, served 12 years as an Army officer, taught at the Academy, then earned a law degree from Harvard Law School before entering Rhode Island politics ([Reed's Senate office](https://www.reed.senate.gov/news/releases/changing-of-the-guard-reed-ascends-to-top-of-senate-armed-services-committee)). Britannica characterizes him as a moderate-to-liberal Democrat whose military background gave him a special focus on defense issues ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Jack-Reed)).

## From West Point to the Senate

Born November 12, 1949, in Providence, Rhode Island, Reed reported to the United States Military Academy on July 3, 1967 — his father, Joseph A. Reed, had been a Navy machinist in World War II who later became a custodian and custodial supervisor in Cranston's public schools ([House History](https://history.house.gov/People/Detail/20232?current_search_qs=%3FTerm%3DSearch%26SearchIn%26PreviousSearch%3DSearch%252cLastName%252c%252c%252c%252c%252cFalse%252cFalse%252cFalse%252c%252cCongress%26CurrentPage%3D860%26SortOrder%3DCongress%26ResultType%3DList%26Command%3D864), [Reed's Senate office](https://www.reed.senate.gov/news/releases/changing-of-the-guard-reed-ascends-to-top-of-senate-armed-services-committee)). While an officer he earned a master's in public policy from Harvard's John F. Kennedy School of Government (1973), then served as a paratrooper in the 2nd Battalion, 504th Parachute Infantry Regiment, 82nd Airborne Division — platoon leader, company commander, and battalion staff officer — before teaching economics and international relations at West Point from 1977 to 1979 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Jack-Reed)). His own office states flatly: "Jack Reed is not a combat veteran." He left active duty as a Captain in August 1979 and the Reserves as a Major in June 1991, earning the Ranger Tab, the Army Commendation Medal with Oak Leaf Cluster, the Senior Parachutist Badge, and the Expert Infantry Badge ([Reed's Senate office](https://www.reed.senate.gov/news/releases/changing-of-the-guard-reed-ascends-to-top-of-senate-armed-services-committee)).

After Harvard Law (J.D., 1982) and D.C. bar admission in 1983, Reed practiced banking and securities law in Providence, then won a seat in the Rhode Island State Senate in 1984, serving from 1985 to 1990 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Jack-Reed)). He moved to the U.S. House of Representatives for Rhode Island's 2nd district in 1991, serving three terms, and in 1996 won the Senate seat of the retiring Claiborne Pell, taking office in 1997 as Rhode Island's 46th U.S. Senator — the eighth U.S. Senator in American history to graduate from West Point ([House History](https://history.house.gov/People/Detail/20232?current_search_qs=%3FTerm%3DSearch%26SearchIn%26PreviousSearch%3DSearch%252cLastName%252c%252c%252c%252c%252cFalse%252cFalse%252cFalse%252c%252cCongress%26CurrentPage%3D860%26SortOrder%3DCongress%26ResultType%3DList%26Command%3D864), [Reed's Senate office](https://www.reed.senate.gov/news/releases/changing-of-the-guard-reed-ascends-to-top-of-senate-armed-services-committee)).

## The Armed Services years

Reed joined the Senate Armed Services Committee in 1998 and has never really left it. He became ranking member in 2015 after Carl Levin retired, working across the aisle with chairmen John McCain and Jim Inhofe, then ascended to chairman on February 3, 2021, after S. Res. 27 put Democrats in charge — the fifth person to lead the committee that century ([Reed's Senate office](https://www.reed.senate.gov/news/releases/changing-of-the-guard-reed-ascends-to-top-of-senate-armed-services-committee)). The House's biographical record lists him as chair in both the 117th and 118th Congresses (2021–2025), after which he returned to ranking member in January 2025 ([House History](https://history.house.gov/People/Detail/20232?current_search_qs=%3FTerm%3DSearch%26SearchIn%26PreviousSearch%3DSearch%252cLastName%252c%252c%252c%252c%252cFalse%252cFalse%252cFalse%252c%252cCongress%26CurrentPage%3D860%26SortOrder%3DCongress%26ResultType%3DList%26Command%3D864)).

The committee oversees the Pentagon and produces the annual National Defense Authorization Act, and Reed has treated the job as a field assignment — 18 trips to Afghanistan, 21 to Iraq ([Reed's Senate office](https://www.reed.senate.gov/news/releases/changing-of-the-guard-reed-ascends-to-top-of-senate-armed-services-committee)). His military background did not make him a reflexive hawk: he notably opposed the 2003 Iraq War, arguing it was a diversion from the larger war on terrorism ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Jack-Reed)). His legislative record beyond defense includes helping create the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and the Office of Financial Research, sponsoring laws that increased funding for pediatric-cancer research and required that drugs and devices be proven safe before use in children, and co-sponsoring legislation to restore and expand the federal Pell Grant program ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Jack-Reed)). He has been reelected in 2002, 2008, 2014, and 2020; his current term ends January 3, 2027 ([House History](https://history.house.gov/People/Detail/20232?current_search_qs=%3FTerm%3DSearch%26SearchIn%26PreviousSearch%3DSearch%252cLastName%252c%252c%252c%252c%252cFalse%252cFalse%252cFalse%252c%252cCongress%26CurrentPage%3D860%26SortOrder%3DCongress%26ResultType%3DList%26Command%3D864)).

## A defense lens on crypto: the 2025 World Liberty Financial letter

Here is the verified story the top biographies don't cover. On November 17, 2025, Reed co-signed a letter with Senator [Elizabeth Warren](/people/elizabeth-warren/) — the two are also, respectively, the top Democrat on Armed Services and the ranking member of the Banking Committee — addressed to Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Attorney General Pamela Bondi, asking what the Treasury and Justice Departments were doing about national-security risks posed by [World Liberty Financial](/organizations/world-liberty-financial/) (WLF), a Trump-affiliated crypto venture ([Senate letter](https://banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/letter_to_bessent_and_bondi_on_wlfi_token_sales.pdf), [Banking Committee](https://www.banking.senate.gov/newsroom/minority/as-congress-considers-potential-market-structure-legislation-warren-and-reed-press-doj-and-treasury-on-national-security-risks-posed-by-trump-affiliated-crypto-ventures-sale-of-tokens-to-illicit-actors)).

The letter drew on a September 18, 2025 report by the watchdog [Accountable.US](/organizations/accountable-us/) — including its "American Sell-Out" report — to allege that WLF sold its $WLFI governance tokens to buyers tied to North Korean state-sponsored hackers (including the [Lazarus Group](/organizations/lazarus-group/)), a sanctioned Russian ruble-backed sanctions-evasion tool, an Iranian crypto exchange, and at least 62 users who also used the mixing service [Tornado Cash](/organizations/tornado-cash/) ([Senate letter](https://banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/letter_to_bessent_and_bondi_on_wlfi_token_sales.pdf)). Because $WLFI holders can "participate in governance" — a "seat at the table" in WLF's own phrasing — the senators argued the sales handed foreign adversaries potential influence over a U.S. financial platform and exposed weak sanctions and anti-money-laundering controls ([Senate letter](https://banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/letter_to_bessent_and_bondi_on_wlfi_token_sales.pdf)).

The letter also pressed a conflict-of-interest claim: DT Marks DEFI LLC, an entity affiliated with Donald J. Trump and his family, holds 22.5 billion $WLFI tokens worth more than $3 billion and claims 75% of token-sale proceeds — meaning, the senators wrote, most of every sale directly enriches the Trump family, "even for sales to entities linked to North Korea and Russia" ([Senate letter](https://banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/letter_to_bessent_and_bondi_on_wlfi_token_sales.pdf)). The letter posed five questions — on DeFi due-diligence requirements, governance-token national-security risks, open investigations, foreign-actor motives, and insulating national-security decisions from crypto interests — with answers due December 1, 2025 ([Senate letter](https://banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/letter_to_bessent_and_bondi_on_wlfi_token_sales.pdf)). An earlier May 19, 2025 Warren-Reed letter about North Korea's $1.5 billion Bybit hack had drawn a June 2025 Treasury response but none from Justice ([Senate letter](https://banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/letter_to_bessent_and_bondi_on_wlfi_token_sales.pdf)).

## FAQ

**Is Senator Jack Reed the same person as Netflix founder Reed Hastings?**
No. Jack Reed (John Francis Reed, born 1949) is the Democratic U.S. Senator from Rhode Island; Reed Hastings is the Netflix co-founder. They share only a similar public name and have no known personal or professional relationship. The FactsLedger profile of Hastings is separate and linked above.

**What is Jack Reed's military background?**
Reed graduated from West Point in 1971 and served as an active-duty Army infantry officer through 1979 — Ranger-qualified, a paratrooper in the 2nd Battalion, 504th Infantry of the 82nd Airborne Division — then taught at West Point and stayed in the Army Reserve until 1991, retiring as a Major. His own office says he is not a combat veteran.

**What did the November 2025 Warren-Reed letter ask DOJ and Treasury to do?**
Dated November 17, 2025, it asked Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Attorney General Pamela Bondi for information on any investigations of World Liberty Financial's governance-token sales to buyers linked to North Korea, Russia, and other illicit actors, citing Accountable.US's September 2025 research. It posed five questions on due diligence, sanctions controls, and conflicts, with answers requested by December 1, 2025.

**Which Senate committees has Jack Reed served on?**
He joined the Armed Services Committee in 1998, became ranking member in 2015, chaired it from February 2021 to January 2025, and resumed ranking-member status in 2025. He also sat on Appropriations' Defense subcommittee, was the most senior Democrat on Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs, and served ex officio on the Select Committee on Intelligence.
