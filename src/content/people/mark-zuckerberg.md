## Overview

Mark Elliot Zuckerberg was a known quantity in programming circles before he was known as the founder of [Facebook](/organizations/facebook/). In his final semester at Phillips Exeter Academy, he and classmate Adam D'Angelo built Synapse, a WinAmp plug-in that learned a listener's musical tastes and generated playlists to match; after Slashdot linked the free download, America Online, WinAmp, and Microsoft expressed interest in buying it, and Zuckerberg later said the offers ran "up to one million... like two million." The pair turned them down. "I don't really like putting a price-tag on the stuff I do. That's just like not the point," he told The Harvard Crimson in a June 2004 interview ([Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2004/6/10/mark-e-zuckerberg-06-the-whiz/?page=3)).

He had been programming since receiving his first computer in the sixth grade; his first significant program was a Risk-style game in which the player faced Julius Caesar, and his first formal instruction came from *C++ for Dummies*. At Harvard he built CourseMatch, which showed students who else was enrolled in their classes, and Facemash, which pitted undergraduates' photos against one another in a "hotter" vote — a site that drew 450 visitors in its first four hours before he took it down and the university's Administrative Board brought him in for breaching security and using students' photos without permission ([Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2004/6/10/mark-e-zuckerberg-06-the-whiz/?page=3)). His own summary of the method: "I don't spend my time making big things. I spend time making small things and then when the time comes I put them together" ([Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2004/6/10/mark-e-zuckerberg-06-the-whiz/?page=3)).

Born May 14, 1984, Zuckerberg launched Facebook from his Harvard dorm room on February 4, 2004, with Chris Hughes, Dustin Moskovitz, and Eduardo Saverin, and remained chief executive of [Meta](/organizations/meta/) Platforms as of September 2026 ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/mark-zuckerberg-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/meta-misled-consumers-case-over-cambridge-analytica-scandal-new-mexico-jury-says-2026-09-25/)). The milestones of that run are also its controversies: a record $5 billion federal privacy order in 2019, the 2021 corporate rebrand to Meta, a sharp 2025 turn in content moderation, and a 2026 jury verdict in New Mexico that found the company misled residents about its data practices ([Federal Trade Commission](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/07/ftc-imposes-5-billion-penalty-sweeping-new-privacy-restrictions-facebook); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/meta-misled-consumers-case-over-cambridge-analytica-scandal-new-mexico-jury-says-2026-09-25/)).

## Building Facebook

Zuckerberg attended Harvard from 2002 to 2004 and launched Facebook on February 4, 2004, from his dorm room with Chris Hughes, Dustin Moskovitz, and Eduardo Saverin ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/mark-zuckerberg-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). The service expanded to Stanford, Columbia, and Yale that March, and the company went public on May 18, 2012 ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/mark-zuckerberg-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). (CNN's own fast-facts page notes that *The Social Network* is a fictional version of these events and should not be used as history ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/mark-zuckerberg-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)).)

On October 28, 2021, Zuckerberg announced the corporate name would change from Facebook to Meta while the Facebook app kept its name; he said the new name reflected a company built around more than one product and put its metaverse plans first ([NPR](https://www.wuga.org/national-news/2021-10-28/facebook-is-changing-its-name-to-meta-mark-zuckerberg-announces)). The rebrand came against the backdrop of Cambridge Analytica fallout and Frances Haugen's scrutiny ([NPR](https://www.wuga.org/national-news/2021-10-28/facebook-is-changing-its-name-to-meta-mark-zuckerberg-announces)) — but no motive beyond Zuckerberg's stated rationale is established here.

## The yearly personal challenges

From 2009, Zuckerberg set himself one public personal challenge each year, a habit he kept going through at least 2018. In 2009 he wore a tie every day — "That first year the economy was in a deep recession and Facebook was not yet profitable," he wrote, "and I wore a tie every day as a reminder." Past challenges included learning to speak Mandarin and eating meat only from animals he killed himself; in 2017 he visited every U.S. state he had not been to yet; and for 2018 he directed the challenge at the company rather than himself, pledging to "fix Facebook" by protecting users from abuse, defending against interference by nation-states, and making sure time spent on the platform was "time well spent" ([Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/article/zuckerbergs-personal-challenge-2018-fix-facebook/)).

## Privacy and the Cambridge Analytica fallout

The 2018 reporting on Cambridge Analytica — a third-party personality quiz that harvested data from roughly 87 million profiles and passed it to the political consulting firm — forced Zuckerberg to apologize and testify before Congress on April 10–11, 2018 ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/mark-zuckerberg-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/meta-misled-consumers-case-over-cambridge-analytica-scandal-new-mexico-jury-says-2026-09-25/)). In July 2019 the FTC announced a $5 billion penalty and a 20-year order requiring an independent board privacy committee and Zuckerberg's own quarterly and annual certifications; the FTC's complaint alleged Facebook deceived users about friend-data sharing and mishandled apps and third-party developers ([Federal Trade Commission](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/07/ftc-imposes-5-billion-penalty-sweeping-new-privacy-restrictions-facebook)). The FTC announced the order as "sweeping new privacy restrictions."

The story came back to court in 2026. Following a 2021 lawsuit by the New Mexico attorney general, a Santa Fe jury found on September 25 that Meta misled residents about data sharing, hate-speech and misinformation enforcement, and preferential policy enforcement ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/meta-misled-consumers-case-over-cambridge-analytica-scandal-new-mexico-jury-says-2026-09-25/)). The jury counted 43,899,720 violations of the state's Unfair Practices Act; state attorneys asked for the maximum $5,000 per violation — about $219 billion in arithmetic — but the final penalty rests with Judge Francis Mathew, and Meta can appeal ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/meta-misled-consumers-case-over-cambridge-analytica-scandal-new-mexico-jury-says-2026-09-25/)). Meta denied selling users' information and said the allegedly misleading statements were cherry-picked snippets that ignored context in which the company acknowledged its handling was not perfect ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/meta-misled-consumers-case-over-cambridge-analytica-scandal-new-mexico-jury-says-2026-09-25/)). New Mexico was not part of a $459 million settlement other states reached with Meta over Cambridge Analytica claims, which is why its case went to trial ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/meta-misled-consumers-case-over-cambridge-analytica-scandal-new-mexico-jury-says-2026-09-25/)).

## Content moderation, elections, and Trump

On August 26, 2024, Zuckerberg wrote that senior Biden administration officials had repeatedly pressured Meta over certain COVID-19 content, including humor and satire, and said he regretted not being more outspoken and would push back in the future ([Associated Press](https://www.wvlt.tv/2024/08/27/zuckerberg-says-white-house-pressured-facebook-over-some-covid-19-content-during-pandemic/?ai)). The White House responded that it encouraged responsible public-health action while expecting companies to make independent choices ([Associated Press](https://www.wvlt.tv/2024/08/27/zuckerberg-says-white-house-pressured-facebook-over-some-covid-19-content-during-pandemic/?ai)). In the same letter he said he would not repeat the 2020 election-infrastructure contributions in 2024, wanting to stay neutral and avoid the appearance of taking sides; the AP reported the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative had supplied $400 million for local election administration in 2020 ([Associated Press](https://www.wvlt.tv/2024/08/27/zuckerberg-says-white-house-pressured-facebook-over-some-covid-19-content-during-pandemic/?ai)). That is Zuckerberg's allegation set against the White House's differing account — not a proven finding of coercion.

The relationship with [Donald Trump](https://factsledger.com/people/donald-trump/) was then rebuilt in public. After years of tension — Trump's accounts were banned after the January 6, 2021 Capitol riot, reinstated in 2023, and Trump had called Meta the "enemy of the people" in March 2024 — Zuckerberg flew to Mar-a-Lago in November 2024 for dinner with Trump ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/world-news/meta-donates-1-mn-to-trump-s-inaugural-fund-to-build-positive-relation-124121200358_1.html)). Meta donated $1 million to Trump's inaugural fund in December 2024, confirmed by a Meta spokesperson; it had made no such donation for Biden or Trump's prior term ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/world-news/meta-donates-1-mn-to-trump-s-inaugural-fund-to-build-positive-relation-124121200358_1.html)).

On January 7, 2025, Meta ended U.S. third-party fact-checking, replaced it with user-written Community Notes, and loosened restrictions on topics like immigration and gender; Zuckerberg framed it as a return to free-expression roots, while Trump said the move was "probably" due to his threats — Trump's own characterization, not an established motive ([Associated Press](https://www.akronnewsreporter.com/2025/01/14/mark-zuckerberg-republican-billionaires-trump-reception/); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/mark-zuckerberg-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). Meta's own January 7, 2025 newsroom announcement was identified during research but was not fetched in full, so its exact scope claims are not repeated here. Zuckerberg cohosted an inauguration-eve reception with Republican billionaire donors including Miriam Adelson, Tilman Fertitta, and Todd Ricketts ([Associated Press](https://www.akronnewsreporter.com/2025/01/14/mark-zuckerberg-republican-billionaires-trump-reception/)). In March 2026, Trump named Zuckerberg to the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology alongside Jensen Huang, Larry Ellison, Sergey Brin, and Lisa Su, co-chaired by David Sacks and Michael Kratsios ([internewscast](https://internewscast.com/news/us/trump-appoints-mark-zuckerberg-jensen-huang-and-larry-ellison-to-new-technology-advisory-council/)). The announcement described an initial group of 13 industry leaders — including Sergey Brin and Lisa Su alongside Huang and Ellison — with the council possibly growing to 24 members ([internewscast](https://internewscast.com/news/us/trump-appoints-mark-zuckerberg-jensen-huang-and-larry-ellison-to-new-technology-advisory-council/)).
