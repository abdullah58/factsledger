**John Logan** is the Chicago-born playwright and screenwriter who wrote the screenplay for *Michael*, the 2026 Michael Jackson biopic — a three-time Academy Award nominee for *Gladiator* (2000), *The Aviator* (2004) and *Hugo* (2011), and a Tony Award winner for his 2010 play *Red* ([IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0517589/); [Press Trust of India via Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/pti-stories/-skyfall-screenwriter-hopes-for-an-oscar-nomination-112110900232_1.html); [Playbill](https://playbill.com/article/moulin-rouges-john-logan-tapped-to-pen-screenplay-for-upcoming-michael-jackson-biopic)). Despite a filmography crowded with blockbusters, Logan has said he thinks of himself foremost as a playwright ([Playbill](https://playbill.com/article/playbill-brief-encounter-with-john-logan-the-hugo-screenwriter-and-red-playwright-com-185893)).

Before Hollywood, Logan spent roughly a decade as a Chicago playwright, writing his first plays while shelving books at the Northwestern University Law Library — a stretch he later described as a long haul rather than an overnight rise ([Playbill](https://playbill.com/article/playbill-brief-encounter-with-john-logan-the-hugo-screenwriter-and-red-playwright-com-185893)). His path runs from the Chicago stage to Oscar-nominated epics to, most recently, the most legally complicated music biopic of the decade. To be explicit about the name: this profile is about John Logan the writer, born 1961 — not the Civil War general and US senator John A. Logan (1826–1886) ([New Hampshire Historical Society](https://www.nhhistory.org/object/549683/logan-john-a-1826-1886)), and not the American poet John Logan (1923–1987) ([Academy of American Poets](https://poets.org/poet/john-logan)).

## A playwright first — the library decade

Long before the Oscar nominations, Logan was a working playwright in Chicago. He shelved books at the Northwestern University Law Library for nearly ten years while writing his first plays, *Never the Sinner* and *Hauptmann*, and has said he earned his living with his pen since he was eighteen, never holding a multi-picture deal or a studio office ([Playbill](https://playbill.com/article/playbill-brief-encounter-with-john-logan-the-hugo-screenwriter-and-red-playwright-com-185893)). He lived in Chicago for twenty-five years and still ranks an evening of live theatre above film, concerts or painting ([Playbill](https://playbill.com/article/playbill-brief-encounter-with-john-logan-the-hugo-screenwriter-and-red-playwright-com-185893)).

That stage-first identity survived his screen success. *Red*, his play about the painter Mark Rothko, premiered at the Donmar Warehouse in London and on Broadway at the Golden Theatre, winning the 2010 Tony Award for Best Play together with the Drama Desk, Drama League and Outer Critics Circle awards ([Playbill](https://playbill.com/article/moulin-rouges-john-logan-tapped-to-pen-screenplay-for-upcoming-michael-jackson-biopic); [IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0517589/)). He later wrote the book for the Broadway stage musicals *Moulin Rouge!* and Sting's *The Last Ship* ([JoBlo](https://www.joblo.com/whistler-camp-john-logan-penny-dreadful-blumhouse/amp/); [Playbill](https://playbill.com/article/moulin-rouges-john-logan-tapped-to-pen-screenplay-for-upcoming-michael-jackson-biopic)).

## Three Oscar nominations and the 2011 pile-up

Logan's breakthrough was *Gladiator* (2000), co-written with David Franzoni and William Nicholson — among his first big-budget features, and his first Oscar nomination ([Looper](https://www.looper.com/967169/writer-john-logan-on-why-gladiator-is-timeless-exclusive/)). It earned roughly $460 million worldwide — 2000's second-highest gross — and won Best Picture ([Looper](https://www.looper.com/967169/writer-john-logan-on-why-gladiator-is-timeless-exclusive/)). Then came *The Aviator* (2004) for Martin Scorsese — his second nomination — and *Hugo* (2011), for Scorsese, his third, for adapted screenplay ([Press Trust of India via Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/pti-stories/-skyfall-screenwriter-hopes-for-an-oscar-nomination-112110900232_1.html); [Playbill](https://playbill.com/article/moulin-rouges-john-logan-tapped-to-pen-screenplay-for-upcoming-michael-jackson-biopic)).

Then the oddity that defines his output: three Logan-scripted films — the animated *Rango*, Shakespeare's *Coriolanus*, and *Hugo* — all reached theaters in 2011 ([Playbill](https://playbill.com/article/playbill-brief-encounter-with-john-logan-the-hugo-screenwriter-and-red-playwright-com-185893)). Logan chalked it up to scheduling kismet: five years on *Hugo*, with *Rango* and *Coriolanus* written in the middle — only the release calendar made it look like a burst ([Playbill](https://playbill.com/article/playbill-brief-encounter-with-john-logan-the-hugo-screenwriter-and-red-playwright-com-185893)). The implication: his writing pace ran years ahead of Hollywood's production schedules. The 2010s added the Bond films *Skyfall* (2012) and *Spectre* (2015), plus *The Last Samurai* and Burton's *Sweeney Todd* ([IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0517589/); [JoBlo](https://www.joblo.com/whistler-camp-john-logan-penny-dreadful-blumhouse/amp/)).

## Penny Dreadful, a directing turn, and the Michael rewrite

Television gave Logan his most complete authorial vehicle: he created Showtime's gothic-horror series *Penny Dreadful*, which ran from 2014 to 2016, writing and executive-producing its twenty-seven episodes ([IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0517589/); [JoBlo](https://www.joblo.com/whistler-camp-john-logan-penny-dreadful-blumhouse/amp/)). He then moved behind the camera with a feature-directorial debut on a Blumhouse horror film, while his latest film, the LGBTQ-focused slasher *They/Them*, streamed on Peacock ([JoBlo](https://www.joblo.com/whistler-camp-john-logan-penny-dreadful-blumhouse/amp/); [Looper](https://www.looper.com/967169/writer-john-logan-on-why-gladiator-is-timeless-exclusive/)).

His most consequential assignment was [*Michael*](/creative-works/michael-2026-film/): retained in 2019 to pen the [Michael Jackson](/people/michael-jackson/) biopic for Graham King's GK Films — the pair had previously collaborated on *Hugo* and *Rango* — and confirmed by Lionsgate's 2022 announcement naming the "three-time Oscar nominated screenwriter" as its writer ([Playbill](https://playbill.com/article/moulin-rouges-john-logan-tapped-to-pen-screenplay-for-upcoming-michael-jackson-biopic); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/michael-jackson-biopic-set-at-lionsgate/)). Then the twist no writer plans for: in 2025, Puck reported that a large portion of Logan's script would need rewriting and reshooting after the discovery of the settlement clause barring any depiction of accuser Jordan Chandler — with Logan, director Antoine Fuqua and King seeking Lionsgate's approval for the changes ([CinemaBlend](http://cinemablend.com/movies/michael-jackson-biopic-has-hit-a-massive-setback-over-unusable-ending); [JoBlo](https://www.joblo.com/michael-jackson-biopic-reshoot-rumor/amp/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is John Logan the screenwriter the same person as the Civil War general?**
No. The screenwriter is the Chicago-born playwright listed by IMDb as born in 1961. The general was John A. Logan (1826–1886), a Union Army major general and later a US senator from Illinois, recorded by the New Hampshire Historical Society. A third John Logan — the American poet (1923–1987) — won the 1982 Lenore Marshall Poetry Prize.

**How many Academy Awards has John Logan been nominated for?**
Three, with no wins: Best Original Screenplay for *Gladiator* (2000) and *The Aviator* (2004), and Best Adapted Screenplay for *Hugo* (2011). A Press Trust of India report confirms all three nominations. On stage he did win: the 2010 Tony for Best Play for *Red*, plus the Drama Desk, Drama League and Outer Critics Circle awards.

**Did John Logan start out as a playwright?**
Yes. He wrote his first plays, *Never the Sinner* and *Hauptmann*, in Chicago while shelving books at Northwestern University's law library for nearly a decade. He has said he still thinks of himself foremost as a playwright — even after three Oscar nominations — and never took a multi-picture studio deal.

**What happened to John Logan's script for the Michael Jackson biopic?**
In 2025, after the film had wrapped, Puck reported that a large portion of Logan's script had to be rewritten and reshot when estate lawyers found a settlement clause barring any depiction of accuser Jordan Chandler. Logan, director Antoine Fuqua and producer Graham King sought Lionsgate's approval to make the script changes.
