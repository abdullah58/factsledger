## Overview

Eric Glyman is the co-founder and co-CEO of [Ramp](/organizations/ramp/), the New York fintech built around a simple inversion: instead of rewarding companies for spending more, its corporate cards and software help them spend less. He previously co-founded Paribus, a price-tracking app Capital One acquired in 2016, and he graduated from [Harvard University](/universities/harvard-university/) in 2012 with a degree in economics ([Inc](https://www.inc.com/brian-contreras/ramp-employees-quit-retention-strategy-launch-startups/91430553)). He started Ramp in 2019 with Karim Atiyeh and Gene Lee after the three left Capital One, and the company has since grown from a corporate card into a finance automation platform serving more than 50,000 customers, passing $1 billion in annualized revenue in October 2025 and reaching a $60 billion valuation in October 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/17/ramp-hits-32b-valuation-just-three-months-after-hitting-22-5b/); [Inc](https://www.inc.com/brian-contreras/ramp-employees-quit-retention-strategy-launch-startups/91430553)).

## Paribus: the first inversion

Glyman's first company applied the same spend-less logic to consumers. After graduating from Harvard in 2012, he took his first job as an analyst at the financial advisory Millstein & Co., and two years later left — with the firm's founder telling him he would be a hypocrite not to support entrepreneurship — to launch Paribus alongside Harvard classmate Karim Atiyeh ([Inc](https://www.inc.com/brian-contreras/ramp-employees-quit-retention-strategy-launch-startups/91430553)). Paribus scanned shoppers' email inboxes for purchase receipts and automatically claimed refunds when a price dropped after purchase, monitoring more than 25 retailers including Amazon, Target and Walmart and taking a 25% commission on the money it recovered ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/investing/103015/how-does-paribus-work-and-make-money.asp)). It launched publicly in 2015 at TechCrunch Disrupt New York, raised $2.2 million in seed funding from General Catalyst, [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/) and Greylock, and was acquired by Capital One in October 2016 for an undisclosed sum, its 12-person team joining the bank ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/investing/103015/how-does-paribus-work-and-make-money.asp)). Glyman stayed on at Capital One, where he led the savings automation team ([Tech:NYC](https://www.technyc.org/leaders/eric-glyman)). The lesson of Paribus — that the money was in the gap between what people paid and what they should have paid — became the seed of Ramp.

## Ramp: the spend-less corporate card

At Capital One, Glyman, Atiyeh and software engineer Gene Lee developed an interest in corporate cards, which they thought were bloated and unduly focused on rewarding spending rather than helping companies spend less, and in the summer of 2019 the three left to soft-launch Ramp ([Inc](https://www.inc.com/brian-contreras/ramp-employees-quit-retention-strategy-launch-startups/91430553)). The contrast with the incumbent model was the pitch: complex rewards programs encourage businesses to spend more and ultimately hurt their bottom line, so Ramp built a card aligned with customers' interests in saving time and money, with unlimited 1.5% cash back and machine-learning insights that flag duplicate software licences and troublesome spending patterns ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/automated-corporate-spend-management-platform-ramp-nets-115m)). The company launched in early 2020 as a corporate card with spend-management software, then expanded into bill pay, procurement, travel and business banking, and by 2025 into AI agents that file expenses, book travel, pay invoices and close books ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/automated-corporate-spend-management-platform-ramp-nets-115m); [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/ai/fintech-unicorn-ramp-500m-raise-iconiq/?ref=antoinebuteau.com)).

Ramp's valuation history is one of the fastest repricing runs in fintech. It reached unicorn status in 2021 on a $115 million round at a $1.6 billion valuation led by D1 Capital Partners and [Stripe](/organizations/stripe/), and by 2024 a $150 million Series D extension valued it at $7.65 billion ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/automated-corporate-spend-management-platform-ramp-nets-115m); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/17/ramp-hits-32b-valuation-just-three-months-after-hitting-22-5b/)). Then 2025 turned frantic: four repricings in eight months, from $13 billion in March to $32 billion in November, on the way to $1 billion in annualized revenue and more than 50,000 customers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/17/ramp-hits-32b-valuation-just-three-months-after-hitting-22-5b/)). In June 2026 the company raised $750 million at a $44 billion valuation, and in October 2026 it announced a $1.85 billion round at $60 billion, with the company saying more than 3% of all U.S. corporate card transactions now run over Ramp's wires ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/ramp-names-karim-atiyeh-co-ceo-glyman-cto-Rahul-Sengottuvelu/824229/); [Inc](https://www.inc.com/brian-contreras/ramp-employees-quit-retention-strategy-launch-startups/91430553)).

## The Ramp Mafia: a founder factory

The part of Glyman's story the funding coverage misses is what happens to the people who work for him. Ramp actively hires people who want to found companies, pitching itself as a place to hone entrepreneurial skills before leaving to start a business, and Glyman says there is "an incredible return to finding people who have strong drive, strong intelligence" even though it comes with heartburn ([Inc](https://www.inc.com/brian-contreras/ramp-employees-quit-retention-strategy-launch-startups/91430553)). Small teams get unusual ownership — engineers call their own shots, do their own hiring and handle customer outreach — and alumni describe the experience as "Y Combinator lite" ([Inc](https://www.inc.com/brian-contreras/ramp-employees-quit-retention-strategy-launch-startups/91430553)). The network has a name, the Ramp Mafia, a nearly 200-member group chat riffing on the PayPal Mafia, and it functions as a brain trust for founders, with members trading introductions to investors, customers and co-founders ([Inc](https://www.inc.com/brian-contreras/ramp-employees-quit-retention-strategy-launch-startups/91430553)).

The loop closes commercially. When former Ramp engineer Yunyu Lin left to co-found Cohere.io, Glyman and Atiyeh invested personally and Ramp became a customer; when Cohere.io drew acquisition interest, Ramp bought it, and Lin and co-founder Rahul Sengottuvelu returned as Ramp's heads of applied AI ([Inc](https://www.inc.com/brian-contreras/ramp-employees-quit-retention-strategy-launch-startups/91430553)). The same pattern played out with Venue, the procurement platform founded by former Ramp employee TK Kong, which Ramp acquired and folded into its procurement infrastructure ([Inc](https://www.inc.com/brian-contreras/ramp-employees-quit-retention-strategy-launch-startups/91430553)). In June 2026 the partnership at the top was formalised: Atiyeh, previously chief technology officer, became co-CEO alongside Glyman, and Sengottuvelu succeeded him as CTO ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/ramp-names-karim-atiyeh-co-ceo-glyman-cto-Rahul-Sengottuvelu/824229/)). Glyman's investors include [Peter Thiel](/people/peter-thiel/)'s [Founders Fund](/organizations/founders-fund/), Greylock Partners and 8VC, all with PayPal roots — a symmetry with the mafia his own company is now building ([Inc](https://www.inc.com/brian-contreras/ramp-employees-quit-retention-strategy-launch-startups/91430553)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is Eric Glyman?**
Eric Glyman is the co-founder and co-CEO of Ramp, the New York fintech whose corporate cards and finance software help companies spend less. He previously co-founded Paribus, a price-tracking app Capital One acquired in 2016, and graduated from Harvard University in 2012 with an economics degree ([Inc](https://www.inc.com/brian-contreras/ramp-employees-quit-retention-strategy-launch-startups/91430553)).

**What did Paribus do?**
Paribus scanned shoppers' email inboxes for purchase receipts and automatically claimed refunds when a price dropped after purchase, monitoring more than 25 retailers and taking a 25% commission on recovered money. Glyman and Karim Atiyeh founded it in 2014, and Capital One acquired it in October 2016 ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/investing/103015/how-does-paribus-work-and-make-money.asp)).

**How is Ramp different from [Brex](/organizations/brex/)?**
Both sell corporate cards to startups, but Ramp's pitch is the opposite of the rewards model: its card and software are designed to help companies spend less, flagging duplicate licences and wasteful spending, rather than rewarding them for spending more ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/automated-corporate-spend-management-platform-ramp-nets-115m)).

**How much is Ramp worth?**
As of October 2026, $60 billion, after a $1.85 billion funding round announced that month. The valuation climbed from $16 billion in June 2025 through $22.5 billion in July and $32 billion in November 2025, then $44 billion in June 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/17/ramp-hits-32b-valuation-just-three-months-after-hitting-22-5b/); [Inc](https://www.inc.com/brian-contreras/ramp-employees-quit-retention-strategy-launch-startups/91430553)).

**Is Eric Glyman still Ramp's CEO?**
Yes, with a shared title: in June 2026 co-founder Karim Atiyeh was elevated from chief technology officer to co-CEO alongside Glyman, formalising a partnership the two described as having run the company as a team for 12 years ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/ramp-names-karim-atiyeh-co-ceo-glyman-cto-Rahul-Sengottuvelu/824229/)).
