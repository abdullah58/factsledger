## Overview

**Ghulam Nabi Azad** is an Indian politician who spent five decades in the Congress, served as chief minister of Jammu and Kashmir (2005–2008), Union health minister from May 2009, and Leader of the Opposition in the Rajya Sabha (2014–2021), before quitting the Congress in August 2022 and founding the Democratic Progressive Azad Party ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/ghulam-nabi-azad-a-loyalist-turned-rebel/article65817377.ece/amp/); [Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/profile/ghulam-nabi-azad-kashmir-dpap-congress-exit/article69264841.ece)).

Azad's career is unusual in [India](/places/india/) for how little of it depended on winning elections in his home region: he won two [Lok Sabha](/organizations/lok-sabha/) terms from Washim in Maharashtra (1980, 1985), was nominated to the Rajya Sabha from Maharashtra in 1990 and 1996, and was described by The Hindu as a five-term Rajya Sabha member ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/profile/ghulam-nabi-azad-kashmir-dpap-congress-exit/article69264841.ece); [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/ghulam-nabi-azad-a-loyalist-turned-rebel/article65817377.ece/amp/)). He was a Union minister at 33, a chief minister, and the Congress's parliamentary manager — and then, in 2022, he walked away from the party that had made him, launched his own party in Jammu and Kashmir, and watched it collapse at the ballot box within two years ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/ghulam-nabi-azad-a-loyalist-turned-rebel/article65817377.ece/amp/); [Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/profile/ghulam-nabi-azad-kashmir-dpap-congress-exit/article69264841.ece)).

This profile is about Ghulam Nabi Azad, the Jammu and Kashmir politician born in the former princely state — not any other person of the same name.

## Five decades in the Congress

Azad rose through the Congress as a youth leader close to the Gandhi family, first coming into contact with Sanjay Gandhi in the 1970s, and he survived every Congress regime that followed — under Indira Gandhi, Rajiv Gandhi, P. V. Narasimha Rao, Sitaram Kesri, and Sonia Gandhi ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/ghulam-nabi-azad-a-loyalist-turned-rebel/article65817377.ece/amp/)). The Hindu's account stresses two assets: proximity to the Gandhis and unusual skill at internal party politics, including a reported role in propping up Rao in 1991 and later in ensuring Sonia Gandhi replaced Kesri in March 1999 ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/ghulam-nabi-azad-a-loyalist-turned-rebel/article65817377.ece/amp/)). Critics inside the party later made the same point as an accusation: Congress veteran Digvijaya Singh noted that Azad lost his deposit in Jammu and Kashmir in 1977, was then fielded from Maharashtra, and was "given five Rajya Sabha terms — that's 30 years" ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/ghulam-nabi-azad-a-loyalist-turned-rebel/article65817377.ece/amp/)). The break came after Rahul Gandhi's elevation as party vice-president in 2013 shifted the party's power matrix; Azad became part of the ginger group known as G-23, and in August 2022 he quit altogether ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/ghulam-nabi-azad-a-loyalist-turned-rebel/article65817377.ece/amp/)).

In a five-page resignation letter to Sonia Gandhi on 26 August 2022, Azad said the Congress had been "comprehensively destroyed," that Rahul Gandhi had demolished the party's consultative mechanism after becoming vice-president in January 2013, and that under the Gandhis the party had lost 39 of the 49 assembly elections held between 2014 and 2022 ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/ghulam-nabi-azad-resignation-letter-primary-membership-positions-congress/article65813518.ece/amp/)). Those election figures are Azad's own claims in his letter, not independently verified tallies. He added that before starting a Bharat Jodo Yatra, the leadership should have undertaken a "Congress jodo" exercise, and he resigned from all positions including primary membership ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/ghulam-nabi-azad-resignation-letter-primary-membership-positions-congress/article65813518.ece/amp/)). The Congress called the resignation "most unfortunate" and questioned the letter's contents and timing ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/ghulam-nabi-azad-resignation-letter-primary-membership-positions-congress/article65813518.ece/amp/)).

## Chief minister and health minister

When the United Progressive Alliance came to power in 2004, Azad was made a Cabinet minister in the Manmohan Singh government and then Jammu and Kashmir Congress president; in 2005, under a post-poll power-sharing agreement with the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP), he became chief minister ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/ghulam-nabi-azad-a-loyalist-turned-rebel/article65817377.ece/amp/)). Supporters remember the 2005–2008 tenure as a "golden period" of governance, according to Frontline — a reputation, not a measured verdict ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/profile/ghulam-nabi-azad-kashmir-dpap-congress-exit/article69264841.ece)). It ended over a land order: his government's decision to transfer 99 acres of forest land to the Shri Amarnathji Shrine Board for use during the annual Amarnath pilgrimage triggered a violent agitation, and the PDP withdrew its support ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/ghulam-nabi-azad-a-loyalist-turned-rebel/article65817377.ece/amp/)). Azad was then drafted back into Delhi politics as a minister in the Manmohan Singh government ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/ghulam-nabi-azad-a-loyalist-turned-rebel/article65817377.ece/amp/)).

As health minister from May 2009, Azad told a 2013 health summit that more than Rs 96,000 crore had been released to 35 state governments under the National Rural Health Mission, that MBBS seats had risen from 33,567 to 51,979, postgraduate seats from 13,838 to 23,931, and that 97 new medical colleges, including six new All India Institutes of Medical Sciences, had been established ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/news-ians/government-committed-to-healthcare-for-all-113081400912_1.html)). The seat expansion is the profile's citation-magnet statistic: MBBS seats grew by more than half on his watch, which — if the reported figures hold — materially widened India's doctor-training capacity ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/news-ians/government-committed-to-healthcare-for-all-113081400912_1.html)). The figures are Azad's own claims as reported by IANS, not audited totals. In January 2022 he was named for the Padma Bhushan, India's third-highest civilian award, for public affairs ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/kalyan-singh-gen-rawat-given-padma-vibhushan-azad-buddhadeb-awarded-padma-bhushan/812785/?amp)).

## Leader of the Opposition and the 2020 Deputy Chairman contest

Azad served as Leader of the Opposition in the Rajya Sabha from 2014 to 2021 ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/profile/ghulam-nabi-azad-kashmir-dpap-congress-exit/article69264841.ece)). His most documented parliamentary moment in that role came on 14 September 2020, when he moved the Opposition's motion for Manoj Jha in the Deputy Chairman election, seconded by Tiruchi Siva and Javed Ali Khan — and then, knowing the Opposition was short of numbers, did not press for a vote, allowing Harivansh Narayan Singh to be re-elected by voice vote ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/nda-candidate-harivansh-narayan-singh-re-elected-as-rajya-sabha-deputy-chairman/article32601308.ece)). The episode is the clearest available illustration of Azad's parliamentary style: field a candidate to mark the Opposition's position, then concede the arithmetic rather than force a losing division. The full contest is covered in the profile of the [2020 Deputy Chairman Rajya Sabha election](/events/2020-deputy-chairman-rajya-sabha-election/). The Hindu also credits his cross-party personal relationships for an unlikely feat — winning a Rajya Sabha election from Jammu and Kashmir in 2015 despite the Congress lacking the requisite numbers ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/ghulam-nabi-azad-a-loyalist-turned-rebel/article65817377.ece/amp/)).

## Breaking away: the DPAP and its collapse

Azad launched his new party on 26 September 2022 in Jammu, unveiling a mustard-white-dark-blue flag and promising 50 per cent of tickets for youth and women in the next assembly polls ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/a-month-after-quitting-congress-ghulam-nabi-azad-launches-new-party-names-it-as-democratic-azad-party/1143932/?amp)). He said the party would not make the restoration of Article 370 an election issue, arguing that only Parliament could restore it and that elections should be fought on roads, water supply and price rise ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/a-month-after-quitting-congress-ghulam-nabi-azad-launches-new-party-names-it-as-democratic-azad-party/1143932/?amp)). The launch filled a vacuum: after the August 2019 abrogation of Article 370, mainstream leaders had been jailed and regional parties sidelined, and nearly 70 Congress leaders from Jammu and Kashmir followed Azad out ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/profile/ghulam-nabi-azad-kashmir-dpap-congress-exit/article69264841.ece)). But the National Conference, the PDP and others branded the DPAP the BJP's "C-team," alleging a secret alliance to split Muslim votes — a tag Azad himself linked to Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](/people/narendra-modi/)'s emotional farewell tribute to him in the Rajya Sabha ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/profile/ghulam-nabi-azad-kashmir-dpap-congress-exit/article69264841.ece)).

The numbers that followed were unambiguous. In the 2024 Lok Sabha election every DPAP candidate lost their deposit and none crossed 4 per cent of the vote: Ghulam Mohd Saroori took 3.56 per cent in Udhampur, Saleem Paray 2.47 per cent in Anantnag-Rajouri, and Aamir Bhat 2.24 per cent in Srinagar ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/profile/ghulam-nabi-azad-kashmir-dpap-congress-exit/article69264841.ece)). In the Assembly election the party contested 23 of 90 seats and won none, with Azad himself largely absent from campaigning, citing poor health ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/profile/ghulam-nabi-azad-kashmir-dpap-congress-exit/article69264841.ece); [The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/india/azad-dissolves-all-units-of-dpap-after-rout-in-assembly-polls-1503420202.html)). Key leaders drifted back to the Congress, and in April 2025 Azad dissolved all DPAP units — state, province, district and block — with reconstitution promised "in due course" ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/india/azad-dissolves-all-units-of-dpap-after-rout-in-assembly-polls-1503420202.html)). Frontline's 2025 assessment is blunt: the party is inactive, most key leaders have rejoined the Congress, and Azad's return to his old party looks unlikely ([Frontline](https://frontline.thehindu.com/profile/ghulam-nabi-azad-kashmir-dpap-congress-exit/article69264841.ece)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why did Ghulam Nabi Azad leave the Congress?**
In his 26 August 2022 resignation letter to Sonia Gandhi, Azad said the party had been "comprehensively destroyed," accused Rahul Gandhi of demolishing its consultative mechanism after 2013, and said he was resigning from all posts including primary membership after nearly five decades.

**What did Azad achieve as health minister?**
Azad said that from May 2009 his ministry released over Rs 96,000 crore to 35 states under the National Rural Health Mission, raised MBBS seats from 33,567 to 51,979, and added 97 medical colleges including six new AIIMS. These are his reported claims, not audited figures.

**What happened to Azad's DPAP in the 2024 elections?**
The DPAP won nothing. Its Lok Sabha candidates all lost their deposits with under 4 per cent of the vote each, and it won none of the 23 Assembly seats it contested in Jammu and Kashmir. In April 2025 Azad dissolved all party units.

**Did Azad support restoring Article 370?**
At his party's launch he said restoration would not be an election issue for the DPAP and that only Parliament could restore it. In September 2024 he said restoration was not possible through the Jammu and Kashmir Assembly, calling rival parties' promises on the issue unrealistic.

**What was Azad's role in the 2020 Rajya Sabha Deputy Chairman election?**
As Leader of the Opposition, Azad moved the Opposition motion for Manoj Jha. The Opposition then did not press for a vote, knowing it was short of numbers, and Harivansh Narayan Singh was re-elected by voice vote. Jha himself framed the contest as a matter of consensus, not a contest between two individuals.
