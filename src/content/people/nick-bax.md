## Overview

Nick Bax is a blockchain researcher and forensic investigator best known for two public contributions: helping trace a wave of six-figure cryptocurrency heists to cracked LastPass password vaults, and debunking a watchdog claim that North Korea's Lazarus Group bought into World Liberty Financial — a false positive, he showed, produced by a joke memecoin contract called Dream Cash ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/senators-trump-linked-wlfi-national-security-threat)).

Bax publishes DPRK (North Korean) crypto research under the handle Nick Bax.eth (@bax1337), and is associated with the cryptocurrency-analysis firm Five I's — an independent hack-investigation report acknowledged "Nick Bax from Five I's" among its contributors ([Maverick Founder report](https://raw2.seadn.io/base/0xb445b5c8deadb38458b857a96cb8b74305a903cd/3fbac730c0886edefdda198f261294/053fbac730c0886edefdda198f261294.pdf)). Krebs on Security identified him in 2023 as director of analytics at Unciphered, a cryptocurrency wallet recovery company ([Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2023/09/experts-fear-crooks-are-cracking-keys-stolen-in-lastpass-breach/)). He is also a co-developer of Ump.eth, a decentralized marketplace protocol, alongside an engineer known as Barabazs ([Bitget Web3](https://web3.bitget.com/th/dapp/ump-eth-25165)), and a Convex Labs job posting describes him as leading its Blockchain Intelligence Team ([Web3.career](https://web3.career/tech-lead-blockchain-intelligence-convexlabs/50580)). Note: this profile is not about the British graphic designer of the same name (born 1970, formerly of The Designers Republic) — a different person ([Wikipedia disambiguation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Bax)).

## Tracing the LastPass-breach crypto heists

From late December 2022, Bax worked with MetaMask's [Taylor Monahan](/people/taylor-monahan/) to investigate a wave of high-dollar cryptocurrency thefts targeting security-conscious people across the crypto industry ([Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2023/09/experts-fear-crooks-are-cracking-keys-stolen-in-lastpass-breach/)). They connected more than 150 victims and over $35 million in stolen crypto to a single likely cause: thieves cracking master passwords from the vaults stolen in the 2022 LastPass breach ([Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2023/09/experts-fear-crooks-are-cracking-keys-stolen-in-lastpass-breach/)). The victims had not suffered the precursor attacks that usually precede big crypto thefts — no email or phone compromises, no SIM swaps — but had all stored their wallet seed phrases in LastPass's "Secure Notes" area before the breaches ([Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2023/09/experts-fear-crooks-are-cracking-keys-stolen-in-lastpass-breach/)). The researchers also found the attackers repeatedly funneled different victims' funds into the same destination wallets across exchanges, which let them link cases together and even identify a multi-million-dollar victim employed at Chainalysis, the blockchain-analysis firm ([Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2023/09/experts-fear-crooks-are-cracking-keys-stolen-in-lastpass-breach/)). Bax ran his own analysis over the collected theft data and reached the same conclusion Monahan had reported ([Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2023/09/experts-fear-crooks-are-cracking-keys-stolen-in-lastpass-breach/)). In March 2025, the U.S. Secret Service and FBI effectively corroborated the findings in a seizure filing tied to a $150 million heist against Ripple co-founder Chris Larsen, and Bax told Krebs the corroboration was "validating" — though he added he would rather see fewer hacks in the first place ([Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2025/03/feds-link-150m-cyberheist-to-2022-lastpass-hacks/)). LastPass, for its part, has said it has seen no definitive proof linking the thefts to its breaches ([Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2025/03/feds-link-150m-cyberheist-to-2022-lastpass-hacks/)).

## The Dream Cash false positive: how a joke memecoin broke the forensics

In September 2025, the watchdog group Accountable.US published "American Sell-Out," alleging that World Liberty Financial had sold tokens to buyers tied to sanctioned networks — including, centrally, a claim that the wallet Shryder.eth had made dozens of transactions with a Treasury-sanctioned Lazarus Group wallet ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/senators-trump-linked-wlfi-national-security-threat)). Senators Elizabeth Warren and Jack Reed cited the report in a November 17, 2025 letter asking the Attorney General and Treasury Secretary to investigate the firm ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/senators-trump-linked-wlfi-national-security-threat)). Bax, who does his own DPRK crypto research, reviewed the cited transactions and concluded they were false positives: the supposed "Lazarus-linked" activity was generated by a joke memecoin contract called Dream Cash, which automatically routes tokens from an address labeled as Lazarus Group to anyone who claims them — and the independent user behind the wallet had claimed them as a joke, not transacted with North Korea ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/senators-trump-linked-wlfi-national-security-threat)). "The worst part of this all (other than my Senator disseminating disinfo), is Shryder wasn't just falsely accused of being a DPRK hacker; it appears his big bag WLFI tokens (~$95k) got frozen as a result of this false positive," Bax wrote ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/senators-trump-linked-wlfi-national-security-threat)). The case illustrates a hazard his work highlights: smart contracts that auto-route tokens make raw address-to-address proximity a meaningless signal, and label-based attribution that skips contract-behavior analysis can falsely accuse — and financially freeze — innocent users ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/senators-trump-linked-wlfi-national-security-threat)).

## DPRK threat research and incident response

Bax is a white-hat hacker with the Security Alliance, the nonprofit collective of blockchain security specialists, and has taken part in its SEAL 911 incident-response work, including the Loopscale case ([Hedge With Crypto](https://www.hedgewithcrypto.com/news/exch-denies-helping-hackers-move-bybits-stolen-assets/); [Incrypted](https://incrypted.com/en/ef-wraps-up-eth-rangers-17-researchers-recover-5-8m-and-find-785-vulnerabilities/)). In the Ethereum Foundation's six-month ETH Rangers program, run with Secureum, The Red Guild, and the Security Alliance, Bax identified dozens of teams carrying DPRK-related risk factors, and program reporting says he independently notified more than 30 project teams that DPRK-linked workers were on their payrolls while coordinating the freezing of hundreds of thousands of dollars those operatives had already received ([Incrypted](https://incrypted.com/en/ef-wraps-up-eth-rangers-17-researchers-recover-5-8m-and-find-785-vulnerabilities/); [CoinSpeaker](https://www.coinspeaker.com/ethereum-foundation-exposed-north-korean-workers-crypto/)). He also produced a Fake VC warning video that executives credited with preventing hacks ([CoinSpeaker](https://www.coinspeaker.com/ethereum-foundation-exposed-north-korean-workers-crypto/)). Separately, after the February 2025 Bybit hack, Bax estimated that the non-KYC exchange eXch had facilitated roughly $30 million in transactions for the Lazarus Group on February 23 — eXch denied laundering for Lazarus while conceding it had inadvertently processed some stolen funds ([Hedge With Crypto](https://www.hedgewithcrypto.com/news/exch-denies-helping-hackers-move-bybits-stolen-assets/)). In March 2025 he warned that North Korean scammers were stealing millions through fake Zoom calls that trick targets into installing malware disguised as an audio-video fix ([CoinMarketCap](http://coinmarketcap.com/community/articles/67d2bf3cf2429b1134bdf4c7/)), and he separately detailed a Microsoft Teams variant in which attackers use fake podcast invites to win screen-control access and drain wallets ([MEXC News](https://www.mexc.com/fi-FI/news/hackers-use-microsoft-teams-to-hijack-crypto-wallets-what-to-know/95267)).

## FAQ

**Who is Nick Bax?**
Nick Bax is a blockchain researcher and forensic investigator who publishes DPRK crypto research as @bax1337. He was director of analytics at the wallet-recovery firm Unciphered, is a white-hat hacker with the Security Alliance, and is associated with the analysis firm Five I's ([Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2023/09/experts-fear-crooks-are-cracking-keys-stolen-in-lastpass-breach/); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/senators-trump-linked-wlfi-national-security-threat)).

**What did Bax prove about the Accountable.US Lazarus allegation?**
He showed the supposed Lazarus Group transactions were false positives from Dream Cash, a joke memecoin contract that auto-routes tokens from a Lazarus-labeled address to anyone who claims them — the user claimed them as a joke, and roughly $95,000 of his WLFI was frozen anyway ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/senators-trump-linked-wlfi-national-security-threat)).

**How did investigators connect the LastPass breach to the crypto heists?**
Bax and Taylor Monahan found victims shared no typical precursor attacks but had all stored seed phrases in LastPass "Secure Notes" before the 2022 breaches; thieves then funneled multiple victims' funds into the same destination wallets ([Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2023/09/experts-fear-crooks-are-cracking-keys-stolen-in-lastpass-breach/)). The FBI and Secret Service later corroborated this in a $150M seizure case ([Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2025/03/feds-link-150m-cyberheist-to-2022-lastpass-hacks/)).

**What is SEAL 911?**
SEAL 911 is the incident-response arm of the Security Alliance, the white-hat hacker collective to which Bax belongs, and it handles live responses to active crypto exploits. Bax has taken part in its incident-response work, including the Loopscale case, and in the Ethereum Foundation's ETH Rangers program alongside it ([Incrypted](https://incrypted.com/en/ef-wraps-up-eth-rangers-17-researchers-recover-5-8m-and-find-785-vulnerabilities/)).
