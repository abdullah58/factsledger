## Overview

Sam Sidiqi's story starts not on a mountain but on a bus in Kabul in 2003: he was on summer break from business school, volunteering at Afghanistan's central bank, when a fellow passenger recognized him by his mother — "Your mother, she was the one who drove the jeep" — decades after she had served there as a Peace Corps Volunteer ([WorldView](https://www.peacecorpsconnect.org/the-jeep-drivers-son/)). The son of an American Peace Corps volunteer and an Afghan father, Sidiqi has since become the first Afghan national reported to summit both Mount Everest and Denali, and the founder of Afghan Peaks, a charity developing skiing and mountain safety in Afghanistan ([WorldView](https://www.peacecorpsconnect.org/the-jeep-drivers-son/); [Ariana News](https://www.ariananews.af/local-mountaineer-becomes-first-afghan-to-summit-mount-everest/)).

## Family background

Katherine, Sidiqi's mother, served as a Peace Corps Volunteer in Afghanistan, where she met and married his Afghan father, Nezamuddin Sidiqi, then remained for six years after her service ended in 1975 ([WorldView](https://www.peacecorpsconnect.org/the-jeep-drivers-son/)). Sidiqi left Afghanistan when he was 11 months old and grew up between cultures, raised in the United States but continually drawn back to Afghanistan through sports, mountaineering, and community building ([WorldView](https://www.peacecorpsconnect.org/the-jeep-drivers-son/)). A Delaware County library event page describes him as a Swarthmore, Pennsylvania resident who grew up in Swarthmore, where his daughter Jasmine now attends school ([Delaware County Library System](https://delcolibraries.libcal.com/event/15248683)); an earlier Alaska report noted he had also spent part of his childhood in Anchorage, attending Huffman Elementary School for a year ([WebCenter Fairbanks](https://www.webcenterfairbanks.com/2021/05/13/afghanistan-born-mit-grad-raises-money-awareness-with-denali-climb/)).

## Business career

Sidiqi graduated from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and studied business at the Wharton School of the [University of Pennsylvania](/universities/university-of-pennsylvania/), including the Lauder Institute at Penn ([WebCenter Fairbanks](https://www.webcenterfairbanks.com/2021/05/13/afghanistan-born-mit-grad-raises-money-awareness-with-denali-climb/); [Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/nrecs-sam-sidiqi-real-estate-africa-offers-way-hedge-currency-risk/)). Before business school, he worked for two years in consulting with Bain & Co. in Boston; after Wharton he spent eight years with Agility Logistics, the Kuwait-headquartered company, working across Kuwait, the United States, Europe, and South Asia ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/nrecs-sam-sidiqi-real-estate-africa-offers-way-hedge-currency-risk/)).

He joined the National Real Estate Company (NREC) of Kuwait as chief executive officer in 2012 ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/nrecs-sam-sidiqi-real-estate-africa-offers-way-hedge-currency-risk/)). In a 2016 interview with Knowledge at Wharton, identified on the page as "Samuel Dean Sidiqi," he described NREC's recent groundbreaking on the $1 billion Reem Mall project in Abu Dhabi — projected at the time to include some 450 stores, 85 food-and-beverage outlets, and the world's largest indoor snow-play park — and the firm's strategy of focusing on North Africa, particularly Egypt, with a historic investment in Libya ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/nrecs-sam-sidiqi-real-estate-africa-offers-way-hedge-currency-risk/)). On risk management, he argued that real estate naturally hedges currency erosion and that political risk is best managed by aligning with durable institutions rather than individuals: "If you're trying to manage political risks, it's thinking through what institutions in that country are going to be around and being aligned with those, rather than … an individual" ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/nrecs-sam-sidiqi-real-estate-africa-offers-way-hedge-currency-risk/)). He also described a leadership test in Afghanistan, where after a contract dispute ended pursuit of U.S. government and NATO business, he rebuilt an operation around Afghan government contracts and moved the staff from expatriate to 100% Afghan ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/nrecs-sam-sidiqi-real-estate-africa-offers-way-hedge-currency-risk/)).

## Denali, 2021

Sidiqi planned a 2020 attempt to summit Denali, North America's tallest peak, to raise funds and awareness for Afghan Peaks, but the COVID-19 pandemic forced a postponement that gave him additional training time ([WebCenter Fairbanks](https://www.webcenterfairbanks.com/2021/05/13/afghanistan-born-mit-grad-raises-money-awareness-with-denali-climb/)). In May 2021 he said he believed he would be "the first Afghanistan-born climber ever to summit Denali" if successful, citing both personal nostalgia for Alaska and a commitment to his homeland's mountain communities ([WebCenter Fairbanks](https://www.webcenterfairbanks.com/2021/05/13/afghanistan-born-mit-grad-raises-money-awareness-with-denali-climb/)). On June 14, 2021, he reached the summit after a difficult final stretch, describing doubts near the end — "I had some doubts there at the end... After I did that I couldn't eat, I could barely eat... I just didn't think I was going to go" — and crediting his teammates' encouragement and ibuprofen with helping him persevere to the top ([Alaska's News Source](https://www.alaskasnewssource.com/2021/06/14/reaching-summit-sam-sidiqi-first-afghanistan-born-mountaineer-top-denali/)). Alaska's News Source reported him as the first Afghanistan-born mountaineer to top Denali ([Alaska's News Source](https://www.alaskasnewssource.com/2021/06/14/reaching-summit-sam-sidiqi-first-afghanistan-born-mountaineer-top-denali/)).

## Everest, 2023

On May 17, 2023, Sidiqi summited Mount Everest and was reported by Ariana News as the first Afghan to reach the summit of the world's highest peak; he messaged the outlet the next day that he had summited the previous day and hiked safely down to base camp ([Ariana News](https://www.ariananews.af/local-mountaineer-becomes-first-afghan-to-summit-mount-everest/)). The climb was again tied to Afghan Peaks, aiming to raise awareness and funds for skiing and mountaineering development in Afghanistan's remote highlands ([Tales of Adventure](https://www.thetalesofadventure.com/adventure-news/category/Afghan+Peaks+charity+for+mountain+sports+in+Afghanistan)). "By climbing Mount Everest, I aim to convey a powerful message to all Afghan boys and girls, empowering them to believe that they can accomplish anything they set their minds to," he said ([Ariana News](https://www.ariananews.af/local-mountaineer-becomes-first-afghan-to-summit-mount-everest/)). He was blunt about the motive in the WorldView profile: "If I wasn't going to be the first Afghan on Everest, I probably wouldn't have climbed it. It was a really good way to help the nonprofit" ([WorldView](https://www.peacecorpsconnect.org/the-jeep-drivers-son/)).

## Aconcagua, 2026

On February 14, 2026, Sidiqi summited Mount Aconcagua (6,962 meters) in Argentina, the highest mountain in the Americas and one of the Seven Summits, planting the Afghan Peaks flag at the top, according to an announcement distributed by Afghan Peaks Charity ([Afghan Peaks Charity](https://uk.advfn.com/stock-market/share-news/Afghan-Peaks-Founder-Samuel-Sidiqi-Summits-Aconcag/97859192)). The expedition was conducted with logistical support from Grajales Expeditions; the company's commercial director said it was supporting Sidiqi in his "mission to become the first Afghan national to stand on the summit of Aconcagua" ([Afghan Peaks Charity](https://uk.advfn.com/stock-market/share-news/Afghan-Peaks-Founder-Samuel-Sidiqi-Summits-Aconcag/97859192)). The announcement describes Afghan Peaks as a 501(c)(3) charitable organization focused on skiing, mountaineering, avalanche awareness, and outdoor education, particularly in the Bamiyan region ([Afghan Peaks Charity](https://uk.advfn.com/stock-market/share-news/Afghan-Peaks-Founder-Samuel-Sidiqi-Summits-Aconcag/97859192)).

## Afghan Peaks and mountain sports in Afghanistan

Sidiqi founded Afghan Peaks to improve mountain safety in Afghanistan through avalanche education, weather-monitoring systems, and sporting initiatives; the organization has sponsored the first woman's ascent of central Afghanistan's tallest peak and supports ski competitions in the country ([WebCenter Fairbanks](https://www.webcenterfairbanks.com/2021/05/13/afghanistan-born-mit-grad-raises-money-awareness-with-denali-climb/)). The nonprofit supports the Afghan Ski Challenge, a grassroots ski race and mountain-sports initiative hosted by the Bamyan Ski Club in central Afghanistan's Bamyan province — a network of skiers, guides, and young athletes building a ski culture in the central highlands despite decades of war and political instability ([WorldView](https://www.peacecorpsconnect.org/the-jeep-drivers-son/)). The club was the subject of *Champions of the Golden Valley* (2024), an Emmy-nominated documentary by former Peace Corps volunteer Baktash Ahadi ([WorldView](https://www.peacecorpsconnect.org/the-jeep-drivers-son/)). The idea grew out of Sidiqi's own skiing — he skied as a child and spent four years in Switzerland in the mid-2000s working for a logistics company, where the hobby became a passion he saw as a tool for community building ([WorldView](https://www.peacecorpsconnect.org/the-jeep-drivers-son/)).

Operating under Taliban governance has required caution: women's skiing, once central to the organization's work, is no longer viable under Taliban jurisdiction, and Sidiqi frames the work deliberately — "This is just skiing. We're not teaching people how to revolt" — stressing that the nonprofit does not threaten local governance or operate as a political instrument ([WorldView](https://www.peacecorpsconnect.org/the-jeep-drivers-son/)). His message from all of it, he said, is simple: "Afghanistan is still there" ([WorldView](https://www.peacecorpsconnect.org/the-jeep-drivers-son/)).

## Author

Sidiqi published the children's picture book *My Daddy Is Climbing Denali* in 2026, with all proceeds supporting Afghan Peaks ([WorldView](https://www.peacecorpsconnect.org/the-jeep-drivers-son/)). The book grew out of conversations with his daughter during his Denali preparations — some questions were simple ("How high is the mountain?"), one stopped him cold ("What happens if you don't come back?") — and its main character, modeled on Sidiqi himself, deliberately does not summit, reinforcing the book's central message that safety matters more than reaching the top ([WorldView](https://www.peacecorpsconnect.org/the-jeep-drivers-son/)). On September 8, 2025, he presented the book at the Swarthmore Public Library, bringing equipment he had used on his Denali and Everest climbs ([Delaware County Library System](https://delcolibraries.libcal.com/event/15248683)).
