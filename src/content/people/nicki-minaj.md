## Overview

Nicki Minaj — born Onika Tanya Maraj in Saint James, Trinidad and Tobago, on December 8, 1982 — is a rapper, singer and songwriter who became one of the most prolific chart performers of the 2010s and 2020s ([Fandango](https://www.fandango.com/people/nicki-minaj-457741/biography); [HipHopWired](https://hiphopwired.com/playlist/nicki-minaj-earns-historic-mark-after-pink-friday-2-goes-no-1/)). After signing with [Lil Wayne](/people/lil-wayne/)'s [Young Money Entertainment](/organizations/young-money-entertainment/) in 2009, she went on to score three Billboard 200 No. 1 albums — *Pink Friday* (2010), *Pink Friday: Roman Reloaded* (2012) and *Pink Friday 2* (2023) — and to hold the most chart-topping debuts of any female rapper, as well as the record for the most US Hot 100 entries by a solo female artist, surpassing Aretha Franklin ([HipHopWired](https://hiphopwired.com/playlist/nicki-minaj-earns-historic-mark-after-pink-friday-2-goes-no-1/); [Fandango](https://www.fandango.com/people/nicki-minaj-457741/biography)).

From late 2025, Minaj became one of the most visible celebrity supporters of President Donald Trump. She first publicly lauded him for his stance on what he called an "existential threat" to Christianity in Nigeria ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/music/2026/09/21/nicki-minaj-defends-trump/91875857007/)), then amplified that support from a United Nations podium, at a Turning Point USA convention, at White House events, and in a *Time* magazine interview in which she said she had privately aligned with Trump long before going public ([The Telegraph India](https://www.telegraphindia.com/entertainment/trinidad-born-rapper-echoes-donald-trump-on-persecution-of-christians-in-nigeria/cid/2133689); [EURweb](https://eurweb.com/nicki-minaj-trump-rose-garden-club-luncheon-white-house/); [Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/why-nicki-minaj-endorses-donald-trump-11975141)). In September 2026 she partnered with the Small Business Administration on its Million Dollar Pitch Competition, called working with the Trump administration an "honor" on *The Ingraham Angle*, and told young voters to "vote red" in the 2026 midterm elections ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/music/2026/09/21/nicki-minaj-defends-trump/91875857007/); [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/09/27/media/nicki-minaj-urges-young-americans-to-vote-red-in-2026-midterms-while-doubling-down-on-trump-support/)).

## From Trinidad to Queens: early life and mixtape years

Minaj was born in 1982 in the Saint James district of Trinidad and Tobago and was initially raised by her grandmother before moving to New York with her mother and siblings at age five ([Fandango](https://www.fandango.com/people/nicki-minaj-457741/biography)). After graduating from New York City's Fiorello H. LaGuardia High School of Music & Art, she built a following through MySpace uploads and underground hip-hop circles ([Fandango](https://www.fandango.com/people/nicki-minaj-457741/biography)). Her first Billboard hit, "I Get Crazy," arrived in 2008, and in 2009 she signed with Lil Wayne's Young Money Entertainment, becoming a fixture on the label's releases and collaborations ([Fandango](https://www.fandango.com/people/nicki-minaj-457741/biography)).

## Chart dominance: *Pink Friday* to *Pink Friday 2*

Her solo breakthrough came in 2010, when debut album *Pink Friday* entered the Billboard 200 at No. 2 and eventually reached No. 1, with seven of its songs appearing simultaneously on the US Hot 100 — a launch that established her rapid-fire delivery and alter-ego-driven style as a commercial force ([Fandango](https://www.fandango.com/people/nicki-minaj-457741/biography)). In 2011 she collected three Grammy nominations, including Best New Artist, toured with Britney Spears, and scored her first Top 10 hit with "Super Bass" ([Fandango](https://www.fandango.com/people/nicki-minaj-457741/biography)). The follow-up, *Pink Friday: Roman Reloaded*, topped the charts in 2012 ([Fandango](https://www.fandango.com/people/nicki-minaj-457741/biography); [HipHopWired](https://hiphopwired.com/playlist/nicki-minaj-earns-historic-mark-after-pink-friday-2-goes-no-1/)).

Between albums, Minaj expanded into film and television: she voiced the mammoth Steffie in *Ice Age: Continental Drift* (2012), took a judging seat on *American Idol* alongside Mariah Carey, starred in her E! reality series *Nicki Minaj: My Truth* (2012), made her live-action film debut as Cameron Diaz's assistant in *The Other Woman* (2014), and hosted the 2014 MTV Europe Music Awards ([Fandango](https://www.fandango.com/people/nicki-minaj-457741/biography)). Her third studio album, *The Pinkprint* (2014), peaked at No. 2 in the US and was preceded by the Sir Mix-a-Lot-sampling "Anaconda," while the Jessie J and Ariana Grande collaboration "Bang Bang" extended her run of hits ([Fandango](https://www.fandango.com/people/nicki-minaj-457741/biography)). She later appeared in *Barbershop: The Next Cut* (2015), and by the time she teamed with Migos on the Top 10 hit "Motorsport," she had surpassed Aretha Franklin's record for the highest number of US Hot 100 entries ever by a solo female act ([Fandango](https://www.fandango.com/people/nicki-minaj-457741/biography)). Her fourth album, the more hip-hop-oriented *Queen* (2018), became her fourth consecutive Top 2 entry on the Billboard 200 ([Fandango](https://www.fandango.com/people/nicki-minaj-457741/biography)).

*Pink Friday 2*, released on December 8, 2023 — Minaj's birthday — debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200 with 228,000 equivalent album units in the week ending December 14, the largest week for a rap album by a woman in the 2020s decade ([HipHopWired](https://hiphopwired.com/playlist/nicki-minaj-earns-historic-mark-after-pink-friday-2-goes-no-1/)). The album gave her the most No. 1s among female rappers and made her the first female rapper with No. 1 albums in two different decades; it was preceded by the Hot 100 chart-topper "Super Freaky Girl" (No. 1 in 2022), "Red Ruby Da Sleeze" and "Last Time I Saw You" ([HipHopWired](https://hiphopwired.com/playlist/nicki-minaj-earns-historic-mark-after-pink-friday-2-goes-no-1/)).

## Late-2025 political turn: from Nigeria to MAGA surrogate

Minaj first publicly lauded Trump for his stance on what he called an "existential threat" facing Christianity in Nigeria ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/music/2026/09/21/nicki-minaj-defends-trump/91875857007/)). On November 18, 2025, she spoke at a US-organized event at the United Nations alongside Ambassador Mike Waltz, where she branded the situation "the deadly threat," thanked Trump for his leadership and for urging swift action "to defend Christians in Nigeria," and said, "No group should ever be persecuted for practicing their religion" ([The Telegraph India](https://www.telegraphindia.com/entertainment/trinidad-born-rapper-echoes-donald-trump-on-persecution-of-christians-in-nigeria/cid/2133689)). She said she was "very nervous" but pledged to keep speaking "in the face of injustice," and framed the advocacy as non-partisan: "It is about uniting people" ([The Telegraph India](https://www.telegraphindia.com/entertainment/trinidad-born-rapper-echoes-donald-trump-on-persecution-of-christians-in-nigeria/cid/2133689)).

A month later she appeared at Turning Point USA's annual convention, sharing a stage with Erika Kirk. "I have the utmost respect and admiration for our president," she said. "I don't know if he even knows this, but he's given so many people hope" ([EURweb](https://eurweb.com/nicki-minaj-trump-rose-garden-club-luncheon-white-house/)). In January 2026 she appeared at the Trump Accounts Summit in Washington, D.C., where Trump brought her onstage and called her the "Queen of Rap"; she told the audience, "I am probably the president's No. 1 fan, and that's not going to change," adding that criticism "does not affect me at all. It actually motivates me to support him more" ([OK! Magazine](https://okmagazine.com/p/nicki-minaj-working-fun-donald-trump-very-grounded/); [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/music/2026/09/21/nicki-minaj-defends-trump/91875857007/)).

In a *Time* interview published May 13, 2026, Minaj explained that she had felt aligned with Trump's policies long before becoming an outspoken ally in late 2025, but had not dared to say so publicly. "It's been ingrained in everyone's brain in the music business that we are supposed to be a Democratic family. I just knew they would not like me supporting Trump," she said ([Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/why-nicki-minaj-endorses-donald-trump-11975141)). According to her account, her perspective shifted after multiple swatting incidents — false 911 calls that brought SWAT teams to her Los Angeles home. She said she asked California Governor Gavin Newsom for help through intermediaries, to no avail: "He just completely ignored me, with all the money I spent in taxes." A Newsom representative told *Entertainment Weekly* the governor "literally has nothing to do with this" ([Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/why-nicki-minaj-endorses-donald-trump-11975141)). Minaj said that after another swatting incident in April 2025, Republican congresswoman Anna Paulina Luna contacted her and connected her with a private security group and federal law enforcement officials. "I was shocked," Minaj said. "I'd never seen anyone in politics treat me that way" ([Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/why-nicki-minaj-endorses-donald-trump-11975141)).

She also told *Time* she believes many entertainers share her views but stay silent. "Many celebrities feel the way I do … but they don't say it. Sometimes you just need one brave person to get the brunt of the impact. I think I am the catalyst for that change," she said ([Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/why-nicki-minaj-endorses-donald-trump-11975141)). She said she believes Trump's claims of voter fraud — "I know that if [Trump] comes out and says it, that he's done his due diligence, and I know that he's not just making it up" — and volunteered to help him in the midterms: "I'll do whatever it is" ([Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/why-nicki-minaj-endorses-donald-trump-11975141)). On Trump's appeal, she offered a pop-culture analogy: "It's the same way Marilyn Monroe represents a vibe. Donald Trump is his own vibe." Of living openly with her views, she said: "I've never felt happier. I've never felt better" ([Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/why-nicki-minaj-endorses-donald-trump-11975141)).

## 2026: the White House, the SBA and "vote red"

On July 6, 2026, Minaj attended a [White House](https://factsledger.com/places/white-house) Rose Garden Club luncheon, where Trump paused his speech to single her out: "We also have a woman that is so respected and so hot and so great and such a great friend of, I don't say conservative, I say of common sense," he said. "She's a fantastic person and she's a woman that's respected by everybody and she's got real talent: Nicki Minaj" ([EURweb](https://eurweb.com/nicki-minaj-trump-rose-garden-club-luncheon-white-house/)). She gave fans a look inside the visit on social media, posting a photo of herself with Trump in the Oval Office captioned, "Whoa!!!! #WhiteHouseBarbie with her favorite President of all time!!!" ([EURweb](https://eurweb.com/nicki-minaj-trump-rose-garden-club-luncheon-white-house/)).

On September 18, 2026, she partnered with the Trump administration on the Small Business Administration's Million Dollar Pitch Competition — described as a Freedom 250 entrepreneurship initiative — serving as one of its celebrity judges ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/music/2026/09/21/nicki-minaj-defends-trump/91875857007/); [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/09/27/media/nicki-minaj-urges-young-americans-to-vote-red-in-2026-midterms-while-doubling-down-on-trump-support/)). The same day, appearing on *The Ingraham Angle* with Laura Ingraham, she said it was an "honor" to work with the administration and with Trump. "He has inspired many people, not just me. And he's — he's fun," she said, calling him smart, relatable and "very grounded": "It's easy to feel like he heard you, he understands you. And I just — I just love him. And I'm not going to ever make excuses for that." Asked what she had to say to critics of her Trump support, she answered: "Absolutely nothing" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/music/2026/09/21/nicki-minaj-defends-trump/91875857007/); [OK! Magazine](https://okmagazine.com/p/nicki-minaj-working-fun-donald-trump-very-grounded/)).

On September 26, 2026, she appeared on Fox News' *My View with Lara Trump*, where she urged young Americans to take part in the November 3 midterm elections and beyond. "Now is not the time to think that your voice will not be heard," she said. "Your voice will not only be heard, but your voice is very necessary right now for the country and for the world."
