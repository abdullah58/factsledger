## Overview

Modi Wiczyk is an [American](/places/united-states/) media executive who co-founded [MRC](/organizations/mrc/) (Media Rights Capital) with [Asif Satchu](/people/asif-satchu/) and, as its co-chief executive, built the film and television studio behind *House of Cards*, *Ozark*, *Ted* and *Knives Out* ([Harvard Club of Southern California](https://hcsc.clubs.harvard.edu/article.html?aid=998); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/mrc-eldridge-todd-boehly-pmrc-reorg/)). From 2018 to 2022 he co-led Valence Media, which put *Billboard*, *[The Hollywood Reporter](/organizations/the-hollywood-reporter/)* and [Dick Clark Productions](/organizations/dick-clark-productions/) under one roof, and in March 2023 he became MRC's co-chairman ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/313954/thr-billboard-owner-consolidates-properties-i.html); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/mrc-promotes-veteran-exec-scott-tenley-to-ceo/)).

Wiczyk graduated from Harvard College in 1993 and from Harvard Business School in 1999 ([Harvard Club of Southern California](https://hcsc.clubs.harvard.edu/article.html?aid=998)). Before MRC he was a partner at the Endeavor Agency, specialising in film and television finance, international sales and corporate advisory work, where he raised more than $900 million in production financing, and before that he headed production at Summit Entertainment ([Harvard Club of Southern California](https://hcsc.clubs.harvard.edu/article.html?aid=998)). He then launched MRC with Satchu — but the year they founded it is disputed: Screen Daily dates the launch to 2003, TheWrap and Harvard's alumni bio say 2006, and Wikipedia gives 2004, so this profile states no single founding year ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/the-talent-packagers/5016606.article); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/guggenheim-capital-invests-200-million-house-cards-producer-media-rights-capital/)).

The studio Wiczyk built worked at unusual scale for an independent: MRC developed, produced and financed television series for HBO, Starz, Netflix, AMC, ABC, Lifetime and Comedy Central, and film projects for Sony, Warner Bros., Universal, Paramount and Fox ([Harvard Club of Southern California](https://hcsc.clubs.harvard.edu/article.html?aid=998)). By August 2022 its productions had earned 111 Emmy nominations, 29 Golden Globes and 12 Academy Awards, and its films had grossed more than $6 billion worldwide ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/mrc-eldridge-todd-boehly-pmrc-reorg/)).

## The talent-deal studio

Wiczyk built MRC as a financier-producer that packaged talent-driven projects and kept ownership stakes in them, rather than as a traditional studio that simply distributed other people's films ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/guggenheim-capital-invests-200-million-house-cards-producer-media-rights-capital/)). The early proof was *Babel*, which he packaged around its key talent: it opened through Paramount Vantage in 2006 and grossed $114 million worldwide ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/the-talent-packagers/5016606.article)). MRC preferred profit participation to large upfront deals — "our deals are fair to everyone, which is why we keep on making them," Wiczyk told Screen Daily — and after the financial crisis its slate shifted from a 50-50 mix of specialty and commercial projects to almost entirely commercial ones ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/the-talent-packagers/5016606.article)). In 2014 a Guggenheim-led investor group put $240 million into MRC for a minority stake, valuing the studio at more than $1 billion according to one source in the report — though another source called that figure "a little high" — which meant Wiczyk and Satchu kept control while gaining the capital to keep scaling ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/guggenheim-capital-invests-200-million-house-cards-producer-media-rights-capital/)).

## Valence Media, Billboard and the PMRC venture

On 1 February 2018, Eldridge Industries — the holding company of former Guggenheim Partners president Todd Boehly — folded its media assets into a new company, Valence Media, combining MRC with the *Hollywood Reporter*–*Billboard* group and Dick Clark Productions, the producer of the Golden Globe Awards ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/313954/thr-billboard-owner-consolidates-properties-i.html)). Wiczyk and Satchu led Valence as co-CEOs, with Boehly as chairman and each division continuing to operate under its own leadership ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/313954/thr-billboard-owner-consolidates-properties-i.html)). That October, MRC formed Civic Center Media, a joint venture with United Talent Agency to back projects involving the agency's members ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/mrc-promotes-veteran-exec-scott-tenley-to-ceo/)). In 2020 Valence rebranded as MRC, and on 23 September [Penske Media Corporation](/organizations/penske-media-corporation/) chief [Jay Penske](/people/jay-penske/) and MRC co-CEOs Satchu and Wiczyk announced PMRC, a joint venture putting PMC's [Variety](/organizations/variety/), *[Rolling Stone](/organizations/rolling-stone/)* and [Music Business Worldwide](/organizations/music-business-worldwide/) together with MRC's *Billboard*, *The Hollywood Reporter* and *[Vibe](/organizations/vibe/)*, with a second venture for MRC to develop film, television and live-event content from the brands' stories ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/variety-parent-penske-media-to-take-over-hollywood-reporter-billboard-in-joint-venture-with-mrc/)).

## The 2022 split and the chairmanship

The Valence experiment ended on 5 August 2022, when Eldridge and MRC completed the separation of their combined media assets ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220805005474/en/Eldridge-and-MRC-Announce-Separation-of-Media-Assets)). MRC, still led by founders and co-CEOs Wiczyk and Satchu, kept its film, television and non-fiction divisions plus its stakes in Civic Center Media and T-Street, while Eldridge kept Dick Clark Productions, its investments in A24, Fulwell 73, Sugar 23 and Luminate, and the Penske Media Corporation brands — and remained a significant minority shareholder in MRC ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/mrc-eldridge-todd-boehly-pmrc-reorg/)). The two founders said their film, television and non-fiction slates had quadrupled during the combination, and that the live-events group had "changed the model for awards shows, bringing them into the streaming age" ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220805005474/en/Eldridge-and-MRC-Announce-Separation-of-Media-Assets)). Seven months later, on 2 March 2023, veteran executive Scott Tenley was promoted to chief executive and Wiczyk and Satchu became chairmen, with Tenley overseeing the studio's divisions, partnerships and shared services ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/mrc-promotes-veteran-exec-scott-tenley-to-ceo/)). Tenley had been at MRC since 2007 and had negotiated the separation seven months earlier ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/mrc-promotes-veteran-exec-scott-tenley-to-ceo/)).

## Frequently asked questions

### Who is Modi Wiczyk?

Modi Wiczyk is an American media executive and the co-founder of MRC (Media Rights Capital), the independent film and television studio behind *House of Cards*, *Ozark*, *Ted* and *Knives Out* ([Harvard Club of Southern California](https://hcsc.clubs.harvard.edu/article.html?aid=998)). He served as MRC's co-chief executive with Asif Satchu for most of its history and became its co-chairman in March 2023 ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/mrc-promotes-veteran-exec-scott-tenley-to-ceo/)).

### What is MRC?

MRC, originally Media Rights Capital, is an American independent film and television studio that develops, finances and produces films and series in partnership with leading creative talent ([Harvard Club of Southern California](https://hcsc.clubs.harvard.edu/article.html?aid=998)). By August 2022 its productions had earned 111 Emmy nominations, 29 Golden Globes and 12 Academy Awards, and its films had grossed more than $6 billion worldwide ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/mrc-eldridge-todd-boehly-pmrc-reorg/)).

### What was Valence Media?

Valence Media was the company formed on 1 February 2018 when Todd Boehly's Eldridge Industries combined MRC with the *Hollywood Reporter*–*Billboard* media group and Dick Clark Productions ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/313954/thr-billboard-owner-consolidates-properties-i.html)). Wiczyk and Satchu ran it as co-CEOs until it rebranded as MRC in 2020 and the combination was unwound in the August 2022 separation ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/variety-parent-penske-media-to-take-over-hollywood-reporter-billboard-in-joint-venture-with-mrc/)).

### How is Modi Wiczyk connected to Billboard?

Through MRC: from 2018 to 2020, the Valence Media group he co-led operated *Billboard* alongside *The Hollywood Reporter* ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/313954/thr-billboard-owner-consolidates-properties-i.html)). In September 2020 he co-announced PMRC, the joint venture under which Penske Media took over day-to-day operations of *Billboard*, *The Hollywood Reporter* and *Vibe* ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)).
