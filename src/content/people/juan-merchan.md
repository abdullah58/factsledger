## Overview

On January 10, 2025, Juan Merchan handed down the most discussed non-punishment in American criminal law: an unconditional discharge for a president-elect convicted of 34 felonies. Merchan could have sent Donald Trump to prison for up to four years. He chose a sentence — no prison time, no fine, no probation — that the Associated Press noted is rare for felony convictions, and that ended the case while locking the jury's verdict into the record ([AP](https://japannews.yomiuri.co.jp/news-services/ap/20250111-232455/)).

His reasoning came in a single sentence directed at Trump's claims of presidential immunity: "Despite the extraordinary breadth of those protections, one power they do not provide is the power to erase a jury verdict" ([AP](https://japannews.yomiuri.co.jp/news-services/ap/20250111-232455/)). Then he wished the incoming president godspeed in his second term in office. Trump, appearing by virtual feed from Mar-a-Lago, called the proceeding a "despicable charade" and promised an appeal ([AP](https://japannews.yomiuri.co.jp/news-services/ap/20250111-232455/)).

A year and a half later, Merchan's name was in the headlines again — this time over his family. In August 2026, the New York Times reported that federal prosecutors in Chicago were investigating Authentic Campaigns, the Democratic fundraising firm where his daughter Loren Merchan had been a vice president until late 2024 ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/who-is-judge-juan-merchan-trumps-hush-money-judges-daughters-firm-under-federal-investigation-101786812629856.html)).

## The sentencing arc

Merchan delayed the sentencing more than once — it had originally been expected well before the 2024 election — and by the time he imposed it, the Supreme Court had refused 5-4 to halt the proceeding ([AP](https://japannews.yomiuri.co.jp/news-services/ap/20250111-232455/)). Prosecutors, for their part, supported the no-penalty outcome while chiding Trump's attacks on the legal system throughout the case; prosecutor Joshua Steinglass told the court that "the verdict in this case was unanimous and decisive, and it must be respected" ([AP](https://japannews.yomiuri.co.jp/news-services/ap/20250111-232455/)). Trump argued to the court that voters had seen the case "firsthand" and elected him anyway.

The half-hour proceeding had an unusual staging. Trump did not come to Manhattan; Merchan said that requiring an in-person appearance from a president-elect ten days before the inauguration would have disrupted the transition, so Trump joined by live audio and video from Florida ([AP](https://japannews.yomiuri.co.jp/news-services/ap/20250111-232455/)). Beside him at Mar-a-Lago sat his defense lawyer, [Todd Blanche](/people/todd-blanche/), with partner Emil Bove in the New York courtroom — both of whom Trump would tap for high-ranking Justice Department posts ([AP](https://japannews.yomiuri.co.jp/news-services/ap/20250111-232455/)).

The result satisfied nobody's narrative completely. It left the felony conviction intact on the record — making Trump the first person to take office as president with a felony conviction — while imposing nothing on him at all.

## Holding the line in the courtroom

The sentencing was the end of a case Merchan had policed with unusual firmness. The most replayed moment came during jury selection, when Trump sat muttering and gesturing toward a prospective juror being questioned about twelve feet away. After she left the room, Merchan raised his voice: "I won't tolerate that. I will not have any jurors intimidated in this courtroom. I want to make that crystal clear" ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/meet-juan-merchan-judge-presiding-over-trumps-criminal-case-2023-04-04/)). The juror was not chosen.

The gag order came in late March 2024, after Merchan set a trial date. He restricted Trump's public statements about witnesses, court staff, and individual prosecutors, saying some of Trump's statements had been threatening or inflammatory ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/meet-juan-merchan-judge-presiding-over-trumps-criminal-case-2023-04-04/)). When Trump disparaged the judge's daughter online, Merchan expanded the order to cover his own relatives and those of Manhattan District Attorney [Alvin Bragg](/people/alvin-bragg/)'s office ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/meet-juan-merchan-judge-presiding-over-trumps-criminal-case-2023-04-04/)). Trump violated the order repeatedly — the Hindustan Times, citing the Independent, reports that Merchan held him in criminal contempt for the violations ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/who-is-judge-juan-merchan-trumps-hush-money-judges-daughters-firm-under-federal-investigation-101786812629856.html)).

The defense twice asked Merchan to step aside, pointing at his daughter's work for a political consulting firm with Democratic clients. He denied both requests ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/meet-juan-merchan-judge-presiding-over-trumps-criminal-case-2023-04-04/)). Trump's public attacks on the judge had begun around the April 2023 arraignment, when he posted on Truth Social calling Merchan biased and saying he "must be changed" ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/who-is-judge-juan-merchan-trumps-hush-money-judges-daughters-firm-under-federal-investigation-101786812629856.html)).

## A career inside the New York system

By the time the hush-money case landed with him, Merchan was a veteran of New York's courts. He started as a prosecutor in the Manhattan district attorney's office and then served as an assistant attorney general in Nassau and Suffolk counties ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/who-is-judge-juan-merchan-trumps-hush-money-judges-daughters-firm-under-federal-investigation-101786812629856.html)). He was appointed a Family Court judge in 2006, moved to the Court of Claims, and in 2009 began serving as an acting justice of the New York County Supreme Court's criminal term, where he has remained since ([NPR](https://www.ksut.org/2023-04-02/what-to-know-about-juan-merchan-the-judge-overseeing-trumps-criminal-case?_amp=true); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/meet-juan-merchan-judge-presiding-over-trumps-criminal-case-2023-04-04/)).

The Trump world was not new to him. In 2022 he presided over the Trump Organization's tax-fraud trial, which ended with the company's conviction and a $1.61 million fine; former chief financial officer Allen Weisselberg served five months ([NPR](https://www.ksut.org/2023-04-02/what-to-know-about-juan-merchan-the-judge-overseeing-trumps-criminal-case?_amp=true)). He also oversaw the fraud and money-laundering case against former Trump aide Steve Bannon, tied to a charity that promised to help build a wall along the U.S.-Mexico border ([NPR](https://www.ksut.org/2023-04-02/what-to-know-about-juan-merchan-the-judge-overseeing-trumps-criminal-case?_amp=true)).

Lawyers who know his courtroom describe a judge who runs it tightly without theatrics. "He's a serious jurist, smart and even tempered," Manhattan defense attorney Ron Kuby told NBC News. "He's not one of those judges who yells at lawyers, and is characterized as a no-nonsense judge. But he's always in control of the courtroom" ([NPR](https://www.ksut.org/2023-04-02/what-to-know-about-juan-merchan-the-judge-overseeing-trumps-criminal-case?_amp=true)). Karen Friedman Agnifilo, who supervised cases Merchan heard while at the Manhattan DA's office, told CNN that he "doesn't let the prosecutors or the defendants create any issues in his courtroom" and that attacking him publicly was unlikely to help Trump — "He's not vindictive in any way like that" ([NPR](https://www.ksut.org/2023-04-02/what-to-know-about-juan-merchan-the-judge-overseeing-trumps-criminal-case?_amp=true)).

He emigrated from Colombia to the United States at age six and grew up in Queens ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/meet-juan-merchan-judge-presiding-over-trumps-criminal-case-2023-04-04/)). He graduated from Baruch College in 1990 and earned his law degree from [Hofstra University](/organizations/hofstra-university/) in 1994 ([NPR](https://www.ksut.org/2023-04-02/what-to-know-about-juan-merchan-the-judge-overseeing-trumps-criminal-case?_amp=true)).

## The 2026 investigation into his daughter's firm

The newest chapter concerns Authentic Campaigns, the Democratic fundraising firm that Loren Merchan once co-owned. According to the Times' reporting, the U.S. attorney's office in Chicago subpoenaed the firm in 2025 for internal communications with major Democratic clients — including Kamala Harris, Joe Biden, New York governor Kathy Hochul, House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries, and the Democratic National Committee ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/who-is-judge-juan-merchan-trumps-hush-money-judges-daughters-firm-under-federal-investigation-101786812629856.html)).

Loren Merchan worked as a vice president at the firm until late 2024 and was named in the investigation. The firm challenged the subpoena with a legal team that included former Manhattan U.S. attorney Preet Bharara; after negotiations, prosecutors narrowed their request to a $468 payment from Harris's presidential campaign and to emails exchanged between Loren Merchan and her father ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/who-is-judge-juan-merchan-trumps-hush-money-judges-daughters-firm-under-federal-investigation-101786812629856.html)). The Times reported that prosecutors had not issued any additional subpoenas to Loren Merchan, the firm's founder Michael Nellis, or others, and that the investigation's current status was unclear ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/who-is-judge-juan-merchan-trumps-hush-money-judges-daughters-firm-under-federal-investigation-101786812629856.html)).

## What this profile does not claim

It does not give Merchan's exact birth year or birthplace — commonly repeated as 1962 and Barranquilla — because those details appear in Wikipedia and mirror pages rather than the sources read for this profile. It does not characterize the Authentic Campaigns investigation beyond what the New York Times reported in August 2026; no charges have been announced and the investigation's status is unclear. It does not claim Merchan faced any formal ethics sanction: the recusal motions against him were denied, and he remained on the case through sentencing. Finally, it does not say the unconditional discharge was imposed because the charges were found wanting — Merchan's stated reason was the constitutional tension between presidential protections and the jury's verdict, and the conviction stands on the record.
