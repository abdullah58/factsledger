## Overview

Kaitlan Collins is a CNN anchor and the network's chief [White House](https://factsledger.com/organizations/the-white-house) correspondent, holding both jobs at once — a dual role CNN described as unprecedented when it was announced in November 2024 ([Editor & Publisher](http://EditorandPublisher.com/stories/cnn-announces-broad-new-role-for-anchor-kaitlan-collins,253162)). She anchors the weeknight 9 p.m. ET program *The Source with Kaitlan Collins* while reporting from the White House on Donald Trump's second administration across all of CNN's platforms ([Editor & Publisher](http://EditorAndPublisher.com/stories/cnn-announces-2025-white-house-and-capitol-hill-teams,253833)). Her direct Trump relation is adversarial and long-running: across nearly a decade of White House coverage, Trump has repeatedly singled her out by name — barring her from a 2018 event, calling her a "nasty person" on live television in 2023, and, in September 2026, revoking her network's White House credentials and then confronting her face-to-face at the United Nations ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/22/trump-cnn-msnow-politico-credential-fight/91890774007/)).

## From Prattville to the White House beat

Collins was born in 1992 in Prattville, Alabama, a suburb of Montgomery, and was raised there by politically disengaged parents — she has said she does not recall them voting or expressing strong opinions about candidates ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/who-is-kaitlan-collins-the-cnn-anchor-who-has-been-rattling-donald-trump-and-his-administration/printarticle/120019865.cms)). (Lower-tier biographical outlets consistently give her birth date as April 7, 1992; see openItems.) She graduated from Prattville High School and then the University of Alabama, where she switched from chemistry to journalism and earned a bachelor's degree in political science and journalism in 2014 ([Editor & Publisher](http://EditorandPublisher.com/stories/cnn-announces-broad-new-role-for-anchor-kaitlan-collins,253162); [The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/who-is-kaitlan-collins-the-cnn-anchor-who-has-been-rattling-donald-trump-and-his-administration/printarticle/120019865.cms)).

Her first journalism job was at *The Daily Caller*, the conservative website founded by Tucker Carlson, where she started in Washington and rose to cover the White House during the 2016 campaign and the opening months of Trump's first term ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/who-is-kaitlan-collins-the-cnn-anchor-who-has-been-rattling-donald-trump-and-his-administration/printarticle/120019865.cms)). In 2017 she crossed to CNN as a White House correspondent — the beat that made her a national figure ([Editor & Publisher](http://EditorandPublisher.com/stories/cnn-announces-broad-new-role-for-anchor-kaitlan-collins,253162)).

## The 2018 ban and the rise to chief correspondent

Collins's adversarial dynamic with Trump dates to his first term. On July 25, 2018, she was serving as the television pool reporter — representing five networks — during an [Oval Office](https://factsledger.com/places/oval-office) photo opportunity with Trump and European Commission president Jean-Claude Juncker. As the event ended, she fired off questions about a secret audio recording of Trump and his former lawyer Michael Cohen discussing a potential payment to a former Playboy model, and about why Vladimir Putin was not accepting Trump's invitation to Washington ([NPR](https://www.wcbe.org/2018-07-26/cnn-reporter-white-house-called-my-questions-inappropriate-banned-me-from-event?_amp=true)). Trump ignored all four questions.

Shortly afterward, Collins was summoned to the office of deputy chief of staff for communications Bill Shine, where she and press secretary [Sarah Huckabee Sanders](/people/sarah-huckabee-sanders/) told her she was disinvited from that afternoon's open press event in the Rose Garden because her questions had been "inappropriate" and "inappropriate for that venue" ([NPR](https://www.wcbe.org/2018-07-26/cnn-reporter-white-house-called-my-questions-inappropriate-banned-me-from-event?_amp=true)). The White House confirmed the disinvitation but offered a different rationale — that Collins had shouted and refused to leave the Oval Office when asked — a version other journalists in the room disputed ([NPR](https://www.wcbe.org/2018-07-26/cnn-reporter-white-house-called-my-questions-inappropriate-banned-me-from-event?_amp=true)). The White House Correspondents' Association condemned the move as "wholly inappropriate, wrong-headed, and weak," and even Fox News issued a statement of solidarity with CNN's right to full press access ([NPR](https://www.wcbe.org/2018-07-26/cnn-reporter-white-house-called-my-questions-inappropriate-banned-me-from-event?_amp=true)).

Collins covered the 2020 election for CNN and, in 2021, was elevated to chief White House correspondent at age 28 — the youngest reporter ever to hold the network's top White House post ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/who-is-kaitlan-collins-the-cnn-anchor-who-has-been-rattling-donald-trump-and-his-administration/printarticle/120019865.cms)). In 2022 she moved to New York to co-anchor *CNN This Morning* alongside Don Lemon and Poppy Harlow, her first full-time anchoring job ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/who-is-kaitlan-collins-the-cnn-anchor-who-has-been-rattling-donald-trump-and-his-administration/printarticle/120019865.cms)).

## The town hall and primetime

On May 10, 2023, Collins moderated CNN's live town hall with Trump in New Hampshire — his first town hall-style event of the 2024 presidential campaign — before an audience of Republican and unaffiliated voters ([Associated Press](https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/news/article/2023/05/11/trump-digs-election-lies-cnn)). Over 70 minutes, she repeatedly fact-checked his false claims about the 2020 election as he steamrolled ahead; at one point, after she pressed him on classified documents by noting that Joe Biden had not defied a subpoena, Trump snapped, "You are a nasty person" ([Associated Press](https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/news/article/2023/05/11/trump-digs-election-lies-cnn); [The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/who-is-kaitlan-collins-the-cnn-anchor-who-has-been-rattling-donald-trump-and-his-administration/printarticle/120019865.cms)).

Weeks later, CNN handed her its 9 p.m. ET hour. *The Source with Kaitlan Collins* premiered in July 2023, airing weeknights between *Anderson Cooper 360°* and *CNN NewsNight* ([Editor & Publisher](http://EditorandPublisher.com/stories/cnn-announces-broad-new-role-for-anchor-kaitlan-collins,253162)). Since launching the show, she has conducted headline interviews with Hillary Clinton, Vice President-elect JD Vance, former Attorney General William Barr, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, and British Prime Minister Rishi Sunak, among others ([Editor & Publisher](http://EditorandPublisher.com/stories/cnn-announces-broad-new-role-for-anchor-kaitlan-collins,253162)). Her industry recognition includes the TIME100 Next list (2024), *Forbes* 30 Under 30 in media (2019), and *Variety*'s New Power of New York (2023) ([Editor & Publisher](http://EditorandPublisher.com/stories/cnn-announces-broad-new-role-for-anchor-kaitlan-collins,253162)).

In November 2024, CNN announced the expanded dual role: Collins would return as chief White House correspondent for Trump's second administration while continuing to anchor *The Source* each weeknight — "the perfect person to lead coverage of the new Trump White House," in the words of CNN chairman and CEO Mark Thompson ([Editor & Publisher](http://EditorandPublisher.com/stories/cnn-announces-broad-new-role-for-anchor-kaitlan-collins,253162)). The January 2025 team announcement confirmed the arrangement: she would predominantly anchor from Washington and travel on assignment with the president for domestic and international events ([Editor & Publisher](http://EditorAndPublisher.com/stories/cnn-announces-2025-white-house-and-capitol-hill-teams,253833)).

## A running feud: 2025–2026

Trump's personal attacks on Collins escalated through the second term. In December 2025, he called her "stupid and nasty" in a Truth Social post about construction on the White House ballroom ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/life/health-wellness/2026/09/24/trump-media-ban-kaitlan-collins-moment/91919143007/)). On February 3, 2026, after Collins pressed him in the Oval Office about the Justice Department's heavily redacted Jeffrey Epstein files and what he would say to survivors who felt they had not received justice, Trump refused to engage with the substance and attacked her instead: "You are the worst reporter," he said, adding, "I don't think I've ever seen you smile. I've known you for 10 years" — and claiming she was not smiling because "you know you're not telling the truth" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/life/health-wellness/2026/09/24/trump-media-ban-kaitlan-collins-moment/91919143007/)). In June 2026, during another Oval Office exchange, he accused her of having "hatred in her eyes," calling CNN "a very corrupt organization, with a corrupt reporter standing right there" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/life/health-wellness/2026/09/24/trump-media-ban-kaitlan-collins-moment/91919143007/)). CNN defended her in a statement as "an exceptional journalist, reporting every day from the White House and the field with real depth and tenacity" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/life/health-wellness/2026/09/24/trump-media-ban-kaitlan-collins-moment/91919143007/)).

## September 2026: the credential ban and the court fight

The feud became institutional on September 18, 2026, when Trump announced on social media that CNN, MS NOW and Politico would be stripped of White House access, saying the outlets "shouldn't be able to constantly write or report FICTION and LIES when they're covering the President of the United States" and framing their reporting as a threat to national security ([Reuters](https://wncy.com/2026/09/24/politico-denied-access-to-white-house-after-judges-order-news-outlet-says/)). Reporters from the three organizations who arrived at the White House on September 19 had their credentials seized ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/22/trump-cnn-msnow-politico-credential-fight/91890774007/)). On September 21, the three outlets sued in federal court in Washington, arguing the ban violated the First Amendment and their due-process rights ([Reuters](https://wncy.com/2026/09/24/politico-denied-access-to-white-house-after-judges-order-news-outlet-says/)).

The dispute immediately disrupted presidential coverage. CNN had been scheduled to operate the television pool for Trump's September 22 trip to the United Nations General Assembly; after the White House blocked the network, ABC, CBS, NBC and Fox News refused to supply a replacement crew, suspending pool coverage in solidarity ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/22/trump-cnn-msnow-politico-credential-fight/91890774007/); [Reuters](https://wncy.com/2026/09/24/politico-denied-access-to-white-house-after-judges-order-news-outlet-says/)). Collins traveled to New York anyway on credentials issued by the United Nations, which the White House ban did not cover. As Trump entered the UN building, she asked him when the war with Iran would end. He responded by taunting her: "You should not be here covering me. You said you weren't going to cover me. You shouldn't be covering me" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/22/trump-cnn-msnow-politico-credential-fight/91890774007/)). Collins replied on air that CNN had never said it would stop covering the president and would "continue to cover the president in his speech, and as he meets with world leaders" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/life/health-wellness/2026/09/24/trump-media-ban-kaitlan-collins-moment/91919143007/)).

At the General Assembly, Trump told reporters he had revoked the credentials because the outlets were "dangerous," alleging they had made "false" and "malicious" statements about the war with Iran and claimed to have sources when they didn't ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/22/trump-cnn-msnow-politico-credential-fight/91890774007/)).

The courts moved fast. After an emergency hearing on September 23, U.S. District Judge [Timothy Kelly](/people/timothy-kelly/) — a Trump appointee from 2017 — issued a 14-day temporary restraining order in the middle of the night, finding that the credential revocations "likely violated their constitutional due process rights" and expressing skepticism that national security was the real motivation, since Trump himself had pointed to the outlets' alleged dishonesty and negativity when announcing the ban ([Reuters](https://wncy.com/2026/09/24/politico-denied-access-to-white-house-after-judges-order-news-outlet-says/); [The Hill](https://cw7az.com/news/judge-orders-white-house-to-immediately-restore-access-to-cnn-ms-now-politico/)). Kelly noted the outlets had received no notice or chance to respond before losing access, and grounded the ruling in a 1977 D.C. Circuit precedent — the same basis as his 2018 ruling restoring CNN correspondent Jim Acosta's credentials ([The Hill](https://cw7az.com/news/judge-orders-white-house-to-immediately-restore-access-to-cnn-ms-now-politico/)). Before the ruling, Trump had predicted he would lose and vowed to appeal, calling Kelly "not a particularly fair judge" ([The Hill](https://cw7az.com/news/judge-orders-white-house-to-immediately-restore-access-to-cnn-ms-now-politico/)).

The return was not immediate: on the morning of September 24, the outlets told the judge they were still being blocked — Politico said its reporter's credentials were confiscated and CNN said staff were denied entry — prompting a request for an emergency hearing. The White House then reinstated access at midday; press operations director Micah Stopperich said in a court filing that badge reinstatement had begun at 7:25 a.m. and that badges were dropped at the entry gate around 9:55 a.m. ([Reuters](https://wncy.com/2026/09/24/politico-denied-access-to-white-house-after-judges-order-news-outlet-says/)). The television pool, however, held its line: its members said they would not reinstate pooled coverage until CNN was formally restored to the pool, and there were no TV pool cameras for Chinese President Xi Jinping's South Lawn arrival that morning ([Reuters](https://wncy.com/2026/09/24/politico-denied-access-to-white-house-after-judges-order-news-outlet-says/)).
