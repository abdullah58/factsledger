## Overview

Ankur Jain is an American entrepreneur and investor best known as the founder and CEO of [Bilt Rewards](/organizations/bilt/), the loyalty platform that lets renters earn points on rent payments ([Global Indian](https://www.globalindian.com/story/cover-story/ankur-jain-the-indian-american-entrepreneur-turning-rent-payments-into-rewards-with-bilt/)). Born in Bellevue, Washington, around 1990, he previously founded the [Kairos Society](/organizations/kairos/) in 2008 and the contacts app Humin in 2012, which Tinder acquired in 2016 ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/christine-lagorio/tinder-acquires-humin-ankur-jain.html)). In December 2025, Forbes ranked him 19th among the world's richest self-made billionaires under 40, with an estimated net worth of $3.4 billion ([Global Indian](https://www.globalindian.com/story/cover-story/ankur-jain-the-indian-american-entrepreneur-turning-rent-payments-into-rewards-with-bilt/)).

Jain grew up in Redmond, Washington, the son of Anu and Naveen Jain, both technology entrepreneurs; his father founded InfoSpace and was briefly a billionaire around 2000 ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/201107/ankur-jain-the-best-connected-21-year-old-in-the-world.html)). He started his first startup, a quiz website, at age 11, and went on to the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania, graduating in 2011 ([Naveen & Anu Jain Family Foundation](https://www.anujainfamilyfoundation.org/ankur-jain-profile/); [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/201107/ankur-jain-the-best-connected-21-year-old-in-the-world.html)). At Wharton he founded Kairos, a network for young entrepreneurs that grew into a venture studio whose portfolio companies are valued at more than $6.5 billion ([Global Indian](https://www.globalindian.com/story/cover-story/ankur-jain-the-indian-american-entrepreneur-turning-rent-payments-into-rewards-with-bilt/)). In 2011 Inc. called him the "Best-Connected 21-Year-Old in the World" ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/201107/ankur-jain-the-best-connected-21-year-old-in-the-world.html)).

Bilt's network now reaches one in four apartment buildings across the [United States](/places/united-states/), with more than 40,000 merchants and partnerships with 70% of the nation's top 100 property managers ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250710461384/en/Bilt-Raises-$250-Million-at-$10.75-Billion-Valuation-to-Accelerate-Housing-and-Neighborhood-Commerce-Platform)). ## From Kairos to Humin and Tinder

Jain founded the Kairos Society in 2008 while studying at Wharton, building it into a network for young entrepreneurs that later became a venture studio focused on housing, healthcare and other essential services ([Global Indian](https://www.globalindian.com/story/cover-story/ankur-jain-the-indian-american-entrepreneur-turning-rent-payments-into-rewards-with-bilt/)). Inc. profiled him in 2011 as the "Best-Connected 21-Year-Old in the World", a Wharton senior who had already built a network of founders, investors and policymakers ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/201107/ankur-jain-the-best-connected-21-year-old-in-the-world.html)). In 2012 he founded Humin, a San Francisco company whose first product organised contacts by context — where people met, where they lived, what they did — cross-referencing calendars, phones and Facebook ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/christine-lagorio/tinder-acquires-humin-ankur-jain.html)). Humin raised $15 million before Tinder, then owned by IAC's Match Group, acquired it in March 2016 for undisclosed terms, taking its technology, intellectual property and most of its team ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/christine-lagorio/tinder-acquires-humin-ankur-jain.html)). Jain joined Tinder as vice president of product, with co-founder David Wyler becoming vice president of partnerships, and stayed until May 2017 ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/christine-lagorio/tinder-acquires-humin-ankur-jain.html); [Naveen & Anu Jain Family Foundation](https://www.anujainfamilyfoundation.org/ankur-jain-profile/)).

## Building Bilt: the regulatory groundwork

After leaving Tinder in May 2017, Jain rejoined Kairos and turned its venture studio toward housing, healthcare and other everyday problems ([Global Indian](https://www.globalindian.com/story/cover-story/ankur-jain-the-indian-american-entrepreneur-turning-rent-payments-into-rewards-with-bilt/)). Bilt grew out of a simple observation: rent is most Americans' largest monthly expense, yet it earned them nothing ([Global Indian](https://www.globalindian.com/story/cover-story/ankur-jain-the-indian-american-entrepreneur-turning-rent-payments-into-rewards-with-bilt/)). Building it meant solving a chicken-and-egg problem — landlords wanted proof of value, rewards partners wanted a large property network, and merchants wanted a customer base — and Jain spent nearly two years approaching property owners in New York without securing commitments ([Global Indian](https://www.globalindian.com/story/cover-story/ankur-jain-the-indian-american-entrepreneur-turning-rent-payments-into-rewards-with-bilt/)). He also spent 18 months in Washington speaking with housing authorities, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, lenders and credit agencies, winning regulatory approvals in October 2019 to have on-time rent payments recognised in credit assessments ([Global Indian](https://www.globalindian.com/story/cover-story/ankur-jain-the-indian-american-entrepreneur-turning-rent-payments-into-rewards-with-bilt/)). True to his stated philosophy of financing from commercial partners before venture capital, he self-funded Bilt first, then brought real-estate owners in as partners and investors ([Global Indian](https://www.globalindian.com/story/cover-story/ankur-jain-the-indian-american-entrepreneur-turning-rent-payments-into-rewards-with-bilt/)).

## Bilt's rise to a $10.75 billion valuation

Bilt launched in June 2021 and grew fast: in January 2024 it raised $200 million led by [General Catalyst](/organizations/general-catalyst/) at a $3.1 billion valuation, with Ken Chenault, General Catalyst's chairman and managing director, becoming Bilt's chairman and NFL commissioner Roger Goodell joining the board ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/bilt-rewards-lands-150-million-for-resident-loyalty-program/)). Existing investors [Left Lane Capital](/organizations/left-lane-capital/), [Camber Creek](/organizations/camber-creek/) and [Prosus](/organizations/prosus/) also participated in that round ([Bilt Rewards](https://www.vcaonline.com/news/2024012406/bilt-rewards-announces-200-million-raise-valuing-company-at-3-1-billion/)). In August 2024 Bilt raised a further $150 million led by Teachers' Venture Growth, taking total funding to $710 million ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/bilt-rewards-lands-150-million-for-resident-loyalty-program/)). In July 2025 it raised $250 million led by General Catalyst and [GID](/organizations/gid/), with $100 million from [United Wholesale Mortgage](/organizations/united-wholesale-mortgage/) ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250710461384/en/Bilt-Raises-$250-Million-at-$10.75-Billion-Valuation-to-Accelerate-Housing-and-Neighborhood-Commerce-Platform)). Bilt's valuation rose from $3.1 billion in January 2024 to $10.75 billion in July 2025 — a roughly 3.5-fold increase in 18 months — and that climb is what carried Jain onto Forbes' under-40 billionaires list ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250710461384/en/Bilt-Raises-$250-Million-at-$10.75-Billion-Valuation-to-Accelerate-Housing-and-Neighborhood-Commerce-Platform); [Global Indian](https://www.globalindian.com/story/cover-story/ankur-jain-the-indian-american-entrepreneur-turning-rent-payments-into-rewards-with-bilt/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is Ankur Jain?** Ankur Jain is an American entrepreneur and investor, the founder and CEO of Bilt Rewards, the loyalty platform that lets renters earn points on rent ([Global Indian](https://www.globalindian.com/story/cover-story/ankur-jain-the-indian-american-entrepreneur-turning-rent-payments-into-rewards-with-bilt/)). He previously founded the Kairos Society and Humin, and served as Tinder's vice president of product ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/christine-lagorio/tinder-acquires-humin-ankur-jain.html)).

**How did Ankur Jain become a billionaire?** Forbes estimated his net worth at $3.4 billion in December 2025, ranking him 19th among the world's richest self-made billionaires under 40 ([Global Indian](https://www.globalindian.com/story/cover-story/ankur-jain-the-indian-american-entrepreneur-turning-rent-payments-into-rewards-with-bilt/)). The estimate followed Bilt's July 2025 funding round, which valued the company at $10.75 billion ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250710461384/en/Bilt-Raises-$250-Million-at-$10.75-Billion-Valuation-to-Accelerate-Housing-and-Neighborhood-Commerce-Platform)).

**What is Bilt Rewards?** Bilt is a loyalty platform and payments network that lets residents earn points on rent, HOA fees, student housing and mortgage payments, and on spending with neighbourhood merchants ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250710461384/en/Bilt-Raises-$250-Million-at-$10.75-Billion-Valuation-to-Accelerate-Housing-and-Neighborhood-Commerce-Platform)). Its network reaches one in four US apartment buildings, with points redeemable for travel, shopping and rent credits ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250710461384/en/Bilt-Raises-$250-Million-at-$10.75-Billion-Valuation-to-Accelerate-Housing-and-Neighborhood-Commerce-Platform)).

**What did Ankur Jain do before Bilt?** He founded the Kairos Society in 2008, then the contacts app Humin in 2012, which Tinder acquired in 2016; he stayed on as Tinder's vice president of product until May 2017 ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/christine-lagorio/tinder-acquires-humin-ankur-jain.html)). He then rejoined Kairos and built its venture studio, from which Bilt emerged ([Global Indian](https://www.globalindian.com/story/cover-story/ankur-jain-the-indian-american-entrepreneur-turning-rent-payments-into-rewards-with-bilt/)).
