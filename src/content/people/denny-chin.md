## Overview

When Judge Denny Chin presides over naturalization ceremonies, he takes down a framed certificate from the wall of his chambers and tells the new American citizens a family story. The certificate is his grandfather's: after tracking down the paperwork, Chin learned the man had been naturalized in 1947 in a Southern District of New York courthouse — "likely the very same courthouse where Chin sits as a judge today," [Fordham Law reported](https://news.law.fordham.edu/blog/2025/11/05/fordham-stein-prize-honors-trailblazing-career-of-judge-denny-chin-78/). It is a quiet ritual, and almost nobody who writes about Chin — the trailblazing federal judge, the man who sentenced Bernard Madoff — ever mentions it.

Chin, born in 1954 in Kowloon, Hong Kong, is a Senior Judge on the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit, a court he joined in 2010 after sixteen years as a district judge in Manhattan ([Fordham](https://www.fordham.edu/school-of-law/faculty/directory/full-time/honorable-denny-chin/)). He was the first Asian American appointed a U.S. District Judge outside the Ninth Circuit, and later the first Asian American appointed to the Second Circuit ([AABANY](https://ad24.aabany.org/hon-denny-chin/)). In 2026, he added an academic capstone that no commodity biography has caught up with yet: with Professor Thomas H. Lee, he published a casebook on *Asian Americans and the Law* ([Fordham](https://www.fordham.edu/school-of-law/faculty/directory/full-time/honorable-denny-chin/)).

One more through-line connects the judge to the site: [Todd Blanche](/people/todd-blanche), who would later become Donald Trump's criminal defense lawyer and acting U.S. attorney general, served as a law clerk for Chin before spending eight years as a federal prosecutor in the Southern District of New York ([AP](https://newswav.com/article/a-look-at-todd-blanche-the-ex-trump-lawyer-who-s-the-president-s-pick-for-a-A2604_dchHgJ)).

## From Hell's Kitchen to the federal bench

Chin's family joined his grandfather in the United States in 1956, when Chin was two, and settled in Hell's Kitchen, then a primarily Irish neighborhood in midtown Manhattan ([Stuyvesant Spectator](https://stuyspec.com/article/from-the-schoolhouse-to-the-courthouse-judge-denny-chin-s-journey-from-a-student-at-stuyvesant-to-the-appellate-bench)). "Life was challenging," Chin recalled: his father was a cook, his mother a seamstress, and the family of seven lived in a one-bedroom apartment ([Stuyvesant Spectator](https://stuyspec.com/article/from-the-schoolhouse-to-the-courthouse-judge-denny-chin-s-journey-from-a-student-at-stuyvesant-to-the-appellate-bench)). He spent his childhood studying at his parents' urging, playing stickball and softball, and reading for hours in the public library near the apartment ([Stuyvesant Spectator](https://stuyspec.com/article/from-the-schoolhouse-to-the-courthouse-judge-denny-chin-s-journey-from-a-student-at-stuyvesant-to-the-appellate-bench)).

The studying paid off: without preparatory courses or materials, he passed the SHSAT and entered Stuyvesant High School in 1967, graduating in 1971 as part of the school's last all-male class, in which Asian Americans made up just 7 percent — about 40 students, all but one of them Chinese ([Stuyvesant Spectator](https://stuyspec.com/article/from-the-schoolhouse-to-the-courthouse-judge-denny-chin-s-journey-from-a-student-at-stuyvesant-to-the-appellate-bench)). He played defensive end on the football team, and he nearly didn't make it out: he scored a 23 on his Advanced Calculus final his senior year, retook it, passed, and was allowed to graduate ([Stuyvesant Spectator](https://stuyspec.com/article/from-the-schoolhouse-to-the-courthouse-judge-denny-chin-s-journey-from-a-student-at-stuyvesant-to-the-appellate-bench)).

At [Princeton University](/universities/princeton-university), where he graduated magna cum laude in 1975, Chin majored in psychology — a choice he second-guesses today, saying he probably would have preferred history and English — and became managing editor of *The Daily Princetonian* ([White House press release](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/white-house-press-release-president-obama-nominates-judge-denny-chin-for-united-states); [Stuyvesant Spectator](https://stuyspec.com/article/from-the-schoolhouse-to-the-courthouse-judge-denny-chin-s-journey-from-a-student-at-stuyvesant-to-the-appellate-bench)). He arrived at Fordham Law School, he later joked, partly "to delay making a hard decision" about a career ([Stuyvesant Spectator](https://stuyspec.com/article/from-the-schoolhouse-to-the-courthouse-judge-denny-chin-s-journey-from-a-student-at-stuyvesant-to-the-appellate-bench)). But the summer after his first year, interning for Judge Henry F. Werker of the Southern District of New York, settled it: "I knew then, after only one summer, that I had made the right decision and that the law was for me," and he decided he would come back one day as a judge ([Fordham Law](https://news.law.fordham.edu/blog/2025/11/05/fordham-stein-prize-honors-trailblazing-career-of-judge-denny-chin-78/)). He graduated in 1978 as managing editor of the *Fordham Law Review* and clerked for Werker for two years ([White House press release](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/white-house-press-release-president-obama-nominates-judge-denny-chin-for-united-states)).

What followed was the classic path in reverse: two years at Davis Polk & Wardwell (1980–82), then four as an Assistant U.S. Attorney in the Southern District of New York (1982–86), then — unusually — a startup of his own, the general-litigation boutique Campbell, Patrick & Chin, founded with two colleagues from the U.S. Attorney's Office ([Hofstra](https://scholarlycommons.law.hofstra.edu/lectures_kaplan/10/)). The firm handled commercial, immigration, and environmental work, but the business side proved hard, and after four years Chin moved to Vladeck, Waldman, Elias & Engelhard, specializing in labor and employment law ([Stuyvesant Spectator](https://stuyspec.com/article/from-the-schoolhouse-to-the-courthouse-judge-denny-chin-s-journey-from-a-student-at-stuyvesant-to-the-appellate-bench)). President Bill Clinton nominated him to the Southern District bench in 1994, and he was confirmed that year ([White House press release](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/white-house-press-release-president-obama-nominates-judge-denny-chin-for-united-states)).

## The Madoff courtroom and other high-profile cases

Chin presided over the Southern District from September 13, 1994 through April 23, 2010, handling both criminal and civil cases, from Megan's Law disputes and the Million Youth March to Al Franken's use of the phrase "Fair and Balanced" in a book title, the Naked Cowboy, the Google Books settlement, and the United Nations Oil for Food program ([Hofstra](https://scholarlycommons.law.hofstra.edu/lectures_kaplan/10/)). He also tried an Afghan warlord charged with conspiring to import heroin ([Hofstra](https://scholarlycommons.law.hofstra.edu/lectures_kaplan/10/)).

The case that defines his public reputation came in 2009: *United States v. Bernard L. Madoff*, in which Chin sentenced the financier to 150 years in prison for running the largest Ponzi scheme in American history ([Fordham Law](https://news.law.fordham.edu/blog/2025/11/05/fordham-stein-prize-honors-trailblazing-career-of-judge-denny-chin-78/)). Other notable matters included *Authors Guild v. Google*, over Google Books fair-use claims, and *Morales v. Portuondo*, a case about attorney-client privilege ([Fordham Law](https://news.law.fordham.edu/blog/2025/11/05/fordham-stein-prize-honors-trailblazing-career-of-judge-denny-chin-78/)).

Two corners of Chin's docket almost never make it into profiles. He presided over the contested will of the ballet dancer Rudolf Nureyev: the will left most of the property to a dance foundation, while family members argued that Nureyev's recent AIDS diagnosis had left him of unsound mind — with testimony about his physical and mental state as he was dying ([Stuyvesant Spectator](https://stuyspec.com/article/from-the-schoolhouse-to-the-courthouse-judge-denny-chin-s-journey-from-a-student-at-stuyvesant-to-the-appellate-bench)). And on the criminal side, the case that stands out to him personally involved a mother of five — also caretaker for her sister — accused of being an accessory to a bank robbery, forcing him to weigh what a prison sentence would do to the children: would they end up in foster care, and what would happen to the sister ([Stuyvesant Spectator](https://stuyspec.com/article/from-the-schoolhouse-to-the-courthouse-judge-denny-chin-s-journey-from-a-student-at-stuyvesant-to-the-appellate-bench))?

Chin's courtroom has also lived a second life on screen: five of his cases, including *Morales v. Portuondo*, became *Law & Order* episodes, and he himself has been portrayed in film twice, including by actor BD Wong, according to Fordham Law ([Fordham Law](https://news.law.fordham.edu/blog/2025/11/05/fordham-stein-prize-honors-trailblazing-career-of-judge-denny-chin-78/)). In October 2009 President Barack Obama nominated him to the Second Circuit; the Senate confirmed him unanimously, 98–0, and he was sworn in on April 26, 2010 ([Stuyvesant Spectator](https://stuyspec.com/article/from-the-schoolhouse-to-the-courthouse-judge-denny-chin-s-journey-from-a-student-at-stuyvesant-to-the-appellate-bench); [Hofstra](https://scholarlycommons.law.hofstra.edu/lectures_kaplan/10/)). These days he works in Thurgood Marshall's exact office at the Thurgood Marshall U.S. Courthouse, keeping Marshall's dictionary on his desk next to a painting of himself presiding over the Madoff case ([Stuyvesant Spectator](https://stuyspec.com/article/from-the-schoolhouse-to-the-courthouse-judge-denny-chin-s-journey-from-a-student-at-stuyvesant-to-the-appellate-bench)).

## A scholar of Asian Americans and the law

Chin has taught at Fordham Law School for forty years, and in 2021 — when he took senior status — he was appointed the Lawrence W. Pierce Distinguished Jurist in Residence and co-founded the Center on Asian Americans and the Law with Professor Thomas H. Lee ([Fordham](https://www.fordham.edu/school-of-law/faculty/directory/full-time/honorable-denny-chin/)). The center formally launched in 2022 ([Fordham Law](https://news.law.fordham.edu/blog/2025/11/05/fordham-stein-prize-honors-trailblazing-career-of-judge-denny-chin-78/)). He currently teaches *Appellate Advocacy and Civil Rights* and *Asian Americans and the Law* at Fordham and has taught the latter at Harvard, Yale, and Columbia law schools as well ([Fordham](https://www.fordham.edu/school-of-law/faculty/directory/full-time/honorable-denny-chin/)). With Lee he then wrote the casebook *Asian Americans and the Law: Cases and Materials*, published in 2026 ([Fordham](https://www.fordham.edu/school-of-law/faculty/directory/full-time/honorable-denny-chin/)).

Alongside his wife Kathy Hirata Chin, he has written, produced, and presented — with a team from the Asian American Bar Association of New York — a series of reenactments of historic cases involving Asian American litigants ([AABANY](https://ad24.aabany.org/hon-denny-chin/)). The project continued into the summer of 2026, when the reenactment *Building Our Legacy: The Murder of Vincent Chin* — about the 1982 homicide considered a pivotal moment in the Asian American civil rights movement — was staged at the U.S. District Court in Manhattan with both Chins joining the post-reenactment discussion ([AABANY blog](https://blog.aabany.org/tag/asian-american/)). In October 2025, Fordham honored him with the Fordham-Stein Prize for a lifetime of extraordinary contributions to the law ([Fordham Law](https://news.law.fordham.edu/blog/2025/11/05/fordham-stein-prize-honors-trailblazing-career-of-judge-denny-chin-78/)).

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not give Chin's exact birth date or birth name, because the only source for "April 13, 1954" and "Check Kong Chin" is Wikipedia, which is not citable here; the profile states only what read sources confirm (born 1954 in Kowloon, Hong Kong).
- It does not repeat the exact 1994 nomination and confirmation dates, which come only from Wikipedia; it states the year, supported by the White House press release.
- The claim that Chin has been portrayed in film twice, including by BD Wong, rests on a single Fordham Law news report and is attributed accordingly.
- The claim that the grandfather's 1947 naturalization took place in the same courthouse where Chin sits preserves Chin's own hedge: he said "likely."
- The Center on Asian Americans and the Law's founding is dated 2021 in Fordham's faculty bio and 2022 in Fordham Law news; this profile treats 2021 as the co-founding upon senior status and 2022 as the formal launch rather than picking one silently.
- This profile does not describe the substance of Chin's appellate opinions or his jurisprudence on the Second Circuit beyond what the read sources document.
