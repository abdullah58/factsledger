## Overview

**This is Gerald J. Ford the Dallas banker (born 1944) — not President Gerald Ford (1913–2006).** Gerald J. Ford is a Texas-born banker and investor who built his fortune buying troubled banks and fixing them: at 31, with two partners and borrowed money, he paid $1.2 million for 64% of the First National Bank of Post, Texas, in 1975; in 2002 he sold Golden State Bancorp — the holding company for the nation's second-largest thrift — to Citigroup for $6 billion ([SMU](https://www.smu.edu/law/news-events/2023/trailblazer-speaker-series-gerald-j-ford)). In December 2003, as chairman and 45% stockholder of Liberté Investors, he backed the acquisition of [USAuto Holdings](/organizations/usauto-holdings/) that renamed the company First Acceptance Corporation — the Nashville insurer profiled on this site — and stayed on as its chairman ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1017907/000104746903040833/a2124990zex-99_1.htm)).

## The buy-fix-sell banking career

Ford's origin story is specific and well-documented by his own law school. Born in 1944 and raised in Pampa, Texas, he worked alongside his father in an auto-body shop as a teenager, applied to exactly one school — Southern Methodist University — graduated with a B.A. in economics in 1966, earned his J.D. from the SMU School of Law in 1969, and never practiced law ([SMU](https://www.smu.edu/law/news-events/2023/trailblazer-speaker-series-gerald-j-ford)). After a stint as general counsel for closely held corporations and six months in Bolivia studying construction financing, he decided he wanted to run something himself ([SMU](https://www.smu.edu/law/news-events/2023/trailblazer-speaker-series-gerald-j-ford)).

The playbook he developed was conservative by design: acquire troubled banks, scrutinize customer loyalty and defaults to limit risk, avoid firms that had lost more than 10–15% of deposits, and stay close to regulators — "Their job is to keep safe banks. Our job is to keep us with safe banks. We're on the same side of the street" ([SMU](https://www.smu.edu/law/news-events/2023/trailblazer-speaker-series-gerald-j-ford)). The deals compounded: Forbes's account, via Arkansas business press, traces his initial fortune to buying a small Texas bank for $1.2 million and selling it for $80 million, and the Golden State Bancorp sale to Citigroup for $6 billion in 2002 was the signature exit ([Talk Business](https://talkbusiness.net/2013/07/metropolitan-national-bank-to-be-acquired-recapitalized/); [SMU](https://www.smu.edu/law/news-events/2023/trailblazer-speaker-series-gerald-j-ford)). Later vehicles included Hilltop Holdings — which he chairs and which acquired PlainsCapital Corporation in a $520 million cash-and-stock deal — and the Ford Financial Funds, Dallas private-equity firms that own California's Mechanics Bank and, in 2013, bought and recapitalized Arkansas's Metropolitan National Bank for $16 million ([SMU](https://www.smu.edu/law/news-events/2023/trailblazer-speaker-series-gerald-j-ford); [Talk Business](https://talkbusiness.net/2013/07/metropolitan-national-bank-to-be-acquired-recapitalized/)).

## Liberté Investors and the birth of First Acceptance

The December 15, 2003 press release is the primary document. Liberté Investors, Inc. (NYSE: LBI) of Dallas announced a definitive agreement to acquire USAuto Holdings — a Tennessee-based non-standard auto insurer — for $76 million in cash plus 13.25 million newly issued Liberté shares, with up to 750,000 more shares tied to 2004 performance targets ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1017907/000104746903040833/a2124990zex-99_1.htm)). "Gerald J. Ford, Liberté's Chairman and 45% stockholder, has agreed to vote his shares in favor of the acquisition," the release states; on closing, Liberté would change its name to First Acceptance Corporation, move its headquarters to Nashville, Tennessee, install USAuto CEO [Stephen J. Harrison](/people/stephen-j-harrison/) as CEO, and keep Ford as chairman ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1017907/000104746903040833/a2124990zex-99_1.htm)). Ford's own comment in the release praised the acquisition of "not only an excellent company, but an outstanding new Chief Executive Officer as well" ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1017907/000104746903040833/a2124990zex-99_1.htm)). A 2005 McMoRan Exploration proxy confirms the aftermath: Ford, then 60, is listed as "Chairman of the Board of First Acceptance Corporation (formerly Liberté Investors Inc.)" and as former chairman and CEO of California Federal Bank, which merged with Citigroup in November 2002 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/64279/000095012905002768/h22503def14a.htm)).

## Philanthropy and the SMU relationship

Ford's giving centers on the school that trained him. He made the $20 million lead gift to build SMU's football stadium — named the Gerald J. Ford Stadium, opened in 2000 — and in 2013 committed another $15 million for the Gerald J. Ford Research and Innovation Building; he has sat on SMU's board of trustees since 1992 and served as a regent of the Texas A&M University System ([SMU](https://www.smu.edu/law/news-events/2023/trailblazer-speaker-series-gerald-j-ford)). He is married with six children; his wife Kelli is an interior designer, and the family lives in Dallas ([SMU](https://www.smu.edu/law/news-events/2023/trailblazer-speaker-series-gerald-j-ford)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Gerald J. Ford the former president?**
No. President Gerald Ford (1913–2006) was the 38th U.S. president. Gerald J. Ford (born 1944) is a Dallas-based banker and investor. The shared name mixes heavily in search results; this profile covers the banker.

**How did Gerald J. Ford make his money?**
Buying and fixing troubled banks, starting in the 1970s: a $1.2 million purchase of 64% of a small Texas bank in 1975, a string of bank turnarounds, and the $6 billion sale of Golden State Bancorp to Citigroup in 2002.

**What was Gerald J. Ford's role in First Acceptance Corporation?**
As chairman and 45% stockholder of Liberté Investors, he backed the December 2003 acquisition of USAuto Holdings; when the merger closed, Liberté renamed itself First Acceptance Corporation and Ford remained chairman.

**What is Hilltop Holdings?**
A bank and insurance holding company chaired by Ford, which acquired PlainsCapital Corporation in 2012. Ford's son Jeremy is Hilltop's president and CEO.
