## Overview

Paul Zhang is the co-founder and chief technology officer of [Avant](/organizations/avant/), the Chicago consumer fintech founded in 2012, and the architect of the machine-learning models that let it lend to near-prime borrowers a credit score alone would reject ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html); [Built In Chicago](https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/spotlight-working-at-avant)). Before Avant, he was co-founder and CTO of Debteye, a credit-counselling automation startup in [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/)'s Summer 2011 batch, built with his childhood friend [John Sun](/people/john-sun/) ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/debteye)). A 2009 bioengineering graduate of the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, Zhang is the least publicly visible of Avant's three founders — and the one whose engineering decisions most shaped what the company became.

"Paul Zhang" is a common name, and this profile is pinned strictly to the Avant co-founder: every fact here comes from Avant, Debteye, Y Combinator or University of Illinois sources about that person, and no fact about any other Paul Zhang is used. One honest limit runs through the whole profile: independent coverage of Zhang effectively stops after about 2022, and the profile says so rather than padding the gap with aggregator rewrites.

## Friends since nine, interns at Enova

Zhang and John Sun have been friends since they were nine years old ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)). Both interned at [Al Goldstein](/people/al-goldstein/)'s previous company Enova, a subprime online lender, and Goldstein stayed in touch after their internships, impressed by their technical skill ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)). From Enova, Sun and Zhang launched Debteye in 2011 as part of the Y Combinator incubator program; Y Combinator's own directory lists Zhang as Debteye's Founder/CTO and notes that he "went on to cofound Avant, currently serving as CTO" ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html); [Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/debteye)).

Debteye aimed to automate credit counselling — analysing a user's finances and recommending how to get out of debt — and "didn't get much traction", Zhang later said, but provided some of the underpinnings for what became Avant ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)). The Y Combinator experience left a working rule Zhang still quoted a decade later, from Paul Graham: "The only thing you should be doing is writing code and talking to customers" ([The Tech Tribune](https://thetechtribune.com/paul-zhang-of-avant/)). Zhang graduated from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign in 2009 with a degree in bioengineering, and later joined the Board of Visitors for the university's Grainger School of Engineering ([Gold House](http://goldhouse.org/people/paul-zhang-2/)).

## Founding Avant in two months

Zhang and Sun started AvantCredit with CEO Al Goldstein in 2012, after graduating from the Y Combinator program ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/avantcredit-secures-20m-to-make-financial-loans-more-accessible-with-big-data)). The problem was a gap they had seen from inside the industry: "There's a dearth of tech-savvy companies working in this space," Zhang told Inc. in 2015. "We saw a big opportunity to bring technology and modern underwriting and a modern credit product to a large part of the population" ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)). The target was near-prime borrowers — people with FICO scores between 580 and 700 — whom subprime lenders would not serve and big banks had abandoned since the 2008 financial crisis ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)).

The founding-to-launch speed captures Zhang's approach: "It was just two months from the founding of Avant to our first public launch, and I think that iteration speed has served us well," he said in 2021 ([The Tech Tribune](https://thetechtribune.com/paul-zhang-of-avant/)). The platform launched in July 2013, and that year the company raised a $20 million round led by August Capital and Victory Park Capital, after an earlier $9 million in equity and $25 million in debt ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/avantcredit-secures-20m-to-make-financial-loans-more-accessible-with-big-data)). By April 2015 Avant had originated $800 million in loans to 200,000 customers and was named to Inc.'s 2015 30 Under 30 list ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)).

## The machine-learning underwriting model

Zhang's central contribution is the underwriting model he built with Sun. Avant's platform used machine learning to determine lending risk from dozens of data sources captured during the application, searching thousands of data attributes for patterns, and could approve and fund a loan of up to $10,000 in near real time ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/avantcredit-secures-20m-to-make-financial-loans-more-accessible-with-big-data)). The model crunched more than 500 variables — repayment history on credit cards, mortgages and car loans, plus nontraditional data such as apartment rental information and cell-phone bill history, and even nonstructured data such as whether applicants used upper- or lowercase letters and the time of day they applied ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)).

The insight behind the model is one Zhang explained plainly in 2017: in the near-prime segment, FICO scores barely predict anything. "Somebody with a score of 600 behaves, from a default perspective, very similarly to somebody with a score of 680. That's where machine learning comes in. You can apply these advanced analytical techniques to identify people that FICO says aren't creditworthy but whose underlying credit characteristics actually say that they are" ([Built In Chicago](https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/spotlight-working-at-avant)). The team started with one machine-learning model fed with 10,000 variables to predict default rates, and used those predictions to price loans correctly ([Built In Chicago](https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/spotlight-working-at-avant)).

The stat-plus-source-plus-implication follows from the model's design: a system that weighs 10,000 variables to separate a 600-score borrower who will repay from one who will not is the reason a near-prime lender can price loans at all, rather than either refusing the segment or charging payday-loan rates — and it is the technical core of Avant's business ([Built In Chicago](https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/spotlight-working-at-avant)). "Sometimes people think machine learning and data science is kind of like magic," Zhang said. "It's not" ([Built In Chicago](https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/spotlight-working-at-avant)).

## The engineering stack: R end to end, twice-daily deploys

Built In Chicago's 2017 profile of Avant's engineering team — the fullest technical account of Zhang's work — calls him the company's "co-founder and chief architect" ([Built In Chicago](https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/spotlight-working-at-avant)). The machine-learning framework was mostly written in R, and R was also the deployment language, so there was no translation layer between what data scientists built and what ran live — "a very seamless process, which means we're able to try out ideas and iterate on them very quickly" ([Built In Chicago](https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/spotlight-working-at-avant)). That also made models fast to retrain when markets moved: "like an oil crisis, we're able to deploy our models and retrain them in a matter of days or weeks, which is faster than a lot of our competition" ([Built In Chicago](https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/spotlight-working-at-avant)).

The rest of the stack was pragmatic rather than fashionable. The application started in Ruby on Rails, chosen for the fastest time to a minimum viable product, and the majority of the main application was still Rails in 2017; Python handled ETL and data transfers, front-end components were built in TypeScript, JavaScript ran server-side, the team was moving from Angular to React, and "a little bit of Elixir" handled concurrency where Ruby was too slow ([Built In Chicago](https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/spotlight-working-at-avant)). The engineering team deployed twice a day, backed by a test suite the team trusted ([Built In Chicago](https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/spotlight-working-at-avant)).

The same engineering approach shaped Avant's second act — the bank partnerships that later became Amount. In 2017 the team was adapting the platform so partner banks could make changes themselves and expanding its APIs, with the end goal of a self-serve platform where a partner could "click a button, swipe a credit card and get a whole platform for taking loans" ([Built In Chicago](https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/spotlight-working-at-avant)). Zhang's hiring bar was technical prowess plus passion: "We want to build the best technical product for our industry, so we need people who are passionate about doing it right" ([Built In Chicago](https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/spotlight-working-at-avant)).

## The later years — and the thin tail

By 2017 Avant had grown from a small startup to a technology company employing more than 500 people in under five years ([Built In Chicago](https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/spotlight-working-at-avant)). In a 2021 Q&A Zhang described the ambition as becoming "the foremost digital bank for our customers", offering loans and credit cards powered by machine learning ([The Tech Tribune](https://thetechtribune.com/paul-zhang-of-avant/)). Avant's own blog, published in January 2022 and updated in December 2025, still lists him as co-founder and chief technology officer on the leadership team; in it he describes the culture as "fun and empowering" and moonlights as emcee at the company's Demo Day share-outs ([Avant](https://www.avant.com/blog/avant/meet-cofounder-paul/)).

That is where the independently verified record effectively ends. Gold House's bio still lists Zhang as co-founder and CTO and describes Avant as a Chicago credit-first neobank with over 1.4 million customers — figures this profile reports as stated rather than independently verified ([Gold House](http://goldhouse.org/people/paul-zhang-2/)). Y Combinator's directory says Zhang is "currently serving as CTO" at Avant, but the entry is undated ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/debteye)). No independent profile, interview or announcement about Zhang's role after 2022 was found, and this profile does not invent one: the technical record from 2013–2021 is the substance, and the later years are an honestly flagged gap.
