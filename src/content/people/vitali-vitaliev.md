## Overview

Vitali Vitaliev (born 1954) is a Ukrainian-born British journalist and author of sixteen books whose career spans two lives: as an investigative reporter in the Soviet Union, and as a newspaper and magazine columnist in Britain and Australia after defecting in January 1990 ([Scottish Geographical Journal](https://doi.org/10.1080/14702541.2022.2095427); [Royal Literary Fund](https://www.rlf.org.uk/posts/projects-that-dont-make-it/)). Born in Kharkiv, in what is now [Ukraine](/places/ukraine/), he became a special correspondent for the satirical magazine *Krokodil* in Moscow, then rebuilt his career in the West — as Features Editor of *E&T* magazine from 2007 to 2020, a Royal Literary Fund fellow at Cambridge, and now a columnist for [Prospect Magazine](/organizations/prospect-magazine/) and *RAIL* magazine ([Jewish Telegraph](http://www.jewish-telegraph.com/prof_359.html); [Magdalene College, Cambridge](https://www.magd.cam.ac.uk/news/search-britains-utopias)).

Vitaliev was born in Kharkiv, a predominantly Russian-speaking city in eastern Ukraine not far from the Russian border, to scientist parents: his father was a particle physicist and his mother a chemical engineer ([Scottish Geographical Journal](https://doi.org/10.1080/14702541.2022.2095427)). Shortly after his birth the family was sent to a closed town in the Moscow region, built around a factory producing atomic bombs, where about 20,000 people lived behind a concrete wall; he spent his first three years there before the family returned to Kharkiv ([Scottish Geographical Journal](https://doi.org/10.1080/14702541.2022.2095427)). He grew up speaking Russian — Ukrainian was optional at school, though he chose to learn it — and studied French and English at Kharkiv University before working as an interpreter and translator ([Scottish Geographical Journal](https://doi.org/10.1080/14702541.2022.2095427); [The Independent](https://isabelwolff.com/us/blog/how-we-met-vitali-vitaliev-clive-james)).

## Soviet investigative journalist

Vitaliev became a journalist in 1981 and worked as a special correspondent for *Krokodil*, the satirical magazine, in Moscow ([Jewish Telegraph](http://www.jewish-telegraph.com/prof_359.html)). The *Jewish Telegraph* describes him as the first Soviet journalist to publicly expose organised crime — the so-called Soviet Mafia — as well as the existence of prostitution, political prisoners and Soviet neo-Nazis, and the *Scottish Geographical Journal* confirms the substance of those investigations, describing how he exposed "the dismal conjunctures between organised crime, state corruption, the arrest of political prisoners and the activities of Soviet 'neo-Nazis'" ([Jewish Telegraph](http://www.jewish-telegraph.com/prof_359.html); [Scottish Geographical Journal](https://doi.org/10.1080/14702541.2022.2095427)). He later told the journal that he had been one of the first investigative journalists in the Soviet Union to investigate the roots of Russian fascism in the 1980s, including in Leningrad ([Scottish Geographical Journal](https://doi.org/10.1080/14702541.2022.2095427)).

His Moscow reporting brought him to British television. In 1988 he first appeared as Clive James's "Moscow Correspondent" on the BBC's *Saturday Night Clive*, broadcast live by satellite from Moscow, and he went on to become a regular on the show ([The Independent](https://isabelwolff.com/us/blog/how-we-met-vitali-vitaliev-clive-james)). James recalled that Vitaliev's writing in Soviet newspapers and journals had got him into trouble and that "we heard the KGB were after him", and called him the first emigre Russian journalist to go on British television and talk about the Russian mafia ([The Independent](https://isabelwolff.com/us/blog/how-we-met-vitali-vitaliev-clive-james)). The attention, and the threats from the criminal underworld and the KGB that followed his exposures, were what forced his defection.

## Defection and a second life in Britain

Vitaliev defected in January 1990, leaving the Soviet Union by train with his then-wife and son Andrei; the family went first to [London](/places/london/) and then to Melbourne, [Australia](/places/australia/), where he spent two years and wrote a column for a national newspaper ([Jewish Telegraph](http://www.jewish-telegraph.com/prof_359.html); [The Independent](https://isabelwolff.com/us/blog/how-we-met-vitali-vitaliev-clive-james)). *The Independent* records that he was a columnist on the *Melbourne Age* for two years before moving to London ([The Independent](https://isabelwolff.com/us/blog/how-we-met-vitali-vitaliev-clive-james)). Harvard honoured him with the Nieman Fellowship in Journalism ([Jewish Telegraph](http://www.jewish-telegraph.com/prof_359.html)).

After returning to [England](/places/england/) in 1992, he wrote for numerous newspapers including *The Guardian* and *The Daily Telegraph*, and was a staff columnist at *The European* ([Jewish Telegraph](http://www.jewish-telegraph.com/prof_359.html)). He had a column in *The Guardian* for a couple of years, and he credits its then editor Alan Rusbridger with playing a big role in helping with his defection and settling in the UK ([Scottish Geographical Journal](https://doi.org/10.1080/14702541.2022.2095427)). From 2007 until his retirement in 2020 he was Features Editor of *E&T* (Engineering and Technology) magazine, writing his "After All" column there for nearly fifteen years ([Scottish Geographical Journal](https://doi.org/10.1080/14702541.2022.2095427)). That column won him Gold TABPI Awards for the best regular column in both 2015 and 2016, and he was repeatedly shortlisted for the PPA UK Columnist of the Year award ([Great British Speakers](https://www.greatbritishtalent.com/talents/vitali-vitaliev/)). He was also part of the *QI* team that won the RTS Award for the best TV entertainment show of 2007 ([Great British Speakers](https://www.greatbritishtalent.com/talents/vitali-vitaliev/)).

He now lives in a small town in Hertfordshire, England, and holds both British and Australian passports ([Muck Rack](https://muckrack.com/vitali-vitaliev/articles)). In 2018 he was appointed a Fellow of the Royal Geographical Society ([Scottish Geographical Journal](https://doi.org/10.1080/14702541.2022.2095427)).

## Books, teaching and current columns

Vitaliev is the author of sixteen books, spanning travel writing, autobiography and fiction, according to the Royal Literary Fund and *RAIL* magazine ([Royal Literary Fund](https://www.rlf.org.uk/posts/projects-that-dont-make-it/); [RAIL magazine](https://www.railmagazine.com/tag/vitali-vitaliev)). His first book, *Special Correspondent: Investigating in the Soviet Union*, was published by Hutchinson in London in 1990, the year he defected ([Harvard Library](https://dccollection.share.library.harvard.edu/items/show/13327)). The travel books that followed made his name in Britain: *Little is the Light*, about Europe's mini-states, was published by Simon & Schuster in the mid-1990s, and *Passport to Enclavia: Travels in Search of a European Identity* appeared in 2008, followed by the memoir *Life as a Literary Device* in 2009, with a foreword by Clive James ([The Independent](https://isabelwolff.com/us/blog/how-we-met-vitali-vitaliev-clive-james); [Jewish Telegraph](http://www.jewish-telegraph.com/prof_359.html)).

His later work turns his lifelong fascination with marginal places into its subject: the *Scottish Geographical Journal* notes his interest in "marginal places, curious places, off-the-beaten-track places, or ones anomalous in terms of their fit — or non-fit — with host (geo)political jurisdictions", and cites *Passport to Enclavia* and the *Atlas of Geographical Curiosities* as examples ([Scottish Geographical Journal](https://doi.org/10.1080/14702541.2022.2095427)). That fascination runs back to childhood: he has said his favourite reading as a schoolboy was train timetables and atlases, and that his desire to escape closed spaces shaped his interest in geography ([Scottish Geographical Journal](https://doi.org/10.1080/14702541.2022.2095427)).

From 2021 to 2024 he was a Royal Literary Fund Writing Fellow and Teaching Associate at Magdalene College, University of Cambridge, tutoring students in writing ([Magdalene College, Cambridge](https://www.magd.cam.ac.uk/news/search-britains-utopias)). The college's farewell piece records that he featured Magdalene itself as one of his top ten utopias in *Trucks in the Garden of Eden*, and that he and the college chaplain conducted a vigil for Ukraine in the chapel in April 2022, shortly after the Russian invasion ([Magdalene College, Cambridge](https://www.magd.cam.ac.uk/news/search-britains-utopias)). He has said the invasion left him paralysed for about ten days before Rusbridger, by then editor of Prospect Magazine, commissioned him to write about Ukraine — the resulting piece was "Ukraine: Memories of home" ([Scottish Geographical Journal](https://doi.org/10.1080/14702541.2022.2095427)).

Since 2025 he has written a regular "Second Life" column for Prospect Magazine, reflecting on his first, Soviet life from the vantage point of his second life in Britain, alongside a fortnightly column for *RAIL* magazine ([Muck Rack](https://muckrack.com/vitali-vitaliev/articles); [RAIL magazine](https://www.railmagazine.com/tag/vitali-vitaliev)). In November 2025 the "Second Life" column took him to the [Banned Books Museum](/organizations/banned-books-museum/) in Tallinn's Old Town, in a piece titled "Second life: A modern museum of banned books reminds me of my old Soviet life", which opens with George Bernard Shaw's maxim "Assassination is the extreme form of censorship" ([Muck Rack](https://muckrack.com/vitali-vitaliev/articles)).
