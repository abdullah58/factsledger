Of the three generals Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth removed on April 2, 2026, David Hodne's name drew the least attention — and the removal made the least sense on paper. Hodne was not a political general. He was the [U.S. Army](/organizations/us-army/)'s soldier-modernization specialist: the 59th commandant of the Infantry School, the director of the Soldier Lethality Cross-Functional Team, the commander of the 4th Infantry Division, and the head of the Futures and Concepts Center ([U.S. Army](https://api.army.mil/e2/c/downloads/2025/12/09/1ba32cfe/mil-bio-hodne-dec.pdf)). Weeks before he was fired, he had stood in Austin, Texas, and told industry and Army leaders alike that his new command would "innovate with urgency" to take on threats from China, Russia, and Iran ([CNN](https://kioncentralcoast.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2026/09/02/army-leadership-void-fuels-fears-about-hegseths-culture-war-fixation/)). Six months into the job, he was gone, with no public explanation.

## The firing

On April 2, 2026, Hegseth fired Hodne alongside Army chief of staff [Gen. Randy George](/people/randy-george/) and Maj. Gen. William Green, the chief of chaplains, three U.S. defense officials told Reuters ([Reuters](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2600859/us-army-chief-of-staff-fired-by-pete-hegseth-sources-say)). The Pentagon confirmed George's immediate retirement but gave no reason for any of the removals, even as the U.S. military was building up forces in the Middle East and carrying out operations against Iran — a moment when, as Reuters noted, firing a general during wartime is nearly without precedent ([Reuters](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2600859/us-army-chief-of-staff-fired-by-pete-hegseth-sources-say)). Hodne had only been promoted to the four-star Transformation and Training Command post the previous October ([Times Now](https://www.timesnownews.com/world/us/us-news/general-randy-george-firing-pete-hegseth-general-david-hodne-major-general-william-green-jr-army-chief-of-staff-article-153985837)).

What officials pointed to, in the days after, was a personnel dispute rather than a policy one. Hegseth had moved to block the promotion of four Army officers to one-star general rank — two of them Black and two of them women — and their removal from a larger candidate list drew questions from senior officials about whether race or gender played a role ([Times Now](https://www.timesnownews.com/world/us/us-news/general-randy-george-firing-pete-hegseth-general-david-hodne-major-general-william-green-jr-army-chief-of-staff-article-153985837)). George and Army Secretary [Dan Driscoll](/people/dan-driscoll/) had resisted the pressure, citing the officers' service records, and George's request for a meeting with Hegseth on the issue was declined ([Times Now](https://www.timesnownews.com/world/us/us-news/general-randy-george-firing-pete-hegseth-general-david-hodne-major-general-william-green-jr-army-chief-of-staff-article-153985837)). No on-record reason was ever given for Hodne's own dismissal.

## The modernizer

Hodne's career was built around the question of how the Army fights its next war. The Army's official biography records a West Point graduate commissioned as an infantry officer in 1991, with a Bachelor of Science in aerospace engineering, a Master of Arts in Military Studies in Unconventional Warfare from American Military University, and an Army War College Fellowship at Georgetown University ([U.S. Army](https://api.army.mil/e2/c/downloads/2025/12/09/1ba32cfe/mil-bio-hodne-dec.pdf)). He commanded at every echelon from company through division — Ranger, Stryker, Cavalry, Mechanized, and Light Infantry formations — and is a Distinguished Member of the 75th Ranger Regiment ([U.S. Army](https://api.army.mil/e2/c/downloads/2025/12/09/1ba32cfe/mil-bio-hodne-dec.pdf)). CNN, months after the firing, still described him as "a widely respected Army Ranger" ([CNN](https://kioncentralcoast.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2026/09/02/army-leadership-void-fuels-fears-about-hegseths-culture-war-fixation/)).

His general-officer assignments trace the Army's modernization architecture. From 2018 to 2021 he served as the 59th Commandant of the U.S. Army Infantry School and Chief of Infantry — August 3, 2018 to August 6, 2021 as commandant — while simultaneously directing the Soldier Lethality Cross-Functional Team, the outfit charged with getting better weapons and gear to individual soldiers faster ([From the Green Notebook](https://fromthegreennotebook.com/2022/05/21/s3-ep-27-maj-gen-david-hodne-culture-character-and-communication/); [U.S. Army](https://api.army.mil/e2/c/downloads/2025/12/09/1ba32cfe/mil-bio-hodne-dec.pdf)). In August 2021 he took command of the 4th Infantry Division and Fort Carson, holding it through 2023 ([From the Green Notebook](https://fromthegreennotebook.com/2022/05/21/s3-ep-27-maj-gen-david-hodne-culture-character-and-communication/)). Then came the futures portfolio: director of the Army Transition Team and, from January 2024, deputy commanding general for futures and concepts at Army Futures Command and director of the Futures and Concepts Center ([U.S. Army](https://api.army.mil/e2/c/downloads/2025/12/09/1ba32cfe/mil-bio-hodne-dec.pdf)).

His combat record underpinned the reputation. He commanded two battalions in combat: 3rd Squadron, 4th U.S. Cavalry in Iraq, and 2nd Battalion, 75th Ranger Regiment in Afghanistan ([From the Green Notebook](https://fromthegreennotebook.com/2022/05/21/s3-ep-27-maj-gen-david-hodne-culture-character-and-communication/)). He also led the activation of Fort Carson's first Stryker brigade combat team and served as executive officer to the commanding general of U.S. Central Command ([From the Green Notebook](https://fromthegreennotebook.com/2022/05/21/s3-ep-27-maj-gen-david-hodne-culture-character-and-communication/)).

## Six months at the top

Hodne assumed command of the Transformation and Training Command on October 2, 2025 ([U.S. Army](https://api.army.mil/e2/c/downloads/2025/12/09/1ba32cfe/mil-bio-hodne-dec.pdf)), and the post was no routine headquarters job. T2COM was a brand-new four-star command created by merging two of the Army's major organizations — the Army Futures Command, seven years old, and the Training and Doctrine Command, inactivated on September 26 after more than 52 years — under one roof in Austin, Texas ([MeriTalk](https://cdn.meritalk.com/articles/army-launches-transformation-training-command/)). The consolidation was the centerpiece of the Army Transformation Initiative, announced in May to streamline the force, cut general-officer billets, and overhaul the Army's requirements and acquisition organizations; as Secretary Dan Driscoll and Chief of Staff Randy George wrote to the force, "Our focus is on filling combat formations with Soldiers" and "every role must sharpen the spear or be cut away" ([MeriTalk](https://cdn.meritalk.com/articles/army-launches-transformation-training-command/)).

At the October 2 activation ceremony at Austin's Lady Bird Johnson Auditorium, Hodne was promoted to four-star general before assuming command, with Command Sgt. Maj. Raymond Harris as his command sergeant major ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/289007/transformation_and_training_command_activated)). The unified headquarters took responsibility for force generation, force design, and force development, with the Army Recruiting Command, Combined Arms Command, and Futures and Concepts Command as subordinate commands ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/289007/transformation_and_training_command_activated)). George, who presided over the ceremony, told the audience that "transformation is not just about product innovation, it's about process innovation… T2COM will help us cut our redundancy, reverse stagnation and push talent and leaders into our fighting formations" ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/289007/transformation_and_training_command_activated)). Hodne framed the mission as turning "war-fighting concepts into war-winning capabilities": "From vision to victory, we enable the Army's continuous transformation, modernizing capabilities, developing leaders, and advancing the profession to deliver decisive readiness" ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/289007/transformation_and_training_command_activated)).

Five months later, in March 2026, Hodne told industry and Army leaders in Austin that the command would "innovate with urgency" to take on threats from China, Russia, and Iran ([CNN](https://kioncentralcoast.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2026/09/02/army-leadership-void-fuels-fears-about-hegseths-culture-war-fixation/)). A month after that, he was fired.

The reaction from inside the institution was dismay at the loss of exactly the kind of leader the moment demanded. "You're taking away three of the most seasoned combat leaders of a generation who are in a unique position to articulate battlefield requirements," a former senior Army official told CNN. "So, I think that the concerns are very real" ([CNN](https://kioncentralcoast.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2026/09/02/army-leadership-void-fuels-fears-about-hegseths-culture-war-fixation/)). Hodne is married to Shelley Hodne, a fellow 1991 West Point graduate, and the couple has two children ([U.S. Army](https://api.army.mil/e2/c/downloads/2025/12/09/1ba32cfe/mil-bio-hodne-dec.pdf)).
