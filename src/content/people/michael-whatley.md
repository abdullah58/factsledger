# Michael Whatley

Michael Whatley is a North Carolina Republican who chaired the Republican National Committee from March 2024 to August 2025 and is the party's nominee for the U.S. Senate in North Carolina's 2026 election. ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Michael_Whatley)) He advanced from the March 3, 2026 Republican primary with 64.6% of the vote and faces former governor Roy Cooper in the November 3 general election. ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Michael_Whatley))

## Early life and education

Whatley was 56 in September 2026, according to the Associated Press, which puts his birth around 1970. ([Associated Press](https://www.akronnewsreporter.com/2026/09/18/attorney-general-todd-blanche-political-events/)) He earned a bachelor's degree in history from the University of North Carolina at Charlotte in 1991, a master's in religion from Wake Forest University in 1993, a master's in theology from the University of Notre Dame in 1994, and a law degree from Notre Dame Law School in 1997. ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Michael_Whatley)) At 14, he volunteered for Jesse Helms's 1984 Senate campaign, and his Christian faith is a central part of his public persona. ([The Assembly](https://www.theassemblync.com/news/politics/michael-whatley-nc-senate/))

## Career before party leadership

Whatley began his career as legal counsel to U.S. Sen. Paul Coverdell of Georgia in 2000, then served as a principal deputy assistant secretary at the U.S. Department of Energy from 2001 to 2003 under President George W. Bush. ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Michael_Whatley)) He spent roughly two decades in Washington, including work on the Bush campaign's 2000 Florida recount team, and later became an energy lobbyist: executive vice president of the Consumer Energy Alliance from 2007 to 2019 and a partner at HBW Resources from 2007 to 2022. ([The Assembly](https://www.theassemblync.com/news/politics/michael-whatley-nc-senate/), [Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Michael_Whatley))

## North Carolina Republican Party chair

Party leadership elected Whatley chairman of the North Carolina Republican Party in June 2019, and he held the post until March 2024. ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Michael_Whatley)) He was credited with helping deliver the state for Trump in 2020 and led the state party's election-integrity effort that year. ([The Assembly](https://www.theassemblync.com/news/politics/michael-whatley-nc-senate/))

## RNC chairman

On March 8, 2024, the RNC elected Whatley chairman at its spring meeting in Houston, following Ronna McDaniel's resignation. He was the only announced candidate and cited President Trump's endorsement in his pitch letter to members. ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Michael_Whatley)) After his election, alongside co-chair Lara Trump, the committee laid off around 60 staffers across its political, communications, and data departments — a move Politico described as Trump's operation moving to take over the party's machinery. ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Michael_Whatley)) Whatley resigned on August 22, 2025, after announcing his Senate run; Joe Gruters succeeded him. ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Michael_Whatley))

## 2026 Senate campaign

Whatley announced his Senate run in July 2025, and Trump gave him a "complete and total endorsement" on Truth Social. ([The Assembly](https://www.theassemblync.com/news/politics/michael-whatley-nc-senate/)) Early contenders for the nomination included former state Rep. Andy Nilsson and Don Brown; Lara Trump considered a run before opting out. ([The Assembly](https://www.theassemblync.com/news/politics/michael-whatley-nc-senate/)) Whatley won the March 3, 2026 primary with 64.6% (405,140 votes) to Brown's 15.6%. ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Michael_Whatley)) In the November 3 general election he faces Democrat Roy Cooper, Green candidate Michael Dublin, Libertarian Shannon Bray, and independent Shaunesi Deberry; his listed endorsements include Trump and Vice President J.D. Vance. ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Michael_Whatley))

His stated positions include opposition to abortion except when the mother's life is at risk — he holds a 100% pro-life rating — though his campaign platform has otherwise remained largely undefined. ([The Assembly](https://www.theassemblync.com/news/politics/michael-whatley-nc-senate/)) His campaign website's stated theme: "I believe in a better North Carolina and a stronger America. More money in your pockets, safe communities, and a strong country." ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Michael_Whatley))

## The Gastonia rally

On September 16, 2026, Trump headlined a Whatley campaign rally at Gastonia Municipal Airport. Trump called Whatley the "law-and-order candidate" and "an incredible champion" for North Carolina after Hurricane Helene, attacked Cooper as "a murderer protector," and touted billions in federal recovery money funneled to the state. ([The Gaston Gazette](https://www.gastongazette.com/story/news/politics/elections/2026/09/16/trump-praises-whatley-attacks-roy-cooper-at-gastonia-nc-event/91800162007/)) Whatley told the crowd he had backed Trump since watching him descend the Trump Tower escalator in 2015 and called him "the greatest president of our lifetime." ([The Gaston Gazette](https://www.gastongazette.com/story/news/politics/elections/2026/09/16/trump-praises-whatley-attacks-roy-cooper-at-gastonia-nc-event/91800162007/)) An East Carolina University poll conducted August 31 to September 3 showed Cooper leading Whatley 46% to 39%, with 14% undecided. ([The Gaston Gazette](https://www.gastongazette.com/story/news/politics/elections/2026/09/16/trump-praises-whatley-attacks-roy-cooper-at-gastonia-nc-event/91800162007/))

## The Blanche appearance and the Hatch Act complaint

Attorney General [Todd Blanche](/people/todd-blanche/) also campaigned for Whatley at the September 16 Gastonia rally, telling the crowd that Americans were safer than they had been two years earlier, crediting Trump and Vice President Vance, and calling Trump "the greatest president of our generation"; his remarks ranged beyond law enforcement into the stock market, inflation, and wage growth. ([Standing Record](https://standingrecord.com/entries/2026/09/16/federal-politicization-of-doj-398f5665/)) The appearance was not isolated: Blanche spoke at the RNC's midterm convention the same week and, in August, appeared alongside Trump at a Nassau County, New York event boosting Republican candidates. ([Standing Record](https://standingrecord.com/entries/2026/09/16/federal-politicization-of-doj-398f5665/))

On September 18, the Associated Press reported that the pattern had drawn a Hatch Act complaint from the watchdog group Public Citizen to the Office of Special Counsel, arguing the appearances violated limits on political activity by federal employees. ([Associated Press](https://www.akronnewsreporter.com/2026/09/18/attorney-general-todd-blanche-political-events/)) The Justice Department called the complaint baseless, saying Blanche's appearances were made in his "personal capacity" and cleared by ethics officials. ([Associated Press](https://www.akronnewsreporter.com/2026/09/18/attorney-general-todd-blanche-political-events/)) Blanche himself said, "I will always say yes to speak when the president of the United States asks me to." ([Associated Press](https://www.akronnewsreporter.com/2026/09/18/attorney-general-todd-blanche-political-events/)) Richard Painter, a White House ethics lawyer under George W. Bush, said the pattern "lends credence to the arguments that the Justice Department is hopelessly politicized." ([Standing Record](https://standingrecord.com/entries/2026/09/16/federal-politicization-of-doj-398f5665/))

## Questions over his North Carolina roots

In June 2026, NBC News reported that Whatley was born in Michigan and lived there until his early high school years, spending roughly three years in Blowing Rock — while he has frequently told voters on the stump that he "grew up" in Blowing Rock; an NBC review found he used some variation of that line at least 15 times since announcing his Senate run. ([North Carolina Democratic Party](https://www.ncdp.org/media/new-from-nbc-news-michael-whatley-exposed-for-repeatedly-lying-to-north-carolinians-about-being-from-north-carolina/)) His campaign website says he was "raised in Blowing Rock" and makes no mention of Michigan roots; his campaign communications director said Whatley "became an adult" in western North Carolina. ([North Carolina Democratic Party](https://www.ncdp.org/media/new-from-nbc-news-michael-whatley-exposed-for-repeatedly-lying-to-north-carolinians-about-being-from-north-carolina/)) Jason Husser, an Elon University political science professor, said the issue could affect voters' perception of Whatley's authenticity. ([North Carolina Democratic Party](https://www.ncdp.org/media/new-from-nbc-news-michael-whatley-exposed-for-repeatedly-lying-to-north-carolinians-about-being-from-north-carolina/)) The characterization remains contested: the Gaston Gazette describes him as "originally from Blowing Rock," while NBC's review concluded records show he spent most of his childhood away from North Carolina. ([The Gaston Gazette](https://www.gastongazette.com/story/news/politics/elections/2026/09/16/trump-praises-whatley-attacks-roy-cooper-at-gastonia-nc-event/91800162007/), [North Carolina Democratic Party](https://www.ncdp.org/media/new-from-nbc-news-michael-whatley-exposed-for-repeatedly-lying-to-north-carolinians-about-being-from-north-carolina/))

## Hurricane Helene role

Trump dubbed Whatley the "Recovery Czar" for Hurricane Helene recovery efforts in western North Carolina. ([The Appalachian](https://theappalachianonline.com/what-to-know-about-michael-whatley-the-wnc-native-running-for-us-senate-in-2026/)) At the Gastonia rally, Trump praised Whatley as an "incredible champion" for the state after Helene and touted the billions in federal recovery money sent to North Carolina. ([The Gaston Gazette](https://www.gastongazette.com/story/news/politics/elections/2026/09/16/trump-praises-whatley-attacks-roy-cooper-at-gastonia-nc-event/91800162007/))
