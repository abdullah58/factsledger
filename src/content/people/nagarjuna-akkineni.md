**Akkineni Nagarjuna Rao** — credited as Nagarjuna or Nagarjuna Akkineni — is an Indian actor whose career of over four decades has been spent primarily in Telugu cinema, with Hindi and Tamil films alongside. The son of veteran actor Akkineni Nageswara Rao, he debuted as a lead actor in *Vikram* (1986), broke through with Mani Ratnam's *Geetanjali* and Ram Gopal Varma's *Shiva* in 1989, and is co-owner of Hyderabad's Annapurna Studios ([Cinemaazi](https://www.cinemaazi.com/people/nagarjuna?ctr=323&filterBy=n&search=&people=); [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/entertainment/movies/nagarjuna-akkineni-on-his-father-anrs-dream-as-annapurna-studios-celebrates-its-50th-year/article69481405.ece)).

Born 29 August 1959 in Madras (present-day Chennai), he first appeared on screen as a child artist in *Sudigundalu* (1967) before his lead debut in *Vikram* (1986), a remake of the Hindi film *Hero* directed by V. Madhusudhana Rao ([Cinemaazi](https://www.cinemaazi.com/people/nagarjuna?ctr=323&filterBy=n&search=&people=)). His 2025 slate — the middle-class Deepak in Sekhar Kammula's *Kuberaa* and an antagonist opposite Rajinikanth in Lokesh Kanagaraj's *Coolie* — shows a star still chasing new registers after four decades ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/entertainment/movies/nagarjuna-akkineni-interview-for-director-sekhar-kammulas-kuberaa-and-lokesh-kanagarajs-coolie-starring-rajinikanth/article69704154.ece)).

## A four-decade Telugu-cinema career

Nagarjuna's lead debut *Vikram* (1986) was a success that launched him, and *Majnu* (1987) plus the blockbuster *Aakhari Poratam* (1988) — in which he starred opposite Sridevi and Suhasini — consolidated his standing before 1989 made him a star twice over: Mani Ratnam's romantic drama *Geetanjali*, which won the National Film Award for Best Popular Film Providing Wholesome Entertainment, and Ram Gopal Varma's debut action film *Shiva*, produced by Annapurna Studios and considered a trendsetter in Telugu cinema ([Cinemaazi](https://www.cinemaazi.com/people/nagarjuna?ctr=323&filterBy=n&search=&people=)). His Hindi debut followed in 1990 with *Shiva*'s Hindi remake. A willingness to experiment with scripts earned him the unofficial nickname "Celluloid Scientist" ([Cinemaazi](https://www.cinemaazi.com/people/nagarjuna?ctr=323&filterBy=n&search=&people=)). As producer-star of the Annapurna Studios banner he made *Ninne Pelladata* (1996), which won the National Film Award for Best Feature Film in Telugu, and the next year he played the 15th-century poet Annamacharya in *Annamayya*, winning a Filmfare Best Actor, his first Nandi Best Actor and a National Award ([Cinemaazi](https://www.cinemaazi.com/people/nagarjuna?ctr=323&filterBy=n&search=&people=)). Cinemaazi's career tally: nine Nandi Awards, three Filmfare Awards South and two National Film Awards ([Cinemaazi](https://www.cinemaazi.com/people/nagarjuna?ctr=323&filterBy=n&search=&people=)). His biographical roles form their own strand — *Rajanna* (2011), *Shirdi Sai* (2012), *Om Namo Venkatesaya* (2017) — and on television he hosted the Telugu *Who Wants to Be a Millionaire*, *Meelo Evaru Koteeswarudu*, and multiple seasons of *Bigg Boss Telugu* ([Cinemaazi](https://www.cinemaazi.com/people/nagarjuna?ctr=323&filterBy=n&search=&people=)).

## The Annapurna Studios steward

Off-screen, Nagarjuna is the co-owner of Annapurna Studios, the Hyderabad complex his father established — foundation stone laid in 1975, opened in 1976, named for his mother — which helped shift Telugu cinema's base from Chennai to Hyderabad and today houses [India](/places/india/)'s first Dolby Atmos Premier Sound Mixing Facility ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/entertainment/movies/nagarjuna-akkineni-on-his-father-anrs-dream-as-annapurna-studios-celebrates-its-50th-year/article69481405.ece)). *Shiva* (1989), which made him a star, was itself an Annapurna Studios production and his first film shot entirely in Hyderabad ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/entertainment/movies/nagarjuna-akkineni-on-his-father-anrs-dream-as-annapurna-studios-celebrates-its-50th-year/article69481405.ece)). Nagarjuna played an active role in founding the studio's film school, inaugurated in 2011; his wife Amala Akkineni took it over after Nageswara Rao's death ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/entertainment/movies/nagarjuna-akkineni-on-his-father-anrs-dream-as-annapurna-studios-celebrates-its-50th-year/article69481405.ece)). Of the school's 600 alumni, more than 200 are actively engaged in filmmaking — a hit rate suggesting the institution is doing exactly what it was built for: producing working filmmakers, not just graduates ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/entertainment/movies/nagarjuna-akkineni-on-his-father-anrs-dream-as-annapurna-studios-celebrates-its-50th-year/article69481405.ece)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the "Nagarjuna" at the IFFI animation launch definitely the Telugu actor?**
Yes. The Tribune's report names only "Nagarjuna," but the ANI wire on the same event — identical quote, identical attendee list — is headlined "Nagarjuna Akkineni" and describes "veteran actor Nagarjuna" in Goa for the 55th IFFI. The matching roster (I&B Secretary Sanjay Jaju, Prasar Bharati's Gaurav Dwivedi, CBC's Yogesh Baweja, Graphiti's Munjal Shroff and Tilak Shetty) pins the identity beyond doubt.

**What are Nagarjuna Akkineni's most important films?**
*Geetanjali* and *Shiva* (1989) made him a star; *Ninne Pelladata* (1996), which he produced and starred in, won the National Film Award for Best Feature Film in Telugu; *Annamayya* (1997), in which he played the 15th-century poet Annamacharya, brought him a Filmfare Best Actor, his first Nandi Best Actor and a National Award.

**What is his connection to Annapurna Studios?**
He is its co-owner. The studio was founded by his father Akkineni Nageswara Rao (opened 1976) and named after Nagarjuna's mother. Nagarjuna drove the creation of its film school, inaugurated in 2011 and now run by his wife Amala Akkineni, with more than 200 of its 600 alumni actively working in filmmaking.

**Is he still active in films?**
Yes. In 2025 he played Deepak, a middle-class man, in Sekhar Kammula's *Kuberaa* opposite Dhanush, Rashmika Mandanna and Jim Sarbh, and played the antagonist opposite Rajinikanth in Lokesh Kanagaraj's *Coolie*. He told The Hindu he would soon begin his 100th film after more than four decades in the industry.
