## Overview

Al Goldstein is an American serial entrepreneur and the co-founder and CEO of [Avant](/organizations/avant/), the Chicago consumer fintech founded in 2012 as AvantCredit, who previously built and sold the online lender CashNetUSA — the business that became Enova International — and co-founded the Chicago real-estate company Pangea Properties ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html); [C-Suite Quarterly](https://csq.com/2026/10/building-a-business-that-outlives-its-founders-al-goldsteins-realization-of-his-own-american-dream/)). Born in [Uzbekistan](/places/uzbekistan/) when it was still part of the Soviet Union, he immigrated to Chicago with his family at age eight, studied finance at the University of Illinois, and spent about fourteen months as a Deutsche Bank investment banker before leaving at 23 to start his first company ([C-Suite Quarterly](https://csq.com/2026/10/building-a-business-that-outlives-its-founders-al-goldsteins-realization-of-his-own-american-dream/)).

Goldstein's career is a study in returning to the same problem with better tools. His first company digitised credit for underbanked Americans; his third, Avant, does the same for middle-income "near-prime" borrowers with machine learning instead of a credit score alone ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/avantcredit-secures-20m-to-make-financial-loans-more-accessible-with-big-data)). Between the two exits sits the part founder profiles usually skip: a failed sports bar, a failed Russian e-commerce bet, and a public contraction at Avant in 2016 ([Gies College of Business, University of Illinois](https://giesbusiness.illinois.edu/news/2018/11/12/goldstein-delivers-cozad-lecture-on-roller-coaster-career-of-serial-entrepreneurship)). The through-line, in his own words, is to "build a business that will stand the test of time, that will outlive us" ([C-Suite Quarterly](https://csq.com/2026/10/building-a-business-that-outlives-its-founders-al-goldsteins-realization-of-his-own-american-dream/)).

## From Uzbekistan to Deutsche Bank

Goldstein was born in Uzbekistan when it was still part of the Soviet Union, and his family immigrated to Chicago when he was eight years old; the University of Illinois dates the emigration to 1988 ([C-Suite Quarterly](https://csq.com/2026/10/building-a-business-that-outlives-its-founders-al-goldsteins-realization-of-his-own-american-dream/); [Gies College of Business, University of Illinois](https://giesbusiness.illinois.edu/news/2018/11/12/goldstein-delivers-cozad-lecture-on-roller-coaster-career-of-serial-entrepreneurship)).

At the University of Illinois he studied finance and mathematics, graduating in 2002, and started his career as an investment banker at Deutsche Bank in New York, working in leveraged finance ([Gies College of Business, University of Illinois](https://giesbusiness.illinois.edu/news/2018/11/12/goldstein-delivers-cozad-lecture-on-roller-coaster-career-of-serial-entrepreneurship); [C-Suite Quarterly](https://csq.com/2026/10/building-a-business-that-outlives-its-founders-al-goldsteins-realization-of-his-own-american-dream/)). The hours were, in his telling, insane, and the conventional path did not hold his attention: "I lasted about 14 months." He left banking to join a mentor and former boss — David Shorr, for whom he had interned in college — in starting a business, with his brother as co-founder and technical lead ([Sramana Mitra](https://www.sramanamitra.com/2014/10/30/aiming-to-disrupt-consumer-credit-al-goldstein-ceo-of-avantcredit-part-1/); [C-Suite Quarterly](https://csq.com/2026/10/building-a-business-that-outlives-its-founders-al-goldsteins-realization-of-his-own-american-dream/)).

## CashNetUSA and the first exit

The business Goldstein started in late 2003 and early 2004 was CashNetUSA, an online lender built on a simple idea for its time: digitise credit for underbanked consumers in the [United States](/places/united-states/) ([StartupFundraising](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/al-goldstein); [Gies College of Business, University of Illinois](https://giesbusiness.illinois.edu/news/2018/11/12/goldstein-delivers-cozad-lecture-on-roller-coaster-career-of-serial-entrepreneurship)). Goldstein served as its president and CEO; his brother, a software engineer, was co-founder and the technical mind, and a close friend from high school served as chief operating officer ([Sramana Mitra](https://www.sramanamitra.com/2014/10/30/aiming-to-disrupt-consumer-credit-al-goldstein-ceo-of-avantcredit-part-1/)). The company grew from zero to about 500 employees in roughly four years on only $4 million of equity capital, and was sold in 2006 to the payday lender Cash America for about $250 million ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html); [StartupFundraising](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/al-goldstein)). Goldstein stayed on to run the business for about two years through the takeout period and left at the end of 2008 ([Sramana Mitra](https://www.sramanamitra.com/2014/10/30/aiming-to-disrupt-consumer-credit-al-goldstein-ceo-of-avantcredit-part-1/)). The business later became part of Enova International, now a publicly traded financial-technology company ([C-Suite Quarterly](https://csq.com/2026/10/building-a-business-that-outlives-its-founders-al-goldsteins-realization-of-his-own-american-dream/)). The early exit taught Goldstein the wrong lesson, by his own account: "It made it seem really easy to build and scale a business. I've learned since building companies is really hard, and everything takes at least 10 years" ([C-Suite Quarterly](https://csq.com/2026/10/building-a-business-that-outlives-its-founders-al-goldsteins-realization-of-his-own-american-dream/)).

## The failures: District Bar and BayRu

After the CashNetUSA sale, Goldstein tried to build the next generation of sports bar. He started District Bar in 2006 and led it for three years, but quickly realised he had no passion for the industry, found it difficult to hire quality talent, and watched too many competitors open similar businesses ([Gies College of Business, University of Illinois](https://giesbusiness.illinois.edu/news/2018/11/12/goldstein-delivers-cozad-lecture-on-roller-coaster-career-of-serial-entrepreneurship)).

A second failure followed a different pattern: in 2012 he joined BayRu as a lead investor. BayRu helped Russian consumers buy products from eBay, and the business grew dramatically until the Russian ruble crashed, making American goods much more expensive for Russian consumers ([Gies College of Business, University of Illinois](https://giesbusiness.illinois.edu/news/2018/11/12/goldstein-delivers-cozad-lecture-on-roller-coaster-career-of-serial-entrepreneurship)). One failed on fit and execution, the other on a currency shock no operator could control.

## Pangea Properties: real estate in the recession

Goldstein got back on his feet with his brother and their chief operating officer, buying distressed real-estate assets and applying better management to apartments for middle-income consumers, starting in Chicago ([Sramana Mitra](https://www.sramanamitra.com/2014/10/30/aiming-to-disrupt-consumer-credit-al-goldstein-ceo-of-avantcredit-part-1/)). The company, Pangea Properties, was co-founded in the heart of the Great Recession — the University of Illinois dates it to 2009; a 2013 company announcement says 2008 — and grew to more than 15,000 apartment units with nearly 500 workers, with Goldstein as executive chairman ([Gies College of Business, University of Illinois](https://giesbusiness.illinois.edu/news/2018/11/12/goldstein-delivers-cozad-lecture-on-roller-coaster-career-of-serial-entrepreneurship)). Pangea ranked No. 720 on the Inc. 5000 ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)).

The fundraising grind behind Pangea is the detail that best explains Goldstein's later discipline: he was rejected by 350 investors the first time he tried to raise capital for Pangea, and stuck with it anyway — the second of two lessons he drew for students in 2018, alongside "it's all about the people" ([Gies College of Business, University of Illinois](https://giesbusiness.illinois.edu/news/2018/11/12/goldstein-delivers-cozad-lecture-on-roller-coaster-career-of-serial-entrepreneurship)).

## Avant: the near-prime bet

Goldstein founded Avant — originally AvantCredit — in 2012 in Chicago with [John Sun](/people/john-sun/) and [Paul Zhang](/people/paul-zhang/), two former Enova interns who had gone through [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/) with Debteye, a credit-counselling automation startup ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/avantcredit-secures-20m-to-make-financial-loans-more-accessible-with-big-data); [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)). He started it when his oldest child was six months old; his wife, a corporate attorney, stepped in to support the operation legally ([C-Suite Quarterly](https://csq.com/2026/10/building-a-business-that-outlives-its-founders-al-goldsteins-realization-of-his-own-american-dream/)). The platform launched in July 2013, and that year the company raised a $20 million round led by August Capital and Victory Park Capital, on top of an earlier $9 million in equity and $25 million in debt ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/avantcredit-secures-20m-to-make-financial-loans-more-accessible-with-big-data)).

The thesis was the gap his first company had circled from the other side: near-prime borrowers — people with FICO scores between 580 and 700 — whom subprime lenders would not serve and big banks had abandoned since the 2008 financial crisis ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)). AvantCredit's platform used machine learning to determine lending risk from dozens of data sources captured during the application, searching thousands of data attributes, and could approve and fund a loan of up to $10,000 in near real time ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/avantcredit-secures-20m-to-make-financial-loans-more-accessible-with-big-data)).

Growth was fast and then, unusually, publicly reversed. By April 2015 Avant had originated $800 million in loans to 200,000 customers, with $300 million in equity from investors including Peter Thiel, KKR and Tiger Global and $700 million in debt capital ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html)). The company grew from zero to 1,000 employees in its first three years — then, in 2016, Goldstein had to shrink it. "Both in people and surface area, we had to cut back," he said in 2026, calling the experience humbling for a founder accustomed to growth ([C-Suite Quarterly](https://csq.com/2026/10/building-a-business-that-outlives-its-founders-al-goldsteins-realization-of-his-own-american-dream/); [Gies College of Business, University of Illinois](https://giesbusiness.illinois.edu/news/2018/11/12/goldstein-delivers-cozad-lecture-on-roller-coaster-career-of-serial-entrepreneurship)). By 2018 the company sat at around 600 employees with $5 billion in loan origination ([Gies College of Business, University of Illinois](https://giesbusiness.illinois.edu/news/2018/11/12/goldstein-delivers-cozad-lecture-on-roller-coaster-career-of-serial-entrepreneurship)). In June 2015 Goldstein won the EY Entrepreneur of the Year Award ([C-Suite Quarterly](https://csq.com/2026/10/building-a-business-that-outlives-its-founders-al-goldsteins-realization-of-his-own-american-dream/)).

## Amount, the bank charter, and what comes next

Avant's most consequential second act began as an accident of its banking relationships. While partnering with banks, Goldstein's team discovered that some institutions were less interested in sending customers to Avant than in licensing the technology Avant had built, as banks faced pressure to modernise their digital offerings ([C-Suite Quarterly](https://csq.com/2026/10/building-a-business-that-outlives-its-founders-al-goldsteins-realization-of-his-own-american-dream/)). That technology was commercialised through Amount, spun out of Avant in January 2020 ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/fis-acquires-fintech-amount-banking-payments-deal/761022/)). Amount raised $81 million later in 2020 in a round led by Goldman Sachs, was valued at $1 billion the following year, and acquired the small-business lending platform Linear Financial Technologies for $175 million in February 2022 ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/fis-acquires-fintech-amount-banking-payments-deal/761022/)). On 24 September 2025, FIS completed its acquisition of Amount on undisclosed terms, with all 158 employees joining FIS and the Chicago location maintained ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/fis-acquires-fintech-amount-banking-payments-deal/761022/)).

Goldstein's next ambition is the one his 2026 profile leads with: Avant has applied for a bank charter, seeking government approval to operate as a bank rather than only as a fintech company ([C-Suite Quarterly](https://csq.com/2026/10/building-a-business-that-outlives-its-founders-al-goldsteins-realization-of-his-own-american-dream/)). "There's a joke that all banks want to be fintechs, and all fintechs want to be banks," he said. "We believe we're now mature enough to actually be a bank ourselves." A 2026 interview summary puts Avant's totals at about $600 million in equity raised plus billions in debt capital, roughly 650 employees, operations in 46 states, and $6 billion in personal loans provided ([StartupFundraising](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/al-goldstein)). The charter application has no verified decision or date, and this profile asserts only that it has been made.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is Al Goldstein?**
Al Goldstein is a Chicago serial entrepreneur, co-founder and CEO of the consumer fintech Avant, and previously co-founder of CashNetUSA (sold in 2006 for about $250 million, now part of Enova International) and of Pangea Properties ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html); [C-Suite Quarterly](https://csq.com/2026/10/building-a-business-that-outlives-its-founders-al-goldsteins-realization-of-his-own-american-dream/)).

**What did Al Goldstein do before Avant?**
He spent about 14 months at Deutsche Bank in leveraged finance, then co-founded CashNetUSA in 2004 and sold it in 2006, ran the failed sports-bar venture District Bar for three years, and co-founded Pangea Properties, a Chicago real-estate company that grew to more than 15,000 apartments ([Gies College of Business, University of Illinois](https://giesbusiness.illinois.edu/news/2018/11/12/goldstein-delivers-cozad-lecture-on-roller-coaster-career-of-serial-entrepreneurship)).

**How much did CashNetUSA sell for?**
About $250 million in 2006, to the public company Cash America, after raising only about $4 million in equity capital ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jeremy-quittner/2015-30-under-30-avant.html); [StartupFundraising](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/al-goldstein)). One trade-press item gives $265 million; this profile follows the $250 million figure.

**What is Amount, and what happened to it?**
Amount was the banking-technology business spun out of Avant in January 2020 so banks could license Avant's lending technology; FIS completed its acquisition of Amount on 24 September 2025, with all 158 employees joining FIS ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/fis-acquires-fintech-amount-banking-payments-deal/761022/)).

**Is Avant becoming a bank?**
Avant has applied for a bank charter, according to an October 2026 profile of Goldstein — but no approval, decision or date has been verified, so the application is all this profile asserts ([C-Suite Quarterly](https://csq.com/2026/10/building-a-business-that-outlives-its-founders-al-goldsteins-realization-of-his-own-american-dream/)).
