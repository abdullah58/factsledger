## Overview

Curtis Sliwa's half-century as New York's red-beret icon ended where it had been built: on talk radio. On October 22, 2025, in the middle of a heated on-air clash with host Sid Rosenberg on "Sid & Friends in the Morning," Sliwa quit 77 WABC live — "You will never see me ever in the studios of WABC again, never" — after station owner John Catsimatidis publicly demanded that he drop out of the mayoral race to consolidate votes behind former governor [Andrew Cuomo](/people/andrew-cuomo) ([Inside Radio](https://www.insideradio.com/free/curtis-sliwa-walks-away-from-wabc-after-heated-on-air-clash/article_35a42d6d-2739-4e30-8988-411b2df2362c.html)). It was the unraveling of a nearly three-decade broadcasting career on the same station that had made him a conservative-media fixture, in defense of a second consecutive losing run that would finish at just 7% of the vote, dogged by accusations that his presence on the ballot made victory harder for the moderate Democrat ([American Enterprise Institute](https://www.aei.org/commentary/qa-curtis-sliwa/)). Two years earlier, the same combative style had produced another live-television spectacle: during a Fox News interview with Sean Hannity, members of Sliwa's Guardian Angels physically accosted a man they accused of being a "migrant" and a shoplifter — grabbing him in a headlock — when he was in fact a Bronx resident, and the NYPD found no evidence he had been shoplifting ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Guardian-Angels)). Those two live-broadcast bookends — the false accusation in 2024, the resignation in 2025 — frame the late career of a man who had spent decades cultivating an untouchable street persona, and they are the part of his story that most accounts have not developed.

## The subway vigilante, founded from a McDonald's night shift

Sliwa was born in Brooklyn in 1954 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Guardian-Angels)). As a student newspaper account records, he and a group of friends first formed a patrol unit known as the "Magnificent 13" in 1977, before it evolved into the Guardian Angels ([The Badger Online](https://thebadgeronline.com/2025/12/the-guardian-angel-who-ran-for-mayor-in-new-york-a-tale-of-cats-crime-and-campaigns/)). The official founding came in 1979: Sliwa, then 24 and working as the night manager of a Bronx McDonald's, was disturbed by crime, gang activity and drug use on the New York City subway, and — inspired by 1970s film antiheroes like *Death Wish*'s Paul Kersey and *Taxi Driver*'s Travis Bickle — convinced a multiracial group of his McDonald's coworkers to ride the subways with him at night. "I wasn't going to live vicariously through these vigilante movies," he later explained in the 2018 documentary *Vigilante: The Incredible True Story of Curtis Sliwa and the Guardian Angels* ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Guardian-Angels)). Once the group began actively patrolling, membership grew "from 13 to 1,000…within a period of a year," by his own account in a 2024 interview ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Guardian-Angels)).

The Angels became instantly recognizable for their uniform — red beret, black pants, and a white T-shirt or red jacket carrying the organization's winged Eye of Providence logo, an eye inside a triangle surrounded by rays of light — and for their operating rules: unarmed, patrolling in groups of eight, trained in self-defense, first aid, mental-health awareness and homeless outreach, with a minimum joining age of 16 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Guardian-Angels)). The organization eventually claimed chapters in U.S. cities from Los Angeles to Washington, D.C., and in countries including the Dominican Republic, Italy, South Korea, Japan, India and Brazil, plus a division dedicated to animal protection ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Guardian-Angels)). Early on, New York mayor Ed Koch was suspicious of Sliwa's intentions but later approved of the group's work; the city police department was less friendly, especially after Sliwa claimed that three off-duty officers had kidnapped and threatened him over "taking their jobs" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Guardian-Angels)).

The fame had a fabricated side that Sliwa himself confessed to. In 1992 he revealed that six of the Guardian Angels' successful crime-busting activities were hoaxes, including the rescue of alleged mugging and rape victims; cofounder Tony Mao admitted he had once drenched himself in gasoline and claimed it had happened when he pounced on two men planning to attack a subway token-booth clerk — stunts the group had rehearsed in the McDonald's where they worked ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Guardian-Angels)). The same year brought the real violence. On June 19, 1992, Sliwa survived an assassination attempt while riding in a yellow taxi on the way to work: one account describes him being shot five times at point-blank range before escaping through the car window and spending days in emergency care ([The Badger Online](https://thebadgeronline.com/2025/12/the-guardian-angel-who-ran-for-mayor-in-new-york-a-tale-of-cats-crime-and-campaigns/)). Sliwa had long targeted the Gambino crime family and its boss John Gotti in the media; at the 2005 racketeering trial of Gotti's son John Angelo Gotti, Sliwa testified that he had been locked inside the cab and shot three times by an agent of the Gottis before escaping through the cab's window. John Angelo Gotti was tried for the alleged kidnapping along with RICO charges, but after three mistrials prosecutors stopped pursuing a case against him ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Guardian-Angels)). As crime in New York decreased through the 1990s, Guardian Angels membership collapsed — the New York chapter was down to roughly 30 to 125 members by 1992 — and the organization shifted toward internet safety with a CyberAngels program founded in 1995 and youth mentoring ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Guardian-Angels)).

## Two runs for City Hall and the radio break

Sliwa ran for mayor of New York City on the Republican ballot in 2021 and again in 2025 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Guardian-Angels)). The 2025 run was also carried on the Protect Animals ballot — an independent party Sliwa created himself — reflecting a second public identity as an animal-rights activist ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Guardian-Angels); [American Enterprise Institute](https://www.aei.org/commentary/qa-curtis-sliwa/)). His animal platform called for expanding no-kill dog shelters, converting vacant storefronts into adoption centers and reevaluating the role of certain zoos — and it was personal: at one point he cared for as many as seventeen rescue cats in his own home ([The Badger Online](https://thebadgeronline.com/2025/12/the-guardian-angel-who-ran-for-mayor-in-new-york-a-tale-of-cats-crime-and-campaigns/)).

The 2025 campaign turned into a siege on his candidacy. Andrew Cuomo — who had lost the Democratic primary to the self-described socialist [Zohran Mamdani](/people/zohran-mamdani) and was running as an independent — needed Sliwa's voters, and Catsimatidis said so bluntly: Sliwa "would make the best mayor," but he had "no shot," and "If Curtis drops out, Cuomo has a path. If not, Mamdani wins" ([The Jewish Voice](https://tjvnews.com/news/politics/curtis-sliwa-quits-wabc-in-fiery-on-air-clash-amid-growing-pressure-to-drop-out-of-nyc-mayoral-race/)). Sliwa, who had been on leave from his radio show to campaign, took the demand as a betrayal from a station he had helped build: "I know there are people there at WABC who are personally attacking me, saying I'm running a lazy campaign," he said, adding, "WABC doesn't stand for 'We Are Broadcasting Conservative.' It stands for 'Always Broadcast Cuomo.'" ([The Jewish Voice](https://tjvnews.com/news/politics/curtis-sliwa-quits-wabc-in-fiery-on-air-clash-amid-growing-pressure-to-drop-out-of-nyc-mayoral-race/)). GOP chairs across all five boroughs stayed with him — including Catsimatidis' own daughter, Manhattan Republican Party chair Andrea Catsimatidis, who said she was "standing firmly with Curtis" ([The Jewish Voice](https://tjvnews.com/news/politics/curtis-sliwa-quits-wabc-in-fiery-on-air-clash-amid-growing-pressure-to-drop-out-of-nyc-mayoral-race/)).

The final break came on October 22, hours before the second and final mayoral debate. New polling showed Mamdani leading by 14 points in the three-way race — but by only four points in a head-to-head matchup against Cuomo if Sliwa were to withdraw ([Inside Radio](https://www.insideradio.com/free/curtis-sliwa-walks-away-from-wabc-after-heated-on-air-clash/article_35a42d6d-2739-4e30-8988-411b2df2362c.html)). On "Sid & Friends in the Morning," Rosenberg pressed Sliwa to step aside so Cuomo could consolidate moderate votes and block Mamdani; Sliwa said the "frenzy" created by his WABC colleagues had endangered him and his wife, Nancy — "I'm hurt. My life is on the line here… If anything happens to me or anything happens to my wife, it's on you guys" — and walked out mid-broadcast ([Inside Radio](https://www.insideradio.com/free/curtis-sliwa-walks-away-from-wabc-after-heated-on-air-clash/article_35a42d6d-2739-4e30-8988-411b2df2362c.html); [The Jewish Voice](https://tjvnews.com/news/politics/curtis-sliwa-quits-wabc-in-fiery-on-air-clash-amid-growing-pressure-to-drop-out-of-nyc-mayoral-race/)). Catsimatidis downplayed it — "I love the guy. Did he have a tough morning? Yeah. It's easy to have a tough morning with Sid… Crap happens. People say a lot of things when they're upset" — and emphasized that Sliwa remained under a 30-year contract and was welcome back anytime: "Curtis is always welcome at WABC. He's part of our family" ([Inside Radio](https://www.insideradio.com/free/curtis-sliwa-walks-away-from-wabc-after-heated-on-air-clash/article_35a42d6d-2739-4e30-8988-411b2df2362c.html)).

Sliwa refused to quit the race and finished with 7% of the vote, dogged to the end by accusations that his presence made Cuomo's win harder; Mamdani went on to win the election ([American Enterprise Institute](https://www.aei.org/commentary/qa-curtis-sliwa/)). On December 29, he sat for a Wall Street Journal interview at a diner on Manhattan's Upper West Side — frequently interrupted, the summary notes, by New Yorkers asking to shake his hand — the red beret still on his head at all times ([American Enterprise Institute](https://www.aei.org/commentary/qa-curtis-sliwa/)). In the 2010s he had resumed leading Guardian Angels patrols on the subways after a series of violent train crimes, including knife attacks; in December 2024 the group announced intensified subway patrols after a woman was set on fire while sleeping on a train in Brooklyn, with founding member Arnaldo Salinas telling the New York Post the group screens out the violent-minded: "If you're one of these Bruce Lee wannabes…we can't use you" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Guardian-Angels)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not assert a specific day or month of birth — only the year, 1954, which Britannica reports. It does not repeat snippet-level claims it could not verify in a full read: the number of marriages, the children, the Courage of Conscience Award, the alleged admission of filing fake police reports for publicity, the Canarsie upbringing and school history, or the reported health battles. It does not treat the allegation that wealthy New Yorkers tried to bribe Sliwa out of the 2025 race as established — the source read for this profile carries it only as his own unsubstantiated claim. It presents the two different accounts of the 1992 shooting (five shots at point-blank range in a student-press account; three shots in his 2005 trial testimony) as reported, not reconciled. And it does not state the final outcome of the WABC falling-out beyond what the sources confirm — that Catsimatidis said Sliwa remained under contract and welcome to return — because the sources do not confirm whether he ever went back.
