## Overview

Sualeh Asif is the Karachi-born co-founder and chief product officer of [Anysphere](/organizations/anysphere/), the San Francisco company behind Cursor, the AI code editor that lets developers use natural language to write code, fix bugs, build features and restructure entire systems ([The News](https://www.thenews.pk/print/1428477-pakistan-s-cursor-moment)). Before Cursor, he represented Pakistan at the International Mathematical Olympiad from 2016 to 2018 and studied at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](/universities/massachusetts-institute-of-technology/) ([The News](https://www.thenews.pk/story/1411713-pakistan-born-co-founder-s-ai-startup-draws-60bn-buyout-option-from-spacex); [The News](https://www.thenews.pk/print/1428477-pakistan-s-cursor-moment)).

Asif's path to Silicon Valley began in Karachi. At MIT he also co-authored a published proof that the video game Tetris stays computationally hard even on narrow boards, a piece of theoretical work that foreshadowed the product role he would take at Cursor ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.14336)). He attended Nixor College in Karachi before going to MIT ([The News](https://www.thenews.pk/story/1411713-pakistan-born-co-founder-s-ai-startup-draws-60bn-buyout-option-from-spacex)). The News describes him, in July 2026, as a 26-year-old from Karachi who co-founded Anysphere ([The News](https://www.thenews.pk/print/1428477-pakistan-s-cursor-moment)). Gold House, a professional organisation profiling Asian Pacific founders, states that he attended MIT on a scholarship and that he leads the product vision and user experience for Cursor ([Gold House](https://goldhouse.org/people/aman-sanger-sualeh-asif/)). TechJuice adds a detail the newspaper profiles skip: Asif spent over three years working as a research assistant at MIT before co-founding Cursor in 2022, and his focus there was the core systems behind Cursor's AI coding engine, the technology that lets developers describe what they want in plain language while the system generates working code, fixes bugs and suggests improvements ([TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/sualeh-asif-aman-sanger-cursor-billionaires-spacex/)).

## A mathematician before he was a founder

Asif's mathematical record is the least generic part of his biography, and it is documented in two very different kinds of source: a national newspaper and a research paper. The News reports that he represented Pakistan in the International Mathematical Olympiad from 2016 to 2018 ([The News](https://www.thenews.pk/story/1411713-pakistan-born-co-founder-s-ai-startup-draws-60bn-buyout-option-from-spacex)), and its later opinion piece repeats that he represented Pakistan at the Olympiad before going to MIT, where he studied machine learning, number theory and performance engineering ([The News](https://www.thenews.pk/print/1428477-pakistan-s-cursor-moment)). Gold House and TechJuice both add that he won a bronze medal during that three-year span ([Gold House](https://goldhouse.org/people/aman-sanger-sualeh-asif/); [TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/sualeh-asif-aman-sanger-cursor-billionaires-spacex/)). That medal claim is attributed here rather than stated flatly, because at least one careful guide to Asif's biography warns that medal specifics are inconsistently sourced across the web.

The research paper is firmer ground, and it is the part of Asif's story that most founder profiles skip entirely. As first-named author, with Michael Coulombe, Erik D. Demaine, Martin L. Demaine, Adam Hesterberg, Jayson Lynch and Mihir Singhal, Asif published "Tetris is NP-hard even with O(1) rows or columns" in 2020 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.14336)). The paper proves that the classic falling-block video game Tetris, in both its survival and board-clearing forms, remains NP-complete even when restricted to 8 columns, or to 4 rows, settling open problems posed over 15 years earlier ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.14336)). On the positive side, it proves that 2-column Tetris, and 1-row Tetris, are polynomial, meaning they can be solved efficiently ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.14336)). The contrast matters for understanding Asif's later work: the paper is about knowing exactly where a problem stops being tractable, and Cursor's product problem is the applied version of the same question, knowing where an AI coding assistant can be trusted to act across a whole codebase and where it cannot.

## Building Cursor, and the SpaceX deal

At MIT, Asif met [Michael Truell](/people/michael-truell/), [Arvid Lunnemark](/people/arvid-lunnemark/) and [Aman Sanger](/people/aman-sanger/), and the four co-founded Anysphere in 2022 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/11/anysphere-raises-8m-from-openai-to-build-an-ai-powered-ide/); [TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/sualeh-asif-aman-sanger-cursor-billionaires-spacex/)). Cursor, their product, is a fork of VS Code, Microsoft's open-source code editor, packing AI-powered tools designed to help developers write code and ask questions about it ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/11/anysphere-raises-8m-from-openai-to-build-an-ai-powered-ide/)). In October 2023 Anysphere announced an $8 million seed round led by OpenAI's Startup Fund, bringing its total raised to $11 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/11/anysphere-raises-8m-from-openai-to-build-an-ai-powered-ide/)). The News describes what Cursor does differently from earlier tools: earlier tools either required manual coding or could only suggest small pieces of code for specific tasks, leaving developers to integrate those pieces manually, while Cursor can make coordinated changes across multiple files, maintain consistency and execute complex instructions end-to-end ([The News](https://www.thenews.pk/print/1428477-pakistan-s-cursor-moment)).

The company's rise was fast. In November 2025 Anysphere closed a $2.3 billion Series D backed by Accel, Thrive Capital, Andreessen Horowitz, DST Global, Coatue, Nvidia and Google, setting a $29.3 billion post-money valuation, more than three times what it had secured just six months earlier ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/biggest-funding-rounds-ai-defense-tech-anysphere/)). The News reported in April 2026 that the company had over $1 billion in annualised revenue, making it one of the fastest-growing AI startups, according to Forbes ([The News](https://www.thenews.pk/story/1411713-pakistan-born-co-founder-s-ai-startup-draws-60bn-buyout-option-from-spacex)). As chief product officer, Asif leads the product vision and user experience for Cursor ([Gold House](https://goldhouse.org/people/aman-sanger-sualeh-asif/)), and TechJuice reports that he owns roughly 4.5% of the company, a stake Forbes estimates will be worth approximately $2.7 billion once the [SpaceX](/organizations/spacex/) deal closes ([TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/sualeh-asif-aman-sanger-cursor-billionaires-spacex/)).

That deal arrived in April 2026, when SpaceX entered into a partnership with Cursor, with a potential $60 billion buyout planned later in the year ([The News](https://www.thenews.pk/story/1411713-pakistan-born-co-founder-s-ai-startup-draws-60bn-buyout-option-from-spacex)). The News' later piece puts Asif's paper value at about $2.7 billion after SpaceX's reported $60 billion deal to buy Anysphere ([The News](https://www.thenews.pk/print/1428477-pakistan-s-cursor-moment)). The acquisition, confirmed in a filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, was expected to close by the third quarter of 2026, with each of Cursor's four co-founders projected to be worth roughly $2.7 billion once the transaction completes ([TechJuice](https://www.techjuice.pk/sualeh-asif-aman-sanger-cursor-billionaires-spacex/)). That is the stat-plus-source-plus-implication at the heart of Asif's profile: a Karachi schoolboy who competed for Pakistan in mathematics olympiads, then proved theorems about Tetris at MIT, now holds a reported stake worth billions in the company whose product he leads.
