## Overview

Before Neil Patel was a media founder, he was a White House man. In the George W. Bush administration, Patel was a longtime deputy to Lewis "Scooter" Libby, the chief of staff to Vice President Dick Cheney — and when Patel rose, he rose high. He became the vice president's chief policy adviser, later his staff secretary: the aide who represented Cheney at economic and domestic policy meetings, managed his policy staff, and controlled the movement of the vice president's classified documents ([AmericanKahani](https://americankahani.com/business/neil-patel-ceo-of-tucker-carlson-network-started-out-in-dc-as-a-policy-advisor-to-vice-president-dick-cheney/)). The Columbia Journalism Review, in its 2012 account of [The Daily Caller](/organizations/the-daily-caller/)'s founding, described Patel's standing as of late 2008 as Cheney's primary adviser on domestic and economic policy ([CJR](https://archives.cjr.org/feature/the_great_right_hype.php)).

The Bush White House valued him enough to nominate him to run the National Telecommunications and Information Administration, the Commerce Department agency that manages federal spectrum and broadband policy. He was not confirmed ([AmericanKahani](https://americankahani.com/business/neil-patel-ceo-of-tucker-carlson-network-started-out-in-dc-as-a-policy-advisor-to-vice-president-dick-cheney/)). Before his White House years, Patel practiced law with Dechert Price & Rhoads and served as counsel to the House Select Committee on U.S. National Security and Military/Commercial Concerns with the People's Republic of China; he holds a B.A. from [Trinity College](/schools/trinity-college/), Hartford, and a J.D. from Georgetown University Law Center, where he was associate editor of the Journal of Law and Policy in International Business ([AmericanKahani](https://americankahani.com/business/neil-patel-ceo-of-tucker-carlson-network-started-out-in-dc-as-a-policy-adviser-to-vice-president-dick-cheney/)).

That is the résumé almost nobody on the search results mentions. Nearly every leading result introduces Patel as what he became afterward: Tucker Carlson's college roommate and the business brain behind the conservative news site the two of them built. The roommate story is real — the Trinity Tripod, the college's student newspaper, confirms that "Neil Patel, the publisher and co-founder of 'The Daily Caller,' is a Trinity alumnus who was Carlson's roommate while they were undergraduates," and that the two graduated in 1991 ([Trinity Tripod](https://tripod.domains.trincoll.edu/2016/11/15/tucker-carlson-92-begins-nightly-fox-news-segment/)). But the White House arc is the one that explains why Carlson kept trusting him with everything.

## The Palm dinner and the $3 million bet

The Daily Caller was born at the Palm restaurant in Washington, just before the 2008 election, at a moment when both men's careers were at hinge points. Carlson had just been fired by MSNBC; Patel, thinking about life after the Bush administration, had already been turning over the idea of starting a website ([CJR](https://archives.cjr.org/feature/the_great_right_hype.php)). "Nobody had figured out the news business model yet," Patel told CJR years later. "The Huffington Post had clearly discovered it for the liberal audience, and that left a gap on the Right" ([CJR](https://archives.cjr.org/feature/the_great_right_hype.php)).

It was Patel who turned a dinner conversation into a funded company. He wrote the business plan and raised the money: $3 million from Foster Friess, the Wyoming GOP donor and mutual-fund manager, who would become the site's anchor investor alongside the two founders ([CJR](https://archives.cjr.org/feature/the_great_right_hype.php)). Carlson's job was the public pitch — at CPAC in February 2009 he announced the site's mission and staff to the conservative movement's annual gathering ([CJR](https://archives.cjr.org/feature/the_great_right_hype.php)). The offices opened near Dupont Circle, stocked with hires that announced serious intent: Megan Mulligan, the Guardian's Washington editor, as executive editor; Matt Labash of The Weekly Standard; former Slate columnist Mickey Kaus; the commentator Matt Lewis; and Virginia "Ginni" Thomas, the activist who is married to the Supreme Court justice ([CJR](https://archives.cjr.org/feature/the_great_right_hype.php)). By June 2009 there were 32 people on the editorial staff ([CJR](https://archives.cjr.org/feature/the_great_right_hype.php)).

The site launched on January 11, 2010, and The Guardian billed it as "the conservative answer to The Huffington Post" ([CJR](https://archives.cjr.org/feature/the_great_right_hype.php)). CJR called the project "the Great Right Hype" — the attempt to do for the conservative audience what the Huffington Post had done for the liberal one ([CJR](https://archives.cjr.org/feature/the_great_right_hype.php)). Carlson ran the editorial side as editor-in-chief until 2016, then left for his Fox News primetime show; Patel ran the business throughout ([AmericanKahani](https://americankahani.com/business/neil-patel-ceo-of-tucker-carlson-network-started-out-in-dc-as-a-policy-advisor-to-vice-president-dick-cheney/)).

## The buyout and the second act

In June 2020, the partnership split in the only way it could. Carlson sold his roughly one-third stake in The Daily Caller, and Patel bought it out, taking a controlling interest ([InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/internet/tucker-carlson-daily-caller-sold-neil-patel)). Friess remained the site's only other main investor; by then the operation employed around 80 people, with Patel as publisher and, effectively, sole owner besides Friess ([InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/internet/tucker-carlson-daily-caller-sold-neil-patel)). Carlson's parting words about Patel, given to The Wall Street Journal, were the clearest public statement of what the partnership had been: "The only reason the Daily Caller still exists is because the guy running it is one of the very rare conservative intellectuals who understands business. He keeps costs in line with revenue" ([AmericanKahani](https://americankahani.com/business/neil-patel-ceo-of-tucker-carlson-network-started-out-in-dc-as-a-policy-advisor-to-vice-president-dick-cheney/)).

Three years later the two men were back together. After [Fox News](/organizations/fox-news/) fired Carlson in April 2023, the Wall Street Journal reported that Patel was working with Carlson to raise money for a new media company that could "potentially use Twitter as its backbone" ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/tucker-carlson-daily-caller-neil-patel-media-company-twitter/)). The venture aimed to drum up hundreds of millions of dollars for a subscription-driven company: free, short versions of Carlson's content on Twitter, with full access behind a paywall on the company's own website and mobile app, and eventually additional hosts and shows ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/tucker-carlson-daily-caller-neil-patel-media-company-twitter/)). TheWrap described Patel as Carlson's roommate at Trinity College and "the co-founder and publisher" of the site, with a history that "dates to their college days" ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/tucker-carlson-daily-caller-neil-patel-media-company-twitter/)).

The company arrived on December 11, 2023 as the [Tucker Carlson](/people/tucker-carlson/) Network, with Patel as chief executive ([AmericanKahani](https://americankahani.com/business/neil-patel-ceo-of-tucker-carlson-network-started-out-in-dc-as-a-policy-advisor-to-vice-president-dick-cheney/)). It launched at $9 a month or $72 a year, offering longer versions of the videos Carlson had been posting to X, and opened pre-sales to viewers ([AmericanKahani](https://americankahani.com/business/neil-patel-ceo-of-tucker-carlson-network-started-out-in-dc-as-a-policy-advisor-to-vice-president-dick-cheney/)). Patel had, in a sense, come full circle: the White House aide who helped bankroll a media startup from a Palm dinner now ran the subscription network built to monetize the most famous cable news host in America after his firing.

Patel lives in Washington, D.C., and Jackson Hole, Wyoming, with his wife Amy and their children Caroline, Bela and Charlie ([AmericanKahani](https://americankahani.com/business/neil-patel-ceo-of-tucker-carlson-network-started-out-in-dc-as-a-policy-advisor-to-vice-president-dick-cheney/)). His media company shares a parent story with Carlson's — but his own begins with Cheney.
