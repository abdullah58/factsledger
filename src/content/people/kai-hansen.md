## Overview

Kai Hansen is a German entrepreneur and angel investor best known as a co-founder of Lieferando, the food delivery platform that became one of the largest in Germany and was acquired by Takeaway.com in 2014 ([EQS](https://www.eqs-news.com/news/corporate/lieferando-de-takeaway-com-acquires-german-food-delivery-website-lieferando/82f2921c-b51c-4fa6-ad7c-d9ee7d1defca)). After the exit he became a business angel, and he backed [Booksy](/organizations/booksy/) twice: in its $4.2 million Series A in March 2017 and again in its $70 million Series C in January 2021 ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2017/03/03/booksy-series-a/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)). An [Inovo](/organizations/inovo-venture-partners/) podcast description calls him "the founder of Lieferando (merged with Takeaway) and a successful business angel" ([Inovo Podcast](https://www.listennotes.com/et/podcasts/inovo-podcast/7-kai-hansen-how-to-invest-kUoVIpy9BXE/)).

**This Kai Hansen is not Kai Michael Hansen, the power-metal guitarist and vocalist who co-founded Helloween and founded Gamma Ray.** The musician — born 17 January 1963 in Hamburg — dominates search results for the name, which is why the investor's profile is easily mistaken or drowned out. Everything in this profile concerns the Berlin-based entrepreneur and investor, not the musician.

## Lieferando: from university idea to exit

Lieferando began in 2009, when Hansen and Jörg Gerbig approached their friend Christoph Gerber at university with an idea: a food delivery platform that worked well in New York could work in Germany ([Catch the Tornado](https://www.catchthetornado.com/podcast/17-challenge-accepted-with-christoph-gerber)). Gerber, who was still a student, recalled: "my friend Kai and Joerg reached out to me and said, we've got this idea for a food delivery platform that works well in New York, and I was just like, whatever it gets me out of Uni, I'm all in" ([Catch the Tornado](https://www.catchthetornado.com/podcast/17-challenge-accepted-with-christoph-gerber)). The host confirmed the date — "It's the year you founded Lieferando.de" — and Gerber agreed ([Catch the Tornado](https://www.catchthetornado.com/podcast/17-challenge-accepted-with-christoph-gerber)).

The company was built in the shadow of the financial crisis, and money was scarce. The first funding round was €15,000, at a €700,000 valuation, raised from individual investors in increments of €10,000 and €30,000 ([Catch the Tornado](https://www.catchthetornado.com/podcast/17-challenge-accepted-with-christoph-gerber)). "All the big players rejected us. Index rejected us, Exit rejected us, Highland Capital rejected us. Everyone, every big name you see in Europe, they rejected Lieferando. And the only one who kind of believed in us was Macquarie Capital," Gerber recalled, calling the Australian investment bank a key success factor ([Catch the Tornado](https://www.catchthetornado.com/podcast/17-challenge-accepted-with-christoph-gerber)).

What Lieferando did well, Gerber said, was focus on product and operations rather than fundraising theatre. The German marketing team was six or seven people, against around 14 for the same market at Delivery Hero, yet "the output is actually better" — a smaller team went straight to the three or four companies offering the best deals instead of running to all of them ([Catch the Tornado](https://www.catchthetornado.com/podcast/17-challenge-accepted-with-christoph-gerber)). And the team did not look at Germany as a market: it looked at each postcode as a market — some 10,000 markets in total, about 3,000 of them in Germany — and asked how it performed in each one ([Catch the Tornado](https://www.catchthetornado.com/podcast/17-challenge-accepted-with-christoph-gerber)). The three founders were, in Gerber's words, "very, very different, all of us" — not "three McKinsey guys" — and that complementarity "was what made the company successful" ([Catch the Tornado](https://www.catchthetornado.com/podcast/17-challenge-accepted-with-christoph-gerber)).

The exit came in April 2014, when Takeaway.com signed an agreement to acquire Lieferando ([EQS](https://www.eqs-news.com/news/corporate/lieferando-de-takeaway-com-acquires-german-food-delivery-website-lieferando/82f2921c-b51c-4fa6-ad7c-d9ee7d1defca)). The combined Lieferando and Lieferservice.de would offer access to 10,000-plus delivery restaurants and around 600,000 orders a month, and the announcement said Takeaway.com had become the largest food delivery website in continental Europe, processing around 1.7 million orders a month for around 27,000 restaurants ([EQS](https://www.eqs-news.com/news/corporate/lieferando-de-takeaway-com-acquires-german-food-delivery-website-lieferando/82f2921c-b51c-4fa6-ad7c-d9ee7d1defca)). The deal also included Lieferando's Polish subsidiary, Pyszne.pl — a Polish link that mirrors the Polish market where Batory built iTaxi and Booksy ([EQS](https://www.eqs-news.com/news/corporate/lieferando-de-takeaway-com-acquires-german-food-delivery-website-lieferando/82f2921c-b51c-4fa6-ad7c-d9ee7d1defca)). Lieferando's management team was to be given senior positions in Takeaway.com ([EQS](https://www.eqs-news.com/news/corporate/lieferando-de-takeaway-com-acquires-german-food-delivery-website-lieferando/82f2921c-b51c-4fa6-ad7c-d9ee7d1defca)).

## The Booksy angel

Hansen's relationship with Booksy began at its Series A. In March 2017, tech.eu reported that Booksy had closed a $4.2 million round led by [OpenOcean](/organizations/openocean/) "along with German investor Kai Hansen and a number of angel investors" ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2017/03/03/booksy-series-a/)). The same round was described by Growth Business as "led by OpenOcean, with participation from Kai Hansen and other notable European angel investors," and it was the round that moved Booksy from its taxi-app origins — its founders had previously built iTaxi — towards a merchant-first subscription model ([Growth Business](https://growthbusiness.co.uk/booksy-raises-3-4m-globalise-mobile-first-bookings-platform-15582/); [tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2017/03/03/booksy-series-a/)).

He stayed on the cap table. In January 2021, TechCrunch's report of Booksy's $70 million Series C — led by [Cat Rock Capital](/organizations/cat-rock-capital/), taking total raised to $119 million — listed the round as "also joined by OpenOcean, Piton Capital, VNV Global, Enern, Kai Hansen, Zach Coelius and Manta Ray Ventures" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)). An investor who writes a cheque in the Series A and is still named in the Series C four years later is not a passing name on a press release; he is part of the European angel network that carried Booksy from its Polish roots into six markets.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is Kai Hansen?**
The Berlin-based entrepreneur who co-founded Lieferando in 2009 and, after its 2014 acquisition by Takeaway.com, became an angel investor — including two rounds of Booksy ([EQS](https://www.eqs-news.com/news/corporate/lieferando-de-takeaway-com-acquires-german-food-delivery-website-lieferando/82f2921c-b51c-4fa6-ad7c-d9ee7d1defca); [tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2017/03/03/booksy-series-a/)). He is not the Helloween/Gamma Ray guitarist of the same name.

**What was Lieferando?**
A German food delivery platform founded in 2009 that, combined with Lieferservice.de, offered access to 10,000-plus delivery restaurants and around 600,000 orders a month before Takeaway.com acquired it in April 2014 ([EQS](https://www.eqs-news.com/news/corporate/lieferando-de-takeaway-com-acquires-german-food-delivery-website-lieferando/82f2921c-b51c-4fa6-ad7c-d9ee7d1defca)).

**How did Lieferando survive being rejected by every big investor?**
Its first funding was €15,000 from individual investors, and its founders focused on product, operations and postcode-level market analysis rather than fundraising — with a German marketing team of six or seven against Delivery Hero's roughly 14 ([Catch the Tornado](https://www.catchthetornado.com/podcast/17-challenge-accepted-with-christoph-gerber)).

**What did Kai Hansen do with Booksy?**
He invested as an angel in the $4.2 million Series A in March 2017 and was named among the investors joining the $70 million Series C in January 2021 ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2017/03/03/booksy-series-a/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)).
