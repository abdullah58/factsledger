## Overview

**Noel Murray** is an American freelance film and television critic and pop culture reporter, based in Conway, Arkansas, whose career has run for "over thirty years" ([Southeastern Film Critics Association](https://www.sefca.net/noel-murray); [Book Hero](https://bookhero.co.nz/products/lost-back-to-the-island-by-noel-murray-9781419750502)). A key contributor to **The A.V. Club** and co-founder of the cinema website **The Dissolve**, his writing on TV, movies, music, and comics has appeared in the **New York Times**, the **Los Angeles Times**, **Vulture**, **Entertainment Weekly**, and **Rolling Stone** ([Book Hero](https://bookhero.co.nz/products/lost-back-to-the-island-by-noel-murray-9781419750502)). He is the critic behind the most-quoted verdict on [*The Bunny Game*](/creative-works/the-bunny-game/): Adam Rehmeier's film was "brutal" and "repellent," he wrote — but "far from artless, or pointless" ([The A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/the-bunny-game-1798173832)).

This profile is about **the Arkansas-based critic** — not the same-named Irish golfer who plays PGA Irish Region events for Massereene, who is an entirely different person ([Irish Golf Desk](https://www.irishgolfdesk.com/news-files/tag/Noel+Murray)).

## A thirty-year career in pop-culture criticism

Murray got his start in journalism writing for the award-winning alt-weekly **Nashville Scene**, and went on to become one of the most prolific contributors to **The A.V. Club**, where his archive spans film, TV, and music coverage across decades ([SEFCA](https://www.sefca.net/noel-murray); [The A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/author/noelmu)). He was one of the co-founders of **The Dissolve**, the late, lamented cinema website, and his work now appears regularly in **The A.V. Club**, the **Los Angeles Times**, the **New York Times**, **Rolling Stone**, and **The Week**, with additional contributions to **PRIMETIMER** ([SEFCA](https://www.sefca.net/noel-murray); [PRIMETIMER](https://www.primetimer.com/about/noel_murray)). He lives in central Arkansas, where he covers film, music, and television ([PRIMETIMER](https://www.primetimer.com/about/noel_murray)). In 2024 he co-authored *LOST: Back to the Island: The Complete Critical Companion to The Classic TV Series* with **Emily St. James** — a 352-page **Abrams Press** hardcover revisiting the **ABC** show's legacy through essays, episode summaries, and cultural analysis, twenty years after its 2004 premiere ([Book Hero](https://bookhero.co.nz/products/lost-back-to-the-island-by-noel-murray-9781419750502)). The 352-page scope matters: this is a full-length work of entertainment criticism from two veteran *LOST* recappers, not a recap blog in hardcover — a natural endpoint for a critic who built his career episode by episode ([Book Hero](https://bookhero.co.nz/products/lost-back-to-the-island-by-noel-murray-9781419750502)).

## The Bunny Game review

Murray's 2012 A.V. Club review of *The Bunny Game* opens by taking the term "torture porn" "more literally than usual": director **Adam Rehmeier**'s film stars **Rodleen Getsic** as a street sex worker "shackled, stripped, and beaten by weathered trucker **Jeff Renfro**" — "and that's pretty much the entire movie" ([The A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/the-bunny-game-1798173832)). He praises the craft underneath the content: shot in stark black and white "with extreme angles, intense close-ups, and jumpy editing," the film "means to have the quality of a nightmare, and at that it succeeds," with a relentless atonal electronic score matching Getsic's near-constant screams ([The A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/the-bunny-game-1798173832)). He also reads the film socially, noting that Rehmeier "connects Renfro's punishment of Getsic to her pre-abduction life of prostitution, when her regular customers also frequently treated her like a slab of meat" ([The A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/the-bunny-game-1798173832)). But he concludes it exposes the limitation of the "'How hardcore can you get?' aesthetic": after its first 20 minutes, "The Bunny Game has done just about all it's going to do — no matter how much Rehmeier varies the compositions" ([The A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/the-bunny-game-1798173832)). He even notes, with dry disapproval, that the DVD extras include a "self-congratulatory" commentary track in which Rehmeier and Getsic claim "every burn, cut, and punch literally happened" ([The A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/the-bunny-game-1798173832)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is Noel Murray the critic?**
Noel Murray is an American freelance critic and pop culture reporter based in Conway, Arkansas, with a career spanning over thirty years. He is a key contributor to The A.V. Club, a co-founder of the cinema website The Dissolve, and his work has appeared in The New York Times, The Los Angeles Times, Rolling Stone, The Week, Vulture, and Entertainment Weekly.

**What did Noel Murray say about The Bunny Game?**
In his 2012 A.V. Club review, Murray called Adam Rehmeier's film "brutal" and "repellent" but "far from artless, or pointless." He praised its stark black-and-white photography, extreme angles, and jumpy editing as achieving "the quality of a nightmare," while arguing the film exhausted its "How hardcore can you get?" aesthetic after about 20 minutes.

**Is Noel Murray the film critic the same person as the golfer?**
No. The critic is an Arkansas-based American writer. The other Noel Murray is an Irish golfer who plays PGA Irish Region events for Massereene, documented across Irish golf coverage. They are different people, and this profile covers only the critic.

**What is LOST: Back to the Island?**
A 352-page critical companion to the ABC television series LOST, co-written by Noel Murray and Emily St. James and published by Abrams Press in 2024. It revisits the show's themes, lore, and cultural impact twenty years after its 2004 premiere through essays, episode summaries, and cultural analysis.
