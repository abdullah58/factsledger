## Overview

Arvid Lunnemark is the Swedish mathematician and programmer who co-founded [Anysphere](/organizations/anysphere/), the company behind Cursor, the AI code editor that became one of the defining developer tools of the AI era ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/11/anysphere-raises-8m-from-openai-to-build-an-ai-powered-ide/)). He arrived at the company through competitive mathematics and programming: a mathematics-olympiad gold medallist for Sweden who had worked briefly as a quantitative trader at Jane Street before studying at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](/universities/massachusetts-institute-of-technology/), where he met his future co-founders ([OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/four-cursor-co-founders-turn-billionaires-in-their-mid-20s-after-spacex-acquisition/); [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/cursor)). In October 2025, at the height of Cursor's success, he left the company he had helped build to found Integrous Research, a venture focused on developing systems for safer AI ([Forbes](https://www.europesays.com/ie/179193/)).

That sequence — elite competition career, then a product built around speed, then a voluntary exit toward safety work — is the through-line of Lunnemark's public record. Most coverage treats the departure as a footnote to a funding round. The timing makes it more than that: he announced his exit on October 17, 2025, weeks before the November funding round that made him a billionaire on paper ([Latent Space](https://latent.space/wiki/anysphere/); [Forbes](https://www.europesays.com/ie/179193/)).

## From Olympiads to the editor: speed as a design principle

Before Anysphere, Lunnemark's record was in competitions and quantitative work. He won medals at the International Olympiad in Informatics and held roles at Stripe, Jane Street, and QuantCo ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/cursor)). Forbes calls him a former mathematics Olympiad champion, and OfficeChai specifies the detail as a mathematics-olympiad gold medal for Sweden, with the Jane Street trading stint coming before MIT ([Forbes](https://www.europesays.com/ie/179193/); [OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/four-cursor-co-founders-turn-billionaires-in-their-mid-20s-after-spacex-acquisition/)). At MIT he studied computer science and mathematics alongside [Michael Truell](/people/michael-truell/), [Sualeh Asif](/people/sualeh-asif/), and [Aman Sanger](/people/aman-sanger/), and the four founded Anysphere in 2022 ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/cursor)). TechCrunch's 2023 account confirms the shape of the founding: Truell met Asif, Lunnemark, and Sanger at MIT, where they became close friends around the goal of an environment that could speed up programming tasks such as debugging ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/11/anysphere-raises-8m-from-openai-to-build-an-ai-powered-ide/)).

Inside Cursor, Lunnemark's contribution was the feel of the tool as much as its models. He described developing the interface, prompt construction, context retrieval, and model behaviour together, often with the same people working across those layers, rather than handing each layer to a separate team ([Latent Space](https://latent.space/wiki/anysphere/)). His design priority was succinct — "Fast is fun" — and he said the team discarded experiments that were not enjoyable to use ([Latent Space](https://latent.space/wiki/anysphere/)). A second, quieter contribution shows the same systems instinct: by 2024 he said that separating input data from prompt rendering made it easier to replay old inputs and evaluate changes to prompt construction, turning prompt engineering from guesswork into something testable ([Latent Space](https://latent.space/wiki/anysphere/)). The team had chosen to build Cursor as a fork of VS Code rather than a plugin precisely so they would own how programmers interacted with increasingly capable models, and Lunnemark's layer-spanning work was how that ownership was exercised in practice ([Latent Space](https://latent.space/wiki/anysphere/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/11/anysphere-raises-8m-from-openai-to-build-an-ai-powered-ide/)).

## Leaving at the peak: Integrous Research and the billionaire round

On October 17, 2025, Lunnemark announced his decision to leave Cursor, saying he had informed the team that day ([Latent Space](https://latent.space/wiki/anysphere/)). His own note, as quoted from his personal blog, was understated: "It is, of course, with a mix of feelings. I'm sad to leave the team and the product; I'm excited for the ideas I get to explore next" ([OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/four-cursor-co-founders-turn-billionaires-in-their-mid-20s-after-spacex-acquisition/)). Forbes, reporting a month later, said the 26-year-old had left to start his own startup called Integrous Research, focused on developing systems for "safer AI", according to its incorporation documents, and that he did not respond to Forbes' request for comment ([Forbes](https://www.europesays.com/ie/179193/)). No fully-read source in this pass describes Integrous Research's products, funding, or team, and this profile claims nothing about them beyond that safety focus.

The departure did not remove him from the company's financial story. In November 2025 Anysphere announced a $2.3 billion Series D at a $29.3 billion valuation, and Forbes estimated each founder — Lunnemark included — held a 4.5% stake worth at least $1.3 billion ([Forbes](https://www.europesays.com/ie/179193/)). Inc. corroborates the round, its investor list of Accel, Thrive Capital, Andreessen Horowitz, and DST plus Coatue, NVIDIA, and Google, and Forbes' conclusion that the valuation made all four founders billionaires ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/ben-sherry/this-ai-coding-startup-just-minted-4-new-billionaires/91265014)). In 2026 the story moved to acquisition: the companies had struck an April agreement giving [SpaceX](/organizations/spacex/) the option to acquire Cursor for $60 billion or pay $10 billion for continued model access, and Latent Space records an August 14, 2026 announcement that SpaceX had completed its acquisition of Cursor ([OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/four-cursor-co-founders-turn-billionaires-in-their-mid-20s-after-spacex-acquisition/); [Latent Space](https://latent.space/wiki/anysphere/)). OfficeChai notes Lunnemark was still counted among the four billionaires, suggesting his stake survived the exit largely intact, while later wealth estimates diverge — about $2.7 billion each on Forbes' 4.5% stakes versus about $5.5 billion each on Bloomberg's roughly 9% stakes — so this profile anchors on the dated November 2025 Forbes figure rather than asserting a single later net worth ([OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/four-cursor-co-founders-turn-billionaires-in-their-mid-20s-after-spacex-acquisition/)).
