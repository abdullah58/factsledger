## Overview

Laura Spiekerman is the co-founder of [Alloy](/organizations/alloy/), the [New York City](/places/new-york-city/)-based identity-decisioning company that helps banks and fintechs automate customer onboarding, transaction monitoring, and credit decisions ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2021/09/alloy-identity-verification-transaction-monitoring-platform-financial-services-kyc-laura-spiekerman/)). She was Alloy's chief revenue officer in its early years and is described as its president in later coverage, and her route to the company ran through Nairobi, Kenya — where she was the first employee of a mobile-payments startup — and an ACH payments startup in the United States, where she met her co-founders ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2017/04/women-nyc-tech-laura-spiekerman-alloy/)). She was named a Notable Woman on Wall Street by Crain's New York Business in 2021 and a finalist for American Banker's inaugural Most Influential Women in Fintech award in 2023 ([AccessWire](https://markets.financialcontent.com/pennwell.bioopticsworld/article/accesswire-2021-8-31-evolve-bank-and-trust-names-shamir-karkal-and-laura-spiekerman-to-its-open-banking-advisory-board); [American Banker](https://www.prweb.com/releases/american-banker-announces-finalists-for-the-most-influential-women-in-fintech-award-celebrating-female-founders-and-thought-leaders-in-financial-technology-879391192.html)).

## From Nairobi to New York: the path to Alloy

Spiekerman started her fintech career as the first employee at Kopo Kopo, after abandoning a plan to go to law school and flying to Nairobi to join the two founders who had built software letting small businesses use M-PESA mobile payments ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2017/04/women-nyc-tech-laura-spiekerman-alloy/)). Watching M-PESA infrastructure change people's financial lives, she has said, inspired her to help modernize financial infrastructure in the United States ([NYC Founder Guide](https://www.nycfounderguide.com/insight/from-mpesa-in-kenya-to-alloys-identity-system-laura-spiekerman-is-drawn-to-infrastructures-big-fixes)). Back in the US she joined a small ACH payments startup — later identified in coverage as Knox Payments — which eventually failed, but which left her increasingly interested in financial services infrastructure and introduced her to her future co-founders ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2017/04/women-nyc-tech-laura-spiekerman-alloy/); [Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/uncategorized/alloy-techstars-venture-capital/)). Before Alloy she also worked on the Research & Investment team at Imprint Capital Advisors and led Business Development & Partnerships at the ACH startup, and she is a Barnard College alumna ([AccessWire](https://markets.financialcontent.com/pennwell.bioopticsworld/article/accesswire-2021-8-31-evolve-bank-and-trust-names-shamir-karkal-and-laura-spiekerman-to-its-open-banking-advisory-board)).

The three founders concluded that while ACH settlement was one big challenge, identity was an even bigger one underpinning financial services, and they formed Alloy in 2015, moving to New York in July that year for the Techstars NYC program ([NYC Founder Guide](https://www.nycfounderguide.com/insight/from-mpesa-in-kenya-to-alloys-identity-system-laura-spiekerman-is-drawn-to-infrastructures-big-fixes)). In the early company, Spiekerman handled conversations with prospects and customers while [Hearn](/people/charles-hearn/) built the product and [Nicholas](/people/tommy-nicholas/) implemented it and gathered feedback ([AWS Startups Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/startups/alloys-global-identity-decisioning-platform-built-on-aws/)). By September 2016 the company had raised about $1.5 million and had five customers ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/uncategorized/alloy-techstars-venture-capital/)). Her advice to infrastructure founders, drawn from that period, was blunt: do not worry about scale or the next hundred customers, focus on delighting one or two reference customers, raise more money than you think you need, and invest in hiring and retention early even when the team is tiny ([NYC Founder Guide](https://www.nycfounderguide.com/insight/from-mpesa-in-kenya-to-alloys-identity-system-laura-spiekerman-is-drawn-to-infrastructures-big-fixes)).

## Building Alloy and arguing for wider access

As Alloy grew, Spiekerman became its public voice on why identity infrastructure matters beyond fraud prevention. In the 2021 Series C interview she described the founding gap — financial firms wanted to onboard customers digitally, but assessing risk was fragmented across outdated, siloed databases — and argued that Alloy's alternative-data sources let banks verify applicants who lack traditional identification, such as underbanked communities, immigrants, and students ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2021/09/alloy-identity-verification-transaction-monitoring-platform-financial-services-kyc-laura-spiekerman/)). The $100 million round led by [Lightspeed Venture Partners](/organizations/lightspeed-venture-partners/) valued the company at $1.35 billion and funded the addition of transaction monitoring to the onboarding platform ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2021/09/alloy-identity-verification-transaction-monitoring-platform-financial-services-kyc-laura-spiekerman/)). A year later, quoted as co-founder and CRO, she tied the company's mission to credit access for people who do not fit traditional creditworthiness profiles, as Alloy raised a further $52 million at a $1.55 billion valuation ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/regulation/2022/fraud-prevention-fintech-alloy-nets-52m-on-1-55b-valuation/)). Clients named in coverage from this period included Ally, [Brex](/organizations/brex/), Petal, and [Ramp](/organizations/ramp/) ([NYC Founder Guide](https://www.nycfounderguide.com/insight/from-mpesa-in-kenya-to-alloys-identity-system-laura-spiekerman-is-drawn-to-infrastructures-big-fixes)).

Alongside the operating role, Spiekerman took on industry positions and advocacy. In August 2021 she joined [Evolve Bank & Trust](/organizations/evolve-bank-and-trust/)'s Open Banking Advisory Board ([AccessWire](https://markets.financialcontent.com/pennwell.bioopticsworld/article/accesswire-2021-8-31-evolve-bank-and-trust-names-shamir-karkal-and-laura-spiekerman-to-its-open-banking-advisory-board)), and in 2023 American Banker listed her, as Alloy's co-founder and President, among the 20 finalists for its first Most Influential Women in Fintech award, which honoured women leaders "paving the way for the industry's future while championing critical issues like diversity, inclusion and mentorship" ([American Banker](https://www.prweb.com/releases/american-banker-announces-finalists-for-the-most-influential-women-in-fintech-award-celebrating-female-founders-and-thought-leaders-in-financial-technology-879391192.html)). As early as 2017 she was arguing publicly that women bring different leadership qualities to decision-making, team cohesion, and risk mitigation, that sexism and unconscious bias were the primary unseen barriers in tech, and that investors needed to stop making "pipeline excuses" for all-male rooms ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2017/04/women-nyc-tech-laura-spiekerman-alloy/)).
