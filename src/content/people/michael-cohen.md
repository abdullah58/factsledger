The Michael Cohen the public met in May 2024 was the most dangerous kind of witness for Donald Trump: the man who had once said he would take a bullet for him, now sitting feet away, pointing him out to a jury. The Cohen of August 2026 is something no 2024 profile could have predicted — the same man calling Trump "boss" on his own radio show, with Trump praising him from the White House. Every existing profile freezes Cohen in the fixer-turned-foe frame; this one leads with the reversal, because it is the verified story competitors have not yet told as one ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/trump-former-fixer-michael-cohen-have-warm-interview-political-low-point-2026-08-21/)).

## The 2026 reversal

On August 21, 2026, Trump had a warm public reunion with Cohen, his former fixer turned foe whose court testimony had contributed to Trump's 2024 conviction. Cohen interviewed Trump on his New York radio program, called him "boss," and delivered a six-minute monologue on friendship and forgiveness. Trump, speaking from the White House, greeted him convivially — "Hello Michael. How are you, Michael?" — and said, "I respect the fact that you recanted everything you said." Cohen closed the interview segment by crooning a lyric from the 1978 Peaches & Herb song: "Reunited, and it feels so good" ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/trump-former-fixer-michael-cohen-have-warm-interview-political-low-point-2026-08-21/)).

Three weeks later came the pardon talk. Asked on September 9, 2026 whether he would consider pardoning Cohen, who is seeking one, Trump said: "If everything is checking out, it's something I would consider," adding that he appreciated that Cohen "came out, although late, [and] rectified the situation." Cohen's own version of what changed was not the word "recantation" but a claim that he felt "pressured and coerced" by prosecutors while testifying against Trump ([Reuters (via SRN News)](https://srnnews.com/trump-says-he-would-consider-pardon-for-former-fixer-michael-cohen/)). Reuters is careful about the distinction; so is this profile. Trump's framing of a full recantation and Cohen's framing of coercion are not the same claim, and neither amounts to a formally filed retraction of sworn testimony ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/trump-former-fixer-michael-cohen-have-warm-interview-political-low-point-2026-08-21/)).

## The fixer

Cohen's early-2000s introduction to Trump was, in the Associated Press's telling, a classic New York real estate story: Cohen was a condo-board member in a Trump building and got involved on Trump's side of a residents-versus-management dispute, and the mogul soon brought him into the company. Before that he had led an eclectic career, from practicing personal injury law to operating a taxi fleet with his father-in-law. The AP dates his Trump Organization tenure from 2006 to 2017; Telegraph India, in its May 2024 trial coverage, says he left a law firm to join Trump's company in 2007. Both versions are on the record; this profile does not choose between them ([Associated Press](https://gmg-wsls-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/news/politics/2024/05/12/michael-cohen-a-challenging-star-witness-in-donald-trumps-hush-money-trial/); [Telegraph India](https://www.telegraphindia.com/world/donald-trumps-fixer-turned-foe-michael-cohen-begins-testifying-at-hush-money-trial/cid/2019575)).

Once inside, he functioned as both a lawyer and what the AP called a "shark-toothed loyalist" — working on some deal-making but spending much of his time threatening lawsuits, berating reporters, and otherwise "maneuvering to neutralize potential reputational dings for his boss." He helped wield the National Enquirer as a kind of house organ, buying up stories or flagging them for purchase. At his 2018 sentencing he attributed it to "blind loyalty": "it was my duty to cover up his dirty deeds, rather than to listen to my own inner voice and my moral compass" ([Associated Press](https://gmg-wsls-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/news/politics/2024/05/12/michael-cohen-a-challenging-star-witness-in-donald-trumps-hush-money-trial/)).

## The trial

The rupture came in 2018, after the FBI raided Cohen's home and office and Trump began distancing himself. Cohen told a federal court that he had paid $130,000 in hush money to the adult-film performer [Stormy Daniels](/people/stormy-daniels/) before the 2016 election, and later pleaded guilty to campaign-finance violations tied to that payment, plus tax evasion, false bank statements, and lying to Congress about how long negotiations over a Trump Tower Moscow project had continued. He was sentenced to three years in prison and served more than a year ([Associated Press](https://gmg-wsls-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/news/politics/2024/05/12/michael-cohen-a-challenging-star-witness-in-donald-trumps-hush-money-trial/); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/trump-former-fixer-michael-cohen-have-warm-interview-political-low-point-2026-08-21/)).

In May 2024 he became the prosecution's star witness in the Manhattan criminal case brought by District Attorney [Alvin Bragg](/people/alvin-bragg/) over how the Trump Organization reimbursed Cohen in 2017 — payments the company booked as legal expenses. The prosecution charged 34 counts of falsifying business records, arguing the records veiled reimbursements for the Daniels payoff to influence the 2016 election; the defense argued Cohen had been paid for legal work and was a liar acting on his own. Cohen opened his testimony by explaining he went to law school at his grandmother's urging even though he "really didn't want to be a lawyer" — then looked left, stood up, identified Trump ("He's wearing a blue and white tie"), and laid out the reimbursement scheme invoice by invoice. The jury convicted Trump on all 34 counts ([Telegraph India](https://www.telegraphindia.com/world/donald-trumps-fixer-turned-foe-michael-cohen-begins-testifying-at-hush-money-trial/cid/2019575); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/trump-former-fixer-michael-cohen-have-warm-interview-political-low-point-2026-08-21/)).

The AP's pre-trial portrait had warned he was "as challenging a star witness as they come": a disbarred lawyer with two federal guilty pleas, an admission of lying under oath multiple times, and — from his October 2023 testimony in [Letitia James](/people/letitia-james/)' civil fraud trial — a testy cross-examination in which he said he had lied even in admitting to some of his earlier falsehoods. One federal judge suggested he had perjured himself either in that testimony or in his guilty plea; the fraud-trial judge, by contrast, found his testimony credible and corroborated ([Associated Press](https://gmg-wsls-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/news/politics/2024/05/12/michael-cohen-a-challenging-star-witness-in-donald-trumps-hush-money-trial/)).

Cohen presided over the trial in character. [Judge Juan Merchan](/people/juan-merchan/) had to exhort prosecutors to tell him to stop posting about the case after he wore a T-shirt depicting Trump behind bars on TikTok; Trump's lawyer Todd Blanche opened by calling him an admitted liar "obsessed" with Trump. Prosecutor Matthew Colangelo told jurors Cohen had made "mistakes" but could be believed anyway — "you don't have to like him," as one defense lawyer put it. "This is the guy Trump chose" ([Associated Press](https://gmg-wsls-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/news/politics/2024/05/12/michael-cohen-a-challenging-star-witness-in-donald-trumps-hush-money-trial/); [Telegraph India](https://www.telegraphindia.com/world/donald-trumps-fixer-turned-foe-michael-cohen-begins-testifying-at-hush-money-trial/cid/2019575)).

## After the split

Since breaking with Trump, Cohen built a second career as his most relentless critic — a podcast called "Mea Culpa," a 2020 memoir in which he called himself "the least reliable narrator on the planet," and a stream of crude social-media attacks on his former boss ([Associated Press](https://gmg-wsls-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/news/politics/2024/05/12/michael-cohen-a-challenging-star-witness-in-donald-trumps-hush-money-trial/)). That persona made the 2026 reconciliation all the more striking: the man who had spent years as the loudest anti-Trump voice in ex-loyalist circles now framing the relationship, in his own monologue, as one where "we forgave." Trump, who is appealing the 2024 conviction, meanwhile told reporters Cohen was being considered for a pardon — the pardon Cohen is seeking, per Reuters ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/trump-former-fixer-michael-cohen-have-warm-interview-political-low-point-2026-08-21/); [Reuters (via SRN News)](https://srnnews.com/trump-says-he-would-consider-pardon-for-former-fixer-michael-cohen/)).

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not resolve whether Cohen joined the Trump Organization in 2006 or 2007; the Associated Press and Telegraph India disagree, and both are recorded here.
- It does not claim Cohen formally recanted his sworn 2024 testimony. "Recantation" is Trump's word from the August 2026 interview; Cohen's own claim is that he felt "pressured and coerced," and no filed legal retraction is established by the sources read in full.
- It does not independently verify the January 2025 unconditional-discharge sentence; that outcome is attributed as reported by a lower-tier outlet, pending confirmation from a higher-tier source.
- It does not give an exact birth date (only that he was 57 when he began testifying in May 2024) or the title of his 2020 memoir; neither is established by the read sources.
