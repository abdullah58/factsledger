## Overview

Celeste Katz — who publishes under the byline Celeste Katz Marston — is an American
political journalist whose career moved from 15 years of New York political reporting to
running a nonprofit local newspaper. She is editor-in-chief of The Concord Bridge, the
nonprofit weekly newspaper of Concord, Massachusetts, a post she took in April 2025 after
joining the paper as news editor in July 2023
([bio](http://celestekatz.nyc/bio),
[The Concord Bridge](https://concordbridge.org/index.php/meet-our-team/)). Before that she
spent more than 15 years at the New York Daily News, then moved through Mic, Newsweek and
Glamour; she co-hosted the 2016 election podcast "Special Relationship," was a 2022
Mirror Award finalist for her Nieman Reports writing, and has worked in radio for both
WBAI FM New York and the Australian Broadcasting Corporation
([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2016/heres-some-of-the-best-political-journalism-of-2016/),
[Newhouse](https://us19.campaign-archive.com/?u=1876186c00eed35a369158499&id=3c1430b7a3)).

## From Providence to the Daily News (1995-2015)

Katz launched her reporting career at The Providence Journal, where she worked from May
1995 to March 2000 covering government, law enforcement, U.S. Navy and Coast Guard
installations, social services and education, and wrote a "Culture Spy" pop culture
column for the paper's statewide lifestyles section
([résumé](http://celestekatz.nyc/experience)). She graduated from Brown University with a
BA in International Relations (foreign policy, diplomacy and global security), where she
was executive editor — and later an alumni trustee — of The Brown Daily Herald and a
two-term president of Slavic House
([bio](http://celestekatz.nyc/bio)). Her résumé also lists early reporting internships at
The Washington Times, The Washington Monthly, The Roanoke Times and The Atlantic
([résumé](http://celestekatz.nyc/experience)).

In April 2000 she joined the New York Daily News as a political correspondent and
columnist, a tenure that ran to September 2015 and spanned four presidential, four
gubernatorial and four mayoral election cycles
([résumé](http://celestekatz.nyc/experience),
[Politicon](http://www.politicon.com/speaker/celeste-katz/)). Her assignments included
the City Hall and State Capitol bureaus, and she ran the paper's "Daily Politics" blog;
outside politics she covered education, crime, general assignment and features, and
specialized in liveblog reporting and anchoring
([bio](http://celestekatz.nyc/bio)). After leaving the Daily News she spent October to
December 2015 as a correspondent for The Boston Globe, contributing freelance
2016-election coverage through standalone stories and liveblogs
([résumé](http://celestekatz.nyc/experience)).

## Mic, "Special Relationship," and the Newsweek firing (2016-2019)

In January 2016 Katz became senior political correspondent for Mic, covering the 2016
presidential race in breaking stories and analysis and across multimedia formats
including Facebook Live and Periscope
([résumé](http://celestekatz.nyc/experience)). While at Mic she co-hosted "Special
Relationship," a pop-up podcast with The Economist that examined the election from a
global perspective; Poynter readers ranked it among the best political journalism of the
2016 cycle, with Katz hosting alongside The Economist's John Prideaux
([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2016/heres-some-of-the-best-political-journalism-of-2016/)).

From September 2017 to February 2018 she was a New York-based senior political writer for
Newsweek, covering the White House, national affairs and voting rights; her
investigations included the workings of the Presidential Advisory Commission on Election
Integrity and an examination of Interior Secretary Ryan Zinke's travel practices that
sparked a federal inquiry
([résumé](http://celestekatz.nyc/experience)). In February 2018 she was dismissed along
with editor-in-chief Bob Roe and executive news director Kenneth Li. The New York Times
reported that the purge targeted employees involved in coverage of the company's
financial and legal troubles: Katz had reported on Newsweek's affairs, including a
January 18 raid by the Manhattan district attorney's office that seized 18 computer
servers, while two other reporters involved in the coverage were locked out of their
accounts and senior writer Matthew Cooper quit in protest
([New York Times](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/international/newsweek-fires-editors-and-reporter-who-investigated-the-company-118020601622_1.html)).
The remaining staff's subsequent exposé revealed a deep financial relationship between
the parent company and the small Christian school Olivet University
([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/morning-media-newsfeed/newsweek-publishes-expose-about-itself-after-staff-firings/)).
From April 2018 to January 2019 she was senior political reporter for Condé Nast's
Glamour, covering national politics and the pivotal 2018 midterm elections, including
Justice Brett Kavanaugh's confirmation hearings
([résumé](http://celestekatz.nyc/experience),
[bio](http://celestekatz.nyc/bio)).

## Radio, freelance work, and a Mirror Award nomination (2019-2025)

Katz has been a U.S. correspondent for Australian Broadcasting Corporation radio since
December 2007, first appearing from the campaign trail during the 2008 presidential
election
([résumé](http://celestekatz.nyc/experience),
[bio](http://celestekatz.nyc/bio)). From 2018 she was a correspondent, producer and host
for WBAI FM New York, co-hosting the public-affairs program "Driving Forces" and
producing and hosting "New York in Crisis," a 16-installment interview series on the
Covid-19 pandemic ([bio](http://celestekatz.nyc/bio)). She guest-hosted three-hour live
programs on Sirius XM's Patriot, Progress, POTUS and Insight channels in 2016-2018
([résumé](http://celestekatz.nyc/experience)).

As a freelancer from January 2019 she reported on politics and the media for Nieman
Reports, NBC Asian America and Cosmopolitan, and co-authored the public-service e-book
"Is This Any Way to Vote? Vulnerable Voting Machines and the Mysterious Industry Behind
Them" (WhoWhatWhy, 2020) with Gabriella Novello
([bio](http://celestekatz.nyc/bio),
[résumé](http://celestekatz.nyc/experience)). Her Nieman Reports piece "Covering
Extremism: 'As Exhausting a Beat as It Is Important'" was named a finalist in the 2022
Mirror Awards' special category for best story on media coverage of the insurrection and
the "Stop the Steal" movement
([Newhouse](https://us19.campaign-archive.com/?u=1876186c00eed35a369158499&id=3c1430b7a3)).
From February 2020 to July 2023 she also worked as an investigator for On Point
Investigations, locating and interviewing witnesses for national law firms handling class
actions involving securities fraud, whistleblower and antitrust matters
([résumé](http://celestekatz.nyc/experience)). She has provided political analysis in TV
appearances on [CNN](/organizations/cnn/), [MSNBC](/organizations/msnbc/) and
[Fox News](/organizations/fox-news/), as well as C-SPAN, CBS, NPR and the BBC, and has
served as a guest lecturer, panelist and debate questioner for the Columbia, NYU and
CUNY journalism schools, NY1, Hofstra and Politicon
([bio](http://celestekatz.nyc/bio),
[résumé](http://celestekatz.nyc/experience),
[Politicon](http://www.politicon.com/speaker/celeste-katz/)).

## Leading The Concord Bridge

Katz joined The Concord Bridge in July 2023 as news editor and was promoted to managing
editor in spring 2024, with the paper announcing the promotion that April
([bio](http://celestekatz.nyc/bio),
[announcement](https://concordbridge.org/index.php/2024/04/04/the-concord-bridge-names-new-managing-editor-assistant-editor-and-art-and-digital-design-editor/)).
In April 2025 she became editor-in-chief, overseeing editorial staff and operations for
the paper's broadsheet print edition and digital coverage
([bio](http://celestekatz.nyc/bio)). The Bridge is a digital-and-print nonprofit that
arose after Gannett eliminated nearly all local journalism from its Massachusetts
weeklies, competing with Gannett's Concord Journal for coverage of the town
([Media Nation](http://dankennedy.net/2022/11/01/michael-reed-tells-ep-that-everything-is-coming-up-gannett/)).
During her tenure as managing editor and editor-in-chief, the paper won 14 awards from
the New England Newspaper & Press Association
([bio](http://celestekatz.nyc/bio)).
