## Overview

Frank Sinatra (1915–1998) was an American singer and actor whose smooth baritone and peerless lyric phrasing made him the defining voice of 20th-century popular music. He sang on the first No. 1 of Billboard's first national chart in 1940 and was still reaching the Hot 100 top 20 in 2023 — an 83-year chart span no other artist matches ([Billboard](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/11/01/the-first-billboard-all-that-was-new-bright-and-interesting-on-the-boards/); [Billboard](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2023/12/20/frank-sinatra-ranks-in-the-billboard-hot-100s-top-20-for-the-first-time-since-1967-with-jingle-bells/)). He won nine competitive Grammy Awards and an Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor, and he remains the first artist ever to win the Grammy for Album of the Year three times ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/frank-sinatra/6581/); [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/frank-sinatra-weve-got-him-under-our-skin/)).

Born Francis Albert Sinatra on December 12, 1915, in Hoboken, New Jersey, in the [United States](/places/united-states/), he was the only child of Italian immigrants — his father Martin, a Sicilian tavern owner and part-time prizefighter, and his mother Natalie, known as Dolly ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Frank-Sinatra)). Inspired by Bing Crosby's crooning, he chose popular singing as a teenager; his career, begun in the swing era, lasted 60 years ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Frank-Sinatra); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/frank-sinatra-mn0000792507)).

## The band singer and the first teen idol

Sinatra's career began with a radio contest. As a member of the local vocal group the Hoboken Four, he won a talent competition in 1935 on Major Bowes' Amateur Hour, and the group toured the country that year before disbanding ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Frank-Sinatra)). In 1939, while singing and waiting tables at the Rustic Cabin in Englewood Cliffs, New Jersey, he was discovered and hired by jazz trumpeter Harry James; the six-month tenure produced 10 commercial recordings, the first made on July 13, 1939 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Frank-Sinatra); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/frank-sinatra-mn0000792507)).

In December 1939 he accepted a more lucrative offer from bandleader Tommy Dorsey, and his 83 recordings with the Dorsey band from 1940 to 1942 became his first major body of work ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Frank-Sinatra)). Dorsey's trombone playing shaped Sinatra's breath control, and the partnership produced his first No. 1: "I'll Never Smile Again" crowned Billboard's first national chart in July 1940 and spent 12 weeks at the summit ([Billboard](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/11/01/the-first-billboard-all-that-was-new-bright-and-interesting-on-the-boards/); [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/frank-sinatra-weve-got-him-under-our-skin/)). By 1942 his fame had eclipsed Dorsey's, and he left the band in September to go solo; his January 1943 appearances at the Paramount Theatre in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) drew near-hysterical crowds of screaming young female fans — the "bobby-soxers" — and he was dubbed "The Voice" ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Frank-Sinatra)).

## Columbia, collapse and the Capitol comeback

Sinatra's solo recording career for Columbia Records began in earnest in November 1944, after a musicians' strike had curtailed his output through most of 1943–44, and his first album, The Voice of Frank Sinatra, topped the album chart in 1946 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Frank-Sinatra); [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/frank-sinatra/6581/)). The Columbia years brought hit after hit, including three No. 1s in 1943 — "There Are Such Things", "In the Blue of the Evening" and the reissued "All or Nothing at All" — but his success crested around 1948 ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/frank-sinatra-mn0000792507)). He lost his voice completely for several months in 1950 from vocal-cord hemorrhaging, his divorce from first wife Nancy Barbato in 1951 and stormy marriage to actress Ava Gardner damaged his reputation, and in 1952 his Columbia contract was not renewed ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Frank-Sinatra)).

The comeback began with a record deal and a film role. Sinatra signed a long-term contract with Capitol Records, and in June 1953 "I'm Walking Behind You" became his first Top Ten hit in a year and a half ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/frank-sinatra-mn0000792507)). That August he played Private Angelo Maggio in the Second World War drama From Here to Eternity, and on March 25, 1954, he won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor for the part ([Oscars.org](https://oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1954/Q?qt-honorees=0)). Working with arranger and conductor Nelson Riddle, he then built the Capitol concept albums that defined the modern LP: In the Wee Small Hours topped the album chart in 1955, Songs for Swingin' Lovers! followed in 1956, and Come Fly with Me and Frank Sinatra Sings for Only the Lonely both reached No. 1 in 1958, with Come Dance with Me! winning the Grammy for Album of the Year in 1959 ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/frank-sinatra-mn0000792507); [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/frank-sinatra-weve-got-him-under-our-skin/)).

## Reprise, the Rat Pack and the 1960s peak

In December 1960, while still under contract to Capitol, Sinatra began recording for his own label, Reprise Records, which launched in April 1961 with Ring-a-Ding Ding!; five new Sinatra albums appeared that year and all reached the Top Ten ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/frank-sinatra-mn0000792507)). On screen, the 1960 heist film Ocean's Eleven crystallized the Rat Pack — Sinatra, Dean Martin and Sammy Davis, Jr. — Las Vegas entertainers who filmed by day at the Sands Hotel and Casino and performed together there by night ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Oceans-Eleven-film-by-Milestone)). Reprise's first single, "The Second Time Around", won the Grammy for Record of the Year, and the label gave Sinatra the artistic control Capitol had not ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/frank-sinatra-mn0000792507)).

The mid-1960s brought his greatest awards run. September of My Years, a ballad collection on turning 50, won Album of the Year at the 1966 Grammys, making him the first artist to win the award twice; A Man and His Music won it again the following year, making him the first three-time Album of the Year winner — a feat only Stevie Wonder and Paul Simon have since equalled ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/frank-sinatra-weve-got-him-under-our-skin/)). In July 1966 "Strangers in the Night" returned him to No. 1 for the first time in 11 years and won his only Grammy for Record of the Year, and in April 1967 "Somethin' Stupid", a duet with his daughter Nancy Sinatra, spent four weeks at No. 1 on the Hot 100 ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/frank-sinatra-weve-got-him-under-our-skin/); [Billboard](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2023/12/20/frank-sinatra-ranks-in-the-billboard-hot-100s-top-20-for-the-first-time-since-1967-with-jingle-bells/)). In March 1969 he released "My Way", with a lyric Paul Anka had crafted for him from a French ballad, and it became his signature song ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/frank-sinatra-mn0000792507)).

## Retirement, return and the long goodbye

In the spring of 1971, aged 55, Sinatra announced his retirement — but he stayed retired only until the fall of 1973, when he returned with a gold-selling album and a television special both called Ol' Blue Eyes Is Back ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/frank-sinatra-mn0000792507)). In this late phase he cut back on records, films and television in favour of live performing, particularly in Las Vegas ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/frank-sinatra-mn0000792507)). He made no new studio albums for six years, then returned in March 1980 with the three-LP set Trilogy: Past, Present, Future, whose most memorable track, "Theme From New York, New York", became his final Top 40 hit — 40 years after his first — and his signature song for the rest of his performing career ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/frank-sinatra-weve-got-him-under-our-skin/)).

The honours kept pace with the longevity. He was recognised at the Kennedy Center Honors in 1983 and awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom by President Ronald Reagan in 1985, and in 1997 Congress authorised a Congressional Gold Medal for him, citing more than 50 albums and more than 60 films ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/105/plaws/publ14/PLAW-105publ14.pdf)). In 1993 he re-signed to Capitol and recorded Duets, re-recording his old favourites with guest singers; it became his biggest-selling album, with sales of more than 3,000,000 copies, and its sequel Duets II won the 1995 Grammy for Best Traditional Pop Vocal Performance ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/frank-sinatra-mn0000792507); [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/frank-sinatra-weve-got-him-under-our-skin/)). He finally retired from performing in 1995, in his 80th year, and died of a heart attack on May 14, 1998, in Los Angeles, aged 82 ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/frank-sinatra-mn0000792507); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Frank-Sinatra)).

## The Billboard bookend: 1940 to 2023

No other artist's chart story spans as far as Sinatra's. He sang on the very first No. 1 of Billboard's first national chart — the "National List of Best Selling Retail Records" published July 27, 1940 — and he was still charting 83 years later: in December 2023 his recording of "Jingle Bells" climbed to No. 16 on the Hot 100, his highest placement since "Somethin' Stupid" topped the chart in 1967 ([Billboard](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/11/01/the-first-billboard-all-that-was-new-bright-and-interesting-on-the-boards/); [Billboard](https://djfrosty.com/2023/12/26/meghan-trainor-cher-chloe-bring-new-holiday-cheer-to-the-hot-100/)). The 2023 run was not a curiosity: the recording drew 16.9 million official U.S. streams in the week of December 8–14, 2023, and 20.9 million the following week, per Luminate — which means a singer who died in 1998 was still reaching the Hot 100's top 20 a quarter-century after his death, on the strength of streaming alone ([Billboard](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2023/12/20/frank-sinatra-ranks-in-the-billboard-hot-100s-top-20-for-the-first-time-since-1967-with-jingle-bells/); [Billboard](https://djfrosty.com/2023/12/26/meghan-trainor-cher-chloe-bring-new-holiday-cheer-to-the-hot-100/)).

The span also frames his Grammy record. The Recording Academy lists nine competitive Grammy wins from 31 nominations, plus three Special Merit Awards — a Lifetime Achievement Award in 1966, a Trustees Award in 1979 and a GRAMMY Legend Award presented in 1994 — and 13 recordings in the GRAMMY Hall of Fame ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/frank-sinatra/6581/); [Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/frank-sinatra-weve-got-him-under-our-skin/)). He was the second Lifetime Achievement recipient, after his hero Bing Crosby, and the second person, after Duke Ellington, to hold both that award and a Trustees Award ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/frank-sinatra-weve-got-him-under-our-skin/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**How many Grammy Awards did Frank Sinatra win?**
The Recording Academy lists nine competitive Grammy wins from 31 nominations, plus three Special Merit Awards: the Lifetime Achievement Award (1966), the Trustees Award (1979) and the GRAMMY Legend Award (1994). He was the first artist to win Album of the Year three times. ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/frank-sinatra/6581/))

**What was Frank Sinatra's first No. 1 song?**
"I'll Never Smile Again", recorded with Tommy Dorsey and His Orchestra, crowned Billboard's first national chart — the "National List of Best Selling Retail Records" — in its July 27, 1940 issue and spent 12 weeks at the summit. It was the biggest hit Dorsey ever had. ([Billboard](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/11/01/the-first-billboard-all-that-was-new-bright-and-interesting-on-the-boards/))

**What is Frank Sinatra's most famous song?**
Britannica names "Strangers in the Night" (1966), "My Way" (1969) and "New York, New York" (1980) as his signature songs. "My Way", with a lyric Paul Anka wrote for him, became his defining statement, while "Theme From New York, New York" was his final Top 40 hit. ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Frank-Sinatra))

**Did Frank Sinatra win an Oscar?**
Yes. He won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor for playing Private Angelo Maggio in From Here to Eternity, on March 25, 1954. He was later nominated for Best Actor for The Man with the Golden Arm (1955). ([Oscars.org](https://oscars.org/oscars/ceremonies/1954/Q?qt-honorees=0))

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim a single settled Grammy total: the Recording Academy counts nine competitive wins, the 1997 Congressional findings say eight, and secondary sources say 11 or 13 ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/artists/frank-sinatra/6581/); [Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/105/plaws/publ14/PLAW-105publ14.pdf)). It does not state a recording year for "Jingle Bells", because Billboard's own syndicated versions disagree — 1948 in one article, 1957 in another ([Billboard](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2023/12/20/frank-sinatra-ranks-in-the-billboard-hot-100s-top-20-for-the-first-time-since-1967-with-jingle-bells/); [Billboard](https://djfrosty.com/2023/12/26/meghan-trainor-cher-chloe-bring-new-holiday-cheer-to-the-hot-100/)). It does not repeat the widely quoted "150 million records sold" figure, which appears only in Wikipedia-derived sources and was not verified from a tier1 or tier2 source in this session. It does not cover the persistent rumours about Sinatra's alleged organised-crime connections, which no fully-read source in this session substantiated as fact, and it makes no claim about the paternity rumours concerning Mia Farrow's son Ronan Farrow, which Britannica reports as disputed by Farrow and Sinatra family members ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Frank-Sinatra)). It does not cover his son Frank Sinatra, Jr. — a singer in his own right; the two Frank Sinatras are different people and should not be confused.
