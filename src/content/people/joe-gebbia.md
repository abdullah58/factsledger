## Overview

Before Joe Gebbia had a government title, he had a government project: the federal retirement system. In February 2025, the Airbnb co-founder announced he was joining the Department of Government Efficiency, and his first assignment was the "slow and paper-based retirement process" for federal employees — a process so manual that completed paperwork was stored in a converted limestone mine in Boyers, Pennsylvania ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/airbnb-cofounder-joe-gebbia-is-excited-to-join-doge/487769)). By June 2025, the Office of Personnel Management had launched a fully online retirement application system; by December 2025, the backlog of applications waiting for processing was larger than ever ([Government Executive](https://www.govexec.com/pay-benefits/2025/12/opms-retirement-application-pile-remains-large-year-draws-close/410105/)). That arc — a designer walking into one of government's most stubborn paper piles, shipping a digital system, and hitting the messy reality of a downsizing wave — is the honest prologue to his current job. On August 21, 2025, President Trump signed an executive order making Gebbia the first-ever Chief Design Officer of the United States, tasked with leading the National Design Studio and its "America by Design" program to modernize federal services ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/who-is-joe-gebbia-airbnb-co-founder-becomes-first-chief-design-officer-plans-to-make-government-services-as-seamless-as-the-apple-app-store/articleshow/123479789.cms?from=mdr)).

Gebbia is best known as the designer among Airbnb's three co-founders — the Rhode Island School of Design graduate who helped turn three air mattresses in a San Francisco apartment into a global hospitality platform ([Rotman School of Management](https://www.rotman.utoronto.ca/news-events-and-ideas/rotman-magazine/back-issues/2025/fall-2025/brian-chesky/)). After stepping back from Airbnb's day-to-day operations in mid-2022, he joined Tesla's board, bought a minority stake in the San Antonio Spurs, and launched Samara, a startup building net-zero tiny houses for backyards ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/09/28/tesla-appoints-airbnb-co-founder-to-board/); [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/why-sports-teams-are-welcoming-private-equity-ownership/); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90809611/inside-an-airbnb-cofounders-latest-venture-building-tiny-backyard-homes?utm_source=newsletters&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=FastCompanyTop10-Nov%2019,%202022&leadId=799878&mkt_tok=NjEwLUxFRS04NzIAAAGINFHpCHflAU40UzbJh)).

## His first government project: the federal retirement system

Gebbia's entry into government came through a post on X in February 2025: "Excited to share I'm bringing my designer brain and start-up spirit into the government," he wrote, adding that his first project would be improving the "slow and paper-based retirement process" for federal employees ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/airbnb-cofounder-joe-gebbia-is-excited-to-join-doge/487769)). The problem was vivid. Elon Musk had complained days earlier at a February 11 Oval Office press conference that only about 10,000 federal employees could retire each month because every application was calculated by hand, written on paper, and then sent down into a mine: "There's a limestone mine where we store all the retirement paperwork," Musk said ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/airbnb-cofounder-joe-gebbia-is-excited-to-join-doge/487769)). The mine was a real place — a converted US Steel facility in Boyers, Pennsylvania, used to store government records since the 1960s ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/airbnb-cofounder-joe-gebbia-is-excited-to-join-doge/487769)). OPM's own chief information officer, Guy Cavallo, had told Federal News Network the prior year that replacing the paper system with an online platform would take "many years" ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/airbnb-cofounder-joe-gebbia-is-excited-to-join-doge/487769)).

Gebbia framed it as a design problem and a moral one. "Since leaving my operating role at Airbnb in 2022, I've been looking for the next digital design challenge. And I can think of few more important ones than volunteering to improve the user experience within our government," he wrote ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/airbnb-cofounder-joe-gebbia-is-excited-to-join-doge/487769)). His announcement carried a video from OPM showing the agency had processed an entire retirement application digitally for the first time — done in about a week, against a 64-day average for the manual process ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/airbnb-cofounder-joe-gebbia-is-excited-to-join-doge/487769)). In interviews that summer he called the old process "an injustice" to civil servants, and promised a fully online digital process taking "just a few days at most" (as reported in the coverage of the launch).

The launch happened on schedule. A May 7, 2025 memo from acting OPM Director Charles Ezell declared that effective June 2, 2025, all new federal retirement applications and supporting documents had to be submitted electronically — any newly created paper packages would be sent back — and that by July 15, 2025, OPM would run everything through its Online Retirement Application (ORA) system and stop accepting paper entirely ([Government Executive](https://www.govexec.com/pay-benefits/2025/12/opms-retirement-application-pile-remains-large-year-draws-close/410105/)).

Then came the part no launch announcement covers. By the end of 2025, OPM's retirement pileup was worse than before the digital system existed. In October and November 2025, 43,737 applications arrived at the Boyers Retirement Operations Center by mail, FedEx, or the electronic ORA system — more than triple the 13,680 received in the same two months of 2024 ([Government Executive](https://www.govexec.com/pay-benefits/2025/12/opms-retirement-application-pile-remains-large-year-draws-close/410105/)). At the end of November 2025, 48,396 applications were waiting for final processing, against 13,844 a year earlier ([Government Executive](https://www.govexec.com/pay-benefits/2025/12/opms-retirement-application-pile-remains-large-year-draws-close/410105/)). And the paperless mandate leaked: in November 2025 alone, OPM received 15,560 paper applications alongside 7,833 digital ones — "apparently, not everyone got the memo," as the federal-workforce press put it ([Government Executive](https://www.govexec.com/pay-benefits/2025/12/opms-retirement-application-pile-remains-large-year-draws-close/410105/)). Much of the surge came from the administration's own "Fork in the Road" deferred-resignation program, which pushed thousands of employees toward retirement at once ([Government Executive](https://www.govexec.com/pay-benefits/2025/12/opms-retirement-application-pile-remains-large-year-draws-close/410105/)). The system Gebbia helped ship was real — but so was the bottleneck.

## Chief Design Officer and "America by Design"

On August 21, 2025, President Trump signed an executive order creating the role of Chief Design Officer of the United States and appointing Gebbia as its first holder, to lead a new National Design Studio inside the Executive Office of the President ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/who-is-joe-gebbia-airbnb-co-founder-becomes-first-chief-design-officer-plans-to-make-government-services-as-seamless-as-the-apple-app-store/articleshow/123479789.cms?from=mdr)). The studio sits under an administrator who reports directly to the White House Chief of Staff, and every federal agency head is required to consult with Gebbia, with initial results due by July 4, 2026 — the 250th anniversary of American independence ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/who-is-joe-gebbia-airbnb-co-founder-becomes-first-chief-design-officer-plans-to-make-government-services-as-seamless-as-the-apple-app-store/articleshow/123479789.cms?from=mdr)).

Gebbia stated his mission on X in the language of product design: "My directive is to update today's government services to be as satisfying to use as the Apple Store: beautifully designed, great user experience, run on modern software" ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/who-is-joe-gebbia-airbnb-co-founder-becomes-first-chief-design-officer-plans-to-make-government-services-as-seamless-as-the-apple-app-store/articleshow/123479789.cms?from=mdr)). The scale is enormous: federal agencies manage more than 26,000 websites, many fragmented and hard to navigate, and White House officials said Gebbia would oversee redesigning roughly 26,000 federal web portals "so they better serve Social Security recipients, veterans, and all citizens" ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/who-is-joe-gebbia-airbnb-co-founder-becomes-first-chief-design-officer-plans-to-make-government-services-as-seamless-as-the-apple-app-store/articleshow/123479789.cms?from=mdr)).

The design world greeted the appointment with both excitement and a warning. In an essay for PRINT Magazine, design executive Ashleigh Axios recalled a spring 2024 conversation with Gebbia at a Rhode Island School of Design gathering in Providence, where he told the story of an early RISD class: the professor warned most students would fail, the first assignment was to design and build a chess set, and Gebbia finished it — "Airbnb was my next chess set," he said, framing every new undertaking as an intimidating challenge solvable through creativity and persistence ([PRINT Magazine](https://www.printmag.com/industry-perspectives/not-just-another-chess-set-us-chief-design-officer/)). Axios's caution was pointed: government design is not a chess set, and "the work of design in government isn't conquest, it's care" — success should be measured not by cleverness or prestige but by everyday lives made easier, safer, and more dignified, and design must clarify truth rather than decorate it ([PRINT Magazine](https://www.printmag.com/industry-perspectives/not-just-another-chess-set-us-chief-design-officer/)).

## Airbnb: from three air mattresses to a global platform

Gebbia's design-first instinct was formed at the Rhode Island School of Design, where he studied both Graphic Design and Industrial Design, took supplementary business courses at Brown University and MIT, served as student body president, and founded the school's basketball team ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/who-is-joe-gebbia-airbnb-co-founder-becomes-first-chief-design-officer-plans-to-make-government-services-as-seamless-as-the-apple-app-store/articleshow/123479789.cms?from=mdr)). It was at RISD that he met Brian Chesky, who would become his roommate and co-founder.

The founding story, as Chesky tells it, starts with unemployment. In 2008, Chesky, Gebbia, and Nathan Blecharczyk decided to rent out their San Francisco apartment to attendees of a big design conference coming to town: they inflated three air mattresses, called it "Air Bed and Breakfast," and to their surprise three designers actually stayed with them ([Rotman School of Management](https://www.rotman.utoronto.ca/news-events-and-ideas/rotman-magazine/back-issues/2025/fall-2025/brian-chesky/)). The insight, even then, was about connection — strangers staying in their home and "walking in our shoes" — more than about lodging ([Rotman School of Management](https://www.rotman.utoronto.ca/news-events-and-ideas/rotman-magazine/back-issues/2025/fall-2025/brian-chesky/)). From there the company followed the Y Combinator mantra of "make something people want," growing from a $70-million company in 2010 to a $30-billion one by 2020 — before losing 80 percent of its business in eight weeks when the pandemic hit ([Rotman School of Management](https://www.rotman.utoronto.ca/news-events-and-ideas/rotman-magazine/back-issues/2025/fall-2025/brian-chesky/)).

Gebbia was Airbnb's designer-founder through that whole run, and he credits the company's survival to its focus on design ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90809611/inside-an-airbnb-cofounders-latest-venture-building-tiny-backyard-homes?utm_source=newsletters&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=FastCompanyTop10-Nov%2019,%202022&leadId=799878&mkt_tok=NjEwLUxFRS04NzIAAAGINFHpCHflAU40UzbJh)). He stepped back from day-to-day operations in mid-2022, continuing as a board member and advisor, and also serving on the board of Airbnb.org, the company's nonprofit arm ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/09/28/tesla-appoints-airbnb-co-founder-to-board/)).

## Samara and the Backyard house

His next act after Airbnb was housing of a different kind. Samara began as an innovation studio inside Airbnb, hunting for new products and new ways to create social change; as its backyard-housing work matured, it spun out into an independent company — "as the product progressed, it got to the stage where it just made more sense to be an independent company," Gebbia said ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90809611/inside-an-airbnb-cofounders-latest-venture-building-tiny-backyard-homes?utm_source=newsletters&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=FastCompanyTop10-Nov%2019,%202022&leadId=799878&mkt_tok=NjEwLUxFRS04NzIAAAGINFHpCHflAU40UzbJh)). Its first product, launched in November 2022, was Backyard: a net-zero, factory-built tiny house for backyards — the formal name for accessory dwelling units, or ADUs ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90809611/inside-an-airbnb-cofounders-latest-venture-building-tiny-backyard-homes?utm_source=newsletters&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=FastCompanyTop10-Nov%2019,%202022&leadId=799878&mkt_tok=NjEwLUxFRS04NzIAAAGINFHpCHflAU40UzbJh)).

The houses come in two sizes — a 430-square-foot studio and a 550-square-foot one-bedroom — and are pitched as a "flexible dwelling that can adapt to new ways of living": space for in-laws, a home office, creative projects, or a rental ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90809611/inside-an-airbnb-cofounders-latest-venture-building-tiny-backyard-homes?utm_source=newsletters&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=FastCompanyTop10-Nov%2019,%202022&leadId=799878&mkt_tok=NjEwLUxFRS04NzIAAAGINFHpCHflAU40UzbJh)). They use less than half the energy of a standard house of the same size, run all-electric off rooftop solar panels, and can generate more electricity than they use ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90809611/inside-an-airbnb-cofounders-latest-venture-building-tiny-backyard-homes?utm_source=newsletters&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=FastCompanyTop10-Nov%2019,%202022&leadId=799878&mkt_tok=NjEwLUxFRS04NzIAAAGINFHpCHflAU40UzbJh)). Samara handles the whole construction process, including surveying and permits — "we were really, really focused on trying to eliminate that friction through the design of the entire customer experience," said co-founder and CEO Mike McNamara ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90809611/inside-an-airbnb-cofounders-latest-venture-building-tiny-backyard-homes?utm_source=newsletters&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=FastCompanyTop10-Nov%2019,%202022&leadId=799878&mkt_tok=NjEwLUxFRS04NzIAAAGINFHpCHflAU40UzbJh)). The studio started at $289,000 and the one-bedroom at $329,000, installation included, launching first in California, where ADU-friendly laws had made backyard building far easier ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90809611/inside-an-airbnb-cofounders-latest-venture-building-tiny-backyard-homes?utm_source=newsletters&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=FastCompanyTop10-Nov%2019,%202022&leadId=799878&mkt_tok=NjEwLUxFRS04NzIAAAGINFHpCHflAU40UzbJh)).

## Tesla board, Spurs stake, and the political turn

2022 was Gebbia's year of portfolio-building. In January he bought an undisclosed minority stake in the NBA's San Antonio Spurs — and the story behind it is pure Gebbia: an Atlanta native, one of his first jobs was as a ball boy for the hometown Hawks, and his "unofficial first startup" was restarting the RISD basketball program, which had been dormant for about 50 years ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/why-sports-teams-are-welcoming-private-equity-ownership/)). "The Spurs are just an incredible team," he said. "What the Bulls were in the '90s is what the Spurs were in the 2000s" ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/why-sports-teams-are-welcoming-private-equity-ownership/)). In September, Tesla appointed him to its board of directors as an independent director, effective September 25, replacing Oracle's Larry Ellison; per the SEC filing, Gebbia waived cash compensation and equity compensation until July 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/09/28/tesla-appoints-airbnb-co-founder-to-board/)).

His political identity shifted in public view. On January 19, 2025 — the day before Trump's inauguration — Gebbia posted on X that he had voted for Trump even though he had been voting for Democrats his "whole life," writing that the Democratic Party "aren't the same party they used to be" ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/airbnb-cofounder-joe-gebbia-is-excited-to-join-doge/487769)). A month later he was inside the administration, working with Elon Musk's efficiency effort.

## Philanthropy

Gebbia signed the Giving Pledge in 2016, and his pledge letter said he wanted to focus on building pathways for creatives and entrepreneurs — particularly those from underprivileged communities ([Inside Philanthropy](https://www.insidephilanthropy.com/home/much-more-to-come-26-young-giving-pledgers)). His giving since has ranged wider: $300,000 to RISD for a scholarship and an endowed fund; $25 million in total to Larkin Street Youth Services and All Home, supporting Bay Area homelessness efforts and families hit by pandemic-era economic hardship; and, alongside his fellow Airbnb co-founders, support for humanitarian efforts for refugees from Ukraine ([Inside Philanthropy](https://www.insidephilanthropy.com/home/much-more-to-come-26-young-giving-pledgers)).

Two 2023 gifts defined the scale of his philanthropy. He donated $25 million to The Ocean Cleanup, the Dutch nonprofit removing plastic from oceans and rivers — the largest private donation in the organization's history — saying its systems "actually work at scale" and needed funding to match ([Chronicle of Philanthropy](https://sandbox.philanthropy.com/news/airbnbs-joe-gebbia-donates-25-million-to-ocean-cleanup-nonprofit/)). The same year he committed $25 million over five years to the Malala Fund, the girls'-education organization founded by Nobel laureate Malala Yousafzai ([Inside Philanthropy](https://www.insidephilanthropy.com/home/much-more-to-come-26-young-giving-pledgers)).
