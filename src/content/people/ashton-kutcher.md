## Overview

In July 2026, Ashton Kutcher walked away from the firm that made him Silicon Valley's most credible celebrity investor. After 11 years, he exited Sound Ventures — the venture capital firm he founded with talent manager and technology investor Guy Oseary — to launch a new firm with veteran VC executive Morgan Beller, aimed at the energy and infrastructure powering artificial intelligence rather than the AI labs themselves ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/victoria-salves/ashton-kutcher-leaving-sound-ventures-to-launch-new-vc-firm-focused-on-artificial-intelligence/91368974)). The split is only partial: Kutcher stays on as an adviser to Sound Ventures, while his former co-founder and general partner [Effie Epstein](/people/effie-epstein/) will advise the new firm ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/victoria-salves/ashton-kutcher-leaving-sound-ventures-to-launch-new-vc-firm-focused-on-artificial-intelligence/91368974)). It is a move that a Harvard Business School scholar told Inc. is genuinely unusual — according to research by Josh Lerner and Diana Noble, only 3.6 percent of venture firms experienced a leadership transition at the top during a five-year period ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/victoria-salves/ashton-kutcher-leaving-sound-ventures-to-launch-new-vc-firm-focused-on-artificial-intelligence/91368974)). Most actors who dabble in investing lend their name to a fund. Kutcher spent a decade and a half becoming the rare exception — and then, at the top of the AI trade, decided to start over.

## The $2.5 million bet

Kutcher became active in technology investing in 2010, when he formed A-Grade Investments with Oseary and billionaire Ron Burkle — Burkle put in $8 million, while Kutcher and Oseary each contributed $1 million of their own ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/in-2011-ashton-kutcher-guy-oseary-and-ron-burkle-invested-2-5-million-in-airbnb-5-years-later-their-stake-was-worth-about-90-million-as-the-startup-became-a-global-giant/articleshow/134336809.cms?from=mdr)). In 2011 the trio put $2.5 million into Airbnb, and Kutcher joined the company as a strategic adviser, working on community engagement and international expansion ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/in-2011-ashton-kutcher-guy-oseary-and-ron-burkle-invested-2-5-million-in-airbnb-5-years-later-their-stake-was-worth-about-90-million-as-the-startup-became-a-global-giant/articleshow/134336809.cms?from=mdr)). By March 2016, Forbes reported that the stake was worth about $90 million — the three investors' collective position, not a personal $90 million windfall for Kutcher ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/in-2011-ashton-kutcher-guy-oseary-and-ron-burkle-invested-2-5-million-in-airbnb-5-years-later-their-stake-was-worth-about-90-million-as-the-startup-became-a-global-giant/articleshow/134336809.cms?from=mdr)). The same portfolio also included Uber, Spotify, Pinterest, Shazam and Warby Parker ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/in-2011-ashton-kutcher-guy-oseary-and-ron-burkle-invested-2-5-million-in-airbnb-5-years-later-their-stake-was-worth-about-90-million-as-the-startup-became-a-global-giant/articleshow/134336809.cms?from=mdr)).

Sound Ventures, the successor firm, became known for early bets on the category-leading AI labs, including OpenAI and Anthropic, alongside companies like Gusto, Brex and Fei-Fei Li's World Labs spatial-intelligence startup ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/victoria-salves/ashton-kutcher-leaving-sound-ventures-to-launch-new-vc-firm-focused-on-artificial-intelligence/91368974)). The 2026 split, by multiple reports, came down to a misalignment on investment stage: Sound Ventures had drifted toward more established startups, while Kutcher wanted to concentrate on younger companies at the early stages — specifically the foundational layers of AI, including energy and specialized engineering breakthroughs ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/victoria-salves/ashton-kutcher-leaving-sound-ventures-to-launch-new-vc-firm-focused-on-artificial-intelligence/91368974)). Beller, his new partner, came from NFX and Meta's Libra project ([HeadlinesBriefing](https://headlinesbriefing.com/private-markets/techcrunch-venture/ashton-kutcher-launches-new-ai-focused-vc-firm-da3a8f55)). "Most of the U.S. VC investment in recent years have gone to the foundational LLMs themselves," Lerner told Inc., adding that the best recent returns had come from "giving capital to proven winners in the hopes that they will prove to be super-winners" ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/victoria-salves/ashton-kutcher-leaving-sound-ventures-to-launch-new-vc-firm-focused-on-artificial-intelligence/91368974)).

## From Kelso to Walden Schmidt

Christopher Ashton Kutcher was born on February 7, 1978, in Cedar Rapids, Iowa, to Diane Finnegan, a Procter & Gamble employee, and Larry Kutcher, a factory worker; he has a fraternal twin brother, Michael, and a sister, Tausha, and grew up in rural Homestead, Iowa ([IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0005110/)). In 1997 he was a biochemical engineering student at the University of Iowa when a local talent scout discovered him ([IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0005110/)). His star-making part came in 1998 as Michael Kelso, the dim-but-lovable pretty boy on Fox's *That '70s Show*, and he later created, hosted and executive produced MTV's prank show *Punk'd* ([IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0005110/); [Parade](https://parade.com/117497/parade/ashton-kutcher-two-and-a-half-men/)).

The defining casting announcement of 2011 television came on Friday, May 13, when CBS and Warner Bros. Television confirmed that Kutcher would replace the fired [Charlie Sheen](/people/charlie-sheen/) as the lead of *Two and a Half Men* ([Parade](https://parade.com/117497/parade/ashton-kutcher-two-and-a-half-men/)). "I can't wait to get to work with this ridiculously talented 2.5 team and I believe we can fill the stage with laughter that will echo in viewers' homes," Kutcher said in the release. "I can't replace Charlie Sheen but I'm going to work my ass off to entertain the hell out of people!" ([Parade](https://parade.com/117497/parade/ashton-kutcher-two-and-a-half-men/)). Show creator Chuck Lorre was openly relieved: "We are so lucky to have someone as talented, joyful and just plain remarkable as Ashton joining our family. Added to that is the deep sigh of relief knowing that our family stays together. If I was any happier, it'd be illegal." ([Parade](https://parade.com/117497/parade/ashton-kutcher-two-and-a-half-men/)). The night before the announcement, Kutcher had teased the casting on Twitter with a riddle — "What's the square root of 6.25?" — whose answer is 2.5 ([Parade](https://parade.com/117497/parade/ashton-kutcher-two-and-a-half-men/)). It was his first regular series acting gig since *That '70s Show* ended in 2006; in between he had been making movies and executive producing television ([Parade](https://parade.com/117497/parade/ashton-kutcher-two-and-a-half-men/)). He stayed on the show through 2015, playing tech billionaire Walden Schmidt ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/in-2011-ashton-kutcher-guy-oseary-and-ron-burkle-invested-2-5-million-in-airbnb-5-years-later-their-stake-was-worth-about-90-million-as-the-startup-became-a-global-giant/articleshow/134336809.cms?from=mdr)). Afterward came Netflix's *The Ranch* (2016–2020) and the 2023 film *Your Place or Mine* ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/us/in-2011-ashton-kutcher-guy-oseary-and-ron-burkle-invested-2-5-million-in-airbnb-5-years-later-their-stake-was-worth-about-90-million-as-the-startup-became-a-global-giant/articleshow/134336809.cms?from=mdr)).

## The Thorn resignation

Kutcher's parallel identity has long been the anti-trafficking advocate. He co-founded the organization that became Thorn — built to combat child sexual exploitation — with his then-wife Demi Moore, and in 2017 he testified before the Senate Foreign Relations Committee on ending human trafficking and modern slavery ([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/splash/ashton-kutcher-resigns-anti-child-abuse-charity-over-support-rapist-danny-masterson-701426)). That credibility is what made September 2023 so damaging. After his *That '70s Show* co-star Danny Masterson was sentenced to 30 years to life in prison for raping two women, Kutcher and his wife Mila Kunis — whom he married in 2015 — wrote letters to Los Angeles Superior Court Judge Charlaine Olmedo in Masterson's support ([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/splash/ashton-kutcher-resigns-anti-child-abuse-charity-over-support-rapist-danny-masterson-701426)). In his letter Kutcher described Masterson as "nothing but a positive influence" and "an extraordinarily honest and intentional human being," and said he did not think Masterson was an "ongoing harm to society"; Kunis called the convicted rapist "an amazing friend, confidant, and above all, an outstanding older brother figure" ([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/splash/ashton-kutcher-resigns-anti-child-abuse-charity-over-support-rapist-danny-masterson-701426)).

When the letters became public, the backlash forced a reckoning. In a statement to Time, Kutcher resigned as chairman of Thorn's board, effective immediately: "After my wife and I spent several days of listening, personal reflection, learning, and conversations with survivors and the employees and leadership at Thorn, I have determined the responsible thing for me to do is resign as Chairman of the Board" ([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/splash/ashton-kutcher-resigns-anti-child-abuse-charity-over-support-rapist-danny-masterson-701426)). "I cannot allow my error in judgment to distract from our efforts and the children we serve," he added, acknowledging that the character statement was "yet another painful instance of questioning victims who are brave enough to share their experiences" — precisely what his organization had worked a decade to reverse ([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/splash/ashton-kutcher-resigns-anti-child-abuse-charity-over-support-rapist-danny-masterson-701426)). He closed with a "heartfelt apology to all victims of sexual violence and everyone at Thorn who I hurt by what I did," while saying he remains proud of Thorn's work over the past ten years ([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/splash/ashton-kutcher-resigns-anti-child-abuse-charity-over-support-rapist-danny-masterson-701426)). The couple later posted a video statement insisting they "support victims" and had not written the letters to "question the legitimacy of the judicial system or the validity of the jury's ruling" ([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/splash/ashton-kutcher-resigns-anti-child-abuse-charity-over-support-rapist-danny-masterson-701426)).
