## The 2025 return: *Tha Carter VI*

On June 6, 2025, Lil Wayne released *Tha Carter VI* through Young Money and Republic Records — his fourteenth studio album, 19 tracks deep, and his first solo album in five years ([Universal Music Canada](https://www.universalmusic.ca/2025/06/06/lil-wayne-releases-tha-carter-vi/)). In sessions with Rolling Stone ahead of the release, Wayne described the record's shift in approach: "If there's one thing about this album that's different, it's me approaching it like, 'Man, what would I sound like on something with such and such?'" ([REVOLT](https://www.revolt.tv/article/lil-wayne-tha-carter-vi-release-date-features-and-msg-debut-explained)).

That curiosity produced one of the strangest guest lists of his career. Opera legend Andrea Bocelli sings "Ave Maria" on a deeply personal cut about Wayne's early health struggles; Bono, MGK, Jelly Roll, Big Sean, 2 Chainz, Wyclef Jean, Kodak Black and his sons Kameron Carter and Lil Novi all appear across the project ([REVOLT](https://www.revolt.tv/article/lil-wayne-tha-carter-vi-release-date-features-and-msg-debut-explained); [Universal Music Canada](https://www.universalmusic.ca/2025/06/06/lil-wayne-releases-tha-carter-vi/)). Wyclef Jean recorded more than 30 tracks with Wayne, longtime collaborator Wheezy returned, a Kanye West-produced song looked unlikely to make the final cut, and a Billie Eilish sample — once mistaken for a full feature — was still being cleared ([REVOLT](https://www.revolt.tv/article/lil-wayne-tha-carter-vi-release-date-features-and-msg-debut-explained)).

The emotional center is "The Days," the Bono collaboration. The track dates to 2013–2014 and was produced by RedOne; on it Wayne raps about lying in a hospital bed after epileptic seizures — seizures he has lived with since childhood — while Bono delivers the hook and Kameron contributes a prepubescent voice note ([U2 Songs](https://www.u2songs.com/discography/lil_wayne_tha_carter_vi_album)). Rolling Stone broke news of the Bono collaboration in April 2025, and the song premiered in marketing for the 2025 NBA Finals; it appears on streaming and digital editions but not on CD or vinyl ([U2 Songs](https://www.u2songs.com/discography/lil_wayne_tha_carter_vi_album); [HipHopDX](https://hiphopdx.com/news/lil-waynes-tha-carter-vi-debuts-number-two-billboard-200/)).

The album art, revealed in an Instagram post on April 22, pairs a childhood photo of Wayne with tattoos superimposed on his face — keeping with the themes of earlier *Carter* covers ([REVOLT](https://www.revolt.tv/article/lil-wayne-tha-carter-vi-release-date-features-and-msg-debut-explained)).

Commercially the album landed short of its predecessors. *Tha Carter VI* debuted at No. 2 on the Billboard 200 with 108,000 equivalent album units — about 73,000 from streaming (97.06 million on-demand streams), roughly 34,000 in pure sales and 1,000 in track-equivalent units — behind Morgan Wallen's *I'm the Problem*, then in its fourth week at No. 1 ([HipHopDX](https://hiphopdx.com/news/lil-waynes-tha-carter-vi-debuts-number-two-billboard-200/); [Hypebeast](https://hypebeast.com/2025/6/lil-wayne-tha-carter-vi-no-2-debut-billboard-200)). The debut was the lowest of any *Carter* entry and of Wayne's entire near-30-year career, and reviews were largely negative ([HipHopDX](https://hiphopdx.com/news/lil-waynes-tha-carter-vi-debuts-number-two-billboard-200/)). The contrast with the past was sharp: *Funeral* (2020) had debuted at No. 1 with 139,000 units, and *Tha Carter V* (2018) opened with 480,000 ([HipHopDX](https://hiphopdx.com/news/lil-waynes-tha-carter-vi-debuts-number-two-billboard-200/)). It was still Wayne's 13th top-10 album, and it topped the Top Rap Albums chart, ending Kendrick Lamar's *GNX* run there ([Hypebeast](https://hypebeast.com/2025/6/lil-wayne-tha-carter-vi-no-2-debut-billboard-200)).

Wayne marked the release the biggest way he could: headlining Madison Square Garden for the first time in his career on release night, then announcing a 34-city tour ([Universal Music Canada](https://www.universalmusic.ca/2025/06/06/lil-wayne-releases-tha-carter-vi/)). He had once told Rolling Stone he would only return to New York for "U2 money" — and the show arrived on the heels of his highly publicized Super Bowl halftime show snub in his native New Orleans ([REVOLT](https://www.revolt.tv/article/lil-wayne-tha-carter-vi-release-date-features-and-msg-debut-explained)). Even as the reviews landed, he kept a famous fan: Justin Bieber posted screenshots of a FaceTime call with his "I'm the One" collaborator, checking in with the GOAT ([HipHopDX](https://hiphopdx.com/news/lil-waynes-tha-carter-vi-debuts-number-two-billboard-200/)).

## The climb: Hollygrove to the *Carter* series

Wayne was born Dwayne Michael Carter Jr. on September 27, 1982, in New Orleans, Louisiana ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/lil-wayne/4037/)). He began rapping and writing lyrics at eight, growing up without a father in the Hollygrove neighborhood, raised by his mother ([History Tools](https://www.historytools.org/people/lil-wayne)). "I just expressed how I grew up," he said of the hunger in his music in a 2010 interview. "How I came from nothing to something" ([History Tools](https://www.historytools.org/people/lil-wayne)). At nine he met rapper and entrepreneur Bryan Williams — Birdman — who signed the young rapper to Cash Money Records before his teens ([History Tools](https://www.historytools.org/people/lil-wayne)).

His career took off in 1997, at 15, when he joined the Hot Boys alongside Juvenile, B.G. and Turk; their debut *Get It How You Live* sold more than 400,000 copies and earned a platinum plaque ([History Tools](https://www.historytools.org/people/lil-wayne)). His solo debut, *Tha Block Is Hot*, arrived in 1999 when he was 17, went platinum and peaked at No. 3 on the Billboard 200 ([History Tools](https://www.historytools.org/people/lil-wayne); [Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/lil-wayne/4037/)).

*Tha Carter* (2004), with "Bring It Back" and "Go D.J.," made him one of rap's elite; *Tha Carter II* (2005) sold more than 238,000 copies in its first week ([History Tools](https://www.historytools.org/people/lil-wayne)). Then came *Tha Carter III* (2008): 3.6 million copies sold, the "Lollipop" / "Got Money" / "A Milli" run, and four Grammy wins including Best Rap Album ([History Tools](https://www.historytools.org/people/lil-wayne)). "Lollipop," featuring Static Major, was his first No. 1 hit ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/lil-wayne/4037/)).

## The Grammy record

[The Recording Academy](/organizations/recording-academy/) credits Wayne with five wins from 28 nominations ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/lil-wayne/4037/)). His first nomination came for 2005, as a featured artist on Destiny's Child's "Soldier" ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/lil-wayne/4037/)). The first wins were the four he took at the 51st GRAMMYs for 2008 — Best Rap Album (*Tha Carter III*) and Best Rap Song ("Lollipop") among them — and the fifth came in 2017 for Best Rap Performance on Chance the Rapper's "No Problem" with 2 Chainz ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/lil-wayne/4037/)). He made his Grammy stage debut at the same ceremony, performing "Swagga Like Us" with M.I.A., T.I., Jay-Z and Kanye West, then joining Robin Thicke, Allen Toussaint and the Dirty Dozen Brass Band for "Big Chief" ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/lil-wayne/4037/)). In 2026 he was nominated again for Best Rap Song for his turn on Tyler, The Creator's "Sticky" with GloRilla and Sexyy Red ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/lil-wayne/4037/)).

## The label builder

Wayne founded [Young Money Entertainment](/organizations/young-money-entertainment/) as an imprint of Cash Money Records; the label has released No. 1 albums from Drake and [Nicki Minaj](/people/nicki-minaj/) ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/artists/lil-wayne/4037/)). In August 2009 he signed Minaj — news of the deal broke August 1, 2009, months after Drake joined the roster — making her the label's new First Lady, with a debut album project planned for 2010 ([HipHopRX](https://www.hiphoprx.com/2009/09/01/nicki-minaj-signs-on-as-new-1st-lady-of-lil-waynes-young-money-label/)).

Three decades in, the business has widened: through Young Money APAA Sports he represents athletes including Travis Hunter, while he still turns up on other artists' records — features for Tyler, The Creator and Cordae among them ([REVOLT](https://www.revolt.tv/article/lil-wayne-tha-carter-vi-release-date-features-and-msg-debut-explained)).
