## Overview

Toby Gabriner is the adtech veteran who took over [AdRoll](/organizations/adroll/) from its co-founder, split it into two businesses, and then renamed the whole company — the executive who turned a retargeting specialist into NextRoll. He had nearly two decades of digital advertising experience before AdRoll, including serving as president of Carat Interactive, CEO of [x+1], president and CEO of Adap.tv, and CEO of Adzilla, and he joined AdRoll first as an advisor before serving as its president ([MRWeb](http://www.mrweb.com/drno/news25311.htm)). In November 2017 he became AdRoll's CEO, replacing co-founder [Aaron Bell](/people/aaron-bell/), who moved to the full-time post of chief product officer ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/09/10/adroll-nextroll/); [MRWeb](http://www.mrweb.com/drno/news25311.htm)).

## The handover and the split

Gabriner's promotion was presented as a partnership handover. Bell, who would oversee engineering, product, user experience, data science, and supply, said he and Gabriner had forged a remarkable partnership over the previous twelve months, and welcomed the chance to draw on his passion for innovation in the new role ([MRWeb](http://www.mrweb.com/drno/news25311.htm)). At the time AdRoll had nearly 500 employees, with six additional offices in New York, Chicago, Tokyo, London, Dublin, and Sydney ([MRWeb](http://www.mrweb.com/drno/news25311.htm)).

Within months Gabriner reorganized the company around the problem he had inherited: a business that had outgrown its label. In February 2018 AdRoll split into [RollWorks](/organizations/rollworks/), focused solely on business-to-business marketing, and the legacy AdRoll business serving small to mid-size eCommerce companies, each with its own profit-and-loss statement under the newly formed AdRoll Group parent ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/ad-exchange-news/adroll-rebrands-splits-b2b-b2c-divisions/)). Gabriner became CEO of the group and president of RollWorks, Bell became chief of product for the group, and Scott Gifis became president of AdRoll ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/ad-exchange-news/adroll-rebrands-splits-b2b-b2c-divisions/)). The rationale, in Gabriner's words, was clarity: positioning the company as all things to all people had become challenging for customers to understand, in terms of what they were getting and what it specialized in ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/ad-exchange-news/adroll-rebrands-splits-b2b-b2c-divisions/)). The timing was strong — 2017 had finished as AdRoll's biggest revenue year, and the year it reached profitability ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/ad-exchange-news/adroll-rebrands-splits-b2b-b2c-divisions/)).

## The NextRoll rename

The second act came in September 2019. The AdRoll Group became NextRoll Inc., a change Gabriner said was designed to reflect the company's moves beyond retargeting: "We have, for the longest time, been pigeonholed as a retargeting company, but the reality is that we have really been evolving" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/09/10/adroll-nextroll/)). The AdRoll retargeting business did not go away — it continued as one business unit — while RollWorks, which offered business-to-business marketing tools, became a second unit, and a third, NextRoll Platform Services, launched as a marketing-technology-as-a-service offering, selling access to AdRoll's underlying technology through application programming interfaces as Channels-as-a-Service and Audiences-as-a-Service ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/09/10/adroll-nextroll/)).

Gabriner was candid that the rename had been planned all along. When RollWorks launched the previous year, both business units had continued under the AdRoll Group umbrella, but that was always, in his words, a temporary placeholder; a year and a half into the RollWorks brand launch, with the brand firmly planted on its own two feet, moving to the NextRoll brand made sense and had always been planned ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/09/10/adroll-nextroll/)). The pitch to customers was the same one behind the split: externalize what AdRoll had built for itself — services customers had already been pulling the company to provide — and make them commercially friendly for developers and other ad products to use ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/09/10/adroll-nextroll/)).
