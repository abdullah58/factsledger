## Overview

Pope John Paul II, born Karol Józef Wojtyła in [Poland](/places/poland/) in 1920, was pope from 16 October 1978 until his death on 2 April 2005 — the first Polish pope, the first non-Italian pope in 455 years, and the holder of the third-longest pontificate in history, whose nine-day pilgrimage to his homeland in June 1979 helped awaken the Solidarity movement and the peaceful collapse of communism in Eastern Europe ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Saint-John-Paul-II)). He was the most travelled pope in history, visiting more than 125 countries, and was canonized in 2014, less than a decade after his death ([US Congress](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-149/issue-167/house-section/article/H11469-1)). This is John Paul II — not John Paul I, whose 33-day reign in 1978 he succeeded — and not any other pope of that name.

## From Wadowice to Kraków

Wojtyła was born on 18 May 1920 in Wadowice; his mother died in 1929, his elder brother Edmund in 1932, and his father in 1941 ([Vatican](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/biografia/documents/hf_jp-ii_spe_20190722_biografia.html)). He enrolled at the Jagiellonian University in 1938, but the Nazis closed it in 1939, and he worked in a quarry and then the Solvay chemical factory from 1940 to 1944 ([Vatican](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/biografia/documents/hf_jp-ii_spe_20190722_biografia.html)). In 1942 he entered the clandestine seminary in Kraków under Archbishop Adam Stefan Sapieha, helped organize the underground Rhapsodic Theatre, and was ordained a priest on 1 November 1946 ([Vatican](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/biografia/documents/hf_jp-ii_spe_20190722_biografia.html)). He earned a doctorate in theology in Rome in 1948, taught ethics at Kraków and Lublin, and wrote *Love and Responsibility* (1960) ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Saint-John-Paul-II)). Made auxiliary bishop in 1958, archbishop in 1964 and cardinal in 1967, he took part in the Second Vatican Council and helped draft *Gaudium et Spes* ([Vatican](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/biografia/documents/hf_jp-ii_spe_20190722_biografia.html)). As cardinal he defied the communist authorities to build a church in Nowa Huta, consecrating its Ark Church in 1977 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Saint-John-Paul-II)).

## Nine days in Poland and the fall of communism

Wojtyła began with the words "Be not afraid!" ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Saint-John-Paul-II)). His nine-day pilgrimage to Poland in June 1979 told Poles their faith meant they had a right to be free, and the sight of themselves in huge crowds showed them their own strength ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Saint-John-Paul-II)). Strikes in Gdańsk fourteen months later produced Solidarity, which the pope urged to press for change peacefully ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Saint-John-Paul-II)). On 13 May 1981 Mehmet Ali Ağca shot him in St Peter's Square and nearly killed him; he forgave Ağca and visited him in prison in 1983 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Saint-John-Paul-II)). Martial law in December 1981 could not break it: Solidarity was legalized in April 1989 and won the June elections, Gorbachev visited the Vatican that year, and the Soviet Union collapsed two years later ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Saint-John-Paul-II)). Thatcher credited him with providing "the main impetus for the revival of Solidarity", and Gorbachev said everything in Eastern Europe "would have been impossible without this pope" ([US Congress](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-149/issue-167/house-section/article/H11469-1)).

## A travelling pope: scale, teaching and sainthood

No pope had governed by presence on this scale. He made 104 international journeys and 146 pastoral visits in [Italy](/places/italy/) ([Vatican](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/biografia/documents/hf_jp-ii_spe_20190722_biografia.html)). He travelled nearly three-quarters of a million miles to more than 125 countries ([US Congress](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-149/issue-167/house-section/article/H11469-1)), a presence no earlier pope had attempted, which carried his moral authority to crowds no predecessor could reach. His first [United States](/places/united-states/) visit, in October 1979, included [Chicago](/places/chicago/) ([US Department of State](https://history.state.gov/departmenthistory/visits/holy-see)), where he drove by the [Copernicus Foundation](/organizations/copernicus-foundation/)'s [Copernicus Center](/organizations/copernicus-center/) from O'Hare airport while the Polish community waited in its parking lot ([Block Club Chicago](https://blockclubchicago.org/2021/07/26/jefferson-parks-copernicus-foundation-marks-50-years-with-history-book-this-place-is-a-legacy/)). He issued 14 encyclicals, promulgated the Catechism of the Catholic Church, and made the first papal visits to synagogues and mosques ([Vatican](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/biografia/documents/hf_jp-ii_spe_20190722_biografia.html); [US Congress](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-149/issue-167/house-section/article/H11469-1)). He canonized 482 saints, and died on 2 April 2005; two million people attended his funeral ([Vatican](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/en/biografia/documents/hf_jp-ii_spe_20190722_biografia.html); [HISTORY](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/May-13/pope-john-paul-ii-shot)). Beatified in 2011 and canonized in 2014, he remains, in Britannica's verdict, the first globally oriented pope, yet one whose slow response to the early clergy abuse crisis tarnished his legacy for many survivors ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Saint-John-Paul-II)).

## FAQ

### Who was Pope John Paul II?

Pope John Paul II, born Karol Józef Wojtyła in Wadowice, Poland, in 1920, was pope from 1978 to 2005. He was the first Polish pope and the first non-Italian pope in 455 years, and his pontificate of more than 26 years was the third longest in history.

### How did John Paul II help end communism in Poland?

His nine-day pilgrimage to Poland in June 1979 told Poles their faith meant they had a right to be free, and the sight of huge orderly crowds showed them their own strength. He then backed the Solidarity trade union and urged nonviolent change until it was legalized and won elections in 1989.

### Who shot Pope John Paul II?

Mehmet Ali Ağca, a 23-year-old Turkish man linked to the Grey Wolves, shot him in St Peter's Square on 13 May 1981. The pope nearly died, forgave Ağca publicly, visited him in prison in 1983, and Ağca was pardoned in June 2000 at the pope's request.

### How much did John Paul II travel?

He made 104 international journeys and 146 pastoral visits in Italy, travelling nearly three-quarters of a million miles to more than 125 countries. More than 17.6 million pilgrims attended his Wednesday General Audiences, and six million attended the 1995 Manila World Youth Day closing Mass.

### When was John Paul II canonized?

He was beatified on 1 May 2011 by Pope Benedict XVI and canonized on 27 April 2014 by Pope Francis, together with Pope John XXIII, less than a decade after his death. His feast day is 22 October, the anniversary of the inauguration of his pontificate.

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not date the assassination attempt to 3 May 1981, as the Vatican's biographical profile does; Britannica, HISTORY and the Congressional Record all give 13 May 1981, which this profile uses and flags as a discrepancy.
- It does not resolve the December 1963 versus 13 January 1964 dates for his appointment as Archbishop of Kraków, or the 1948 versus 1953 dating of his second doctorate; both are flagged in the open items.
- It does not assert that he visited 129 countries; the tier1 source read in full says "more than 125", and the higher figure was not verified here.
- It does not claim he was hosted inside the Copernicus Center: the Block Club Chicago history piece, read in full on 2026-11-28, says he drove by it from O'Hare airport in 1979 while the Polish community waited in the parking lot.
- It does not diagnose his final illness; Parkinson's disease is widely reported but was not confirmed by the Vatican until 2003, so the profile describes only his visibly declining health.
- It does not assess the full extent of his role in the collapse of the [Soviet Union](/places/soviet-union/) beyond the sourced Thatcher and Gorbachev assessments; historians continue to debate how far moral authority, rather than economic and political forces, drove that collapse.
- "Pope John Paul II" here means Karol Józef Wojtyła, pope 1978–2005 — not John Paul I, who reigned for 33 days in 1978, and not Nicolaus Copernicus, namesake of the Chicago foundation linked above.
