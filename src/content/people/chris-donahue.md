The most famous photograph of the end of America's longest war shows a lone soldier in night-vision green, boarding the last C-17 out of Kabul at 11:59 p.m. on August 30, 2021 — one minute before the deadline ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/news/your-military/2026/06/24/gen-christopher-donahue-to-unexpectedly-relinquish-command-of-army-europe-and-africa/)). That soldier was Maj. Gen. Christopher Donahue, then commander of the 82nd Airborne Division, the last American to leave Afghanistan after nearly 20 years of war ([Associated Press](https://www.military.com/top-army-general-who-was-last-us-soldier-to-leave-afghanistan-is-suddenly-leaving-his-post)). Five years later, as a four-star general commanding U.S. Army forces across Europe and Africa, Donahue was forced out after just 18 months in the job — the latest senior officer to fall in Defense Secretary [Pete Hegseth](/people/pete-hegseth/)'s reshaping of the Pentagon ([Associated Press](https://www.military.com/top-army-general-who-was-last-us-soldier-to-leave-afghanistan-is-suddenly-leaving-his-post)).

## The operator

Donahue is a graduate of the United States Military Academy at West Point, commissioned in 1992 ([U.S. Army Europe and Africa](https://www.europeafrica.army.mil/Who-We-Are/Leadership/Leaders-Article-View/Article/1418980/commanding-general/)). He spent the defining stretch of his career in the shadows: more than two decades in Army special operations, including commanding Delta Force units in Iraq and Afghanistan ([Associated Press](https://www.military.com/top-army-general-who-was-last-us-soldier-to-leave-afghanistan-is-suddenly-leaving-his-post)). The Army's own biography of him describes 16 years across special-operations organizations — platoon leader, troop commander, squadron commander, brigade commander — plus a tour as director of operations for Joint Special Operations Command ([U.S. Army Europe and Africa](https://www.europeafrica.army.mil/Who-We-Are/Leadership/Leaders-Article-View/Article/1418980/commanding-general/)). He deployed more than 20 times, in support of operations from Iraqi Freedom and Enduring Freedom to Inherent Resolve, Atlantic Resolve and the Sudan crisis ([U.S. Army Europe and Africa](https://www.europeafrica.army.mil/Who-We-Are/Leadership/Leaders-Article-View/Article/1418980/commanding-general/)).

His other assignments read like a tour of the Army's hardest jobs: commander of the NATO Special Operations Component and U.S. Joint Special Operations in support of Operation Freedom's Sentinel, deputy director for special operations and counter-terrorism on the Joint Staff, commandant of the U.S. Army Infantry School, and deputy commanding general of the 4th Infantry Division ([U.S. Army Europe and Africa](https://www.europeafrica.army.mil/Who-We-Are/Leadership/Leaders-Article-View/Article/1418980/commanding-general/)). His military education included the Naval War College and a U.S. Army War College fellowship at Harvard University ([U.S. Army Europe and Africa](https://www.europeafrica.army.mil/Who-We-Are/Leadership/Leaders-Article-View/Article/1418980/commanding-general/)).

## Kabul

Donahue commanded the 82nd Airborne Division from July 2020 to March 2022 ([Associated Press](https://www.military.com/top-army-general-who-was-last-us-soldier-to-leave-afghanistan-is-suddenly-leaving-his-post)). In August 2021 he oversaw security at Hamid Karzai International Airport during the frenzied U.S. withdrawal, and on the night of August 30 he became the last U.S. soldier to depart the country ([Associated Press](https://www.military.com/top-army-general-who-was-last-us-soldier-to-leave-afghanistan-is-suddenly-leaving-his-post)). The night-vision photograph of him boarding the final cargo plane became one of the defining images of the end of a war that had begun with the September 11, 2001 attacks ([Associated Press](https://www.military.com/top-army-general-who-was-last-us-soldier-to-leave-afghanistan-is-suddenly-leaving-his-post)).

Within the [Army](/organizations/us-army/), Donahue's leadership during the evacuation drew bipartisan praise, and he was widely seen as a top officer who could one day lead the service or even become chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff ([Associated Press](https://www.military.com/top-army-general-who-was-last-us-soldier-to-leave-afghanistan-is-suddenly-leaving-his-post)). He went on to command the XVIII Airborne Corps — the 39th commander, based at Fort Bragg — before his final assignment ([U.S. Army Europe and Africa](https://www.europeafrica.army.mil/Who-We-Are/Leadership/Leaders-Article-View/Article/1418980/commanding-general/)).

## Europe and Africa

Donahue assumed command of U.S. Army Europe and Africa in December 2024, dual-hatted as commander of NATO's Allied Land Command ([U.S. Army Europe and Africa](https://www.europeafrica.army.mil/Who-We-Are/Leadership/Leaders-Article-View/Article/1418980/commanding-general/)). His 18-month tenure centered on a single big idea: the Eastern Flank Deterrence Initiative, a warfighting concept designed to integrate U.S. and NATO land forces in support of the alliance's regional defense plans ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/293686/usareur_af_commander_relinquishes_command)). The Army's account of his command describes a digital backbone connecting forces and nations, a common operating picture, and the integration of manned and unmanned systems at scale — built for a battlefield saturated with drones and electronic warfare ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/293686/usareur_af_commander_relinquishes_command)).

At his July 2, 2026 relinquishment ceremony at Clay Kaserne in Wiesbaden, Germany, the supreme allied commander for Europe, Gen. Alexus Grynkewich, offered the verdict: "(Donahue) saw the need to change, developed a plan, inspired others and built the processes to ensure it endures well beyond his tenure — and Allies have bought in" ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/293686/usareur_af_commander_relinquishes_command)). Donahue himself told the crowd: "I love this team — it has been the honor of a lifetime to be a part of it. I'm proud of what we built and I have absolute confidence in what you will build next" ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/293686/usareur_af_commander_relinquishes_command)).

## The forced exit

The Atlantic first reported on June 23, 2026 that Donahue would step down; the Army confirmed the next day that he would relinquish command on July 2, offering no reason for the abrupt change ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/news/your-military/2026/06/24/gen-christopher-donahue-to-unexpectedly-relinquish-command-of-army-europe-and-africa/)). "Gen. Christopher Donahue, commanding general of U.S. Army Europe and Africa and commander of NATO's Allied Land Command, will relinquish command on July 2, 2026," Army spokeswoman Cynthia O. Smith said ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/news/your-military/2026/06/24/gen-christopher-donahue-to-unexpectedly-relinquish-command-of-army-europe-and-africa/)). Maj. Gen. Christopher Norrie, his deputy, would perform the duties of commanding general until a permanent successor is nominated by the president and confirmed by the Senate ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/article/293686/usareur_af_commander_relinquishes_command)).

Donahue's exit fit a pattern. He was the latest in a line of nearly two dozen top military leaders to retire or depart early under Hegseth, who has pursued thinning the top ranks with the mantra "less generals, more GIs" ([Associated Press](https://www.military.com/top-army-general-who-was-last-us-soldier-to-leave-afghanistan-is-suddenly-leaving-his-post)). In April, Hegseth had asked Army Chief of Staff [Gen. Randy George](/people/randy-george/) to step down ahead of the typical four-year term ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/news/your-military/2026/06/24/gen-christopher-donahue-to-unexpectedly-relinquish-command-of-army-europe-and-africa/)). CBS News reported, via SOFX, that Donahue had "earned the ire" of Hegseth — though the two had met in person only once, in February 2025 ([SOFX](https://www.sofx.com/army-general-who-was-last-u-s-soldier-to-leave-afghanistan-to-relinquish-europe-command/)).

An Army official, speaking anonymously about sensitive discussions, told the AP that Donahue's departure came as the Army was discussing downgrading U.S. Army Europe and Africa from a four-star to a three-star command — a move that would come amid Hegseth's criticism of European allies and a six-month Pentagon review of American forces in Europe "designed to ensure that NATO is moving fast and irreversibly toward Europe leading, stepping up to take primary responsibility for the defense of Europe" ([Associated Press](https://www.military.com/top-army-general-who-was-last-us-soldier-to-leave-afghanistan-is-suddenly-leaving-his-post)).
