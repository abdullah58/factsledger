Ghislaine Maxwell's legal options have run out one by one — and the last of them closed in August 2026, when a federal judge threw out her post-conviction challenge in blunt terms. Judge Paul Engelmayer of the Southern District of New York rejected Maxwell's bid to overturn her sex-trafficking conviction, ruling that her claims her constitutional rights were violated at trial and sentencing were "all meritless and all or almost all are frivolous" ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2025351)). Months earlier, in a virtual February 2026 appearance before the [House Oversight Committee](/organizations/house-oversight-committee/), she had invoked the Fifth Amendment's protection against self-incrimination — while her lawyer announced on the same day that she would agree to testify if granted clemency ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)). The convicted associate of Jeffrey Epstein, serving a 20-year federal sentence, is now the only person ever convicted of a crime in connection with Epstein's abuse ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2025351)).

## The interview with the deputy attorney general

In late July 2025, Deputy Attorney General [Todd Blanche](/people/todd-blanche/) sat down with Maxwell at the federal prison in Tallahassee, Florida, for two days of questioning — July 24 and 25 ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2025-08-22/justice-department-releases-transcripts-from-its-conversations-with-ghislaine-maxwell)). It was a highly unusual encounter: such a senior Justice Department official rarely conducts a prison interview personally, and also present were Diego Pestana, the acting associate deputy attorney general, FBI special agent Spencer Horn, and deputy U.S. Marshal Mark Beard ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2025-08-22/justice-department-releases-transcripts-from-its-conversations-with-ghislaine-maxwell)). On August 22, 2025, the department released what it said was the complete transcript and audio recording — hundreds of pages, marked "redacted" — with Blanche posting the files and saying they were shared "in the interest of transparency" ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2025-08-22/justice-department-releases-transcripts-from-its-conversations-with-ghislaine-maxwell)).

Her lawyer, David Markus, said she answered everything: "Ms. Maxwell answered every question. She did not refuse to respond and did not dodge any question. She supported her answers with documents and other objective evidence" ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2025-08-22/justice-department-releases-transcripts-from-its-conversations-with-ghislaine-maxwell)). On President Trump, she said she may have met him around 1990 through her father, that he "was always very cordial and very kind to me," and that she never saw him in any inappropriate setting: "The President was never inappropriate with anybody. In the times that I was with him, he was a gentleman in all respects" ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2025-08-22/justice-department-releases-transcripts-from-its-conversations-with-ghislaine-maxwell)). She said she did not consider Trump and Epstein "close friends" and did not recall ever seeing Trump at Epstein's house ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2025-08-22/justice-department-releases-transcripts-from-its-conversations-with-ghislaine-maxwell)).

Asked about former President Bill Clinton, she said she did not believe he had ever received a massage — the only time Epstein and Clinton spent together, she said, was on Epstein's plane, and "he didn't, because I was there" ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2025-08-22/justice-department-releases-transcripts-from-its-conversations-with-ghislaine-maxwell)). Others who came up included Bill Gates, Chris Tucker, Kevin Spacey, and Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.; she said she knew of no inappropriate activity involving any of them, though she volunteered that she and Epstein had once gone "dinosaur bone hunting in the Dakotas" with Kennedy ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2025-08-22/justice-department-releases-transcripts-from-its-conversations-with-ghislaine-maxwell)).

Behind the interview sat an unmistakable pitch. Maxwell's legal team appeared to be angling for clemency in some form, and within days of the sit-down she was moved from the Florida prison to a minimum-security prison camp in Bryan, Texas — a transfer officials did not explain ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2025-08-22/justice-department-releases-transcripts-from-its-conversations-with-ghislaine-maxwell)). The transcript release came the same day the Justice Department shared a first wave of Epstein and Maxwell files with members of the House Oversight Committee, which had subpoenaed the records ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2025-08-22/justice-department-releases-transcripts-from-its-conversations-with-ghislaine-maxwell)).

## From the Maxwell empire to Epstein

Ghislaine Maxwell was born on Christmas Day, 1961, in Maisons-Laffitte, France, the youngest child of Czechoslovak-born Robert Maxwell — originally Jan Ludvik Hoch — a British publishing magnate who later served two terms as a Labour member of Parliament, and Elisabeth Maxwell, a French Holocaust researcher ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)). Days after her birth, her 15-year-old brother was severely injured in a car accident and remained in a coma until his death in 1968 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)). The family grew enormously wealthy and prominent through Robert Maxwell's acquisitions — including The Daily Mirror in Britain and the New York Daily News in the United States — living in a 53-room mansion near Oxford where they entertained political figures, celebrities, and royalty ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)). She attended private schools in Oxford and Somerset, then Balliol College at the University of Oxford, earning a degree in modern history and languages in 1985 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)).

In the 1980s she became a director of Oxford United Football Club, which her father had rescued from bankruptcy, later ran Maxwell Corporate Gifts, a company created with her father's help, and managed the European, his weekly English-language newspaper ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)). In January 1991 she moved to New York as her father's representative at the New York Daily News and became a fixture of the social scene ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)).

Later that year the empire collapsed. Robert Maxwell mysteriously disappeared from his 187-foot yacht, the Lady Ghislaine — named for his daughter in 1986 — near the Canary Islands, and his nude body was found floating in the ocean; the autopsy was inconclusive, with the unofficial conclusion being suicide, though some investigators speculated about his alleged ties to Mossad ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)). Shortly after his death it emerged that he had secretly siphoned some $1.2 billion from two of his flagship public companies and employee pension funds; two of Ghislaine's siblings, Ian and Kevin, were charged with fraud but acquitted in 1996, and the family quickly lost its social standing in Britain ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)).

She met Jeffrey Epstein in the early 1990s, soon after joining the Daily News. They were romantically involved for at least part of the decade and stayed close until his 2019 arrest and imprisonment; each benefited differently — Epstein was introduced to wealthy figures in her social circle, while Maxwell, whose own resources had withered after her father's death, resumed a wealthy lifestyle ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)). From the early 1990s she also worked as his professional assistant, hiring and managing his household staff ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)). In 2008, after investigations into his alleged sexual assault of some 40 underage girls in Florida, Epstein pleaded guilty to state charges of soliciting prostitution and soliciting a minor for prostitution and served 13 months under a plea deal that let him spend six days a week in his Palm Beach office ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)).

## Arrest, conviction, and the appeals

Starting in 2015, Maxwell herself faced lawsuits accusing her of sex trafficking of underage girls through deceit and false promises; among the plaintiffs was Virginia Giuffre, who had gone public with her account in 2011 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)). She sold her New York home and by 2017 had effectively gone into hiding — even her lawyers claimed not to know where she lived ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)). In July 2020, almost a year after Epstein killed himself in a Manhattan jail cell while awaiting trial, Maxwell was arrested at her home in New Hampshire; it was later revealed she had been married since 2016 to Scott Borgerson, the head of a technology company ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)).

In December 2021 she was convicted on five federal charges related to sex trafficking of underage girls, and in June 2022 she was sentenced to 20 years in prison ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)). After the Second Circuit declined to reverse the conviction, she took her case to the Supreme Court in April 2025; her lawyers argued that the 2007 Florida non-prosecution and plea agreement federal prosecutors had made with Epstein also shielded his associates and should have barred her prosecution in New York ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/us/2025/10/06/us-supreme-court-rejects-ghislaine-maxwell-appeal-of-conviction-in-epstein-sex-trafficking-case/)). On October 6, 2025, at the start of its 2025–26 term, the Court declined to hear the appeal, letting the lower court's decision stand without explaining its reasoning ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/us/2025/10/06/us-supreme-court-rejects-ghislaine-maxwell-appeal-of-conviction-in-epstein-sex-trafficking-case/)). "We are, of course, deeply disappointed," said her lawyer David Oscar Markus. "But this fight isn't over" ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/us/2025/10/06/us-supreme-court-rejects-ghislaine-maxwell-appeal-of-conviction-in-epstein-sex-trafficking-case/)).

That fight's final round played out against the backdrop of the Epstein Files Transparency Act, which Congress passed and Trump signed in November 2025, requiring the Justice Department to release nearly all remaining Epstein documents by December 19 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)). The department missed the deadline but released some 3 million documents on January 30, 2026, saying no other files existed ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ghislaine-Maxwell)). Maxwell's team then argued the releases contained exculpatory material deliberately withheld from her defense at trial — the theory Engelmayer rejected in August 2026 ([Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/2025351)).
