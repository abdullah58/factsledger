## The 2018 ban: twelve days that defined the job

On November 7, 2018, the day after the midterm elections, President Trump held a contentious press conference in which he traded barbs with CNN's chief [White House](/places/white-house/) correspondent, Jim Acosta, over a migrant caravan Trump had described as an "invasion." Trump called Acosta "a rude, terrible person," an intern moved in to take Acosta's microphone, and hours later Sarah Huckabee Sanders announced that the White House was temporarily suspending Acosta's hard pass. ([NPR Illinois](https://www.nprillinois.org/generationlisten/2018-11-07/white-house-revokes-press-pass-of-cnns-jim-acosta))

Her statement said Acosta had placed "his hands on a young woman just trying to do her job as a White House intern," behavior she called "absolutely unacceptable." The [White House Correspondents' Association](/organizations/white-house-correspondents-association/) strongly objected. According to the U.S. Press Freedom Tracker, the intern had in fact been trying to take the microphone from Acosta, and Sanders tweeted a video of the encounter that had been doctored. ([NPR Illinois](https://www.nprillinois.org/generationlisten/2018-11-07/white-house-revokes-press-pass-of-cnns-jim-acosta), [U.S. Press Freedom Tracker](https://pressfreedomtracker.us/all-incidents/cnns-jim-acosta-has-white-house-press-pass-suspended/))

The ban kept Acosta off the White House grounds indefinitely. He was denied a day pass on November 8 and blocked from covering Trump's event in Paris on November 9. On November 13, CNN sued the White House, arguing the revocation violated Acosta's First and Fifth Amendment rights. ([U.S. Press Freedom Tracker](https://pressfreedomtracker.us/all-incidents/cnns-jim-acosta-has-white-house-press-pass-suspended/))

On November 16, Judge Timothy Kelly granted a 14-day temporary restraining order reinstating the pass — on due-process grounds, not First Amendment grounds. That same night, Sanders and communications director Bill Shine sent CNN a "preliminary decision" to revoke the pass again once the order expired. Three days later, the White House restored the pass permanently and CNN dropped the lawsuit. Alongside the restoration, the White House announced new press-conference rules: one question per reporter, yield the floor after two questions, and possible revocation for anyone who refused. ([U.S. Press Freedom Tracker](https://pressfreedomtracker.us/all-incidents/cnns-jim-acosta-has-white-house-press-pass-suspended/))

Sanders, who served as the 31st White House press secretary from July 21, 2017, to July 1, 2019, was the third woman to hold the job — and, in her own office's telling, "the very first mom." She is one of five people to hold the role under Trump, a line that now runs from Sean Spicer to [Karoline Leavitt](/people/karoline-leavitt/). ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/08/12/trump-white-house-press-secretaries/91277749007/), [Office of the Governor of Arkansas](https://governor.arkansas.gov/administration/governor-sanders/))

## From campaign to briefing room

Politics was the family business. Sanders joined the Trump presidential campaign as a senior advisor in February 2016, after years working for her father, former Arkansas governor and presidential candidate Mike Huckabee — she was political director for his 2008 Iowa caucus win. She had also served as a senior advisor to Tom Cotton's 2014 Senate campaign, campaign manager for John Boozman's 2010 Senate campaign, and worked at the Department of Education under President George W. Bush. ([Office of the Governor of Arkansas](https://governor.arkansas.gov/administration/governor-sanders/))

After leaving the White House on July 1, 2019, she wrote the New York Times bestseller *Speaking for Myself*, became a Fox News contributor, and served on the board of the Fulbright Foreign Scholarship program before returning to Arkansas. ([Office of the Governor of Arkansas](https://governor.arkansas.gov/administration/governor-sanders/))

## The governorship: LEARNS and year three

On January 10, 2023, Sanders was inaugurated as the 47th governor of Arkansas — the first woman to hold the office and, at the time, the youngest sitting governor in the country. In her first 100 days she delivered the Republican response to President [Joe Biden](/people/joe-biden/)'s State of the Union address. Her administration's signature domestic achievement has been Arkansas LEARNS, a wide-ranging education overhaul built around teacher pay raises and universal school choice, alongside public-safety and prison reforms, tax cuts, and the Natural State Initiative promoting the outdoors. ([Office of the Governor of Arkansas](https://governor.arkansas.gov/administration/governor-sanders/))

Her 2025 legislative package, as characterized by the state business press, was among the most productive in recent Arkansas history: "Bell to Bell, No Cell" banning smartphones and smartwatches in public schools, Healthy Moms, Healthy Babies maternal-health measures, Arkansas ACCESS higher-education reform, universal free breakfasts for all public school students, and even bike lifts for mountain-bike trails. ([Arkansas Money & Politics](https://armoneyandpolitics.com/2026-primer-gov-sarah-huckabee-sanders/))

Now in year three of her first term, the agenda continues. She designated Blanchard Springs Caverns as Arkansas's 52nd state park — the outlet credits her with growing tourism to more than 50 million visitors and a $10 billion economic impact. She signed a roughly $6 billion memorandum of understanding with Taiwan to buy Arkansas farm products, repealed the state's grocery tax, and pushed a 3,000-bed prison in Franklin County while adding some 1,500 beds to existing facilities to work through a backlog of 176,000 unserved warrants. Other planks include a Medicaid work requirement, restrictions on junk-food purchases with food stamps, the faith-based anti-poverty 10:33 Initiative, and Arkansas Plate, a farm-to-school program. ([Arkansas Money & Politics](https://armoneyandpolitics.com/2026-primer-gov-sarah-huckabee-sanders/))

Sanders grew up in Pine Bluff and Texarkana, attended Little Rock Central High School and Ouachita Baptist University, and is married to Bryan Sanders, with three children — Scarlett, Huck, and George — and a golden retriever named Traveler. ([Office of the Governor of Arkansas](https://governor.arkansas.gov/administration/governor-sanders/))
