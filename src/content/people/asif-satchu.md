## Overview

Asif Satchu is an American media executive who co-founded the independent film and television studio [MRC](/organizations/mrc/) with [Modi Wiczyk](/people/modi-wiczyk/), led it through the Valence Media roll-up that brought Billboard and [The Hollywood Reporter](/organizations/the-hollywood-reporter/) under the same roof, and split from backer Todd Boehly's Eldridge Industries in 2022 ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/the-talent-packagers/5016606.article); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/313954/thr-billboard-owner-consolidates-properties-i.html); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/mrc-eldridge-todd-boehly-pmrc-reorg/)).

Satchu and Wiczyk, both Harvard Business School graduates, launched MRC, an independent studio in the [United States](/places/united-states/), in 2003 and ran it as co-chairmen and co-CEOs ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/the-talent-packagers/5016606.article)). The studio's model gave artists ownership and profit participation rather than large upfront fees, and it produced House of Cards, the first series Netflix ordered directly, alongside films including Ted, Knives Out and Baby Driver ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/the-talent-packagers/5016606.article); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2011/03/15/netflix-gets-into-the-original-content-game-buys-upcoming-show-for-100m/); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/mrc-eldridge-todd-boehly-pmrc-reorg/)). From 2018 to 2022 Satchu also co-ran the trade press itself: Valence Media owned Billboard and The Hollywood Reporter, and the PMRC venture then put those titles alongside [Variety](/organizations/variety/) under [Penske Media Corporation](/organizations/penske-media-corporation/) ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/313954/thr-billboard-owner-consolidates-properties-i.html); [Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)).

## Before MRC: finance, storage and supply chains

The fullest published account of Satchu's pre-Hollywood career is a single biographical profile whose claims could not be independently corroborated in this session ([Virtual Globetrotting](https://virtualglobetrotting.com/map/asif-satchus-house/)). According to that account, Satchu worked as an investment professional at the hedge fund Tiger Management Company, the private equity fund Westbrook Partners, and Morgan Stanley before turning to operating businesses ([Virtual Globetrotting](https://virtualglobetrotting.com/map/asif-satchus-house/)). He then founded and led SMKT, described as a billion-dollar supply chain management company, and built StorageNow into Canada's third-largest self-storage company ([Virtual Globetrotting](https://virtualglobetrotting.com/map/asif-satchus-house/)). The same profile says he graduated from McGill University and Harvard Business School, and that his brother Reza Satchu was one of his early business partners ([Virtual Globetrotting](https://virtualglobetrotting.com/map/asif-satchus-house/)). The Harvard part is independently corroborated: trade reporting describes both Satchu and Wiczyk as Harvard Business School graduates ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/the-talent-packagers/5016606.article)). That finance-first background shaped MRC, a studio run like an investment vehicle, financing slates with bank facilities rather than owning distribution ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/the-talent-packagers/5016606.article)).

## Founding MRC and the talent-first studio

Satchu and Wiczyk launched MRC in 2003, with Wiczyk leaving the talent agency Endeavor to set it up, and the pair running it as co-chairmen and co-CEOs ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/the-talent-packagers/5016606.article)). Their model was artist-friendly: MRC preferred profit participation over large upfront deals, so talent shared in both hits and flops ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/the-talent-packagers/5016606.article)). The approach depended on a close relationship with William Morris Endeavor and on packaging films around stars, as with Babel, which opened through Paramount Vantage in 2006 and grossed $114 million worldwide ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/the-talent-packagers/5016606.article)). Financing followed the same discipline: in September 2008, amid the financial crisis, Satchu and Wiczyk announced a three-year, $350 million revolving credit facility led by JPMorgan Chase and Comerica, with $295 million closed ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/media-rights-capital-scores-350m-revolving-credit-facility/4040995.article)). In 2010 MRC signed a five-year deal with Universal Pictures for 20 features, and in 2011 Netflix outbid HBO and AMC for House of Cards, reportedly committing about $100 million for 26 episodes without seeing a pilot ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/the-talent-packagers/5016606.article); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2011/03/15/netflix-gets-into-the-original-content-game-buys-upcoming-show-for-100m/)).

## Valence Media and the Billboard years

In February 2018, Eldridge Industries — the holding company of investor Todd Boehly — contributed its media assets, including The Hollywood Reporter, [Dick Clark Productions](/organizations/dick-clark-productions/) and Billboard, and merged them with MRC into a new company called Valence Media ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/313954/thr-billboard-owner-consolidates-properties-i.html)). Satchu and Wiczyk led Valence as co-CEOs, with Boehly as chairman, and a minority stake in the film distributor A24 also moved under it ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/313954/thr-billboard-owner-consolidates-properties-i.html)). Each division kept operating under its existing leadership, and at Dick Clark Productions Allen Shapiro became executive chairman and Mike Mahan CEO ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/313954/thr-billboard-owner-consolidates-properties-i.html)). On 23 September 2020, Penske Media Corporation and MRC formed PMRC, announced by PMC chief [Jay Penske](/people/jay-penske/) alongside Satchu and Wiczyk, to operate Billboard, The Hollywood Reporter and [Vibe](/organizations/vibe/) with Variety, [Rolling Stone](/organizations/rolling-stone/) and [Music Business Worldwide](/organizations/music-business-worldwide/), ending a rivalry of some 90 years between The Hollywood Reporter and Variety on undisclosed terms ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)).

## The 2022 split from Eldridge

On 5 August 2022 the merger unwound: MRC kept its film, television and non-fiction studio plus Civic Center Media and T-Street stakes, while Eldridge retained Dick Clark Productions, A24, Fulwell 73, Sugar 23, Luminate and Penske Media Corporation, staying on as a significant minority shareholder in MRC ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220805005474/en/Eldridge-and-MRC-Announce-Separation-of-Media-Assets); [Kirkland & Ellis](https://www.kirkland.com/news/press-release/2022/08/kirkland-advises-media-rights-capital-in-separation-of-combined-media-assets-with-eldridge)). Boehly had first invested $240 million in MRC in 2014 at a $1 billion valuation, and analysts consulted by TheWrap valued the studio below $500 million in 2022 — an MRC insider disputed that figure, but the gap implies how far streaming-era economics had compressed the business ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/todd-boehly-mrc-breakup-hfpa-golden-globes/)). The break was acrimonious, driven by the Golden Globes crisis: NBC's cancellation of the 2022 telecast cost Dick Clark Productions about $30 million ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/todd-boehly-mrc-breakup-hfpa-golden-globes/)). At the split MRC's productions had earned 111 Emmy, 29 Golden Globe, 12 Oscar and 2 Grammy nominations, with its films grossing more than $6 billion worldwide ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/mrc-eldridge-todd-boehly-pmrc-reorg/)). MRC had eight shows in production, including The Great at Hulu, a Ted prequel at Peacock and five shows at Apple TV+ ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/todd-boehly-mrc-breakup-hfpa-golden-globes/)).
