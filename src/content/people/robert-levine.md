# Robert Levine

## Overview

Robert Levine was the father of [Chuck Lorre](/people/chuck-lorre/) — born Charles Michael Levine — the television writer and producer behind *Two and a Half Men*, *The Big Bang Theory* and *Young Sheldon* ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)). His entire public record runs through his son's childhood biography: when Lorre was about five, the family moved from New York City to Long Island, and there Robert Levine ran a small luncheonette where the teenage Lorre often helped out ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)). He is not the celebrity-spouse physician widowed from Mary Tyler Moore, the Yale bioethicist, or any of the other Robert Levines who turn up in obituary databases — this profile exists only to disambiguate him as the father in the Chuck Lorre family context of the FactsLedger entity queue.

## Family

Levine and his wife, [Miriam Levine](/people/miriam-levine/), had two children: Charles Michael Levine — later Chuck Lorre — and an elder daughter, Joan ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)). Miriam was a homemaker who later worked in retail ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)). One small detail of family life survives in the Britannica account: the young Lorre developed his love of comedy by watching stand-ups such as Bob Hope and Don Rickles on *The Ed Sullivan Show* with his dad ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Chuck-Lorre)). That is all any fully-read source records about Robert Levine as a person — a father, a luncheonette counter on Long Island, and evenings in front of the television with his son.
