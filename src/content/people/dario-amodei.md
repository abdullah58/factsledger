## Overview

Dario Amodei is an American artificial intelligence researcher and entrepreneur who co-founded Anthropic and serves as its chief executive officer ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/anthropic-ipo-prospectus-shows-key-executives-board-2026-09-28/)). Before Anthropic, he was vice president of research at OpenAI, where he set the organization's overall research direction and led the efforts to build GPT-2 and GPT-3 ([Hertz Foundation](https://www.hertzfoundation.org/people/dario-amodei/)). His own biography credits him as a co-inventor of reinforcement learning from human feedback, the technique that became central to aligning large language models with human preferences ([Dario Amodei](https://darioamodei.com/)).

Born in San Francisco in 1983 ([Britannica Money](https://www.britannica.com/money/Dario-Amodei)), Amodei trained first as a physicist and biophysicist — his Princeton doctorate centered on the electrophysiology of neural circuits — before moving into deep learning at Baidu, Google Brain and OpenAI. In December 2020 he left OpenAI with his sister [Daniela Amodei](/people/daniela-amodei/), Jack Clark, Chris Olah and a handful of other researchers, and in early 2021 they founded Anthropic as a public benefit corporation dedicated to building AI systems that are steerable, interpretable and safe ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei); [Dario Amodei](https://darioamodei.com/)).

Amodei is one of the AI industry's most prominent dual voices: he warns urgently about catastrophic AI risks — including before the UN Security Council — while also writing at length about AI's potentially radical benefits, most notably in his October 2024 essay "Machines of Loving Grace" ([Britannica Money](https://www.britannica.com/money/Dario-Amodei); [Machines of Loving Grace](https://darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace)). That dual posture has repeatedly put him in public conflict with the Trump administration in 2026 — over export controls, a Pentagon blacklisting and, most recently, a series of [White House](https://factsledger.com/organizations/the-white-house) meetings in September 2026 ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/28/trump-ai-anthropic-technology-concerns/91983058007/); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/trump-host-zuckerberg-anthropics-amodei-other-ai-titans-tuesday-2026-09-29/)).

## From physics and biology to machine learning

Amodei grew up in a household shaped by making things with his hands and fixing public institutions. His father, Riccardo, was a leathersmith, and his mother, Elena Engel, worked in construction renovating public libraries in Berkeley and San Francisco; the family moved from apartment to apartment following library building sites, and Amodei was born while his parents were living in a single-room-occupancy residence in San Francisco's Tenderloin ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). His activism began early: at Caltech he became a campus voice for gay rights, a period that strained his relationship with his more conservative father, and the two did not speak for three years ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)).

He earned his undergraduate physics degree from [Stanford University](/universities/stanford-university/) in 2006 ([Britannica Money](https://www.britannica.com/money/Dario-Amodei)). That same year, his father — who had been told he had three to five years to live with hepatitis C, an illness that went from 50 percent fatal to 95 percent curable within about four years — died ([Britannica Money](https://www.britannica.com/money/Dario-Amodei); [Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). The loss redirected Amodei's scientific life: he moved from theoretical physics into biology, working in professor Michael Berry's retina lab at Princeton, and later did postdoctoral work at the Stanford University School of Medicine under [Parag Mallick](/people/parag-mallick/) studying the proteins around tumors ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)).

His Princeton doctorate, completed in 2011, was titled "Network-Scale Electrophysiology: Measuring and Understanding the Collective Behavior of Neural Circuits"; he worked on statistical-mechanics models of neural circuits and on novel devices for intracellular and extracellular recording, and won the Hertz Thesis Prize in 2011 ([Hertz Foundation](https://www.hertzfoundation.org/people/dario-amodei/)). The Hertz Foundation describes his graduate studies as biophysics and physics, while his own biography calls it a doctorate in biophysics — a wording difference noted rather than resolved here ([Hertz Foundation](https://www.hertzfoundation.org/people/dario-amodei/); [Dario Amodei](https://darioamodei.com/)). As a Stanford postdoc, he applied mass spectrometry to network models of the cellular proteome and to the search for cancer biomarkers ([Hertz Foundation](https://www.hertzfoundation.org/people/dario-amodei/)).

## OpenAI, scaling laws and the GPT years

Amodei entered industry AI research in November 2014 at Baidu, where he was working on speech recognition when he noticed something that would define his career: the models simply kept getting better as he added more data, made them larger and trained them longer. That empirical regularity — the seed of the neural scaling laws — convinced him that scaling compute and data was the reliable path to more capable systems ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). He left Baidu in 2015, spent roughly ten months as a senior research scientist on Google's Brain team, and joined OpenAI in 2016 ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/anthropic-ipo-prospectus-shows-key-executives-board-2026-09-28/)).

At OpenAI, Amodei rose to vice president of research, a role he held until 2020, setting the organization's overall research direction ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/anthropic-ipo-prospectus-shows-key-executives-board-2026-09-28/); [Hertz Foundation](https://www.hertzfoundation.org/people/dario-amodei/)). He led the efforts behind GPT-2 — including early work on training models from human feedback that would evolve into RLHF — and then directed the GPT-3 project, which was handed an extraordinary 50 to 60 percent of the entire company's compute for a roughly hundred-fold scale-up costing tens of millions of dollars ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). GPT-3's emergent capabilities, from coding to translation, stunned outside researchers, and Amodei led several teams focused on long-term safety research alongside the capability work, including efforts on interpretability and on embedding human preferences and values into future powerful systems ([Hertz Foundation](https://www.hertzfoundation.org/people/dario-amodei/)).

But Amodei's group — a tight-knit circle some colleagues nicknamed "the pandas" — increasingly clashed with OpenAI's leadership over safety, model-release decisions, personnel, deployment and the company's external representation. Amodei has said plainly that leaders "have to be trustworthy people" whose motivations are sincere, and that working for those without that sincerity means "contributing to something bad" ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). In December 2020, he, Jack Clark, his sister Daniela, researcher Chris Olah and a handful of others departed OpenAI ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)).

## Founding Anthropic and building Claude

Anthropic was formed in the heart of the Covid pandemic, its 15 to 20 early employees meeting entirely over Zoom during the pandemic's second wave before eventually gathering for weekly lunches in San Francisco's Precita Park, bringing their own chairs ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). The name came from a spreadsheet of candidates — including Aligned AI, Generative, Sponge, Swan, Sloth and Sparrow Systems — with "Anthropic," connoting something human-centered and human-oriented, also happening to have an available domain name in early 2021 ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). Amodei has served as co-founder, CEO and director since 2021, according to Anthropic's September 2026 IPO prospectus ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/anthropic-ipo-prospectus-shows-key-executives-board-2026-09-28/)).

The company's early mission was to build leading large language models, implement safe practices that would pressure competitors to follow, and publish what it learned apart from its models' core technical details ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). Early backers included former Google CEO Eric Schmidt, who invested in Amodei the person more than the concept, and the now-disgraced FTX CEO Sam Bankman-Fried, who reportedly directed $500 million for a 13.56 percent stake — kept to non-voting shares and off the board — a decision Amodei later said was vindicated when Bankman-Fried's behavior "turned out to be much, much, much more extreme and bad than I ever imagined" ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). Amodei told *Big Technology* in July 2025 that Anthropic had raised nearly $20 billion in total, including $8 billion from Amazon and $3 billion from Google — his figures, reported as stated ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)).

Rather than chase the consumer chatbot race opened by ChatGPT, Amodei initially aimed Anthropic at business customers — pharmaceutical companies, airlines, insurers — on the theory that demanding enterprise use cases would force the models to get genuinely better ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). Ironically, it was a consumer product that broke the company open: the Claude chatbot, released in July 2023, drew rave reviews for its high-EQ personality, and the company soon found itself hiring more people in a day than it had employed in its entire first year ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). Amodei has described Anthropic's revenue as growing roughly tenfold each year — from zero to $100 million in 2023, $100 million to $1 billion in 2024, and to roughly $4.5 billion annualized by the first half of 2025 — while acknowledging the company remains deeply unprofitable, expected to lose about $3 billion in 2025 (again, his figures, as stated in the interview) ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). The company released its Claude Code coding tool in February 2025 and introduced Claude 4 at its first developer conference in May 2025 ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)).

Amodei's strategy for safety leadership has been to publish and pressure rather than merely to warn. Anthropic released a Responsible Scaling Policy setting boundaries for when to train and release models depending on their risks, and Amodei has framed the approach as a "race to the top" — encouraging others to emulate Anthropic's safety practices rather than racing to the bottom on caution ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). In 2024, Jan Leike, the former head of OpenAI's "Superalignment" team, joined Anthropic to co-lead its alignment science team ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)).

## "Machines of Loving Grace": the case for AI's upside

In October 2024, Amodei published a roughly 14,000-word essay, "Machines of Loving Grace," laying out what a world with powerful AI might look like "if everything goes right" ([Sherwood News](https://sherwood.news/tech/anthropic-ai-manifesto-dario-amodei-machines-of-loving-grace/); [Machines of Loving Grace](https://darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace)). The essay opens by rejecting the "pessimist or doomer" label his risk warnings have earned him: "one of my main reasons for focusing on risks is that they're the only thing standing between us and what I see as a fundamentally positive future," he writes, arguing that most people underestimate both how radical AI's upside could be and how bad its risks could be ([Machines of Loving Grace](https://darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace)).

His central prediction is what he calls the "compressed 21st century": that after powerful AI is developed, "we will in a few years make all the progress in biology and medicine that we would have made in the whole 21st century" — compressing 50 to 100 years of biological progress into 5 to 10 years ([Machines of Loving Grace](https://darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace)). By "powerful AI" — he says he dislikes the term AGI — he means systems smarter than a Nobel Prize winner across most relevant fields, millions of instances running at 10 to 100 times human speed: in his phrase, "a country of geniuses in a datacenter" ([Machines of Loving Grace](https://darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace)). He suggested such systems could arrive as early as 2026, and organized his predictions around five areas with the greatest potential to improve the quality of human life: biology and physical health, neuroscience and mental health, economic development and poverty, peace and governance, and work and meaning ([Machines of Loving Grace](https://darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace)).

The essay also explains why Anthropic talks so much about risks and so little about benefits: to maximize leverage, since market forces will drive the benefits but the risks are not predetermined; to avoid sounding like self-interested propagandists; to avoid grandiosity; and to avoid the "sci-fi baggage" that makes radical claims sound unserious ([Machines of Loving Grace](https://darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace)). Yet he insists a positive vision is essential: "at the end of it all, there has to be something we're fighting for, some positive-sum outcome where everyone is better off... Fear is one kind of motivator, but it's not enough: we need hope as well" ([Machines of Loving Grace](https://darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace)). Reviewing the essay, *Sherwood News* noted he avoided the phrase "artificial general intelligence" for the vaguer "powerful AI," and quoted his estimate that "5-10 years is a reasonable timeline for a good fraction (maybe 50%) of AI-driven health benefits to propagate to even the poorest countries in the world" ([Sherwood News](https://sherwood.news/tech/anthropic-ai-manifesto-dario-amodei-machines-of-loving-grace/)).

In December 2025, Amodei was named among TIME's 2025 Person of the Year, the "Architects of AI" — an eight-person cover lineup alongside Mark Zuckerberg, Elon Musk, Sam Altman, Lisa Su, Jensen Huang, Demis Hassabis and Fei-Fei Li ([New York Post](https://nypost.com/2025/12/13/media/jimmy-kimmel-lambasts-times-architects-of-ai-cover/)).

## Safety warnings, export controls and the Pentagon fight

Amodei's safety advocacy has made him one of Washington's most watched AI figures — and one of its most contested. He has argued before the UN Security Council for a global moratorium on AI progress past a certain level of capability ([Britannica Money](https://www.britannica.com/money/Dario-Amodei)). His calls for GPU export controls to China drew a sharp rebuke from Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, who said of him, "He believes that AI is so scary that only they should do it." Amodei called that "the most outrageous lie I've ever heard," insisting he has always wanted to spark a "race to the top" of safety practices ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). He wrote a *New York Times* op-ed opposing a proposed ten-year federal moratorium on AI regulation, and told *Axios* that up to 50 percent of entry-level white-collar jobs could disappear within five years ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)).

In June 2026, the tension with the U.S. government turned concrete. Anthropic released Claude Fable 5 for general use on June 9, while its sibling model Claude Mythos 5 — sharing the same underlying model but with additional safeguards for biology, cybersecurity and AI R&D — remained restricted to trusted partners in Anthropic's Project Glasswing for defensive cybersecurity work ([NeoTeo](https://www.neoteo.com/en/white-house-reportedly-missed-august-ai-safety-guidance-deadline-amid-anthropic-mythos-dispute)). On June 12, the U.S. government issued an export-control directive restricting foreign nationals' access to both models, including Anthropic's own foreign-national employees; because it could not reliably verify users' nationality in real time, Anthropic disabled both models for all customers worldwide ([NeoTeo](https://www.neoteo.com/en/white-house-reportedly-missed-august-ai-safety-guidance-deadline-amid-anthropic-mythos-dispute)).

The government cited national-security concerns and gave the company what Anthropic described as "verbal evidence of a potential narrow, non-universal jailbreak" of Fable 5 — a technique Anthropic downplayed, saying other major models, including OpenAI's GPT-5.5, could also be affected ([Sherwood News](https://sherwood.news/tech/anthropic-ai-manifesto-dario-amodei-machines-of-loving-grace/)). According to *The Wall Street Journal*, as reported by Sherwood, the administration's action was prompted by discussions that Amazon CEO Andy Jassy had with officials including Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, after Amazon researchers found ways to evade some of Fable 5's safety restrictions ([Sherwood News](https://sherwood.news/tech/anthropic-ai-manifesto-dario-amodei-machines-of-loving-grace/)). The controls were lifted in stages: Mythos 5's were lifted on June 26, with access restored for a set of U.S. organizations, and Fable 5's on June 30, with global access returning July 1 — after 19 days offline ([NeoTeo](https://www.neoteo.com/en/white-house-reportedly-missed-august-ai-safety-guidance-deadline-amid-anthropic-mythos-dispute)).

Separately, the Pentagon designated Anthropic in a national-security blacklisting, which the company is suing to overturn; on September 25, 2026, an appeals court ruled in Anthropic's favor ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/28/trump-ai-anthropic-technology-concerns/91983058007/); [Sherwood News](https://sherwood.news/tech/anthropic-ai-manifesto-dario-amodei-machines-of-loving-grace/)). And on June 2, 2026, President Trump signed Executive Order 14409, directing agencies to design a voluntary framework for frontier-model oversight; the White House reportedly missed its August deadline to publish public guidance on AI safety reviews ([NeoTeo](https://www.neoteo.com/en/white-house-reportedly-missed-august-ai-safety-guidance-deadline-amid-anthropic-mythos-dispute)). The stakes behind the scrutiny were underlined by the [UK AI Security Institute](/organizations/uk-ai-security-institute/)'s evaluation of Claude Mythos Preview in simulated cyber tasks: a 73 percent success rate on expert-level capture-the-flag challenges, and a 32-step simulated corporate-network attack completed in 3 of 10 attempts, averaging 22 steps against an estimated 20 hours for a human — on a test network with no active defenders ([NeoTeo](https://www.neoteo.com/en/white-house-reportedly-missed-august-ai-safety-guidance-deadline-amid-anthropic-mythos-dispute)).
