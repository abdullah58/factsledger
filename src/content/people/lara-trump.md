## Overview

Lara Trump is the daughter-in-law of Donald Trump — married to his son [Eric Trump](/people/eric-trump/) since 2014 — who did something no other Trump has done: she ran the Republican National Committee ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/who-is-lara-trump-and-how-is-she-related-to-donald-trump-5-facts-101773541431733.html)).

The standard telling covers the RNC and the Fox News show. The more revealing biography is the one her search results never quite assemble: a decade-long arc from pastry chef and TV producer to party co-chair, and then a remarkable three-time refusal of the U.S. Senate — 2022, 2024, and 2026 — before landing the weekend primetime slot where, USA TODAY noted, she became the first family member of a sitting president ever to host a television program ([The Associated Press](https://www.wcia.com/news/national/ap-us-news/ap-takeaways-how-lara-trump-is-reshaping-the-republican-party/amp/); [USA TODAY](https://www.aberdeennews.com/story/entertainment/tv/2025/03/08/lara-trump-my-view-fox-news-show/80701035007/)).

## The three-time Senate flirtation

Lara Trump has thought about running for the Senate three times — and each time, the door opened and she walked past it. She first considered a run in 2022 when North Carolina's Richard Burr retired, then decided against it ([The Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/news/international/us/trumps-daughter-in-law-lara-just-made-a-bold-political-move-and-its-turning-heads/amp_articleshow/122909829.cms)). In December 2024, fresh off the election victory, she told the AP she was open to filling the Senate seat that would be vacated if Marco Rubio were confirmed as secretary of state: "It is something I would seriously consider," she said. "If I'm being completely transparent, I don't know exactly what that would look like. And I certainly want to get all of the information possible if that is something that's real for me. But yeah, I would 100% consider it" ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/lara-trump-to-step-down-from-republican-party-post-eyes-senate-seat/)). To host *My View*, she then took her name out of consideration for the Florida seat, where she now lives ([USA TODAY](https://www.aberdeennews.com/story/entertainment/tv/2025/03/08/lara-trump-my-view-fox-news-show/80701035007/)).

The third came in 2026. After Senator Thom Tillis — a Republican from North Carolina who had opposed Trump's "Big Beautiful Bill" — said he would not seek reelection, Lara had reportedly been thinking about the seat since June, according to Politico ([The Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/news/international/us/trumps-daughter-in-law-lara-just-made-a-bold-political-move-and-its-turning-heads/amp_articleshow/122909829.cms)). Trump had encouraged her to run; instead, in the twist, he chose to back RNC chair Michael Whatley, who will be endorsed by both Trump and Lara ([The Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/news/international/us/trumps-daughter-in-law-lara-just-made-a-bold-political-move-and-its-turning-heads/amp_articleshow/122909829.cms)). Her own explanation, given in the USA TODAY interview, was motherhood: "For me, being the mom of two young kids who are 5 and 7 right now, my kids need me. They want me around. That doesn't last forever," she said, noting that "there's a reason you don't see a lot of moms of school-age children as United States senators" — there are only four in the chamber ([USA TODAY](https://www.aberdeennews.com/story/entertainment/tv/2025/03/08/lara-trump-my-view-fox-news-show/80701035007/)).

## Rebranding the RNC

Lara Trump was unanimously elected RNC co-chair on March 8, 2024, at the committee's Houston meeting, alongside North Carolina GOP leader Michael Whatley — part of a Trump-backed overhaul that ousted Ronna McDaniel and Drew McKissick ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/lara-trump-to-step-down-from-republican-party-post-eyes-senate-seat/)). The AP's profile of her first months captured the method: she and Whatley promised sweeping changes, and they delivered them by merging the GOP and the Trump campaign into a single operation ([The Associated Press](https://www.wcia.com/news/national/ap-us-news/ap-takeaways-how-lara-trump-is-reshaping-the-republican-party/amp/)).

She was an unusually public co-chair — television interviews, right-wing media appearances, a few gaffes (including claiming on Newsmax that the RNC had filed lawsuits in "81 states"), and, she told the AP, a "big, big learning curve" — backed by the political experience of having worked on both of Trump's previous presidential campaigns: "You'd be hard pressed to find someone who has had as much political experience as I have in any campaign right now" ([The Associated Press](https://www.wcia.com/news/national/ap-us-news/ap-takeaways-how-lara-trump-is-reshaping-the-republican-party/amp/)). The results were measurable: the RNC brought in $76 million in April and $65.6 million in March, compared with $10.6 million in February ([The Associated Press](https://www.wcia.com/news/national/ap-us-news/ap-takeaways-how-lara-trump-is-reshaping-the-republican-party/amp/)). She also pushed Republicans toward a tactic Trump had long denounced — mail-in voting — backing the "Swamp the Vote" early-voting drive ([The Associated Press](https://www.wcia.com/news/national/ap-us-news/ap-takeaways-how-lara-trump-is-reshaping-the-republican-party/amp/)).

On December 8, 2024, she announced she would step down at the RNC's next meeting: "The job I came to do is now complete," she posted on X, citing fundraising, election integrity, and voter turnout as goals met ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/lara-trump-to-step-down-from-republican-party-post-eyes-senate-seat/)). The re-election of Whatley and the selection of KC Crosbie as her successor followed in January 2025 ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/lara-trump-to-step-down-from-republican-party-post-eyes-senate-seat/)).

## From pastry chef to primetime

Before Trump-world, Lara Trump had a distinctly ordinary career. Raised in Wilmington, North Carolina, she earned a communications degree from North Carolina State University, studied pastry arts at the French Culinary Institute in New York City, and worked as a personal trainer ([Sunday Guardian Live](https://sundayguardianlive.com/trending/who-is-lara-trump-american-politician-daughter-in-law-of-donald-trump-check-her-age-career-net-worth-husband-family-how-tall-is-lara-trump-personal-life-more-251295/)). From 2012 to 2016 she worked in mainstream entertainment news as a story coordinator and producer for the syndicated program *Inside Edition* ([Sunday Guardian Live](https://sundayguardianlive.com/trending/who-is-lara-trump-american-politician-daughter-in-law-of-donald-trump-check-her-age-career-net-worth-husband-family-how-tall-is-lara-trump-personal-life-more-251295/)).

The political turn came with the 2016 and 2020 campaigns, where she was a senior surrogate, produced digital content, launched the "Women for Trump" coalition, and hosted the online *Real News Update* broadcasts ([Sunday Guardian Live](https://sundayguardianlive.com/trending/who-is-lara-trump-american-politician-daughter-in-law-of-donald-trump-check-her-age-career-net-worth-husband-family-how-tall-is-lara-trump-personal-life-more-251295/)). In March 2021, Fox News hired her as a paid contributor; she parted ways with the network in late 2022 after Donald Trump announced his 2024 reelection campaign, to avoid conflicts of interest ([Sunday Guardian Live](https://sundayguardianlive.com/trending/who-is-lara-trump-american-politician-daughter-in-law-of-donald-trump-check-her-age-career-net-worth-husband-family-how-tall-is-lara-trump-personal-life-more-251295/)).

Then came television again — this time as a host. *My View with Lara Trump* debuted on [Fox News](/organizations/fox-news/) on February 22, 2025, airing Saturdays at 9 p.m. ET ([USA TODAY](https://www.aberdeennews.com/story/entertainment/tv/2025/03/08/lara-trump-my-view-fox-news-show/80701035007/)). The premiere drew 2.4 million viewers, beating ESPN's college basketball coverage, and featured Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard, Attorney General Pam Bondi, and White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt ([USA TODAY](https://www.aberdeennews.com/story/entertainment/tv/2025/03/08/lara-trump-my-view-fox-news-show/80701035007/)). The hire drew criticism — the Daily Beast accused her of trying to paint her father-in-law as "pro-feminist"; The New Yorker dubbed her "The New Trump-Family Megaphone" — which she met head-on: "Oh, you mean someone would criticize something that a Trump did? Of course there will be critics" ([USA TODAY](https://www.aberdeennews.com/story/entertainment/tv/2025/03/08/lara-trump-my-view-fox-news-show/80701035007/)).

Her stated pitch is transparency and "common sense" commentary, plus a willingness to book Democrats — she named Pennsylvania Senator John Fetterman and HBO host Bill Maher as desired guests — and to interview the president himself, which her father-in-law had joked was the only way she'd get a huge audience ([USA TODAY](https://www.aberdeennews.com/story/entertainment/tv/2025/03/08/lara-trump-my-view-fox-news-show/80701035007/)). Alongside the show, she has pursued a music career: a charting 2023 cover of Tom Petty's "I Won't Back Down," the country-pop track "Hero," an electro-pop collaboration with French Montana titled "No Days Off," and — per Politico — a sixth Christian pop single ([Sunday Guardian Live](https://sundayguardianlive.com/trending/who-is-lara-trump-american-politician-daughter-in-law-of-donald-trump-check-her-age-career-net-worth-husband-family-how-tall-is-lara-trump-personal-life-more-251295/); [The Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/news/international/us/trumps-daughter-in-law-lara-just-made-a-bold-political-move-and-its-turning-heads/amp_articleshow/122909829.cms)).

She and Eric have two children: Eric "Luke" Trump (born 2017) and Carolina Dorothy Trump (born 2019) ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/us-news/who-is-lara-trump-and-how-is-she-related-to-donald-trump-5-facts-101773541431733.html)).
