## Overview

Erkin Adylov is the founder and chief executive of [Behavox](/organizations/behavox/), an artificial-intelligence company that helps banks, asset managers and hedge funds monitor employees' communications and trades for misconduct. A former [Goldman Sachs](/organizations/goldman-sachs/) equities analyst and [GLG](/organizations/man-glg/) partner who once ran a $2 billion fund, he founded Behavox in 2014 and has led it from a compliance startup to a controls platform the company says is used by more than 100 financial institutions ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/03/20/how-purpose-built-ai-is-transforming-compliance-from-detective-controls-into-preventive-controls/)).

Adylov's route into financial technology ran through energy research and the trading floor rather than through software. In March 2007 he co-authored a Chatham House paper on Asian national oil companies investing abroad, alongside Keun-Wook Paik, Valerie Marcel, Glada Lahn and John V. Mitchell ([Chatham House](https://www.chathamhouse.org/sites/default/files/public/Research/Energy%2C%20Environment%20and%20Development/r0307anoc.pdf)). He went on to a master's degree at the London School of Economics, then worked as a research analyst at Goldman Sachs before becoming a portfolio manager at GLG Partners, where he managed the GLG Financials Fund with about $2bn in assets under management ([Citi GPS](https://ir.citi.com/CiDxU7p7pAittTmqzfMCS9%2F91IS21vIjJXbn3wjpSEYiTXJ8FvEPRWx8WmmrKNgBSzDi8E2mGOI%3D)). Citi's 2018 profile of him also records that he is a British national who splits his time between [London](/places/london/) and [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) ([Citi GPS](https://ir.citi.com/CiDxU7p7pAittTmqzfMCS9%2F91IS21vIjJXbn3wjpSEYiTXJ8FvEPRWx8WmmrKNgBSzDi8E2mGOI%3D)).

## From energy research to the trading floor

Adylov's earliest documented work is not in software but in energy markets. The Chatham House paper he co-authored in March 2007, *Trends in Asian NOC Investment Abroad*, examined how state-owned oil companies from China, India, Japan, South Korea and Malaysia were buying into overseas exploration and production, and found that Chinese companies alone had invested at least $27bn in overseas upstream projects between 1995 and 2006 ([Chatham House](https://www.chathamhouse.org/sites/default/files/public/Research/Energy%2C%20Environment%20and%20Development/r0307anoc.pdf)). Its near-term production focus included Angola, Nigeria, [Kazakhstan](/places/kazakhstan/) and Iran — the kind of country-level, data-heavy analysis that later defined his approach to finance ([Chatham House](https://www.chathamhouse.org/sites/default/files/public/Research/Energy%2C%20Environment%20and%20Development/r0307anoc.pdf)).

From there he moved into the institutions his company would later monitor. After his master's degree at the London School of Economics, he joined Goldman Sachs as a research analyst, then moved to GLG Partners as a portfolio manager, co-managing the GLG Financials Fund with about $2bn in assets under management ([Citi GPS](https://ir.citi.com/CiDxU7p7pAittTmqzfMCS9%2F91IS21vIjJXbn3wjpSEYiTXJ8FvEPRWx8WmmrKNgBSzDi8E2mGOI%3D)). A later industry biography describes him as a partner at GLG who "ran a $2 billion hedge fund" at "one of the world's largest investment firms" ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/amltechforum/speaker/erkin-adylov/)). The same biography credits him with introducing artificial intelligence and machine learning to the compliance and conduct space, working across numerous languages and communication channels at multinational firms ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/amltechforum/speaker/erkin-adylov/)).

## Founding Behavox — and the customer who became the investor

Adylov founded Behavox in 2014 on a premise drawn directly from his trading-floor experience: banks and asset managers generate enormous volumes of internal data and use almost none of it to understand behaviour ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/amltechforum/speaker/erkin-adylov/)). The early financing was modest — $400,000 from unnamed investors in 2014, $1m in 2015, an undisclosed Series A co-led by [Hoxton Ventures](/organizations/hoxton-ventures/) and [Promus Ventures](/organizations/promus-ventures/) in 2016, then a $20m Series A1 in 2017 led by [Citigroup](/organizations/citigroup/) and backed by [Index Ventures](/organizations/index-ventures/) ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/02/25/behavox-spies-100m-vision-fund-investment/)). The product aggregated employees' internal communications and applied machine learning to flag compliance breaches, insider threats and bad conduct, while also automating customer-relationship work ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/02/25/behavox-spies-100m-vision-fund-investment/)).

A 2018 interview in Citi's *Bank of the Future* report shows how the platform worked in practice at the time. Behavox's systems were handling 3.5 million phone calls and more than 26 million emails, chats and messages a day, across 20 to 30 communication channels from voice calls to WhatsApp, Bloomberg and Slack ([Citi GPS](https://ir.citi.com/CiDxU7p7pAittTmqzfMCS9%2F91IS21vIjJXbn3wjpSEYiTXJ8FvEPRWx8WmmrKNgBSzDi8E2mGOI%3D)). Its machine-learning search could cut a hopeless keyword trawl down to size: when regulators wanted transactions linked to The Cheesecake Factory, a legacy keyword search for "cake" returned nearly 15,000 references, mostly about food, while Behavox narrowed the relevant content to about 50 to 60 items ([Citi GPS](https://ir.citi.com/CiDxU7p7pAittTmqzfMCS9%2F91IS21vIjJXbn3wjpSEYiTXJ8FvEPRWx8WmmrKNgBSzDi8E2mGOI%3D)). Adylov told Citi the platform cost banks nearly five times less than a legacy system, and that the core software had taken three years to build ([Citi GPS](https://ir.citi.com/CiDxU7p7pAittTmqzfMCS9%2F91IS21vIjJXbn3wjpSEYiTXJ8FvEPRWx8WmmrKNgBSzDi8E2mGOI%3D)).

Recognition followed quickly. Behavox was named to the CB Insights AI 100 in 2019, chosen from more than 3,000 companies, and joined Tech Nation's Future Fifty programme the same year in the Data Analytics category ([AIThority](https://aithority.com/machine-learning/anodot-named-to-the-2019-cb-insights-ai-100-list-of-most-innovative-ai-startups/); [FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/new-wave-of-uk-banking-pioneers-takes-centre-stage-in-tech-nations-future-fifty-programme-for-2019/)). In 2020 it appeared on the [Forbes](/organizations/forbes/) FinTech 50, one of four companies in the Wall Street & Enterprise category ([FinTechtris](https://www.fintechtris.com/blog/2019/2/26/24-of-forbes-fintech50-san-francisco)), and Adylov himself was named one of Goldman Sachs' 100 Most Intriguing Entrepreneurs of 2020, with chief executive David M. Solomon praising the summit's "impressive future leaders" ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20201014005190/en/Behavox-Founder-and-CEO-Erkin-Adylov-Honored-by-Goldman-Sachs-as-Entrepreneurial-Leader)).

The defining financing came in February 2020, when [SoftBank](/organizations/softbank-group/)'s Vision Fund 2 invested $100 million ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/02/25/behavox-spies-100m-vision-fund-investment/)). What made the round unusual is that SoftBank had already stress-tested the product as a customer: SoftBank Investment Advisers bought the platform and used it before investing, after what the company described as extensive due diligence ([Tech Startups](https://techstartups.com/2020/02/24/softbank-leads-100-million-investment-behavox-worlds-end-end-data-operating-platform/)). Its managing partner, [Munish Varma](/people/munish-varma/), said Behavox was "at the forefront of the AI revolution" and that the firm believed in its mission "to organise all corporate communications data on earth" ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/02/25/behavox-spies-100m-vision-fund-investment/)). By then Behavox's customers included banks such as Jefferies, hedge funds such as Balyasny Asset Management and mining companies such as Anglo American, alongside sovereign wealth funds and private equity firms, with 2019 revenue growth coming from Canada, Japan, Singapore and the [United States](/places/united-states/) ([Tech Startups](https://techstartups.com/2020/02/24/softbank-leads-100-million-investment-behavox-worlds-end-end-data-operating-platform/)). After the round, Adylov and key employees remained the largest shareholders ([Tech Startups](https://techstartups.com/2020/02/24/softbank-leads-100-million-investment-behavox-worlds-end-end-data-operating-platform/)).

## From communications surveillance to a unified controls platform

For six years after the SoftBank round, Behavox took no new equity. The company says it has been profitable since 2023, and it funded expansion partly with debt: a $70 million facility from Hercules Capital in autumn 2024, used to support the acquisition of [Mosaic Smart Data](/organizations/mosaic-smart-data/) and an investment in [b-next](/organizations/b-next/) ([Financial IT](https://financialit.net/news/fundraising-news/behavox-raises-175-million-hps-investment-partners-part-blackrock-accelerate)). In 2025 it introduced Polaris, a trade-surveillance platform covering market-abuse detection across all 10 major asset classes, operating independently or alongside its Quantum communications-surveillance product, with agentic AI pulling related chats, emails, voice and archive records into a single case ([Financial IT](https://financialit.net/news/fundraising-news/behavox-raises-175-million-hps-investment-partners-part-blackrock-accelerate)).

Adylov explained the strategy in a March 2026 interview: customers had pushed the company beyond communications surveillance because they were tired of defending a different system for every risk — "one system and one story for comms, a different one for trades, another for archiving, and a separate stack for policies" ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/03/20/how-purpose-built-ai-is-transforming-compliance-from-detective-controls-into-preventive-controls/)). Purpose-built AI, he said, delivers four to five times more true positives than lexicon-based systems, with far fewer false positives — a specific performance claim, backed by the company's own reporting rather than independent testing ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/03/20/how-purpose-built-ai-is-transforming-compliance-from-detective-controls-into-preventive-controls/)). Where rivals bolt generic language models onto compliance tools, Behavox built its own large language models and AI stack, which Adylov argued was necessary because "in our world, the AI isn't a convenience feature — it is the control" ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/03/20/how-purpose-built-ai-is-transforming-compliance-from-detective-controls-into-preventive-controls/)). The company says it has invested over $200m in research and development, has had AI in production for three years across more than 100 institutions — including a central bank and a regulator as customers — and monitors communications in 15 languages across more than 70,000 employees ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2026/03/20/how-purpose-built-ai-is-transforming-compliance-from-detective-controls-into-preventive-controls/)).

The next financing completed the transformation. In June 2026, HPS Investment Partners, part of [BlackRock](/organizations/blackrock/), made a $175 million preferred-equity investment — Behavox's first equity financing since 2020 — joining a cap table that already included SoftBank, Citigroup, Index Ventures and Hoxton Ventures ([Financial IT](https://financialit.net/news/fundraising-news/behavox-raises-175-million-hps-investment-partners-part-blackrock-accelerate)). The company said it had grown sevenfold since SoftBank's investment, fully repaid and retired the $70 million Hercules facility, and counted SoftBank Investment Advisers in the [United Kingdom](/places/united-kingdom/) and [SoftBank Corporation](/organizations/softbank-corporation/) in Japan among its customers ([Financial IT](https://financialit.net/news/fundraising-news/behavox-raises-175-million-hps-investment-partners-part-blackrock-accelerate)). Adylov said the investment "recognizes the strong platform we have built and positions us to pursue strategic acquisitions that expand our capabilities" ([Financial IT](https://financialit.net/news/fundraising-news/behavox-raises-175-million-hps-investment-partners-part-blackrock-accelerate)).

The first of those acquisitions followed within months. In October 2026 Behavox acquired Apiax, a Zurich firm founded in 2017 whose software turns financial regulation into machine-readable rules inside a firm's own systems, extending the platform into cross-border and content compliance alongside its Pathfinder regulatory-change product ([FinTech Futures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/m-a/behavox-enhances-regulatory-intelligence-offering-with-apiax-acquisition)).
