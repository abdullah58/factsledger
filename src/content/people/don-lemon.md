## Overview

On January 30, 2026, Don Lemon walked out of a federal courthouse in Los Angeles facing charges no American broadcast journalist of his stature had ever confronted. Federal agents had arrested him overnight, and a federal grand jury in Minnesota had indicted him alongside eight other defendants on charges of conspiracy and interfering with the First Amendment rights of worshippers. The case turned on his livestreamed coverage of an anti-ICE protest that disrupted a January 18 church service at Cities Church in St. Paul, Minnesota. ([Associated Press](https://srnnews.com/journalist-don-lemon-arrested-after-protest-that-disrupted-minnesota-church-service/))

Lemon struck a defiant tone outside the courthouse, telling reporters, "I will not be silenced." He said he had spent his entire career covering the news and had no intention of stopping now. ([Associated Press](https://srnnews.com/journalist-don-lemon-arrested-after-protest-that-disrupted-minnesota-church-service/)) He was released without having to post money, was even granted permission to travel to France in June while the case is pending, and his lawyer said he plans to plead not guilty and fight the charges in Minnesota. ([Associated Press](https://srnnews.com/journalist-don-lemon-arrested-after-protest-that-disrupted-minnesota-church-service/))

The prosecution has a strange procedural history behind it. A federal magistrate judge had already declined to approve Lemon's arrest, citing a lack of evidence, and the chief federal appeals court judge in Minnesota rejected the prosecutor's appeal of that decision, writing that there was "no evidence" of any criminal behavior in Lemon's work. ([NPR](https://www.kedt.org/2026-01-30/feds-arrest-don-lemon-minnesota-journalist-and-2-others-over-church-protest))

In his livestream, Lemon said he was inside the church as a journalist. He interviewed the pastor and the protesters, and the Washington Post's review of his footage found he spent roughly 45 minutes inside, that his exchanges were mostly calm, and that the footage does not show him joining the chants that disrupted the service. ([NPR](https://www.kedt.org/2026-01-30/feds-arrest-don-lemon-minnesota-journalist-and-2-others-over-church-protest)) Attorney General Pam Bondi took personal credit for the arrests, saying they had occurred at her direction. Journalists, press advocates and Democratic lawmakers accused the Trump administration of trying to intimidate independent reporters, and CNN — Lemon's former employer — said the arrest raised "profoundly concerning questions about press freedom and the First Amendment." ([NPR](https://www.kedt.org/2026-01-30/feds-arrest-don-lemon-minnesota-journalist-and-2-others-over-church-protest))

## Building his own network

While those federal charges were still pending, Lemon did something few fired anchors have attempted: he built his own television network. On September 4, 2026, his company Lemon Media Network announced The Don Lemon Channel, a 24/7 television network available across linear and streaming platforms, launched in partnership with Swerve TV, an operator and distributor of free ad-supported streaming television. ([Swerve TV](https://swerve.tv/news_announcements/lemon-media-to-launch-the-don-lemon-channel/))

The channel carries twice-daily live programs, daily news drops, archival shows and partner programming from other independent voices, including The Jim Acosta Show, Josh the Progressive, The Monique Pressley Show, Michael Fanone and Dr. Frita. ([Swerve TV](https://swerve.tv/news_announcements/lemon-media-to-launch-the-don-lemon-channel/); [Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2026/09/04/the-don-lemon-channel-launches-with-swerve-tv/)) "We built this network on the idea that people want the truth delivered straight without a corporate boss deciding what makes air," Lemon said in the announcement. "Almost eleven million followers later, they are still asking for more. So, we are giving them more. More hours, more voices, and now a channel on their televisions. That is what America is demanding. We go where the audience is." ([Swerve TV](https://swerve.tv/news_announcements/lemon-media-to-launch-the-don-lemon-channel/))

The channel grew out of The Don Lemon Show, which he has streamed live twice a day, Monday through Friday, on YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, Substack, Facebook and Twitch, building an audience of more than 1.4 million subscribers. ([UrbanGeekz](https://urbangeekz.com/2026/09/former-cnn-anchor-don-lemon-returns-to-tv-with-new-channel/); [Cord Cutters News](https://cordcuttersnews.com/former-cnn-anchor-don-lemon-is-launching-a-free-streaming-channel/)) The show has won Webby Awards for Best Video Podcast Host, including the People's Voice Award, and four NAACP Image Awards, including Outstanding News and Information. ([Cord Cutters News](https://cordcuttersnews.com/former-cnn-anchor-don-lemon-is-launching-a-free-streaming-channel/))

## The Musk fight

Before the channel, there was the fight with [Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk/). In August 2024, Lemon sued Musk and X over the cancelled partnership that was supposed to bring his show to the platform. ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2024/8/2/ex-cnn-host-don-lemon-sues-elon-musk-over-cancelled-talk-show-deal)) The deal collapsed hours after a tense 90-minute interview in which Lemon pressed Musk on drug use, his political beliefs and the 2024 presidential election. Musk cancelled the partnership before it formally took effect, though the interview aired as planned on YouTube, podcasts and X. ([The Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/life/entertainment-elon-musk-sued-by-former-cnn-anchor-don-lemon-over-cancelled-x-show-judge-clears-way-for-trial-4129532/))

In September 2024 a judge rejected Musk's bid to dismiss the lawsuit. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/judge-denies-elon-musks-bid-dismiss-don-lemons-lawsuit-over-x-partnership-2024-09-23/)) In July 2025 a U.S. court cleared the way for Lemon's fraud and misappropriation claims against Musk to proceed to trial, again rejecting Musk's attempt to have the case thrown out. ([The Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/life/entertainment-elon-musk-sued-by-former-cnn-anchor-don-lemon-over-cancelled-x-show-judge-clears-way-for-trial-4129532/))

## Seventeen years at CNN

Lemon spent 17 years at [CNN](/organizations/cnn/), joining as a correspondent out of New York in September 2006. ([UrbanGeekz](https://urbangeekz.com/2026/09/former-cnn-anchor-don-lemon-returns-to-tv-with-new-channel/)) He reported and anchored on the scene for some of the defining breaking-news stories of the era: the death of Michael Jackson in 2009, the Sandy Hook Elementary School shooting in 2012, the Boston Marathon bombing in 2013, the shooting of Michael Brown in 2014, the Pulse nightclub shooting in Orlando in 2016, and Hurricane Florence in 2018. ([UrbanGeekz](https://urbangeekz.com/2026/09/former-cnn-anchor-don-lemon-returns-to-tv-with-new-channel/))

In 2014 he became the permanent host of CNN Tonight, the program that replaced Piers Morgan Live; in 2021 the show was retitled Don Lemon Tonight. ([UrbanGeekz](https://urbangeekz.com/2026/09/former-cnn-anchor-don-lemon-returns-to-tv-with-new-channel/)) In 2022 the network named him a co-anchor of its new morning program, CNN This Morning, alongside [Kaitlan Collins](/people/kaitlan-collins/) and Poppy Harlow. ([UrbanGeekz](https://urbangeekz.com/2026/09/former-cnn-anchor-don-lemon-returns-to-tv-with-new-channel/))

It ended abruptly. In February 2023 Lemon drew a backlash after saying on air that Republican presidential candidate Nikki Haley was not "in her prime," remarks widely condemned as sexist. He apologized and stepped away from the morning show for three days before returning. ([UrbanGeekz](https://urbangeekz.com/2026/09/former-cnn-anchor-don-lemon-returns-to-tv-with-new-channel/)) Weeks later, Variety published a report in which current and former colleagues, including Soledad O'Brien, Kyra Phillips and Nancy Grace, described a pattern of disrespectful treatment toward women at the network; his spokesperson denied the allegations. ([UrbanGeekz](https://urbangeekz.com/2026/09/former-cnn-anchor-don-lemon-returns-to-tv-with-new-channel/)) On April 24, 2023, CNN fired him, ending a contract that was supposed to run through 2026. ([The Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/life/entertainment-elon-musk-sued-by-former-cnn-anchor-don-lemon-over-cancelled-x-show-judge-clears-way-for-trial-4129532/))

## Early career

Lemon's path to cable news ran through local television. He started out as a news assistant at WNYW in New York City while still attending Brooklyn College. ([Los Angeles Watts Times](https://www.lawattstimes.com/index.php?option=com_content&view=article&id=1596:lemon-zest-don-lemon-the-we-were-there-the-march-on-washington-interview&catid=27:arts-culture&Itemid=117)) He worked as a weekend news anchor for WBRC in Birmingham, Alabama and WCAU in Philadelphia, then became an anchor and investigative reporter for KTVI, the Fox affiliate in St. Louis, and for [Fox](/organizations/fox-news/)'s Chicago affiliate. ([UrbanGeekz](https://urbangeekz.com/2026/09/former-cnn-anchor-don-lemon-returns-to-tv-with-new-channel/))

His breakthrough into national network news came in the early 2000s, when he moved to New York to work for NBC News as a correspondent, appearing on Today, NBC Nightly News and MSNBC programming. In 2003 he joined NBC affiliate WMAQ-TV in Chicago as a reporter and local news co-anchor, where he won three Emmys for local reporting. ([UrbanGeekz](https://urbangeekz.com/2026/09/former-cnn-anchor-don-lemon-returns-to-tv-with-new-channel/)) A 2022 tribute entered into the Congressional Record describes him as a multiple Emmy and Edward R. Murrow award-winning anchor who reported for NBC News' New York operations on Today, Weekend Today, NBC Nightly News and MSNBC. ([Congressional Record](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-168/issue-192/extensions-of-remarks-section/article/E1255-1)) The Murrow came for his coverage of the capture of the Washington, D.C. snipers. ([TV Guide](https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/don-lemon/bio/3030048394/))

## Books

Lemon is the author of two books. His 2011 memoir, Transparent, was the occasion for him to come out publicly as gay. In an NPR interview recorded a month before the book's release, he said his livelihood was "on the line" and that he did not know whether viewers or the network would accept him. ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/2011-05-16/livelihood-on-the-line-anchor-reveals-hes-gay?_amp=true); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/cnn-host-comes-out-closet-27439/)) His second book, This Is the Fire: What I Say to My Friends About Racism, was published on March 16, 2021 by Little, Brown and debuted atop the [New York Times](/organizations/new-york-times/) nonfiction best-seller list, topping both the combined print and e-book and the standard hardcover lists. ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/don-lemons-this-is-the-fire-nyt-bestseller/))

## Personal life

Lemon was born on March 1, 1966 in Baton Rouge, Louisiana, and graduated from Baton Rouge public schools before earning his degree in broadcast journalism from Brooklyn College. ([Congressional Record](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-168/issue-192/extensions-of-remarks-section/article/E1255-1)) He met real estate broker Tim Malone in 2016. On April 6, 2024, the couple married at the Fifth Avenue Presbyterian Church in New York City, in a ceremony officiated by Linda Thomas-Greenfield, then the United States ambassador to the United Nations, and attended by about 140 guests. ([Entertainment Tonight](https://www.etonline.com/don-lemon-marries-tim-malone-in-star-studded-wedding-ceremony-223045?amp); [Black Enterprise](https://www.blackenterprise.com/don-lemon-marries-tim-malone-new-york-city/))
