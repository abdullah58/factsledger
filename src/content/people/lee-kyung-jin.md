Lee Kyung-jin is a South Korean actress whose fifty-year career runs from 1980s "national little sister" TV stardom through a breast-cancer battle that dropped her to 38 kilograms, to a late international turn: at 61 she played the adult Ayla — the grown-up [Kim Eun-ja](/people/kim-eun-ja/) — in [Turkey](/places/turkey/)'s 2017 Oscar entry *[Ayla: The Daughter of War](/creative-works/ayla-the-daughter-of-war/)* ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)).

## Overview

Lee Kyung-jin (이경진) is a South Korean actress who has worked continuously from the 1970s into the 2020s, moving from ingenue stardom to the mother roles that define veteran Korean TV actresses ([Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/entertainment/2024/11/20/2024112015194019515)). Born in Seoul in 1956 — database records give October 2 — she entered acting through MBC's 7th open talent recruitment, though Korean newspapers disagree on whether that intake was 1974 ([MK Star Today](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/hot-issues/8537052)) or 1975 ([Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/entertainment/2024/11/20/2024112015194019515)). In the 1980s her "pure" looks made her the original "national little sister," ranked with Jang Mi-hee and Yoo Ji-in ([MK Star Today](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/hot-issues/8537052)). After a 2012 breast-cancer diagnosis she refused a recommended total mastectomy, endured 16 rounds of chemotherapy and 38 rounds of radiation, and was declared cancer-free ([Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/entertainment/2024/11/20/2024112015194019515)). At 61 she took her most internationally visible role, the adult Ayla in *Ayla: The Daughter of War* ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)), and she was still appearing in new films in 2025 ([HanCinema](https://www.hanCinema.net/korean_Lee_Kyung-jin-filmography.html)).

## From "national little sister" to the mother of the drama

Debuting through MBC's 7th open talent intake in the mid-1970s, Lee Kyung-jin rose on what the Korean press prized as a "pure" image — delicate features and an unshowy presence that made her, in Maeil Business Newspaper's phrase, the original "national little sister" ([MK Star Today](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/hot-issues/8537052)). The 1980s put her shoulder to shoulder with Jang Mi-hee and Yoo Ji-in across dramas and advertising, the classic double track of a top TV star ([MK Star Today](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/hot-issues/8537052)). The 1990s brought the transition every actress of her generation faced: the ingenue aged into the mother, and Lee made that pivot her second career, playing protagonists' mothers in series such as *Mongsil's Sister* and *You and I* ([Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/entertainment/2024/11/20/2024112015194019515)). Later decades kept her in constant work — *Phoenix*, *Capital Scandal*, *On Air* — then the 2010s daily-drama circuit and a 2018 variety turn as the oldest new cast member of SBS's *Burning Youth* ([MK Star Today](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/hot-issues/8537052)).

## The treatment she refused — and the 38-kilogram battle

In 2012 Lee was diagnosed with breast cancer, and the first plan called for a total mastectomy ([Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/entertainment/2024/11/20/2024112015194019515)). She refused. After fellow actress Go Doo-shim — honorary ambassador for Seoul National University Hospital — arranged a second opinion there, doctors said the breast did not need to be removed at all, news she later said finally let her sleep ([MK](https://www.mk.co.kr/en/hot-issues/11997961)). Money Today reports she endured 16 rounds of chemotherapy and 38 rounds of radiation instead of the recommended mastectomy and was declared cancer-free — a grueling regimen that, by her own account, left her working through schedules in a wig after losing her hair — she kept acting through treatment ([Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/entertainment/2024/11/20/2024112015194019515)). The diagnosis followed a brutal year: within twelve months she had buried her older sister and mother, then finished renovating her house — "I felt I couldn't die because it would be too unfair" ([MK](https://www.mk.co.kr/en/hot-issues/11997961)). At her lowest her weight fell to 38 kilograms ([MK](https://www.mk.co.kr/en/hot-issues/11997961)).

## Adult Ayla: a 61-year-old's Oscar-entry role

In 2017 Lee took the role that introduced her to audiences outside Korea: the adult Ayla in *Ayla: The Daughter of War*, directed by [Can Ülkay](/people/can-ulkay/) ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)). The film dramatizes the true story of Turkish sergeant [Süleyman Dilbirliği](/people/suleyman-dilbirligi/), who found the orphaned girl who became Kim Eun-ja during the [Korean War](/events/korean-war/), cared for her for fifteen months, and was reunited with her sixty years later after a 2010 TRT documentary made their story public ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)). Child actress [Kim Seol](/people/kim-seol/) played the young Ayla; Lee, then 61, played Ayla grown — the present-day Kim Eun-ja — in the film's 2010 sequences ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633); [HanCinema](https://www.hanCinema.net/korean_Lee_Kyung-jin-filmography.html)). Made as a Turkey–[South Korea](/places/south-korea/) co-production marking sixty years of diplomatic relations, it became Turkey's submission for the foreign-language film Oscar at the 90th Academy Awards ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)). The part was small but symbolically exact: a veteran Korean actress embodying a real Korean woman at the center of a Turkish national story.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who did Lee Kyung-jin play in *Ayla: The Daughter of War*?**
She played the adult Ayla — the grown-up Kim Eun-ja — appearing in the film's 2010 sequences, while child actress Kim Seol played the young Ayla of the Korean War years ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633); [HanCinema](https://www.hanCinema.net/korean_Lee_Kyung-jin-filmography.html)). She was 61 at the time of the production.

**How did she survive breast cancer?**
Diagnosed in 2012, she refused a recommended total mastectomy and, after a second opinion arranged by Go Doo-shim at Seoul National University Hospital, underwent 16 rounds of chemotherapy and 38 rounds of radiation instead — and was declared cancer-free ([Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/entertainment/2024/11/20/2024112015194019515); [MK](https://www.mk.co.kr/en/hot-issues/11997961)). Her weight fell to 38 kilograms during treatment ([MK](https://www.mk.co.kr/en/hot-issues/11997961)).

**Did she debut in 1974 or 1975?**
Korean press disagrees: MK Star Today reports she debuted through MBC's 7th open talent intake in 1974 ([MK Star Today](https://www.mk.co.kr/news/hot-issues/8537052)), while Money Today gives 1975 for the same 7th intake ([Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/entertainment/2024/11/20/2024112015194019515)). This profile records both rather than picking one.

**What has she done most recently?**
Her 2020s credits include Queen Moon Jeong in *Joseon Survival* (2019), *Amor Fati* (2021), a special appearance as Soo-jin's mother in Jason Yu's thriller *Sleep* (2022), *Three Bold Siblings* (2022), and Taek-seon's mother in *Virus* (2025) ([HanCinema](https://www.hanCinema.net/korean_Lee_Kyung-jin-filmography.html)).

**Is she the same Lee Kyung-jin credited on the 2009 game *MagnaCarta 2*?**
Probably not. The credit appears only on IMDb-derived aggregates and in no Korean filmography database, suggesting a namesake conflation on a shared name page; this profile excludes it ([openItems](#)).
