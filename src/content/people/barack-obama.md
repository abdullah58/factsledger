## Overview

Barack Hussein Obama II is the 44th President of the [United States](/places/united-states/), serving from 2009 to 2017, and the first African American to hold the office. Born on 4 August 1961 in Honolulu, Hawaii, he worked as a community organizer in [Chicago](/places/chicago/) before graduating from Harvard Law School, where he was the first Black president of the Harvard Law Review, and entering Illinois politics ([The White House](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/1600/presidents/barackobama); [Britannica](https://mendel-preview.dev-ext.britannica.com/summary/Barack-Obama)). He was elected to the Illinois Senate in 1996 and to the U.S. Senate in 2004, won the presidency in 2008 after defeating [Hillary Clinton](/people/hillary-clinton/) for the Democratic nomination and John McCain in the general election, and was re-elected in 2012 ([Britannica](https://mendel-preview.dev-ext.britannica.com/summary/Barack-Obama)). His presidency is defined by the Affordable Care Act, the 2009 [Nobel Peace Prize](/events/nobel-prize/), and the operation that killed Osama bin Laden — and by a Chicago afterlife that culminated in the Obama Presidential Center opening in Jackson Park in June 2026 ([ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/1039482/obama-presidential-center-in-chicagos-jackson-park-set-to-open-on-juneteenth-2026)).

## From Honolulu to the Senate

Obama was born in Honolulu to a Kenyan father and a mother from Kansas, and was raised with help from his grandparents — his grandfather served in Patton's army, his grandmother rose through a bank's ranks ([The White House](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/1600/presidents/barackobama)). After college, funded by scholarships and student loans, he moved to Chicago, where he worked with a group of churches to help rebuild communities devastated by the closure of local steel plants ([The White House](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/1600/presidents/barackobama)). He graduated from Columbia University in 1983 and entered [Harvard University](/universities/harvard-university/)'s law school in 1988, where he was elected the first Black president of the Harvard Law Review in the spring of 1990 ([Britannica](https://mendel-preview.dev-ext.britannica.com/summary/Barack-Obama); [Harvard Law School](https://hls.harvard.edu/today/a-commander-in-chief/)). After graduating in 1991 he returned to Chicago, led a voter-registration drive, taught constitutional law at the University of Chicago, and was elected to the Illinois Senate in 1996 ([The White House](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/1600/presidents/barackobama)). In 2004 he was elected to the U.S. Senate, and in February 2007 he announced his candidacy for president in Springfield, Illinois ([Harvard Law School](https://hls.harvard.edu/today/a-commander-in-chief/)).

## Two terms in the White House

Obama took office during the deepest economic recession since the Great Depression, and his first term centred on recovery and health care ([Britannica](https://mendel-preview.dev-ext.britannica.com/summary/Barack-Obama)). He signed the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act into law on 23 March 2010; around 20 million more people gained health insurance than before the law, which required coverage of pre-existing conditions, banned lifetime dollar limits and let young adults stay on their parents' plans until age 26 — coverage on that scale, keeping hospitals open that would otherwise have closed, was the law's measurable outcome ([KHSU / NPR](https://www.khsu.org/2020-03-23/10-years-of-the-affordable-care-act)). In October 2009 he received the Nobel Peace Prize for his efforts to strengthen international diplomacy, the third sitting president so honoured ([Harvard Law School](http://hls.harvard.edu/today/obama-wins-2009-nobel-peace-prize-video/)). On 1 May 2011 he announced that a U.S. operation had killed Osama bin Laden in Abbottabad, Pakistan ([govinfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/DCPD-201100314/html/DCPD-201100314.htm)). Re-elected in 2012, he announced the normalisation of relations with Cuba in 2014 and in 2016 became the first sitting president to visit the island in more than 80 years ([Britannica](https://mendel-preview.dev-ext.britannica.com/summary/Barack-Obama)).

## Chicago, the Copernicus Center, and the Presidential Center

Chicago runs through Obama's public life more than any other place: it is where he organised, taught, launched his Senate career and returned as president. On 25 November 2014 he spoke at the [Copernicus Center](/organizations/copernicus-center/), the [Jefferson Park](/places/jefferson-park/) venue of the [Copernicus Foundation](/organizations/copernicus-foundation/), to rally support for the immigration executive actions he had announced five days earlier, including a programme to defer deportation for potentially more than four million people; the same day he met privately for seven minutes with Chicago's newly installed Archbishop Blase Cupich ([National Catholic Reporter](https://www.ncronline.org/blogs/immigration-and-church/obama-cupich-meet-chicago-talk-immigration)). The visit captured his Chicago method — making a national case from a neighbourhood institution. That bond is now built in Jackson Park, where the Obama Presidential Center opened to the public on 19 June 2026, Juneteenth, a day after its dedication. The 19.3-acre campus, announced in 2016 with groundbreaking in 2021, holds a museum tower inscribed with the words "You Are America" from his 2015 Selma speech, a forum building, a public library branch and youth facilities ([ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/1039482/obama-presidential-center-in-chicagos-jackson-park-set-to-open-on-juneteenth-2026)).

## FAQ

### Who is Barack Obama?

Barack Obama is the 44th President of the United States, serving from 2009 to 2017, and the first African American to hold the office. Born in Honolulu in 1961, he was a Chicago community organizer, Harvard Law Review president, Illinois state senator and U.S. senator before winning the presidency in 2008 and re-election in 2012.

### What did Obama do before becoming president?

He worked as a community organizer in Chicago, graduated from Harvard Law School in 1991 — the first Black president of the Harvard Law Review — then led a voter-registration drive and taught constitutional law at the University of Chicago. He was elected to the Illinois Senate in 1996 and to the U.S. Senate in 2004.

### What is the Affordable Care Act?

The Affordable Care Act, signed on 23 March 2010, is the health reform law that expanded insurance coverage in the United States. Around 20 million more people gained coverage than before the law, which required insurers to cover pre-existing conditions, banned lifetime dollar limits and let young adults stay on parents' plans until 26.

### Why did Obama receive the Nobel Peace Prize?

He received the 2009 Nobel Peace Prize for his extraordinary efforts to strengthen international diplomacy and cooperation between peoples, with special importance attached to his vision of a world free of nuclear weapons. He was the third sitting U.S. president to receive it, after Theodore Roosevelt and Woodrow Wilson.

### What is the Obama Presidential Center?

It is a museum, library and community campus in Jackson Park, Chicago, commemorating Obama's presidency. The 19.3-acre site, announced in 2016, was dedicated on 18 June 2026 and opened to the public on 19 June 2026, Juneteenth, with a museum tower, forum building, public library branch and youth facilities.
