## Overview

Reuven Yaron (1924–2014) was an [Israeli](/places/israel/) legal historian who
spent most of his career at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, where he taught
Roman law and, from the early 1960s, ancient Near Eastern law
([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/yaron-reuven)).

He should not be confused with the composer of the same name — a Croatian-born
Israeli soldier killed in the 1956 Suez War, who is the subject of the Wikipedia
article titled "Reuven Yaron"
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuven_Yaron)). This profile is about
the legal historian born in Vienna in 1924 and deceased in 2014, documented by
his university and by the Israel Law Review
([Hebrew University Faculty of Law](https://en.law.huji.ac.il/people/reuven-yaron);
[Israel Law Review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/israel-law-review/article/abs/professor-reuven-yaron-selected-list-of-publications/6FE118AB80D2B69F0DB6DE3B63A71FB2)).

What set Yaron apart from a typical university scholar was the second career he
built inside Israel's public institutions: he directed the Jewish National and
University Library in Jerusalem, chaired the Israel Broadcasting Authority, and
served as state archivist
([Hebrew University Faculty of Law](https://en.law.huji.ac.il/people/reuven-yaron)).
His scholarship, meanwhile, earned him the description of "an excellent Romanist
and a leading historian of ancient Oriental law in the contemporary period,"
noted for his mastery of the sources and philology, methodological acumen, and
selection of fundamental topics
([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/yaron-reuven)).

## A British training, a Jerusalem career

Yaron was born in Vienna, Austria, in 1924 and settled in Palestine in 1939
([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/yaron-reuven)).
He studied law in the first class of the Hebrew University's Faculty of Law and
then wrote his doctoral thesis at the University of Oxford
([Hebrew University Faculty of Law](https://en.law.huji.ac.il/people/reuven-yaron)).
After a year teaching jurisprudence at the University of Aberdeen in 1956–57, he
returned to Jerusalem in 1957 to teach Roman law at the Hebrew University,
adding ancient Near Eastern law to his teaching from 1962
([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/yaron-reuven)).

In 1967 he became dean of the Hebrew University's law faculty, serving until
1971; encyclopedia.com dates his appointment to 1967, while the university's
own page gives the full 1967–1971 tenure
([Hebrew University Faculty of Law](https://en.law.huji.ac.il/people/reuven-yaron);
[Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/yaron-reuven)).
His deanship coincided with the faculty's move into the renovated campus on Mt.
Scopus, making it the first faculty of the university to return to the
institution's original buildings
([Hebrew University Faculty of Law](https://en.law.huji.ac.il/people/reuven-yaron)).
He was appointed professor in 1968
([Hebrew University Faculty of Law](https://en.law.huji.ac.il/people/reuven-yaron)),
and his publisher later listed him as Professor Emeritus of Roman Law at the
Hebrew University, with fields of interest spanning ancient legal history —
early oriental law, biblical law and talmudic law
([Magnes Press](https://www.magnespress.co.il/en/contributor/Reuven_Yaron)).

## Scholarship: Elephantine, Eshnunna, and the law of antiquity

Yaron's early work took up the Aramaic legal papyri from Elephantine. His
*Introduction to the Law of the Aramaic Papyri* (Oxford University Press, 1961) was followed by a Hebrew
study of the same documents, *Ha-Mishpat shel Mismekhei Yeb* ("Laws of the
Elephantine Documents," 1961)
([Israel Law Review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/israel-law-review/article/abs/professor-reuven-yaron-selected-list-of-publications/6FE118AB80D2B69F0DB6DE3B63A71FB2);
[Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/yaron-reuven)).
His first English book, *Gifts in Contemplation of Death in Jewish and Roman
Law* (Oxford University Press, 1960), compared deathbed gifts across the two
legal traditions, and a Hebrew volume of *Meḥkarim be-Mishpat Romi* ("Studies
in Roman Law") followed in 1968
([Israel Law Review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/israel-law-review/article/abs/professor-reuven-yaron-selected-list-of-publications/6FE118AB80D2B69F0DB6DE3B63A71FB2);
[Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/yaron-reuven)).

The work that defined his reputation was *The Laws of Eshnunna* (Jerusalem:
Magnes Press of the Hebrew University, 1969), an edition and study of an
ancient Mesopotamian law collection, published as a 220-page volume
([National Library of Israel](https://www.nli.org.il/en/books/NNL_ALEPH990021988030205171/NLI)).
It drew sustained international attention: the University of Tübingen's library
catalog records eleven scholarly reviews published between 1969 and 1973, in
journals including the *Bulletin of the School of Oriental and African
Studies*, *Biblica*, *Orientalia*, the *Revue d'Assyriologie* and the *Journal
of the Royal Asiatic Society*, by reviewers including D. J. Wiseman, Ze'ev W.
Falk and G. R. Driver
([University of Tübingen](https://ocb.uni-tuebingen.de/Record/KEI00085583/Details)).

Nearly twenty years later Yaron produced a second revised edition (Magnes
Press–Brill, 1988), which his publisher described as "in many respects a new
book": new material from Tell Haddad, Ishcali and Ebla had come to light,
comparison with the Code of Hammurabi was expanded, continuing controversies
over the *muškenum* and *imdat šarrim* were revisited, and the English
translation was reconsidered in many small details
([Magnes Press](https://www.magnespress.co.il/book/The_Laws_of_Eshnunna-185);
[Israel Law Review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/israel-law-review/article/abs/professor-reuven-yaron-selected-list-of-publications/6FE118AB80D2B69F0DB6DE3B63A71FB2)).

He kept publishing into his seventies: an article on Hammurabi in the
*Tijdschrift voor Rechtsgeschiedenis* (1991), a piece on women in Genesis in a
1992 festschrift, a study of social problems and policies in the ancient Near
East (1993), and an analysis of stylistic conceits in biblical law in the 1995
Jacob Milgrom festschrift
([Israel Law Review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/israel-law-review/article/abs/professor-reuven-yaron-selected-list-of-publications/6FE118AB80D2B69F0DB6DE3B63A71FB2)).
In 1995 the *Israel Law Review* published a full retrospective bibliography of
his work: five books (1960–1988) and 74 journal articles and book chapters
(1955–1995)
([Israel Law Review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/israel-law-review/article/abs/professor-reuven-yaron-selected-list-of-publications/6FE118AB80D2B69F0DB6DE3B63A71FB2)).
An encyclopedia assessment of his method said his work "testifies to the
necessity of a background in non-Jewish legal developments for an understanding
of Jewish ones"
([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/yaron-reuven)).

## Public roles beyond the academy

In 1973 Yaron was appointed director of the Jewish National and University
Library in Jerusalem; he resigned in 1978 to become head of the Israel
Broadcasting Authority
([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/yaron-reuven)).
The Hebrew University also records that he served as state archivist, alongside
the library directorship and the broadcasting chairmanship
([Hebrew University Faculty of Law](https://en.law.huji.ac.il/people/reuven-yaron)).
He wrote for the general press as well: *Ha'aretz* carried his "The Right to
Doubt" in 1983 and "A Just Decision," on the Ginossar case, in 1993
([Israel Law Review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/israel-law-review/article/abs/professor-reuven-yaron-selected-list-of-publications/6FE118AB80D2B69F0DB6DE3B63A71FB2)).
Yaron died in 2014
([Hebrew University Faculty of Law](https://en.law.huji.ac.il/people/reuven-yaron)).
