# Mira Nair

## Overview

Before Mira Nair ever made a feature film, she made a decision about what kind of filmmaker she would be. She had made three documentaries — on Muslim street life in Old Delhi, on the Indian diaspora, on two aging cabaret dancers — and then, in her own telling, she chose to move into features for one reason: creative control over production and story ([DGA](https://www.dga.org/Craft/VisualHistory/Interviews/Mira-Nair)). That instinct — to own the work rather than rent a chair on someone else's set — ran through everything after it: the profits of her first feature went not to a bigger career but to a trust for street children in India, and two decades later she was building a film school in Uganda ([Ford Foundation](https://www.fordfoundation.org/about/the-art-of-change-meet-our-fellows/mira-nair/)). Nair is the Indian-American director of *Monsoon Wedding*, *Salaam Bombay!*, *Mississippi Masala* and *The Namesake* ([Ford Foundation](https://www.fordfoundation.org/about/the-art-of-change-meet-our-fellows/mira-nair/)) — and, since November 2025, the person most search results reduce to a single relationship: the mother of New York City mayor [Zohran Mamdani](/people/zohran-mamdani/) ([USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/movies/2025/11/09/mira-nair-director-zohran-mamdani-mom/87182803007/)).

## From theatre to cinema verite

Nair was born and raised in Rourkela, India, and studied at Delhi and Harvard universities ([Ford Foundation](https://www.fordfoundation.org/about/the-art-of-change-meet-our-fellows/mira-nair/)). Born in 1957 in Odisha, she completed a degree in sociology at Delhi University before attending Harvard, where she shifted her focus to filmmaking ([Times of India](https://www.timesofindia.indiatimes.com/life-style/relationships/love-sex/untold-love-story-of-new-york-city-mayor-zohran-mamdani-parents-how-mira-nair-mahmood-mamdani-met/articleshow/125133463.cms)). What shaped her first was not film but theatre — Shakespearean and Jatra traveling troupes in her youth, and then acting with the Theatre Action Group while at Delhi University ([DGA](https://www.dga.org/Craft/VisualHistory/Interviews/Mira-Nair)). She kept acting at Harvard until she took a course at MIT with Richard Leacock, a pioneer of cinema verite, and switched her major to film ([DGA](https://www.dga.org/Craft/VisualHistory/Interviews/Mira-Nair)). Her graduation thesis was her first documentary, *Jama Masjid Street Journal* (1979) ([DGA](https://www.dga.org/Craft/VisualHistory/Interviews/Mira-Nair)).

She then worked as an assistant editor in New York City until she received funding for her second documentary, *So Far from India* (1983), followed by *India Cabaret* (1984), a portrait of two aging strippers ([DGA](https://www.dga.org/Craft/VisualHistory/Interviews/Mira-Nair)). Her early documentaries, as the *Times of India* later summarized, addressed social issues — her career began with what she called stories of the marginalized, people who "if you're not in the stream at all, you're virtually invisible" ([Times of India](https://www.timesofindia.indiatimes.com/life-style/relationships/love-sex/untold-love-story-of-new-york-city-mayor-zohran-mamdani-parents-how-mira-nair-mahmood-mamdani-met/articleshow/125133463.cms); [Ford Foundation](https://www.fordfoundation.org/about/the-art-of-change-meet-our-fellows/mira-nair/)).

## Salaam Bombay! and the trust it funded

Her narrative feature debut, *Salaam Bombay!* (1988), was shot with documentary techniques and street people rather than professional actors, telling the story of an 11-year-old boy living on the streets ([DGA](https://www.dga.org/Craft/VisualHistory/Interviews/Mira-Nair); [USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/movies/2025/11/09/mira-nair-director-zohran-mamdani-mom/87182803007/)). It won the Camera d'Or at Cannes and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film — with film critic Roger Ebert giving it a four-star rave, declaring it one of the year's best movies ([Ford Foundation](https://www.fordfoundation.org/about/the-art-of-change-meet-our-fellows/mira-nair/); [USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/movies/2025/11/09/mira-nair-director-zohran-mamdani-mom/87182803007/)).

What happened next is the part that separates Nair from most directors of her generation. In 1998 she used the profits from *Salaam Bombay!* to create the Salaam Baalak Trust, a nonprofit that works with street children in India ([Ford Foundation](https://www.fordfoundation.org/about/the-art-of-change-meet-our-fellows/mira-nair/)). The trust still supports children in Delhi and Mumbai — a direct line from the film that made her name to the children it was made about.

## Mississippi Masala and a marriage

Nair followed up with *Mississippi Masala* (1991), a romance starring Denzel Washington that explored the expulsion of the Indian minority from Uganda during the reign of Idi Amin ([USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/movies/2025/11/09/mira-nair-director-zohran-mamdani-mom/87182803007/)). While conducting research for the film in Kampala in 1989, she came across a book by the Ugandan scholar Mahmood Mamdani — *From Citizen to Refugee: Uganda Asians Come to Britain* — and interviewed him; Mamdani later told *The Telegraph India* that they "fell in love almost instantly — as though it was scripted" ([Times of India](https://www.timesofindia.indiatimes.com/life-style/relationships/love-sex/untold-love-story-of-new-york-city-mayor-zohran-mamdani-parents-how-mira-nair-mahmood-mamdani-met/articleshow/125133463.cms)). They married in 1991, around the time Nair discovered she was pregnant with their son Zohran, who was born that October ([Times of India](https://www.timesofindia.indiatimes.com/life-style/relationships/love-sex/untold-love-story-of-new-york-city-mayor-zohran-mamdani-parents-how-mira-nair-mahmood-mamdani-met/articleshow/125133463.cms); [USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/movies/2025/11/09/mira-nair-director-zohran-mamdani-mom/87182803007/)). [Mahmood Mamdani](/people/mahmood-mamdani/) is the Columbia University professor whose anthropological scholarship on colonialism has been the subject of its own FactsLedger profile.

## Monsoon Wedding and the later films

In 2001 Nair's film *Monsoon Wedding*, about a wedding ceremony in Delhi, was awarded the Golden Lion, the top prize at the Venice Film Festival ([USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/movies/2025/11/09/mira-nair-director-zohran-mamdani-mom/87182803007/); [DGA](https://www.dga.org/Craft/VisualHistory/Interviews/Mira-Nair)). Around the same career arc she directed Uma Thurman and Gena Rowlands in the HBO film *Hysterical Blindness*, Reese Witherspoon in the historical drama *Vanity Fair*, and Hilary Swank as Amelia Earhart in the biographical drama *Amelia* ([USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/movies/2025/11/09/mira-nair-director-zohran-mamdani-mom/87182803007/)). Her later features include *The Namesake* (2006), adapted from Jhumpa Lahiri's novel about Indian immigrant families in New York — a story of "losing a parent in a foreign country" she described as deeply personal ([DGA](https://www.dga.org/Craft/VisualHistory/Interviews/Mira-Nair); [USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/movies/2025/11/09/mira-nair-director-zohran-mamdani-mom/87182803007/)) — and *The Reluctant Fundamentalist* (2012), starring Riz Ahmed, which explored xenophobia in the wake of the September 11 attacks ([Ford Foundation](https://www.fordfoundation.org/about/the-art-of-change-meet-our-fellows/mira-nair/); [USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/movies/2025/11/09/mira-nair-director-zohran-mamdani-mom/87182803007/)).

In 2005 she established the Maisha Film Lab in Kampala, Uganda, a nonprofit training initiative for emerging East African filmmakers ([Ford Foundation](https://www.fordfoundation.org/about/the-art-of-change-meet-our-fellows/mira-nair/)). Her most recent feature was *Queen of Katwe* (2016), starring Lupita Nyong'o and David Oyelowo, based on the true story of the Ugandan chess prodigy Phiona Mutesi ([Ford Foundation](https://www.fordfoundation.org/about/the-art-of-change-meet-our-fellows/mira-nair/)). Zohran Mamdani had a small acting appearance in the film and served as its third assistant director and music supervisor, performing a rap song on the soundtrack ([USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/movies/2025/11/09/mira-nair-director-zohran-mamdani-mom/87182803007/)). In 2022 she was an executive producer on the Disney+ series *National Treasure: Edge of History* and directed its first episode ([USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/movies/2025/11/09/mira-nair-director-zohran-mamdani-mom/87182803007/)).

## Recognition and the credo

For her directing, Nair has been nominated for an Academy Award, a Golden Globe, and multiple BAFTA and Cesar awards, in addition to her Venice Golden Lion win ([DGA](https://www.dga.org/Craft/VisualHistory/Interviews/Mira-Nair)). In 2012 she received the Padma Bhushan from the president of India ([Ford Foundation](https://www.fordfoundation.org/about/the-art-of-change-meet-our-fellows/mira-nair/)).

One of the most revealing stories about her comes from a 2018 appearance at the Jaipur Literature Festival, where she said she had turned down an offer to direct a *Harry Potter* movie to make *The Namesake* instead — and that her then-14-year-old son had helped her decide, telling her, "Mama, many good directors can make 'Harry Potter,' but only you can make 'The Namesake'" ([USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/movies/2025/11/09/mira-nair-director-zohran-mamdani-mom/87182803007/)). She called it "such a liberating and clarifying statement," one that has informed "how I have lived my life" ([USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/movies/2025/11/09/mira-nair-director-zohran-mamdani-mom/87182803007/)).

Her public philosophy, given to the Ford Foundation, is the credo that anchors her career: "I've always been drawn to stories of those who are considered marginalized... I'm very much about telling stories of those who are my own. If we don't tell our own stories, no one else will" ([Ford Foundation](https://www.fordfoundation.org/about/the-art-of-change-meet-our-fellows/mira-nair/)). For his Mother's Day 2025 Instagram post, Zohran Mamdani quoted that same credo back to her, saying his mom "brought me into this world, helped me understand it, and taught me 'if we don't tell our own stories, no one else will'" ([USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/movies/2025/11/09/mira-nair-director-zohran-mamdani-mom/87182803007/)).
