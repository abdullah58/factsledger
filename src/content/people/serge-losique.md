## Overview

Serge Losique, the film professor who founded the Montreal World Film Festival and ran it from 1977 until its final edition in 2018, died on September 8, 2026, at the age of 94 at Granby hospital, after several weeks of hospitalization ([Cinoche.com](https://www.cinoche.com/actualites/serge-losique-fondateur-du-festival-des-films-du-monde-seteint-a-94-ans); [7 Jours](https://www.7jours.ca/2026/09/14/serge-losique-pere-de-anne-marie-losique-et-fondateur-du-festival-des-films-du-monde-est-decede-a-94-ans)). The death was announced on September 14 by the Rugicomm agency, and no interviews were granted ([7 Jours](https://www.7jours.ca/2026/09/14/serge-losique-pere-de-anne-marie-losique-et-fondateur-du-festival-des-films-du-monde-est-decede-a-94-ans)). He left three children — Jacques, François and Anne-Marie Losique — and three grandchildren, Patrick, Éric and Jinju ([Cinoche.com](https://www.cinoche.com/actualites/serge-losique-fondateur-du-festival-des-films-du-monde-seteint-a-94-ans)). In Quebec's film world he was, to the end, one of its most distinctive and most contested figures: a scholar who built a four-decade international festival, feuded for years with the government agencies that funded it, and kept it alive largely on personal determination after the money ran out ([Cinoche.com](https://www.cinoche.com/actualites/serge-losique-fondateur-du-festival-des-films-du-monde-seteint-a-94-ans); [Offscreen](https://offscreen.com/notes/view/serge-losique-1931-september-8-2026)).

## From Yugoslavia to Concordia

Losique was born in Yugoslavia in 1931 and arrived in Quebec in the 1960s ([Cinoche.com](https://www.cinoche.com/actualites/serge-losique-fondateur-du-festival-des-films-du-monde-seteint-a-94-ans)). He had studied literature in France and earned a doctorate in Paris, where he befriended Henri Langlois, the co-founder of the Cinémathèque française, an association that would shape his whole career ([Offscreen](https://offscreen.com/notes/view/serge-losique-1931-september-8-2026)). Settling in Montreal, he taught in the French department at Concordia University (then Sir George Williams), first French, then cinema ([Cinoche.com](https://www.cinoche.com/actualites/serge-losique-fondateur-du-festival-des-films-du-monde-seteint-a-94-ans); [Offscreen](https://offscreen.com/notes/view/serge-losique-1931-september-8-2026)).

In 1968, inspired by Langlois, he founded the Conservatory of Cinematographic Art, a university repertory cinema dedicated to introducing the great works of world film to a wide public ([Cinoche.com](https://www.cinoche.com/actualites/serge-losique-fondateur-du-festival-des-films-du-monde-seteint-a-94-ans); [Offscreen](https://offscreen.com/notes/view/serge-losique-1931-september-8-2026)). It closed its regular screenings in 1998 after accumulating an impressive archive of 16mm and 35mm prints ([Offscreen](https://offscreen.com/notes/view/serge-losique-1931-september-8-2026)). A year later, in 1969, he organized the first Canadian Student Film Festival, which was later absorbed into the World Film Festival's programming ([Offscreen](https://offscreen.com/notes/view/serge-losique-1931-september-8-2026)).

One of his less widely remembered achievements was persuading [Jean-Luc Godard](/people/jean-luc-godard/) to come to Montreal in 1978 to deliver a series of fourteen public lectures on film history at Concordia ([Offscreen](https://offscreen.com/notes/view/serge-losique-1931-september-8-2026)). The lectures were partly published in French in 1980 and went on to become a recognized inspiration for Godard's mammoth video essay *Histoire(s) du cinéma* ([Offscreen](https://offscreen.com/notes/view/serge-losique-1931-september-8-2026)).

## The festival, the funding war, and the rival that collapsed

In 1977 Losique launched the Festival des Films du Monde, the Montreal World Film Festival, driven by the same mission that animated the Conservatory: giving the general public a cinematographic education ([Cinoche.com](https://www.cinoche.com/actualites/serge-losique-fondateur-du-festival-des-films-du-monde-seteint-a-94-ans); [7 Jours](https://www.7jours.ca/2026/09/14/serge-losique-pere-de-anne-marie-losique-et-fondateur-du-festival-des-films-du-monde-est-decede-a-94-ans)). Through the 1980s and 1990s it was one of the international festival circuit's busiest stops, rolling out red carpets on Sainte-Catherine Street for filmmakers and stars from around the world ([Cinoche.com](https://www.cinoche.com/actualites/serge-losique-fondateur-du-festival-des-films-du-monde-seteint-a-94-ans)).

The break came in September 2004, when Telefilm Canada and its Quebec counterpart SODEC stripped the festival of its funding over concerns about its administration and a perceived lack of transparency in its finances ([ScreenDaily](https://www.screendaily.com/montreal-festivals-cauchard-lambasts-telefilms-clarkson/4034142.article)). The funders had commissioned a report on the country's major festivals from the private firm Secor; Losique and his team refused to cooperate with it, and the resulting report gave the festival poor marks ([Playback](https://playbackonline.ca/2005/12/05/mtl-20051205/)). The withdrawn support amounted to about $1 million a year ([Playback](https://playbackonline.ca/2005/12/05/mtl-20051205/)). Losique responded by suing Telefilm — and then by seeking, unsuccessfully, a court injunction — when the agencies awarded the money to L'Équipe Spectra, the organizer of Montreal's jazz festival, for a brand-new international film event ([ScreenDaily](https://www.screendaily.com/montreal-festivals-cauchard-lambasts-telefilms-clarkson/4034142.article); [Playback](https://playbackonline.ca/2005/12/05/mtl-20051205/)).

There was one more twist before the rival even opened: Losique held the legal domain to the name "Festival International de film de Montréal," forcing the newcomer to adopt the awkward English name "New Montreal FilmFest" ([Playback](https://playbackonline.ca/2005/12/05/mtl-20051205/)). The rival's inaugural edition, which opened on September 18, 2005, played to near-empty houses, and the event was terminated in early 2006 after its first year, with reported losses of $850,000 ([Playback](https://playbackonline.ca/2005/12/05/mtl-20051205/); [CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/news/entertainment/new-montreal-filmfest-folds-1.585164)). Losique called publicly for an inquiry into what he termed the rival's humiliating failure ([CBC News](https://www.cbc.ca/news/entertainment/new-montreal-filmfest-folds-1.585164)). By 2007, with the lawsuits dropped, SODEC agreed to resume financing — at $255,000, down from $425,000 in 2004 — and Telefilm resumed contributing at $94,000, down from $425,000 ([ScreenDaily](https://www.screendaily.com/montreal-festivals-cauchard-lambasts-telefilms-clarkson/4034142.article)).

## A correction on the timeline

This rewrite corrects a significant error in the earlier version of this profile, which dated Losique's Telefilm lawsuit and the rival festival's collapse to 2018–2019. The funding cut, the lawsuit, and the New Montreal FilmFest's failure all belong to 2004–2006, as the ScreenDaily and Playback reporting above shows. What actually happened in 2018–2019 was a different fight: in July 2018 Revenu Québec took Losique and the festival to court over roughly $500,000 in alleged back taxes, and Losique paid close to $33,000 toward the debt so that year's festival could proceed as scheduled ([CTV News](https://www.ctvnews.ca/montreal/article/serge-losique-pays-portion-of-back-taxes-so-montreal-world-film-festival-can-take-place/)). That 42nd edition, in August 2018, proved to be the festival's last: both obituaries record that Losique directed the FFM from its 1977 founding through its final edition in 2018 ([Cinoche.com](https://www.cinoche.com/actualites/serge-losique-fondateur-du-festival-des-films-du-monde-seteint-a-94-ans); [7 Jours](https://www.7jours.ca/2026/09/14/serge-losique-pere-de-anne-marie-losique-et-fondateur-du-festival-des-films-du-monde-est-decede-a-94-ans)).
