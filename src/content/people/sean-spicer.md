# Sean Spicer

## From the briefing room to the front row

The pop-culture memory of Sean Spicer is fixed in early 2017: the podium, the combative briefings, the Melissa McCarthy impersonation. What the leading profiles miss — and what the verified record of the last two years shows — is that Spicer became a documentary producer about the very people whose defections would make 2026 news: the [Front Row Joes](/organizations/front-row-joes/), the superfans who travel from rally to rally to sit directly behind Donald Trump. When two of those Joes broke with Trump in September 2026, Spicer was the person [Politico](/organizations/politico/)'s Playbook called for an assessment, and his answer carried an electoral warning. ([SRN News](https://srnnews.com/audio-news/sean-spicer-explores-trumps-front-row-joes-in-new-documentary/), [DNYUZ](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/28/trumps-most-obsessed-groupies-turn-after-dagger-in-heart-betrayal-a-fraud-and-liar/))

Spicer produced *Trump's Front Row Joes* to explore why some supporters have attended dozens of Trump rallies, a documentary he discussed on *The Seth Leibsohn Show* in July 2024. The film streamed via SalemNOW. ([SRN News](https://srnnews.com/audio-news/sean-spicer-explores-trumps-front-row-joes-in-new-documentary/), [The Seth Leibsohn Show](https://omny.fm/shows/sethleibsohnshow/july-16-2024-sean-spicer))

Two years later, when Playbook reported that longtime Joes [Rick Frazier](/people/rick-frazier/) — who says he attended more than 100 Trump rallies — and [Trisha Hope](/people/trisha-hope/) were publicly breaking with Trump over the Epstein files and the Iran war, Spicer told Playbook that defections from loyalists like Frazier make him "more concerned heading into November about the softer voters who helped put Trump over the top without ever fully identifying with MAGA." ([DNYUZ](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/28/trumps-most-obsessed-groupies-turn-after-dagger-in-heart-betrayal-a-fraud-and-liar/))

His second line from that interview became the story's most-quoted sentence: "Can another candidate even really pull off a rally? It's like asking where Deadheads go after the Grateful Dead broke up." ([DNYUZ](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/28/trumps-most-obsessed-groupies-turn-after-dagger-in-heart-betrayal-a-fraud-and-liar/))

## Press secretary, January–July 2017

Spicer came to [the White House](/organizations/the-white-house/) from the Republican National Committee, where he had spent several years leading communications before helping Trump's campaign in the general election. He was close to [White House](/places/white-house/) chief of staff Reince Priebus, the former RNC chair, and to several lower-ranking aides in the White House communications operation. ([Associated Press](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1132821/media))

His tenure got off to a rocky start. On Trump's first full day in office, Spicer lambasted journalists over coverage of the crowd size at the inauguration and stormed out of the briefing room without answering questions. His daily press briefings became must-see television for a time, until he took a more behind-the-scenes role and deputy press secretary Sarah Huckabee Sanders largely took over the briefings, turning them into an off-camera event. ([Associated Press](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1132821/media))

Spicer, often fiery in tense on-camera exchanges, became a cultural figure in a way few press secretaries have — chiefly through McCarthy's indelible *Saturday Night Live* impersonation, which portrayed him as a hostile figure tearing through the briefing room on a portable podium. The AP noted that he "remained loyal to Trump but he frequently battled perceptions that he was not plugged in to what the president was thinking," with Trump watching and critiquing his performance from the Oval Office. ([Associated Press](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1132821/media))

The end came on July 21, 2017, when Spicer resigned as press secretary, his decision linked to the appointment of New York financier Anthony Scaramucci as the new White House communications director. Two people with knowledge of the decision told the AP that Spicer objected to Trump's vision for the press operation. TheWrap describes him as Trump's first press secretary, six months on the job, succeeded by Sarah Sanders. (AFP put the tenure at seven months; the profile follows the AP's "first six months" framing.) ([Associated Press](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1132821/media), [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/former-white-house-press-secretary-sean-spicer-launching-show-with-newsmax-tv/), [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/content/1164781.shtml))

## The post-White House media arc

Spicer's pivot to media took a detour through the ballroom. In 2019 he joined the season 28 cast of ABC's *Dancing with the Stars*, a casting choice that drew controversy when announced in August. For his debut he wore a flamboyant neon-green shirt and white pants, opened with a bongo drum solo, and salsa-danced to the Spice Girls' "Spice Up Your Life" with professional partner Lindsay Arnold — sliding across the floor on his knees, hands stretched above his head, for the finale. His outfit and moves drew mockery online; one widely quoted post said the neon-green ruffle shirt was "not the most humiliating thing Sean Spicer has ever done in public." ([Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/content/1164781.shtml)) He was eliminated sixth despite consistently being the lowest scorer, with *DWTS* host Tom Bergeron criticizing the show for his inclusion. ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/former-white-house-press-secretary-sean-spicer-launching-show-with-newsmax-tv/))

In early 2020 he launched *Spicer & Co.* on Newsmax TV, debuting March 3 at 6 p.m. ET and airing weeknights with a focus on Washington news. "We are going to offer viewers a unique perspective and insights into what's really happening, who is up, who's down and most importantly how it's going to affect their lives," Spicer said in a statement, adding that he had "a front row seat to how Washington works at the highest levels — in the White House, Congress, the Pentagon and K Street." Newsmax CEO Christopher Ruddy called him "a success as White House press secretary because he connected to ordinary Americans." ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/former-white-house-press-secretary-sean-spicer-launching-show-with-newsmax-tv/))
