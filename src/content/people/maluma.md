## Overview

Maluma — born Juan Luis Londoño Arias on 28 January 1994 in Medellín, Colombia — is a Colombian singer, songwriter, rapper and actor who became one of Latin music's biggest global stars, carrying Spanish-language reggaeton and pop urbano to the top of the charts in the [United States](/places/united-states/) and worldwide ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). His stage name is built from his family: the first syllables of his mother Marlli, his father Luis and his sister Manuela ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)).

He began recording at 16, signed to Sony Music on the strength of an early single, and broke through in the United States with his second album, *Pretty Boy, Dirty Boy*, in 2015 ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). His third album, *F.A.M.E.*, won him a Latin Grammy in 2018, and his 2020 single "Hawái" became the first No. 1 on Billboard's new Global Excl. U.S. chart — a milestone that ties his name directly to the history of Billboard's global charts ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/latin-grammy-winners-karol-g-rosalia-maluma-more-share-excitement-social-media/); [Music Ally](https://musically.com/2020/09/15/billboard-and-mrc-launch-two-new-global-music-charts/)). He has since added a film career, a FIFA World Cup anthem and a sixth studio album, *Don Juan*, to a catalogue that runs from 2012 to 2026 ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)).

## From Medellín football to a Sony deal

Maluma grew up in Medellín, where he pursued football for eight years and sang for pleasure at birthday parties, weddings and celebrations before YouTube turned his focus exclusively to music ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). His stage name predates his career: it was already a tattoo on his left leg, assembled from the first syllables of Marlli, Luis and Manuela, before he adopted it as a performer ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). In 2011, at 16, he cut his first independent single, "Farandulera"; its internet popularity and airplay on Colombian radio drew the attention of Sony Music, which signed him ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). His debut album, *Magia*, followed in 2012, with singles "Obsesión", "Loco" and "Miss Independent" earning consistent airplay and charting across Colombia and Latin America ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). The next single, "La Temperatura", entered the North American charts and earned him a Latin Grammy nomination in 2013, and in 2014 he was the featured vocalist on Elvis Crespo's "Ole Brazil", recorded for the World Cup ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)).

## Breakthrough and the "Cuatro Babys" backlash

Early 2015 brought the compilation *PB DB. The Mixtape*, with new singles "Me Gustas Tanto" and "Climax" — the new tracks charted and set the stage for a brand-new album — and then the breakthrough album *Pretty Boy, Dirty Boy*, produced by Walter Kolm, which landed on the upper rungs of the Latin Pop, Urban and Tropical charts in the United States ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). In 2016 he released "Chantaje" with fellow Colombian star Shakira, whose video passed one billion views online within five months ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). The same year brought his first public controversy: "Cuatro Babys" drew a backlash from listeners who called out the song's objectification of women, a petition circulated to have it removed from digital platforms, and Maluma apologized — the song stayed up ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)).

## F.A.M.E. and the first Latin Grammy

In 2017 Maluma was named Artist of the Year at the Premios Lo Nuestro, Univision's Latin music awards show, and began writing songs in English as well as Spanish for his next album ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). The November 2017 single "Corazón", featuring Brazilian singer Nego do Borel, peaked at No. 5 on Hot Latin Songs ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). *F.A.M.E.*, his third studio album, arrived in May 2018, produced mainly by his Medellín camp — Édgar "Edge" Barrera and the Rude Boyz, Kevin ADG and Chan el Genio — with duets by Timbaland, Prince Royce and Jason Derulo ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). The album reached the top spot on the Latin Albums chart and No. 37 on the Billboard 200 ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). On 15 November 2018 it won him his first Latin Grammy, Best Contemporary Pop Vocal Album, at the 19th Latin Grammy Awards in Las Vegas ([Grammy.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/latin-grammy-winners-karol-g-rosalia-maluma-more-share-excitement-social-media/)).

## "Hawái" and the first Billboard global charts

The most durable fact of Maluma's career is a chart first. In September 2020, Billboard and its data partner MRC launched the Billboard Global 200 and the Billboard Global Excl. U.S. — the first authoritative rankings of the top songs worldwide, built from data across more than 200 territories ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/2020/09/15/billboard-and-mrc-launch-two-new-global-music-charts/)). Maluma's "Hawái" was No. 1 on the first Global Excl. U.S. chart, the ranking that covers every territory outside the United States ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/2020/09/15/billboard-and-mrc-launch-two-new-global-music-charts/)). The song, from his fifth album *Papi Juancho*, then topped the Hot Latin Songs chart for nine consecutive weeks, and — boosted by a remix featuring The Weeknd, the Canadian star's first-ever Latin collaboration — peaked at No. 12 on the Hot 100, Maluma's highest ranking there ([Billboard](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2020/12/18/hawai-vs-blinding-lights-breaking-down-the-weeknds-latin-collaborations-with-maluma-rosalia/)). That run matters because it shows a Spanish-language reggaeton single climbing the American mainstream chart on its own terms, with the remix amplifying rather than replacing the original ([Billboard](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2020/12/18/hawai-vs-blinding-lights-breaking-down-the-weeknds-latin-collaborations-with-maluma-rosalia/)).

## Awards, the Madonna moment and 11:11

The 2019 album *11:11* kept the momentum going: released that May with singles "HP" and "11 P.M." and collaborators including Ozuna, Madonna, Daddy Yankee and Ricky Martin, it entered the Top Latin Albums chart at No. 1 in June while *F.A.M.E.* was still in the Top 20 ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). That year Maluma also collaborated with Madonna on "Medellín" from her album *Madame X*, and the pair performed the song at the [Billboard Music Awards](/events/billboard-music-awards/) in a high-tech, hologram-driven staging ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). *11:11* was nominated for Best Latin Pop Album at the 62nd Grammy Awards, announced in November 2019 ([The Current](https://www.thecurrent.org/feature/2019/11/19/full-list-of-2020-grammy-nominees)). At the 2020 MTV Video Music Awards, staged at outdoor locations around [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) because of the pandemic, Maluma and J Balvin won Best Latin for "Qué Pena" ([Entertainment Tonight](https://www.etonline.com/2020-mtv-vmas-the-complete-winners-list-152093)).

## Acting, the World Cup and Don Juan

*Papi Juancho* itself, released in August 2020 with appearances by Yandel, Yomo, Lenny Tavárez and Justin Quiles, peaked at No. 2 on Billboard's Top Latin Albums chart and No. 34 on the Billboard 200 ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). In 2021 he released the #7DJ (7 Días en Jamaica) EP and the chart-topping single "Sobrio" ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). In 2022 he took a starring role alongside Jennifer Lopez in the romantic comedy *Marry Me* and sang on its soundtrack, and recorded "Tukoh Taka" with [Nicki Minaj](/people/nicki-minaj/) and Myriam Fares for the 2022 FIFA World Cup ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). His sixth album, *Don Juan*, arrived in August 2023 as a 25-track set named after an alter ego, with collaborations spanning Carin Leon, Jowell & Randy, Ryan Castro, Don Omar, J Balvin and Yandel ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)). In March 2026 he released "BOTERO" with Arcángel and NTG, heralding a seventh studio album, *Loco X Volver* ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/maluma-mn0002966736)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is Maluma?**
Maluma is the stage name of Juan Luis Londoño Arias, a Colombian singer, songwriter, rapper and actor born on 28 January 1994 in Medellín, Colombia. He records reggaeton, Latin pop and urbano, signed to Sony Music, and broke through in the United States with *Pretty Boy, Dirty Boy* in 2015.

**What is Maluma's biggest song?**
"Hawái", from his 2020 album *Papi Juancho*, is his defining chart hit: it was the first No. 1 on Billboard's Global Excl. U.S. chart, topped Hot Latin Songs for nine consecutive weeks, and peaked at No. 12 on the Hot 100 with a remix featuring The Weeknd.

**Has Maluma won a Latin Grammy?**
Yes. He won his first Latin Grammy on 15 November 2018, when *F.A.M.E.* took Best Contemporary Pop Vocal Album at the 19th Latin Grammy Awards in Las Vegas. His album *11:11* was later nominated for Best Latin Pop Album at the 62nd Grammy Awards.

**What films has Maluma appeared in?**
He starred alongside Jennifer Lopez in the 2022 romantic comedy *Marry Me*, playing Bastian, her pop-star fiancé, and sang on the film's soundtrack. He also recorded "Tukoh Taka" with Nicki Minaj and Myriam Fares for the 2022 FIFA World Cup.

**How did Maluma get his stage name?**
He built it from his family: the first syllables of his mother Marlli, his father Luis and his sister Manuela. The name was a tattoo on his left leg before he began singing professionally and adopted it as his stage name.
