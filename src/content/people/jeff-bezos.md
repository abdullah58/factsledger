## Overview

Jeff Bezos told his high-school class of 1982 that he wanted to colonize space to save the Earth [BestColleges](https://www.bestcolleges.com/news/2021-2/10/29/did-jeff-bezos-go-to-college/). Everything after that — the hedge fund, the bookstore in a garage, the everything store — was, in a sense, the fundraising. By September 2026, the dream had its biggest receipt: [Blue Origin](/organizations/blue-origin/) raised $10 billion from outside investors, its first outside round, valuing the company at about $140 billion, with Bezos adding another $2 billion of his own to bring his cumulative personal commitment to roughly $30 billion [Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2026/09/25/blue-origin-raises-10bn/). Most profiles treat Blue Origin as a billionaire's side project; the documented record says the space company was the point all along, and [Amazon](/organizations/amazon/) was how an 18-year-old paid for it.

## The dream came first

The space fixation is the oldest documented fact about Bezos's ambitions. As a teenager, he worked the breakfast shift at McDonald's as a short-order cook, became a National Merit Scholar, and attended the Student Science Training Program at the University of Florida [BestColleges](https://www.bestcolleges.com/news/2021-2/10/29/did-jeff-bezos-go-to-college/). In his valedictorian speech at Miami Palmetto Senior High, he talked about colonizing space as a way to save the Earth [BestColleges](https://www.bestcolleges.com/news/2021-2/10/29/did-jeff-bezos-go-to-college/).

At Princeton, he enrolled as a physics major, inspired by Stephen Hawking, and ran the university's chapter of Students for the Exploration and Development of Space [BestColleges](https://www.bestcolleges.com/news/2021-2/10/29/did-jeff-bezos-go-to-college/). He attended seminars by Princeton physicist Gerard K. O'Neill, whose 1976 book "The High Frontier" laid out plans for moving industry into space [BestColleges](https://www.bestcolleges.com/news/2021-2/10/29/did-jeff-bezos-go-to-college/). He switched to a double major in electrical engineering and computer science only after concluding he would never be at the top of the field in physics — he wanted to go where he could "make a contribution" — and graduated in 1986 summa cum laude, elected to the Tau Beta Pi engineering honor society [BestColleges](https://www.bestcolleges.com/news/2021-2/10/29/did-jeff-bezos-go-to-college/).

He was born Jeffrey Preston Jorgensen on January 12, 1964, in Albuquerque, New Mexico, to a teenage mother; after his parents divorced, his mother married Cuban immigrant Miguel Bezos in 1968, who adopted four-year-old Jeff and gave him his surname [HistoryTools](https://www.historytools.org/people/jeff-bezos-complete-biography).

## Amazon as the funding engine

After Princeton, Bezos worked at a fintech startup and Bankers Trust before joining the hedge fund D.E. Shaw, where he became its youngest senior vice president [BestColleges](https://www.bestcolleges.com/news/2021-2/10/29/did-jeff-bezos-go-to-college/); [HistoryTools](https://www.historytools.org/people/jeff-bezos-complete-biography). In 1994, after noticing that web usage was growing 2,300% a year, he quit, took $300,000 in seed money from his parents, made a cross-country road trip to Seattle, and started Amazon.com out of his garage [BestColleges](https://www.bestcolleges.com/news/2021-2/10/29/did-jeff-bezos-go-to-college/); [HistoryTools](https://www.historytools.org/people/jeff-bezos-complete-biography). *Inc.*'s contemporary account adds texture: Bezos invited 300 friends and acquaintances to test the site, and within a month of going live the company had sold books in all 50 states and 45 countries; within two months sales topped $20,000 a week [Inc.](http://www.inc.com/30years/articles/jeff-bezos.html). He told *Inc.* in 1997: "We're going to be unprofitable for a long time. And that's our strategy" — and Amazon earned its first full-year profit in 2003 [Inc.](http://www.inc.com/30years/articles/jeff-bezos.html).

The company went public in 1997, raising $54 million [HistoryTools](https://www.historytools.org/people/jeff-bezos-complete-biography), and survived the dot-com crash that killed its rivals [Inc.](http://www.inc.com/30years/articles/jeff-bezos.html). Bezos never returned to academia — he received an honorary doctorate from Carnegie Mellon in 2008 and was later elected to the National Academy of Engineering [BestColleges](https://www.bestcolleges.com/news/2021-2/10/29/did-jeff-bezos-go-to-college/). In July 2021 he stepped down as Amazon's CEO to focus on his other passions, including space [BestColleges](https://www.bestcolleges.com/news/2021-2/10/29/did-jeff-bezos-go-to-college/).

Meanwhile the funding model for the original dream was formalized: in 2017 Bezos said his business model was to "sell about $1 billion a year of Amazon stock" and invest it in Blue Origin, which he had founded in 2000 [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/jeff-bezos-blue-origin-outside-investment/). The company now employs some 15,000 people and holds a $3.4 billion NASA award to build a crew-capable lunar landing system [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/jeff-bezos-blue-origin-outside-investment/). In July 2021, at 57, Bezos flew aboard New Shepard in the company's first human spaceflight [HistoryTools](https://www.historytools.org/people/jeff-bezos-complete-biography). The road has been rough: New Glenn first flew in January 2025, its April 2026 mission failed to put payloads in proper orbit — grounding the rocket under FAA oversight — and a May 2026 hot-fire test explosion damaged the Cape Canaveral pad [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/jeff-bezos-blue-origin-outside-investment/); [Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2026/09/25/blue-origin-raises-10bn/). Internal projections reviewed by the *Wall Street Journal* put revenue at roughly $800 million in 2025 and $1.4 billion in 2026, climbing to more than $30 billion by 2030 [Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2026/09/25/blue-origin-raises-10bn/).

## The Post, philanthropy, and the handoff

In August 2013, Bezos reached a deal to acquire the Washington Post Co.'s newspaper publishing business for $250 million — a personal acquisition, explicitly not Amazon's — telling staff "the Post's values will not change" [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2013/breaking-jeff-bezos-buying-washington-post-250m/). His philanthropy came late and large: in January 2018 he gave $33 million to TheDream.US, a scholarship fund for undocumented immigrants brought to the U.S. as children, and that September unveiled the $2 billion Day One Fund focused on homelessness and Montessori-inspired preschools in low-income communities [BestColleges](https://www.bestcolleges.com/news/2021-2/10/29/did-jeff-bezos-go-to-college/). By 2022, the empire he built from the garage had made him one of the world's richest people [BestColleges](https://www.bestcolleges.com/news/2021-2/10/29/did-jeff-bezos-go-to-college/). He married novelist MacKenzie Tuttle — whom he met at D.E. Shaw — in 1993; they had four children and divorced in 2019 [HistoryTools](https://www.historytools.org/people/jeff-bezos-complete-biography).
