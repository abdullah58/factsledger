## Overview

Mike Butcher MBE is a British technology journalist who was [TechCrunch](/organizations/techcrunch/)'s editor-at-large for European startups, joining TechCrunch UK & Ireland as editor in October 2006 and remaining its most prominent European voice for nearly two decades ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2006/10/25/mike-butcher-joins-techcrunch-uk/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/author/mike-butcher/page/136/)). TechCrunch's own author page now describes him as "formerly Editor-at-large," and names him one of the most influential people in European technology according to Wired UK ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/author/mike-butcher/page/136/)). Beyond journalism he built institutions: the co-working network TechHub, the Europas startup awards, and the non-profits Techfugees, TechVets and Startup Coalition — and in 2016 he was appointed MBE for services to the UK technology industry and journalism ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/author/mike-butcher/page/136/); [Techfugees](https://techfugees.com/our-team/)). In the [Booksy](/organizations/booksy/) story he is not a bystander: he wrote the TechCrunch report of the $70 million Series C in January 2021, the article on which much of the Booksy record rests ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)).

Butcher's beat was European startups and venture capital, covered from [London](/places/london/) for a largely American publication — a position that made him the English-speaking world's main chronicler of the continent's startup scene. He interviewed Tony Blair, Dmitry Medvedev, Kevin Spacey, Lily Cole, Pavel Durov and Jimmy Wales, among other tech leaders and celebrities, and broadcast regularly on BBC News, Sky News, CNBC, Channel 4, Al Jazeera and Bloomberg ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/author/mike-butcher/page/136/)). He also advised UK Prime Ministers and the Mayor of London on tech startup policy, served as a judge on The Apprentice UK, and was named by GQ magazine as one of the 100 Most Connected Men in the UK ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/author/mike-butcher/page/136/)).

## From trade press to TechCrunch

Butcher's career began in the trade press of the early web. In 1994 he started writing about the business of websites for Newspaper Focus magazine; in 1996 he joined New Media Age, then a newsletter, and in 1998 took over as editor, relaunching it from a 14-page title into a full-blown magazine ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2006/10/25/mike-butcher-joins-techcrunch-uk/)). In 2000 he helped launch the US-owned Industry Standard magazine in London, and in the media slump of 2001 he went freelance, writing for the Financial Times, The Times, The Irish Times, The Guardian, Broadcast, Marketing Week, New Media Age, Mobile Content News, Paid Content, Media Week and The New Statesman, and launching or relaunching websites for Media Week, UK Press Gazette, New Media Age and Netimperative ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2006/10/25/mike-butcher-joins-techcrunch-uk/)). He also wrote the E-Business Briefing newsletter for E-Consultancy for over two years, and in October 2004 was voted "One of the 100 Innovators of the UK Internet Decade" by e-consultancy.com and GfK NOP ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2006/10/25/mike-butcher-joins-techcrunch-uk/)).

That background — a working journalist who had built publications, not just written for them — was the point of his hiring. When TechCrunch announced on October 25, 2006 that Butcher was joining TechCrunch UK & Ireland as its new editor, founder Michael Arrington wrote that he was "an empassioned technologist and entrepreneur but I am not and never have been a professional journalist," and that Butcher's arrival would let the site better serve its growing UK community ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2006/10/25/mike-butcher-joins-techcrunch-uk/)). Butcher went on to become European editor and founding Editor-at-Large, the role under which he covered the continent's startups for the next eighteen years — a run that included the rise of the London "Silicon Roundabout" cluster, which he helped publicise, and the funding cycles that carried companies like Booksy from seed rounds to nine-figure raises.

## The institution builder

Butcher's second career ran alongside the journalism: building the infrastructure European founders lacked. He co-founded TechHub, a community and workspace for tech entrepreneurs that merged a thriving community with office space for fast-moving startups, and founded The Europas, the annual awards that became a Top 100 listing of European startups ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/author/mike-butcher/page/136/); [Techfugees](https://techfugees.com/our-team/)). He also co-founded Startup Coalition, the non-profit lobby for the UK digital economy, and TechVets, which helps ex-soldiers into the cyber-security industry ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/author/mike-butcher/page/136/)).

The most consequential of these was Techfugees, founded in 2015 as the European refugee crisis peaked. Frustrated by the lack of technological solutions for people on the move — most of whom had mobile phones — Butcher, then editor of TechCrunch, kickstarted the idea by organising a one-day conference for people to showcase ideas to help refugees, followed by a hackathon at which people wrote software collaboratively ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/britains-tech-community-links-up-with-charities-to-help-migrants-with-mobiles)). "I called up about five friends and then, like the A-team, off we went," he said, hoping the project would be replicated around the world ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/britains-tech-community-links-up-with-charities-to-help-migrants-with-mobiles)). In an open-plan London office, the team worked on laptops surrounded by pizza boxes, "a world away from the refugee camps and fences on Europe's borders in headlines that summer" ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/britains-tech-community-links-up-with-charities-to-help-migrants-with-mobiles)). The hackathon produced My Refuge, a website and app described by its founder as "AirBnB for refugees, with no money involved," an SMS network for refugees to message several people at once where smartphones and signal were patchy, and Migreat, a site showing what visas or permits are needed to move country ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/britains-tech-community-links-up-with-charities-to-help-migrants-with-mobiles)). Techfugees went on to become a foundation — Butcher remains its Founder & Chairman — and its team page records him as one of the most influential journalists in European technology according to Wired UK, the Daily Telegraph and the Evening Standard ([Techfugees](https://techfugees.com/our-team/)).

## The Booksy connection

Butcher's Booksy coverage is the reason he appears in this entity loop, and it shows his editorial judgement at work. His January 27, 2021 report of the $70 million Series C — led by [Cat Rock Capital](/organizations/cat-rock-capital/), taking total raised to $119 million — is the primary record of the round, the six markets, the 13 million consumers, the Lavito acquisition and the Versum merger that carried Booksy into [Mexico](/places/mexico/) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)). His own analysis in the piece was that Booksy had "cracked the international expansion game ahead of most competitors," which tended to stay local to their countries of origin, naming [Treatwell](/organizations/treatwell/), [Styleseat](/organizations/styleseat/), [Vagaro](/organizations/vagaro/) and [Mindbody](/organizations/mindbody/) as the locally-rooted rivals — and that the opportunity was now to use the war chest "to roll-up other local players" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)). That framing — international expansion as the differentiator, acquisitions as the next phase — became the standard account of Booksy's strategy, and it was Butcher's.
