## Overview

Nir Klar is the Israeli engineer who co-founded [BlueVine](/organizations/bluevine/) with [Eyal Lifshitz](/people/eyal-lifshitz/) in 2013 and has served as the company's chief technology officer ever since, leading its research and development centre from Tel Aviv ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article.aspx?did=1001003147)). While Lifshitz became the public face of the small-business fintech — its chief executive, fundraiser, and spokesman — Klar built the platform itself: the online invoice-factoring system that became a digital banking service for some 175,000 American small businesses, and the company [Valley National Bancorp](/organizations/valley-national-bancorp/) agreed to buy for approximately $340 million in September 2026 ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)). Almost everything independently documented about Klar runs through that one company; no source read for this profile records his education, his employers before BlueVine, or his personal background.

## Co-founding BlueVine and building the platform

BlueVine began in 2013 as an online-only business financing company, founded by Lifshitz and Klar to modernise invoice factoring — paying small businesses upfront for their unpaid invoices instead of making them wait for customers to pay ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article.aspx?did=1001003147)). Klar took the CTO role from the start: Globes described the pair in January 2015 as "CEO Eyal Lifshitz and CTO Nir Klar," and every subsequent report keeps him in that job ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article.aspx?did=1001003147)). The company was headquartered in Palo Alto, California, with offices in Tel Aviv, where Klar was based ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article.aspx?did=1001003147)); one later Globes report instead describes the company as founded in Tel Aviv, and the two framings are flagged rather than reconciled here, since the consistent core across sources is a 2013 founding by the two men with an early Tel Aviv presence ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article.aspx?did=1001496489)).

The platform Klar led grew through the funding rounds that marked BlueVine's first decade. In January 2015 the company raised $18.5 million led by 83North and Lightspeed Ventures, months after the beta launch of its cloud-based invoice-financing service in March 2014 ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article.aspx?did=1001003147)). A year later it closed a $40 million Series C led by Menlo Ventures, bringing total funding to $64 million, after funded invoices grew twelvefold in 2015 ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-israeli-fintech-startup-bluevine-raises-40m-1001096851)). The model was factoring made fast: BlueVine typically bought 85 to 90 percent of an invoice's value on day one and paid the rest, minus a fee, once the customer paid, using machine-learning models to price risk ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/01/20/bluevine-raises-40-million-to-help-small-businesses-with-cash-flow/)). By 2020 the company had added term loans, lines of credit, and an advanced business checking account to the original factoring product ([AWS Startups Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/startups/bluevine-collaborates-with-aws-to-provide-small-business-relief-loans/)), and a $102.5 million Series F in late 2019 had valued it at roughly $700–800 million ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)).

## The public record: what Klar has said

Klar has rarely spoken on the record, and the fullest account of his own views is a single Calcalistech report from a Tel Aviv fintech conference in June 2019, where he appeared as BlueVine's co-founder and CTO ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctech/articles/0,7340,L-3764995,00.html)). His argument that day was that American banks, especially large ones, do not like lending to small businesses: with more than 50 million small businesses in the United States, loan requests take a long time to process, are rarely approved, and when granted come in low sums ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctech/articles/0,7340,L-3764995,00.html)). Against that, he put BlueVine's numbers — "We loaned $2 billion," he said, "and our great achievement is that most of it has already been repaid" — and its speed, with some loans approved within 20 minutes ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctech/articles/0,7340,L-3764995,00.html)). At the time the company employed 300 people, 100 of them in Israel, and had raised $140 million in equity alongside $400 million in credit lines, figures Klar gave himself ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctech/articles/0,7340,L-3764995,00.html)). He also noted that as artificial intelligence spread through finance, regulatory scrutiny was rising to keep it fair and legal, and that capital in fintech was plentiful — "even huge corporations like Amazon are entering the domain" ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctech/articles/0,7340,L-3764995,00.html)).

The clearest window into Klar's technical work came a year later, in an AWS Startups Blog post he co-authored about BlueVine's pandemic relief product ([AWS Startups Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/startups/bluevine-collaborates-with-aws-to-provide-small-business-relief-loans/)). BlueVine had run on Amazon Web Services since 2013, and when the US government's [Paycheck Protection Program](/events/paycheck-protection-program/) opened in 2020, Klar's team built a product in a matter of days that used Amazon Textract to automatically process and analyse tens of thousands of PPP application pages per day ([AWS Startups Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/startups/bluevine-collaborates-with-aws-to-provide-small-business-relief-loans/)). The post says the effort helped thousands of businesses obtain funds and saved more than 400,000 jobs — its own claim, not independently verified — and its author note describes Klar as "Founder and CTO of BlueVine," based in Tel Aviv and "leading the R&D center in building the next generation of financial and banking platform" ([AWS Startups Blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/startups/bluevine-collaborates-with-aws-to-provide-small-business-relief-loans/)).

## Scale, retrenchment, and the Valley National acquisition

By December 2024 BlueVine employed about 500 people worldwide, with 150 in its Israel development centre, when it cut 100 jobs — 30 of them in Israel — in a round its chief executive described as a drive for efficiency and a path to profitability ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article.aspx?did=1001496489)). Klar is not quoted in that report; it matters here only as the scale of the organisation whose technology he still led. Two years later the company he co-founded was sold: on September 28, 2026, Valley National Bancorp announced it would acquire Bluevine for approximately $340 million, 75 percent in cash and 25 percent in stock, gaining a digital platform serving about 175,000 small businesses, roughly $2.1 billion in deposits, and some 180 engineers and technology professionals, including the Tel Aviv team ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)). The announcement names only one founder's future: Lifshitz will join Valley as Head of Small Business Banking when the deal closes ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)). What happens to Klar — whether he stays with the combined bank, as one niche blog claims without the sourcing this profile requires, or moves on — is not stated in any source read here, and this profile does not guess.
