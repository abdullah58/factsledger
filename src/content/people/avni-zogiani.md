## Overview

Avni Zogiani is a Kosovo anti-corruption activist who founded the civil-society movement Çohu — the Organisation for Democracy, Anti-Corruption and Dignity — and later carried that fight into government as a deputy minister ([Front Line Defenders](https://www.frontlinedefenders.org/en/profile/avni-zogiani)). The London School of Economics, which invited him to speak at a 2012 debate on Kosovo's economic development, identified him as Çohu's founder and called the organization "a leading Kosovo NGO working in the field of economic development" ([London School of Economics](http://www.lse.ac.uk:443/LSEE-Research-on-South-Eastern-Europe/Assets/Documents/Publications/Annual-Newsletter/Newsletter-2012.pdf)). That founder detail is the kind of verified fact most English-language references to Zogiani skip: it anchors a career running from war-crimes reporting and investigative journalism, through a decade and a half confronting organized crime and political corruption, to senior executive posts — and, in 2025–2026, a proposed education ministry portfolio and a defamation judgment against him.

## From journalism to Çohu: founding an anti-corruption movement

Before Çohu, Zogiani worked as a journalist with the daily Koha Ditore and belonged to the Institute for War and Peace Reporting's war-crimes reporting network ([Institute for War and Peace Reporting](https://iwpr.net/global-voices/regional-report-kosovo-kla-veteran-under-investigation)). By 2006 he was working as an independent analyst: Freedom House's Nations in Transit report on Kosovo names him, alongside Jeta Xharra, as one of two independent analysts who assisted Bashkim Rrahmani in preparing its Kosovo chapter ([Freedom House](https://www.refworld.org/reference/annualreport/freehou/2006/50834)).

Çohu itself was established in September 2005 by young scholars and journalists, with Zogiani as its driving figure ([Front Line Defenders](https://www.frontlinedefenders.org/en/profile/avni-zogiani)). Front Line Defenders describes it as "a leading organization in Kosovo in the fight against organized crime and political corruption," and notes that its Kosovo Centre for Investigative Journalism branch offered training and research support to journalists ([Front Line Defenders](https://www.frontlinedefenders.org/en/profile/avni-zogiani)). The movement's standing was recognized internationally: at an OSCE regional conference on investigative journalism in Prishtinë/Priština on 22 October 2011, Zogiani spoke as "Director of Çohu, a local NGO" ([OSCE](https://omik.osce.org/kosovo/84339)), and the LSE's 2012 newsletter records his appearance as the second speaker — after Iraj Hashi, Professor of Economics at Staffordshire University — at its 13 March 2012 panel debate "Challenges of Economic Development in Kosovo: Between Corruption and Institution Building" ([London School of Economics](http://www.lse.ac.uk:443/LSEE-Research-on-South-Eastern-Europe/Assets/Documents/Publications/Annual-Newsletter/Newsletter-2012.pdf)).

## Confrontation and threats: the "Siamese twins" interview

Zogiani's anti-corruption work put him in direct conflict with powerful interests. Front Line Defenders has documented threats against him and his family connected to his reporting on corruption and organized crime ([Front Line Defenders](https://www.frontlinedefenders.org/en/profile/avni-zogiani)). In an interview with Koha Ditore, archived by the UN mission in Kosovo, he described the fusion he saw at the top of public life: "politics, crime, and corruption have become a type of symbiosis... Siamese twins" ([United Nations Mission in Kosovo](https://media.unmikonline.org/node/101964)). In the same interview he argued that EULEX, the EU rule-of-law mission, was constrained by the European Union's interest in concluding negotiations between Kosovo and Serbia — and he said embassy officials had told him that Çohu's actions were "not in line with their interests in Kosovo" ([United Nations Mission in Kosovo](https://media.unmikonline.org/node/101964)).

## Into electoral politics and government

Zogiani's first bid for formal power failed: in 2016 he was eliminated from the contest for director of Kosovo's Anti-Corruption Agency (AKK), with the parliamentary commission instead shortlisting Shaip Havolli and Burim Sadiku ([Telegrafi](https://telegrafi.com/avni-zogiani-eliminohet-nga-gara-per-drejtor-te-antikorrupsionit-ja-kush-eshte-favoriti/)). He then stood unsuccessfully for a seat in the Kosovo Assembly in the June 2017 elections ([Telegrafi](https://telegrafi.com/zogiani-vetem-vv-mund-ti-jap-fund-pushtetit-te-ldk-se-ne-fushe-kosove/)).

Government office came in 2020, when Prime Minister [Albin Kurti](/people/albin-kurti/) appointed him Deputy Minister of Infrastructure and Environment, serving under Minister Lumir Abdixiku ([Telegrafi](https://telegrafi.com/en/Kurti-appoints-Avni-Zogiani-as-Deputy-Minister-of-Infrastructure-and-Environment/)). In the 2021 local elections he ran as Vetëvendosje's candidate for mayor of Fushë-Kosovë, campaigning on accountable administration and better education, health and water services ([Telegrafi](https://telegrafi.com/zogiani-vetem-vv-mund-ti-jap-fund-pushtetit-te-ldk-se-ne-fushe-kosove/)).

On 16 November 2023 the Office of the Prime Minister moved him from Deputy Minister of Environment, Spatial Planning and Infrastructure to Deputy Minister of Finance, Labor and Transfers ([Office of the Prime Minister of Kosovo](https://kryeministri.rks-gov.net/en/news/two-deputy-ministers-are-given-new-responsibilities/)). He was still in that post on 3 March 2025, when the Prime Minister's Office listed him as Deputy Minister of Finance, Labor and Transfers at a Western Balkans Investment Framework meeting — the last independently verified record of him in office ([Office of the Prime Minister of Kosovo](https://kryeministri.rks-gov.net/en/news/kosovo-and-the-western-balkans-investment-framework-discuss-investment-priorities/)). During Kosovo's post-election government-formation deadlock later that year, a cabinet lineup put forward by Glauk Konjufca named Zogiani as the proposed Minister of Education and Science — but the government was never formed ([Gazeta Express](https://www.gazetaexpress.com/en/the-name-of-the-new-government-cabinet-proposed-by-konjufca-is-published/)).

## The 2026 defamation ruling

On 21 May 2026 the Basic Court of Pristina partly upheld a defamation claim brought by Ferat Shala over a Facebook post Zogiani published on 27 December 2023, in which he alleged a Shala-family monopoly in the public sector in connection with the referral of the Sovereign Fund law ([Epoka e Re](https://www.epokaere.com/gjykata-themelore-e-denon-avni-zogianin-per-shpifje-ndaj-ferat-shales/)). The court awarded Shala €2,000 in non-material damages plus 8% interest and €270.40 in procedural costs, ordered Zogiani to delete the post, and left him the right to appeal ([Epoka e Re](https://www.epokaere.com/gjykata-themelore-e-denon-avni-zogianin-per-shpifje-ndaj-ferat-shales/)).
