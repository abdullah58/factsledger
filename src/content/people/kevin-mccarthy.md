## Overview

Kevin McCarthy spent nine years climbing toward the speaker's gavel, held it for 269 days, and became the first speaker in American history to lose it to a mid-session vote. Then, instead of fading into a pundit chair, he turned up a year later on the board of a pet-pharmaceutical company chasing a vaccine for Valley Fever — a disease he had already spent years fighting through Congress ([Anivive Lifesciences](https://wire.expertini.com/article/former-speaker-of-the-house-kevin-mccarthy-appointed-to-anivive-s-board-of-directors-2024-10-21/)).

That post-Congress turn is the part of McCarthy's story the leading biographies leave out. They all stop at the same place: the January 2023 15-ballot election, the October 3, 2023 ouster, the resignation at the end of the year. This profile starts where those accounts end — with the October 2024 appointment to Anivive Lifesciences' board, where McCarthy's role as co-founder of the Congressional Valley Fever Task Force and co-sponsor of the FORWARD Act made him, in the company's words, a natural pick to help launch what it calls the world's first fungal vaccine ([Anivive Lifesciences](https://wire.expertini.com/article/former-speaker-of-the-house-kevin-mccarthy-appointed-to-anivive-s-board-of-directors-2024-10-21/)).

McCarthy, born January 26, 1965, in Bakersfield, California, served as the 55th Speaker of the U.S. House from January 7 to October 3, 2023, and as a House Republican leader in various roles for nearly a decade before that ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)).

## The Valley Fever thread

The Anivive announcement, dated October 21, 2024, reads like a continuation rather than a reinvention. McCarthy grew up in Kern County, in the heart of Valley Fever country, and as a congressman he had co-founded the Congressional Valley Fever Task Force and co-sponsored the FORWARD Act to advance research, funding, and awareness ([Anivive Lifesciences](https://wire.expertini.com/article/former-speaker-of-the-house-kevin-mccarthy-appointed-to-anivive-s-board-of-directors-2024-10-21/)). The company's founder and CEO, Dylan Balsz, said McCarthy's commitment to fighting the disease and his understanding of government processes would be invaluable as Anivive prepared to launch a vaccine ([Anivive Lifesciences](https://wire.expertini.com/article/former-speaker-of-the-house-kevin-mccarthy-appointed-to-anivive-s-board-of-directors-2024-10-21/)).

Valley Fever is a fungal infection with a genuinely large footprint: Anivive cites nearly 100 million people and 30 million dogs living in affected regions, with infection rates up 600 percent over a decade, billions in annual health-care costs, and no existing preventative vaccine ([Anivive Lifesciences](https://wire.expertini.com/article/former-speaker-of-the-house-kevin-mccarthy-appointed-to-anivive-s-board-of-directors-2024-10-21/)). The company said its fungal vaccine had shown efficacy and safety in more than a dozen studies across multiple species, that it had received a $33 million National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Disease contract in June 2024 to scale manufacturing and fund clinical trials in humans, that it was targeting a 2025 launch for the canine vaccine, and that it was working with the U.S. Department of Agriculture to make the vaccine available to endangered zoo species affected by the disease ([Anivive Lifesciences](https://wire.expertini.com/article/former-speaker-of-the-house-kevin-mccarthy-appointed-to-anivive-s-board-of-directors-2024-10-21/)).

This is the verified answer to "what is Kevin McCarthy doing now" — and it is absent from the biography pages that dominate search. It connects directly to his pre-speakership record: the California district he represented sits in Valley Fever territory, and the task-force work was his, not a post-hoc adornment.

## A 269-day speakership

McCarthy's tenure as speaker lasted from January 7 to October 3, 2023 — 269 days — ending when the House voted 216 to 210 to remove him, the first time in U.S. history a speaker had been ousted in the middle of a term ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/world/us-former-house-speaker-kevin-mccarthy-to-resign-from-congress-at-year-end/1875244/)). The removal came at the hands of a group of right-wing Republicans, led by Representative Matt Gaetz of Florida, who believed McCarthy had betrayed them by cutting a stopgap funding deal with Democrats to avert a government shutdown ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)).

The vote's mechanics were historic on their own. Eight Republicans joined all the Democrats to remove him; earlier the same day, a procedural vote to block the challenge failed 208 to 218, with 11 Republicans voting to let the challenge proceed and Democrats declining to save McCarthy — angered, in part, by his decision to let an impeachment inquiry into President Joe Biden go forward ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician); [Bloomberg](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report/mccarthy-ouster-means-more-turmoil-as-next-shutdown-fight-looms)).

The fallout was immediate and institutional. Representative Patrick McHenry took over as acting speaker, and the House was expected to hold a new speaker election on October 11 ([Bloomberg](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report/mccarthy-ouster-means-more-turmoil-as-next-shutdown-fight-looms)). Moody's Investors Service, then the only major credit grader still giving the U.S. a top rating, warned that its confidence in the country was wavering over "governance" concerns, and Goldman Sachs told clients the ouster raised the risk of a government shutdown the following month ([Bloomberg](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report/mccarthy-ouster-means-more-turmoil-as-next-shutdown-fight-looms)). McCarthy himself refused to run for the post again, telling colleagues the conference should pick somebody else ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)).

Britannica describes the tenure as the third-shortest for a speaker in American history; the ANI wire feed calls it the second-shortest — a discrepancy the sources do not resolve, so this profile claims only the day count.

## How he won the gavel

Getting elected had been its own ordeal. On January 3, 2023, a bloc of 19 hard-line conservatives, drawn almost entirely from the far-right Freedom Caucus, denied McCarthy the votes he needed — the first time since 1923 that a speakership vote had gone past a single ballot ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)). Over successive days and ballots, his opponents kept blocking him despite a raft of concessions, and the stagnation embarrassed the [Republican Party](/organizations/republican-party/)'s leadership badly enough that speculation mounted about a compromise replacement — with Majority Leader-elect Steve Scalise of Louisiana, who stayed loyal to McCarthy, topping the list of alternatives ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)).

The price of the gavel was the weapon that later removed him. McCarthy agreed to a rules change letting any single member offer a motion to remove the speaker at any time — a condition Gaetz held over him throughout his nine-month tenure ([Bloomberg](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report/mccarthy-ouster-means-more-turmoil-as-next-shutdown-fight-looms)). In the early hours of January 7, 2023, McCarthy was elected on the 15th ballot, with help from controversial lawmakers including Marjorie Taylor Greene ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)).

## The long climb

McCarthy's rise was unusually fast for a man from Bakersfield. The youngest of three children of a homemaker mother and an assistant fire chief father, he grew up in the middle-class College Heights neighborhood of a city dominated by agriculture and oil production, in a family that had lived in Kern County for four generations ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)). A childhood speech impediment, treated with a therapist, was later cited as the cause of his occasional awkwardness as a public speaker; he played tight end on the Bakersfield High football team and was inducted into the school's athletic hall of fame in 2014 — an honor he and the school agreed had more to do with his politics than his athletic prowess ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)).

His origin story, told often on the trail, begins in October 1984, when the 20-year-old McCarthy bought a lottery ticket that won $5,000. He invested the winnings in the stock market and used the profits to buy a small business — Kevin O's Deli, operating out of a yogurt shop owned by his aunt and uncle in Bakersfield — withdrawing from school in the process ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)). He sold the deli and used the proceeds to return to college in 1987, earning a bachelor's in business in 1989 and an MBA in 1994 from California State University, Bakersfield ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)).

Politics had already found him: in 1987 he began working for influential Republican Congressman Bill Thomas, first as an intern, then as a longtime staffer ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)). He chaired the California Young Republicans in the 1990s and the Young Republican National Federation from 1999 to 2001, won a Kern Community College District board seat in 2000, entered the California Assembly in 2002, and became its Republican minority leader at 38 — a rise Britannica attributes to Thomas's influence and McCarthy's fundraising talent ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)). When Thomas retired in 2006, McCarthy won his mentor's safe House seat ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)).

In Washington he moved quickly: chief deputy whip in 2009, majority whip in 2011 after Republicans took the House, coauthor of *Young Guns: A New Generation of Conservative Leaders* (2010) with Eric Cantor and Paul Ryan, and majority leader in 2014 after Cantor lost his seat in a primary ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)). The speakership seemed his when John Boehner was effectively forced out in 2015 — but McCarthy's candidacy collapsed under a combination of a denied affair rumor, the party right's belief that he was not conservative enough, and an untimely gaffe: boasting that the House's Republican-led Benghazi investigation had undercut Hillary Clinton's popularity, he broke the unspoken rule of never acknowledging the probe's political motivation ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)). He withdrew; Ryan became speaker.

## The Trump years

Where Ryan had an uneasy relationship with Donald Trump, McCarthy quickly developed a rapport with the new president — defending him on Russian meddling in the 2016 election and on the Ukraine phone call that led to the first impeachment ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)). After Ryan chose not to run for reelection in 2018 and Democrats took the House, the Republican caucus chose McCarthy as minority leader ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)).

January 6, 2021, cracked that relationship: McCarthy said from the House floor that "the president bears responsibility for Wednesday's attack on Congress by mob rioters" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)). By the end of the month he was traveling to Trump's Florida home seeking rapprochement, responding to the base's continued support for the former president — and in October 2021 a New York Times report revealed that, in the attack's aftermath, McCarthy had told Republican colleagues he planned to advise Trump to resign ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)).

He also shaped the House's handling of January 6: after Speaker Nancy Pelosi refused to seat two of his nominees for the investigating committee, McCarthy withdrew the rest and branded the process a "sham," blocking Republican involvement — only for Representatives Adam Kinzinger and Liz Cheney to defy the directive, after which the Republican National Committee censured them and Cheney was stripped of her party leadership role ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)). Some Republicans, including Trump, later criticized McCarthy for leaving the committee without a countervailing Republican presence once its findings began damaging Trump's reputation ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)).

## Leaving Congress

On December 6, 2023, two months after his ouster, McCarthy announced in a Wall Street Journal op-ed that he would leave Congress at the end of the year, after a 17-year stint largely spent at the leadership table ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/world/us-former-house-speaker-kevin-mccarthy-to-resign-from-congress-at-year-end/1875244/)). "No matter the odds, or personal cost, we did the right thing," he wrote, adding that he had no regrets ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/world/us-former-house-speaker-kevin-mccarthy-to-resign-from-congress-at-year-end/1875244/)). According to Britannica, Representative Lauren Boebert had recommended [Mike Johnson](/people/mike-johnson/) of Louisiana as McCarthy's replacement during the succession scramble ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)). Vince Fong, a former McCarthy staff member running with his ex-boss's endorsement, won the May 2024 special election to finish the term ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)).

In his private life, McCarthy married Judy Wages — the classmate he had met in a high-school biology class — in 1992; they have a son, Connor, and a daughter, Meghan ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Kevin-McCarthy-American-politician)).
