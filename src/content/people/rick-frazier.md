## Overview

Rick Frazier is a founding member of the Front Row Joes, the loose band of [Donald Trump](https://factsledger.com/people/donald-trump/) loyalists who have built portions of their lives around traveling to as many of his rallies as possible and staking out the front row ([Playbook via Andrew Tobias](https://andrewtobias.com/theyre-leaving-maga-2/)). He says he has attended more than 100 Trump rallies ([Playbook via Crooks and Liars](https://crooksandliars.com/2026/09/light-switch-went-even-trump-s-front-row)). In a September 28, 2026 [Politico Playbook](/organizations/politico/) piece — "Donald Trump's Joes are starting to feel like they overpaid for the front row" — he became one of two prominent members to publicly break with the president, alongside Trisha Hope ([Playbook via Andrew Tobias](https://andrewtobias.com/theyre-leaving-maga-2/)).

## A founding Joe — and the group's content creator

Playbook identified Frazier as a founding Joe, and quoted him saying he creates content for the group — a role beyond rally attendance that the reporting surfaced but most reprints skipped ([Playbook via Digby's Hullabaloo](https://digbysblog.net/2026/09/28/when-this-counts-as-good-news/)). He told Playbook he had been to more than 100 Trump rallies, a figure that made him one of the group's most committed road followers ([Playbook via Andrew Tobias](https://andrewtobias.com/theyre-leaving-maga-2/)).

The Front Row Joes had been documented as a phenomenon well before the break. Former Trump White House press secretary Sean Spicer produced a 2024 documentary, "Trump's Front Row Joes," described as "a closer look at those Trump supporters who have attended dozens of the former president's rallies" ([The Seth Leibsohn Show](https://omny.fm/shows/sethleibsohnshow/july-16-2024-sean-spicer)). Playbook's own framing was that the Joes "once represented unbreakable, unconditional, unprecedented devotion" ([Playbook via Andrew Tobias](https://andrewtobias.com/theyre-leaving-maga-2/)).

## The September 2026 break

Frazier's stated reasons were the same two issues that moved Trisha Hope: the administration's handling of the Jeffrey Epstein files and the war in Iran. "Epstein crimes and feeding the industrial military complex … those are big nos for me," he told Playbook. "He promoted himself as a no-war president. That didn't work out" ([Playbook via Crooks and Liars](https://crooksandliars.com/2026/09/light-switch-went-even-trump-s-front-row)). He said of Trump simply: "he can't be trusted" ([Playbook via Andrew Tobias](https://andrewtobias.com/theyre-leaving-maga-2/)).

He was less theatrical than Hope — she called Trump "a fraud and liar" and threatened to lobby Congress for a third impeachment — and more quietly done with politics altogether. "I'm to the point of not going to the polls in November," Frazier said, meaning the November 3 midterm elections ([Playbook via Digby's Hullabaloo](https://digbysblog.net/2026/09/28/when-this-counts-as-good-news/)).

## A caveat the headlines skipped

Frazier himself dampened the defection narrative. He estimated that "95% of the Front Row Joes are unwavering," even as he personally broke away ([Playbook via Digby's Hullabaloo](https://digbysblog.net/2026/09/28/when-this-counts-as-good-news/)). Playbook added its own electoral math: Frazier lives in Ohio and Hope lives in Texas — "states where 5% or less could make all the difference come Nov. 3" ([Playbook via Digby's Hullabaloo](https://digbysblog.net/2026/09/28/when-this-counts-as-good-news/)).

## What this profile does not claim

It does not claim his "more than 100 rallies" figure has been independently confirmed; it is his own stated number. It does not repeat the reported personal detail that he lost a daughter to fentanyl: a CPAC episode page made that claim, but the page failed to load this session, so it stays unverified.
