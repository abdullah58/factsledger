## Overview

Zach Coelius is an American entrepreneur and angel investor based in Silicon Valley. He founded and led Triggit, a demand-side advertising platform, from its launch in 2008 to its acquisition by Gravity4 in March 2015 ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/ad-exchange-news/distressed-triggit-scooped-up-by-gravity4-gurbaksh-chahals-merger-machine/)). As an angel he led the first AngelList syndicate into Cruise Automation, the self-driving startup General Motors acquired for $1 billion in 2016 ([Startup Grind](https://www.startupgrind.com/events/details/startup-grind-chisinau-presents-meet-zach-coelius-angel-investor-from-silicon-valley/)). And in the [Booksy](/organizations/booksy/) story he appears twice: as an investor in the $13.2 million Series B in July 2018 and again among the investors joining the $70 million Series C in January 2021 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/07/06/booksy-the-worldwide-booking-system-raises-13-2-million/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)).

One fact about Coelius is widely repeated and wrong, and it is worth correcting at the outset: several event and podcast biographies say Triggit was "sold to LinkedIn" in 2015. It was not. Both contemporaneous reports of the deal — AdExchanger on March 20, 2015 and FinSMEs on March 29, 2015 — state that the acquirer was Gravity4, the roll-up company founded by serial entrepreneur Gurbaksh Chahal ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/ad-exchange-news/distressed-triggit-scooped-up-by-gravity4-gurbaksh-chahals-merger-machine/); [FinSMEs](https://www.finsmes.com/2015/03/gravity4-acquires-triggit.html)). The LinkedIn claim is an error that has propagated through speaker biographies; this profile follows the contemporaneous reporting.

## Triggit: the pivot, the raise, the distressed exit

Triggit was launched in 2008 by Coelius, his sister Susan Coelius Keplinger (as chief operating officer) and chief technology officer Ryan Tecco ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/ad-exchange-news/distressed-triggit-scooped-up-by-gravity4-gurbaksh-chahals-merger-machine/)). It began as a drag-and-drop JavaScript tool for authoring ads, then pivoted in October 2009 into a demand-side platform — software that lets advertisers buy display inventory through real-time bidding ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2010/06/24/triggit-raises-4-2-million-for-its-demand-side-ad-platform/)).

The pivot worked well enough to attract serious money. In June 2010 Triggit raised $4.2 million, led by Spark Capital and Foundry Group, with Reid Hoffman among the investors; the company had previously raised $1 million from Floodgate and angel investors ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2010/06/24/triggit-raises-4-2-million-for-its-demand-side-ad-platform/)). At the time it was serving 15 billion impressions a day with just 13 employees ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2010/06/24/triggit-raises-4-2-million-for-its-demand-side-ad-platform/)). Triggit was an early leader in Facebook Exchange, the social network's real-time-bidding exchange, and that position was both its making and its undoing: Facebook Exchange "proved to be a rough market," and by 2015 the company had been acquired "after years of not making money" ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/ad-exchange-news/distressed-triggit-scooped-up-by-gravity4-gurbaksh-chahals-merger-machine/)).

AdExchanger's headline said it plainly: "Distressed Triggit Scooped Up By Gravity4, Gurbaksh Chahal's Merger Machine" ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/ad-exchange-news/distressed-triggit-scooped-up-by-gravity4-gurbaksh-chahals-merger-machine/)). Nearly all of Triggit's roughly 40 employees — including five in Brazil — went to work for Gravity4, while the founders left the company; Coelius himself was unavailable for comment ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/ad-exchange-news/distressed-triggit-scooped-up-by-gravity4-gurbaksh-chahals-merger-machine/)). Chahal said Triggit "has built a strong customer base that includes dozens of leading global brands and will be instrumental to Gravity4's growth," and that Gravity4 would keep expanding the customer base and the product ([FinSMEs](https://www.finsmes.com/2015/03/gravity4-acquires-triggit.html)). Terms were not disclosed ([FinSMEs](https://www.finsmes.com/2015/03/gravity4-acquires-triggit.html)).

## The angel career

Coelius's reputation as an investor rests on Cruise Automation. He led the first AngelList syndicate to invest in Cruise, the self-driving startup later acquired by GM for $1 billion in 2016 ([Startup Grind](https://www.startupgrind.com/events/details/startup-grind-chisinau-presents-meet-zach-coelius-angel-investor-from-silicon-valley/); [Quicket](https://www.quicket.co.za/events/38558-heavy-chef-masterclass-zach-coelius-don-packett-taking-a-project-from-concept-t/)). An [Inovo](/organizations/inovo-venture-partners/) podcast description calls him a "4x entrepreneur and angel investor based in Silicon Valley" and notes the same Cruise investment ([Inovo Podcast](https://www.listennotes.com/da/podcasts/inovo-podcast/zach-coelius-how-to-get-3kBIQKb_AcY/)). Both the syndicate role and the "4x entrepreneur" label come from event and podcast biographies — tier3 sources — and are flagged as such; no tier1 or tier2 source was found confirming the Cruise syndicate.

A podcast interview states that his fund, Coelius Capital, raised its $33 million Fund II in three weeks; that, too, is podcast-only and is stated here as a podcast-reported claim, not a verified fact. What is solidly documented is the pattern it describes: an operator-turned-angel who writes early cheques into companies he understands — advertising technology, then autonomy, then the European booking market.

## The Booksy connection

Coelius backed Booksy in two rounds. TechCrunch's July 6, 2018 report of the $13.2 million Series B names him among the investors; the round valued the company's scale at 2.5 million bookings a month and noted that Booksy had launched in the US in 2017 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/07/06/booksy-the-worldwide-booking-system-raises-13-2-million/)). Two and a half years later, TechCrunch's January 27, 2021 report of the $70 million Series C listed the round as "also joined by OpenOcean, Piton Capital, VNV Global, Enern, Kai Hansen, Zach Coelius and Manta Ray Ventures," taking total raised to $119 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)). He appears alongside [Kai Hansen](/people/kai-hansen/) in both rounds — two European angels who had each built and exited a company of their own, now backing a Polish-founded booking platform's expansion into six markets.
