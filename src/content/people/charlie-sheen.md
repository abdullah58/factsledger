The story Charlie Sheen tells about himself now begins with a death. *The Book
of Sheen*, his 2025 memoir, opens with this line: "On September 3, 1965, in
New York City, at 10:58 p.m., I was born dead" — his mother Janet and his
father, actor Martin Sheen (born Ramón Antonio Gerard Estevez), watched in
panic as Dr. Irwin Chabon revived the infant
([The New York Times](https://published.pdf.pagesuite.com/fea9d757-caf1-44de-a806-73630a1ba0bf/f6ef7547-8847-4d7d-8797-099e473212cf/5b9d6579-8899-4bac-8e86-6a67fe9536d0/9c7ca0c4-f444-4090-8f28-58af7a13a9f0/4cbe1e74-14a5-488c-a088-13bb91b33ed1/lowres.pdf)).
He calls writing the book the hardest and most rewarding job of his life, and
an "all-access, backstage pass to the truth" — the first time, he says, the
stories come "directly from the actual guy" rather than through "the eyes and
pens of others" ([The New York Times](https://published.pdf.pagesuite.com/fea9d757-caf1-44de-a806-73630a1ba0bf/f6ef7547-8847-4d7d-8797-099e473212cf/5b9d6579-8899-4bac-8e86-6a67fe9536d0/9c7ca0c4-f444-4090-8f28-58af7a13a9f0/4cbe1e74-14a5-488c-a088-13bb91b33ed1/lowres.pdf)).
The memoir arrived alongside the Netflix documentary *aka Charlie Sheen*, and
in August 2026 he announced a new podcast, *Mostly Sheenius*, in which he
would interview figures from entertainment, sports, music, and pop culture —
Reuters noting that it reverses his long-held position as the celebrity in the
interviewee's chair, asking the questions for once instead of dodging them
([bdnews24](https://bdnews24.com/people/144a9149183b)). The life he describes
is startlingly ordinary now: five kids, grandchildren, smoothies and pedicures
with his daughter Lola, sports on television, and nearly eight years of
memories of addiction — alcohol, cocaine, pills, crack — that he keeps close
as a warning system rather than burying
([The New York Times](https://published.pdf.pagesuite.com/fea9d757-caf1-44de-a806-73630a1ba0bf/f6ef7547-8847-4d7d-8797-099e473212cf/5b9d6579-8899-4bac-8e86-6a67fe9536d0/9c7ca0c4-f444-4090-8f28-58af7a13a9f0/4cbe1e74-14a5-488c-a088-13bb91b33ed1/lowres.pdf)).

The contrast only makes sense against the scale of the fall. Born Carlos Irwin
Estevez, Sheen grew up inside the industry — his father already a major star,
his brother Emilio Estevez building his own career — and emerged in the 1980s
in *Platoon* before *Wall Street*, *Major League*, and *Hot Shots!* made him a
genuine film draw ([bdnews24](https://bdnews24.com/people/144a9149183b)). In
2003 he took the lead on CBS's *Two and a Half Men* as Charlie Harper, and the
sitcom made him one of the highest-paid actors on television — nearly $2
million per episode at his peak, with the show drawing some 15 million viewers
per episode ([Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/two-and-a-half-men-creator-chuck-lorre-says-charlie-sheen-2011-meltdown-ratings-gold-12128128);
[The New York Times](https://published.pdf.pagesuite.com/fea9d757-caf1-44de-a806-73630a1ba0bf/f6ef7547-8847-4d7d-8797-099e473212cf/5b9d6579-8899-4bac-8e86-6a67fe9536d0/9c7ca0c4-f444-4090-8f28-58af7a13a9f0/4cbe1e74-14a5-488c-a088-13bb91b33ed1/lowres.pdf)).
His increasingly chaotic personal life eventually collided with the show: in
January 2011 production halted so he could enter rehab for the third time, and
then came the public barrage — on the *Alex Jones Show* he called Lorre a
"clown" and a "charlatan" with an "un-evolved mind," told TMZ he "violently
hate[d]" the writer as a "stupid, stupid little man," and posted an open
letter calling him a "contaminated little maggot"
([Entertainment Tonight](https://www.etonline.com/two-and-a-half-men-creator-chuck-lorre-shares-how-he-and-charlie-sheen-reconciled-exclusive-214777);
[TV Insider](http://www.tvinsider.com/1112164/charlie-sheen-chuck-lorre-bookie-reunion-two-and-a-half-men-feud/)).
The trigger had been Lorre's own provocation — a vanity card at the end of a
*Two and a Half Men* episode that called out Sheen's substance abuse
([TV Insider](http://www.tvinsider.com/1112164/charlie-sheen-chuck-lorre-bookie-reunion-two-and-a-half-men-feud/)).
CBS and [Warner](https://factsledger.com/organizations/warner-bros/) Bros. canceled the rest of the season, terminated his contract,
banned him from their production lot, and on March 7, 2011, he was fired —
two months later, on May 13, Ashton Kutcher was confirmed as his replacement
([Entertainment Tonight](https://www.etonline.com/two-and-a-half-men-creator-chuck-lorre-shares-how-he-and-charlie-sheen-reconciled-exclusive-214777);
[Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/two-and-a-half-men-creator-chuck-lorre-says-charlie-sheen-2011-meltdown-ratings-gold-12128128)).
The "tiger blood," "Adonis DNA," and "winning" interviews made him one of the
defining celebrity spectacles of the era ([bdnews24](https://bdnews24.com/people/144a9149183b)).
He then sued Lorre and Warner Bros. for $100 million in Los Angeles Superior
Court; the case settled, with Sheen reportedly receiving a rumored $25 million
payout ([TV Insider](http://www.tvinsider.com/1112164/charlie-sheen-chuck-lorre-bookie-reunion-two-and-a-half-men-feud/)).

The wreckage kept moving after the cameras stopped. He returned to television
in 2012 with FX's *Anger Management*, and in 2015 publicly disclosed that he
was HIV-positive, later reflecting on his fear of being exposed and alleging
that people who knew about the diagnosis had attempted to extort him
([bdnews24](https://bdnews24.com/people/144a9149183b)). He has been married
three times — to Donna Peele, Denise Richards, and Brooke Mueller — and has
five children ([bdnews24](https://bdnews24.com/people/144a9149183b)). The
sitcom, meanwhile, ran four more seasons with Kutcher and ended in 2015 with
a parting shot: Charlie Harper returned home and was crushed by a falling
piano on his own stoop — performed by a body double, since Sheen had declined
Lorre's offer to appear himself, not finding the scene "funny" — after which
Lorre appeared on camera and said "Winning!," the catchphrase from Sheen's
2011 meltdown, before his own onscreen alter-ego met the same death-by-piano
fate ([TV Insider](http://www.tvinsider.com/1112164/charlie-sheen-chuck-lorre-bookie-reunion-two-and-a-half-men-feud/)).

Then, improbably, came the reunion. On December 12, 2017, Sheen got sober
after decades of addiction, a decision he has traced in part to a moment with
his daughter Sam that forced him to confront how his addiction was affecting
his children, according to ABC News
([The New York Times](https://published.pdf.pagesuite.com/fea9d757-caf1-44de-a806-73630a1ba0bf/f6ef7547-8847-4d7d-8797-099e473212cf/5b9d6579-8899-4bac-8e86-6a67fe9536d0/9c7ca0c4-f444-4090-8f28-58af7a13a9f0/4cbe1e74-14a5-488c-a088-13bb91b33ed1/lowres.pdf);
[bdnews24](https://bdnews24.com/people/144a9149183b)). In 2023 Lorre reached
out: "I called his agent… they put me in touch with Charlie, and I said,
'Here's a funny idea,'" Lorre told *Entertainment Tonight*
([Entertainment Tonight](https://www.etonline.com/two-and-a-half-men-creator-chuck-lorre-shares-how-he-and-charlie-sheen-reconciled-exclusive-214777)).
Sheen was "gracious and enthusiastic and generous," the two talked on the
phone for an hour, and Lorre sent a script asking Sheen to play an exaggerated
version of himself in the new Max comedy *Bookie* — Sebastian Maniscalco's
sports-betting series, in which Sheen's character casually claims to own
"Babe Ruth's autopsy report"
([Entertainment Tonight](https://www.etonline.com/two-and-a-half-men-creator-chuck-lorre-shares-how-he-and-charlie-sheen-reconciled-exclusive-214777)).
Sheen asked for adjustments — notably, "Can we not do the drug addict
Charlie?" — so his character is sober but runs a regular poker game at a
rehab he knows well ("That's a great rehab, I've been here many times")
([Entertainment Tonight](https://www.etonline.com/two-and-a-half-men-creator-chuck-lorre-shares-how-he-and-charlie-sheen-reconciled-exclusive-214777)).
At the table read the two men hugged: "It was closure. It was healing. And it
was a big weight off my heart," Lorre said
([Entertainment Tonight](https://www.etonline.com/two-and-a-half-men-creator-chuck-lorre-shares-how-he-and-charlie-sheen-reconciled-exclusive-214777)).

The feud's epilogue keeps arriving, and Lorre is still narrating it. In a
September 2026 *Playboy* interview promoting his own memoir, *Sitcom: A
Memoir* (released October 13, 2026), Lorre looked back fifteen years and
offered this: "Charlie's testosterone-cream, crack, and vodka meltdown was
ratings gold, and everyone eagerly cashed in on it"
([Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/two-and-a-half-men-creator-chuck-lorre-says-charlie-sheen-2011-meltdown-ratings-gold-12128128)).
Sheen, for his part, has taken ownership in blunter terms than he used at the
time. To Yahoo Entertainment in 2021: "There were 55 different ways for me to
handle that situation, and I chose number 56… it was desperately juvenile."
And on the memeification of his misery: "I'm so glad that I traded early
retirement for a f---ing hashtag"
([Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/two-and-a-half-men-creator-chuck-lorre-says-charlie-sheen-2011-meltdown-ratings-gold-12128128)).
Sheen left the sitcom after 178 episodes
([bdnews24](https://bdnews24.com/people/144a9149183b)). He is no longer simply
the star of *Two and a Half Men* or the face of "winning" — he is trying to
become the narrator of his own story, and this time, he seems determined to
tell it without the mask ([bdnews24](https://bdnews24.com/people/144a9149183b)).
