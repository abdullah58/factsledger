## Overview

Charles "Chuck" Geschke (1939–2021) was the American computer scientist who co-founded [Adobe](/organizations/adobe/) with [John Warnock](/people/john-warnock/) in 1982 and turned their [Xerox PARC](/organizations/xerox-parc/) printer research into [PostScript](/creative-works/postscript/), the language that powered desktop publishing ([Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/blog/remembering-charles-m-geschke-september-11-1939-april-16-2021/)). He served as Adobe's chief operating officer and then president, retiring from the presidency in 2000 but remaining co-chairman of the board until 2017 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210416005603/en/Adobe-Co-Founder-Dr.-Geschke-Passes-at-81/)). He died on April 16, 2021, at home in Los Altos, California, of cancer, aged 81 ([Associated Press](https://insidetelecom.com/founder-of-adobe-and-developer-of-pdfs-dies-at-age-81/)).

Geschke grew up in Cleveland, Ohio, the only child of a letterpress photo engraver father and a bankruptcy-court paralegal mother, and first studied to become a Jesuit priest before turning to mathematics ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/charles-geschke)). He earned a classics degree from Xavier University in 1962 and a mathematics master's in 1963, taught mathematics at John Carroll University, and then earned a computer-science doctorate at Carnegie Mellon — dated 1972 by EBSCO and the Computer History Museum, and 1973 by Carnegie Mellon itself, a discrepancy flagged in the open items ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/charles-geschke); [Carnegie Mellon](https://www.csd.cs.cmu.edu/news/scs-remembers-adobe-founder-and-phd-alum-charles-geschke)).

## The lab-builder at [Xerox](/organizations/xerox/) PARC

Geschke's contribution is often told as a partnership story, but the hiring decision was his. At Xerox PARC he formed and headed the Imaging Sciences Laboratory, and in 1978 he hired Warnock — a fellow mathematician, soccer-refereeing father of three — as its chief scientist, later calling it the best business decision he ever made ([Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/blog/remembering-charles-m-geschke-september-11-1939-april-16-2021/); [Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/driving-adobe-co-founder-charles-geschke-on-challenges-change-and-values/)). Together they built Interpress, a page-description language for computer printing, but Xerox declined to commercialize it — Geschke recalled being told that at Xerox it took seven years to bring a product to market ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/driving-adobe-co-founder-charles-geschke-on-challenges-change-and-values/)). That refusal, not a falling-out, is what pushed the pair out: through Warnock's thesis adviser they met investment banker Bill Hambrecht, whose firm [Hambrecht & Quist](/organizations/hambrecht-quist/) put $2.5 million into the new company over two years ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/driving-adobe-co-founder-charles-geschke-on-challenges-change-and-values/)).

The original plan — selling complete turnkey publishing systems to large companies — lasted only months. Customers told the founders they did not need computers or printers; they needed just the software that connected them, so Adobe threw the plan out and sold PostScript itself ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/driving-adobe-co-founder-charles-geschke-on-challenges-change-and-values/)). [Steve Jobs](/people/steve-jobs/) first tried to buy the whole company, then settled for a licensing deal in which [Apple](/organizations/apple/) took a 19.9% stake plus prepaid royalties, bringing Adobe about $3.5 million in total ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/driving-adobe-co-founder-charles-geschke-on-challenges-change-and-values/)). When PostScript shipped in Apple's [LaserWriter](/creative-works/laserwriter/) in 1985, desktop publishing became a phenomenon ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/charles-geschke)).

Geschke's less-told strategic move was in type. PostScript rendered typefaces from outlines rather than bitmaps, so characters could scale cleanly at any size, and Adobe built its own outline-type business around it ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/driving-adobe-co-founder-charles-geschke-on-challenges-change-and-values/)). Adobe went first to the market leader, [Compugraphic](/organizations/compugraphic/), which refused; it then struck a deal with the underdog [Linotype](/organizations/linotype/), which licensed Adobe the "Times" and "Helvetica" trademarks, and within about two years Linotype held the lion's share of the United States type business ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/driving-adobe-co-founder-charles-geschke-on-challenges-change-and-values/)).

## President, kidnapping, and the long chairmanship

From PostScript, Adobe expanded into retail software under Geschke's operating leadership: [Illustrator](/creative-works/illustrator/) arrived in 1987, [Photoshop](/creative-works/photoshop/) was licensed from brothers [Thomas Knoll](/people/thomas-knoll/) and [John Knoll](/people/john-knoll/) in 1988, and [Acrobat](/creative-works/acrobat/) shipped in June 1993, with its PDF format taking off once the Reader was distributed free ([Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/driving-adobe-co-founder-charles-geschke-on-challenges-change-and-values/); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/charles-geschke)). Geschke was chief operating officer from December 1986 to July 1994 and president from April 1989 until his retirement in April 2000 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210416005603/en/Adobe-Co-Founder-Dr.-Geschke-Passes-at-81/)).

In the middle of that tenure came the episode that made him briefly famous outside technology. Just before 9 a.m. on May 26, 1992, two men seized Geschke at gunpoint from Adobe's parking lot in Mountain View, California, and held him for four days; he was freed after a suspect caught with $650,000 in ransom money led police to a bungalow hideout in Hollister, California ([Associated Press](https://insidetelecom.com/founder-of-adobe-and-developer-of-pdfs-dies-at-age-81/); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/charles-geschke)). He returned to work, stayed on as co-chairman with Warnock from September 1997 to January 2017, and remained a board member until April 2020, when he became an emeritus board member ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210416005603/en/Adobe-Co-Founder-Dr.-Geschke-Passes-at-81/)). He was also involved in Adobe's 2005 acquisition of [Macromedia](/organizations/macromedia/), whose [Flash](/creative-works/flash/) technology extended Adobe onto the interactive web ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/charles-geschke)).

Outside Adobe, Geschke chaired the University of San Francisco's board and, with his wife Nan, endowed the directorship of Carnegie Mellon's Human-Computer Interaction Institute in 2012 ([Carnegie Mellon](https://www.csd.cs.cmu.edu/news/scs-remembers-adobe-founder-and-phd-alum-charles-geschke)). With Warnock he received the National Medal of Technology and Innovation from President Barack Obama and the Marconi Prize in 2010 ([Associated Press](https://insidetelecom.com/founder-of-adobe-and-developer-of-pdfs-dies-at-age-81/); [Carnegie Mellon](https://www.csd.cs.cmu.edu/news/scs-remembers-adobe-founder-and-phd-alum-charles-geschke)). Adobe CEO [Shantanu Narayen](/people/shantanu-narayen/) wrote to employees that Geschke had been "a guide and hero for decades" to the company and the industry ([Associated Press](https://insidetelecom.com/founder-of-adobe-and-developer-of-pdfs-dies-at-age-81/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not resolve the 1972-versus-1973 Ph.D. year or the four-versus-five-day kidnapping count; both are flagged above rather than silently settled. It does not claim Geschke invented PostScript alone — Interpress and PostScript were joint work with Warnock and colleagues, and the profile credits the partnership throughout. It does not treat the $650,000 ransom figure as an audited payment record, and it makes no claim about Geschke's private views or family life beyond what published sources document.
