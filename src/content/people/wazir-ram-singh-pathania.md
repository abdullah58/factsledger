**Wazir Ram Singh Pathania (1824–1849)** was the minister — not the king — of the hill state of Nurpur who led an armed uprising against the British in 1848, was captured unarmed at his daily worship, and died in exile in Rangoon on 11 November 1849 ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/governor-unveils-statue-of-wazir-ram-singh/); [[Himachal Pradesh](/places/himachal-pradesh/) Government](http://himachalpr.gov.in/%28S%2830zwyj5jvbhngd5hlhvwmkny%29%29/OnePressRelease.aspx?Language=1&ID=36255)). His story, long kept alive in Himachali folk song, reached a new generation when *[Krish, Trish and Baltiboy](/creative-works/krish-trish-and-baltiboy/)* dramatised him in its Season 2, "Bharat Hain Hum" ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/entertainment/second-season-of-animated-series-krish-trish-and-baltiboy-bharat-hain-hum-launched-at-iffi/)).

## A minister's son and the annexation of Nurpur

Ram Singh Pathania was born on 10 April 1824 at Bassa Waziran village in Nurpur — today part of Kangra district, Himachal Pradesh — to Shyam Singh, himself a minister to Raja Veer Singh of Nurpur ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/remembering-wazir-ram-singh-pathania-rebel-of-nurpur-who-defied-british-rule/)). The title he carries, *wazir*, means minister: he was the kingdom's chief minister, never its raja. Following the British-Sikh Convention of 1846, the British extended control over the hill states and annexed Nurpur, reducing the minor ruler Jaswant Singh to a titular position ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/remembering-wazir-ram-singh-pathania-rebel-of-nurpur-who-defied-british-rule/)). Rs 5,000 — that single figure explains the uprising better than any battle narrative: The Tribune reports the British acquired the rights to Nurpur from the boy-king Jaswant Singh for "a mere 5,000 rupees," a transaction that turned a succession question into a humiliation the hill Rajputs would not swallow ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/remembering-wazir-ram-singh-pathania-rebel-of-nurpur-who-defied-british-rule/)).

## The 1848 uprising and the battle at Shahpur

Unwilling to submit, Ram Singh Pathania joined forces with the Rajas of Kangra and Jaswan, raised the banner of rebellion against the British Empire, was declared the minister of Nurpur, and led an uprising to restore his homeland's honour and sovereignty ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/remembering-wazir-ram-singh-pathania-rebel-of-nurpur-who-defied-british-rule/)). Governor Shiv Pratap Shukla later singled out the battle at Shahpur as the testimony of his military prowess — fought, in the Governor's telling, with "a handful of companions" ([Himachal Pradesh Government](http://himachalpr.gov.in/%28S%2830zwyj5jvbhngd5hlhvwmkny%29%29/OnePressRelease.aspx?Language=1&ID=36255)). A tertiary e-magazine account adds a surprise night attack on 14 August 1848 on the Shahpur Kandi Fort, with the Nurpur flag hoisted and Jaswant Singh proclaimed king — but no tier1 or tier2 source confirms that date or the fort's full name, so it stays flagged, not asserted ([PreSense](https://www.prpoint.com/ezine/presense0924.pdf)).

## Captured at worship, exiled to Rangoon

Unable to defeat him in open battle, the British resorted to deceit. While Ram Singh was engaged in his daily worship, he was captured — the tertiary account places the ambush at his morning rituals on the banks of the Ravi — sentenced to life imprisonment, and first sent to Kalapani before being transferred to Rangoon ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/remembering-wazir-ram-singh-pathania-rebel-of-nurpur-who-defied-british-rule/); [PreSense](https://www.prpoint.com/ezine/presense0924.pdf)). There he endured brutal treatment and attained martyrdom on 11 November 1849 ([Himachal Pradesh Government](http://himachalpr.gov.in/%28S%2830zwyj5jvbhngd5hlhvwmkny%29%29/OnePressRelease.aspx?Language=1&ID=36255)). Both The Tribune and the state government's press release agree on the date and the place. Every tier1 source gives his age at death as 24 — yet the same sources' dates (born 10 April 1824, died 11 November 1849) work out to 25 years and 7 months, an arithmetic wrinkle the sources never address and this profile flags rather than resolves ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/governor-unveils-statue-of-wazir-ram-singh/)).

## Statue, anniversary, and the animated afterlife

On 7 October 2024, Governor Shiv Pratap Shukla unveiled Pathania's statue at the Ram Singh Pathania Memorial in Nurpur, Kangra district ([Himachal Pradesh Government](http://himachalpr.gov.in/%28S%2830zwyj5jvbhngd5hlhvwmkny%29%29/OnePressRelease.aspx?Language=1&ID=36255)). Shukla described Pathania as having led "the first organised armed revolt against British rule in India" — carried by the state's press release and the national press, but best read as official commemoration rather than settled historiography ([The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/shimla/hp-governor-honors-wazir-ram-singh-pathania-the-unsung-hero-of-indias-first-armed-revolt/articleshow/114026334.cms)). In November 2025 his 176th death anniversary was observed at his native Basa Waziran; the folk ballad "Koi killa Pathania jor ladiya, koi beta Wazir da zor ladiya," sung by the late bard Kaka Darvesh, keeps his memory alive ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/himachal/remembering-wazir-ram-singh-pathania-rebel-of-nurpur-who-defied-british-rule/)). The newest vessel is animation: *Krish, Trish and Baltiboy* Season 2, "Bharat Hain Hum," launched at the International Film Festival of India with Pathania of Himachal Pradesh featured alongside Birsa Munda of Jharkhand and others, in 12 Indian and 7 international languages for audiences in 150 countries ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/entertainment/second-season-of-animated-series-krish-trish-and-baltiboy-bharat-hain-hum-launched-at-iffi/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Was Wazir Ram Singh Pathania a king?**
No. *Wazir* means minister: he was the chief minister of the hill state of Nurpur, serving the minor king Jaswant Singh, and his father Shyam Singh had been a minister to Raja Veer Singh before him. Lead-index summaries that call him the "Raja of Nurpur" are wrong; the rajas were Veer Singh and then the boy-king Jaswant Singh.

**When and how did he die?**
On 11 November 1849 at Rangoon (now Yangon, Myanmar), after a life-imprisonment sentence and exile first to Kalapani. The British captured him by deceit while he was at his daily worship, having failed to defeat him in open battle. Every source gives his age as 24, though the stated dates work out to 25 — a discrepancy this profile flags openly.

**Who suppressed his 1848 rebellion?**
The tier1 sources read for this profile do not name the suppressing officer. Indian exam-preparation materials name Commissioner "Lawrance/Lawrence," but those pages were not fetched and read in full, so the name is not asserted here — it is recorded in the profile's open items as an unverified lead.

**Where can I encounter his story today?**
Two places: the statue unveiled on 7 October 2024 at the Ram Singh Pathania Memorial in Nurpur, Kangra district, and Season 2 ("Bharat Hain Hum") of the animated series *Krish, Trish and Baltiboy*, launched at the International Film Festival of India, which features him among its dramatised freedom fighters in 12 Indian and 7 international languages.

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not call him a raja or king; he was the wazir (minister) of Nurpur under the minor Jaswant Singh.
- It does not date his death to 1848; every tier1 source gives 11 November 1849.
- It does not assert the 14 August 1848 date or the Shahpur Kandi Fort name for the battle; that detail rests on a single tertiary account.
- It does not name the British officer who suppressed the revolt; the "Commissioner Lawrence" lead is unverified.
- It does not repeat the tertiary-only claims that he killed a British officer related to Prime Minister Robert Peel, or that hundreds of villagers from "Gujarat" joined him.
- It does not present the Governor's "first organised armed revolt in India" description as settled historiography; it is reported as official commemoration.
- It does not resolve the age-24 arithmetic discrepancy or the Kangra-vs-Kalapani first-detention detail; both are flagged in the open items.
