## Overview

Lloyd Austin is the retired four-star general who became the nation's first Black defense secretary — and whose four years at the Pentagon were bookended by a secrecy scandal about his own health. The Defense Department's inspector general reported in January 2025 that his decision to keep his prostate-cancer hospitalization secret from the White House had created "unnecessary" security risks, though no adverse consequences resulted; officials told investigators, "We're not notifying anybody," and Austin himself texted his chief of staff from the hospital: "I don't want my health to be a media circus" ([AP via Akron News Reporter](https://www.akronnewsreporter.com/2025/01/15/pentagon-watchdog-finds-ex-defense-secretary-austins-hospitalization-secrecy-created-unnecessary-risks/)).

That ending framed a tenure the Associated Press summed up as "marred by Afghanistan but buoyed by Ukraine" — three major military crises and a global pandemic across four years, led by a man who had spent 41 years of his life in uniform ([AP via SRN News](https://srnnews.com/austin-the-first-black-defense-secretary-ends-his-term-marred-by-afghanistan-but-buoyed-by-ukraine/)).

## From Mobile to the Pentagon

Lloyd James Austin III was born on August 8, 1953, in Mobile, Alabama, to Lloyd James Austin Jr., a postal worker, and Aletia Taylor Austin, a homemaker; in high school he was captain of his varsity basketball team ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/lloyd-austin-fast-facts)). He graduated from West Point in 1975 with a bachelor's degree, later earning a master's in counselor education from Auburn University in 1986 and a master's in management and leadership from Webster University in 1989, with a stint at the Army War College in 1996–1997 ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/lloyd-austin-fast-facts)).

He commanded at the corps, division, battalion and brigade levels. He earned a Silver Star — awarded for gallantry in action — for leading troops from the front in the initial 2003 invasion of Iraq, went on to command all combat operations in Iraq as commanding general of U.S. Forces in Iraq, served as the 33rd vice chief of staff of the Army, and led U.S. Central Command, where he oversaw the military campaign against ISIS ([war.gov](https://www.war.gov/About/Biographies/Biography/Article/2473326/secretary-of-defense-lloyd-j-austin-iii/); [AP via SRN News](https://srnnews.com/austin-the-first-black-defense-secretary-ends-his-term-marred-by-afghanistan-but-buoyed-by-ukraine/)). He retired in April 2016 and joined the boards of Raytheon Technologies, Nucor and Tenet Healthcare ([war.gov](https://www.war.gov/About/Biographies/Biography/Article/2473326/secretary-of-defense-lloyd-j-austin-iii/)).

Along the way he collected a string of firsts: the first African American to lead an Army corps in combat, to command a U.S. Army division, to serve as Army vice chief of staff and to lead U.S. Central Command — before the biggest one, the Pentagon itself ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/lloyd-austin-fast-facts)). His decorations include five Defense Distinguished Service Medals, the Silver Star and two Legions of Merit ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/lloyd-austin-fast-facts)).

## A waiver, a 93-2 vote, and a 60-day reckoning

President-elect [Joe Biden](/people/joe-biden/) nominated Austin on December 8, 2020, praising in an Atlantic essay his crisis performance, understanding of civil-military relations and commitment to diversity ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/lloyd-austin-fast-facts)). Because federal law requires a seven-year cooling-off period for recently retired officers, Congress had to grant him a waiver — only the second ever, after the one given to Jim Mattis in 2017. The Senate passed it on January 21, 2021, and confirmed Austin 93-2 the next day ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/lloyd-austin-fast-facts)).

He opened his confirmation hearing with a pledge that set the tone: "We also have to rid our ranks of racists and extremists" ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/lloyd-austin-fast-facts)). On February 2, 2021, he moved on two fronts at once — purging 42 Pentagon advisory boards and, through press secretary [John Kirby](/people/john-kirby/), ordering a 60-day force-wide stand-down to confront extremism in the ranks, weeks after more than 230 people with a military background were arrested in connection with the January 6 attack on the Capitol ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/lloyd-austin-fast-facts); [AP via SRN News](https://srnnews.com/austin-the-first-black-defense-secretary-ends-his-term-marred-by-afghanistan-but-buoyed-by-ukraine/)).

Weeks later came his first military action: on February 25, 2021, Austin approved an F-15Es strike on Iranian-backed militants in Syria, the first known Biden-era military operation ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/lloyd-austin-fast-facts)). He then steered the Pentagon through a divisive COVID-19 vaccine mandate for the force, recommending it on August 25, 2021 ([AP via SRN News](https://srnnews.com/austin-the-first-black-defense-secretary-ends-his-term-marred-by-afghanistan-but-buoyed-by-ukraine/); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/lloyd-austin-fast-facts)).

## Three crises

What will define Austin's tenure is the stretch from August 2021 to the Red Sea. The U.S. withdrawal from Afghanistan had been negotiated by the previous administration, which left only 2,500 U.S. forces in the country when Biden took office. Biden's decision to proceed led to a chaotic two weeks in August 2021: the Air Force evacuated more than 124,000 people in 17 days, and a suicide bomber killed 13 U.S. service members and 170 Afghans just days before the last American left ([AP via SRN News](https://srnnews.com/austin-the-first-black-defense-secretary-ends-his-term-marred-by-afghanistan-but-buoyed-by-ukraine/)). Austin later said the world had not come to an end as many predicted, though he acknowledged "some horrible things have happened in terms of women's rights" ([AP via SRN News](https://srnnews.com/austin-the-first-black-defense-secretary-ends-his-term-marred-by-afghanistan-but-buoyed-by-ukraine/)).

Then came Ukraine. As Russia massed troops along the border, Austin created the Ukraine Defense Contact Group, a coalition of more than 50 partner nations that has sent more than $126 billion in weapons and training to Kyiv in the three years since ([AP via SRN News](https://srnnews.com/austin-the-first-black-defense-secretary-ends-his-term-marred-by-afghanistan-but-buoyed-by-ukraine/)). After Hamas launched its October 7, 2023, attacks on Israel, Austin directed two aircraft carriers to sail immediately to the region; when the conflict spilled into the Red Sea, the [U.S. Navy](/organizations/us-navy/) fought Iranian-backed Houthi drones and anti-ship ballistic missiles — and ballistic missiles fired at Israel directly by Iran — in what the AP described as the most intense running sea battle since World War II ([AP via SRN News](https://srnnews.com/austin-the-first-black-defense-secretary-ends-his-term-marred-by-afghanistan-but-buoyed-by-ukraine/)).

## The hospitalization he tried to hide

Austin is known as a deeply private man, and that desire for privacy produced the scandal of his final year. He underwent surgery for prostate cancer on December 22, 2023, and was admitted to the hospital on January 1, 2024, with complications. The next day he transferred all of his operational authorities to Deputy Secretary Kathleen Hicks — without explanation. The department did not reveal his hospitalization until January 5, and the White House was informed only on January 4 ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/lloyd-austin-fast-facts); [AP via Akron News Reporter](https://www.akronnewsreporter.com/2025/01/15/pentagon-watchdog-finds-ex-defense-secretary-austins-hospitalization-secrecy-created-unnecessary-risks/)).

The inspector general's January 15, 2025, report found the episode created "unnecessary" security risks for the department, even though no adverse consequences resulted; the Pentagon kept no record of what drove the decisions, and the White House and Congress were left in the dark for days ([AP via Akron News Reporter](https://www.akronnewsreporter.com/2025/01/15/pentagon-watchdog-finds-ex-defense-secretary-austins-hospitalization-secrecy-created-unnecessary-risks/)). The secrecy drew bipartisan congressional anger and prompted new notification processes ([AP via Akron News Reporter](https://www.akronnewsreporter.com/2025/01/15/pentagon-watchdog-finds-ex-defense-secretary-austins-hospitalization-secrecy-created-unnecessary-risks/)).

At his farewell ceremony on January 16, 2025, at Joint Base Myer-Henderson Hall, Austin told the assembled forces they were "the most lethal fighting force on Earth" — and used the occasion to warn against proposals to fire so-called "woke" generals, remove women from combat roles or bar transgender troops from service ([AP via SRN News](https://srnnews.com/austin-the-first-black-defense-secretary-ends-his-term-marred-by-afghanistan-but-buoyed-by-ukraine/)).

## After the Pentagon

Austin is married to Charlene Denise (Banner) Austin; the couple wed in the early 1980s and he is stepfather to Reginald Hill and Christopher Hill ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/lloyd-austin-fast-facts)). In June 2025, Politico reported that he was launching a management consulting firm, Clarion Strategies, with former U.S. permanent representative to NATO Julianne Smith and former assistant secretary for legislative affairs Rheanne Wirkkala ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/lloyd-austin-fast-facts)).
