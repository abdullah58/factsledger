## Overview

Marc Greenberg is an American finance executive who spent 16 years in finance and strategy leadership at Pixar, served as chief financial officer of mortgage-technology company [Blend](/organizations/blend/) through its 2021 initial public offering and the 2022 collapse in mortgage refinancing, and has been chief financial officer of Altruist, a custodian for independent financial advisers, since June 2023 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230608005187/en/Altruist-Hires-Former-Blend-CFO-to-Charter-Path-to-Profitability)).

At Blend, Greenberg's formal title was Head of Finance — he was also the company's principal financial officer, according to the [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](/organizations/us-securities-and-exchange-commission/) — although company releases describe him as its chief financial officer ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1855747/000185574725000017/R20.htm); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230608005187/en/Altruist-Hires-Former-Blend-CFO-to-Charter-Path-to-Profitability)). He joined Blend in 2018, helped scale it from 300 to more than 2,000 full-time employees and grow revenue to more than $300 million a year, and led it to its 2021 listing ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230608005187/en/Altruist-Hires-Former-Blend-CFO-to-Charter-Path-to-Profitability); [Mount Tamalpais College](https://www.mttamcollege.edu/meet-the-newest-members-of-mtcs-board-of-trustees/)). When rising interest rates crushed refinancing in 2022, he became the public face of Blend's retrenchment, announcing successive rounds of layoffs and spending cuts on earnings calls ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-reports-133-98-million-loss-in-q3-layoffs-of-100-positions/)). He left Blend in spring 2023 and joined Altruist two months later ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1855747/000185574725000017/R20.htm); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230608005187/en/Altruist-Hires-Former-Blend-CFO-to-Charter-Path-to-Profitability)).

## From Ernst & Young to Pixar

Greenberg began his career in public accounting at Ernst & Young, working from the firm's [Baltimore](/places/baltimore/) office and serving technology companies on the East Coast ([Mount Tamalpais College](https://www.mttamcollege.edu/meet-the-newest-members-of-mtcs-board-of-trustees/)). He drove back and forth between Baltimore and Washington, D.C., then two hotbeds of startup activity, and gradually moved deeper into the technology niche ([Stampli](https://www.stampli.com/blog/podcast/leaders-of-modern-finance-ep39/)). In the late 1990s he moved to California, joining a company called Savi Software before moving to Pixar, then an up-and-coming animation studio that straddled entertainment and software development ([CandorIQ](https://www.candoriq.com/webinar/the-hr-finance-collaboration-building-great-workplaces-w-business-impact); [Stampli](https://www.stampli.com/blog/podcast/leaders-of-modern-finance-ep39/)). At Pixar he worked first in corporate accounting, then in financial planning and analysis, then as controller on the film side, spending his last seven years there as divisional chief financial officer ([CandorIQ](https://www.candoriq.com/webinar/the-hr-finance-collaboration-building-great-workplaces-w-business-impact)). Although officially a financial controller, he also worked on Pixar's software development and technology innovation, and took an interest in the entertainment side of the business, drawn to Pixar's approach to storytelling ([Stampli](https://www.stampli.com/blog/podcast/leaders-of-modern-finance-ep39/)). His 16 years at Pixar ended when he sought new experiences and moved to Blend ([Mount Tamalpais College](https://www.mttamcollege.edu/meet-the-newest-members-of-mtcs-board-of-trustees/)).

## Blend: growth, IPO and the refinance collapse

Greenberg joined Blend Labs, a [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) company whose software helps banks and mortgage lenders take borrowers from application to closing, and served as its chief financial officer from 2018 through 2023 ([Mount Tamalpais College](https://www.mttamcollege.edu/meet-the-newest-members-of-mtcs-board-of-trustees/)). During the mortgage boom he helped scale the company from 300 to more than 2,000 full-time employees and grow revenue to more than $300 million annually, and led it through its initial public offering in 2021, which one trade publication put at $360 million ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230608005187/en/Altruist-Hires-Former-Blend-CFO-to-Charter-Path-to-Profitability); [Connect Money](https://www.connectmoney.com/?p=6527)). Blend also bought [Title365](/organizations/title365/), a national title insurance and settlement services provider, from [Mr. Cooper](/organizations/mr-cooper-group/) in 2021 for $422 million ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/04/21/blend-labs-laying-off-200-employees-as-mortgage-refinancings-plummet/); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-reports-133-98-million-loss-in-q3-layoffs-of-100-positions/)). That bet turned against the company when interest rates rose and refinancing volumes collapsed, taking the title business down with them. Greenberg put it plainly on the company's last earnings call, as reported by [Inman](/organizations/inman/): "In our legacy title business, the transaction volume was driven heavily by refinance and obviously, that's been negatively affected in a significant way" ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/04/21/blend-labs-laying-off-200-employees-as-mortgage-refinancings-plummet/)).

The rounds are recorded in Blend's filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1855747/000185574725000017/R20.htm)). In the third quarter of 2022 Blend reported a $133.98 million loss, with Title365 segment revenue down 65 percent year over year to $19.3 million and a $57.9 million impairment that wrote off the unit's intangible assets and goodwill in full ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-reports-133-98-million-loss-in-q3-layoffs-of-100-positions/)). Greenberg told analysts the November cuts reflected "anticipated lower origination and refi volume for the rest of the year and into 2023", and said Blend had cut more than 500 positions including backfills since early 2022, expected to yield $69 million in annual savings ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-reports-133-98-million-loss-in-q3-layoffs-of-100-positions/)). He also held to the goal of cutting the quarterly non-GAAP net operating loss by 50 percent by the end of 2023 ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-reports-133-98-million-loss-in-q3-layoffs-of-100-positions/)).

## Leaving Blend

Blend announced on 10 January 2023 that Greenberg would leave his role as head of finance in the first quarter, alongside head of legal Crystal Sumner, as part of a fourth round of layoffs cutting about 340 jobs — 28 percent of its [United States](/places/united-states/) workforce — expected to save more than $100 million a year ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-cuts-workforce-shakes-up-leadership/)). Amir Jafari, a former chief financial officer of Reputation.com and Plastiq, was named to a new role as head of finance and administration ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-cuts-workforce-shakes-up-leadership/)). The formal break came a little later than the announcement suggested: Greenberg stepped down as Head of Finance and principal financial officer effective 16 March 2023, and his last day of employment was 3 April 2023 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1855747/000185574725000017/R20.htm)). Under a retention agreement, he received a $0.9 million cash bonus for staying through 31 March 2023, plus a severance package including nine weeks of severance ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1855747/000185574725000017/R20.htm)). His departure closed a five-year run that had taken Blend from a fast-growing private fintech to a listed company and then through the sharpest mortgage downturn in years ([CandorIQ](https://www.candoriq.com/webinar/the-hr-finance-collaboration-building-great-workplaces-w-business-impact); [Mount Tamalpais College](https://www.mttamcollege.edu/meet-the-newest-members-of-mtcs-board-of-trustees/)).

## Chief financial officer at Altruist

Two months after leaving Blend, Greenberg was appointed chief financial officer of Altruist, a [Los Angeles](/places/los-angeles/) company that combines a self-clearing custodian with digital advisory software for independent registered investment advisers ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230608005187/en/Altruist-Hires-Former-Blend-CFO-to-Charter-Path-to-Profitability)). He filled a vacancy left by Grace Mellis, who had been Altruist's first chief financial officer, hired in 2021, and departed in June 2022 ([Connect Money](https://www.connectmoney.com/?p=6527)). Altruist was growing fast when he arrived: it had tripled assets under management in 2022, posted revenue growth of more than 1,700 percent year on year, announced a $112 million Series D, and acquired the custodial platform Shareholders Service Group, bringing 1,600 advisers aboard ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230608005187/en/Altruist-Hires-Former-Blend-CFO-to-Charter-Path-to-Profitability); [Connect Money](https://www.connectmoney.com/?p=6527)). In March 2023 it had ended its relationship with Apex Fintech Solutions to run its own custody and clearing platform ([Connect Money](https://www.connectmoney.com/?p=6527)). Greenberg describes Altruist as "Shopify for financial advisors" — infrastructure that lets an adviser run a practice, from performance reporting and billing to trading equities ([CandorIQ](https://www.candoriq.com/webinar/the-hr-finance-collaboration-building-great-workplaces-w-business-impact)). The company had about 450 employees, based in Culver City in Los Angeles, with its broker-dealer in Dallas ([CandorIQ](https://www.candoriq.com/webinar/the-hr-finance-collaboration-building-great-workplaces-w-business-impact)).

## How Greenberg runs finance

Greenberg describes the chief financial officer's job as resource allocation: deciding how to deploy people and money to build products, solve problems and create efficiencies, and building a company and a culture to be proud of ([CandorIQ](https://www.candoriq.com/webinar/the-hr-finance-collaboration-building-great-workplaces-w-business-impact)). His operating phrases are "context over control" — giving staff the information to make decisions themselves — and transparency about goals, metrics and trade-offs, including sharing financial dashboards with the whole company ([CandorIQ](https://www.candoriq.com/webinar/the-hr-finance-collaboration-building-great-workplaces-w-business-impact)). The people side is not an afterthought: he was interim head of people at Blend during the pandemic, and says the experience made him sympathetic to the partnership between finance and human resources ([CandorIQ](https://www.candoriq.com/webinar/the-hr-finance-collaboration-building-great-workplaces-w-business-impact)). At Blend he built a deliberately flexible virtual office, with no mandatory hours, project- and task-based productivity, company office space, coworking memberships and weekly check-ins between managers and staff ([Stampli](https://www.stampli.com/blog/podcast/leaders-of-modern-finance-ep39/)). He prioritised his team's needs over profit-and-loss statements, trusting that the numbers would follow if the workers were looked after ([Stampli](https://www.stampli.com/blog/podcast/leaders-of-modern-finance-ep39/)). At Altruist he tracks a mix of financial and people measures — revenue, net profit and earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, alongside assets under management, adviser numbers, engagement and turnover — and shares them openly ([CandorIQ](https://www.candoriq.com/webinar/the-hr-finance-collaboration-building-great-workplaces-w-business-impact)).

## Boards and community roles

Outside his executive roles, Greenberg has served on nonprofit and university boards. In August 2025 he was appointed to the Board of Trustees of Mount Tamalpais College, a college in the San Francisco Bay Area ([Mount Tamalpais College](https://www.mttamcollege.edu/meet-the-newest-members-of-mtcs-board-of-trustees/)). He is a member, former chair and former treasurer of the board of We Care Services for Children, a nonprofit that supports children with special needs ([Mount Tamalpais College](https://www.mttamcollege.edu/meet-the-newest-members-of-mtcs-board-of-trustees/)). He has also served on the board of advisors of the Robert H. Smith School of Business at the University of Maryland and sits on the board of trustees of the University of Maryland College Park Foundation ([Mount Tamalpais College](https://www.mttamcollege.edu/meet-the-newest-members-of-mtcs-board-of-trustees/)).
