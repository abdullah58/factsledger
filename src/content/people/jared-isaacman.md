## Overview

Jared Isaacman — often billed as a billionaire astronaut — reached the top of NASA by a route no predecessor took, and then survived a nomination process with no close precedent: tapped by Donald Trump in December 2024, withdrawn in May 2025 in the middle of Trump's public rift with [Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk/), then renominated on November 4, 2025, and confirmed by the Senate 67-30 on December 17, 2025 ([Astronomy](https://www.astronomy.com/science/senate-confirms-isaacman-as-nasa-administrator/); [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/people/jared-isaacman/)). Sixteen Democrats joined 51 Republicans in voting yes; all 30 votes against came from Democrats ([Reuters via SRN](https://srnnews.com/us-senate-confirms-private-astronaut-musk-ally-jared-isaacman-as-nasa-chief/)). He took charge of an agency of roughly 14,000 employees that had just lost about a fifth of its workforce and faced a proposed budget cut of roughly a quarter, with a mandate to beat China back to the Moon this decade ([Reuters via SRN](https://srnnews.com/us-senate-confirms-private-astronaut-musk-ally-jared-isaacman-as-nasa-chief/)).

The career behind him reads like two different lives stitched together. A teenager who dropped out of high school to start a payments company in his parents' basement, then used the fortune to buy his way into orbit — and turned both of his missions into fundraisers, with Inspiration4 alone raising more than $250 million for St. Jude Children's Research Hospital ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/people/jared-isaacman/)).

## The payments dropout and the fighter-jet fleet

Isaacman grew up in New Jersey as the youngest of four children of Donald and Sandra Marie Isaacman ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/people/jared-isaacman/)). At 16 he left high school and founded United Bank Card, a payment-processing company, in his parents' basement; the company was later renamed Shift4 ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/people/jared-isaacman/); [The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/world/2024-09-12-jared-isaacman-the-billionaire-behind-historic-spacewalk)). Shift4 went public in 2020, and Isaacman left his role as CEO in 2025 ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/people/jared-isaacman/)). The company handles payments for a third of restaurants and hotels in the United States — Hilton, Four Seasons, KFC and Arby's among them — according to Forbes reporting carried by The Star ([The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/world/2024-09-12-jared-isaacman-the-billionaire-behind-historic-spacewalk)).

Flying was the parallel obsession. He started pilot lessons in 2004, eventually logging more than 8,000 flight hours, qualifying on multiple military jets, and flying in more than 100 airshows ([The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/world/2024-09-12-jared-isaacman-the-billionaire-behind-historic-spacewalk); [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/people/jared-isaacman/)). In 2009 he circumnavigated the globe in a light jet — a Cessna Citation CJ2 — in 61 hours, 51 minutes and 15 seconds, setting world speed records while raising money for Make-A-Wish ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/people/jared-isaacman/)). In 2011 he co-founded Draken International, which trains U.S. military pilots and operates one of the world's largest private fleets of fighter jets; he sold the company in 2019, a majority stake going to Blackstone for a nine-figure sum ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/people/jared-isaacman/); [The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/world/2024-09-12-jared-isaacman-the-billionaire-behind-historic-spacewalk)). He earned a bachelor's degree in Aeronautics from Embry-Riddle in 2011 and received an honorary doctorate from the university in 2024 ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/people/jared-isaacman/)).

## Buying his way to orbit — and raising a quarter-billion for St. Jude

Time magazine estimated that Isaacman paid $200 million for all four seats aboard the SpaceX craft that flew Inspiration4 ([The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/world/2024-09-12-jared-isaacman-the-billionaire-behind-historic-spacewalk)). The September 2021 mission was the first all-civilian orbital spaceflight, aboard SpaceX's Dragon; the three-day flight orbited Earth and raised over $250 million for St. Jude ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/people/jared-isaacman/); [The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/world/2024-09-12-jared-isaacman-the-billionaire-behind-historic-spacewalk)). "That was a heck of a ride for us," he radioed after the splashdown in the Atlantic. "We're just getting started" ([The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/world/2024-09-12-jared-isaacman-the-billionaire-behind-historic-spacewalk)).

Three years later he led Polaris Dawn, again bankrolling and commanding the flight. The mission reached an altitude of almost 900 miles — the farthest humans had traveled from Earth since Apollo 17 — and Isaacman became the first non-professional astronaut to perform a spacewalk, in the mission's first commercial EVA ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/people/jared-isaacman/); [The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/world/2024-09-12-jared-isaacman-the-billionaire-behind-historic-spacewalk)). Stepping out of the capsule he radioed, "Back at home we all have a lot of work to do. But from here Earth sure looks like a perfect world" ([The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/world/2024-09-12-jared-isaacman-the-billionaire-behind-historic-spacewalk)). Both missions incorporated dozens of science experiments aimed at advancing crewed spaceflight ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/people/jared-isaacman/)).

## The nomination that died and came back

Trump first tapped Isaacman for the NASA job in late 2024, but in May 2025 abruptly withdrew the nomination following the public rift with Musk, citing Isaacman's previous affiliations with Democratic politicians ([Astronomy](https://www.astronomy.com/science/senate-confirms-isaacman-as-nasa-administrator/)). Musk had advocated for Isaacman's nomination when Trump was elected in 2024, and Isaacman faced confirmation-hearing questions over his closeness to Musk, whose company holds about $15 billion in NASA contracts ([Reuters via SRN](https://srnnews.com/us-senate-confirms-private-astronaut-musk-ally-jared-isaacman-as-nasa-chief/)).

During the leadership gap, Trump appointed Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy as acting administrator while Duffy kept running the Department of Transportation ([Astronomy](https://www.astronomy.com/science/senate-confirms-isaacman-as-nasa-administrator/)). On November 4, 2025, Trump renominated Isaacman, posting on Truth Social that he was well-suited to lead NASA into a "bold new era" ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/people/jared-isaacman/); [Astronomy](https://www.astronomy.com/science/senate-confirms-isaacman-as-nasa-administrator/)). Isaacman's vision for that era had already leaked ahead of the vote in "Project Athena," a 62-page document he had prepared during his first nomination period in May: a commercial-first strategy that could eliminate legacy programs like the Space Launch System. Isaacman called it a "living" draft with outdated parts; one former NASA official called it "bizarre and careless," according to Politico ([Astronomy](https://www.astronomy.com/science/senate-confirms-isaacman-as-nasa-administrator/)). On December 1, a coalition of 36 former NASA astronauts — including former administrator Charlie Bolden — signed a letter endorsing Isaacman, citing his flight experience ([Astronomy](https://www.astronomy.com/science/senate-confirms-isaacman-as-nasa-administrator/)). Two weeks later the Senate confirmed him ([Reuters via SRN](https://srnnews.com/us-senate-confirms-private-astronaut-musk-ally-jared-isaacman-as-nasa-chief/)).

## Running NASA: a moon base and the Starliner rescue

Earlier in 2026, Isaacman announced plans to build a "moon base," fulfilling a Congressional mandate to establish a permanent presence in the Moon's orbit or on its surface ([CW7](https://cw7az.com/news/investing-in-america-nasa-administrator-jared-isaacman-on-first-steps-for-moon-base/)). He had hoped to launch parts of it in 2026, but an explosion of [Blue Origin's](/organizations/blue-origin/) New Glenn rocket delayed some deliveries ([CW7](https://cw7az.com/news/investing-in-america-nasa-administrator-jared-isaacman-on-first-steps-for-moon-base/)). He aimed instead at 2027 for a monthly cadence of landers and rovers on the lunar south pole — rovers, drones and experiments dropped ahead of the astronauts, so that by Artemis IV they would find infrastructure and rovers waiting for them ([CW7](https://cw7az.com/news/investing-in-america-nasa-administrator-jared-isaacman-on-first-steps-for-moon-base/)). The base, he said, would teach NASA how to live away from Earth — and later help it get to Mars ([CW7](https://cw7az.com/news/investing-in-america-nasa-administrator-jared-isaacman-on-first-steps-for-moon-base/)).

On September 28, 2026, Isaacman stood with NASA's Low Earth Orbit Program manager Dana Weigel at Kennedy Space Center to announce new missions for Boeing's Starliner spacecraft — the first since the capsule's botched 2024 test flight stranded astronauts Suni Williams and Butch Wilmore at the ISS for nine months ([AFP via CGTN](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-09-29/NASA-announces-new-missions-for-Boeing-s-Starliner-spacecraft-1QPeUM64FP2/p.html)). An uncrewed Starliner mission would fly by December at the earliest "to validate the recent remediation effort," followed by a crewed Starliner Two mission in 2028, Isaacman told reporters ([AFP via CGTN](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-09-29/NASA-announces-new-missions-for-Boeing-s-Starliner-spacecraft-1QPeUM64FP2/p.html)). Getting a crewed Starliner to work matters because SpaceX has said it plans to end production of its reusable Crew Dragon capsules, the only other American spacecraft carrying astronauts to the ISS since 2020 ([AFP via CGTN](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-09-29/NASA-announces-new-missions-for-Boeing-s-Starliner-spacecraft-1QPeUM64FP2/p.html)).

Isaacman resides in Pennsylvania with his wife, Monica, and their two daughters ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/people/jared-isaacman/)).
