## Overview

Pedro Franceschi is a Brazilian entrepreneur and the co-founder and chief executive of [Brex](/organizations/brex/), the San Francisco corporate-card fintech he built with [Henrique Dubugras](/people/henrique-dubugras/) after the pair sold their teenage Brazilian payments company, [Pagar.me](/organizations/pagar-me/), to Stone in 2016. A Stanford dropout and [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/) alumnus, he ran Brex as co-CEO from 2017, became its sole CEO in June 2024, and still leads it inside Capital One, which acquired Brex for $5.15 billion in 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/); [Capital One](https://www.capitalone.com/about/newsroom/capital-one-completes-acquisition-of-brex/)).

What sets Franceschi apart from the usual founder-CEO story is the division of labour behind it. While Dubugras was the external face — fundraising, banking partners, regulators — Franceschi was the internal builder: he built Brex's core financial infrastructure from scratch and led the entire organisation for six years ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/12/fintech-brex-abandons-co-ceo-model-talks-ipo-cash-burn-and-plans-for-a-secondary-sale/)). That builder's record, capped by the 2024–2025 turnaround he led as sole CEO, is the substance of this profile.

## Teenage hacker to payments founder

Franceschi grew up in Rio de Janeiro, [Brazil](/places/brazil/), interested in his parents' Mac computers from the age of six and learning to program at eight ([Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/english/twice-burnout-before-26-the-co-ceo-of-12b-brex-tells-how-he-regained-balance/)). By 13 he was speaking at a TEDx event, and his early iPhone jailbreaking drew a legal threat from Apple — an episode his mother, like Dubugras's, had tried to stop ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/05/how-the-22-year-old-founders-of-brex-built-a-billion-dollar-business-in-less-than-2-years/); [Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/english/twice-burnout-before-26-the-co-ceo-of-12b-brex-tells-how-he-regained-balance/)). At 16 he met Dubugras, a teenager from São Paulo who shared his appetite for coding, and the two started Pagar.me, a Brazilian online payments processor they pitched as the "Stripe of Brazil" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/05/how-the-22-year-old-founders-of-brex-built-a-billion-dollar-business-in-less-than-2-years/)).

Pagar.me was a real business before either founder reached college: it raised $30 million, grew to a staff of 100, and was processing up to $1.5 billion in transactions when it sold to Stone in 2016 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/05/how-the-22-year-old-founders-of-brex-built-a-billion-dollar-business-in-less-than-2-years/)). The pair then enrolled at [Stanford University](/universities/stanford-university/) in the fall of 2016, but left within months for Y Combinator's Winter 2017 cohort ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/05/how-the-22-year-old-founders-of-brex-built-a-billion-dollar-business-in-less-than-2-years/)).

## Building Brex: the internal co-CEO

The pair entered Y Combinator pitching a virtual-reality startup called Beyond, abandoned it after about three weeks, and started Brex in April 2017, dropping out of Stanford to run it full time ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/05/how-the-22-year-old-founders-of-brex-built-a-billion-dollar-business-in-less-than-2-years/)). The idea came from their own frustration: banks saw small businesses as a risk they would not take, so founders could not get a corporate card. Brex underwrote instead on who had invested in a startup, the equity held, cash balance and spending patterns — no personal guarantee, no deposit, no credit history ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/brex-launches-credit-card-for-startups-raises-57-million-from-y-combinator-paypal-founders-others)).

The card launched publicly in June 2018 after a private launch with some 1,000 customers, including Flexport, Algolia and [Affirm](/organizations/affirm/), backed by $57 million in announced funding — a $7 million April 2017 round from Y Combinator and Ribbit Capital, then a $50 million Series B from Y Combinator Continuity, [Peter Thiel](/people/peter-thiel/), Max Levchin, Yuri Milner and former Visa chief Carl Pascarella ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/brex-launches-credit-card-for-startups-raises-57-million-from-y-combinator-paypal-founders-others)). Four months later a $125 million Series C valued Brex at $1.1 billion, making the 22-year-olds among the youngest unicorn founders in history ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/05/how-the-22-year-old-founders-of-brex-built-a-billion-dollar-business-in-less-than-2-years/)). By January 2022 a $300 million Series D-2 had lifted the valuation to $12.3 billion, and Franceschi, then 25, was counted among the youngest Brazilian billionaires ([Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/english/twice-burnout-before-26-the-co-ceo-of-12b-brex-tells-how-he-regained-balance/)).

Inside the company, Franceschi's job was the product and the plumbing. He built the core financial infrastructure from scratch — a vertically integrated stack reaching down to the Mastercard, ACH and money-movement rails, rather than one rented from Stripe or Marqeta — and, by the company's own account, led the entire organisation over the six years to 2024, growing it to more than 30,000 customers, including over 130 publicly traded companies ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/12/fintech-brex-abandons-co-ceo-model-talks-ipo-cash-burn-and-plans-for-a-secondary-sale/)). The pace took a personal toll: he has said he went through two burnouts before turning 26, and credits therapy, meditation and an "athlete's routine" — plus deliberately building a succession culture — with regaining his balance ([Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/english/twice-burnout-before-26-the-co-ceo-of-12b-brex-tells-how-he-regained-balance/)). In March 2022, at the height of the boom, Coupang appointed him to its board of directors ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220316006153/en/)).

## Sole CEO, the Brex 3.0 turnaround, and the Capital One sale

The boom did not last. Brex laid off 136 people in October 2022 and another 282 — about 20% of staff — in January 2024, leaving around 1,000 workers, and in June 2024 the founders ended the co-CEO model they had run since inception: Franceschi became sole CEO and Dubugras board chairman ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/12/fintech-brex-abandons-co-ceo-model-talks-ipo-cash-burn-and-plans-for-a-secondary-sale/)). The stated reasons were faster decision-making at scale and a structure public investors would prefer ahead of a possible IPO; Franceschi said cash burn had already halved over the prior year, with cash-flow positivity targeted for 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/12/fintech-brex-abandons-co-ceo-model-talks-ipo-cash-burn-and-plans-for-a-secondary-sale/)).

What followed was the defining stretch of Franceschi's tenure as sole chief executive. Under the "Brex 3.0" operating model launched in January 2024 — a layoff, a near-total leadership change, an internal valuation reset, a return to office, and a rebuild of product and go-to-market — Brex grew 49% in August 2025 and recorded its first operating-cash-flow-positive month in company history ([Brex](https://www.brex.com/journal/the-turnaround-is-over)). In an October 2025 memo to staff, Franceschi declared "the turnaround is over", crediting a doctrine with no silver bullets: aggressively improving the few things that matter every day and letting compound interest do the rest ([Brex](https://www.brex.com/journal/the-turnaround-is-over)). The competitive context had also shifted — [Ramp](/organizations/ramp/), Brex's chief rival in expense management, had raised its valuation from $13 billion in March 2024 to $32 billion by November 2025, while Brex's own peak valuation was behind it ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)).

The exit came on 22 January 2026, when Capital One announced it would acquire Brex for $5.15 billion in cash and stock — less than half the $12.3 billion peak, but a roughly 700-fold return for earliest backer Ribbit Capital ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/); [Capital One](https://www.capitalone.com/about/newsroom/capital-one-to-acquire-brex/)). The deal completed on 7 April 2026, with Franceschi continuing as CEO of Brex inside Capital One ([Capital One](https://www.capitalone.com/about/newsroom/capital-one-completes-acquisition-of-brex/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is Pedro Franceschi?**
A Brazilian entrepreneur from Rio de Janeiro who co-founded the corporate-card fintech Brex with Henrique Dubugras in 2017, after the pair built and sold the Brazilian payments processor Pagar.me as teenagers. He is Brex's sole CEO since June 2024 and continues to lead it inside Capital One ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/12/fintech-brex-abandons-co-ceo-model-talks-ipo-cash-burn-and-plans-for-a-secondary-sale/); [Capital One](https://www.capitalone.com/about/newsroom/capital-one-completes-acquisition-of-brex/)).

**What was Pagar.me?**
A Brazilian online payments company Franceschi and Dubugras started as teenagers, often called the "Stripe of Brazil". It raised $30 million, grew to 100 staff and was processing up to $1.5 billion in transactions when Stone acquired it in 2016 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/05/how-the-22-year-old-founders-of-brex-built-a-billion-dollar-business-in-less-than-2-years/)).

**Why did Brex end its co-CEO model?**
The founders said two chief executives slowed decisions as the company grew, and that public investors would prefer a single CEO ahead of a possible IPO. Franceschi, the internal co-CEO who built the financial infrastructure, became sole CEO; Dubugras became chairman ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/12/fintech-brex-abandons-co-ceo-model-talks-ipo-cash-burn-and-plans-for-a-secondary-sale/)).

**What is Brex 3.0?**
The operating model Franceschi launched in January 2024: a layoff, leadership overhaul, valuation reset, return to office, and a rebuild of product and sales. By August 2025 Brex had grown 49% and turned operating cash-flow positive for the first time, which Franceschi called the end of the turnaround ([Brex](https://www.brex.com/journal/the-turnaround-is-over)).

**Does Franceschi still run Brex after the Capital One deal?**
Yes. Capital One's announcement said he would continue to lead Brex, and its completion release on 7 April 2026 confirmed "Franceschi will continue as CEO of Brex" ([Capital One](https://www.capitalone.com/about/newsroom/capital-one-to-acquire-brex/); [Capital One](https://www.capitalone.com/about/newsroom/capital-one-completes-acquisition-of-brex/)).
