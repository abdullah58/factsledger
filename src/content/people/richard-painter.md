## Overview

Richard Painter is an American lawyer and law professor whose path ran from corporate-securities scholarship through the White House ethics office and into electoral politics. He holds the S. Walter Richey Professor of Corporate Law chair at the University of Minnesota Law School and served as the chief White House ethics lawyer under President George W. Bush from February 2005 to July 2007 ([U.S. Congress](https://www.congress.gov/115/meeting/house/106221/witnesses/HHRG-115-PW13-Bio-PainterR-20170712.pdf)).

## The scholar behind a corporate-fraud law

Before he became known as an ethics commentator, Painter made a lasting mark on federal corporate law. Section 307 of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002 — which directed the Securities and Exchange Commission to write rules requiring securities lawyers to report violations up the ladder to client boards of directors — "is based on a proposal made in an April 2002 letter to SEC Chairman Harvey Pitt" and on Painter's earlier law-review article, "Lawyer Disclosure of Corporate Fraud: Establishing a Firm Foundation," published in the 1996 SMU Law Review ([U.S. Congress](https://www.congress.gov/115/meeting/house/106221/witnesses/HHRG-115-PW13-Bio-PainterR-20170712.pdf)). The provision drew bipartisan sponsorship in the Senate from John Edwards, Mike Enzi, and John Corzine ([U.S. Congress](https://www.congress.gov/115/meeting/house/106221/witnesses/HHRG-115-PW13-Bio-PainterR-20170712.pdf)).

His academic foundation was unusually credentialed for an ethics specialist. He graduated from Harvard College in 1984 with a B.A. summa cum laude in history, earned his J.D. from Yale Law School in 1987, and clerked for Judge John T. Noonan Jr. of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the [Ninth Circuit](https://factsledger.com/organizations/ninth-circuit/) in 1987–88 ([U.S. Congress](https://www.congress.gov/115/meeting/house/106221/witnesses/HHRG-115-PW13-Bio-PainterR-20170712.pdf)). He then practiced at Sullivan & Cromwell in New York (1988–1991) and Finn Dixon & Herling in Stamford, Connecticut (1991–1993), before entering academia at the University of Oregon and later the University of Illinois College of Law ([U.S. Congress](https://www.congress.gov/115/meeting/house/106221/witnesses/HHRG-115-PW13-Bio-PainterR-20170712.pdf)). He has also served as an associate reporter for the American Law Institute's Principles of Government Ethics project since 2014 ([U.S. Congress](https://www.congress.gov/115/meeting/house/106221/witnesses/HHRG-115-PW13-Bio-PainterR-20170712.pdf)).

## Chief ethics lawyer in the Bush White House

In February 2005 Painter joined the Bush White House as Associate Counsel to the President — a commissioned officer in the White House Counsel's Office — and became the chief ethics lawyer for the president, White House staff, and the president's nominees to Senate-confirmed executive branch positions ([U.S. Congress](https://www.congress.gov/115/meeting/house/106221/witnesses/HHRG-115-PW13-Bio-PainterR-20170712.pdf)). He headed a four-to-six-lawyer ethics office, ran ethics screening of Supreme Court nominees and preparation for confirmation, served as the liaison between the White House and the Office of Government Ethics, and held a top-secret security clearance ([U.S. Congress](https://www.congress.gov/115/meeting/house/106221/witnesses/HHRG-115-PW13-Bio-PainterR-20170712.pdf)). He left the post in July 2007 ([U.S. Congress](https://www.congress.gov/115/meeting/house/106221/witnesses/HHRG-115-PW13-Bio-PainterR-20170712.pdf)).

Decades later he still cited that experience to describe how gift rules worked in practice. In a November 2025 interview he recalled that in the Bush White House, gifts to the president were brought to his attention and "only in very, very rare circumstances did the president accept the gift," noting that Congress caps personal-capacity foreign gifts to the president at $480 ([NPR/KNKX](https://www.knkx.org/2025-11-14/do-foreign-gifts-to-trump-that-align-with-policy-changes-raise-ethical-concerns)).

## Trump-era critic, CREW vice-chair, and the emoluments fight

Painter's turn toward public criticism of [Donald Trump](https://factsledger.com/people/donald-trump/) began almost immediately after the 2016 election. In December 2016 he co-authored "The Emoluments Clause: Its Text, Meaning and Application to Donald J. Trump" with Norman Eisen and Laurence H. Tribe for the Brookings Institution's Governance Studies paper series ([U.S. Congress](https://www.congress.gov/115/meeting/house/106221/witnesses/HHRG-115-PW13-Bio-PainterR-20170712.pdf)). He became vice-chair of Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington (CREW), the watchdog group that pursued emoluments litigation against Trump ([UPenn Fels Institute of Government](https://www.fels.upenn.edu/news-events/public-policy-practice-richard-painter)).

By 2018 he was a fixture of cable news — the Associated Press described him as making "frequent appearances on CNN, MSNBC and other cable TV networks" as a visible Trump critic ([Associated Press](https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/prominent-trump-critic-painter-enters-minnesota-senate-race)). His earlier books included "Getting the Government America Deserves: How Ethics Reform Can Make a Difference" (Oxford University Press, 2009) and "Taxation Only with Representation: The Conservative Conscience and Campaign Finance Reform" (2016) ([U.S. Congress](https://www.congress.gov/115/meeting/house/106221/witnesses/HHRG-115-PW13-Bio-PainterR-20170712.pdf)), and he later co-authored "American Nero: The History of the Destruction of the Rule of Law, and Why Trump Is the Worst Offender" with Peter Golenbock (BenBella Books) ([Simon & Schuster](https://www.simonandschuster.net/books/American-Nero/Richard-Painter/9781948836012)).

## Two runs for office as a Democrat

In April 2018 Painter announced he was leaving the Republican Party and entering the special election for Al Franken's former Minnesota Senate seat as a Democrat, challenging appointed Senator Tina Smith in the August DFL primary ([Associated Press](https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/prominent-trump-critic-painter-enters-minnesota-senate-race)). He said opposition to Trump was the campaign's motivation: "I'm running against Donald Trump and every one of his collaborators in the Republican Party. I'm going to put my country first. This isn't about party" ([Associated Press](https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/prominent-trump-critic-painter-enters-minnesota-senate-race)). He lost the primary to Smith, said the campaign had drawn national attention to ethics issues and single-payer health care, and returned to teaching ([Star Tribune](https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/sen-tina-smith-holds-off-richard-painter-will-face-karin-housley-in-historic-matchup)).

Four years later he ran again as a DFL candidate in the May 24, 2022 special primary for Minnesota's 1st Congressional District, the seat left vacant by the death of Rep. Jim Hagedorn ([KAUS Radio](https://www.myuscountry.com/absentee-voting-has-started-for-special-primary-election-for-vacant-minnesota-u-s-1st-congressional-district-seat/)).

## Ethics voice in the second Trump term

Painter has remained a go-to ethics commentator during Trump's second presidency. In November 2025 he told NPR he viewed gifts to Trump from Swiss executives — a Rolex desk clock and an engraved gold bar, reported amid a Swiss tariff cut — as inappropriate, saying "it appears as if there's a quid pro quo of a gift from foreign interests to the president in return for a concession on tariffs," and warning that accepting such gifts from foreign private interests, while "not technically prohibited" under the Emoluments Clause, sets a "very, very dangerous precedent" ([NPR/KNKX](https://www.knkx.org/2025-11-14/do-foreign-gifts-to-trump-that-align-with-policy-changes-raise-ethical-concerns)).

In September 2026 he was quoted by the Associated Press on Attorney General [Todd Blanche](/people/todd-blanche/)'s speeches at Republican political events, saying: "It lends credence to the arguments that the [Justice Department](https://factsledger.com/organizations/us-department-of-justice/) is hopelessly politicized. And that's not where we want to be right now" ([Associated Press](https://www.reflector.com/news/national/attorney-general-blanches-appearances-at-political-events-blur-justice-department-boundaries/article_3ad55000-669b-5f0c-aeb9-87d91030f8f8.html)). The AP report noted that attorneys general have typically avoided political rallies to protect the perception of impartiality in prosecutions, while the Justice Department said Blanche's appearances were reviewed by its ethics officials and made in his personal capacity ([Associated Press](https://www.reflector.com/news/national/attorney-general-blanches-appearances-at-political-events-blur-justice-department-boundaries/article_3ad55000-669b-5f0c-aeb9-87d91030f8f8.html)).
