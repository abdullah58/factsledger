Florin Costin Șoavă's playing career is a matter of public record — 22 caps for Romania, a league title with Rapid București, four years at Spartak Moscow — but what no English-language profile tells is the second act: at 47, after his last senior coaching job in 2013, he came home to Mehedinți County to build a brand-new fourth-tier club from nothing, and won promotion in its first season ([iAMsport.ro](https://iamsport.ro/fotbal/special/renasterea-lui-florin-soava-face-spectacol-in-liga-4-dar-viseaza-la-superliga-orice-antrenor-isi-doreste-pe-cine-vede-favorita-la-titlu-cei-de-la-fcsb-s-au-crezut-zmei-id42743.html), [GOLAZO.ro](https://golazo.ro/florin-soava-liga-4-antrenor-drobeta-turnu-severin-145667)).

## The comeback at Drobeta

Șoavă was named the first head coach of [CS Drobeta-Turnu Severin](/organizations/cs-drobeta/) in the summer of 2025, a newly founded municipal club with promotion to Liga III as its stated objective; the club presented him as a former Romanian international and a native of Mehedinți County ([Sport.ro](https://www.sport.ro/fotbal-intern/sunt-doua-echipe-cu-acelasi-nume-chiar-si-la-drobeta-turnu-severin-una-dintre-ele-este-antrenata-de-florin-soava.html)). He won his debut on the bench, a 2–0 friendly against Sănătatea Breznița Ocol at the Municipal stadium, with goals from Iorgulescu and Brucă ([GOLAZO.ro](https://golazo.ro/florin-soava-liga-4-antrenor-drobeta-turnu-severin-145667)).

In a mid-season interview, Șoavă described the scale of the rebuild: on 1 August he had no players at all; the championship began on 10 September; the squad was assembled in a few weeks, built almost entirely around young players from the Oltenia region, supplemented with experienced heads ([iAMsport.ro](https://iamsport.ro/fotbal/special/renasterea-lui-florin-soava-face-spectacol-in-liga-4-dar-viseaza-la-superliga-orice-antrenor-isi-doreste-pe-cine-vede-favorita-la-titlu-cei-de-la-fcsb-s-au-crezut-zmei-id42743.html)). After 12 rounds the record was near-perfect: first place, 11 wins, one draw, 78 goals scored and only 9 conceded ([iAMsport.ro](https://iamsport.ro/fotbal/special/renasterea-lui-florin-soava-face-spectacol-in-liga-4-dar-viseaza-la-superliga-orice-antrenor-isi-doreste-pe-cine-vede-favorita-la-titlu-cei-de-la-fcsb-s-au-crezut-zmei-id42743.html)).

He framed the project in three stages: promotion in the first year, a competitive Liga III side in the second, then the play-off and Liga II in year two — while admitting every coach dreams of one day taking a Liga I bench. His UEFA A licence qualifies him to be an assistant in the SuperLigă and a head coach in Liga II, and he described the working conditions as above the competition's level: the Municipal ground homologated for SuperLigă, an excellent training pitch at SeverNav, and a synthetic pitch in the works ([iAMsport.ro](https://iamsport.ro/fotbal/special/renasterea-lui-florin-soava-face-spectacol-in-liga-4-dar-viseaza-la-superliga-orice-antrenor-isi-doreste-pe-cine-vede-favorita-la-titlu-cei-de-la-fcsb-s-au-crezut-zmei-id42743.html)).

The plan held. Reporting at the start of June 2026 had Drobeta at 24 wins and 1 draw with a 168–15 goal difference, already crowned Mehedinți county (Liga IV) champions ([ProSport](https://liga2.prosport.ro/liga-4/cs-drobeta-este-noua-campioana-judeteana-de-la-mehedinti-19389737)). The club completed the county league-and-cup double and earned promotion to Liga III through the play-off in late June 2026. With promotion sealed, the club changed head coach: Transfermarkt lists Flavius Stoican as manager from 16 July 2026, succeeding debut-season coach Șoavă ([Transfermarkt](https://www.transfermarkt.co.in/florin-soava/profil/spieler/29253/sort/matches)).

## Playing career

Born 24 July 1978 in Gârla Mare, Romania, Șoavă is a 1.88 m defender who played primarily as a centre-back, with left-back and defensive midfield as alternate roles ([Transfermarkt](https://www.transfermarkt.co.in/florin-soava/profil/spieler/29253/sort/matches); [ESPN](https://www.espn.ph/football/player/bio/_/id/19117/florin-soava)). He came through the youth system of Electroputere Craiova and began his senior career there in 1996, moving to Extensiv Craiova and then Universitatea Craiova before joining Rapid București in 2001 ([national-football-teams.com](https://www.national-football-teams.com/old/player/5400/Florin_Soava.html?countrysort=score.goals&countrydirection=asc&countrypage=1&clubsort=score.goals&clubdirection=desc&clubpage=1)).

At Rapid he played 63 league matches across three seasons and won the Divizia A title in 2002–03, when the club finished first ([national-football-teams.com](https://www.national-football-teams.com/old/player/5400/Florin_Soava.html?countrysort=score.goals&countrydirection=asc&countrypage=1&clubsort=score.goals&clubdirection=desc&clubpage=1)). In 2004 he moved to Spartak Moscow, where he spent four seasons in Russia's Premier Liga — 52 league appearances and one goal — with a 2005–06 loan spell at Krylia Sovetov (26 appearances, 2 goals) ([national-football-teams.com](https://www.national-football-teams.com/old/player/5400/Florin_Soava.html?countrysort=score.goals&countrydirection=asc&countrypage=1&clubsort=score.goals&clubdirection=desc&clubpage=1)). A short 2008 spell at Khimki (14 appearances, 1 goal) preceded a return to Universitatea Craiova in 2009, and he finished his career at Arsenal Kyiv in Ukraine from 2010 to 2012, retiring on 1 July 2013 ([national-football-teams.com](https://www.national-football-teams.com/old/player/5400/Florin_Soava.html?countrysort=score.goals&countrydirection=asc&countrypage=1&clubsort=score.goals&clubdirection=desc&clubpage=1); [Transfermarkt](https://www.transfermarkt.co.in/florin-soava/profil/spieler/29253/sort/matches)).

## Romania

Șoavă earned 22 FIFA caps for Romania between 2000 and 2006, without scoring, plus one non-FIFA appearance ([national-football-teams.com](https://www.national-football-teams.com/old/player/5400/Florin_Soava.html?countrysort=score.goals&countrydirection=asc&countrypage=1&clubsort=score.goals&clubdirection=desc&clubpage=1); [Transfermarkt](https://www.transfermarkt.co.in/florin-soava/profil/spieler/29253/sort/matches)). His appearances included Euro 2004 qualifiers — among them the 2–0 win over Bosnia & Herzegovina in June 2003 — and 2006 World Cup qualifiers, including the 5–1 win in Andorra in September 2004 ([national-football-teams.com](https://www.national-football-teams.com/old/player/5400/Florin_Soava.html?countrysort=score.goals&countrydirection=asc&countrypage=1&clubsort=score.goals&clubdirection=desc&clubpage=1)). His final internationals came in May 2006, friendlies against Uruguay and Northern Ireland ([national-football-teams.com](https://www.national-football-teams.com/old/player/5400/Florin_Soava.html?countrysort=score.goals&countrydirection=asc&countrypage=1&clubsort=score.goals&clubdirection=desc&clubpage=1)).

## Early coaching years

Before Drobeta, Șoavă's coaching career was stop-start. His last engagement before the comeback was in 2013, when he took Sporting Roșiori and missed promotion to Liga III ([GOLAZO.ro](https://golazo.ro/florin-soava-liga-4-antrenor-drobeta-turnu-severin-145667)). He had previously coached Sporting Roșiori for one season and then CSO Filiași, where he was dismissed after a single day ([GOLAZO.ro](https://golazo.ro/florin-soava-liga-4-antrenor-drobeta-turnu-severin-145667)). In the iAMsport interview he described a long subsequent spell at Liga III level — six years — followed by a period of stagnation before he "started over" at Drobeta ([iAMsport.ro](https://iamsport.ro/fotbal/special/renasterea-lui-florin-soava-face-spectacol-in-liga-4-dar-viseaza-la-superliga-orice-antrenor-isi-doreste-pe-cine-vede-favorita-la-titlu-cei-de-la-fcsb-s-au-crezut-zmei-id42743.html)).
