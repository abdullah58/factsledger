# Dan Driscoll

By the time Dan Driscoll handed in his resignation on August 31, 2026, the Pentagon had already watched him leave once. In April he had publicly insisted he had no plans to go — "I have no plans to depart or resign as the Secretary of the Army," he told The Washington Post, according to the International Business Times — yet by late summer the Wall Street Journal was reporting that he expected to step down by year's end, and his family had already moved out of the Army secretary's official residence back to North Carolina ([IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.sg/who-dan-driscoll-army-secretary-reportedly-plans-pentagon-exit-after-clash-pete-hegseth-92666)).

The ending was the culmination of a months-long rupture with Defense Secretary [Pete Hegseth](/people/pete-hegseth/) over who actually runs the Army — and what it is allowed to become. Hegseth had fired Army Chief of Staff Gen. Randy George in April, pushed out Navy Secretary John Phelan the same month, and, according to CNN's reporting, two other generals as well ([Associated Press](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/01/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-is-stepping-down-after-18-months-on-the-job-white-house-says/); [ANI](https://www.lokmattimes.com/international/us-army-secretary-dan-driscoll-resigns-report/)). Under Hegseth's former senior military aide, Gen. Christopher LaNeve, installed as acting Army chief of staff, the service was already dismantling the drone modernization program Driscoll had championed: in August, officials told the AP that a Europe-based Army unit building its own drones had been ordered to end its efforts and revert to being a traditional infantry battalion ([Associated Press](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/01/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-is-stepping-down-after-18-months-on-the-job-white-house-says/)).

Driscoll, 41 at the time of his departure, was the youngest person to serve as Secretary of the Army and the 26th overall ([IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.sg/who-dan-driscoll-army-secretary-reportedly-plans-pentagon-exit-after-clash-pete-hegseth-92666)). His resignation left the Army without a Senate-confirmed civilian leader during an active Iran conflict in which, DefenseScoop reported, the service was at the forefront of defending against Tehran's missile and drone attacks ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/08/31/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-submits-resignation/)).

## From the Blue Ridge to Baghdad

Daniel P. Driscoll was born in 1985 in Boone, North Carolina, and raised in nearby Banner Elk in the state's western mountains ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2025/04/whos-who-in-defense-daniel-driscoll-secretary-of-the-army/)). His family story is steeped in the service he would later lead: his grandfather served in the Army during World War II as a decoder, and his father served during Vietnam as an infantryman, according to the Army's official biography ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/Leaders/sa)).

He attended Watauga High School, then the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, where he earned a B.S. in business administration in three years — reportedly to join the military sooner ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2025/04/whos-who-in-defense-daniel-driscoll-secretary-of-the-army/); [U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/Leaders/sa)). He was commissioned in 2007 as an armor officer through Officer Candidate School and led a cavalry platoon with the 10th Mountain Division at Fort Drum, New York ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/Leaders/sa)). In 2009 he deployed to Baghdad in support of Operation Iraqi Freedom, serving in Iraq from October 2009 to July 2010 ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/Leaders/sa); [Associated Press](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/01/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-is-stepping-down-after-18-months-on-the-job-white-house-says/)). He left active duty in 2011 as a first lieutenant ([Associated Press](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/01/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-is-stepping-down-after-18-months-on-the-job-white-house-says/)). His awards include the Army Commendation Medal, the Ranger Tab, and the Combat Action Badge ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/Leaders/sa)).

## Yale, Wall Street, and a long-shot campaign

After leaving the service, Driscoll went to Yale Law School on the GI Bill, graduating with a J.D. in 2014 ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2025/04/whos-who-in-defense-daniel-driscoll-secretary-of-the-army/)). While there he worked in the school's Veterans Legal Services Clinic, interned for the Senate Committee on Veterans Affairs, and interned for Ninth Circuit Chief Judge Alex Kozinski ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2025/04/whos-who-in-defense-daniel-driscoll-secretary-of-the-army/)). It was at Yale that he became friends with [JD Vance](/people/jd-vance/), his classmate and future vice president — a relationship that would later carry unusual weight inside the Trump administration ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2025/04/whos-who-in-defense-daniel-driscoll-secretary-of-the-army/)).

From law school he moved into the financial world: investment banking, private equity, and venture capital. He ultimately served as chief operating officer of a $200 million venture capital fund ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/Leaders/sa); [Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2025/04/whos-who-in-defense-daniel-driscoll-secretary-of-the-army/)).

In 2020 he ran as a Republican in the crowded primary for western North Carolina's 11th congressional district, campaigning on national security, jobs, and lower taxes — telling the Asheville Citizen Times that his "mountain values" meant "a fierce independence to do what's right, a strong desire to build a community and support your neighbors, an ability to be scrappy and make things work" ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2025/04/whos-who-in-defense-daniel-driscoll-secretary-of-the-army/)). He finished with about 8% of the vote in a field eventually won by Madison Cawthorn ([Associated Press](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/01/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-is-stepping-down-after-18-months-on-the-job-white-house-says/)).

Driscoll went on to serve as a senior adviser to Vance and advised Donald Trump's 2024 presidential campaign ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2025/04/whos-who-in-defense-daniel-driscoll-secretary-of-the-army/); [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/international/us-army-secretary-driscoll-submits-resignation-white-house/article71413517.ece)). When Trump nominated him for Army secretary in late 2024, he called Driscoll "a disruptor and change agent" ([Associated Press](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/01/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-is-stepping-down-after-18-months-on-the-job-white-house-says/)).

## A reformer's tenure

Confirmed by the Senate 66–28 on February 25, 2025, and sworn in the same day as the 26th Secretary of the Army ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/Leaders/sa); [Associated Press](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/01/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-is-stepping-down-after-18-months-on-the-job-white-house-says/)). At his Armed Services Committee hearing he had promised to be a soldier-focused leader, telling senators the Army "threw the dart at the dartboard and then drew the bullseye around where the dart landed" on recruiting — noting the service had its fewest active-duty soldiers since World War II — and pledging to expand the defense industrial base "from, call it, five to seven to 25 to 50" suppliers ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2025/04/whos-who-in-defense-daniel-driscoll-secretary-of-the-army/)).

As the [Army](/organizations/us-army/)'s top civilian, he oversaw operations, modernization, and resource allocation for nearly one million Active, Guard, and Reserve soldiers plus more than 265,000 Army civilians ([U.S. Army](https://www.army.mil/Leaders/sa)). His signature project was the Army Transformation Initiative: he pushed drone and counter-drone marketplaces, created Operation Jailbreak — an effort to get the Army's disparate military systems to talk to each other, taking lessons from Ukraine's commercial-tech improvisation — and courted a broader swath of the defense industry beyond the big primes ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/08/31/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-submits-resignation/)). Early on he criticized the prime contractors' role in the Army's technological stagnation, then said he had "over-blamed" them ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/08/31/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-submits-resignation/)). He became known for blunt public language — including calling a Dronebuster counter-drone weapon "fucking terrible" ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/08/31/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-submits-resignation/)).

His tenure also had an unusual diplomatic dimension: the administration tapped him as a key negotiator in efforts to end the Russia-Ukraine war ([Associated Press](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/01/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-is-stepping-down-after-18-months-on-the-job-white-house-says/)). He signed a statement of intent with NATO allies at Eurosatory in June 2026 to expand access to the Army's UAS Marketplace, launched the "Right to Integrate" hackathon with industry leaders, and signed a memo directing the Army to favor commercially available software over custom-built systems ([GovConWire](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-resignation)). In June he helped commission a second group of technology executives into the Army's Executive Innovation Corps, a reserve unit built to pull private-sector expertise into military modernization ([IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.sg/who-dan-driscoll-army-secretary-reportedly-plans-pentagon-exit-after-clash-pete-hegseth-92666)). CNN reported that under his leadership, private enterprises pledged more than $25 billion for data centers, critical mineral refineries, and dining facilities on Army installations ([ANI](https://www.lokmattimes.com/international/us-army-secretary-dan-driscoll-resigns-report/)).

## The break with Hegseth

The fault line opened publicly in April 2026, when Hegseth abruptly removed Gen. Randy George — the Army's most senior general — with no public explanation. Driscoll was George's ally and told Congress that after the firing, he and his family drove to George's house: "and we all gave him a hug" ([Associated Press](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/01/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-is-stepping-down-after-18-months-on-the-job-white-house-says/)). In the same testimony he acknowledged the civilian leadership's prerogative: "the civilian leadership, the design of our system, is that they get to pick the leaders that they want" ([Associated Press](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/01/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-is-stepping-down-after-18-months-on-the-job-white-house-says/)). Gen. Christopher Donahue, the Army's commander in Europe and Africa, unexpectedly stepped down in June ([Associated Press](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/01/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-is-stepping-down-after-18-months-on-the-job-white-house-says/)).

By August, the reporting had moved from friction to exit planning: the Journal said Driscoll expected to leave by year's end, and one account relayed that he had long indicated 23 to 24 months was the expected tenure of an Army secretary in the Trump administration ([IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.sg/who-dan-driscoll-army-secretary-reportedly-plans-pentagon-exit-after-clash-pete-hegseth-92666)).

On August 31 he submitted his resignation to President Trump after speaking with him about the state of the Army, according to a U.S. Army official; the Journal broke the news first, and the White House confirmed it ([Associated Press](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/01/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-is-stepping-down-after-18-months-on-the-job-white-house-says/); [Military.com](https://www.military.com/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-resigns-after-months-of-friction-with-defense-secretary)). White House spokeswoman Anna Kelly said Driscoll had been "highly effective in advancing President Trump's agenda," citing his leadership during military operations, his push for readiness, and his role assisting Russia-Ukraine negotiations ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/international/us-army-secretary-driscoll-submits-resignation-white-house/article71413517.ece)). Driscoll's own farewell post framed it as the end of a family calling: "As a third-generation Army veteran, it has been the honor of a lifetime to serve," he wrote, adding that the modernization gains "could not have been made... without the support of Secretary Hegseth" ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/08/31/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-submits-resignation/)).

Bipartisan praise followed. Rep. Steve Womack, an Arkansas Republican, called him "a transformative leader who brought common sense and discipline to the Department of the Army" ([Associated Press](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/01/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-is-stepping-down-after-18-months-on-the-job-white-house-says/)). Rep. Jason Crow, a Colorado Democrat, said it had been "nice... having somebody who was smart, that knew the policy," but worried the Pentagon was "in some cases, rudderless" ([Associated Press](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/01/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-is-stepping-down-after-18-months-on-the-job-white-house-says/)). Sen. Jack Reed, the Armed Services Committee's ranking Democrat, placed the blame squarely on the defense secretary: "Secretary Hegseth is cultivating a culture where dissent is punished and competence is secondary to personal allegiance" ([Associated Press](https://dnyuz.com/2026/09/01/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-is-stepping-down-after-18-months-on-the-job-white-house-says/)).

Under Secretary Michael Obadal stood next in the statutory line of succession, potentially serving as acting secretary if the resignation was accepted ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/08/31/army-secretary-dan-driscoll-submits-resignation/)).
