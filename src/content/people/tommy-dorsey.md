## Overview

Tommy Dorsey (1905–1956) was an American trombonist and bandleader whose smooth, singing trombone made his orchestra the most popular of the swing era — and whose 1940 recording of "I'll Never Smile Again" topped Billboard's first-ever national chart for 12 weeks ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Tommy-Dorsey); [Billboard](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/11/01/the-first-billboard-all-that-was-new-bright-and-interesting-on-the-boards/)).

Born in Mahanoy Plane, Pennsylvania, in the [United States](/places/united-states/), Dorsey was the younger brother of bandleader Jimmy Dorsey, with whom he first found fame before leading his own band from 1935 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Tommy-Dorsey)). His theme song, "I'm Getting Sentimental Over You," gave him his nickname, "The Sentimental Gentleman of Swing," and his band launched [Frank Sinatra](/people/frank-sinatra/), who called him his main influence ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Tommy-Dorsey)). Three of his Victor recordings are in the Grammy Hall of Fame, and in 1996 the U.S. Postal Service put the brothers on a commemorative stamp ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/awards/hall-of-fame-award/); [Smithsonian National Postal Museum](https://postalmuseum.si.edu/object/npm_1998.2010.387)). He died in 1956, aged 51; Jimmy led the band until his death the next year ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Tommy-Dorsey)). This is the trombonist, not the gospel composer Thomas A. Dorsey (1899–1993), "the father of black gospel music" ([Early Gospel Music](https://www.earlygospel.com/miscellany-composers/)).

## From Pennsylvania coal country to the Dorsey Brothers Orchestra

Thomas Francis Dorsey Jr. was born on November 19, 1905, in Mahanoy Plane, Pennsylvania, the second son of music teacher and marching-band director Thomas Francis Dorsey Sr. and Theresa Langton Dorsey ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Tommy-Dorsey)). Taught first by their father, Tommy began on trumpet and trombone before settling on the trombone, though his trumpet playing stayed exceptional ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Tommy-Dorsey)). As teenagers the brothers formed Dorsey's Novelty Six in 1920; by 1922, renamed Dorsey's Wild Canaries, it was among the first jazz bands broadcast on radio in the Baltimore area ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Tommy-Dorsey)). In the 1920s they played in the Scranton Sirens, the California Ramblers and the orchestras of Jean Goldkette and Paul Whiteman, then worked as session musicians in New York ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tommy-dorsey-mn0000934072)). From 1927 they recorded as the Dorsey Brothers Orchestra for OKeh, charting with "Coquette" in June 1928 and scoring a Top Ten hit with "Let's Do It (Let's Fall in Love)," featuring Bing Crosby, in 1929 ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tommy-dorsey-mn0000934072)).

## His own band and the swing-era peak

The brothers' volatile relationship ended the partnership in May 1935, when Tommy walked out ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tommy-dorsey-mn0000934072)). That fall Tommy took over the remnants of the disbanded Joe Haymes Orchestra, signed to RCA Victor, and scored a No. 1 with "On Treasure Island" that December ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tommy-dorsey-mn0000934072)). "I'm Getting Sentimental Over You" became his theme song and the source of his nickname, "The Sentimental Gentleman of Swing" ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Tommy-Dorsey)). The hits came fast: three more No. 1s in 1936, then 18 Top Ten hits in 1937, including "Marie" and "The Dipsy Doodle" ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tommy-dorsey-mn0000934072)). His band ranged from smooth dance tunes to hot swing, with the Clambake Seven for harder jazz; sidemen included Bunny Berigan and Buddy Rich, and Sy Oliver and Bill Finegan arranged ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Tommy-Dorsey)). Though rivals such as Benny Goodman, Artie Shaw, Glenn Miller and Harry James outranked him at times, Dorsey was the most popular bandleader of the swing era — and RCA Victor's biggest-selling artist until Elvis Presley ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tommy-dorsey-mn0000934072)).

## The first Billboard No. 1 — and a chart record

In January 1940 Dorsey hired 24-year-old Frank Sinatra from Harry James: Sinatra became the band's main attraction for nearly three years and later called Dorsey his main musical influence, crediting his phrasing and breath control ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Tommy-Dorsey)). In 1940 the band's recording of "I'll Never Smile Again," with Sinatra and the Pied Pipers, made music history: when Billboard published its first national chart — the 10-position "National List of Best Selling Retail Records" — in its July 27, 1940 issue, Dorsey's record stood at No. 1 for 12 weeks ([Billboard](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/11/01/the-first-billboard-all-that-was-new-bright-and-interesting-on-the-boards/)). Written by Ruth Lowe as a tribute to her late husband, the song became the biggest hit Dorsey ever had and Sinatra's first major hit ([GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/frank-sinatra-weve-got-him-under-our-skin/)). Across the 1940s and 1950s the orchestra ran up around 286 Billboard chart hits, about seventeen of them No. 1s — a tally that makes Dorsey not just a swing-era star but the first artist to top an American chart ([MeTV](https://www.metv.com/stories/ken-curtis-was-a-singer-before-taking-the-role-on-gunsmoke)).

## War, decline, television — and an early death

After the United States entered the Second World War in December 1941, the American Federation of Musicians struck on August 1, 1942, halting recording, and Sinatra left that September ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tommy-dorsey-mn0000934072)). He carried on through films and reissues, scoring six Top Ten hits in 1945 once the strike settled ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tommy-dorsey-mn0000934072)). He broke up his orchestra in December 1946 as the big bands faded, though he soon reorganised it ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tommy-dorsey-mn0000934072)). The brothers reunited in 1947 to play themselves in The Fabulous Dorseys, and in 1953 Jimmy joined Tommy's orchestra; the band was again billed as the Dorsey Brothers Orchestra ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Tommy-Dorsey); [AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tommy-dorsey-mn0000934072)). From a Statler Hilton residency in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/), they launched TV's Stage Show in summer 1954, on which Elvis Presley made his TV debut in January 1956 ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tommy-dorsey-mn0000934072); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Tommy-Dorsey)). Dorsey died in Greenwich, Connecticut, on November 26, 1956, aged 51 — sedated by sleeping pills after a heavy meal, he choked to death ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tommy-dorsey-mn0000934072)).

## Honours and legacy

Dorsey's recordings outlived the swing era. The Recording Academy's Grammy Hall of Fame, established in 1973 for recordings at least 25 years old, has inducted three of his Victor singles: "I'll Never Smile Again" in 1982, and "I'm Getting Sentimental Over You" and "Marie" in 1998 ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/awards/hall-of-fame-award/)). Official recognition came literally in 1996, when the U.S. Postal Service issued a 32-cent Tommy and Jimmy Dorsey stamp in New York City on September 11, part of its Big Band Leaders set in the Legends of American Music series, alongside Count Basie, Glenn Miller and Benny Goodman ([Smithsonian National Postal Museum](https://postalmuseum.si.edu/object/npm_1998.2010.387)). His orchestra even outlived him on the charts: under Warren Covington it scored a final million-selling Top Ten hit in November 1958 with "Tea for Two Cha Cha" ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tommy-dorsey-mn0000934072)). His sidemen and singers — Bunny Berigan, Buddy Rich, Frank Sinatra and Jo Stafford among them — went on to define popular music in the late 1940s and early 1950s ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tommy-dorsey-mn0000934072)).

## Frequently asked questions

### Who was Tommy Dorsey?

Tommy Dorsey (1905–1956) was an American trombonist, composer and bandleader of the big band era, nicknamed "The Sentimental Gentleman of Swing" for his smooth trombone style ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Tommy-Dorsey)). After co-leading the Dorsey Brothers Orchestra with his older brother Jimmy, he formed his own band in 1935 and became, in AllMusic's judgement, the most popular bandleader of the swing era ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tommy-dorsey-mn0000934072)).

### What was Tommy Dorsey's biggest hit?

His biggest hit was "I'll Never Smile Again," recorded with Frank Sinatra and the Pied Pipers in 1940 ([GRAMMY.com](https://www.grammy.com/news/frank-sinatra-weve-got-him-under-our-skin/)). It topped Billboard's first-ever national chart, the "National List of Best Selling Retail Records," for 12 weeks from July 27, 1940, and was Sinatra's first major hit ([Billboard](https://radiox.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/11/01/the-first-billboard-all-that-was-new-bright-and-interesting-on-the-boards/)). Songwriter Ruth Lowe wrote it as a tribute to her late husband.

### Was Tommy Dorsey related to Jimmy Dorsey?

Yes — Tommy was the younger brother of bandleader Jimmy Dorsey, by 21 months ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tommy-dorsey-mn0000934072)). The two formed Dorsey's Novelty Six in 1920 and led the Dorsey Brothers Orchestra together until Tommy walked out in May 1935 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Tommy-Dorsey)). They reunited in the 1950s, with Jimmy joining Tommy's band in 1953 and hosting the TV series Stage Show together.

### What Tommy Dorsey recordings are in the Grammy Hall of Fame?

Three of his Victor singles are in the Grammy Hall of Fame: "I'll Never Smile Again" (1940, with Frank Sinatra and the Pied Pipers), inducted in 1982; "I'm Getting Sentimental Over You" (1936), inducted in 1998; and "Marie" (1937, vocal by Jack Leonard), also inducted in 1998 ([Recording Academy](https://www.grammy.com/awards/hall-of-fame-award/)). The Hall honours recordings at least 25 years old.

### How did Tommy Dorsey die?

Dorsey died on November 26, 1956, at his home in Greenwich, Connecticut, a week after his 51st birthday ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Tommy-Dorsey)). According to AllMusic, sedated by sleeping pills after a heavy meal, he accidentally choked to death ([AllMusic](https://www.allmusic.com/artist/tommy-dorsey-mn0000934072)). His brother Jimmy continued to lead the band until his own death in 1957.
