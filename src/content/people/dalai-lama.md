## Overview

The 14th Dalai Lama, Tenzin Gyatso, born Lhamo Thondup on 6 July 1935 in Taktser, Tibet, is the spiritual leader of Tibetan Buddhism and head of the Geluk school, who has lived in exile in Dharamshala, [India](/places/india/), since 1959 and won the [Nobel Prize](/events/nobel-prize/) for Peace in 1989 for his nonviolent campaign for Tibet ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Dalai-Lama-14th); [NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1989/lama/facts/)). He was born in what is now Qinghai province, [China](/places/china/), recognised as the reincarnation of the 13th Dalai Lama as a small child, and enthroned in Lhasa in 1940 — yet he is the first Dalai Lama to become a global public figure, describing himself as a "simple Buddhist monk" ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Dalai-Lama-14th)).

This profile is about the 14th Dalai Lama only; the office stretches back over 600 years to the 14th century ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Dalai-Lama)).

## A lineage stretching back 600 years

The Dalai Lama leads the Gelukpa (Geluk, or Yellow Hat) order of Tibetan Buddhists, and until 1959 was both the spiritual and the temporal ruler of Tibet ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Dalai-Lama)). The title joins the Tibetan word *lama* — teacher or leader — with the Mongolian *ta-le*, meaning "ocean", suggesting breadth and depth of wisdom ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Dalai-Lama)). Dalai Lamas are regarded as *tulkus*, reincarnated lamas held to be the same enlightened being reborn across lives, and as manifestations of Avalokiteshvara, the bodhisattva of compassion ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Dalai-Lama)).

The line began with Gendun Drupa (1391–1474), founder and abbot of Tashi Lhunpo Monastery, who was only later designated the 1st Dalai Lama ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Dalai-Lama)). The title itself was first carried by the 3rd, Sonam Gyatso (1543–88), who received it from the Mongol chief Altan Khan in the 16th century; it was then applied posthumously to his two predecessors ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Dalai-Lama)).

Temporal power came with the 5th Dalai Lama, given control of Tibet in 1642, who commissioned the Potala Palace in Lhasa in 1645; construction was finished in 1694, and the palace later became a museum and a [UNESCO](/organizations/unesco/) World Heritage Site ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Dalai-Lama-14th); [EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/social-sciences-and-humanities/dalai-lama)).

## From Taktser to the Potala Palace

Tenzin Gyatso was born Lhamo Thondup — also spelled Dhondup — on 6 July 1935 in the small hamlet of Taktser, in the Amdo region of northeastern Tibet, to a farming family ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Dalai-Lama-14th); [NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1989/lama/facts/)).

After the 13th Dalai Lama died in Lhasa on 17 December 1933, a regent sent out search parties to find his reincarnation ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Dalai-Lama-14th)). One party reached Amdo, where the boy passed tests that included recognising the search-party leader and selecting personal items that had belonged to the 13th Dalai Lama; he was proclaimed the next Dalai Lama and recognised as the incarnation of the 13th in 1937 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Dalai-Lama-14th)). His family was held for ransom by a powerful Chinese warlord until the Tibetan government paid for their release, and the child was taken to Lhasa, where he was enthroned on 22 February 1940 and given the name Jetsun Jamphel Ngawang Lobsang Yeshe Tenzin Gyatso ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Dalai-Lama-14th)).

In February 1959, amid mounting turmoil, he sat his final examination for the Geshe Lharampa degree — the highest scholastic achievement in the Geluk school, equivalent to a doctorate in Buddhist philosophy — and passed with honours ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Dalai-Lama-14th); [Office of His Holiness the Dalai Lama](https://www.dalailama.com/the-dalai-lama/biography-and-daily-life/brief-biography)).

## Exile, democratisation and the Nobel Prize

After China's communists took control in 1949 and asserted authority over Tibet, the 15-year-old Dalai Lama was asked to assume the role of head of state, which he did on 17 November 1950 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Dalai-Lama-14th)). On 23 May 1951 a Tibetan delegation in Beijing signed, under duress, the Seventeen-Point Agreement ceding control of Tibet to China ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Dalai-Lama-14th)).

A popular uprising in Lhasa on 10 March 1959, sparked by rumours that the Chinese authorities planned to kidnap him, was crushed by Chinese troops, and the Dalai Lama escaped disguised as a Tibetan soldier on the night of 17 March ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Dalai-Lama-14th)). He crossed into India on 31 March 1959, where he was granted political asylum, and in 1960 established the Tibetan government-in-exile in Dharamshala, Himachal Pradesh ([HISTORY](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/march-31/dalai-lama-begins-exile); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Dalai-Lama-14th)).

In exile he moved the Tibetan administration towards democracy: in 1963 he presented a draft democratic constitution, and the resulting Charter of Tibetans in Exile enshrines freedom of speech, belief, assembly and movement ([Office of His Holiness the Dalai Lama](https://www.dalailama.com/the-dalai-lama/biography-and-daily-life/brief-biography)). In May 1990 the exile administration was fully democratised, and in September 2001 the Tibetan electorate directly elected the Kalon Tripa, the chairman of the cabinet, for the first time ([Office of His Holiness the Dalai Lama](https://www.dalailama.com/the-dalai-lama/biography-and-daily-life/brief-biography)).

His peace work centred on two proposals. On 21 September 1987, addressing members of the [United States](/places/united-states/) Congress in Washington, DC, he proposed a Five-Point Peace Plan: Tibet as a zone of peace; an end to China's population-transfer policy; respect for Tibetans' human rights and democratic freedoms; protection of Tibet's environment, with no nuclear weapons or waste; and earnest negotiations on Tibet's future status ([Office of His Holiness the Dalai Lama](https://www.dalailama.com/the-dalai-lama/biography-and-daily-life/brief-biography)). On 15 June 1988, addressing the European Parliament in Strasbourg, he elaborated this into a self-governing democratic Tibet in association with the People's Republic of China, with China retaining foreign policy and defence — the proposal Britannica's timeline calls the Middle Way Approach ([Office of His Holiness the Dalai Lama](https://www.dalailama.com/the-dalai-lama/biography-and-daily-life/brief-biography); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Dalai-Lama-14th)).

The Nobel Peace Prize followed on 5 October 1989, "for advocating peaceful solutions based upon tolerance and mutual respect in order to preserve the historical and cultural heritage of his people", with the medal presented in Oslo on 10 December 1989 ([NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1989/lama/facts/); [NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1989/lama/speedread/)). His prize was the first awarded on ecological grounds, and his plan proposed turning Tibet into a demilitarised, ecologically stable buffer state between major Asian powers — a proposal China rejected ([NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1989/lama/speedread/)). His office reports travel to more than 67 countries on six continents, over 150 awards and honorary doctorates, and more than 110 books authored or co-authored ([Office of His Holiness the Dalai Lama](https://www.dalailama.com/the-dalai-lama/biography-and-daily-life/brief-biography)). He is the first Dalai Lama to become a global figure ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Dalai-Lama-14th)).

## Retirement and the question of succession

On 14 March 2011 the Dalai Lama wrote to the Tibetan Parliament-in-exile asking to be relieved of his temporal authority, and on 29 May 2011 he signed the document formally transferring it to the democratically elected leader, ending the 368-year-old tradition of the Dalai Lamas functioning as both the spiritual and the temporal head of Tibet ([Office of His Holiness the Dalai Lama](https://www.dalailama.com/the-dalai-lama/biography-and-daily-life/brief-biography)).

Succession had been the open question of his later years. In 2007 the Chinese government passed a law declaring that the reincarnations of Tibetan lamas would be determined with Chinese officials' oversight and final say, barring anyone outside the country — including the 14th Dalai Lama — from involvement ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Dalai-Lama)). In guidelines published on 24 September 2011, the Dalai Lama stated that recognising a successor would rest with the Gaden Phodrang Trust, and warned that no recognition should be given to a candidate chosen for political ends by anyone, including agents of the People's Republic of China ([Office of His Holiness the Dalai Lama](https://www.dalailama.com/the-dalai-lama/biography-and-daily-life/brief-biography)).

On 2 July 2025, four days before his 90th birthday, he publicly affirmed that the institution of the Dalai Lama will continue after his death — there will be a 15th Dalai Lama — and that the Gaden Phodrang Trust has sole authority to recognise the future reincarnation, with no Chinese involvement ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Dalai-Lama)). He has also said his successor, if recognised, would not be born in a country ruled by China but in a free country, and warned that his reincarnation could be "hijacked and politicized" by the Chinese government ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/social-sciences-and-humanities/dalai-lama)). His memoir *Voice for the Voiceless* was published on 11 March 2025, and on 1 February 2026 he won a Grammy Award for Best Audio Book, Narration, and Storytelling Recording ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/world/asia/dalai-lama-fast-facts)).
