## Overview

The page-one story about ZACH Theatre is a boilerplate: oldest continuously operating theatre in Texas, named for a film star, three stages on Lady Bird Lake. The verified-but-rarely-shared part is what the company is doing now. Its 2025/26 season carries three regional premieres — Dear Evan Hansen, Come From Away, and Suzan-Lori Parks' Sally & Tom — sold through a new Zach XP membership at $39 per month that admits members to every current and upcoming production. ([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/austin/article/Zach-Theatre-Unveils-202526-Season-Featuring-DEAR-EVAN-HANSEN-COME-FROM-AWAY-More-20250507)) The access layer beneath that season gets even less coverage: a 35-minute interactive show written for babies as young as six months, a Pay-What-You-Will program setting aside more than 300 seats per season for families, and adult acting classes returning in February 2026 after the theatre spent years without adult programming. ([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/austin/article/CAMPFIRE-to-Return-to-Zach-Theater-This-Fall-20250911)) ([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/austin/article/Zach-Theater-To-Add-Adult-Acting-Classes-20251208))

Beneath the present tense sits one of the longest-lived institutions in American regional theatre. ZACH Theatre is a professional producing theatre company based at 202 S. Lamar Blvd in Austin, Texas, on the south shore of Lady Bird Lake, founded in 1932 as the Austin Civic Theater and described by local press as the oldest theatre company in Texas. ([Tribeza](https://tribeza.com/arts/zach-theatre-hires-managing-director-jamie-herlich-mcialwain/))

## From community theater to professional house

The company's roots run through the University of Texas. Producing artistic director Dave Steakley told The Daily Texan in 2026 that the Curtain Club, a UT organization, started a community theater known as Austin's Little Theater in the 1920s, and that "in 1932, it became Austin Civic Theater." Steakley joined the staff in 1991 with an explicit mandate: "part of my job was to turn the theater from a community theater into a professional theater." The constant through the decades, he said, was "the focus on Austin artists first." ([The Daily Texan](https://thedailytexan.com/2026/08/03/qa-zach-theater-artistic-director-dave-steakley-talks-new-play-sally-tom/))

The financial arc of that conversion is unusually well documented. American Theatre reported in 2013 that ZACH was a $3-million troupe as of spring 2011; a $23-million capital campaign begun in 2006 funded the 420-seat Topfer Theatre, which opened in fall 2012, and that season the company's budget more than doubled to $7 million. The magazine itemized the economics: the Kleberg Stage production of August: Osage County cost $169,000 ($21,000 for production, $17,000 for the set) against a $220,000 Harvey in the Topfer, with average ticket prices of $34 and $49.57 respectively. Finance leadership told the magazine that 70 percent of the institution's income was earned — from tickets, concessions, and in-house products — a share that buffered it through the 2008 and 2011 downturns, though one 2010–11 show still had to be entirely rethought on budget grounds. ([American Theatre](https://www.americantheatre.org/2013/11/01/show-me-the-money-zach-theatre/))

Leadership turned over at the top of the 2020s. Elisabeth Challener, managing director for 15 years and the person who ran the Topfer capital campaign, left to co-found a Broadway production company. She was replaced by Jamie Herlich McIalwain, a Seattle native with an MFA in Arts Leadership from Seattle University and 20 years in the field, most recently chief advancement officer at Seattle Repertory Theatre, who took over with a mandate to rebuild the organization after the COVID shutdown. ([Tribeza](https://tribeza.com/arts/zach-theatre-hires-managing-director-jamie-herlich-mcialwain/))

## Venues and programming

The Topfer Theatre — 420 seats on a 32,000-square-foot footprint with the People's Plaza — nearly doubled the company's capacity when it joined the campus, which also includes the 230-seat thrust-style Kleberg Stage and the Whisenhunt, home to the Junior League of Austin Studio. The company also programs at a North Campus. ([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/austin/article/ZACH-Theatre-Awarded-Impact-Austin-Grant-for-Arts-Across-the-Curriculum-Program-20130615)) ([American Theatre](https://www.americantheatre.org/2013/11/01/show-me-the-money-zach-theatre/)) ([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/austin/article/CAMPFIRE-to-Return-to-Zach-Theater-This-Fall-20250911))

The 2025/26 season, announced in May 2025, is built around regional premieres. Murder on the Orient Express opened Winter 2025 at the Topfer, followed by Dear Evan Hansen in Spring 2026 — billed as one of the first regional productions of the six-time Tony winner — Sally & Tom in Summer 2026 at the Kleberg, a new Annie at the Topfer that summer, and Come From Away in Fall 2026 as Austin's regional premiere. Dave Steakley's adaptation of A Christmas Carol returned for its 11th year alongside A Charlie Brown Christmas, while the Family Series added the bilingual Zapata: A Folklórico Superhero Musical — commissioned by ZACH itself — and a remount of its 2015 Charlotte's Web. Season subscriptions started at $240, with the $39-per-month Zach XP membership covering all productions. ([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/austin/article/Zach-Theatre-Unveils-202526-Season-Featuring-DEAR-EVAN-HANSEN-COME-FROM-AWAY-More-20250507))

Sally & Tom is more than a slot in that lineup. Steakley told The Daily Texan he has been a friend of playwright Suzan-Lori Parks since his early years as an artistic director, and ZACH's staging was among the first regional productions after the play's debut at The Public Theater, running through August 23, 2026. ([The Daily Texan](https://thedailytexan.com/2026/08/03/qa-zach-theater-artistic-director-dave-steakley-talks-new-play-sally-tom/))

## Education and community

ZACH's education arm operates at a scale few page-one results convey. In 2013 the company won a $100,000 Impact Austin grant to expand its Arts Across the Curriculum program, which trains teachers to use creative drama in language arts, science, history, and math. Built with mindPOP, Austin ISD, and the Kennedy Center, the program grew from a pilot in three Title I elementary schools and 15 classrooms to a plan for 16 schools, 60 teachers, and 300 to 1,500 at-risk students, with a University of Texas evaluation measuring its effect; teachers reported students testing 30 percentage points above the district average in reading benchmarks after drama-based instruction. The company said it was serving nearly 28,000 Central Texas children annually through its education and outreach programs, and had brought more than 15,000 students from low-income schools to youth shows at reduced ticket prices in a single year. ([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/austin/article/ZACH-Theatre-Awarded-Impact-Austin-Grant-for-Arts-Across-the-Curriculum-Program-20130615))

The youngest end of that audience gets its own show: Campfire, a 35-minute interactive performance for ages six months to five years, written by Director of Youth Programming Nat Miller and Christina Freeman and directed by Miller, ran October 5–December 14, 2025 at the Junior League of Austin Studio in the Whisenhunt and was scheduled for February 21–May 9, 2026 at the North Campus. Since the prior season, the Family Series Pay-What-You-Will program has reserved more than 300 seats per season for families to reserve at whatever price works for them. ([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/austin/article/CAMPFIRE-to-Return-to-Zach-Theater-This-Fall-20250911))

Adults were a gap in ZACH's education offerings for years — long enough that in January 2013 the improv school Merlin Works moved its classes to ZACH precisely because the theatre had a strong youth program but no adult classes. ZACH employee Nat Miller, now the company's Director of Education, championed that move, and Merlin Works' Second Sunday shows ran at 8pm on the 230-seat Kleberg Stage. [Shana Merlin](/people/shana-merlin/) ([Merlin Works](https://www.merlin-works.com/2012/11/06/big-announcement-merlin-works-is-moving/)) The gap is finally closing: in February 2026 ZACH launched its own adult acting classes — 12-week semesters, weekdays 6–9pm, capped at 12 participants, covering Meisner and Stanislavski technique at the main campus and the North Campus. "As a professional theater with a long history of serving young people, we are excited to create a space where adults can explore acting," Miller said. ([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/austin/article/Zach-Theater-To-Add-Adult-Acting-Classes-20251208))

## What this profile does not claim

- The founding year is given as 1932, following Austin press coverage; some sources date the founding to 1933. This profile does not resolve that discrepancy.
- The widely repeated claim that the company was renamed for Austin-born film star Zachary Scott in 1968 was not verified against a fully read source in this research, so it is not claimed here.
- Financial figures end in 2013 — a $7-million budget and 70 percent earned income — and audience figures are date-stamped (95,000 adults and youth in 2013; 140,000 patrons annually in 2022). This profile does not give a current budget or current attendance, and the FY2022 audit surfaced during research was not readable in full, so its figures are not cited.
- The 2025/26 season is described as announced; the profile does not claim the Fall 2026 productions played as scheduled.
- This profile is about the theatre company, not the people profiled under similar names — Zach Witkoff and Zach Yadegari have their own profiles.
- Merlin Works ended its classes and shows in 2025; the profile does not claim any current residency at ZACH.
