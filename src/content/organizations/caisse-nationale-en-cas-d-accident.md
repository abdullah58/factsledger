## Overview

The Caisse nationale en cas d'accident was a French state accident-insurance fund, created by the law of 11 July 1868 inside the [Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations](/organizations/caisse-des-depots-et-consignations/), redeployed in 1899 to insure employers against the new work-accident liability law, and absorbed into the life fund in 1959 to form the Caisse Nationale de Prévoyance, the direct ancestor of [CNP Assurances](/organizations/cnp-assurances/).

It was the state's answer to a practical problem of the industrial age: when a worker was killed or permanently disabled, who paid the pension, and what happened if the employer could not? For most of its ninety-one years the fund was a small public insurer working through the Treasury and postal network, offering employers a state-backed alternative to private companies ([CNUM](https://cnum.cnam.fr/pgi/sresrech.php?P228.28/0338)). Its name is written both "en cas d'accident" and "en cas d'accidents"; both forms refer to the same fund ([Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000299325/); [Assemblée Nationale](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/15/questions/QANR5L15QE199)).

## Created in 1868, alongside the death fund

The law of 11 July 1868 created two state insurance funds at once: one for death and one for accidents ([CNP Assurances](https://www.cnp.fr/le-groupe-cnp-assurances/newsroom/publications/2020/le-groupe-cnp/actionnaires-individuels/assemblee-generale/document-d-enregistrement-universel-2019)). CNP's own history likewise dates both funds to 1868. The accident fund was instituted by that law, as a contemporary ministerial circular records, and it sat within the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations alongside the death fund and the older retirement fund ([CNUM](https://cnum.cnam.fr/pgi/sresrech.php?P228.28/0338)). For its first thirty years it was a quiet corner of the French state: the 1868 law had set up the institution, but the legal framework that would give it real work — a general employer-liability regime for workplace accidents — did not arrive until the law of 9 April 1898, which recognised employer liability for accidents at work and is described by jurist J.-J. Dupeyroux as the first great French social law ([Sénat](https://www.senat.fr/rap/r98-0452/r98-04528.html)).

## The 1899 redeployment: insuring the 1898 law

The law of 24 May 1899 and the decree of 26 May 1899 authorised the fund to insure the risks created by the 1898 law: an employer could insure with it either for the constitution of annuities alone, or additionally for funeral costs, daily allowances and medical costs due until the annuities were constituted ([CNUM](https://cnum.cnam.fr/pgi/sresrech.php?P228.28/0338)). Employers reached it through the state's own capillaries — the trésoriers-payeurs généraux, the receveurs particuliers des finances, the percepteurs and the receveurs des postes in every département ([CNUM](https://cnum.cnam.fr/pgi/sresrech.php?P228.28/0338)). The premium was the employer's alone: the worker paid nothing and could not validly consent to wage deductions for it, and whatever insurer the employer chose, victims had absolute certainty of receiving their court-fixed pensions — if the employer or its insurer defaulted, the Caisse nationale des retraites stood as subsidiary debtor ([CNUM](https://cnum.cnam.fr/pgi/sresrech.php?P228.28/0338)).

## A small public fund in a private market

By 1936 the fund's world was dominated by private insurers: 133 private companies covered workplace accidents in France, according to a Sénat report, so the state accident fund operated as a small public alternative inside a predominantly private market ([Sénat](https://www.senat.fr/rap/r98-0452/r98-04528.html)). That marginality is the thread running through its history — a state fund that never displaced the private market, but guaranteed a floor beneath it, and was eventually folded into a larger state vehicle to end its isolation. The end came by decree no. 59-863 of 18 July 1959, which incorporated the accident fund into the [Caisse nationale d'assurance sur la vie](/organizations/caisse-nationale-d-assurance-sur-la-vie/), which took the name Caisse Nationale de Prévoyance ([Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000299325/)). The decree was signed by Prime Minister Michel Debré, Labour Minister Paul Bacon and Finance Minister Antoine Pinay, and took effect on 19 July 1959 ([Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000299325/)). That successor later became an EPIC in 1987 and the société anonyme CNP Assurances in 1992, seated in [Paris](/places/paris/) ([Assemblée Nationale](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/15/questions/QANR5L15QE199)).

## FAQ

### What was the Caisse nationale en cas d'accident?

It was a French state accident-insurance fund, created by the law of 11 July 1868 inside the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations. From 1899 it insured employers against their liability for workplace accidents under the law of 9 April 1898, paying annuities for death or permanent incapacity plus funeral, daily-allowance and medical costs.

### How did an employer use the fund?

An employer applied through the fund's agents in the départements — the trésoriers-payeurs généraux, receveurs particuliers des finances, percepteurs and receveurs des postes. The employer alone paid the premium; the worker paid nothing. Cover could be taken for annuities alone, or for annuities plus funeral costs, daily allowances and medical costs.

### What happened to it in 1959?

Decree no. 59-863 of 18 July 1959 incorporated the accident fund into the Caisse nationale d'assurance sur la vie, and the combined fund took the name Caisse Nationale de Prévoyance. Its governing legislation was carried over to the new body, which later became CNP Assurances in 1992.

### Is it the same as the Swiss fund of the same name?

No. A Swiss body called the Caisse nationale d'assurance en cas d'accident also existed — the Jura cantonal archives hold a dossier on it covering 1951–1989, with Swiss annual reports. It is the Swiss national accident-insurance fund, a different entity from the French fund described here.
