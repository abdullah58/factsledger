## Overview

WELL Health Technologies Corp. is a Vancouver-based digital health company, listed on the Toronto Stock Exchange under the symbol WELL (and on the OTCQX as WHTCF), that has grown by buying medical clinics and health-software businesses and wiring them together with its own technology ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/well-health-to-acquire-myhealth-for-c$206m-shares-jump-almost-10-2021-06-08)). It was founded in 2017 by chairman and chief executive officer Hamed Shahbazi — reportedly beginning as a network of yoga studios before switching into healthcare the following year — and by late 2026 operated 275 outpatient clinics supporting more than five million patient visits a year ([Canadian Healthcare Technology](https://www.canhealth.com/2025/11/26/competition-bureau-is-investigating-well-health/); [Dow Jones Newswires](https://www.tradingview.com/news/DJN_DN20260922002459:0/)).

This profile covers WELL Health Technologies Corp., the Canadian public company — not any other business using the "Well" name, and not HEALWELL AI Inc., the separately listed AI company in which WELL took a controlling interest in 2025. Most English-language coverage of WELL is either company-issued or stock-market chatter; this profile is built around what independent journalism has actually documented: the acquisition spree, and the regulatory scrutiny that followed it.

## Building Canada's largest clinic network

WELL's strategy, as Shahbazi described it in an interview, is vertical integration: "acquiring clinical and digital assets and bringing them together in one network" — owning the clinics while also owning the electronic-medical-records and digitization businesses that serve them ([Investing News Network](https://investingnews.com/daily/life-science-investing/medical-device-investing/well-health-canada-lags-digitizing-healthcare/)). Early on that meant a modest footprint of 19 clinics and 180 physicians supporting roughly 600,000 patient visits a year, plus an EMR business ([Investing News Network](https://investingnews.com/daily/life-science-investing/medical-device-investing/well-health-canada-lags-digitizing-healthcare/)).

The defining deal came in June 2021, when WELL agreed to acquire MyHealth Partners Inc. — one of Canada's largest specialty healthcare providers, with 48 diagnostic centres across Ontario — for C$206 million plus a conditional earnout of up to C$60 million, paid in a mix of shares and debt ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/well-health-to-acquire-myhealth-for-c$206m-shares-jump-almost-10-2021-06-08)). The combination created what Shahbazi called "the largest and most capable non-governmental owner-operator of outpatient medical clinics in Canada," with 74 clinics and an expected annualized run rate of roughly C$400 million in revenue and C$100 million in EBITDA ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/well-health-to-acquire-myhealth-for-c$206m-shares-jump-almost-10-2021-06-08)). MyHealth brought about 760 healthcare professionals, more than 500,000 annual patient visits, some 160 physicians and 600 other clinicians, and a referral network of over 10,000 physicians — along with its own acquisition machine, having completed 25 deals for 33 independent clinics over eight years with a pipeline of 125 more targets ([Canadian Healthcare Technology](https://www.canhealth.com/2021/06/09/well-health-to-acquire-myhealth-for-206-million/)). The debt portion was arranged by a six-bank syndicate led by the Royal Bank of Canada, with Eight Capital advising WELL ([Canadian Healthcare Technology](https://www.canhealth.com/2021/06/09/well-health-to-acquire-myhealth-for-206-million/)).

WELL also expanded south of the border: in February 2021 it announced the acquisition of CRH Medical Corporation, a US anesthesia-services company, for US$4.00 per share — about US$292.7 million in equity consideration and a total transaction value near US$369.2 million, funded in part by a C$295.5 million equity offering ([Kalkine Media](https://kalkinemedia.com/ca/stocks/healthcare/well-health-tsxwell-crh-stocks-skyrocket-on-acquisition-news)). By September 2025 the company owned 227 medical clinics across primary care, diagnostics, and executive health, and it reported $1 billion in revenue for the first nine months of 2025 — most of it from patient services — alongside a $27.5 million net loss ([Canadian Healthcare Technology](https://www.canhealth.com/2025/11/26/competition-bureau-is-investigating-well-health/)).

## The software side — and the scrutiny it attracted

Alongside clinics, WELL built a health-software arm under the WELLSTAR brand, and it is one of Canada's three major providers of medical-record software for doctors' offices, alongside TELUS Health and QHR Technologies, a Loblaw-owned subsidiary ([Canadian Healthcare Technology](https://www.canhealth.com/2025/11/26/competition-bureau-is-investigating-well-health/)). The company has said it plans to take WELLSTAR public ([Canadian Healthcare Technology](https://www.canhealth.com/2025/11/26/competition-bureau-is-investigating-well-health/)).

That software expansion is now under regulatory scrutiny. In November 2025, Canada's Competition Bureau filed an application in Federal Court seeking a court order to obtain information from WELL Health, with a hearing set for December 9 — an investigation reported by the *Globe and Mail*'s Chris Hannay ([Canadian Healthcare Technology](https://www.canhealth.com/2025/11/26/competition-bureau-is-investigating-well-health/)). The concern is that some of WELL's biggest 2025 acquisitions are lessening competition in AI transcription and medical-record software. The probe centers on WELL's April 1, 2025 acquisition of a controlling interest in HEALWELL AI and HEALWELL's concurrent acquisition of Orion Health Holdings, a New Zealand–based medical-record software provider — a company that had gone public in early 2021 as MCI Onehealth, sold a minority stake and its clinics to WELL in 2023, and renamed itself HEALWELL ([Canadian Healthcare Technology](https://www.canhealth.com/2025/11/26/competition-bureau-is-investigating-well-health/)). The bureau argued the vertical integration could make it hard for rival technology companies to sell to WELL's clinics or integrate with its products; it contacted WELL on May 15, received a pre-merger notification form on June 20 along with a letter explaining the failure to notify before closing, and met WELL's team — including Shahbazi on October 20 — before seeking internal documents and communications going back to 2022, including how the companies plan to store, use, and monetize patient data ([Canadian Healthcare Technology](https://www.canhealth.com/2025/11/26/competition-bureau-is-investigating-well-health/)). WELL told the *Globe and Mail* it was "working collaboratively" with the bureau ([Canadian Healthcare Technology](https://www.canhealth.com/2025/11/26/competition-bureau-is-investigating-well-health/)).

The company faces pressure in the United States as well: it is in the process of divesting its US subsidiary Circle Medical, which has been under investigation in the United States over its billing practices ([Canadian Healthcare Technology](https://www.canhealth.com/2025/11/26/competition-bureau-is-investigating-well-health/)). Meanwhile, WELL is pushing its clinic network into new territory — in September 2026 it launched WELL Research, a consolidated clinical-trial platform offering contract-research-organization trial management, site-management services, and bioanalytical services, built on its 275-clinic network, provider network, patient-consent platform, and protocol-participant matching ([Dow Jones Newswires](https://www.tradingview.com/news/DJN_DN20260922002459:0/)).
