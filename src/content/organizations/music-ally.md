## Overview

Music Ally is a London-based music-business information company founded in 2002 by Paul Brindley and Steve Mayall. It publishes a daily news Bulletin, research reports and marketing guides for the global music industry, and runs training, events and a startups programme, funded mainly by subscriptions rather than advertising. Stuart Dredge, its Head of Insight, has written the daily Bulletin since joining as an Editor in 2007 ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/about/); [Jaybird Communications](https://jaybirdcom.com/pass-the-mic-a-conversation-with-stuart-dredge-of-music-ally/)).

The company launched with a mission to help the global music industry "thrive from digital disruption rather than fear it", publishing news and analysis of how technology was changing the business alongside strategy and research for music companies navigating that shift ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/about/)). Its clients span the music and technology sectors and include all of the major labels, many independents, music publishers and management companies, plus platforms such as Spotify, Amazon, Facebook and Google ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/about/)).

## Founding and early years

Music Ally was founded in 2002, in the years when file-sharing service Napster and then Apple's iTunes and the iPod were overturning how recorded music was distributed and paid for ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/about/); [Jaybird Communications](https://jaybirdcom.com/pass-the-mic-a-conversation-with-stuart-dredge-of-music-ally/)). Paul Brindley and Steve Mayall were its co-founders, and Brindley remains chief executive today ([Jaybird Communications](https://jaybirdcom.com/pass-the-mic-a-conversation-with-stuart-dredge-of-music-ally/); [Music Ally](https://musically.com/about/)). The company's early output was news and analysis of technology's impact on the industry, plus strategy and research to help music companies navigate it, paid for largely by subscriptions rather than advertising ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/about/)).

Stuart Dredge, who had been a consumer-technology and then mobile-industry journalist covering downloads and ringtones, joined as an Editor in 2007 after working at Informa alongside Mayall, and began writing the morning news bulletin he still writes today ([Jaybird Communications](https://jaybirdcom.com/pass-the-mic-a-conversation-with-stuart-dredge-of-music-ally/)). He became Head of Insight in 2021, and also served as a Contributing Editor for The Guardian from 2012 to 2016 ([Jaybird Communications](https://jaybirdcom.com/pass-the-mic-a-conversation-with-stuart-dredge-of-music-ally/)). He added artificial-intelligence music to the coverage beat more than a decade ago, after the startup Jukedeck won a pitching contest at a tech conference ([Jaybird Communications](https://jaybirdcom.com/pass-the-mic-a-conversation-with-stuart-dredge-of-music-ally/)).

## What Music Ally publishes

For its global network of subscribers, Music Ally publishes the daily news Bulletin, Country Profiles of music markets around the world, Insight Reports, the Focus podcast, and regular Sandbox Guides, with insight articles on new tools and notable campaigns ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/about/)). Its free weekly newsletter, The Knowledge, goes out every Friday with an at-a-glance overview of the industry's news, trends and resources ([Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/best-music-industry-newsletters-to-subscribe-to-in-2026/)). Its other podcast, The Price of Music, is produced by Music Ally and hosted by Stuart Dredge and Joe Sparrow, translating the money behind the week's developments — streaming royalties, ticketing, publishing, artificial intelligence and legislation — into plain conversation ([Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/10-music-business-podcasts-you-should-be-listening-to-right-now/)).

### Beyond publishing

Music Ally provides hands-on marketing services to artists, labels and managers, and training focused on smarter music marketing that is used by professionals around the world ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/about/)). Its stated editorial roadmap looks to hundreds of millions of new music consumers in India, China, Latin America and Africa, and to streaming's growth in physical-dominated markets such as Japan ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/about/)). It has an office in Medellín, Colombia, a fully localised Japanese-language version, and publishes business information in Chinese through a partnership with Kanjian Music ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/about/)).

## Startups, events and awards

Music Ally has long acted as a sifter and mentor for music startups. It was a selection partner for Midemlab, the startup competition of the Midem conference, whose 2020 edition — the 13th — drew submissions from 49 countries, with 20 finalists chosen by Music Ally, Plus 8 Equity and The Lynk; chief executive Paul Brindley sat on the jury, and winners received a one-year Music Ally subscription among their prizes ([Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/midemlab-names-2020-music-tech-finalists/); [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/midemlab-names-jury-adds-live-music-category/)). Midemlab 2020 drew 226 submissions from 49 countries, a scale that shows why a specialist filter like Music Ally was useful to the competition in the first place ([Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/midemlab-names-2020-music-tech-finalists/)). In September 2022 Music Ally launched its own programme, SI:X, an annual global showcase, contest and mentorship support programme for early-stage music startups ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/ethics/)).

### SI:X

SI:X returned for a second year in 2023 backed by Amazon Music, ASCAP, The Raine Group, Red Bull Media House, Reed Smith and Universal Music Group, with six categories, 36 startups highlighted in the Q3 Startups Report, and six winners receiving mentoring and an invitation to present at an international music business conference in January 2024 ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Music-Ally-SIX-Press-Release-2023_24.pdf)). Applicants had to have been founded no earlier than 1 January 2018 and employ no more than 200 people, and first-year winners included Daaci, Curve and Hawkr ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Music-Ally-SIX-Press-Release-2023_24.pdf)). The 2025/26 edition ran in association with Amazon Music, ASCAP Lab, PUSH, Reed Smith, Rock Paper Scissors and Universal Music Group ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/ethics/)).

### Awards, conferences and Artist Ally

The company also judges and celebrates music marketing. Its Sandbox Campaigns of the Year report honours 50 detailed case studies of the best, most innovative and interesting music marketing campaigns of the year, and in 2024 it launched the official Music Ally Marketing Awards event to give digital marketing teams in music a stage ([Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/music-marketing-awards-highlight-creative-innovation/)). Its conferences include Music Ally Connect, an annual international industry conference held in London each January; Music Ally NEXT, held in London each June; the Music Ally Publishing Summit each October; and the annual online Music Ally Marketing Week ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/ethics/)). In 2026 it launched Artist Ally, a free initiative for artists, songwriters and producers, opening with a five-day online summit from 27 April to 1 May built around creativity, marketing and fanbase development, career growth and monetisation, changemaking, and live and touring, with speakers including LaRussell, K Camp and RuthAnne ([Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/artist-ally-free-online-summit-to-empower-music-creators/)).

## Music Ally and Billboard

Music Ally's most-cited relationship with Billboard is as a chronicler of its charts. The company describes itself as used to "reporting on the ins and outs in Billboard's US music charts" ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/2020/09/15/billboard-and-mrc-launch-two-new-global-music-charts/)). On 15 September 2020 it reported that Billboard and data partner MRC had launched two new rankings, the Billboard Global 200 and the Billboard Global Excl. US, collecting data from more than 200 territories on official-only streams across subscription and ad-supported tiers of leading digital platforms, plus downloads from key online retailers ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/2020/09/15/billboard-and-mrc-launch-two-new-global-music-charts/)).

The first Global 200 number one was WAP by Cardi B and Megan Thee Stallion, while Maluma's Hawái topped the first chart excluding the [United States](/places/united-states/) ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/2020/09/15/billboard-and-mrc-launch-two-new-global-music-charts/)). Music Ally noted the obvious comparison — Spotify's Global Top 50, where WAP was also number one at the time — and that Spotify's chart missed markets such as China and South Korea ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/2020/09/15/billboard-and-mrc-launch-two-new-global-music-charts/)). The two publications are otherwise independent: Music Ally is a London-based trade publication, while Billboard is the US chart publisher whose rankings it reports on, not a sister title or owner.

## Business model and editorial independence

Music Ally is a multi-pronged company whose journalism is mainly funded by individual and corporate subscriptions, with sponsor-supported free versions for independent labels, managers, publishers and collecting societies ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/ethics/)). Its five largest corporate subscribers by value in 2025 were Spotify, Universal Music, Warner Music, Sony Music and Google, and none of them accounted for more than 20% of its revenues that year — a spread that means no single subscriber dominates its income, which is the practical basis for the independence it claims ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/ethics/)). Its stated policy is that it never charges for editorial coverage, and that its news, Sandbox content, Insight Reports and analysis are free from commercial consideration and published at the editorial team's discretion ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/ethics/)).

### Independence and disclosure

That independence claim sits alongside disclosed commercial relationships. Partner content runs under the Co-Labs vertical and is clearly labelled as not created by the editorial team, and the company publishes a list of its current and recent commercial partnerships so readers can judge its coverage ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/ethics/)). It holds an equity stake in Indie Music Rights, with a disclosure statement appearing whenever that company is mentioned in editorial coverage ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/ethics/)). It also states that it does not publish AI-generated news stories: AI tools may help sift information or analyse data, but all news is sourced, analysed, written, edited and published by humans ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/ethics/)). The company is accredited as a Living Wage Employer and describes itself as an equal opportunities employer ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/ethics/)).
