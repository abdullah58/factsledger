## Overview

Promus Ventures is an early-stage venture capital firm founded in the [United States](/places/united-states/) in 2012 by managing partner Mike Collett, investing in deep-tech startups that use innovative technology to solve complex problems ([Delano](https://delano.lu/article/meet-the-vc-promus-ventures)). Based in [Chicago](/places/chicago/), with offices in [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) and Luxembourg, it had invested in roughly 100 companies globally by early 2023, mainly at an early stage and opportunistically in later-stage rounds ([Delano](https://delano.lu/article/meet-the-vc-promus-ventures); [Delano](https://delano.lu/article/europe-still-behind-us-in-spac)). Its best-known bets are in space — Rocket Lab, Spire and ICEYE — and it manages Orbital Ventures, a €120 million Luxembourg-anchored fund dedicated to early-stage space companies ([Private Equity Wire](https://privateequitywire.com/news/promus-ventures-closes-eur120m-space-fund)).

The firm is small by venture-capital standards: as of April 2022 it had eight employees, five of them based in Luxembourg, and five funds in total ([Delano](https://delano.lu/article/europe-still-behind-us-in-spac)). Its public profile is correspondingly thin — most of what is independently known about it comes from Luxembourg press coverage of the Orbital Ventures fund and two interviews with its Luxembourg-based partner, Pierre Festal — but the portfolio it has built includes two companies that went on to list on public markets and three exits worth a combined $1 billion-plus at the prices reported.

## The founder and the thesis

Mike Collett founded Promus Ventures in 2012 after two decades in finance and investing ([Delano](https://delano.lu/article/meet-the-vc-promus-ventures); [Space & Satellite Professionals International](https://sspipodcast.libsyn.com/better-satellite-world-deep-diving-from-space-episode-4-space-is-not-software)). Before Promus he was founder and managing partner of the Masters Capital Nanotechnology Fund, a venture capital firm, and before that a vice president in Merrill Lynch's mergers and acquisitions group ([Space & Satellite Professionals International](https://sspipodcast.libsyn.com/better-satellite-world-deep-diving-from-space-episode-4-space-is-not-software)). He holds a BA in mathematics and a BA in English from Vanderbilt University and an MBA in finance from Washington University in St. Louis, and he lives in Chicago ([Space & Satellite Professionals International](https://sspipodcast.libsyn.com/better-satellite-world-deep-diving-from-space-episode-4-space-is-not-software)).

The firm's stated investment thesis is deliberately simple — "People. Product. Potential." — and it looks for founding teams that, in its own words, "run through walls" to build companies solving significant pain points the founders have often experienced personally ([Promus Ventures](https://us12.campaign-archive.com/?u=cf5606e10850059e196dadeff&id=26352e1ffa)). In interviews, partner Pierre Festal puts the same idea in operational terms: when assessing a potential investment, the firm focuses first and foremost on the quality of the founding team, its vision and its ability to execute, and only then examines the technology, product, business model, market and competitive dynamics ([Delano](https://delano.lu/article/meet-the-vc-promus-ventures)). Festal has said that "a vast majority of our decision rests" on the team ([Silicon Luxembourg](http://www.siliconluxembourg.lu/a-fund-squarely-focused-on-the-space-sector/)).

That team-first screen is applied across a broad deep-tech remit: space, automation and robotics, applied artificial intelligence and machine learning, and synthetic biology, among other areas ([Delano](https://delano.lu/article/meet-the-vc-promus-ventures)). The firm invests in the US, the EU, the UK and New Zealand, and describes itself as an early-stage investor that will also take opportunistic later-stage positions ([Delano](https://delano.lu/article/europe-still-behind-us-in-spac); [Delano](https://delano.lu/article/meet-the-vc-promus-ventures)).

## A deep-tech portfolio

Promus's portfolio is dominated by companies building hard technology rather than consumer software. Its named investments include Rocket Lab (Nasdaq: RKLB), which provides spacecraft, satellite components, launch services and on-orbit management; Whoop, a wearable that provides fitness and health feedback with customised coaching; Spire (NYSE: SPIR); Mapbox; ICEYE; Bellabeat; Swift Navigation; AngelList; Isotropic Systems; Halter, whose solar-powered cow collars help farmers monitor cattle health and build virtual fences; and Cobalt Robotics ([Delano](https://delano.lu/article/meet-the-vc-promus-ventures)). Collett's investments have also included FLYR and Kensho, and the firm's earlier natural-language-processing and AI bets included StatMuse and CrowdFlower ([Space & Satellite Professionals International](https://sspipodcast.libsyn.com/better-satellite-world-deep-diving-from-space-episode-4-space-is-not-software); [Promus Ventures](https://us12.campaign-archive.com/?u=cf5606e10850059e196dadeff&id=26352e1ffa)).

The exits are the firm's clearest public scorecard. Kensho, which offers AI solutions including audio transcription and text enrichment, was acquired by S&P Global in 2018 for approximately $550 million; Gauss Surgical, a medtech company whose AI-enabled platform monitors blood loss during surgery, was acquired by Stryker in 2021 for $160 million; and Figure Eight, which uses human intelligence to help train machine-learning algorithms, was acquired by Appen in 2019 for $300 million ([Delano](https://delano.lu/article/meet-the-vc-promus-ventures)). At the prices reported, the three priced exits total just over $1 billion — a notable return profile for a firm that, in 2022, employed only eight people.

## Orbital Ventures: the space fund

Space is the firm's signature sector, and in 2020 it formalised that focus with a dedicated fund. Orbital Ventures, managed by Promus Ventures and anchored in Luxembourg, made its first close in January 2020 and closed at €120 million in 2021 ([Private Equity Wire](https://privateequitywire.com/news/promus-ventures-closes-eur120m-space-fund)). The fund targets early-stage, space-related companies across a broad spectrum: space-based telecommunications networks and services; geospatial location-based services and applications; space-related technologies and innovation such as robotics, sensors and data; Earth observation and environmental monitoring; transportation and logistics efficiency and safety; and space resource utilisation and exploration ([Private Equity Wire](https://privateequitywire.com/news/promus-ventures-closes-eur120m-space-fund)).

What makes Orbital Ventures unusual is its capital base. The European Investment Fund, the financing arm of the European Investment Bank Group, committed €40 million — a third of the fund — and the Luxembourg Government, via the Ministry of the Economy and the National Credit and Investment Institution (SNCI), also invested ([Private Equity Wire](https://privateequitywire.com/news/promus-ventures-closes-eur120m-space-fund)). Other limited partners include Banque Internationale à Luxembourg (BIL), BGL BNP Paribas, OHB, Post Luxembourg, SES and Spuerkeess, and the fund's limited partners are all European and predominantly public ([Delano](https://delano.lu/article/europe-still-behind-us-in-spac)). Festal has said the public-sector weight changes nothing about how the fund operates: "We are a financial investor whose objective is to maximise returns for LPs" ([Delano](https://delano.lu/article/europe-still-behind-us-in-spac)).

A portion of the fund must be deployed in companies with a link to Luxembourg — headquartered there or planning to build a presence ([Delano](https://delano.lu/article/europe-still-behind-us-in-spac)). That condition has had visible effects: Lunar Outpost, one of the fund's portfolio companies, established its European base in Luxembourg and works with the Luxembourg Space Agency on various projects ([Silicon Luxembourg](http://www.siliconluxembourg.lu/a-fund-squarely-focused-on-the-space-sector/)). By April 2022 the fund held 10 startups ([Delano](https://delano.lu/article/europe-still-behind-us-in-spac)).

The fund's rationale, as Festal puts it, is that space technology reaches far beyond launch companies and satellites: earth observation, satellite communications and innovations first invented for space now apply to real-world problems in other sectors ([Silicon Luxembourg](http://www.siliconluxembourg.lu/a-fund-squarely-focused-on-the-space-sector/)). Promus says it has been investing in space since 2014, naming Rocket Lab, Spire and ICEYE as its leading space bets ([Private Equity Wire](https://privateequitywire.com/news/promus-ventures-closes-eur120m-space-fund)). Looking ahead, Festal said in 2023 that the firm could consider raising an opportunity fund to make later-stage investments in its best companies, while continuing to deploy Orbital Ventures ([Silicon Luxembourg](http://www.siliconluxembourg.lu/a-fund-squarely-focused-on-the-space-sector/)).

## The Behavox investment

Promus's most direct link to the FactsLedger corpus is [Behavox](/organizations/behavox/), the AI compliance-surveillance company. Behavox closed an undisclosed Series A round co-led by [Hoxton Ventures](/organizations/hoxton-ventures/) and Promus Ventures in 2016, after raising $400,000 from unnamed investors in 2014 and another $1 million in 2015 ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/02/25/behavox-spies-100m-vision-fund-investment/)). Promus's own newsletter from July 2016 confirms the co-lead and dates the investment to only days after the Brexit referendum, describing Behavox as a [London](/places/london/)-based company "building the next generation compliance surveillance platform for banks, hedge funds, traders, brokers and other financial institutions utilizing NLP, voice analytics and advanced neural networks" ([Promus Ventures](https://us12.campaign-archive.com/?u=cf5606e10850059e196dadeff&id=26352e1ffa)).

The timing mattered to the firm. Collett wrote at the time that while others were debating whether London would lose its position as Europe's startup centre after the vote, Promus disagreed: London, in his view, would "remain the European center of startup investing in the forseeable future," and the firm was "excited to help the talented Behavox team build their company as well as scale into the US" ([Promus Ventures](https://us12.campaign-archive.com/?u=cf5606e10850059e196dadeff&id=26352e1ffa)). The bet fit the firm's pattern — an earlier set of natural-language-processing and AI investments, including Kensho, StatMuse and CrowdFlower, had shown it the power of machine-learning software in data-heavy verticals ([Promus Ventures](https://us12.campaign-archive.com/?u=cf5606e10850059e196dadeff&id=26352e1ffa)). Behavox went on to raise a $20 million Series A1 round led by Citigroup in 2017 and $100 million from SoftBank's Vision Fund 2 in 2020 ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/02/25/behavox-spies-100m-vision-fund-investment/)).
