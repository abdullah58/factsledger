## Overview

Eastman Kodak Company is the Rochester, New York imaging company that made photography a mass pastime: George Eastman's 1888 Kodak camera, the Brownie of 1900, and Kodachrome color film made it the dominant photographic company of the 20th century ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company)). Kodak also invented the first digital camera in 1975 ([Digital Camera World](https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/cameras/digital-cameras/kodak-didnt-want-you-to-see-this-and-kept-this-revolutionary-camera-secret-for-25-years)), yet filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2012, and emerged in 2013 as a smaller business focused on commercial printing and chemicals ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company)).

The company was incorporated in 1901 as the successor to a business established in Rochester in 1880 by George Eastman, who perfected the method of making photographic dry plates and built a machine that mechanically produced dry, precoated plates ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company)). Before 1880, photographers had to coat a plate with fresh, wet chemicals each time they wanted to take a picture, so Eastman's dry plates were the first step toward photography anyone could do ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company)). The company is headquartered in Rochester, New York, and is known for its yellow-and-red logo and stylized "K" ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company)). Today it is a publicly traded company (NYSE: KODK) describing itself as a leading global manufacturer focused on commercial print and advanced materials and chemicals ([Eastman Kodak Company](https://www.kodak.com/en/company/press-release/fourth-quarter-full-year-2024-financial-results/)).

## The camera that made photography everybody's business

Eastman's system was built on a simple bargain: a cheap camera, with the money made on film and processing. The 1888 Kodak camera was the first camera simple and portable enough to be used by large numbers of amateur photographers, sold with film sealed inside so that the whole unit was mailed back to Rochester for processing and replacement ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company)). The first model took round pictures 6.4 cm (2.5 in) in diameter and carried a roll of film for 100 exposures ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)). The Eastman Company was formed in 1889, and the business took the name Eastman Kodak Company in 1892 ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)).

The Brownie, introduced in 1900, pushed the price down further and added a removable film container, so the whole camera no longer needed to be sent back to the plant ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company)). Kodak kept widening its reach: Tennessee Eastman was founded in 1920 as a wholly owned subsidiary to make the chemicals its film products needed, and the company joined the Dow Jones Industrial Average on July 18, 1930, staying a constituent for 74 years ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)). The Starmatic of 1959, the first automatic Brownie camera, sold 10 million units over five years, and the Instamatic of 1963 made point-and-shoot photography cheaper and easier still ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)).

Kodachrome, introduced in 1935, was the first commercially successful color film, renowned for vivid color reproduction and archival durability, and it was used by professional photographers, photojournalists, and amateurs alike ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company)). Demand for it fell sharply as digital photography spread, and on June 22, 2009 Kodak said it would discontinue the film after a 74-year run, noting that only one processing facility remained; the last rolls were accepted for development by Dwayne's Photo in Parsons, Kansas, until December 30, 2010 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company)). In the 1960s Kodak introduced the Instamatic series of still and movie cameras, followed by the Disc camera series in 1982 and an 8-millimeter video camera in 1984 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company)). In January 2017, Kodak announced it was bringing back its Ektachrome film ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)).

Beyond cameras, Kodak grew into a chemicals and pharmaceuticals conglomerate and then partly retreated from it. In 1988 it bought Sterling Drug for $5.1 billion, while Eastman Chemical — a Kodak subsidiary founded in 1920 to supply its chemical needs — was spun off as a separate corporation in 1993 ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)). Kodak kept innovating in imaging: in 1986 its scientists created the world's first megapixel sensor, capable of recording 1.4 million pixels ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)).

## Kodak in court: the patent wars

Kodak's history is also a history of patent fights — first as a defendant, then as an aggressor. In 1981 Kodak was sued by Polaroid for infringement of its instant-picture patents, and the suit ran for five years before the court finally found in favour of Polaroid in 1986 ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)). Two decades later, Kodak was on the receiving end of another patent campaign — this time over the JPEG image standard.

The most far-reaching case came in April 2004, when Forgent Networks sued 31 companies for allegedly infringing a patent for a data-compression technique used in the JPEG standard for digital images ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1702489/forgent-sues-pc-makers-over-jpeg-patent-claims.html)). Eastman Kodak Co. was among the named defendants, alongside [Apple](/organizations/apple/), [Dell](/organizations/dell/), [Hewlett-Packard](/organizations/hp/), [IBM](/organizations/ibm/), Macromedia and Xerox, after Forgent was unable to negotiate licensing agreements with them ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1702489/forgent-sues-pc-makers-over-jpeg-patent-claims.html)). Forgent sought damages and an injunction prohibiting the companies from selling products with the allegedly infringing technology ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1702489/forgent-sues-pc-makers-over-jpeg-patent-claims.html)). The patent at issue was U.S. Patent No. 4,698,672, and the Independent JPEG Group had said in 2002 that Forgent's patent related to a different type of compression technology than the one used in the JPEG standard ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1702489/forgent-sues-pc-makers-over-jpeg-patent-claims.html)).

Forgent had announced in 2002 that it held the patent and planned to seek licensing agreements from any company selling products that compress or store digital images, and by April 2004 it had reached agreements with over 30 companies and received $90 million in licensing revenue ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1702489/forgent-sues-pc-makers-over-jpeg-patent-claims.html)). The campaign then began to unravel: in May 2006 the [U.S. Patent and Trademark Office](/organizations/us-patent-and-trademark-office/) rejected 19 of the patent's 47 claims on reexamination requested by the [Public Patent Foundation](/organizations/public-patent-foundation/), which said it had found prior art, overturning the broadest claims including the description of "a method for processing digital signals … to reduce the amount of data utilized" ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/180138/jpeg.html)). Forgent said it would "vigorously defend" the rejected claims, and said it had by then secured licensing deals with more than a dozen of the companies it sued, generating about $105 million in revenue ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/180138/jpeg.html)). Forgent Networks later renamed itself [Asure Software](/organizations/asure-software/) ([Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/company-profiles/asure-software/)), and the JPEG campaign — which collected more than $110 million from over 60 companies — is the central episode of that company's earlier life ([BetaNews](http://betanews.com/article/forgent-settles-jpeg-patent-litigation/)).

## Bankruptcy, reinvention, and the 2025 survival warning

Kodak invented the technology that displaced it. In 1975 Steven Sasson, then an electrical engineer at Kodak, designed and built the world's first working, self-contained digital camera in less than a year — a 3.6 kg device that recorded 0.01-megapixel black-and-white images on a data cassette ([Digital Camera World](https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/cameras/digital-cameras/kodak-didnt-want-you-to-see-this-and-kept-this-revolutionary-camera-secret-for-25-years)). In 1991 Kodak launched the Kodak Professional Digital Camera System (DCS), the first commercially available digital single-lens reflex camera — a digital back mounted on a Nikon F3 body, shown at photokina in 1990 ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)). In 2003 it introduced the EasyShare LS633, the first camera to feature an AMOLED display ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)). By the early 21st century, facing significant declines in the consumer market for cameras and other products related to film photography, Kodak placed new emphasis on digital photography products ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company)). But the digital business never replaced film: in January 2004 Kodak announced it would stop selling traditional film cameras in Europe and North America and cut up to 15,000 jobs, around a fifth of its workforce ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)).

The company then sold off pieces of itself. On August 1, 2006 it agreed to divest its digital camera manufacturing operations to Flextronics, keeping high-level design, research and development, and all intellectual property and patents ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)). On January 10, 2007 it agreed to sell Kodak Health Group to Onex Corporation for $2.35 billion in cash plus up to $200 million in additional future payments ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)). In January 2009 it posted a $137 million fourth-quarter loss and announced plans to cut up to 4,500 jobs ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)). During 2011 its shares fell more than 80 percent, and in January 2012 the New York Stock Exchange warned that its average closing price had been below $1.00 for 30 consecutive days; shares that had traded in the $90 range in 1997 closed at 76 cents on January 3, 2012 ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)).

On January 19, 2012 Kodak filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection, its stock was delisted from the NYSE and moved to OTC trading, and it ended the day down 35% at $0.36 a share ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)). It filed citing rising pension plan costs, declining profitability, and mounting competition in the digital era, and during the proceedings it ended production of digital cameras and other consumer digital imaging products ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company)). On February 9, 2012 it announced it would exit the digital image capture business entirely ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)). On December 20, 2012 it announced plans to sell its digital imaging patents for about $525 million to some of the world's biggest technology companies ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)). On April 29, 2013 it agreed with the U.K. Kodak Pension Plan to spin off its Personalized Imaging and Document Imaging businesses and settle $2.8 billion in pension-plan claims ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)). Kodak emerged from bankruptcy in September 2013, a much smaller company focused on commercial printing and imaging services for corporate clients ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company)).

The smaller Kodak is a commercial-print and chemicals business with a pharmaceutical sideline. For full-year 2024 it reported consolidated revenues of $1.043 billion, down 7% from $1.117 billion in 2023, with GAAP net income of $102 million and operational EBITDA of $26 million ([Eastman Kodak Company](https://www.kodak.com/en/company/press-release/fourth-quarter-full-year-2024-financial-results/)). Its Advanced Materials & Chemicals group built a cGMP facility for manufacturing regulated and unregulated pharmaceutical products, scheduled to begin production in 2025 ([Eastman Kodak Company](https://www.kodak.com/en/company/press-release/fourth-quarter-full-year-2024-financial-results/)). By August 2025 the company was nearing completion on the plant, already made unregulated key starting materials for pharmaceuticals, and expected production at the retrofitted facility to start later that year ([Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/shoot_newsbriefs/kodak-cautions-on-business-operations-but-remains-confident-it-can-meet-debt-obligations/)).

In August 2025 Kodak cautioned investors that there was "substantial doubt" about its ability to remain in business, citing debt maturing within 12 months and the absence of committed financing or sufficient liquidity to meet those obligations ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company)). The disclosure came as the company reported a $26 million second-quarter loss, and it had $155 million of cash and cash equivalents as of June 30, 2025, with $70 million held within the U.S. ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company); [Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/shoot_newsbriefs/kodak-cautions-on-business-operations-but-remains-confident-it-can-meet-debt-obligations/)). Kodak said the going-concern language was essentially a required disclosure and that it was confident it could pay off a significant portion of its term loan well before it became due and amend, extend or refinance the rest ([Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/shoot_newsbriefs/kodak-cautions-on-business-operations-but-remains-confident-it-can-meet-debt-obligations/)). Its shares slid more than 25% in midday trading after the disclosure ([Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/shoot_newsbriefs/kodak-cautions-on-business-operations-but-remains-confident-it-can-meet-debt-obligations/)).

What the headlines largely missed is that the warning was resolved within months. In its quarterly report for the period ended September 30, 2025, filed on November 6, 2025, Kodak said it had extended the maturity dates of its term loans, settled approximately $2.1 billion of pension obligations under the Kodak Retirement Income Plan, and would transfer the remaining liabilities of approximately $15 million for missing participants to the Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation by the end of November 2025 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/31235/000119312525269608/kodk-20250930.htm)). On that basis, the filing states, the prior conditions that raised substantial doubt about Kodak's ability to continue as a going concern "have been resolved" ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/31235/000119312525269608/kodk-20250930.htm)). The pension-plan termination the board approved in January 2025, effective March 31, 2025, was the mechanism: settling the obligation freed the surplus to pay down debt ([Eastman Kodak Company](https://www.kodak.com/en/company/press-release/fourth-quarter-full-year-2024-financial-results/)).

**What is Eastman Kodak Company?**
Eastman Kodak Company is an American imaging company headquartered in Rochester, New York, founded on George Eastman's dry-plate business of 1880 and incorporated in 1901 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company)). It made photography a mass pastime with the Kodak camera and the Brownie, and today focuses on commercial print, advanced materials and chemicals, and film ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company); [Eastman Kodak Company](https://www.kodak.com/en/company/press-release/fourth-quarter-full-year-2024-financial-results/)).

**Did Kodak invent the digital camera?**
Yes. In 1975 Steven Sasson, an electrical engineer at Kodak, designed and built the world's first working, self-contained digital camera, and in 1991 Kodak launched the Professional Digital Camera System, the first commercially available digital single-lens reflex camera ([Digital Camera World](https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/cameras/digital-cameras/kodak-didnt-want-you-to-see-this-and-kept-this-revolutionary-camera-secret-for-25-years); [Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)). Kodak invented the technology but was slow to turn it into a business.

**Why did Kodak go bankrupt?**
Kodak filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on January 19, 2012, citing rising pension plan costs, declining profitability, and mounting competition in the digital era ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company)). Film sales had collapsed, its shares had fallen more than 80% during 2011, and its digital camera business never replaced the film profits it lost ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/ap7087/kodak-ltd)).

**Is Kodak still in business?**
Yes. Kodak emerged from bankruptcy in September 2013 as a smaller company focused on commercial printing and imaging services for corporate clients ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Eastman-Kodak-Company)). It reported $1.043 billion of revenue for 2024, still makes motion-picture and still film, and was building a pharmaceutical manufacturing plant in 2025 ([Eastman Kodak Company](https://www.kodak.com/en/company/press-release/fourth-quarter-full-year-2024-financial-results/); [Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/shoot_newsbriefs/kodak-cautions-on-business-operations-but-remains-confident-it-can-meet-debt-obligations/)).

**Was Kodak sued over the JPEG patent?**
Yes. In April 2004 Forgent Networks sued 31 companies, including Eastman Kodak, alleging their use of JPEG image compression infringed U.S. Patent No. 4,698,672, and sought damages and an injunction ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1702489/forgent-sues-pc-makers-over-jpeg-patent-claims.html)). In 2006 the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office rejected 19 of the patent's 47 claims on reexamination, overturning its broadest claims ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/180138/jpeg.html)).
