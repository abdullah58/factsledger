## Overview

Ribbit Capital is a Palo Alto venture capital firm founded in 2012 by Meyer "Micky" Malka that invests exclusively in financial services technology around the world. Its early bets on Coinbase, Robinhood, Nubank and [Brex](/organizations/brex/) made it one of fintech's defining investors — most strikingly Brex, where a $7 million round it led in 2017 returned roughly 700-fold when Capital One agreed to buy the company for $5.15 billion in January 2026 ([LAVCA](https://www.lavca.org/ribbit-capital-debuts-with-100m-early-stage-fund/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)).

Ribbit describes its mission as changing the world of finance by backing visionary financial-services entrepreneurs; by May 2023 its portfolio held more than 120 private and public company investments across six continents, spanning payments, lending, insurance, crypto assets and financial infrastructure ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/comments/s7-04-23/s70423-186379-340322.pdf)). The firm scaled from a $100 million first fund to a $420 million sixth fund in January 2020 and a $1.15 billion seventh fund in January 2022 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/03/fintech-focused-ribbit-capital-raises-1-15b-in-seventh-fund-according-to-sec-filing/)). Malka appears on the Forbes Midas List of top tech investors ([TrueBridge Capital Partners](https://truebridgecapital.com/forbes/)).

## An operator's fund

Before Ribbit, Malka had built and sold five financial-services companies across three continents, including the Latin American online bank Patagon and Brazil's Banco Lemon ([LAVCA](https://www.lavca.org/ribbit-capital-debuts-with-100m-early-stage-fund/); [insights4vc](https://insights4vc.substack.com/p/what-ribbit-sees-before-everyone)). He raised Ribbit's first fund as its sole general partner, closing a $100 million fund in July 2012 after initially targeting $70–80 million; its backers included Spain's BBVA and Silicon Valley Bank alongside institutions and wealthy individuals ([LAVCA](https://www.lavca.org/ribbit-capital-debuts-with-100m-early-stage-fund/)). The fund was deliberately small — Malka said the low-hundreds-of-millions size would keep him "in check" — and he promised founders extreme availability, telling them he would be "a top number on the speed dial" alongside their wives, mothers and friends ([LAVCA](https://www.lavca.org/ribbit-capital-debuts-with-100m-early-stage-fund/)). His remit from the start was global, targeting markets including the [United States](/places/united-states/), Canada, [Brazil](/places/brazil/), the U.K., Spain, Italy, South Africa and Turkey ([LAVCA](https://www.lavca.org/ribbit-capital-debuts-with-100m-early-stage-fund/)).

The funds grew as the early bets matured — about $125 million for Fund II in 2014 and about $226 million for Fund III in 2016 ([insights4vc](https://insights4vc.substack.com/p/what-ribbit-sees-before-everyone)). Malka describes the firm not as a venture fund but as "a company that happens to deploy capital," a line that captures its hands-on, operator-first style ([insights4vc](https://insights4vc.substack.com/p/what-ribbit-sees-before-everyone)).

## The early bets that built the reputation

Ribbit's first fund bought bitcoin directly and backed Coinbase, participating as an existing investor in its $25 million Series B in December 2013, when the exchange had about 600,000 wallets ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/12/12/coinbase-raises-25m-from-andreessen-horowitz-to-build-its-bitcoin-wallet-and-merchant-services/); [insights4vc](https://insights4vc.substack.com/p/what-ribbit-sees-before-everyone)). It backed Robinhood's Series A in 2014, with Malka joining the board early, and stayed with the company through its crises ([insights4vc](https://insights4vc.substack.com/p/what-ribbit-sees-before-everyone)). The same pattern repeated abroad: Ribbit backed Nubank in Brazil and participated in Revolut's $66 million Series B in July 2017, a round led by Index Ventures that took the startup's total raised to $83 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/03/fintech-focused-ribbit-capital-raises-1-15b-in-seventh-fund-according-to-sec-filing/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/07/11/revolut-raises-66-million-for-its-global-banking-alternative/)). By January 2021 its portfolio also included Credit Karma and [Affirm](/organizations/affirm/) ([Walmart](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2021/01/11/walmart-announces-creation-of-new-fintech-startup)).

The defining test of that loyalty came in February 2021, days after the GameStop short squeeze left Robinhood facing a clearinghouse collateral demand that had spiked to about $3 billion. Ribbit led a $3.4 billion convertible-debt rescue — $1 billion announced on January 29 and a further $2.4 billion — alongside ICONIQ, Andreessen Horowitz, Sequoia, Index Ventures and NEA ([FX News Group](https://fxnewsgroup.com/forex-news/retail-forex/robinhood-ups-funding-round-to-3-4-billion/); [Finovate](https://finovate.com/robinhood-raises-3-4-billion-in-whirlwind-weekend-funding/)). Malka said Robinhood "has served millions of people who have felt left behind by America's financial system" and that Ribbit was "confident that Robinhood will emerge stronger" ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/robinhood-raises-3-4-billion-in-whirlwind-weekend-funding/)).

Brex is the purest illustration of Ribbit's early-conviction model. The founders — [Henrique Dubugras](/people/henrique-dubugras/) and [Pedro Franceschi](/people/pedro-franceschi/), who dropped out of [Stanford University](/universities/stanford-university/) as freshmen to join [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/)'s winter 2017 batch — raised about $7 million in spring 2017 in a round led by Ribbit, which they chose for Malka's fintech expertise; [Y Combinator Continuity](/organizations/y-combinator-continuity/) later led the Series B ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=17418813); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)). When Capital One agreed in January 2026 to acquire Brex for $5.15 billion — less than half its $12.3 billion peak — that early bet had multiplied roughly 700-fold, even after dilution ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)). [Kleiner Perkins](/organizations/kleiner-perkins/), DST Global and [Peter Thiel](/people/peter-thiel/) had joined the early bet; later-stage investors who entered at $7.4 billion or higher took a haircut, while Brex's rival [Ramp](/organizations/ramp/) raced the other way, from a $13 billion valuation in March 2025 to $32 billion that November ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)).

## Building companies, not just backing them

In January 2021 Walmart and Ribbit announced a fintech startup together — majority-owned by Walmart, with Malka on its board — to build financial products for Walmart's customers and its 1.6 million associates ([Walmart](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2021/01/11/walmart-announces-creation-of-new-fintech-startup); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/investment-tracker/2024/walmart-ribbit-lead-fintech-one-300-million-dollar-funding-round/)). The venture is now known as One, offering installment loans, debit cards, payments and early wage access; in December 2024 Walmart and Ribbit were reportedly leading a funding round of more than $300 million that would value it at $2.5 billion before the new money — though the round had not closed when reported ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/investment-tracker/2024/walmart-ribbit-lead-fintech-one-300-million-dollar-funding-round/)).

Ribbit has also tried building vehicles beyond funds. Its SPAC, Ribbit LEAP, listed with a deliberately long-term structure — a $100 million forward-purchase agreement and extended lock-ups — but after speaking to more than 100 potential partners it found none that met its bar, and in August 2022 it liquidated, returning about $403 million to investors; in its letter to investors Ribbit conceded the thesis behind the approach was "wrong" ([SPAC Insider](https://old.spacinsider.com/2022/08/03/ribbit-leap-to-liquidate-403m-trust/)). Alongside the flagship funds, Malka also directs Bullfrog Capital, a parallel later-stage vehicle that itself raised $1.15 billion — as much as the seventh flagship fund ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/03/fintech-focused-ribbit-capital-raises-1-15b-in-seventh-fund-according-to-sec-filing/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Ribbit Capital?**
Ribbit Capital is a Palo Alto venture capital firm founded in 2012 that invests only in financial services technology, globally. Its portfolio includes Coinbase, Robinhood, Nubank, Revolut, Affirm and Brex, and by May 2023 it held more than 120 investments across six continents ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/comments/s7-04-23/s70423-186379-340322.pdf); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/03/fintech-focused-ribbit-capital-raises-1-15b-in-seventh-fund-according-to-sec-filing/)).

**Who founded Ribbit Capital?**
Meyer "Micky" Malka, a Venezuelan entrepreneur who had built and sold five financial-services companies before turning investor. He raised a $100 million first fund in 2012 as sole general partner and remains managing partner. He also appears on the Forbes Midas List of top tech investors ([LAVCA](https://www.lavca.org/ribbit-capital-debuts-with-100m-early-stage-fund/); [TrueBridge Capital Partners](https://truebridgecapital.com/forbes/)).

**How much did Ribbit Capital make on Brex?**
Ribbit led Brex's roughly $7 million round in spring 2017. When Capital One agreed to buy Brex for $5.15 billion in January 2026, that early bet had multiplied somewhere in the neighborhood of 700-fold, even after dilution across later rounds ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)).

**Did Ribbit Capital rescue Robinhood in 2021?**
Yes. After the GameStop squeeze left Robinhood facing a clearinghouse demand of about $3 billion, Ribbit led a $3.4 billion convertible-debt round in February 2021 — $1 billion announced January 29 plus a further $2.4 billion — with ICONIQ, Andreessen Horowitz, Sequoia, Index Ventures and NEA ([FX News Group](https://fxnewsgroup.com/forex-news/retail-forex/robinhood-ups-funding-round-to-3-4-billion/)).

**What happened to Ribbit LEAP?**
Ribbit LEAP was Ribbit's SPAC. After more than 100 partner conversations produced no deal meeting its bar, it liquidated in August 2022, returning about $403 million to investors. Ribbit conceded in its investor letter that the thesis behind its long-term SPAC structure was "wrong" ([SPAC Insider](https://old.spacinsider.com/2022/08/03/ribbit-leap-to-liquidate-403m-trust/)).
