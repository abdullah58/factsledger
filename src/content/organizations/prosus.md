## Overview

Prosus is the Amsterdam-listed technology investor that holds Naspers' international internet assets, above all a large stake in China's Tencent. Created when the South African group Naspers listed those assets in September 2019, Prosus debuted with a market value of about €120.5 billion, instantly becoming Europe's largest internet company by that measure ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/ipo-creates-prosus-the-1205b-internet-giant-youve-never-heard-of?plm=11)). It is not a consumer brand: unlike a traditional internet business, Prosus offers no digital services under its own name, but holds a portfolio of businesses across classifieds, food delivery and financial services ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/ipo-creates-prosus-the-1205b-internet-giant-youve-never-heard-of?plm=11)).

This profile covers that company — the Dutch-listed vehicle, not its South African parent Naspers and not Tencent, the Chinese company in which it is the largest shareholder. Under chief executive Fabricio Bloisi, who took the helm in 2024, Prosus is trying to become less of a holding company and more of an operator, buying food-delivery and classifieds businesses outright while slowly selling Tencent shares to fund buybacks of its own stock ([Bloomberg](https://www.moneyweb.co.za/news/companies-and-deals/prosus-profit-jumps-as-bets-on-e-commerce-businesses-pay-off/); [Dow Jones Newswires](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/20260629749/prosus-targets-5-billion-buyback-more-acquisitions-to-drive-growth)).

## The 2019 listing: why Naspers built Prosus

Naspers, the South African group, had a problem of scale: its stake in Tencent had grown so large that it dominated the Johannesburg Stock Exchange, and investors buying Naspers were effectively getting its other holdings for free because the market valued the group below the Tencent stake alone ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/ipo-creates-prosus-the-1205b-internet-giant-youve-never-heard-of?plm=11)). The listing was the fix — move the international internet assets into a Dutch vehicle listed in Amsterdam, open them to a wider range of investors than South Africa's capital markets offered, and shrink Naspers' own weighting on the Johannesburg exchange from around 25% to 15% ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/ipo-creates-prosus-the-1205b-internet-giant-youve-never-heard-of?plm=11)). The market's first verdict was enthusiastic: against a Euronext reference price of €58.70, Prosus shares opened at €76, touched €77.40 intraday and closed the first day at €74.19 ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/ipo-creates-prosus-the-1205b-internet-giant-youve-never-heard-of?plm=11)).

## The Tencent stake and the discount problem

Everything about Prosus starts with one 2001 decision, when Naspers backed Tencent with a $34 million investment ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/ipo-creates-prosus-the-1205b-internet-giant-youve-never-heard-of?plm=11)). By the 2019 listing that had become a 31.1% stake worth $130 billion according to Reuters — the holding that dominated the portfolio ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/ipo-creates-prosus-the-1205b-internet-giant-youve-never-heard-of?plm=11)). The rest was real but secondary: a 28% stake in Russian internet group Mail.ru said to be worth $1.4 billion, food-delivery holdings in Takeaway.com, Delivery Hero and Swiggy, and financial-services businesses including Netherlands-based PayU and California-headquartered Wibmo ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/ipo-creates-prosus-the-1205b-internet-giant-youve-never-heard-of?plm=11)). The discount never went away: the stake stayed so large that it caused a persistent gap between the Tencent holding's value and the rest of the business, so Prosus has spent years selling Tencent shares in small amounts to fund buybacks of its own stock ([Bloomberg](https://www.moneyweb.co.za/news/companies-and-deals/prosus-profit-jumps-as-bets-on-e-commerce-businesses-pay-off/)). The Tencent gains also fund the group: Prosus sold $2 billion of noncore holdings in its last fiscal year and expects to offload more in the year through March 2027, deploying the gains into its own operations ([Dow Jones Newswires](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/20260629749/prosus-targets-5-billion-buyback-more-acquisitions-to-drive-growth)).

## From holding company to operator under Bloisi

Bloisi's answer to the discount is to make the rest of the business worth more. Since taking the helm in 2024 he has pushed the e-commerce portfolio toward profitability and spent heavily on businesses Prosus runs itself: a €4.1 billion deal for Just Eat Takeaway.com, plus Despegar and La Centrale — $8 billion of acquisitions in one fiscal year ([Bloomberg](https://www.moneyweb.co.za/news/companies-and-deals/prosus-profit-jumps-as-bets-on-e-commerce-businesses-pay-off/); [Dow Jones Newswires](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202606291793/prosus-plans-5-billion-buyback-more-investments-to-drive-growth-update)). The numbers moved with him: for the year ended 31 March 2026 revenue jumped to $9.705 billion from $6.17 billion, core headline earnings per share rose 24% to $3.78 and adjusted EBITDA reached $1.05 billion, beating the $947.3 million analyst forecast ([Dow Jones Newswires](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/20260629749/prosus-targets-5-billion-buyback-more-acquisitions-to-drive-growth); [Bloomberg](https://www.moneyweb.co.za/news/companies-and-deals/prosus-profit-jumps-as-bets-on-e-commerce-businesses-pay-off/)). The buyback is the figure to watch: Prosus plans $5 billion more of them over the coming year, funded by selling Tencent stock while the stake, worth nearly $111 billion, keeps throwing off cash ([Dow Jones Newswires](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202606291793/prosus-plans-5-billion-buyback-more-investments-to-drive-growth-update)). Yet the market is unconvinced: Prosus shares closed at €37.48 in Amsterdam and had dropped 29% in 2026 ([Bloomberg](https://www.moneyweb.co.za/news/companies-and-deals/prosus-profit-jumps-as-bets-on-e-commerce-businesses-pay-off/)).

## Prosus and Bilt Rewards

Prosus Ventures, the group's venture arm, has backed [Bilt Rewards](/organizations/bilt/) twice: in the $150 million October 2022 round led by [Left Lane Capital](/organizations/left-lane-capital/), which valued Bilt at $1.5 billion, and again in the $200 million January 2024 round led by General Catalyst, which valued Bilt at $3.1 billion ([Bloomberg](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/real-estate/bilt-startup-turning-rent-into-points-valued-at-1-5-billion); [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/technology/rent-loyalty-program-bilt-rewards-valuation-skyrockets-to-15b-116053); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/24/bilt-rewards-brings-in-200m-led-by-general-catalyst-doubles-valuation-to-3-1b/)). The January 2024 round also brought Ken Chenault, chairman and managing director of General Catalyst and former chairman and chief executive of American Express, onto Bilt's board ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/24/bilt-rewards-brings-in-200m-led-by-general-catalyst-doubles-valuation-to-3-1b/)). Bilt launched in June 2021 out of Kairos, the startup studio led by its founder and chief executive Ankur Jain, so Prosus sits in Bilt's cap table alongside the venture studio that incubated it ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/24/bilt-rewards-brings-in-200m-led-by-general-catalyst-doubles-valuation-to-3-1b/)). With the January 2024 round, Bilt had raised $413 million in total funding since that Kairos launch ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/24/bilt-rewards-brings-in-200m-led-by-general-catalyst-doubles-valuation-to-3-1b/)). The position is small next to the Tencent stake, but it shows the other side of Prosus: not just a holder of mature assets, but a venture investor in the same US consumer companies its operating businesses serve.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Prosus?** Prosus is an Amsterdam-listed technology investor holding Naspers' international internet assets, above all a large stake in China's Tencent ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/ipo-creates-prosus-the-1205b-internet-giant-youve-never-heard-of?plm=11)). It owns and operates businesses in food delivery, classifieds and payments, including Just Eat Takeaway.com and iFood, rather than selling services under its own consumer brand ([Dow Jones Newswires](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/20260629749/prosus-targets-5-billion-buyback-more-acquisitions-to-drive-growth)).

**How is Prosus different from Naspers?** Naspers is the South African parent company, based in Cape Town, which listed its international internet assets as Prosus in September 2019 and retained at least a 73% stake ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/ipo-creates-prosus-the-1205b-internet-giant-youve-never-heard-of?plm=11)). Prosus is the separately listed Dutch vehicle; buying Prosus shares is not the same as buying Naspers shares, and neither makes an investor a direct Tencent shareholder.

**Why does Prosus trade below the value of its Tencent stake?** Because the Tencent holding is so large it dominates the portfolio, the market has long valued Prosus below the stake alone — the discount Naspers hoped the 2019 listing would close ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/ipo-creates-prosus-the-1205b-internet-giant-youve-never-heard-of?plm=11)). Prosus sells Tencent shares gradually and uses the proceeds to buy back its own stock, betting the discount narrows as its operating businesses grow ([Bloomberg](https://www.moneyweb.co.za/news/companies-and-deals/prosus-profit-jumps-as-bets-on-e-commerce-businesses-pay-off/)).

**Who runs Prosus?** Fabricio Bloisi, who took the helm as chief executive in 2024 ([Bloomberg](https://www.moneyweb.co.za/news/companies-and-deals/prosus-profit-jumps-as-bets-on-e-commerce-businesses-pay-off/)). He is focused on food-delivery platforms and gaining market share in Latin America, Europe and India, and has said the group made three big acquisitions in a year, spending $6 billion in Europe and $2 billion in Latin America ([Bloomberg](https://www.moneyweb.co.za/news/companies-and-deals/prosus-profit-jumps-as-bets-on-e-commerce-businesses-pay-off/)).

**Does Prosus still own Tencent shares?** Yes. Prosus remains the biggest shareholder in Tencent, with a stake worth nearly $111 billion as of June 2026 ([Dow Jones Newswires](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202606291793/prosus-plans-5-billion-buyback-more-investments-to-drive-growth-update)). It has been selling the stake down gradually since 2022 to fund its open-ended buyback programme, so the percentage falls over time while the dollar value remains the cornerstone of the portfolio.
