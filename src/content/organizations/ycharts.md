## Overview

YCharts is a Chicago-based investment research and client engagement platform founded in 2009 by Shawn Carpenter and Ara Anjargolian, serving more than 15,000 financial professionals across North America with data, charting, portfolio tools and, since 2026, an AI agent called "Y" ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/860696/ycharts-unveils-specialized-ai-agent-for-financial-advisors-and-asset-managers/); [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20260901333051/ycharts-makes-inc-5000-list-10th-consecutive-year)). It calls itself "The Financial Terminal of the Web", positioning its web-based platform as the middle ground between free services such as Yahoo Finance and a [Bloomberg](/organizations/bloomberg/) terminal costing more than $20,000 a year ([HPA (Forbes)](https://hpa.vc/news_articles/ycharts-financial-terminal-delivers-powerful-data-analytics/); [Chicago Tribune](https://hpa.vc/news_articles/ycharts-announces-6-million-funding-hires-morningstar-executive/)). Private equity firm LLR Partners bought the company in an all-cash growth recapitalization in 2020, when YCharts expected to pass $15 million in annual recurring revenue ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/10/09/ycharts-sells-to-pe-firm-in-all-cash-transaction-as-it-looks-to-pass-15m-arr-this-year/)).

YCharts sells to the wealth management industry rather than to traders or the general public: its customers are registered investment advisors, broker-dealers and asset managers who use it to research securities, build portfolios and communicate with clients ([MutualFunds.com](https://www.mutualfunds.com/expert-opinion/q-and-a-sean-brown-ceo-ycharts/)). That focus, plus a decade of consecutive appearances on the Inc. 5000 list of America's fastest-growing private companies, makes it one of the more durable independent survivors of the post-2008 wave of advisor-technology startups — a company that grew from a charting website into an all-in-one advisor platform without ever taking the terminal-makers head on.

## From a Google insight to the financial terminal of the web

Shawn Carpenter developed the idea for YCharts during three and a half years at [Google](/organizations/google/), where he worked on the revenue-intelligence team with access to Google's global query database, looking for ways to help advertisers reach audiences more efficiently ([HPA (Forbes)](https://hpa.vc/news_articles/ycharts-financial-terminal-delivers-powerful-data-analytics/)). Seeing the analytical power Google applied to its own data, he concluded financial services could use something similar — powerful visual tools working across large amounts of data ([HPA (Forbes)](https://hpa.vc/news_articles/ycharts-financial-terminal-delivers-powerful-data-analytics/)). He founded YCharts in Chicago in 2009 with Ara Anjargolian, aiming it at financial planners who had left big wire houses to set up on their own and needed something better than Yahoo Finance and less expensive than a Bloomberg terminal ([HPA (Forbes)](https://hpa.vc/news_articles/ycharts-financial-terminal-delivers-powerful-data-analytics/)).

Early backing came from Chicago's own investor community. A 2010 Series A was led by Hyde Park Angels and I2A Fund, and Morningstar — itself Chicago-based — led the Series B and C rounds and at one point owned 20% of the company, with Reed Elsevier another major investor ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/10/09/ycharts-sells-to-pe-firm-in-all-cash-transaction-as-it-looks-to-pass-15m-arr-this-year/); [HPA (Forbes)](https://hpa.vc/news_articles/ycharts-financial-terminal-delivers-powerful-data-analytics/)). In May 2015 YCharts closed a $6 million Series C led by Morningstar, taking total funding raised to $14.5 million, and hired Jeremy Diamond — Morningstar's former head of buy-side solutions and a former YCharts board member — as chief revenue officer ([Chicago Tribune](https://hpa.vc/news_articles/ycharts-announces-6-million-funding-hires-morningstar-executive/)). Carpenter said the company had reached product/market fit in August 2014, after originally estimating it would take four to five years to build a product competitive with Bloomberg, S&P Capital IQ and [Reuters](/organizations/reuters/) ([Chicago Tribune](https://hpa.vc/news_articles/ycharts-announces-6-million-funding-hires-morningstar-executive/)).

## The product: advisor research at a fraction of terminal prices

YCharts provides information in a useful visual form, but it does not provide investment advice — a deliberate boundary its founder stressed from the start ([HPA (Forbes)](https://hpa.vc/news_articles/ycharts-financial-terminal-delivers-powerful-data-analytics/)). Around 2016 the platform covered equities, 42,000 mutual funds, exchange-traded funds and 400,000 economic indicators, with charting, advanced screens and an Excel plug-in that let users run their own models through the platform's data via a full API ([HPA (Forbes)](https://hpa.vc/news_articles/ycharts-financial-terminal-delivers-powerful-data-analytics/)). A 2019 interview put the economic-indicator count at more than 300,000, alongside data on all mutual funds and ETFs, stocks and popular indices ([MutualFunds.com](https://www.mutualfunds.com/expert-opinion/q-and-a-sean-brown-ceo-ycharts/)). The platform was built for investors with horizons of three months or longer rather than short-term traders, and at the time it did not offer fixed income and had limited foreign-exchange coverage ([HPA (Forbes)](https://hpa.vc/news_articles/ycharts-financial-terminal-delivers-powerful-data-analytics/)). Its company pages publish annual revenue series for listed firms — its [Asure Software](/organizations/asure-software/) page, for example, lists annual revenue of $140.54 million for the year ended 31 December 2025, against $119.79 million for 2024 ([YCharts](https://ycharts.com/companies/ASUR/revenues_annual)).

The pricing gap against the incumbents was the core of the pitch. Tiered pricing ran from under $500 a year for students and individual investors to $4,000 a year for professional advisors, against a Bloomberg subscription costing more than $20,000 a year ([HPA (Forbes)](https://hpa.vc/news_articles/ycharts-financial-terminal-delivers-powerful-data-analytics/); [Chicago Tribune](https://hpa.vc/news_articles/ycharts-announces-6-million-funding-hires-morningstar-executive/)). Carpenter's argument was that clients of comprehensive terminals use only a tiny fraction of what is in the system, so YCharts would build the parts most people actually use and do them well ([Chicago Tribune](https://hpa.vc/news_articles/ycharts-announces-6-million-funding-hires-morningstar-executive/)). The approach found an audience: the site drew about a million monthly visitors and more than 500 free trials a day, and YCharts' charts were syndicated to financial news sites including [Forbes](/organizations/forbes/), Yahoo and Motley Fool ([HPA (Forbes)](https://hpa.vc/news_articles/ycharts-financial-terminal-delivers-powerful-data-analytics/)).

Over time the product moved beyond charting into the full advisor workflow. Model Portfolios, its newest product at the time of a 2019 interview, lets asset managers and wealth advisors scale the production, testing and evaluation of model investment strategies and generate customized reports ([MutualFunds.com](https://www.mutualfunds.com/expert-opinion/q-and-a-sean-brown-ceo-ycharts/)). More than 25 asset management firms had armed their wholesalers with YCharts by then, and each client was paired with a dedicated account manager — customer support the company presents as a differentiator, with its largest product launches born from intensive work on client use cases ([MutualFunds.com](https://www.mutualfunds.com/expert-opinion/q-and-a-sean-brown-ceo-ycharts/)).

## New leadership, a new owner, and the AI turn

In October 2016 Sean Brown became chief executive, replacing founder Shawn Carpenter, who left to launch RepIQ, an account-based sales platform using machine learning and big data ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/emerging-technologies/2475746/ycharts-new-ceo-shuffles-execs-hones-strategy)). Brown had previously been president and managing director of Interactive Data's 7Ticks low-latency infrastructure business ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/emerging-technologies/2475746/ycharts-new-ceo-shuffles-execs-hones-strategy)). Under Brown the company kept growing: by 2019 it counted nearly 5,000 wealth advisors, asset managers, broker-dealers and individual investors as users, and by 2026 it reported more than 15,000 financial professionals across North America ([MutualFunds.com](https://www.mutualfunds.com/expert-opinion/q-and-a-sean-brown-ceo-ycharts/); [citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/860696/ycharts-unveils-specialized-ai-agent-for-financial-advisors-and-asset-managers/)).

The defining transaction of Brown's tenure came on 9 October 2020, when YCharts announced it had been sold to LLR Partners in an all-cash deal the companies called a growth recapitalization — structured so the firm would not be stripped of talent in pursuit of near-term earnings ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/10/09/ycharts-sells-to-pe-firm-in-all-cash-transaction-as-it-looks-to-pass-15m-arr-this-year/)). YCharts told TechCrunch it expected to surpass $15 million in annual recurring revenue that year, having grown its top line at a compound annual growth rate of 30–40% for the past several years ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/10/09/ycharts-sells-to-pe-firm-in-all-cash-transaction-as-it-looks-to-pass-15m-arr-this-year/)). Brown said LLR offered more than capital — domain experience, a dedicated fintech team and relevant operational expertise — and that as part of the buyout of existing shareholders LLR would fund capital onto YCharts' balance sheet for product, sales and marketing investment ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/10/09/ycharts-sells-to-pe-firm-in-all-cash-transaction-as-it-looks-to-pass-15m-arr-this-year/)). The deal also set up YCharts to pursue complementary acquisitions, and its previous investors — Hyde Park Angels, I2a, REV Venture Partners (backed by RELX) and Morningstar — exited ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/10/09/ycharts-sells-to-pe-firm-in-all-cash-transaction-as-it-looks-to-pass-15m-arr-this-year/)).

Growth since then has been steady rather than explosive, and the recognition has compounded: YCharts was named to the Inc. 5000 list for a tenth consecutive year in September 2026, a streak the company pairs with a 2026 Wealth Management Industry Awards finalist nod in Portfolio Analytics for its Individual Bond Data offering, which adds bond-level transparency to portfolio analysis ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20260901333051/ycharts-makes-inc-5000-list-10th-consecutive-year)). In the 2026 T3/Inside Information Technology Survey it was named a "Software All Star" in five categories, and it won a Wealthie award for the AI Chat feature it rolled out in 2025 ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/860696/ycharts-unveils-specialized-ai-agent-for-financial-advisors-and-asset-managers/)).

The newest chapter is agentic AI. On 16 June 2026 YCharts launched "Y", a specialized AI agent built on [Anthropic](/organizations/anthropic/)'s Claude that operates natively inside the platform, accepting plain-language requests and executing workflows end to end — building portfolios, running reports, screening securities and generating client-ready content — and running continuously in the background to monitor markets, refresh commentary and execute scheduled reports ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/860696/ycharts-unveils-specialized-ai-agent-for-financial-advisors-and-asset-managers/)). Unlike a general-purpose chatbot, Y works inside the YCharts data and tool ecosystem, and it was built with compliance guardrails, customizable disclosure language and what the company describes as 16 years of regulatory experience ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/860696/ycharts-unveils-specialized-ai-agent-for-financial-advisors-and-asset-managers/)). Y is included in all subscriptions, with a monthly credit allotment and additional usage packages from 16 July 2026 ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/860696/ycharts-unveils-specialized-ai-agent-for-financial-advisors-and-asset-managers/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is YCharts?**
YCharts is a Chicago-based investment research and client engagement platform for wealth management professionals, offering data, charting, portfolio construction and client-communication tools. Founded in 2009, it serves more than 15,000 financial professionals across North America ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/860696/ycharts-unveils-specialized-ai-agent-for-financial-advisors-and-asset-managers/)).

**Who founded YCharts?**
Shawn Carpenter and Ara Anjargolian founded YCharts in Chicago in 2009. Carpenter conceived the idea while working on Google's revenue-intelligence team, where he saw how powerful visual tools could work across large amounts of data ([HPA (Forbes)](https://hpa.vc/news_articles/ycharts-financial-terminal-delivers-powerful-data-analytics/)).

**Who owns YCharts?**
Private equity firm LLR Partners has owned YCharts since October 2020, when it bought the company in an all-cash transaction described as a growth recapitalization. The price was not disclosed, and previous investors including Morningstar and Hyde Park Angels exited ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/10/09/ycharts-sells-to-pe-firm-in-all-cash-transaction-as-it-looks-to-pass-15m-arr-this-year/)).

**How is YCharts different from a Bloomberg terminal?**
YCharts positions itself between free services such as Yahoo Finance and a Bloomberg terminal: professional pricing was under $4,000 a year per user against more than $20,000 for Bloomberg, and it covers the data most advisors actually use — equities, funds, ETFs and economic indicators — rather than the full terminal feature set ([Chicago Tribune](https://hpa.vc/news_articles/ycharts-announces-6-million-funding-hires-morningstar-executive/)).

**What is "Y", YCharts' AI agent?**
Launched on 16 June 2026, Y is an AI agent built on Anthropic's Claude that works natively inside YCharts, executing advisor workflows from plain-language requests — building portfolios, running reports, screening securities and generating client-ready content — and it is included in all subscriptions ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/860696/ycharts-unveils-specialized-ai-agent-for-financial-advisors-and-asset-managers/)).
