## Overview

Meritech Capital Partners is a Palo Alto, [California](/places/california/), venture firm founded in 1999 that backs technology companies after they have found product-market fit, investing in what it calls "market leaders in markets that matter" ([Meritech Capital](https://www.meritechcapital.com)). Its best-known bets include [Salesforce](/organizations/salesforce/), [Facebook](/organizations/facebook/), [Snowflake](/organizations/snowflake/) and [Datadog](/organizations/datadog/), and the firm says it has partnered with more than 60 companies through IPOs across 200-plus investments since 1999 ([CFI.co](https://cfi.co/awards/industries/technology/2023/meritech-capital-partners-most-innovative-technology-venture-capital-investor-us-2023/)).

Meritech's place in the site's own graph is concrete: it participated in [ClickUp](/organizations/clickup/)'s $400 million Series C in October 2021, the round that valued the productivity platform at $4 billion post-money ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2224795)). The firm is based in the [United States](/places/united-states/) at 245 Lytton Avenue, Suite 125, in downtown Palo Alto ([SEC Form D](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1812097/0001812097-20-000001.txt)).

## Founding: a fund built to fill the gap

Meritech was born out of a specific hole in the late-1990s venture market. In the early 1990s, companies typically went from early-stage venture capital straight to an IPO, with nothing in between. Then the big buyers of IPOs got bigger and stopped wanting small transactions, pulling the IPO market and early-stage venture capital apart — and, in co-founder Rob Ward's words, "there was a hole in the market. That's what Meritech was formed to take advantage of. We created a firm, but we were also involved in creating the market" ([Verkada](https://www.verkada.com/ca/blog/a-verkada-investors-playbook-rob-ward-on-25-years-of-growth-investing/)).

The firm was set up in 1999 as a jointly sponsored late-stage vehicle. A company release from October 2000 states it "was co-founded and sponsored by five leading venture firms: Accel Partners, Brentwood Venture Capital, Oak Investment Partners, Redpoint Ventures and Worldview Technology Partners" ([Vivace Networks](http://host.web-print-design.com/vivace/pr1.htm)). Contemporary coverage in June 1999 had named only four of the five — omitting Redpoint — so the five-firm list rests on the later, enumerating source. By June 1999 the firm had already raised $600 million for late-stage investments and was on its way to $1 billion in commitments, with Paul Madera as managing director and Accel temporarily housing the team; each sponsor firm held a veto over every investment ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/opinion/show-them-the-late-stage-money-754550)).

Paul Madera co-founded Meritech in 1999 and remains a general partner, leading its consumer, enterprise and fintech practices; before venture investing he worked at Morgan Stanley and, earlier still, flew fighters for the US Air Force ([SF Bay Hispanic Chamber of Commerce](https://www.sfbayhcc.com/palo-alto/investment-financial-holdings-ecosystem/meritech-capital-partners)). Ward, the other verified co-founder, co-founded the firm the same year ([Verkada](https://www.verkada.com/ca/blog/a-verkada-investors-playbook-rob-ward-on-25-years-of-growth-investing/)). Mike Gordon was a managing director in the firm's early years — he is quoted as such in the October 2000 Vivace release — but whether he was a co-founder is not settled by any source read for this profile. Early on, Meritech's business was leading big late-stage rounds: it led Vivace Networks' $83 million third round in October 2000 ([Vivace Networks](http://host.web-print-design.com/vivace/pr1.htm)).

## The growth-stage model

Ward's definition of growth capital is the firm's operating manual in one paragraph: it means "coming in once a company already has a product in market and has established product-market fit. The customers are already starting to purchase more, it's really taking off, and it's just a question of adding more fuel to the fire" ([Verkada](https://www.verkada.com/ca/blog/a-verkada-investors-playbook-rob-ward-on-25-years-of-growth-investing/)). Where early-stage investors can be "'or' investors" — loving the product but hating the team, or the reverse — Meritech, Ward says, is an "'and' investor": "You have to have it all — the right market *and* the right product *and* the right team *and* the right customer validation *and* the right financial model."

That selectivity is structural, not just rhetoric. The firm describes itself as "a small, equal partnership" that works "with a handful of founders that define each generation," with no separate platform or "value-add" teams — "the partners do all the work" — and a stated preference for "low in volume, high in conviction" ([Meritech Capital](https://www.meritechcapital.com)). Its fourth operating principle is a performance claim in itself: "We're only as good as our last founder reference" — followed by the firm's headline numbers, "60+ IPOs across 200+ investments since 1999" ([Meritech Capital](https://www.meritechcapital.com)). The economics of the model show up in the holding pattern: co-founder Paul Madera said in 2020 that the median time from investment to exit was about 3.5 years, and that the firm generally holds stock in portfolio companies after they go public ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/meritech-capital-800-million-for-seventh-fund/)).

Cheque sizes grew with the funds. In 1999 the pitch was the ability "to write $30 million to $40 million checks in a timely fashion," in Accel's James Breyer's words, at a then-modest 1.5% management fee plus 20% of profits ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/opinion/show-them-the-late-stage-money-754550)). By 2020 the firm generally wrote cheques from $10 million to $100 million ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/meritech-capital-800-million-for-seventh-fund/)).

## The portfolio: from Salesforce to Snowflake

The firm's record is best read as a list of companies it joined before they became obvious. CFI.co's 2023 award citation puts it plainly: Meritech "has partnered with more than 50 next-generation companies through IPOs, including Salesforce, Facebook, Tableau, Datadog and UiPath" ([CFI.co](https://cfi.co/awards/industries/technology/2023/meritech-capital-partners-most-innovative-technology-venture-capital-investor-us-2023/)). Ward's own account of the names he backed adds Snowflake, Duo, Looker, [Roblox](/organizations/roblox/) and [NetSuite](/organizations/netsuite/) to that list, along with Salesforce, Tableau and the others — work he says landed him on the Forbes Midas List multiple times ([Verkada](https://www.verkada.com/ca/blog/a-verkada-investors-playbook-rob-ward-on-25-years-of-growth-investing/)). Pulse2's 2020 fund coverage names Datadog, Coupa Software and Facebook as early investments, all of which reached IPOs ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/meritech-capital-800-million-for-seventh-fund/)). The firm's current founder showcase — the founders it chooses to display — runs from Marc Benioff (Salesforce) and Mark Zuckerberg (Meta) through Frank Slootman (Snowflake) and the Datadog founders to newer names such as [Clay](/organizations/clay/), Kalshi, Cognition and Framer ([Meritech Capital](https://www.meritechcapital.com)).

## Meritech Insights and Benchmarking: the firm's public product

The least-covered part of Meritech's story — and the one no directory profile tells — is that the firm gives away its own research apparatus. In April 2020 it established Meritech Insights, a free database of comprehensive non-GAAP financials and imputed metrics for public SaaS companies; two years later it introduced a revamped version, Meritech Benchmarking, a free web application with trading history for almost all public SaaS companies plus valuation tools, operational KPIs, financial metrics and a comparison table with sortable filters ([CFI.co](https://cfi.co/awards/industries/technology/2023/meritech-capital-partners-most-innovative-technology-venture-capital-investor-us-2023/)). The product is a natural extension of the investment model: a firm that has spent 25 years judging SaaS companies on growth, retention and valuation multiples turned its internal benchmarks into a public good, and the benchmarking work — including the S-1 teardowns by general partner Alex Clayton — has become one of the firm's most visible calling cards. It was also the stated reason for the firm's 2023 CFI.co award as the Most Innovative Technology Venture Capital Investor in the US ([CFI.co](https://cfi.co/awards/industries/technology/2023/meritech-capital-partners-most-innovative-technology-venture-capital-investor-us-2023/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Meritech Capital Partners?** It is a Palo Alto growth-stage venture capital firm founded in 1999 that invests in technology companies after they have found product-market fit, backing "market leaders in markets that matter" ([Meritech Capital](https://www.meritechcapital.com)).

**Who founded Meritech Capital Partners?** Paul Madera and Rob Ward co-founded the firm in 1999 as a late-stage vehicle sponsored by five established venture firms ([SF Bay Hispanic Chamber of Commerce](https://www.sfbayhcc.com/palo-alto/investment-financial-holdings-ecosystem/meritech-capital-partners); [Verkada](https://www.verkada.com/ca/blog/a-verkada-investors-playbook-rob-ward-on-25-years-of-growth-investing/)).

**How much money has Meritech raised?** Its last four flagship funds total $3.03 billion: Fund V $500 million (2014), Fund VI $630 million (2018), Fund VII $800 million (2020) and Fund VIII $1.1 billion (2022), with the last two verified to the dollar in SEC filings ([SEC Form D](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1929740/000192974022000001/0001929740-22-000001.txt)).

**Which famous companies has Meritech backed?** Salesforce, Facebook, Snowflake, Datadog, Roblox, Tableau and UiPath are among the 50-plus companies it partnered with through IPOs, alongside earlier names such as Coupa, NetSuite, Duo and Looker ([CFI.co](https://cfi.co/awards/industries/technology/2023/meritech-capital-partners-most-innovative-technology-venture-capital-investor-us-2023/)).

**What is Meritech Benchmarking?** A free web application the firm launched in 2022 (as Meritech Insights in 2020) publishing non-GAAP financials, valuation multiples, operational KPIs and trading history for almost every public SaaS company ([CFI.co](https://cfi.co/awards/industries/technology/2023/meritech-capital-partners-most-innovative-technology-venture-capital-investor-us-2023/)).
