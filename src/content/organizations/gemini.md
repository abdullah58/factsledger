## Overview

Gemini is an American cryptocurrency exchange and custodian founded in 2014 by Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss, operating as a New York-chartered trust company. It built its reputation on asking regulators for permission first, then nearly lost it when its Gemini Earn lending program froze about $900 million of customer money in 2022 — money that was eventually returned in full. It listed on Nasdaq as GEMI in September 2025, and in February 2026 it pulled out of the UK, EU and Australia to refocus on the United States and prediction markets ([Reuters](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025:newsml_L3N3UZ178:0-winklevoss-twins-crypto-exchange-gemini-valued-at-4-4-billion-in-strong-nasdaq-debut/); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/357070/crypto-exchange-gemini-slashes-jobs-amid-exit-from-europe-australia)).

This profile is about the cryptocurrency exchange — not Google's Gemini AI model. The two share only a name, and nothing here concerns the AI product. Gemini's parent company is Gemini Space Station, Inc., and its operating company, Gemini Trust Company, LLC, is a limited purpose trust company chartered by the New York State Department of Financial Services (NYDFS) in October 2015 ([NYDFS](http://www.dfs.ny.gov/reports_and_publications/press_releases/pr1510051)). Tyler Winklevoss is chief executive officer and Cameron Winklevoss is president ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/357070/crypto-exchange-gemini-slashes-jobs-amid-exit-from-europe-australia)).

## A regulated start: charter, launch, and a stablecoin

Gemini's founding idea was that a crypto exchange should be regulated like a bank before it traded a single coin. The twins applied to NYDFS for a trust-company charter in July 2015, and on October 5, 2015 the regulator granted it after reviewing the company's anti-money-laundering, capitalization, consumer-protection and cyber-security standards — making Gemini the third virtual-currency firm to receive a charter or license from NYDFS, after itBit and Circle ([NYDFS](http://www.dfs.ny.gov/reports_and_publications/press_releases/pr1510051)). The exchange opened for trading on October 8, 2015, for individual and institutional customers, charging 0.25% to buyers and sellers, with bitcoin held in multi-signature cold storage and fiat deposits at a New York bank eligible for FDIC insurance ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/10/05/winklevoss-twins-receive-approval-to-launch-bitcoin-exchange-gemini/)). Tyler Winklevoss said the brothers had deliberately not applied for a BitLicense because they wanted a trust charter so the exchange could be used and trusted by both "Main Street and Wall Street" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/10/05/winklevoss-twins-receive-approval-to-launch-bitcoin-exchange-gemini/)).

The regulatory-first posture extended to product. On September 10, 2018, Gemini launched the Gemini dollar (GUSD), a stablecoin built on Ethereum and strictly pegged 1:1 to the US dollar, with Gemini Trust Company holding corresponding dollar deposits ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2018/09/10/gemini-launches-nydfs-regulated-crypto-pegged-to-the-dollar)). NYDFS had reviewed and approved the token, the backing fiat was held at State Street Bank with FDIC pass-through deposit insurance within limits, and independent auditor BPM was to review the holdings monthly ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2018/09/10/gemini-launches-nydfs-regulated-crypto-pegged-to-the-dollar)). Gemini also acquired the NFT marketplace Nifty Gateway in 2019, a business it would close in late January 2026 ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/357070/crypto-exchange-gemini-slashes-jobs-amid-exit-from-europe-australia)).

## The Earn collapse and the regulatory reckoning

The hardest test of Gemini's regulated-exchange identity came from a lending product. In December 2020, Genesis Global Capital agreed with Gemini to offer Gemini customers the chance to loan crypto to Genesis for interest, and from February 2021 Gemini Earn was offered to retail investors: customers tendered crypto to Genesis, Gemini acted as agent, and Gemini deducted an agent fee sometimes as high as 4.29% from the returns Genesis paid ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-7)). In November 2022 Genesis announced it would not allow Earn investors to withdraw their crypto, because it lacked sufficient liquid assets; at the time Genesis held approximately $900 million in investor assets from 340,000 Gemini Earn investors ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-7)). NYDFS later put the damage differently: Genesis defaulted on approximately $1 billion in loans from Earn customers, harming over 200,000 customers including almost 30,000 New Yorkers, and Gemini terminated the program in January 2023 ([NYDFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/reports_and_publications/press_releases/pr202402282)).

The legal consequences arrived in layers. On January 12, 2023, the [US Securities and Exchange Commission](/organizations/us-securities-and-exchange-commission/) charged Genesis and Gemini with the unregistered offer and sale of securities to retail investors through Earn, alleging violations of Sections 5(a) and 5(c) of the Securities Act of 1933 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-7)). On February 28, 2024, NYDFS announced a settlement: Gemini committed to return at least $1.1 billion to Earn customers through the Genesis bankruptcy, contribute $40 million to the bankruptcy for their benefit, and pay a $37 million fine for "significant failures that threatened the safety and soundness of the company" ([NYDFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/reports_and_publications/press_releases/pr202402282)). The regulator found Gemini had failed to vet Genesis, an unregulated third party, failed to maintain adequate reserves, and let an unregulated affiliate, Gemini Liquidity, LLC, collect hundreds of millions of dollars in fees from customers ([NYDFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/reports_and_publications/press_releases/pr202402282)). Gemini also settled with New York Attorney General Letitia James in 2024 for $50 million, meant to pay 230,000 investors allegedly defrauded by the program ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/sec-agrees-to-dismiss-gemini-lawsuit/810508/)).

A separate, older matter ran in parallel. The CFTC sued Gemini in 2022, alleging false or misleading statements during the agency's review of a proposed bitcoin futures contract; the case settled through a consent order entered in January 2025 imposing a $5 million civil penalty, which Gemini paid, plus a permanent injunction barring false or misleading statements to the CFTC ([CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/judge-rejects-gemini-cftc-bid-vacate-5m-settlement)). In May 2026 the CFTC joined Gemini's motion to vacate that order, saying the complaint should not have been filed — but on October 15, 2026, US District Judge Alvin Hellerstein denied the motion, ruling the enforcement shift was a change in policy, not law, and that Gemini had chosen to settle weeks before trial; the penalty and injunction stand ([CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/judge-rejects-gemini-cftc-bid-vacate-5m-settlement)).

The Earn story ended better for customers than almost any comparable crypto collapse: through the Genesis bankruptcy proceedings between May and June 2024, Earn customers received a full, 100% return of their crypto assets in kind ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/sec-agrees-to-dismiss-gemini-lawsuit/810508/)). On January 23, 2026, the SEC and Gemini filed a joint stipulation asking the federal court in Manhattan to dismiss the 2023 lawsuit, the SEC saying that because investors had been made whole, dismissal was appropriate ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/24/sec-drops-lawsuit-against-winklevoss-twins-gemini-crypto-exchange/)).

## Going public — and pulling back

Gemini filed to go public on June 6, 2025, confidentially submitting a draft Form S-1 registration statement to the SEC for a proposed IPO of its Class A common stock ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2025-06-06-winklevoss-founded-crypto-exchange-gemini-files-draft-statement-to-go-public-357330)). It priced the offering at $28 per share, selling 15.2 million shares and raising $425 million at a valuation of about $3.3 billion, with the offering 20 times oversubscribed ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/09/10/winklevoss-backed-gemini-prices-ipo-at-usd28-share-values-crypto-exchange-at-more-than-usd3b)). [Goldman Sachs](/organizations/goldman-sachs/) led the underwriting alongside Citigroup, [Morgan Stanley](/organizations/morgan-stanley/) and Cantor, and [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/) agreed to buy $50 million of Class A stock in a private placement at the IPO price ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2025/09/10/winklevoss-backed-gemini-prices-ipo-at-usd28-share-values-crypto-exchange-at-more-than-usd3b)). Shares began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker GEMI on Friday, September 12, 2025, opened at $37.01 and closed 32.2% higher, valuing the company at $4.4 billion — making Gemini the third US-listed crypto exchange, after Coinbase and Bullish ([Reuters](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025:newsml_L3N3UZ178:0-winklevoss-twins-crypto-exchange-gemini-valued-at-4-4-billion-in-strong-nasdaq-debut/)). It listed in the same 2025 fintech IPO wave as [Chime](/organizations/chime/), which had gone public that June, in what [Reuters](/organizations/reuters/) called the IPO market's busiest week since July 2021 ([Reuters](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025:newsml_L3N3UZ178:0-winklevoss-twins-crypto-exchange-gemini-valued-at-4-4-billion-in-strong-nasdaq-debut/)). The listing came with a loss-making balance sheet: Gemini reported a net loss of $282.5 million in the first half of 2025, up from $41.4 million a year earlier, while the Winklevoss twins held about 75 million shares worth roughly $2.78 billion by Reuters' calculations from SEC filings ([Reuters](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025:newsml_L3N3UZ178:0-winklevoss-twins-crypto-exchange-gemini-valued-at-4-4-billion-in-strong-nasdaq-debut/)).

Five months later the company reversed its global expansion. On February 5, 2026, Gemini announced it would exit the United Kingdom, European Union and Australia, cut its workforce by about 25%, and focus on the United States and prediction markets ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/357070/crypto-exchange-gemini-slashes-jobs-amid-exit-from-europe-australia)). Customer accounts in the affected regions moved to withdrawal-only mode on March 5, 2026, with full closure on April 6, 2026, and users could transfer balances to eToro via a partnership ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/357070/crypto-exchange-gemini-slashes-jobs-amid-exit-from-europe-australia)). The founders wrote that Gemini had expanded to more than 60 countries and was "stretched thin", that foreign markets "have proven hard to win", and that "America has the world's greatest capital markets" ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/357070/crypto-exchange-gemini-slashes-jobs-amid-exit-from-europe-australia)). The pivot's centerpiece was prediction markets: Gemini had earned a CFTC license in December 2025 to open a US prediction-market platform, which had since drawn more than 10,000 users and $24 million in trading volume ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/357070/crypto-exchange-gemini-slashes-jobs-amid-exit-from-europe-australia)). The market's verdict was visible in the share price: Gemini traded at $6.77 on February 5, 2026, roughly three-quarters below its $28 IPO price ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/357070/crypto-exchange-gemini-slashes-jobs-amid-exit-from-europe-australia)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim the Earn investor counts are consistent across sources — the SEC, NYDFS and the Attorney General settlement give different figures, and each is stated with its source. It does not claim the court has entered the dismissal of the SEC's Earn lawsuit, only that the SEC and Gemini jointly asked for it in January 2026. It does not present the CFTC consent-order facts from the CFTC's own release, which could not be opened in full; they are cited to a report of the court order, and a future refresh should cite the CFTC release directly. It does not assert a current share price, headcount, or valuation beyond February 5, 2026, and it does not claim the Winklevoss twins' post-IPO voting power, which was reported but not verified from a source read in full. Finally, it does not describe Google's Gemini AI model, a different product that shares only the name.
