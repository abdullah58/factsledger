## Overview

Suncorp Group is a Brisbane-based general insurance group operating across Australia and New Zealand, formed in 1996 when the Queensland government insurer Suncorp merged with Metway Bank and the Queensland Industry Development Corporation ([QUT](https://www.qut.edu.au/news?news-id=107542)). Insurance trade press describes it as the country's largest general insurance group ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/companies/suncorp/53088/?p=36)), while independent analysts describe a dominant market position in Australian and New Zealand general insurance ([Morningstar Australia](https://www.morningstar.com.au/stocks/peak-profit-asx-listed-insurer)). Since ANZ completed its $4.9 billion acquisition of Suncorp Bank on 31 July 2024, the group has been a dedicated, pure-play insurer ([Insurance News](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/daily/suncorp-keen-to-get-on-with-the-job-as-bank-sale-closes)). Its chief executive is Steve Johnston ([Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/suncorp-reports-1-8bn-profit-in-fy2025/)).

## From a Queensland government office to a listed group

Suncorp's story starts in government, not in the private market. It began life in 1916 as the State Accident Insurance Office, soon renamed the State Government Insurance Office (SGIO), which served as the Queensland Government's sole workplace-compensation provider until 1978, when the Workers' Compensation Board took over ([QUT](https://www.qut.edu.au/news?news-id=107542)). Its roots run further back, to 1902, when the Queensland Agricultural Bank opened its first branch lending to rural communities ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/companies/suncorp/53088/?p=36)).

By the 1970s the still government-owned SGIO had grown into a major financial institution with an explicitly social role, including rescuing struggling building societies, opening the SGIO Building Society in 1976, and investing in Castlemaine Perkins and the Bank of Queensland when they were under takeover threat ([QUT](https://www.qut.edu.au/news?news-id=107542)). SGIO became Suncorp in 1985, and in 1996 Suncorp merged with Metway and the Queensland Industry Development Corporation, at which point the Queensland Government sold its shareholding ([QUT](https://www.qut.edu.au/news?news-id=107542)). In 2001 the renamed Suncorp Metway acquired AMP's Australian general insurance business, doubling its insurance customers ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/companies/suncorp/53088/?p=36)).

## A house of brands, crowned by the Promina merger

Suncorp sells insurance less under its own name than through specialist brands, each aimed at a distinct segment ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/companies/suncorp/53088/?p=36)). AAMI covers home, landlord, strata, contents, car and business lines; GIO is the brand used outside Queensland; Bingle sells no-frills low-cost car insurance; Apia specialises in over-50s cover (around 700,000 customers); Shannons targets motoring enthusiasts; Terri Scheer specialises in landlord insurance (around 200,000 customers); CIL covers caravans and recreational vehicles; and Vero sells through brokers to small and larger businesses ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/companies/suncorp/53088/?p=36)). In New Zealand the key brands are Vero, AA Insurance and Asteron Life ([Morningstar Australia](https://www.morningstar.com.au/stocks/peak-profit-asx-listed-insurer)).

The brand house reached its modern shape with the Promina merger, finalised on 20 March 2007, when the two groups began operating as one ([Suncorp ASX announcement](https://www.marketindex.com.au/data-api/api/v1/announcements/XASX:SUN:XX186322/pdf/inline/aoz-suncorp-promina-finalise-merger)). The merger left the group unmistakably insurance-heavy — and highly exposed to Australian weather claims.

## Becoming a pure-play insurer — and what that exposes

The decisive break came with the bank sale. Announced in July 2022, the $4.9 billion deal was initially opposed by the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission in August 2023, then authorised by the Australian Competition Tribunal in February 2024 and approved by the federal Treasurer in June 2024, subject to binding commitments including maintaining the combined regional branch footprint for at least three years and no net job losses among Suncorp Bank employees in Australia as a direct result of the deal over the same period ([FST Media / The Adviser](https://www.fst.net.au/news/financial-services/over-1m-aussies-to-be-anz-customers-following-suncorp-acquisition)). ANZ completed the acquisition on 31 July 2024, taking in about 3,000 staff and 1.2 million customers, with a transitional licence to use the Suncorp Bank brand for five to seven years ([FST Media / The Adviser](https://www.fst.net.au/news/financial-services/over-1m-aussies-to-be-anz-customers-following-suncorp-acquisition)). Expected net sale proceeds were about $4.1 billion — distinct from the $4.9 billion headline price — with most to be returned to shareholders ([Insurance News](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/daily/suncorp-keen-to-get-on-with-the-job-as-bank-sale-closes)). The deal also carried Queensland commitments, including a disaster response centre of excellence in Brisbane and a Townsville regional hub set to employ about 120 people ([Insurance News](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/daily/suncorp-keen-to-get-on-with-the-job-as-bank-sale-closes)).

In FY2025 it reported net profit after tax of $1.823 billion, up from $1.197 billion in 2024, and gross written premiums of $15.009 billion, up from $14.1 billion ([Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/suncorp-reports-1-8bn-profit-in-fy2025/)). That profit included a $252 million one-off gain on the bank sale and $99 million for New Zealand Life, while natural hazard experience of $1.355 billion came in $205 million below allowance; the year brought 17 declared weather events and more than 120,000 natural hazard claims ([Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/suncorp-reports-1-8bn-profit-in-fy2025/)). The concentration risk is structural: Queensland makes up around 25% of Australian gross written premiums, where large natural peril events have been larger and more frequent, and reinsurance mitigates the risk only partly, at a cost that rises after large events ([Morningstar Australia](https://www.morningstar.com.au/stocks/peak-profit-asx-listed-insurer)). Morningstar put FY2025 cash profit up 8.3% at $1.5 billion and the underlying margin at 11.9%, the top end of the 10–12% target range, with FY2026 guidance at mid-single-digit premium growth, while rating the shares no-moat and above its $16 fair value estimate ([Morningstar Australia](https://www.morningstar.com.au/stocks/peak-profit-asx-listed-insurer)). The final step is already scheduled: in September 2026 Suncorp Bank told its 1.2 million customers it would progressively move their products, services and digital platforms to ANZ by June 2027, signalling the eventual retirement of the Suncorp Bank brand ([FST Media / The Adviser](https://www.fst.net.au/news/financial-services/over-1m-aussies-to-be-anz-customers-following-suncorp-acquisition)).
