## Overview

Beckett Grading Services (BGS) is the trading-card grading arm of Beckett, the collectibles company founded in 1984 by collector Dr. James Beckett ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/breaking-news-beckett-purchased-by-grading-rivals-collectors-psa)). Launched in 1999, it authenticates, grades and seals sports, gaming and non-sports cards in tamper-evident holders, printing four subgrades on its labels ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating); [Beckett Collectibles](https://beckett.com/grading)). Since December 2025 it has belonged to Collectors, the parent company of rival grader [PSA](/organizations/professional-sports-authenticator/) ([PocketMonsters.Net](https://pocketmonsters.net/news/8843)).

This profile covers BGS, the grading service — not Beckett's magazines or price guides as a separate subject. It follows BGS from a price-guide publisher's side business into one of the hobby's top three graders ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)), through a decade of lost share, and into its sale to its biggest rival's parent and its 2026 relaunch. Beckett's current headquarters for drop-off and pickup is in Plano, [Texas](/places/texas/) ([Beckett Collectibles](https://beckett.com/grading)).

## How BGS grades: subgrades, half points and the Black Label

BGS grades on a 1-to-10 scale in half-point steps, so a card that falls just short of pristine can score 9.5 rather than dropping a full point ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)). What sets its slabs apart is transparency about how that number was reached: every card is assessed across four criteria — centering, corners, edges and surface — and those four subgrades are printed directly on the label, a practice Beckett describes as unique among grading companies ([Beckett Collectibles](https://beckett.com/grading)). Every card is also examined for trimming, doctoring and alteration before grading begins, and is then sealed in a tamper-evident holder ([Beckett Collectibles](https://beckett.com/grading)). Beckett's cases were described by its own grading vice-president as sonically sealed, with an inner sleeve protecting the card — a casing detail he presented as a further point of difference ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)).

The label colour tells a buyer the tier at a glance: black for a Perfect Pristine 10, gold for Pristine 10 and Gem Mint 9.5, and silver for all other grades ([Beckett Collectibles](https://beckett.com/grading)). The Black Label standard is exacting on centering alone — 50/50 all around on the front and 55/45 or better on the back — with corners and edges perfect to the naked eye and virtually flawless under intense scrutiny, and a surface free of print spots, scratches and metallic print lines ([Beckett Collectibles](https://beckett.com/grading)). Collectors who want the breakdown without the premium can also submit at a lower tier: Beckett lists a Base service at $14.95 per card without subgrades and $17.95 with them, Standard at $34.95, Express at $79.95 and Priority at $124.95, with estimated turnarounds from 5 business days at Priority to 90-plus business days at Base ([Beckett Collectibles](https://beckett.com/grading)). Its coverage spans sports, gaming and non-sports cards, "from rookie cards and Pokémon to Star Wars and beyond" ([Beckett Collectibles](https://beckett.com/grading)).

## From price guides to the Collectors portfolio

Beckett began as a publishing business: it was founded in 1984 and became one of the hobby's top magazines and price guides before expanding into grading in 1999, when PSA was already the established leader and Beckett was, in its own grading chief's words, "playing catch up" with a business that had just started ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)). The grading operation grew in stages — vintage grading and the Beckett Collectors Club in 2001, tamper-proof holders in 2005 and an online submission platform in 2007 ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/breaking-news-beckett-purchased-by-grading-rivals-collectors-psa)). By the time of the history article's reporting, BGS had graded over 12 million items ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)).

That early standing did not hold. Sports Collectors Digest summarised Beckett as one of the hobby's leading grading and authentication companies "before struggling financially and losing much of its market share over the past decade" ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/breaking-news-beckett-purchased-by-grading-rivals-collectors-psa)). The 2024 numbers show the slide in concrete terms: on GemRate figures, Beckett graded 327,000 sports cards and 329,000 trading-card-game cards, each down 15 percent on the year, for third place in both categories at 3 percent of sports share and 4 percent of trading-card-game share ([Sports Collectors Daily](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/trading-card-grading-data-2024/)). The same figures show why collectors kept using it anyway: Beckett had the second-highest gem rate in sports, at 38 percent, and the highest in trading-card games, at 66 percent ([Sports Collectors Daily](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/trading-card-grading-data-2024/)). Its most-graded card that year was the 2023 Upper Deck Young Guns Connor Bedard rookie, with nearly 1,100 copies leaving its Texas offices ([Sports Collectors Daily](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/trading-card-grading-data-2024/)).

Ownership changed twice in quick succession. Collēctīvus, which owns hobby companies including Southern Hobby and Dragon Shield, purchased Beckett in December 2024 ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/breaking-news-beckett-purchased-by-grading-rivals-collectors-psa)). A year later, on 15 December 2025, Collectors — the company behind PSA — entered a definitive agreement to acquire Beckett, adding it to subsidiaries including PSA, PCGS, SGC and Card Ladder ([PocketMonsters.Net](https://pocketmonsters.net/news/8843)). Collectors said Beckett would remain an independent brand with its own operations, customer experience and grading standards, that orders at PSA and Beckett would continue normally, and that there would be no change in pricing as part of the acquisition ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/breaking-news-beckett-purchased-by-grading-rivals-collectors-psa)). In early 2026 its PSA president repeated the independence pledge, saying Beckett and SGC would keep their own grading standards, teams and brand identities even as Collectors shared resources for efficiency, anti-fraud work and technology ([Sports Collectors Daily](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/collectors-commits-to-expanding-sgc-refreshing-beckett-labels-and-quick-to-market-options/)).

The deal immediately drew political scrutiny. New York Congressman Pat Ryan demanded that the Federal Trade Commission open an investigation, arguing in a letter that Collectors had consolidated "over 80% of grading volume, leaving only one independent competitor" through its acquisitions of PSA in 2021, SGC in February 2024 and Beckett in December 2025 ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/breaking-news-congressman-calls-for-federal-trade-commission-to-investigate-beckett-sale-to-psa)). Ryan asked whether Collectors had acquired SGC and Beckett specifically to eliminate competition, whether Collēctīvus had functioned as a pass-through entity to evade merger scrutiny, and what safeguards prevent coordination across the three nominally independent brands ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/breaking-news-congressman-calls-for-federal-trade-commission-to-investigate-beckett-sale-to-psa)). That letter is a demand for an investigation, not a finding against Collectors, and neither Collectors nor Beckett had commented publicly since the initial announcement at the time of that report ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/breaking-news-congressman-calls-for-federal-trade-commission-to-investigate-beckett-sale-to-psa)).

BGS also sits inside the wider collectibles economy rather than apart from it: to sell a card on [Alt](/organizations/alt/), its condition first had to be judged by one of the established grading groups — PSA, BGS or SGC — before it was sent to Alt's Vault ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/11/collectible-card-trading-platform-alt-raises-75m-as-it-eyes-other-asset-categories-and-preps-its-mobile-launch/)).

## The 2026 relaunch and the volume rebound

Five months after the purchase, Beckett moved to reclaim its own identity: it retired the fragmented "B-Star" branding, restored a modernised version of its iconic "B with laurels" crest, and unveiled a refined grading label with a larger final grade, improved spacing and unified subgrades and autograph details, on a thicker label that eliminates shifting inside the holder ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/beckett-brings-back-heritage-logo-launches-new-card-grading-label)). The new label carries anti-counterfeit technology — advanced holographics, microprinting, UV-reactive details and a QR code linking directly to the card's verification page with grading details and population data — and autograph authentication gained a two-step verification process ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/beckett-brings-back-heritage-logo-launches-new-card-grading-label)). All card grading and autograph authentication services were brought under a single "One Beckett" brand to eliminate customer confusion ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/beckett-brings-back-heritage-logo-launches-new-card-grading-label)). The label change was presented as visual only: in early 2026 Collectors said subgrades were not going anywhere and grading standards were not changing ([Sports Collectors Daily](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/collectors-commits-to-expanding-sgc-refreshing-beckett-labels-and-quick-to-market-options/)).

The commercial rebound followed the rebrand. In July 2026, Beckett graded items were up 10 percent on the month and had more than doubled year over year, up 115 percent, thanks to huge increases in trading-card-game submissions — up 27 percent in a month and 159 percent year over year ([Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/collectibles/july-card-grading-hits-another-record-beckett-cgc-surging)). Sports Illustrated linked the resurgence in part to Beckett joining Collectors' portfolio, noting that the August 2025 GemRate report had made it look as if Beckett were slowly losing share and might soon cease to exist ([Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/collectibles/july-card-grading-hits-another-record-beckett-cgc-surging)). That turnaround — from a 15 percent decline in both major categories in 2024 to triple-digit growth in mid-2026 — is the part of the BGS story that standard grading guides, which stop at the scale and the slab, do not tell.

## What this profile does not claim

- The exact closing date of the Collectors acquisition is not claimed: December 2025 is the definitive agreement announcement, and a 2026 report treats Beckett as already purchased, but no source read gives a closing date.
- No headquarters move is claimed: an older account calls Beckett Dallas-based, while its current page gives headquarters in Plano, Texas. Both are in the Dallas area, but no source read states a move or a move date.
- The combined Collectors share is not asserted as a precise figure: Congressman Ryan's letter says over 80 percent, while other 2025 reporting rounds it to about 79 percent.
- The FTC matter is stated only as a demand for an investigation: no FTC finding, ruling or enforcement action was reported in the sources read.
- Beckett's published prices and turnaround times are its current listed tiers and will change; they are not a quote for any individual submission.
- The "over 12 million items graded" figure is from the history article's reporting period, not a current cumulative total, and is not presented as one.
- Some niche guides date BGS to 1992; that claim contradicts tier2 reporting (Beckett founded 1984, BGS launched 1999) and was not used.
