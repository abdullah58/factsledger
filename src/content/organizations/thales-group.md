## Overview

Thales is Europe's largest defence-technology group: a French conglomerate building military radars, missiles, avionics and electronic-warfare systems alongside civilian aerospace equipment and a fast-growing cybersecurity business. In 2025 it reported sales of €22.14 billion — up 8.8% organically — and adjusted operating earnings of €2.74 billion, up 14%, powered by its core defence business and demand for avionics and space activities ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/thales-profits-boosted-by-defence-business-avionics-2026-03-03/)). The group employs roughly 85,000 people and organises itself into three segments: Defence, Aerospace, and Cyber and Digital ([Defense & Tech](https://defenseandtech.com/thales-group-company-profile-products-employees-revenue/)).

## The cybersecurity acquisition arc

Thales spent the 2010s and 2020s buying its way into digital security. The pivotal deal was the €4.8 billion takeover of chipmaker Gemalto, completed in April 2019 after clearing multiple regulatory hurdles, which Reuters described as boosting Thales' presence in digital identity and security services ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/business/french-group-thales-completes-48-billion-euro-takeover-of-gemalto-idUSKCN1RE0BH/)). Chairman and CEO Patrice Caine called it the creation of "a giant in digital identity and security" ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/business/french-group-thales-completes-48-billion-euro-takeover-of-gemalto-idUSKCN1RE0BH/)).

The follow-up was Imperva: a $3.6 billion purchase of the US cybersecurity firm from Thoma Bravo, completed in December 2023, adding 5,800 security experts and an expected €2.4 billion of 2024 cybersecurity revenue. It was Thales' ninth digital-security acquisition in nine years — Tesserent in Australia and S21sec & Excellium in Europe among the others — and the second-largest in group history after Gemalto ([Airforce Technology](https://www.airforce-technology.com/newsletters/signal-thales-completes-acquisition-of-imperva-in-cybersecurity-push-2/)). By 2025, cybersecurity remained the group's softest segment even as defence boomed — order intake in Cyber and Digital was flat to slightly down in the first nine months of 2025, a weak spot investors are watching ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/thales-profits-boosted-by-defence-business-avionics-2026-03-03/)).

## Two blocking shareholders

Thales' governance is distinctive: the French state and Dassault Aviation each hold roughly 26.6% — dual blocking positions that give both effective vetoes over strategic direction ([Defense & Tech](https://defenseandtech.com/thales-group-company-profile-products-employees-revenue/)). The arrangement reflects the company's origins: Thomson-CSF was nationalised in 1982, privatised and renamed Thales in December 2000, and Dassault acquired its stake from Alcatel-Lucent in 2008–2009. The state-Dassault pact has shaped leadership and strategy ever since, anchoring Thales as a national champion even as it sells worldwide — from Rafale sensors for France to missiles for the UK, Ukraine and Spain.

For 2026, Thales guided to an operating margin of 12.6–12.8% and underlying revenue growth of 6–7%, "with new orders continuing to outstrip sales" — the rearmament cycle continuing to fund the order book, which stood at €52.4 billion in mid-2026 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/thales-profits-boosted-by-defence-business-avionics-2026-03-03/); [Defense & Tech](https://defenseandtech.com/thales-group-company-profile-products-employees-revenue/)).
