## Four names, one wrestling company

Most coverage of WWE starts in 2023, with the Endeavor merger. The longer story starts in 1953, when the promotion began as the Capitol Wrestling Corporation ([TheSportster](https://www.thesportster.com/wwf-name-change-wwe-explained-court-world-wildlife-fund/?pubDate=20250226)). It became a major player in the National Wrestling Alliance's northeastern territory before a dispute with the NWA over booking decisions led it to break away and become the World Wide Wrestling Federation in 1963 ([TheSportster](https://www.thesportster.com/wwf-name-change-wwe-explained-court-world-wildlife-fund/?pubDate=20250226)). In 1979 the company shortened its name to the World Wrestling Federation — a change that coincided with control passing from founder Vincent J. McMahon to his son, Vincent K. McMahon ([TheSportster](https://www.thesportster.com/wwf-name-change-wwe-explained-court-world-wildlife-fund/?pubDate=20250226)).

The younger McMahon bought the company outright from his father in 1982 ([Associated Press](https://www.wkyufm.org/news/2023-04-03/ufc-and-wwe-will-team-up-to-form-a-21-4-billion-sports-entertainment-company)) and took the regional promotion national — wiping out rival territories, securing syndicated television deals, selling videotapes, and signing popular talent from competing promotions ([TheSportster](https://www.thesportster.com/wwf-name-change-wwe-explained-court-world-wildlife-fund/?pubDate=20250226)). He staged the first WrestleMania in 1985 ([Associated Press](https://www.wkyufm.org/news/2023-04-03/ufc-and-wwe-will-team-up-to-form-a-21-4-billion-sports-entertainment-company)) and took WWE public in 1999 ([Cageside Seats](https://www.cagesideseats.com/wwe/2023/9/12/23869747/wwe-ufc-merger-official-tko-group-launches-nyse-endeavor-purchase-vince-mcmahon)).

The final rename came from a courtroom, not a marketing department. The World Wildlife Fund — the panda charity, founded in 1961 — had been fighting the wrestling company over the "WWF" initials since 1989, when the Fund objected to the Federation's trademark filing; a 1994 agreement restricted how the wrestling company could use the initials, and the 1997 "scratch logo" plus the WWF.com domain pushed the dispute back into court ([TheSportster](https://www.thesportster.com/wwf-name-change-wwe-explained-court-world-wildlife-fund/?pubDate=20250226)). In 2002 the Fund took the Federation to court in the UK and won ([TheSportster](https://www.thesportster.com/wwf-name-change-wwe-explained-court-world-wildlife-fund/?pubDate=20250226)). The rebrand was announced on May 5, 2002, with a "Get the F Out" ad campaign, and the first show under the WWE banner aired the next night on Raw ([TheSportster](https://www.thesportster.com/wwf-name-change-wwe-explained-court-world-wildlife-fund/?pubDate=20250226)).

## The $21.4 billion wedding

On April 3, 2023, Endeavor Group Holdings and WWE announced they would combine the UFC and WWE into a new publicly traded company valued at $21.4 billion — $12.1 billion for the UFC, $9.3 billion for WWE ([Associated Press](https://www.wkyufm.org/news/2023-04-03/ufc-and-wwe-will-team-up-to-form-a-21-4-billion-sports-entertainment-company)). Endeavor would take a 51% controlling interest; existing WWE shareholders would hold 49% ([Associated Press](https://www.wkyufm.org/news/2023-04-03/ufc-and-wwe-will-team-up-to-form-a-21-4-billion-sports-entertainment-company)). The leadership was set at the announcement: Endeavor CEO Ari Emanuel as CEO, Vince McMahon as executive chairman, Dana White continuing as UFC president, and Nick Khan as WWE president, over an 11-member board split six-to-five between Endeavor and WWE appointees ([Associated Press](https://www.wkyufm.org/news/2023-04-03/ufc-and-wwe-will-team-up-to-form-a-21-4-billion-sports-entertainment-company)).

The deal closed on September 12, 2023, and the new company — [TKO Group Holdings](/organizations/tko-group-holdings/) — began trading on the New York Stock Exchange under the "TKO" ticker the same day ([Endeavor](https://www.cagesideseats.com/wwe/2023/9/12/23869747/wwe-ufc-merger-official-tko-group-launches-nyse-endeavor-purchase-vince-mcmahon)). The launch announcement described a company serving more than one billion fans, reaching viewers in 180 countries, and producing more than 350 live events a year ([Endeavor](https://www.cagesideseats.com/wwe/2023/9/12/23869747/wwe-ufc-merger-official-tko-group-launches-nyse-endeavor-purchase-vince-mcmahon)). For the first time in the company's history, a McMahon was no longer its majority owner — Vince McMahon held roughly 16% of the new company ([Cageside Seats](https://www.cagesideseats.com/wwe/2023/9/12/23869747/wwe-ufc-merger-official-tko-group-launches-nyse-endeavor-purchase-vince-mcmahon)).

The fit was not as odd as it looked. Stars such as Brock Lesnar and Ronda Rousey had already crossed between WWE and the [UFC](/organizations/ufc/), and McMahon framed the deal as the company's next evolution: "Together, we will be a $21+ billion live sports and entertainment powerhouse with a collective fanbase of more than a billion people," he said in the announcement ([Associated Press](https://www.wkyufm.org/news/2023-04-03/ufc-and-wwe-will-team-up-to-form-a-21-4-billion-sports-entertainment-company)). At the time, WWE was coming off a year with $1.3 billion in revenue, 16 billion social video views in the final quarter of 2022, nearly 94 million YouTube subscribers, and more than 20 million TikTok followers ([Associated Press](https://www.wkyufm.org/news/2023-04-03/ufc-and-wwe-will-team-up-to-form-a-21-4-billion-sports-entertainment-company)).

## The chairman leaves

Four months after the TKO launch, the McMahon era ended abruptly. On January 26, 2024, Vince McMahon resigned as TKO's executive chairman, one day after former WWE employee Janel Grant filed a federal lawsuit in Connecticut accusing him and another former executive of serious sexual misconduct — including allegations that McMahon forced Grant into a sexual relationship to keep her job and offered her to a star wrestler for sex ([Associated Press](https://www.wkyufm.org/news/2024-01-26/wrestling-icon-vince-mcmahon-resigns-from-wwe-parent-company-after-sex-abuse-suit)). McMahon denied wrongdoing, calling the suit "replete with lies" and "a vindictive distortion of the truth," and said he was leaving "out of respect" for WWE and TKO ([Associated Press](https://www.wkyufm.org/news/2024-01-26/wrestling-icon-vince-mcmahon-resigns-from-wwe-parent-company-after-sex-abuse-suit)). He had stepped down as WWE's CEO in 2022 amid an investigation into allegations matching those in the lawsuit ([Associated Press](https://www.wkyufm.org/news/2024-01-26/wrestling-icon-vince-mcmahon-resigns-from-wwe-parent-company-after-sex-abuse-suit)).

## The streaming wars come for wrestling

WWE's next act has been written in media-rights deals. On January 23, 2024, the company announced that [Netflix](/organizations/netflix/) would become the exclusive U.S. home of its flagship weekly series Raw starting in January 2025 — ending a 31-year run on linear television — with Netflix also carrying WWE's other series outside the United States ([Polygon](https://www.polygon.com/24047814/wwe-raw-netflix-exclusive-deal-streaming/)). The deal was reported at about $5 billion over ten years, nearly double WWE's previous NBCUniversal/Peacock arrangement, and TKO president Mark Shapiro called it "transformative" ([Polygon](https://www.polygon.com/24047814/wwe-raw-netflix-exclusive-deal-streaming/)).

Then came the premium live events. In August 2025 WWE and ESPN announced a five-year U.S. deal covering all WWE PLEs — WrestleMania, SummerSlam, the Royal Rumble, Survivor Series, Money in the Bank — valued at $1.6 billion, or $325 million a year, according to the Wall Street Journal's reporting ([Cultaholic](https://cultaholic.com/posts/wwe-ppvs-moving-to-espn-in-united-states-as-part-of-16-billion-deal)). That was a steep step up from the $900 million Peacock deal it replaced ([Cultaholic](https://cultaholic.com/posts/wwe-ppvs-moving-to-espn-in-united-states-as-part-of-16-billion-deal)). The partnership started earlier than planned: on September 20, 2025, the first-ever Wrestlepalooza streamed on ESPN's new direct-to-consumer service from Indianapolis, making ESPN the exclusive U.S. home of WWE PLEs ([ESPN](http://espnpressroom.com/press-release/wwe-premium-live-events-to-debut-on-espn-platforms-in-the-u-s-beginning-september-20-with-first-ever-wrestlepalooza/)). Crown Jewel followed on October 11 and Survivor Series on November 29 ([ESPN](http://espnpressroom.com/press-release/wwe-premium-live-events-to-debut-on-espn-platforms-in-the-u-s-beginning-september-20-with-first-ever-wrestlepalooza/)).
