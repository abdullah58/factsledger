## Overview

Vibe is an [American](/places/united-states/) music and entertainment magazine founded by Quincy Jones that has covered hip-hop and R&B since 1993. Launched with a test issue in 1992 and regular issues from September 1993, it became the glossy showcase for Black music and culture, won a National Magazine Award in 2002, died twice in print — in 2009 and 2014 — survived as a digital brand, and returned to print in June 2026 ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2009/07/02/arts/music/02vibe.html); [Longreads](https://longreads.com/2018/10/02/25-years-of-vibe-magazine/)). This profile is about Vibe the magazine — not Vibe Capital, the venture fund founded by Teachable founder Ankur Nagpal, a separate entity with no connection to the magazine and profiled separately on this site. The magazine passed through Time Warner, Miller Publishing, the Wicks Group, InterMedia Partners, Yucaipa, Spin Media and the Hollywood Reporter–Billboard group before landing in 2020 with [Penske Media Corporation](/organizations/penske-media-corporation/), which operates it alongside Billboard, [The Hollywood Reporter](/organizations/the-hollywood-reporter/), Variety, [Rolling Stone](/organizations/rolling-stone/) and [Music Business Worldwide](/organizations/music-business-worldwide/) ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/234209/harsh-vibe-hip-hop-mag-shuttered-again.html?edition=); [Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)).

## Founding and the first decade

Quincy Jones launched Vibe in 1993 in partnership with Time Inc. — the publishing arm of Time Warner, which the sources name interchangeably — after pitching the concept as an urban music and lifestyle magazine in the mould of Rolling Stone ([Longreads](https://longreads.com/2018/10/02/25-years-of-vibe-magazine/); [The HistoryMakers](https://www.thehistorymakers.org/sites/default/files/A2014_204_EAD.pdf)). The idea was born from Jones's friendship with Steven J. Ross, then chairman of Time Warner, and it arrived on newsstands where Black faces rarely appeared unless they had been charged with a crime ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2009/07/02/arts/music/02vibe.html)). A test issue appeared in 1992 and regular issues began in September 1993, with Jonathan Van Meter as founding editor ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2009/07/02/arts/music/02vibe.html)). Emil Wilbekin, a founding editor who became editor-in-chief in 1999, led the magazine to the coveted National Magazine Award for General Excellence in 2002 ([The HistoryMakers](https://www.thehistorymakers.org/sites/default/files/A2014_204_EAD.pdf)). His successors — Alan Light, Danyel Smith (twice), Emil Wilbekin and Mimi Valdés — kept the formula: hip-hop and R&B given prestige-glossy design and photography ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2009/07/02/arts/music/02vibe.html); [Longreads](https://longreads.com/2018/10/02/25-years-of-vibe-magazine/)).

The magazine's temper showed early. Jones intervened when Van Meter planned a cover of Madonna posed on basketball star Dennis Rodman, telling him, "Over my dead fucking body! That's the way you blow an urban magazine," and Van Meter left after the fight ([Longreads](https://longreads.com/2018/10/02/25-years-of-vibe-magazine/)).

Its writers chronicled the East Coast–West Coast rap feud and the rise and deaths of Tupac Shakur and the Notorious B.I.G. — the latter murdered outside a Vibe after-party in 1997 — while photographers David LaChapelle, Albert Watson and Ellen von Unwerth shot covers of artists from Mary J. Blige to Young Jeezy to Jay-Z ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2009/07/02/arts/music/02vibe.html); [The HistoryMakers](https://www.thehistorymakers.org/sites/default/files/A2014_204_EAD.pdf)).

## Ownership churn, shutdown and revival

Miller Publishing, led by former Time executive Robert Miller, bought it in 1996 and acquired Spin the following year, and the Wicks Group bought it from Miller in 2006 ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/234209/harsh-vibe-hip-hop-mag-shuttered-again.html?edition=)). The economics were collapsing: ad pages fell from 1,242 in 2005 to 792 in 2008, a 36% drop in three years, and then a further 42% in the first quarter of 2009 alone ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/234209/harsh-vibe-hip-hop-mag-shuttered-again.html?edition=); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/108972/stop-the-music-vibe-shuttered.html?edition=13206)). That advertising collapse, not a single bad year, killed the first print run. Wicks tried to cut its way out first: it closed the Vibe Vixen sister title in 2007, cut circulation 25% and frequency from 12 to 10 issues a year in February 2009, slashed salaries 10–15% and put staff on a four-day week, and cut the rate base to 600,000 from 800,000 ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/108972/stop-the-music-vibe-shuttered.html?edition=13206); [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/performance-marketing/updated-vibe-magazine-folds-112763/)). None of it worked. On 30 June 2009 the entire Vibe Media Group shut down effective that day, with chief executive Steve Aaron citing the company's inability to secure backing or restructure a "huge debt" ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/performance-marketing/updated-vibe-magazine-folds-112763/)).

The revival came within weeks. In August 2009 the Wicks Group sold Vibe to InterMedia Partners — which had invested $6 million in the title in 2007 — in partnership with Blackrock Capital and Uptown Media Group, who relaunched it as a web-focused brand with a quarterly print edition and plans to hire 16 to 20 new staff ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/234209/harsh-vibe-hip-hop-mag-shuttered-again.html?edition=); [Journal-isms](https://www.journal-isms.com/archive-646/)). Jones himself had tried to buy the magazine back to run it online, but he was not involved in the new venture ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2009/07/02/arts/music/02vibe.html); [Journal-isms](https://www.journal-isms.com/archive-646/)). Control passed in February 2011 to Yucaipa Companies, the holding company of billionaire Ron Burkle, who had invested alongside Magic Johnson ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/234209/harsh-vibe-hip-hop-mag-shuttered-again.html?edition=)).

The Yucaipa era was short. In April 2013 Vibe magazine, vibe.com and vibevixen.com were sold to Spin Media, the former Buzz Media, for an undisclosed sum ([The Source](https://thesource.com/2013/04/25/vibe-magazine-sold-to-spinmedia-may-only-be-available-online/)). As it had with Spin, Spin Media ended Vibe's print edition in September 2014 and laid off 19 staffers, mostly in video, photo and sales, while the website continued ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/234209/harsh-vibe-hip-hop-mag-shuttered-again.html?edition=)). By then circulation had fallen to less than half its peak of over 800,000 ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/234209/harsh-vibe-hip-hop-mag-shuttered-again.html?edition=)). The final print cover featured Drake and Kevin Hart ([BallerAlert](https://balleralert.com/vibe-magazine-returns-to-print-first-issue-drops-next-week/)).

## The Billboard years and the PMRC era

In December 2016 The Hollywood Reporter–Billboard Media Group acquired SpinMedia's music assets — Spin, Vibe and Stereogum — in a deal that pushed the group's revenues past $100 million a year, with a combined audience of 45 million readers a month ([MusicRow](https://musicrow.com/2016/12/the-hollywood-reporter-billboard-acquires-spinmedia-assets/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/291890/hollywood-reporterbillboard-media-buys-spinmedia.html)). The group's stated aim was to "own the topic of music online" ([MusicRow](https://musicrow.com/2016/12/the-hollywood-reporter-billboard-acquires-spinmedia-assets/)). In January 2020 Spin was sold to Next Management Partners and Stereogum was sold back to its own leadership — but "Vibe, which was also part of the 2016 deal, will remain with the company" ([Stereogum](https://stereogum.com/2070280/billboard-the-hollywood-reporter-media-group-selling-stereogum-to-founder-scott-lapatine/news)). On 23 September 2020, Penske Media Corporation and MRC announced PMRC, a joint venture grouping Billboard, The Hollywood Reporter and Vibe with [Variety](/organizations/variety/), Rolling Stone and Music Business Worldwide, with PMC overseeing day-to-day operations of the three MRC titles; financial terms were not disclosed ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/variety-parent-penske-media-to-take-over-hollywood-reporter-billboard-in-joint-venture-with-mrc/); [Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)).

The structure shifted again in August 2022, when MRC and Eldridge separated their combined media assets: Eldridge retained [Dick Clark Productions](/organizations/dick-clark-productions/) and Penske Media Corporation, "which includes The Hollywood Reporter, Variety, Billboard, Rolling Stone, Vibe, Music Business Worldwide" and other assets ([Kirkland & Ellis](https://www.kirkland.com/news/press-release/2022/08/kirkland-advises-media-rights-capital-in-separation-of-combined-media-assets-with-eldridge)). That makes Vibe, today, a Penske Media brand by way of Eldridge — a long way from the Time Warner joint venture Jones started in 1992.

## The Vibe Awards and the wider brand

Vibe's most visible brand extension was its televised awards show. The first Vibe Awards aired as a two-hour UPN special, "Vibe Awards: Beats, Style, Flavor," on 21 November 2003, co-hosted by [Queen Latifah](/people/queen-latifah/) ([The Futon Critic](http://thefutoncritic.com/news/2003/10/28/cbs-upn-promise-sweeps-to-remember-16228/6218/)). The third annual show, held on 12 November 2005 at Sony Studios in Culver City and aired on UPN three days later, was the high-water mark: Mariah Carey won four awards, including Artist of the Year and Album of the Year for *The Emancipation of Mimi*, and the announced bill included [Lil Wayne](/people/lil-wayne/) among the performers ([BMI](https://www.bmi.com/news/20051115_mariah_carey_leads_winners_at_vibe_awards); [The Futon Critic](http://thefutoncritic.com/news/2005/10/26/ciara-ludacris-pharrell-young-jeezy-keyshia-cole-david-banner-paul-wall-and-lil-wayne-to-perform-at-the-third-annual-vibe-awards-on-upn-honoring-hip-hop-and-r-and-bs-biggest-brightest-stars-tuesday-nov-15-at-800-pm-on-upn-19627/20051026upn01/)). Under Wilbekin, Vibe Ventures oversaw vibe.com, mobile, books and Vibe TV, and Wilbekin executive-produced the first Vibe Awards ([The HistoryMakers](https://www.thehistorymakers.org/sites/default/files/A2014_204_EAD.pdf)). A sister title, Vibe Vixen, aimed at women readers, was closed by the Wicks Group in 2007 after its rate base of 425,000 could not sustain it ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/performance-marketing/updated-vibe-magazine-folds-112763/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/108972/stop-the-music-vibe-shuttered.html?edition=13206)). The pattern was consistent: Vibe was repeatedly more valuable as a brand than as a print business.

## Print, digital and the 2026 return

For twelve years after September 2014, Vibe lived only online. The website continued through the Spin Media years and the Billboard era, until the brand joined forces with Rolling Stone in 2025 and announced a return to print in May 2026 ([HipHopWired](https://hiphopwired.com/3055278/vibe-announces-return-of-print-magazine/)). The first print edition went on sale on 2 June 2026 as a quarterly, printed on high-quality paper and sold through specialty newsstands and the Rolling Stone online store, with the first run deliberately limited to 1,000 copies ([HipHopWired](https://hiphopwired.com/3055278/vibe-announces-return-of-print-magazine/); [BallerAlert](https://balleralert.com/vibe-magazine-returns-to-print-first-issue-drops-next-week/)). Quincy Jones died in November 2024, aged 91, so the founder did not live to see the print edition return to newsstands ([BallerAlert](https://balleralert.com/vibe-magazine-returns-to-print-first-issue-drops-next-week/)). A 1,000-copy run is a collector's object, not a mass-market revival — a prestige legacy brand inside a large media group, not the standalone magazine empire Jones imagined in 1992.

## Frequently asked questions

**When was Vibe magazine founded?**
Vibe began with a test issue in 1992, produced by Time Warner, and regular issues started in September 1993 ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2009/07/02/arts/music/02vibe.html)). Founder Quincy Jones launched it in partnership with Time Inc., with Jonathan Van Meter as founding editor ([Longreads](https://longreads.com/2018/10/02/25-years-of-vibe-magazine/)).

**Who owns Vibe now?**
Vibe is operated by Penske Media Corporation through the PMRC joint venture with MRC, announced in September 2020 ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/variety-parent-penske-media-to-take-over-hollywood-reporter-billboard-in-joint-venture-with-mrc/)). After MRC and Eldridge separated their assets in 2022, Eldridge retained Penske Media Corporation, which includes Vibe alongside Billboard, Variety and Rolling Stone ([Kirkland & Ellis](https://www.kirkland.com/news/press-release/2022/08/kirkland-advises-media-rights-capital-in-separation-of-combined-media-assets-with-eldridge)).

**Why did Vibe stop printing in 2014?**
Spin Media ended the print edition in September 2014 and laid off 19 staffers, keeping only the website ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/234209/harsh-vibe-hip-hop-mag-shuttered-again.html?edition=)). Circulation had fallen to less than half its peak of over 800,000 as readers moved online, and the title had already shut down once before, in 2009 ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/234209/harsh-vibe-hip-hop-mag-shuttered-again.html?edition=)).

**Is Vibe the same as Vibe Capital?**
No. Vibe Capital is a venture fund founded by Teachable founder Ankur Nagpal in 2021, and it has no connection to the magazine. This profile covers only Vibe the music magazine, founded by Quincy Jones in 1993 and now operated by Penske Media Corporation.

**What were the Vibe Awards?**
A televised awards show produced by the magazine, first aired as a two-hour UPN special on 21 November 2003, co-hosted by Queen Latifah ([The Futon Critic](http://thefutoncritic.com/news/2003/10/28/cbs-upn-promise-sweeps-to-remember-16228/6218/)). At the third annual show in 2005, Mariah Carey won four awards, including Artist of the Year ([BMI](https://www.bmi.com/news/20051115_mariah_carey_leads_winners_at_vibe_awards)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim a single founding year: the test issue appeared in 1992 and regular issues began in September 1993 ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2009/07/02/arts/music/02vibe.html)). It does not name David Salzman as a co-founder, because no fully-read source confirms him, and it gives no single settled circulation figure, because the NYT's figure of about 800,000 and the Audit Bureau's 817,825 differ. It does not claim the Vibe Awards ran on UPN through 2006 or on VH1 Soul in 2007, and it does not detail the 2004 taping incident beyond BMI's note that the show was marred by violence ([BMI](https://www.bmi.com/news/20051115_mariah_carey_leads_winners_at_vibe_awards)). It does not claim the PMRC joint venture still operates Vibe by that name after the 2022 MRC–Eldridge separation, and it names no current editor-in-chief. Finally, it does not claim the 2026 print return is a mass-market revival: the verified facts are a quarterly schedule and a 1,000-copy first run ([BallerAlert](https://balleralert.com/vibe-magazine-returns-to-print-first-issue-drops-next-week/)).
