## Overview

Roche Holding — F. Hoffmann-La Roche — is a Basel-based Swiss healthcare company built around two divisions, pharmaceuticals and diagnostics. Founded in 1896 by Fritz Hoffmann-La Roche, it grew from a cough-syrup startup into an oncology and diagnostics leader that fully acquired the biotech pioneer Genentech in 2009 ([Pharmaphorum](https://pharmaphorum.com/articles/a_history_of-_roche)). Unusually for a company of its scale, it remains under founding-family control: the Hoffmann-Oeri and Engelhorn families control at least 50% of the voting rights through a voting pool created decades ago ([Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/financials/roche-produces-hidden-billionaires-as-cancer-drugs-dominate)). Today its defining strategic bet is breaking into the obesity-drug market dominated by Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk — a race in which [Pfizer](/organizations/pfizer/), too, is fielding a rival candidate targeting 2028 approval ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/drugmakers-bulk-up-with-bets-weight-loss-treatments-2026-09-30/)).

## A century of firsts, from vitamins to Valium to Genentech

Hoffmann-La Roche was founded in 1896 with an industrial-manufacturing vision for medicines, and expanded internationally almost immediately — by 1914 it had offices in Milan, New York, St. Petersburg and London ([Pharmaphorum](https://pharmaphorum.com/articles/a_history_of-_roche)). Early milestones piled up: in 1934 it became the first company to mass-produce synthetic vitamin C (sold as Redoxon), and in 1957 it introduced the benzodiazepine class of tranquilizers — including Valium — which sedated patients without drowsiness ([Pharmaphorum](https://pharmaphorum.com/articles/a_history_of-_roche)). The history is not unblemished: in 1976 a dioxin contamination accident at a subsidiary-owned chemical factory in Seveso, Italy, became one of Europe's defining industrial disasters ([Pharmaphorum](https://pharmaphorum.com/articles/a_history_of-_roche)). Roche later refocused on pharmaceuticals and diagnostics alone, and in March 2009 — after eight months of negotiation — completed its full acquisition of Genentech, the biotech pioneer in which it had held a majority stake since 1990, anchoring a personalised-medicine strategy built on pairing drugs with companion diagnostics ([Pharmaphorum](https://pharmaphorum.com/articles/a_history_of-_roche)). Its oncology franchise — Herceptin, Avastin, Rituxan — became the world's best-selling cancer-drug portfolio ([Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/financials/roche-produces-hidden-billionaires-as-cancer-drugs-dominate)).

## The obesity race: enicepatide up, emugrobart out

Roche's late entry into obesity runs through its $2.7 billion purchase of US biotech Carmot Therapeutics in 2023, which brought the dual-acting GLP-1/GIP candidate enicepatide ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/drugmakers-bulk-up-with-bets-weight-loss-treatments-2026-09-30/)). In September 2026 the drug posted its strongest data yet: in a 447-patient mid-stage trial, overweight or obese patients with type 2 diabetes on the highest 24mg weekly dose lost an average 15.5% of body weight at 48 weeks while meeting the trial's blood-sugar goals — about 62% reached HbA1c levels below 5.7%, back in the non-diabetic range, with 2% discontinuing over side effects ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/roches-obesity-drug-boosts-blood-sugar-control-drives-155-weight-loss-mid-stage-2026-09-22/)). An earlier mid-stage trial had shown 22.7% weight loss in patients without diabetes, and Roche plans late-stage glycemic-control and cardiovascular-outcome studies starting in the first half of 2027 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/roches-obesity-drug-boosts-blood-sugar-control-drives-155-weight-loss-mid-stage-2026-09-22/)). A second candidate, developed with Denmark's Zealand Pharma, managed up to 10.7% weight loss in its own mid-stage study ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/drugmakers-bulk-up-with-bets-weight-loss-treatments-2026-09-30/)).

The same month brought a setback: Roche discontinued the muscle-sparing obesity antibody emugrobart after an interim analysis of the phase-2 Gyminda trial — which paired it with a GLP-1 — concluded success was unlikely ([Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/roche-drops-muscle-sparing-obesity-antibody-after-flunked-interim-analysis)). The antibody, designed to block myostatin so patients keep muscle while losing weight, had already failed to boost muscle growth in spinal muscular atrophy and facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy trials; Roche returned the rights to Chugai, which plans to resume SMA development ([Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/roche-drops-muscle-sparing-obesity-antibody-after-flunked-interim-analysis)). Roche still projects each of its remaining obesity assets could exceed 3 billion Swiss francs in peak sales, in a market analysts expect to top $100 billion annually within a decade ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/roches-obesity-drug-boosts-blood-sugar-control-drives-155-weight-loss-mid-stage-2026-09-22/)).
