## Overview

Prospect is a British monthly current-affairs magazine, founded in London in October 1995 by journalist David Goodhart and former Conservative MP Derek Coombs, and published today by Sir Clive Cowdery's Resolution Group as part of its not-for-profit public-interest activities ([InPublishing](https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/alan-rusbridger-to-be-next-editor-of-prospect-magazine-18741)). It specialises in long-form essays on politics, economics and current affairs, and runs the Top 100 Public Intellectuals lists, compiled with *Foreign Policy* magazine, and its annual Think Tank Awards ([Amnesty International UK](https://www.amnesty.org.uk/knowledge-hub/all-resources/amnesty-announces-worlds-top-intellectual-coming-belfast/)).

The magazine was conceived as an essay-based monthly general-interest title — a form then unknown in Britain — after Goodhart, a senior *Financial Times* correspondent, covered German reunification from Bonn ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/prospect-ceo-mark-beard-editor-alan-rusbridger/)). Coombs, its founding chairman, wanted an intellectual magazine to counter what he called the "dumbing down" of the British press, with space for science and thought pieces from across the political spectrum ([InPublishing](https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/editor-turns-reporter-22983)). It appears in print ten times a year, including bumper winter and summer double issues, alongside a website, app, newsletters and two weekly podcasts ([Prospect Magazine](https://prospectmagazine.substack.com/about)).

## Founding and ownership

Prospect launched with its first issue in October 1995 ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/prospect-ceo-mark-beard-editor-alan-rusbridger/)). Founded by Goodhart and Coombs, the magazine was later bought by the Resolution Foundation, the think tank Cowdery founded in 2005, and is now published by the Resolution Group as part of its not-for-profit public-interest activities, with Cowdery as chairman of the magazine ([InPublishing](https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/editor-turns-reporter-22983); [InPublishing](https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/alan-rusbridger-to-be-next-editor-of-prospect-magazine-18741)). The Resolution Foundation's own account says Resolution Media, which invests in media assets, is the magazine's publisher ([Resolution Foundation](https://www.resolutionfoundation.org/about-us/team/clive-cowdery/)).

The title is not profitable and is sustained by a philanthropic owner: chief executive Mark Beard, who joined from *The Economist* in January 2023, said the plan is for two-thirds of funding to come from reader revenue — subscriptions and newsstand — and one-third from advertising, sponsorship and events ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/prospect-ceo-mark-beard-editor-alan-rusbridger/)). Prospect operates independently of the Resolution Foundation, and shares a large Georgian house just off Parliament Square with it, with *The Spectator* and *UnHerd* as near neighbours ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/prospect-ceo-mark-beard-editor-alan-rusbridger/)).

## Editors and editorial stance

| Editor | Tenure | Background |
| --- | --- | --- |
| David Goodhart | 1995–2010 | Founding editor; former *Financial Times* senior correspondent |
| Bronwen Maddox | 2010–2016 | Editor and chief executive; formerly of *The Times* |
| Tom Clark | 2016–2021 | Former *Guardian* leader writer; stayed on as contributing editor |
| Alan Rusbridger | 2021–2025 | Former editor-in-chief of *The Guardian* (1995–2015) |
| Philip Collins | Announced Sep 2025 | Contributing editor at *The Observer* |

Five editors have led the magazine since its founding. Goodhart edited the title from its launch until the 2010 transition, when Maddox — previously chief foreign commentator, foreign editor and US editor at *The Times* — took over as editor and chief executive ([Chatham House](https://www.chathamhouse.org/about-us/our-people/bronwen-maddox)). Clark succeeded her in 2016 and edited the magazine for five years before Rusbridger, announced in July 2021, took the chair that September ([InPublishing](https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/alan-rusbridger-to-be-next-editor-of-prospect-magazine-18741)). Rusbridger left after four years, and Philip Collins was announced as his successor in September 2025 ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.substack.com/p/alan-rusbridger-to-leave-prospect)).

On positioning, Rusbridger pitched the magazine as filling "a big gap in the centre, The New Statesman is on one side and The Spectator is on the other", arguing that readers want a magazine offering different viewpoints and treating all ideas with respect ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/prospect-ceo-mark-beard-editor-alan-rusbridger/)). A 2024 profile of the magazine describes its long, thoughtful reads as work that "often challenge[s] conventional thinking in a magazine that has writers from the left and the right and doesn't reside in any camp" ([InPublishing](https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/editor-turns-reporter-22983)). One critical essay describes its point of view as "center-left, and broadly liberal", while noting that Goodhart and Maddox themselves called it "contrarian" — "a magazine which gives voice to the argumentative centre ground" — and that contributors have ranged from the conservative philosopher Sir Roger Scruton and Conservative MP Rory Stewart to Gordon Brown, who wrote for it in 2009 while prime minister ([Consilium](https://consilium.org.au/why-isnt-jordan-peterson-on-this-list-of-the-worlds-top-fifty-intellectuals/)).

Under Rusbridger the magazine ran a newsroom of ten — a sharp contrast with the 800-strong newsroom he had led at *The Guardian* — and he returned to reporting himself, including a March 2024 cover investigation headlined "How The Government Captured The BBC" and a long-read on GB News backer Sir Paul Marshall ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/prospect-ceo-mark-beard-editor-alan-rusbridger/); [InPublishing](https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/editor-turns-reporter-22983)).

## Circulation and reach

Prospect has never matched the scale of *The Atlantic*, the far more established American news monthly, which now has more than a million subscribers; its brand of thoughtful, less partisan writing "has failed to take off" in the same way, in Press Gazette's assessment ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/prospect-ceo-mark-beard-editor-alan-rusbridger/)). Its circulation has drifted down over the past decade even as its digital audience has grown.

The 2020 ABC average of 32,258 ([InPublishing](https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/alan-rusbridger-to-be-next-editor-of-prospect-magazine-18741)) compares with a combined print-plus-digital figure of about 31,000 in 2024 ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/prospect-ceo-mark-beard-editor-alan-rusbridger/)). Rusbridger put the subscriber base at around 32,000 in early 2024 and said "another 10,000 or so would put it into the safety zone" ([InPublishing](https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/editor-turns-reporter-22983)). Digital subscriptions cost £49 a year, or £79 bundled with print, with no print-only option and three free articles a month on the website ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/prospect-ceo-mark-beard-editor-alan-rusbridger/)). Its two weekly podcasts are *The Prospect Podcast* and *Media Confidential*, hosted by Rusbridger with former *Financial Times* editor Lionel Barber, with an 85% listen-through rate ([Prospect Magazine](https://prospectmagazine.substack.com/about); [Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/prospect-ceo-mark-beard-editor-alan-rusbridger/)).

## Signature projects: thinkers lists and Think Tank Awards

Prospect's most widely noticed editorial project is its ranking of public intellectuals. In July 2004 it published a "Top Hundred British Intellectuals" list to mark its hundredth issue; the following year it collaborated with *Foreign Policy* on a list of the world's top 100 public intellectuals and asked readers to vote for the single most influential ([Consilium](https://consilium.org.au/why-isnt-jordan-peterson-on-this-list-of-the-worlds-top-fifty-intellectuals/)). More than 20,000 people voted, and Noam Chomsky won with 4,827 votes, ahead of Umberto Eco (2,464), Richard Dawkins (2,188), Václav Havel (1,990) and Christopher Hitchens (1,884) — a result Amnesty International UK later cited when describing Chomsky as the world's leading public intellectual ([Consilium](https://consilium.org.au/why-isnt-jordan-peterson-on-this-list-of-the-worlds-top-fifty-intellectuals/); [Amnesty International UK](https://www.amnesty.org.uk/knowledge-hub/all-resources/amnesty-announces-worlds-top-intellectual-coming-belfast/)).

A second Prospect/*Foreign Policy* list followed in 2008, drawing around half a million voters, with the Turkish political leader Fethullah Gülen first; *Foreign Policy* then took over the "Global Thinkers" list from 2009, while Prospect ran its own "World Thinkers" polls in 2013 (Dawkins first, over 10,000 voters), 2015 (Thomas Piketty first, under 3,000 voters) and 2019 ([Consilium](https://consilium.org.au/why-isnt-jordan-peterson-on-this-list-of-the-worlds-top-fifty-intellectuals/)).

The magazine also runs annual Think Tank Awards, which reached their 20th edition when the Royal United Services Institute won the Foreign Affairs Award for its study tracking the UK's Integrated Review on Security, Defence and Foreign Policy ([Royal United Services Institute](https://www.rusi.org/news-and-comment/rusi-news/rusi-wins-award-best-think-tank-foreign-affairs)).

## The 2025 Banned Books Museum feature

In November 2025 Prospect published a personal-essay feature by Vitali Vitaliev, "Second life: a modern museum of banned books reminds me of my old Soviet life", about the [Banned Books Museum](/organizations/banned-books-museum/) in [Tallinn](/places/tallinn/) ([Prospect Magazine](https://www.prospectmagazine.co.uk/views/lives/71630/second-life-a-modern-museum-of-banned-books-reminds-me-of-my-old-soviet-life)). [Vitaliev](/people/vitali-vitaliev/) compared the museum to his own experience of Soviet-era censorship, describing a collection of around 400 titles from more than 100 countries, including works by [George Orwell](/people/george-orwell/), Vladimir Nabokov, Mikhail Bulgakov, Nelson Mandela and the Dalai Lama, alongside Soviet-era samizdat publications ([Prospect Magazine](https://www.prospectmagazine.co.uk/views/lives/71630/second-life-a-modern-museum-of-banned-books-reminds-me-of-my-old-soviet-life)).
