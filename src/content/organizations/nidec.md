## Overview

Nidec is a Kyoto-based manufacturer of electric motors and drives, founded in 1973 by Shigenobu Nagamori and described by The [Japan](/places/japan/) Times as the world's largest maker of precision motors ([The Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/business/2026/10/01/markets/nidec-shares-sink/)). With more than 100,000 employees, it makes motors of all sizes — used in pumps, air conditioners, robotics, driver-assistance systems and heat management in vehicles — plus sensors and electronic devices ([Nikkei Asia](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/companies/nidec-says-it-found-hundreds-of-cases-of-quality-control-misconduct)). After decades of acquisition-driven growth, the company is now defined by a sprawling 2025–26 governance crisis: a third-party accounting probe, two consecutive auditor disclaimers, a Tokyo Stock Exchange "special alert," and two CEOs departing.

## From Kyoto workshop to global motor giant

The company began in 1973 in a small Kyoto workshop founded by Nagamori — the classic "monozukuri" rags-to-riches story of a Japanese precision manufacturer ([Nikkei Asia](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/companies/nidec-says-it-found-hundreds-of-cases-of-quality-control-misconduct)). It grew through hard-disk-drive spindle motors and a long run of acquisitions into a diversified motor empire. More recently Nidec bet heavily on two frontiers: EV components, developing an e-axle traction motor combining an EV's gear, motor and power-control electronics — a bet then battered by demand uncertainty and heavy price competition in China — and AI infrastructure, where it told investors in 2024 that demand for its water-cooling modules for generative-AI data centres was expanding rapidly and could one day reach ¥1 trillion in sales ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/japans-nidec-lifts-annual-profit-outlook-after-q1/)). Even before the scandal, the cracks showed: in early 2024 Nidec revised down two years of operating profit after determining that some sales at a subsidiary had been "recorded in an inflated manner" ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/japans-nidec-lifts-annual-profit-outlook-after-q1/)).

## The accounting and quality scandals

The crisis broke into the open in 2025, when a third-party committee was established to investigate suspected irregularities; in March 2026 the company flagged a nearly $890 million expected hit on net assets, and an independent investigation report identified falsification and fabrication of testing and inspection results ([Dow Jones](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202609292866/nidec-set-to-release-delayed-results-as-ceo-resigns-cto-steps-in)). In September 2026 a company investigation separately disclosed 844 cases of quality-control misconduct spanning a decade — most involving unapproved changes to product materials or manufacturing processes, others faked product tests and false labeling of origin — carried out, officials said, by workers under intense pressure to achieve results; the company insisted no safety problems were found and there would be no massive recalls ([Nikkei Asia](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/companies/nidec-says-it-found-hundreds-of-cases-of-quality-control-misconduct)).

The financial fallout was severe. Delayed FY2026 accounts booked ¥632 billion ($4 billion) in writedowns plus ¥482.5 billion in charges tied to accounting irregularities, and auditor PwC Japan withheld its opinion on the results — for the second consecutive year ([The Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/business/2026/10/01/markets/nidec-shares-sink/)). Shares plunged as much as 20% in a day, their worst intraday drop since September 2025, losing more than 31% across that week after the Japanese magazine Diamond reported on the losses ([The Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/business/2026/10/01/markets/nidec-shares-sink/)). CEO Mitsuya Kishida resigned in late September 2026 after a review found he had "made statements or engaged in conduct in relation to financial reporting that could not necessarily be regarded as appropriate"; CTO Michio Kaida succeeded him ([Dow Jones](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202609292866/nidec-set-to-release-delayed-results-as-ceo-resigns-cto-steps-in)). The Tokyo Stock Exchange had put Nidec's stock on "special alert" in October 2025, giving it one year to improve internal controls or risk delisting — a deadline that falls in late October 2026 ([Reuters](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/business/nidec-aims-october-audit-sign-new-ceo-vows-restore-trust-6423901)). Kaida has said the company is working with PwC Japan toward an audit sign-off in October and plans to shift resources from home-appliance and automotive businesses toward energy infrastructure, AI-related products, semiconductors and machine tools ([Reuters](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/business/nidec-aims-october-audit-sign-new-ceo-vows-restore-trust-6423901)).
