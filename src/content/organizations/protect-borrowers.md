## Overview

Protect Borrowers is a nonprofit consumer advocacy organisation in the [United States](/places/united-states/) — formerly the Student Borrower Protection Center (SBPC) — founded in 2018 by Seth Frotman, a former top student-loan official at the [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](/organizations/consumer-financial-protection-bureau/) who quit the agency accusing it of abandoning the borrowers it was created to protect ([UC Irvine School of Law](https://www.law.uci.edu/news/press-releases/Student-Loan-Law-Initiative.html); [Nonprofit Quarterly](https://nonprofitquarterly.org/student-loan-watchdog-who-resigned-from-feds-forms-new-nonprofit/)). Its own description is blunt: "We investigate financial abuses, take predatory companies to court, and push for policies to protect working people from debt traps" ([Protect Borrowers](https://protectborrowers.org/following-reports-of-bilt-card-2-0-meltdown-cfpb-must-protect-borrowers-warn-congress/)). It began as a student-debt watchdog and has since widened its brief to all consumer debt — the rename from SBPC to Protect Borrowers happened between May 2025 and August 2026, and its March 2026 letter to the CFPB over the [Bilt](/organizations/bilt/) card meltdown is the clearest marker of the new, broader mission ([Protect Borrowers](https://protectborrowers.org/following-reports-of-bilt-card-2-0-meltdown-cfpb-must-protect-borrowers-warn-congress/)).

The scale of the problem it addresses is what made a single-issue group grow into a general one. When SBPC launched, 44 million Americans owed more than $1.5 trillion in student loans and more than one in four borrowers was behind on payments ([UC Irvine School of Law](https://www.law.uci.edu/news/press-releases/Student-Loan-Law-Initiative.html)). By 2020 there were 43.1 million borrowers, one in four past due or in default, and more than a million defaults a year — one every 26 seconds ([U.S. Senate Committee on Health, Education, Labor, and Pensions](https://www.help.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/2332dfe2-bb42-68d0-fb95-32cc6369563a/Pierce%20Testimony.pdf)). The group argues the same machinery — servicers, collectors and a regulator that looks away — now harms borrowers across every kind of consumer debt, which is why its 2026 letter targeted a rent rewards card rather than a student loan.

## A watchdog born from a resignation

Protect Borrowers began with a resignation letter. In August 2018 Seth Frotman, the CFPB's Assistant Director and Student Loan Ombudsman, quit and wrote to acting director Mick Mulvaney that "the Bureau has abandoned the very consumers it is tasked by Congress with protecting" ([Nonprofit Quarterly](https://nonprofitquarterly.org/student-loan-watchdog-who-resigned-from-feds-forms-new-nonprofit/)). Three months later he launched the Student Borrower Protection Center with a startup grant from the Sandler Foundation and a partnership with UC Irvine School of Law, whose Student Loan Law Initiative paired the group with professors Dalié Jiménez and Jonathan Glater ([UC Irvine School of Law](https://www.law.uci.edu/news/press-releases/Student-Loan-Law-Initiative.html); [Nonprofit Quarterly](https://nonprofitquarterly.org/student-loan-watchdog-who-resigned-from-feds-forms-new-nonprofit/)). Frotman brought credibility and a team: at the CFPB he had led the unit that returned more than $750 million to student loan borrowers, and his deputies included Mike Pierce and Bonnie Latreille, with Persis Yu of the National Consumer Law Center publicly backing the launch ([UC Irvine School of Law](https://www.law.uci.edu/news/press-releases/Student-Loan-Law-Initiative.html); [Nonprofit Quarterly](https://nonprofitquarterly.org/student-loan-watchdog-who-resigned-from-feds-forms-new-nonprofit/)). The pitch was simple: student debt, at $1.53 trillion second only to mortgages, had no dedicated watchdog ([Nonprofit Quarterly](https://nonprofitquarterly.org/student-loan-watchdog-who-resigned-from-feds-forms-new-nonprofit/)).

## What it actually does

The group works the way an investigator would: it files open-records requests, publishes what it finds, sues, and lobbies. Its guaranty-agency work is the clearest example — documents obtained from state and federal open-records requests showed the agencies that insure, service and collect student loans, relics of the defunct Federal Family Education Loan Program, kept seizing defaulted borrowers' wages after being explicitly ordered to stop during COVID, and refused or failed to return millions in illicitly seized wages ([Protect Borrowers](https://protectborrowers.org/resource/advocates-highlight-evidence-that-guaranty-agencies-harmed-borrowers-and-violated-department-of-education-orders-during-covid/)). It also works through governments: its launch projects included States for Student Borrower Protection in a dozen states, a cities programme with [New York City](/places/new-york-city/), San Francisco and the District of Columbia, and a campaign for a California Borrower Bill of Rights ([UC Irvine School of Law](https://www.law.uci.edu/news/press-releases/Student-Loan-Law-Initiative.html)). In May 2025 its executive director, Mike Pierce — a former CFPB student-loan regulator under both Obama and Trump — told the Senate that more than 21 million Americans faced immediate financial harm as protections were pulled back ([U.S. Senate Committee on Health, Education, Labor, and Pensions](https://www.help.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/2332dfe2-bb42-68d0-fb95-32cc6369563a/Pierce%20Testimony.pdf)).

## The Bilt letter — and the rename

The March 2026 letter is the moment Protect Borrowers stopped being only a student-debt group. Writing to the Trump CFPB, it urged risk-based supervision over Bilt and its issuer-processor [Cardless](/organizations/cardless/), plus public enforcement where appropriate, and sent the same letter to the leaders of the Senate Banking and House Financial Services committees ([Protect Borrowers](https://protectborrowers.org/following-reports-of-bilt-card-2-0-meltdown-cfpb-must-protect-borrowers-warn-congress/)). The complaint was concrete: missed rent and mortgage payments, late fees, and customer service run by agentic AI that trapped users in "doom-loops, detours, and dead-ends"; one Boston law student saw her rent paid almost a week late despite an immediate charge and a two-business-day promise ([Protect Borrowers](https://protectborrowers.org/following-reports-of-bilt-card-2-0-meltdown-cfpb-must-protect-borrowers-warn-congress/)). The letter called Bilt's move to Column Bank "disastrous" and noted [Wells Fargo](/organizations/wells-fargo/) had exited after losing tens of millions, per the [Wall Street Journal](/organizations/wall-street-journal/) ([Protect Borrowers](https://protectborrowers.org/following-reports-of-bilt-card-2-0-meltdown-cfpb-must-protect-borrowers-warn-congress/)). More than $360 million in CFPB redress was lost or at risk by mid-2025, per a Protect Borrowers memo cited by the National Association of Consumer Advocates ([National Association of Consumer Advocates](https://www.consumeradvocates.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NACA_Voughthrg_HFSC072026.pdf)) — a figure that explains why a group built to watch student loans now writes about credit cards.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Protect Borrowers?** A nonprofit consumer advocacy organisation, formerly the Student Borrower Protection Center, that investigates financial abuses, sues predatory companies and pushes for policies to protect working people from debt traps ([Protect Borrowers](https://protectborrowers.org/following-reports-of-bilt-card-2-0-meltdown-cfpb-must-protect-borrowers-warn-congress/)). It began with student debt in 2018 and has widened its brief to all consumer debt.

**Who founded it?** Seth Frotman, the CFPB's former Assistant Director and Student Loan Ombudsman, launched it in November 2018 after resigning from the Bureau in August that year ([UC Irvine School of Law](https://www.law.uci.edu/news/press-releases/Student-Loan-Law-Initiative.html); [Nonprofit Quarterly](https://nonprofitquarterly.org/student-loan-watchdog-who-resigned-from-feds-forms-new-nonprofit/)). Its executive director as of May 2025 was Mike Pierce, a former CFPB student-loan regulator.

**Why did it write to the CFPB about Bilt?** In March 2026 it urged the Bureau to supervise Bilt and its issuer-processor Cardless after the Bilt 2.0 relaunch produced missed rent and mortgage payments, late fees and AI customer-service failures ([Protect Borrowers](https://protectborrowers.org/following-reports-of-bilt-card-2-0-meltdown-cfpb-must-protect-borrowers-warn-congress/)). It alleged possible violations of the CARD Act, the Truth in Lending Act and the ban on unfair, deceptive or abusive practices.

**Is it part of the government?** No. It is an independent nonprofit, launched with a Sandler Foundation startup grant and a partnership with UC Irvine School of Law ([Nonprofit Quarterly](https://nonprofitquarterly.org/student-loan-watchdog-who-resigned-from-feds-forms-new-nonprofit/); [UC Irvine School of Law](https://www.law.uci.edu/news/press-releases/Student-Loan-Law-Initiative.html)). It often works against the government — its 2020 guaranty-agency findings accused agencies of defying Education Department orders.

**When did it change its name?** The exact date is unverified: it still called itself the Student Borrower Protection Center in Senate testimony on 21 May 2025, and was "Protect Borrowers (formerly Student Borrower Protection Center)" by 7 August 2026 ([U.S. Senate Committee on Health, Education, Labor, and Pensions](https://www.help.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/2332dfe2-bb42-68d0-fb95-32cc6369563a/Pierce%20Testimony.pdf); [Protect Borrowers](https://protectborrowers.org/following-reports-of-bilt-card-2-0-meltdown-cfpb-must-protect-borrowers-warn-congress/)). The rename tracks its widening from student debt to all consumer debt.
