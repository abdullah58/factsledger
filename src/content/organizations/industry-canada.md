## Overview

Industry Canada was the name of [Canada](/places/canada/)'s federal industry department from 1993 to 2015 — the department now known by the applied title Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED). Formed by merging four predecessor departments in 1993 and established by statute in 1995, it held one of the broadest mandates in Ottawa, covering industry, science, telecommunications, competition, bankruptcy, patents and consumer affairs ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?idnumber=155550&app=fonandcol)).

The name change that ended the Industry Canada era was narrower than it looked. The department was renamed for communication purposes in November 2015, but its legal name remains Industry Canada in policy instruments and legal documents, including contracts ([Government of Canada (CanadaBuys)](https://canadabuys.canada.ca/sites/default/files/webform/tender_notice/27096/crowdsource-data-service.pdf)). This profile is about that federal department — the same institution that now operates as ISED — not the unrelated Department of Industry that existed from 1963 to 1969, and not any provincial industry ministry ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?idnumber=155550&app=fonandcol)).

## Four departments into one

The department was assembled, not founded from scratch. Its antecedents included the Department of Industry, Trade and Commerce, the Department of Regional Economic Expansion, the Department of Regional Industrial Expansion, the Department of Industry, Science and Technology (ISTC) and the Department of Consumer and Corporate Affairs (CCA) ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?idnumber=155550&app=fonandcol)). The new ministry took shape in mid-1993, when Order in Council 1993-1487, dated 25 June 1993, combined ISTC and CCA and transferred control of units including Spectrum Management Operations, the Communications Research Centre and investment review ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?idnumber=155550&app=fonandcol)). CCA dated to 1967 and handled consumer affairs, corporate securities, mergers, bankruptcies and, through the Canadian Intellectual Property Office, patents, copyrights, trade marks and industrial designs ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?idnumber=155550&app=fonandcol)). It was already operating under its new name before Parliament passed the law creating it: the Department of Industry Act, assented to on 16 March 1995, repealed the ISTC Act and officially established the Department of Industry ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?idnumber=155550&app=fonandcol)).

## What the law gave the minister

The 1995 Act handed the minister one of the widest federal remits. Section 4 lists fifteen areas: industry and technology, trade and commerce, science, consumer affairs, corporations and corporate securities, competition and restraint of trade including mergers and monopolies, bankruptcy and insolvency, patents, copyrights, trade-marks, industrial designs and integrated circuit topographies, legal metrology, telecommunications, investment, small businesses and tourism ([Justice Laws Website](https://laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-9.2/20050401/P1TT3xt3.html)). The minister also took on regional economic development in [Ontario](/places/ontario/) and Quebec and served as Registrar General of Canada, registering documents issued under the Great Seal ([Justice Laws Website](https://laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-9.2/20050401/P1TT3xt3.html)). Its stated objectives were to strengthen the national economy, promote sustainable development, increase the international competitiveness of Canadian industry, encourage the fullest use of science and technology, and promote consumer interests ([Justice Laws Website](https://laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-9.2/20050401/P1TT3xt3.html)). The minister could make loans, guarantee repayments, provide loan or credit insurance, and make grants and contributions, and could draw on customs data that the Minister of National Revenue was obliged to share ([Justice Laws Website](https://laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-9.2/20050401/P1TT3xt3.html)).

## The portfolio, the airwaves, and the 2015 renaming

The Minister of Industry was responsible for 13 federal departments and agencies in the Industry Portfolio, including the Atlantic Canada Opportunities Agency, the Business Development Bank of Canada, the Canadian Space Agency, the Competition Tribunal, Copyright Board Canada, Canada Economic Development for Quebec Regions, the National Research Council of Canada, the [Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council](/organizations/natural-sciences-and-engineering-research-council/), the [Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council](/organizations/social-sciences-and-humanities-research-council/), the Standards Council of Canada, Statistics Canada and Western Economic Diversification Canada ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/Home/Record?idnumber=155550&app=fonandcol)). It managed Canada's airwaves and oversaw bankruptcy, incorporation, intellectual property and measurement systems, while its Communications Research Centre researched wireless spectrum management ([Government of Canada (CanadaBuys)](https://canadabuys.canada.ca/sites/default/files/webform/tender_notice/27096/crowdsource-data-service.pdf)). The renaming arrived with Justin Trudeau's first cabinet: on 4 November 2015 Navdeep Bains was named minister of innovation, science and economic development, and the Privy Council Office confirmed the new title was the working title for the minister of industry ([The Wire Report](https://www.thewirereport.ca/2015/11/04/no-more-industry-minister-bains-becomes-innovation-minister/)).

## FAQ

### What was Industry Canada?

Industry Canada was the federal department responsible for industry, science, telecommunications, competition, bankruptcy, intellectual property and consumer affairs in Canada from 1993 to 2015. It was formed by merging predecessor departments in 1993, established by statute in 1995, and renamed Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada in 2015.

### Why was Industry Canada renamed in 2015?

The renaming came with Justin Trudeau's first cabinet, sworn in on 4 November 2015, when Navdeep Bains was named minister of innovation, science and economic development. The Privy Council Office confirmed the new title was the working title for the minister of industry, signalling a shift in emphasis toward innovation and science.

### What did the department actually do?

Its mandate was to make Canadians more productive and competitive in the global knowledge-based economy. In practice it managed Canada's airwaves, oversaw bankruptcy, incorporation, intellectual property and measurement systems, financed and researched industry, encouraged scientific research, and protected consumer interests across the country.

### Is Industry Canada the same thing as ISED?

Yes. Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada is the same federal department under a new applied title adopted in November 2015. The legal name remains the Department of Industry under the 1995 Act, so "Industry Canada" still appears in policy instruments and legal documents.
