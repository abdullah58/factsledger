## Overview

Delta Lloyd Group was a Dutch insurance, pension, banking and asset-management group first established in 1807, making it one of the Netherlands' oldest insurers ([AFP](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/22/12/2014/dutch-insurer-fined-over-using-confidential-info)). Headquartered in Amsterdam and listed on Euronext Amsterdam and Brussels, it served 4.2 million commercial and retail clients in the Netherlands and Belgium under four brands — Delta Lloyd, ABN AMRO Insurance, BeFrank and OHRA — and employed more than 5,100 people ([AFM](https://www.afm.nl/downloadregisterfile.aspx?type=openbaarmaking-voorwetenschap&enc=7Rpj0BBaMD5lzfwUlyQ9TIe+XbKYS1JE7+GVfXY2PxNqAT3NVNKp3QCQcI2WUb5gS/PYmhvg5EA+E7FpgiengZj0NLAMUDbP/oPnUtKH27VA0/CZGu7E4hLGdYYwwWpdO6Jsv7HS8MKduAz9H7w8L41n5SE3EbspSQq7yZO1GRQaA0LNFEGV33oj3rMUC/1wkieNnWtMHfewtC7rR7F6H0jf9APUEbvUS/Vdw5YKi57Y3wajA8BwYwN89lToLBxCxyNNuxTynfuQdT/1MWbCb1NlingFQjmGR6dM3aS1M5PrxSLYcGt7dwosqjZOCK1RMulX9vdUtxsc0D2JjWkEkzRJLD9pAxu1dNiT/UqGsd2d4lmK1PhVqz4IdzxQ3tC4)). A former unit of UK insurer Aviva, it spent its final independent years battered by capital rules and regulatory clashes before bigger Dutch rival NN Group took it over in 2017 ([Domain-b](https://www.Domain-b.com/finance/insurance/insurance-general/dutch-insurer-delta-lloyd-rejects-2-7-bn-takeover-bid-from-nn-group)). Delta Lloyd N.V. ceased to exist on June 1, 2017, and its brand was retired within two years ([AFM](https://www.afm.nl/downloadregisterfile.aspx?type=openbaarmaking-voorwetenschap&enc=7Rpj0BBaMD5lzfwUlyQ9TIe+XbKYS1JE7+GVfXY2PxNqAT3NVNKp3QCQcI2WUb5gS/PYmhvg5EA+E7FpgiengZj0NLAMUDbP/oPnUtKH27VA0/CZGu7E4hLGdYYwwWpdO6Jsv7HS8MKduAz9H7w8L41n5SE3EbspSQq7yZO1GRQaA0LNFEGV33oj3rMUC/1wkieNnWtMHfewtC7rR7F6H0jf9APUEbvUS/Vdw5YKi57Y3wajA8BwYwN89lToLBxCxyNNuxTynfuQdT/1MWbCb1NlingFQjmGR6dM3aS1M5PrxSLYcGt7dwosqjZOCK1RMulX9vdUtxsc0D2JjWkEkzRJLD9pAxu1dNiT/UqGsd2d4lmK1PhVqz4IdzxQ3tC4); [Intelligent Insurer](https://www.intelligentinsurer.com/insurance/friese-of-nn-group-highlights-delta-lloyd-acquisition-in-2018-results-17683)).

## Two centuries of insurance, then a bruising decade

Delta Lloyd's lineage stretches back to 1807, and by 2014 it was among the oldest insurers in the Netherlands with 5,135 employees across the Netherlands, Belgium and Germany ([AFP](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/22/12/2014/dutch-insurer-fined-over-using-confidential-info)). Its modern form was shaped by British ownership: the group was long a unit of UK insurer Aviva, which floated part of it on Euronext Amsterdam in 2009 and sold down the rest in stages, leaving Delta Lloyd an independent listed company ([Domain-b](https://www.Domain-b.com/finance/insurance/insurance-general/dutch-insurer-delta-lloyd-rejects-2-7-bn-takeover-bid-from-nn-group)).

Independence proved punishing. In December 2014 the Dutch central bank fined Delta Lloyd €22.8 million and ordered the dismissal of its chief financial officer, ruling the insurer had traded on confidential information about forthcoming changes to interest-rate calculations ([AFP](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/22/12/2014/dutch-insurer-fined-over-using-confidential-info)). Reuters later described a "turbulent two-year period" that included the departure of top executives after clashing with the Dutch Central Bank and two new share issues to strengthen the capital base — with the shares down more than 50 percent since July 2015 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-delta-lloyd-m-a-nn-group-idUSKBN14C0CF/)). New stringent insurance solvency rules had knocked roughly 70 percent off the stock since the start of 2015, and an earlier €650 million rights issue had faced opposition from major shareholders ([Domain-b](https://www.Domain-b.com/finance/insurance/insurance-general/dutch-insurer-delta-lloyd-rejects-2-7-bn-takeover-bid-from-nn-group)).

## The takeover battle

On October 2, 2016, NN Group — the Netherlands' largest insurer by market capitalisation — made a conditional proposal for a cash offer at €5.30 per share, valuing Delta Lloyd at €2.4 billion ([Intelligent Insurer](https://www.intelligentinsurer.com/insurance/delta-lloyd-rejects-nn-group-s-takeover-proposal-9840)). Delta Lloyd's supervisory and executive boards rejected it, arguing the bid "substantially undervalues" the company, its prospects and its strategic opportunities: the 29 percent premium to the October 4 closing price was below market norms for cash deals, and only 11 percent above its undisturbed six-month high of €4.79 ([Intelligent Insurer](https://www.intelligentinsurer.com/insurance/delta-lloyd-rejects-nn-group-s-takeover-proposal-9840)). Chairman Hans van der Noordaa insisted the company had a clear strategy and a solid, improving capital position — though the boards conceded that Dutch insurance consolidation would create significant synergies, and that Delta Lloyd shareholders deserved an adequate share of that value ([Domain-b](https://www.Domain-b.com/finance/insurance/insurance-general/dutch-insurer-delta-lloyd-rejects-2-7-bn-takeover-bid-from-nn-group); [Intelligent Insurer](https://www.intelligentinsurer.com/insurance/delta-lloyd-rejects-nn-group-s-takeover-proposal-9840)).

The standoff lasted under three months. A rival bid never materialised: a source close to the deal told Reuters that Dutch insurer ASR had considered a bid but quickly decided it was too risky, while Chinese insurer Anbang — owner of Dutch insurer Vivat — dropped the idea after facing opposition from regulators ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-delta-lloyd-m-a-nn-group-idUSKBN14C0CF/)). On December 23, 2016, the two companies announced agreement on a recommended offer at €5.40 per share in cash — just 1.9 percent above the rejected bid — valuing Delta Lloyd at €2.5 billion ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-delta-lloyd-m-a-nn-group-idUSKBN14C0CF/)). Delta Lloyd acknowledged that continuing capital concerns had influenced the decision; the €5.40 price equated to a 55 percent premium to its average share price in the three months before NN's initial approach, and Van der Noordaa agreed to step down as CEO once the deal completed ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-delta-lloyd-m-a-nn-group-idUSKBN14C0CF/)).

Completion followed swiftly in 2017. The offer was declared unconditional in April after 79.9 percent of shares were tendered, with settlement on April 12; by the end of the month NN Group held over 93 percent ([Intelligent Insurer](https://www.intelligentinsurer.com/insurance/nn-group-posts-jump-in-profits-bullish-on-delta-lloyd-acquisition-11971)). The triangular legal merger of Delta Lloyd into NN Group Bidco was executed on May 31 and took effect on June 1, 2017 — the day Delta Lloyd N.V. "ceased to exist" and its shares traded for the last time, with remaining shareholders receiving NN Group shares ([AFM](https://www.afm.nl/downloadregisterfile.aspx?type=openbaarmaking-voorwetenschap&enc=7Rpj0BBaMD5lzfwUlyQ9TIe+XbKYS1JE7+GVfXY2PxNqAT3NVNKp3QCQcI2WUb5gS/PYmhvg5EA+E7FpgiengZj0NLAMUDbP/oPnUtKH27VA0/CZGu7E4hLGdYYwwWpdO6Jsv7HS8MKduAz9H7w8L41n5SE3EbspSQq7yZO1GRQaA0LNFEGV33oj3rMUC/1wkieNnWtMHfewtC7rR7F6H0jf9APUEbvUS/Vdw5YKi57Y3wajA8BwYwN89lToLBxCxyNNuxTynfuQdT/1MWbCb1NlingFQjmGR6dM3aS1M5PrxSLYcGt7dwosqjZOCK1RMulX9vdUtxsc0D2JjWkEkzRJLD9pAxu1dNiT/UqGsd2d4lmK1PhVqz4IdzxQ3tC4)).

## What happened to the brand

NN Group moved fast to erase the Delta Lloyd identity. During 2018 the combined company aligned systems and portfolios and rebranded Delta Lloyd's products and offices to NN; by year-end, CEO Lard Friese said the rebranding of Delta Lloyd's products and services to Nationale-Nederlanden was "virtually complete" ([Intelligent Insurer](https://www.intelligentinsurer.com/insurance/friese-of-nn-group-highlights-delta-lloyd-acquisition-in-2018-results-17683)). The legal entities followed: with approval from the Dutch Central Bank, Delta Lloyd Life and Delta Lloyd Non-life were merged into NN Life and NN Non-life with effect from January 1, 2019, with NN assuming all of their assets and liabilities ([Intelligent Insurer](https://www.intelligentinsurer.com/nn-group-merges-life-and-non-life-in-the-netherlands-17300)). The integration was part of a cost programme targeting €400 million in savings by 2020, of which €289 million had been achieved by the end of 2018 ([Intelligent Insurer](https://www.intelligentinsurer.com/insurance/friese-of-nn-group-highlights-delta-lloyd-acquisition-in-2018-results-17683)).
