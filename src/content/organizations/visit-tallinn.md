## Overview

Visit Tallinn is the official tourism portal and visitor authority of [Tallinn](/places/tallinn/), the capital of Estonia, run by the Tallinn City Tourist Office & Convention Bureau ([Visit Tallinn](https://visittallinn.ee/eng/professional/statistics-surveys/reports/surveys)). It is the first stop for visitors to the city: its Tourist Information Centre on Niguliste 2 gives up-to-date information on museums, attractions, tours, events, restaurants and shopping, books tour guides, hands out free city maps, and sells the Tallinn Card — the city's all-in-one sightseeing pass ([Visit Tallinn](https://www.visittallinn.ee/eng/visitor/plan/good-to-know/tourist-information)).

The organisation behind the portal is a city body, not a private travel company. The Tallinn City Tourist Office & Convention Bureau has carried out Tallinn's foreign visitors' survey since 2002 and takes part in different research projects on the city's tourism ([Visit Tallinn](https://visittallinn.ee/eng/professional/statistics-surveys/reports/surveys)). That research role is what makes Visit Tallinn more than a listings site: it is the [City of Tallinn](/organizations/city-of-tallinn/)'s own instrument for understanding who visits, how long they stay, and what they spend — and for turning those answers into marketing, conferences and low-season campaigns.

## What Visit Tallinn is and does

The visitor-facing core of Visit Tallinn is the Tallinn Tourist Information Centre. Visitors can get up-to-date information on the city's museums and attractions, tours, events, restaurants and shopping possibilities, and can book Tallinn tour guides through the centre ([Visit Tallinn](https://www.visittallinn.ee/eng/visitor/plan/good-to-know/tourist-information)). The centre offers free city maps and free public Wi-Fi, and sells the Tallinn Card, Piletilevi event tickets, the Ühiskaart public transport card, and stamps and postcards ([Visit Tallinn](https://www.visittallinn.ee/eng/visitor/plan/good-to-know/tourist-information)). One thing it deliberately does not do: the centre "does not offer booking services for hotels, restaurants or similar establishments" ([Visit Tallinn](https://www.visittallinn.ee/eng/visitor/plan/good-to-know/tourist-information)). In summer it is open daily from 9 am to 6 pm; in winter, Monday to Saturday 10 am to 5 pm and Sundays 10 am to 3 pm, closed on 1 January and 25–26 December ([Visit Tallinn](https://www.visittallinn.ee/eng/visitor/plan/good-to-know/tourist-information)).

The portal's listings are the city's official record of what is worth seeing, and they include the small and the unusual alongside the famous. Its attractions listings include the [Banned Books Museum](/organizations/banned-books-museum/), the one-room museum of censored books in the Old Town ([Visit Tallinn](https://visittallinn.ee/eng/visitor/see-do/things-to-do/attractions-museums/181031/banned-books-museum)). That kind of listing is the point of the organisation: a visitor who arrives knowing nothing about Tallinn gets a single, city-run source for what exists, where it is, and how to reach it.

## The tourism it serves

The city Visit Tallinn promotes had a record year in 2024. Tourism grew by 7 percent to 3.18 million international visits, and overnight stays in the city's accommodation establishments totalled 3.31 million — the highest figure on record, above the 3.27 million of 2019, the last pre-pandemic year ([City of Tallinn](https://www.tallinn.ee/en/news/tallinns-international-tourism-breaks-records-grows-and-diversifies)). According to Statistics Estonia, 1.31 million international tourists stayed in Tallinn's accommodation establishments, an 11 percent increase from 2023, and international tourists spent an estimated €1 billion in the city ([City of Tallinn](https://www.tallinn.ee/en/news/tallinns-international-tourism-breaks-records-grows-and-diversifies)). The stat that anchors the story is the length of stay: nearly 60 percent of foreign guests opted to stay overnight, and more than half of all visits (58 percent) were multi-day stays, growing 9 percent against 5 percent for single-day visits ([City of Tallinn](https://www.tallinn.ee/en/news/tallinns-international-tourism-breaks-records-grows-and-diversifies)). The implication is a city that is converting day-trippers — many arriving on the ferries from Helsinki — into overnight guests who spend more, which is exactly the shift the tourist office's strategy targets.

The fastest-growing markets in overnight stays were Germany, the United Kingdom, the United States and various Asian countries, with growth of 18 to 27 percent, while the largest markets, Finland and Latvia, remained stable ([City of Tallinn](https://www.tallinn.ee/en/news/tallinns-international-tourism-breaks-records-grows-and-diversifies)). The city's goal, in the words of Deputy Mayor Margot Roose in February 2025, is "to establish Tallinn as a year-round destination", and the head of tourism, Evelin Tsirk, pointed to the main obstacle: seasonality, with an almost threefold difference between January (124,000 overnight stays) and July (344,000) ([City of Tallinn](https://www.tallinn.ee/en/news/tallinns-international-tourism-breaks-records-grows-and-diversifies)). The city's answer includes a €350,000 international conference support programme for 2025 and leisure marketing aimed at low-season periods, including the Tallinn Bun Festival initiative ([City of Tallinn](https://www.tallinn.ee/en/news/tallinns-international-tourism-breaks-records-grows-and-diversifies)).
