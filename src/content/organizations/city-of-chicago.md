## Overview

The City of Chicago is the municipal corporation that governs [Chicago](/places/chicago/) — a home-rule, mayor–council government incorporated as a city on March 4, 1837, serving about 2.7 million residents across roughly 228 square miles ([Office of the City Clerk](https://chicityclerk.com/about-city-government-chicago-city-council); [City of Chicago Investor Relations](https://www.cityofchicagoinvestors.com/city-of-chicago-il/about/i125)).

It is not the place itself: this profile covers the government corporation — its mayor, its 50-member City Council, its departments, its budget, and its debts — while the place profile covers the city as a geographic and human community. The corporation governs the third most populous city in the [United States](/places/united-states/), led by Mayor Brandon Johnson as chief executive, and it employs 36,206 budgeted full-time workers ([City of Chicago Investor Relations](https://www.cityofchicagoinvestors.com/city-of-chicago-il/about/i125)). The 2020 census counted 2,746,388 residents; the U.S. Census Bureau's July 1, 2025 estimate was 2,731,585, down 0.6% since 2020 ([U.S. Census Bureau](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/chicagocityillinois/PST045224)). The city's largest employers include [Amazon](/organizations/amazon/) with 32,000 workers and [JPMorgan Chase](/organizations/jpmorgan-chase/) with 14,058 ([City of Chicago Investor Relations](https://www.cityofchicagoinvestors.com/city-of-chicago-il/about/i125)).

## The corporation and its officers

Chicago's government rests on home rule. Under the Illinois Constitution of 1970, the City is a home-rule unit of local government, and its powers are exercised by four elected officers — the Mayor, the City Clerk, the City Treasurer, and the City Council ([Office of the City Clerk](https://chicityclerk.com/about-city-government-chicago-city-council); [City of Chicago Investor Relations](https://www.cityofchicagoinvestors.com/city-of-chicago-il/about/i125)). Its roots run to the 1770s, when Jean Baptiste Point du Sable settled at the mouth of the Chicago River, and to its incorporation as a city on March 4, 1837, when its first charter created six wards ([Office of the City Clerk](https://chicityclerk.com/about-city-government-chicago-city-council)). Mayor Brandon Johnson, who assumed office on May 15, 2023, is the chief executive: he administers the city departments, proposes the budget, signs legislation into law, and appoints department commissioners ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Chicago,_Illinois)). City Clerk Anna Valencia keeps the Council's records and the corporate seal, City Treasurer Melissa Conyears-Ervin manages the City's cash, investments, and pension funds, and City Comptroller Michael D. Belsky oversees its finances ([Office of the City Clerk](https://chicityclerk.com/about-city-government-chicago-city-council); [City of Chicago Investor Relations](https://www.cityofchicagoinvestors.com/city-of-chicago-il/about/i125)).

## The City Council and the ward system

The City Council is the corporation's legislative branch: 50 alderpersons, one elected from each of the city's 50 wards to a four-year term, plus the Mayor and City Clerk as members ([Office of the City Clerk](https://chicityclerk.com/about-city-government-chicago-city-council)). Alderpersons are nominated by petition and elected in February of the year before a presidential election, with an April run-off if nobody wins a majority ([Office of the City Clerk](https://chicityclerk.com/about-city-government-chicago-city-council)). The Council adopts the budget, approves mayoral appointees, levies taxes, buys and sells land, and makes or amends the city's laws ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Chicago,_Illinois)). It works through sixteen standing committees, meets at 10:00 a.m. on the second and fourth Wednesdays of each month, and needs a majority for a quorum ([Office of the City Clerk](https://chicityclerk.com/about-city-government-chicago-city-council)). It elects a President Pro Tempore to preside when the Mayor is absent and a Vice-Mayor to serve as interim mayor if the office falls vacant ([Office of the City Clerk](https://chicityclerk.com/about-city-government-chicago-city-council)). The Mayor, a department, an alderperson, a committee, or a citizen may introduce legislation, which becomes law once the full Council approves it ([Office of the City Clerk](https://chicityclerk.com/about-city-government-chicago-city-council)).

## Budget, workforce, and debt

Each summer city departments send spending projections to the Office of Budget Management, the Mayor proposes a budget by October 15, the Council holds hearings, and the adopted Annual Appropriation Ordinance takes effect on January 1 — state law requires a balanced budget ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Chicago,_Illinois)). For fiscal 2026 the Council closed a projected $1.2 billion gap with a $16.6 billion budget adopted on December 20, 2025, rejecting the Mayor's October proposal and head tax while raising the cloud-computing tax to 15% ([Civic Federation](https://www.civicfed.org/chicagos-fy2026-adopted-budget)). It took effect on January 1, 2026, without the Mayor's signature or veto; over $500 million of the roughly $1 billion tax-increment-financing surplus went to Chicago Public Schools, and $232.6 million to the City itself ([Civic Federation](https://www.civicfed.org/chicagos-fy2026-adopted-budget)). The corporation is large: 36,206 budgeted full-time positions in 2025, $11.1 billion in bonded debt at the end of 2025, and a 2026 Corporate Fund budget of $6.25 billion ([City of Chicago Investor Relations](https://www.cityofchicagoinvestors.com/city-of-chicago-il/about/i125)). Personnel and benefits consume 70% of the operating budget, and pension contributions plus debt service take 40% of the total budget ([Civic Federation](https://www.civicfed.org/chicagos-fy2026-adopted-budget)).

Chicago's four pension funds carried a combined $35.6 billion in unfunded liabilities, with two of the four funds below 25% funded, at the most recent actuarial valuations in 2024 ([Civic Federation](https://www.civicfed.org/chicagos-fy2026-adopted-budget)) — and because those legacy costs already absorb 40% of the total budget, they crowd out the money available for the services the corporation exists to deliver. Cutting the advance pension payment in the Mayor's proposal had already prompted S&P Global Ratings to revise its outlook on the City's debt from stable to negative ([Civic Federation](https://www.civicfed.org/chicagos-fy2026-adopted-budget)).

## FAQ

### Is the City of Chicago the same thing as Chicago?

No. Chicago is the place — a city of about 2.7 million people on Lake Michigan — while the City of Chicago is the municipal government corporation that runs it ([Office of the City Clerk](https://chicityclerk.com/about-city-government-chicago-city-council)). This profile covers the corporation: its mayor, its 50-member City Council, its departments, its budget, and its debts. The place profile covers the city as a place.

### Who runs the City of Chicago?

Mayor Brandon Johnson is the chief executive; he assumed office on May 15, 2023, and the next mayoral election is in 2027 ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Chicago,_Illinois)). He works alongside the 50-member City Council, City Clerk Anna Valencia, and City Treasurer Melissa Conyears-Ervin. The Mayor administers departments and proposes the budget; the Council adopts it and makes the city's laws.

### How big is the City of Chicago's budget?

The Council adopted a $16.6 billion budget for fiscal 2026 on December 20, 2025, closing a projected $1.2 billion gap ([Civic Federation](https://www.civicfed.org/chicagos-fy2026-adopted-budget)). The 2026 Corporate Fund budget, the main operating fund, totals $6.25 billion ([City of Chicago Investor Relations](https://www.cityofchicagoinvestors.com/city-of-chicago-il/about/i125)). The adopted budget rejected the Mayor's head tax, raised the cloud-computing tax to 15%, and directed a $232.6 million tax-increment-financing surplus to the City.

### How many people work for the City of Chicago?

The City budgeted 36,206 full-time equivalent positions in 2025 ([City of Chicago Investor Relations](https://www.cityofchicagoinvestors.com/city-of-chicago-il/about/i125)). Those workers deliver public safety, street maintenance, sanitation, water and sewer services, health, cultural, aviation, city development, and human services. Personnel costs with benefits consume 70% of the operating budget, which is why staffing levels dominate every budget debate ([Civic Federation](https://www.civicfed.org/chicagos-fy2026-adopted-budget)).

### What is the City's pension problem?

Chicago's four pension funds carried $35.6 billion in combined unfunded liabilities at their 2024 valuations, with two funds below 25% funded — among the worst-funded in the nation ([Civic Federation](https://www.civicfed.org/chicagos-fy2026-adopted-budget)). The FY2026 budget restored the full advance pension payment, but 2025 legislation boosting Tier 2 police and fire benefits added to the City's long-term exposure.
