## The 2026 partnership sprint

In September 2026, Qatar's sovereign wealth fund signed its biggest American deal of the year. QIA and J.P. Morgan Asset Management entered a preliminary $20 billion strategic partnership: $15 billion in customized global equity portfolios built to match QIA's needs, and $5 billion in a private markets initiative supplying senior financing to middle-market firms in the United States. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/qatars-wealth-fund-jp-morgan-asset-management-launch-20-billion-partnership-2026-09-21/))

QIA's chief executive, Mohammed Saif Al-Sowaidi, framed the deal as a long game. "This partnership establishes a new platform for growth: one that aligns our ambition to build resilient, forward-looking investment capabilities and strengthens ties between the US and Qatar," he said. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/qatars-wealth-fund-jp-morgan-asset-management-launch-20-billion-partnership-2026-09-21/))

It was the second mega-alliance of 2026. In January, QIA had signed a deal to expand its existing partnership with Goldman Sachs, targeting $25 billion. Together, the two arrangements committed $45 billion of Qatari capital to American asset managers in under nine months. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/qatars-wealth-fund-jp-morgan-asset-management-launch-20-billion-partnership-2026-09-21/))

The timing reflected pressure at home. Reuters reported that Qatar was facing financial strain from the Iran war, which had made it impossible for the Gulf state to export liquefied natural gas reliably. With its core revenue engine disrupted, Doha was pushing diversification — including a move to expand and restructure its financial sector. The same weekend the J.P. Morgan deal was announced, Qatar's prime minister unveiled a new QIA division dedicated to developing domestic investments. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/qatars-wealth-fund-jp-morgan-asset-management-launch-20-billion-partnership-2026-09-21/))

That domestic turn is a departure for an institution built to invest Qatar's surplus abroad. With estimated assets near $580 billion — the sovereign wealth data firm SWFI put it closer to $600 billion in July 2026, making it the world's ninth-largest sovereign fund — QIA has spent two decades deploying oil and gas wealth across global markets. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/qatars-wealth-fund-jp-morgan-asset-management-launch-20-billion-partnership-2026-09-21/); [Marhaba Qatar](https://marhaba.qa/qatar-investment-authority-qia/))

## The Kushner connection

One of QIA's most politically sensitive placements is in the United States. In 2024, [Affinity Partners](/organizations/affinity-partners/) — the investment firm [Jared Kushner](/people/jared-kushner/) launched in 2021 after leaving the White House — secured $1.5 billion of extra capital from the Abu Dhabi-based fund Lunate and QIA. Affinity's assets under management jumped 60 percent that year to $4.8 billion, up from $3 billion, according to SEC filings. Kushner said the increase had closed before Donald Trump's re-election. ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/kushners-affinitys-assets-jump-48-billion-after-gulf-cash-injection-2025-03-28/?utm_campaign=Daily%20Newsletter&utm_source=hs_email&utm_medium=email&_hsenc=p2ANqtz-9BmqvhtQx6qPy6oZVYKoPllUpeWKa3ddHAdMNOFy8l5CRfWM_XAg4mBQ1GmhGmpRq5MpQq))

Affinity's flagship investor remains Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, which seeded the firm with $2 billion. The 2024 Gulf top-up broadened that capital base to Abu Dhabi and Doha. Some observers see a political dimension in the arrangement: a Chatham House research associate told AGBI it was "a form of geoeconomic statecraft," with Gulf governments using sovereign funds to build relationships with influential Americans. The company has said its sovereign backers are passive investors, kept informed through standard reporting. ([Arabian Gulf Business Insight](https://www.agbi.com/analysis/trade/2026/01/kushners-affinity-blurs-the-lines-between-politics-and-gulf-capital/))

## Origins and mandate

QIA was established in 2005 by Amiri Decision No. 22. Its mandate, as the authority describes it on its own site, is "developing, investing and managing the State reserve funds and other property assigned to it by the Supreme Council for Economic Affairs and Investment." ([Qatar Investment Authority](http://qia.qa/en/About/Pages/Tax-Policy.aspx))

That Supreme Council — the body that sets Qatar's broader economic policy — is the one QIA answers to: it approves the fund's investment policy and oversees its performance. The institution was created with a long-term objective — to diversify the Qatari economy and state finances beyond hydrocarbon revenue — alongside a medium-term goal of funding infrastructure, education and environmental projects, and a shorter-term aim of investing prudently to maximize returns. ([Marhaba Qatar](https://marhaba.qa/qatar-investment-authority-qia/))

QIA does not publish its holdings, and most information about its portfolio comes from media reporting rather than disclosure. ([Marhaba Qatar](https://marhaba.qa/qatar-investment-authority-qia/))

## Restructures and green pivots

The fund has been reshaped repeatedly. In 2016 it was restructured to concentrate about $100 billion of investments in local companies inside a dedicated unit, Qatar Investments. In 2020 it revised its sustainability strategy: no new fossil-fuel investments, a commitment to sustainability across its portfolio, and a stated aim to double green investments. It was a founding member of the One Planet Sovereign Wealth Fund Working Group and adopted its 2018 climate framework. ([Marhaba Qatar](https://marhaba.qa/qatar-investment-authority-qia/))

Amiri Decision No. 34 of 2023 reorganized the authority under an enhanced governance framework. The following year brought the 2024 initiatives: an Active Asset Management Initiative and a $1 billion venture-capital fund of funds designed to back local and international venture capital funds operating in Qatar. ([Marhaba Qatar](https://marhaba.qa/qatar-investment-authority-qia/))

That VC vehicle gained traction. It anchored twelve venture-capital firms in its first year, and in February 2026 QIA extended it with another $2 billion — doubling down on the startup pipeline. A further restructure followed in March 2026, with QIA announcing a new board of directors. ([Marhaba Qatar](https://marhaba.qa/qatar-investment-authority-qia/))

## How it invests

QIA describes its approach as strategic asset-class management, with the authority aiming to be a preferred partner for direct investment. Its direct-investment focus spans real estate, infrastructure, technology, retail, healthcare and financial services, alongside active investment-management mandates. ([Marhaba Qatar](https://marhaba.qa/qatar-investment-authority-qia/))

The 2020 sustainability overhaul built environmental and social targets into that approach. Beyond the ban on new fossil-fuel investments, QIA set out to double its green investments and cut carbon emissions, reduce energy and water consumption, and expand waste recycling across its portfolio, with workforce initiatives running in parallel. The stated goal was to position the fund as a regional leader in responsible investing — an ambition that sits alongside the fund's continued lack of published holdings, which means outside observers still assess its portfolio from filings and reporting rather than disclosure. ([Marhaba Qatar](https://marhaba.qa/qatar-investment-authority-qia/))
