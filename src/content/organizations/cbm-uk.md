## Overview

The public record of CBM UK contains two legal events that no single page on
the internet puts together. The first is the 2017 absorption of an
international mental-health charity — an event the Charity Commission's
register then records a second time, formally, five years later. The second is
the 2019 division of the international federation CBM UK belonged to, after
which the UK charity became a founding member of a new Dutch-registered
federation while the old programme-implementing body merged into its German
sister organisation. Both events are verified in primary documents — a charity
trade publication and the Charity Commission register for the first, audited
financial statements for the second — and neither is explained in the
fundraising boilerplate that fills page one of the search results.

Christian Blind Mission (United Kingdom) Limited — CBM UK — is the British
member association of the CBM Global Disability Inclusion federation, a
Cambridge-based charity that funds and supports disability-inclusive
development in some of the world's poorest places
([UK Charity Commission](https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/en/charity-search/-/charity-details/1058162/full-print),
[CBM Global Disability Inclusion](https://cbm-global.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Consolidated-Fin-Statements-Audit-report.pdf)).
Its governing objects cover treatment and care of people with physical or
mental disabilities of all kinds — including blindness and preventative
treatment — plus education and training for disabled people, their families
and their carers, with the emphasis on developing-world countries
([UK Charity Commission](https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/en/charity-search/-/charity-details/1058162/full-print)).
The poverty–disability cycle is not just rhetoric for this charity: "END THE
CYCLE OF POVERTY & DISABILITY" is one of its legally registered working names
([UK Charity Commission](https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/en/charity-search/-/charity-details/1058162/full-print)).

## A 1996 charity inside a century-old mission

CBM UK was established in 1996 — its Memorandum and Articles were
incorporated on 19 January and the Charity Commission granted standard
registration on 19 September of that year
([UK Charity Commission](https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/en/charity-search/-/charity-details/1058162/full-print)).
It celebrated its 21st anniversary with an evening gathering at the Royal
Society in London on 17 May 2017, thanking UK supporters and attended by, among
others, the then Shadow Foreign Secretary Emily Thornberry, Paralympic
wheelchair racer Anne Wafula-Strike MBE, actress Trudie Goodwin and Lord
Blunkett
([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/cbm-uks-21st-anniversary-highlighting-urgent-need-to-support-people-with-disabilities-overseas-622752074.html)).
Chief executive Kirsty Smith used the occasion to warn that people with
disabilities — one in seven of the world's population — were being left behind
even as global poverty fell, and that disabled people were still routinely
denied schooling, work and basic social acceptance
([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/cbm-uks-21st-anniversary-highlighting-urgent-need-to-support-people-with-disabilities-overseas-622752074.html)).

The British charity sits inside a much older movement: in written evidence to
Parliament in February 2019, CBM UK described CBM as a global charity with
"over a century's experience of working with people with disabilities, and
more than 600 programmes across the world," and identified itself as "a Member
Association of the CBM Federation"
([UK Parliament](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/99742/pdf/)).
At the time of the 2017 anniversary, CBM UK worked with local partners to run
programmes in 14 countries across Africa, Asia and Latin America, while
contributing to work in over 60 countries as part of the global federation,
with the worldwide movement helping around 30 million people a year
([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/cbm-uks-21st-anniversary-highlighting-urgent-need-to-support-people-with-disabilities-overseas-622752074.html)).

## Absorbing BasicNeeds: announced 2017, completed 2022

On 20 July 2017, the international mental-health charity
[BasicNeeds](/organizations/basicneeds/) — profiled separately here for its
own community-based mental-health model — became a wholly-owned subsidiary of
CBM UK after running into difficulty raising money for its programmes
([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/basicneeds-merged-christian-disability-charity/management/article/1440733)).
The arithmetic behind the deal was stark: BasicNeeds had an annual income of
about £1.6 million and had approached CBM UK, with an income of roughly £6
million, proposing the merger to prevent the closure of its mental-health
programmes, then running in 14 countries
([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/basicneeds-merged-christian-disability-charity/management/article/1440733)).
Five of BasicNeeds' UK-based staff joined the CBM UK team; three UK roles and
one IT role in Sri Lanka were lost; no jobs or funding were cut from the
overseas programmes; both charities stayed registered with the Charity
Commission while CBM UK's trustees took over BasicNeeds' governance; and the
merged operation was based at CBM UK's Cambridge headquarters — BasicNeeds had
formerly been based in Leamington Spa
([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/basicneeds-merged-christian-disability-charity/management/article/1440733)).
BasicNeeds chief executive Adrian Sell continued to oversee the programmes and
joined CBM UK's leadership team, and CBM UK's Kirsty Smith said the merger
would expand the charity's mental-health work alongside its programmes for
people affected by blindness and other disabilities, meeting "this huge and
neglected need"
([Third Sector](https://www.thirdsector.co.uk/basicneeds-merged-christian-disability-charity/management/article/1440733)).

The public story did not end at the press announcement. The Charity
Commission's register records a further event for CBM UK: "19 May 2022: Funds
received from 1079599 BASICNEEDS" — the charity number 1079599 being
BasicNeeds' own registration — the formal completion of the transfer, five
years after the two charities announced it
([UK Charity Commission](https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/en/charity-search/-/charity-details/1058162/full-print)).

## The 2019 federation split — and which CBM is which

Two years after the BasicNeeds deal, the international structure around CBM UK
itself was reorganised. According to the audited consolidated financial
statements of CBM Global Disability Inclusion, the former federation — then
comprising ten members — "decided to divide themselves into two separate
entities": CBM International (into which CBM Germany and the former CBM US
activities were merged) and CBM Global Disability Inclusion, which established
itself as a new federation
([CBM Global Disability Inclusion](https://cbm-global.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Consolidated-Fin-Statements-Audit-report.pdf)).
The arrangements were formalised in September 2019, with a two-year transition
period starting 1 January 2020, during which CBM Global built itself out as a
standalone international NGO and country offices were transferred across
through 2021
([CBM Global Disability Inclusion](https://cbm-global.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Consolidated-Fin-Statements-Audit-report.pdf)).

The new federation is a Dutch legal entity — CBM Global Disability Inclusion
Vereniging, registered with the Netherlands Chamber of Commerce on 9 September
2019 (registration 75787032) with its statutory seat in Amstelveen — and its
members are CBM Australia, CBM Ireland, CBM Kenya, CBM New Zealand, CBM
Switzerland and CBM UK; Italy chose to operate as a unitary INGO
([CBM Global Disability Inclusion](https://cbm-global.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Consolidated-Fin-Statements-Audit-report.pdf)).
The UK charity is not a passive member: CBM UK's William Edwin M Godfrey
chaired the CBM Global board in 2021
([CBM Global Disability Inclusion](https://cbm-global.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Consolidated-Fin-Statements-Audit-report.pdf)).
This is the distinction that matters for anyone reading about CBM today: CBM
UK is a national member association of the new federation, not the old
programme-implementing CBM International, and not CBM Christoffel-Blindenmission
e.V., the German entity that absorbed CBM International. The federation's
stated mission is "Fighting to end the cycle of poverty and disability," and
its four core areas are community-based inclusive development, humanitarian
action, inclusive eye health and neglected tropical diseases, and community
mental health — the last of which formally carries the BasicNeeds inheritance
([CBM Global Disability Inclusion](https://cbm-global.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Consolidated-Fin-Statements-Audit-report.pdf)).

## Programmes, money and a role in British aid policy

CBM UK's programme model works through local partners rather than its own
field staff: cataract surgery, eye-health services, inclusive education,
livelihoods and assistive devices, plus advocacy for disability inclusion
([UK Charity Commission](https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/en/charity-search/-/charity-details/1058162/full-print),
[CBM Global Disability Inclusion](https://cbm-global.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Consolidated-Fin-Statements-Audit-report.pdf)).
The register lists its operating footprint as Bangladesh, Indonesia, Kenya,
Malawi, Nepal, Nigeria, Rwanda, Tanzania, Uganda, Zambia and Zimbabwe — plus,
unusually for an "overseas disability charity," Germany, Scotland and Northern
Ireland
([UK Charity Commission](https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/en/charity-search/-/charity-details/1058162/full-print)).
A concrete example of the model is the 2020 See the Way appeal, launched
online by Diane Louise Jordan: it raised £2,882,712, of which £1,320,921 was
UK government match funding under the UK Aid Match scheme — every public pound
doubled — financing a new three-year programme to improve access to sight-saving
eye-health services in Malawi with long-term partner Nkhoma Eye Hospital and
eye surgeon Dr Vincent Moyo
([Cambridge Network](https://www.cambridgenetwork.co.uk/news/cbm-charity-raises-ps28m-help-people-see-way-brighter-future-2020-appeal)).
The appeal's backstory also fixes the charity on the map: it had recently
relocated from Oakington to Mercer's Row, Cambridge — the address the register
still shows as 1 Munro House, 20 Mercers Row
([Cambridge Network](https://www.cambridgenetwork.co.uk/news/cbm-charity-raises-ps28m-help-people-see-way-brighter-future-2020-appeal),
[UK Charity Commission](https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/en/charity-search/-/charity-details/1058162/full-print)).

Financially, the charity is mid-sized by British INGO standards. For the year
to 31 December 2025 it reported total income of £6,523,632 — £3.93 million in
donations and legacies, £2.20 million from charitable activities, and £489,528
from three government grants — against total expenditure of £7,623,355,
including £6.02 million on charitable activities and £1.60 million on raising
funds; it employs 34 people, has ten trustees who receive no remuneration, and
operates no trading subsidiaries
([UK Charity Commission](https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/en/charity-search/-/charity-details/1058162/full-print)).
The five-year financial history shows 2021 as the peak year at £13.48 million
income, with government grants reaching £3.33 million that year before falling
back to £489,528 in 2025
([UK Charity Commission](https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/en/charity-search/-/charity-details/1058162/full-print)).

CBM UK also plays a less visible role at home: auditing British aid policy
itself. In February 2019 it submitted detailed written evidence to the House
of Commons International Development Committee's inquiry into DFID's work on
disability — reporting that it had audited a DFID programme and found partners
were not systematically including disabled people, urging the department to
build permanent in-house disability expertise, improve disability-disaggregated
data, and strengthen mental-health capacity if the UK was to "step up as a
global leader"
([UK Parliament](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/99742/pdf/)).
The submission shows a charity that acts as a watchdog on the system that
funds it, not only as a recipient.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not restate the worldwide federation's reach figures (~30
million people a year in 2017; 600+ programmes in 2019) as CBM UK's own —
they describe the international movement the charity belongs to, not the
British organisation's programme accounts. It does not attribute a cause to
the 2021 income peak of £13.48 million, because the register states the figure
without explaining it. It does not fill in the five years between the 2017
BasicNeeds announcement and the 2022 register transfer beyond what the
documents record. It does not treat CBM UK as the same entity as the old CBM
International or the German CBM Christoffel-Blindenmission e.V.; it is a
member association of the separate CBM Global Disability Inclusion federation.
It does not speculate on what the register's Germany, Scotland and Northern
Ireland entries mean operationally. A later register event — funds received
from the Jubilee Pentecostal Church, recorded 12 February 2026 — post-dates
this profile's last update and is noted here, not asserted in the body.
