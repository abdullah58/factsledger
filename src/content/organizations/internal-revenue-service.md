## Overview

The Internal Revenue Service (IRS) is the tax agency of the United States — a bureau of the [Department of the Treasury](/organizations/us-department-of-the-treasury/) that collected $5.1 trillion in fiscal year 2024, about 96% of federal revenue ([IRS](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USIRS/bulletins/3e29e39)). Founded in 1862 to finance the Civil War and renamed the IRS in 1953 ([National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/chicago/finding-aids/irs)), it processes more than 266 million returns but entered 2026 without a Senate-confirmed commissioner after losing about 28% of its workforce in 2025 ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-trump-administrations-irs-cuts-undermined-filing-season-performance/); [Senate Finance](https://www.finance.senate.gov/ranking-members-news/wyden-schumer-warren-warn-that-bessents-term-as-acting-irs-commissioner-has-expired-sound-alarm-over-leadership-vacuum-amid-chaos-at-irs)).

Organized under Internal Revenue Code Section 7801 to carry out the Treasury Secretary's responsibilities ([IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/about-irs)), its stated mission is to "Provide America's taxpayers top quality service by helping them understand and meet their tax responsibilities and to enforce the law with integrity and fairness to all" ([IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/about-irs)). Its work spans processing returns, collecting unpaid taxes, auditing filers, and pursuing criminal tax investigations.

## From Civil War bureau to a $5.1 trillion collector

Congress created the Office of Commissioner of Internal Revenue on July 1, 1862, and the Bureau of Internal Revenue began collecting taxes to finance the Civil War ([IRS.gov](http://www.irs.gov/ru/about-irs/previous-irs-commissioners)). The 1862 law imposed the nation's first progressive income tax — 3% on income over $600 and 5% on income over $10,000 — collected through district assessors in every state and territory not in rebellion ([National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/chicago/finding-aids/irs)). The Civil War income tax expired in 1872; the Supreme Court struck down a later income tax as unconstitutional in 1895; and the 16th Amendment, ratified in 1913, restored its constitutional basis ([National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/chicago/finding-aids/irs)). The 1913 act imposed a 1% levy on incomes over $3,000 ($4,000 for couples), touching less than 1% of the population ([National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/chicago/finding-aids/irs)). The bureau was renamed the Internal Revenue Service in a 1953 Treasury reorganization; the 1998 IRS Restructuring and Reform Act set five-year terms for Senate-confirmed commissioners ([National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/chicago/finding-aids/irs); [IRS.gov](http://www.irs.gov/ru/about-irs/previous-irs-commissioners)). In fiscal year 2024 the IRS collected $5.1 trillion, processed more than 266 million returns and other forms, and closed 505,514 audits producing $29 billion in recommended additional tax ([IRS](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USIRS/bulletins/3e29e39)).

## The 2025–2026 upheaval

### A 28% smaller workforce

The agency shrank from about 103,000 employees in January 2025 to about 77,000 by May, per the Treasury Inspector General ([AP](https://jcpost.com/posts/192e329a-a90d-41f1-9d6c-129bc946c033)). Brookings put the full-year cut at 31,000 employees (about 28%): 20,000+ left through deferred-resignation programs and thousands of probationary employees were terminated ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-trump-administrations-irs-cuts-undermined-filing-season-performance/)). The Taxpayer Services Division had lost about 11,000 staff (26%) by January 2026, overtime hours rose 12% in the first nine months of 2025, average paper-return processing climbed from 27 to 36 days, and phone "Level of Service" fell from 70% to 56% while average wait times rose from 8 to 14 minutes ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-trump-administrations-irs-cuts-undermined-filing-season-performance/)). To fill gaps, the agency involuntarily reassigned roughly 1,200 IT and HR employees to return processing — many had no prior taxpayer-services experience, and hundreds failed their initial certification exams ([Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-trump-administrations-irs-cuts-undermined-filing-season-performance/)).

### Seven leaders in one year

Seven commissioner or acting-commissioner transitions occurred in 2025 alone: Danny Werfel departed in January, followed by acting stints from Douglas O'Donnell, Melanie Krause, Gary Shapley, and Michael Faulkender ([Senate Finance](https://www.finance.senate.gov/ranking-members-news/wyden-schumer-warren-warn-that-bessents-term-as-acting-irs-commissioner-has-expired-sound-alarm-over-leadership-vacuum-amid-chaos-at-irs); [IRS.gov](http://www.irs.gov/ru/about-irs/previous-irs-commissioners)). Krause resigned over an IRS–DHS deal to share immigrants' tax data with ICE, and Shapley's appointment triggered a fight between Elon Musk and Bessent ([AP](https://jcpost.com/posts/192e329a-a90d-41f1-9d6c-129bc946c033)). Billy Long — a former Missouri congressman and auctioneer confirmed 53–44 — served June 17 to August 8, becoming the shortest-tenured Senate-confirmed commissioner since 1862 ([AP](https://jcpost.com/posts/192e329a-a90d-41f1-9d6c-129bc946c033)). Bessent has served as acting commissioner since, while the administration created a new "chief executive officer" role for Frank Bisignano — three Senate Democrats call it an unauthorized "fake job" ([Senate Finance](https://www.finance.senate.gov/ranking-members-news/wyden-schumer-warren-warn-that-bessents-term-as-acting-irs-commissioner-has-expired-sound-alarm-over-leadership-vacuum-amid-chaos-at-irs)). They argue Bessent's acting authority expired March 6, 2026, under the Federal Vacancies Reform Act, with no nominee submitted ([Senate Finance](https://www.finance.senate.gov/ranking-members-news/wyden-schumer-warren-warn-that-bessents-term-as-acting-irs-commissioner-has-expired-sound-alarm-over-leadership-vacuum-amid-chaos-at-irs)).

### The end of Direct File

Direct File — the IRS's free online filing service — was piloted in 2024 with Inflation Reduction Act funding, made permanent under the Biden administration, and expanded for the 2025 season ([AP](https://www.thebanner.com/politics-power/national-politics/irs-direct-file-MXJEZ6NOQRDZNLLBRIB2CO3Y7Y/)). It drew 296,531 accepted returns in 2025, up from 140,803 in 2024, before the Trump administration put the program in limbo amid its federal-government cuts ([AP](https://www.thebanner.com/politics-power/national-politics/irs-direct-file-MXJEZ6NOQRDZNLLBRIB2CO3Y7Y/)). The Associated Press reported in April 2025 that the administration planned to eliminate it after IRS staff assigned to Direct File were told to stop development work ([AP](https://www.thebanner.com/politics-power/national-politics/irs-direct-file-MXJEZ6NOQRDZNLLBRIB2CO3Y7Y/)). In November 2025, IRS product manager Cynthia Noe emailed state comptrollers that "IRS Direct File will not be available in Filing Season 2026. No launch date has been set for the future" ([AP](https://www.thebanner.com/politics-power/national-politics/irs-direct-file-MXJEZ6NOQRDZNLLBRIB2CO3Y7Y/)). Bessent told reporters that "better alternatives" exist and "the private sector can do a better job" ([AP](https://www.thebanner.com/politics-power/national-politics/irs-direct-file-MXJEZ6NOQRDZNLLBRIB2CO3Y7Y/)).

## What happens to a tax-exempt complaint after it's filed

Anyone — an employee, a donor, a member of the public, even a competitor — can file a referral against a tax-exempt organization using IRS Form 13909, the Tax-Exempt Organization Complaint (Referral) form ([FedLaws](https://fedlaws.org/how-to-report-a-nonprofit-form-13909-evidence-and-state-filings/)). It goes to the IRS's EO Classification office in Dallas — by mail to 1100 Commerce Street, by fax to 214-413-5415, or by email to eoclass@irs.gov — and may be filed anonymously ([FedLaws](https://fedlaws.org/how-to-report-a-nonprofit-form-13909-evidence-and-state-filings/)). Named filers receive an acknowledgment letter, but that is the last they will hear: Section 6103 of the Internal Revenue Code bars the IRS from disclosing whether it examined the referral or what it found ([UncleFed](https://www.unclefed.com/Tax-News/2008/nrfs08-13.html)). An experienced EO revenue agent reviews each referral under a "reasonable belief" standard; referrals that meet it can go to a committee of career EO managers and agents that decides monthly whether to open an examination ([UncleFed](https://www.unclefed.com/Tax-News/2008/nrfs08-13.html)). The IRS's own complaints page confirms this Dallas route, handled by its Tax-Exempt/Government Entities Division ([IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/public-disclosure-and-availability-of-exempt-organizations-returns-complaints-about-noncompliance)). That confidentiality rule is why, when [Accountable.US](/organizations/accountable-us/) filed such a complaint on June 13, 2024, against the Epoch Public Foundation and The Epoch Times Association to trigger a civil investigation of their 2021 and 2022 returns, no public record of the IRS's response exists ([NPR](https://www.ksut.org/2024-06-15/money-laundering-charges-raise-questions-about-the-direction-of-the-epoch-times)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When and why was the IRS founded?**
Congress created the Office of Commissioner of Internal Revenue on July 1, 1862, and the Bureau of Internal Revenue to collect taxes financing the Civil War, including the first progressive income tax ([IRS.gov](http://www.irs.gov/ru/about-irs/previous-irs-commissioners)). The income tax was abolished after ten years, reinstated in 1913, and the agency was renamed in 1953 ([National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/chicago/finding-aids/irs)).

**Who leads the IRS right now?**
No one Senate-confirmed: Billy Long was removed on August 8, 2025, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has served as acting commissioner since ([AP](https://jcpost.com/posts/192e329a-a90d-41f1-9d6c-129bc946c033)). Senate Democrats say Bessent's acting authority expired March 6, 2026, under the Federal Vacancies Reform Act, and call the newly created "CEO" role for Frank Bisignano a "fake job" ([Senate Finance](https://www.finance.senate.gov/ranking-members-news/wyden-schumer-warren-warn-that-bessents-term-as-acting-irs-commissioner-has-expired-sound-alarm-over-leadership-vacuum-amid-chaos-at-irs)).

**How much money does the IRS collect?**
In fiscal year 2024 the IRS collected $5.1 trillion in gross taxes, accounting for about 96% of all federal government funding ([IRS](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USIRS/bulletins/3e29e39)). It processed more than 266 million returns and other forms and issued close to $553 billion in refunds that year ([IRS](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USIRS/bulletins/3e29e39)).

**How do I report a nonprofit's suspected tax violations to the IRS?**
File IRS Form 13909, the Tax-Exempt Organization Complaint (Referral) form, with the IRS EO Classification office in Dallas — by mail, fax (214-413-5415), or email (eoclass@irs.gov); anonymous filings are accepted ([FedLaws](https://fedlaws.org/how-to-report-a-nonprofit-form-13909-evidence-and-state-filings/)). Named filers get an acknowledgment letter, but confidentiality law bars the IRS from telling you the outcome ([UncleFed](https://www.unclefed.com/Tax-News/2008/nrfs08-13.html)).
