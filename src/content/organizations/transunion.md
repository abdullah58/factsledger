## Overview

TransUnion is one of the three nationwide consumer reporting agencies in the [United States](/places/united-states/) — the companies whose files decide whether Americans get a mortgage, a car loan or a credit card — and it is the smallest of the three by revenue, with $4,183.8 million in 2024 ([TransUnion](https://investors.transunion.com/~/media/Files/T/Transunion-IR-V2/annual-reports/2024/2024-annual-report.pdf)). It began in 1968 as the holding company of a railway tank-car leasing business, the Union Tank Car Company, and it is still run from Chicago, where it employs more than 13,000 associates across more than 30 countries ([TransUnion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1552033/000155203324000072/exhibit99109302024.htm); [1library.net](https://1library.net/article/late-s-onwards-consumer-history-of-credit-bureaux.zx83kpwq)). Its recent history is a two-part story: a roughly $4.5 billion push into identity data, and a repeat-offender enforcement record that culminated in its complaint relief rate roughly halving in late 2025 ([TransUnion](https://newsroom.transunion.com/transunion-accelerates-growth-of-identity-based-solutions--with-agreement-to-acquire-neustar-for-31-billion/); [ProPublica](https://qoshe.com/propublica/joel-jacobs/credit-bureaus-are-leaving-more-mistakes-on-frustrated-consumers-reports-under-trump-s-cfpb/188133152)).

TransUnion's files now carry rent as well as loans: [Bilt](/organizations/bilt/) reports members' on-time rent payments to TransUnion, [Experian](/organizations/experian/) and [Equifax](/organizations/equifax/) free of charge, and a TransUnion study cited by Bilt found scores rose by nearly 60 points on average when rent payments were included in a credit file ([Bilt](https://newsroom.biltrewards.com/free-rent-reporting-to-renters)). The company describes its mission as "Information for Good" — making trust possible by ensuring each person is reliably represented in the marketplace ([TransUnion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1552033/000155203324000072/exhibit99109302024.htm)). Growth re-accelerated to 9% in 2024 after a subdued period for many of its end-markets. The company also runs a direct-to-consumer business: in early 2025 it announced a free US credit education and monitoring offer, including a daily TransUnion credit score and report, aimed at the tens of millions of consumers who visit its digital properties each year. And its Global Disputes Platform, launched in India in 2024, is the system meant to handle the complaints whose outcomes ProPublica tracked ([TransUnion](https://investors.transunion.com/~/media/Files/T/Transunion-IR-V2/annual-reports/2024/2024-annual-report.pdf)).

## From tank cars to credit files

TransUnion's origin is the strangest of the three bureaus: it was founded in 1968 as the holding company of the Union Tank Car Company, a railway equipment leasing business whose genealogy runs back to the Star Tank Line of 1866 and Standard Oil's purchase of it in 1873 ([1library.net](https://1library.net/article/late-s-onwards-consumer-history-of-credit-bureaux.zx83kpwq)). The company sensed broader opportunities in credit reporting and, in 1969, took over the Credit Bureau of Cook County, which held 3.6 million card files in 400 seven-drawer cupboards ([1library.net](https://1library.net/article/late-s-onwards-consumer-history-of-credit-bureaux.zx83kpwq)). Its own 10-K describes the founding as "a provider of regional credit reporting services", and by the late 1970s TransUnion and Equifax had emerged as the industry's leaders, before the Fair Credit Reporting Act of 1970 set the first ground rules for a business that had previously been largely unregulated ([TransUnion](https://investors.transunion.com/~/media/Files/T/Transunion-IR-V2/annual-reports/2024/2024-annual-report.pdf); [1library.net](https://1library.net/article/late-s-onwards-consumer-history-of-credit-bureaux.zx83kpwq)). The Marmon Group bought the tank-car business in 1981, and TransUnion later expanded abroad, buying South Africa's Information Trust Corporation in 1993 ([1library.net](https://1library.net/article/late-s-onwards-consumer-history-of-credit-bureaux.zx83kpwq)).

## The identity pivot

Under Christopher A. Cartwright, president and chief executive since May 2019, TransUnion has spent roughly $4.5 billion turning a credit bureau into an identity company ([TransUnion](https://investors.transunion.com/~/media/Files/T/Transunion-IR-V2/annual-reports/2024/2024-annual-report.pdf)). It completed the roughly £1 billion purchase of Callcredit, the UK's second-largest consumer credit bureau, in June 2018, then agreed in September 2021 to buy Neustar for $3.1 billion in cash, gaining the OneID identity-resolution platform and building on earlier identity assets — the TLO data assets, the iovation device reputation network and Tru Optik ([TransUnion](https://newsroom.transunion.com/transunion-completes-acquisition-of-callcredit/); [TransUnion](https://newsroom.transunion.com/transunion-accelerates-growth-of-identity-based-solutions--with-agreement-to-acquire-neustar-for-31-billion/)). The transformation continued inside the company: a multi-year programme to modernise its technology on a single cloud platform, OneTru, and to move work to global capability centres is expected to deliver roughly $200 million of annual free cash flow savings ([TransUnion](https://investors.transunion.com/~/media/Files/T/Transunion-IR-V2/annual-reports/2024/2024-annual-report.pdf)). In January 2025 it agreed to acquire the largest consumer credit bureau in Mexico, extending the same playbook south ([TransUnion](https://investors.transunion.com/~/media/Files/T/Transunion-IR-V2/annual-reports/2024/2024-annual-report.pdf)).

## A repeat offender's record

In January 2017 the [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](/organizations/consumer-financial-protection-bureau/) ordered it to pay more than $13.9 million in restitution and a $3 million penalty for deceiving consumers about the credit scores it sold: the scores were based on VantageScore and were not typically the scores lenders used, yet TransUnion marketed them with "free" or "$1" offers that rolled into subscriptions usually costing $16 or more a month, without clearly disclosing the negative-option billing ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-orders-transunion-and-equifax-pay-deceiving-consumers-marketing-credit-scores-and-credit-products/)). The order required truthful marketing, express informed consent and easy cancellation — and the Bureau later sued alleging violations of it ([ProPublica](https://qoshe.com/propublica/joel-jacobs/credit-bureaus-are-leaving-more-mistakes-on-frustrated-consumers-reports-under-trump-s-cfpb/188133152)). Then the relief data turned: TransUnion's rate of resolving complaints in consumers' favour held steady for years, then began plunging in the summer of 2025 and by October was providing relief roughly half as often ([ProPublica](https://qoshe.com/propublica/joel-jacobs/credit-bureaus-are-leaving-more-mistakes-on-frustrated-consumers-reports-under-trump-s-cfpb/188133152)) — a halving that means a consumer disputing an error in late 2025 was about half as likely to get it fixed as a year earlier, as the regulator that policed it was being wound down.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is TransUnion?** TransUnion is one of the three nationwide consumer reporting agencies in the United States, alongside Experian and Equifax, and a global information and insights company based in Chicago ([TransUnion](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1552033/000155203324000072/exhibit99109302024.htm)). Lenders use its files and scores to judge creditworthiness, and it also sells identity, fraud and marketing data. Its shares trade on the New York Stock Exchange as TRU.

**How did TransUnion start?** It was founded in 1968 as the holding company of the Union Tank Car Company, a railway tank-car leasing business, and moved into credit reporting in 1969 by taking over the Credit Bureau of Cook County ([1library.net](https://1library.net/article/late-s-onwards-consumer-history-of-credit-bureaux.zx83kpwq)). Its own 10-K calls the founding "a provider of regional credit reporting services". The Marmon Group bought the tank-car business in 1981.

**Why did the CFPB fine TransUnion in 2017?** The Bureau found TransUnion deceived consumers about the credit scores it sold — VantageScore-based scores lenders did not typically use — and lured them into subscriptions usually costing $16 or more a month ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-orders-transunion-and-equifax-pay-deceiving-consumers-marketing-credit-scores-and-credit-products/)). It paid more than $13.9 million in restitution plus a $3 million penalty.

**What is Neustar, and why did TransUnion buy it?** Neustar is an identity-resolution company TransUnion agreed to buy for $3.1 billion in cash in September 2021, gaining its OneID platform ([TransUnion](https://newsroom.transunion.com/transunion-accelerates-growth-of-identity-based-solutions--with-agreement-to-acquire-neustar-for-31-billion/)). Neustar, based in Reston, Virginia, was expected to add about $575 million of revenue and $115 million of Adjusted EBITDA in 2021. The deal was the centrepiece of TransUnion's shift from credit bureau to identity company.

**Can rent payments appear on my TransUnion report?** Yes. Bilt reports members' on-time rent payments to TransUnion, Experian and Equifax free of charge, and a TransUnion study cited by Bilt found scores rose by nearly 60 points on average when rent was included ([Bilt](https://newsroom.biltrewards.com/free-rent-reporting-to-renters)). Only on-time payments are reported.
