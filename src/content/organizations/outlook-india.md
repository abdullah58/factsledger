## Overview

Outlook is a weekly news magazine published in [India](/places/india/), launched on 11 October 1995 with Vinod Mehta as its founding editor-in-chief, and still publishing from New Delhi thirty years later ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/story/13149_Outlook-the-journey-so-far)). It entered the market as the challenger to *India Today*, then the undisputed leader among news magazines with a circulation of 4 lakh, and built its reputation on independent reporting — a stance that, in 2016, brought a criminal complaint against its publisher, editor and a writer ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/story/13149_Outlook-the-journey-so-far); [NWMI](https://nwmindia.org/initiatives/statements/quash-fir-against-outlook/)).

The first issue hit the news stands on 11 October 1995 with a circulation of 50,000 copies, and its very first cover story brought the Kashmir dispute to the fore, evoking strong reactions from various corners of the country ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/story/13149_Outlook-the-journey-so-far)). Mehta, who had previously launched *The Sunday Observer*, *The Indian Post*, *The Independent* and the Delhi edition of *The Pioneer*, was the magazine's founding editor-in-chief and remained in the post for 17 years ([BestMediaInfo](https://bestmediainfo.com/2012/02/vinod-mehta-calls-it-a-day-at-outlook)). The magazine is owned by the Raheja Group ([Telugu360](https://www.telugu360.com/outlook-sacks-its-editor-in-chief-krishna-prasad-and-appointed-former-repoter-rajesh/)).

## The launch that broke a monopoly

When Outlook launched, *India Today* was, in the trade press's words, "a formidable force to reckon with and the undisputed leader among news magazines", and its rivals *The Week* and *Frontline* were strong players in their own markets ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/story/13149_Outlook-the-journey-so-far)). Outlook's opening was a weekly where the incumbent was a fortnightly rhythm in practice: as one media buyer put it at the time, "for a news magazine, the need was to deliver faster news and analysis. Fortnightly magazines failed to fulfill this requirement of readers. Outlook recognised this and launched at a time when the market was just waiting for a weekly magazine" ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/story/13149_Outlook-the-journey-so-far)). The publisher, Maheshwar Peri, put the strategy bluntly: "We have never hesitated in calling a spade a spade" ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/story/13149_Outlook-the-journey-so-far)).

The numbers tell the growth story. ABC figures for July–December 2000 put *India Today* at 428,546 and Outlook at 230,146 — still half its rival's size — but Mehta insisted the objective was not to overtake *India Today* in circulation, arguing that in the big cities the two ran "neck in neck" and that Outlook's readership was younger and more educated ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/interviews/155_our-objective-is-not-to-overtake-india-today-in-circulation)). By its tenth anniversary the magazine's circulation was around 3 lakh, and the cover price had risen from Rs 10 to Rs 15 in April 2002 without, the group said, denting circulation ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/story/13149_Outlook-the-journey-so-far)). The group expanded into a Hindi edition, *Outlook Saptahik* — written in Hindi for Hindi readers rather than translated, with a circulation of 1.20 lakh concentrated in Uttar Pradesh and Bihar — plus *Outlook Money* (earlier *Intelligent Investor*), *Outlook Traveller*, a city magazine called *City Limits*, and an Indian edition of *Marie Claire* announced for 2006 ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/story/13149_Outlook-the-journey-so-far)). Mehta described the group's three core areas as news and current affairs, travel, and investment and business, and claimed *Intelligent Investor* outsold both *Business India* and *Business Today* ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/interviews/155_our-objective-is-not-to-overtake-india-today-in-circulation)).

## Editors: Mehta, Prasad, Ramachandran, Sinha

Mehta retired in February 2012 after 17 years as editor-in-chief, continuing with the group as Advisor, and Krishna Prasad — editor since October 2008, when he took charge on 15 September after the post had lain vacant for two years following Sandipan Deb's departure — replaced him as editor-in-chief of the Outlook Group ([BestMediaInfo](https://bestmediainfo.com/2012/02/vinod-mehta-calls-it-a-day-at-outlook); [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/marketing/22173_Krishna-Prasad-appointed-editor-of-Outlook-magazine)). Prasad had been a senior editor and in a consulting position since Outlook's launch in 1995, left for *The Times of India* in 2006 to edit the broadsheet *Vijay Times*, and returned to Outlook in 2008 ([afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/news/marketing/22173_Krishna-Prasad-appointed-editor-of-Outlook-magazine); [Telugu360](https://www.telugu360.com/outlook-sacks-its-editor-in-chief-krishna-prasad-and-appointed-former-repoter-rajesh/)).

Prasad's tenure ended abruptly in August 2016, days after the magazine published its trafficking investigation (see below): the group's executive director and publisher, Indranil Roy, told staff by email that Prasad had been removed and that former Outlook reporter Rajesh Ramachandran — then with the *Economic Times* — would take charge on 16 August as the magazine's third editor-in-chief ([Telugu360](https://www.telugu360.com/outlook-sacks-its-editor-in-chief-krishna-prasad-and-appointed-former-repoter-rajesh/)). A decade later the magazine reached its 30th anniversary under editor Chinki Sinha, who had joined in 2021 and presided over a run of heavyweight long reads featuring writers such as Perumal Murugan, Pico Iyer, Amitava Kumar, Janice Pariat and Sudarshan Shetty ([Indiantelevision.com](https://www.indiantelevision.com/iworld/enews/aasheesh-sharma-turns-the-page-at-outlook%3B-joins-as-digital-editor-260104?amp)). In January 2026 Aasheesh Sharma joined as digital editor in time for the 30th anniversary double edition ([Indiantelevision.com](https://www.indiantelevision.com/iworld/enews/aasheesh-sharma-turns-the-page-at-outlook%3B-joins-as-digital-editor-260104?amp)), and in May 2026 Sinha resigned, writing that "editors come and go, but words, photos, memories, friendships, and the spirit of togetherness remain" — and noting that much of her team, including the managing editor, foreign affairs editor, entertainment and culture editor and social media lead, had also left ([exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/people-movement-news/editors-come-and-go-but-words-photos-and-spirit-of-togetherness-remain-chinki-sinha-154513.html)).

## The 2016 investigation and its cost

The defining press-freedom episode in Outlook's history is the investigation "Operation #Beti Uthao", published on 25 July 2016 by writer Neha Dixit. It brought to the fore the plight of 31 tribal girls — all minors — who had been trafficked from Assam to Gujarat and Punjab and indoctrinated into Hindutva ideology far from home, while their families were unable to trace or contact their daughters ([NWMI](https://nwmindia.org/initiatives/statements/quash-fir-against-outlook/)). On Saturday, 6 August 2016, an FIR was registered at the Latasil Police Station in Guwahati on a complaint by BJP spokesperson and advocate Bijan Mahajan and the state Assistant Solicitor General of India, Subhash Chandra Kayal, against publisher Indranil Roy, editor Krishna Prasad and writer Neha Dixit, under Section 153A of the Indian Penal Code — a cognisable offence relating to the promotion of disharmony and enmity between different groups ([NWMI](https://nwmindia.org/initiatives/statements/quash-fir-against-outlook/)).

The Network of Women in Media, India, called the complaint an attempt to intimidate and silence the three, arguing that "instead of conducting an inquiry into the trafficking of young girls and acting against the culprits, these complaints and charges serve to harass, intimidate and, ultimately, censor and silence journalists", and demanded the case be quashed ([NWMI](https://nwmindia.org/initiatives/statements/quash-fir-against-outlook/)). The stat that anchors the story is the count itself — 31 minors, trafficked across three states — and the implication is the one the NWMI drew: the legal response targeted the messenger rather than the trafficking the magazine had exposed. Within ten days of the FIR, Prasad was out as editor-in-chief ([Telugu360](https://www.telugu360.com/outlook-sacks-its-editor-in-chief-krishna-prasad-and-appointed-former-repoter-rajesh/)).
