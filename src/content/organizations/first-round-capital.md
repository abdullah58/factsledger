## Overview

First Round Capital is a seed-stage venture capital firm founded in 2004 by Josh Kopelman and Howard Morgan that backs technology startups at their earliest stage, often writing a company's first formal round of funding ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/investor-spotlight-first-round-capital-an-early-uber-backer-that-lives-up-to-its-name)). Based in [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) with offices in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) and Philadelphia, it is best known as the first institutional investor in Uber, a bet that came to define the firm's seed-first identity ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/investor-spotlight-first-round-capital-an-early-uber-backer-that-lives-up-to-its-name)).

This profile covers the venture firm First Round Capital — not the funding round it is named after — and follows it from its founding through its fund history, its signature exits, and its seed investment in the trading-card platform [Alt](/organizations/alt/).

## The seed-first model

Kopelman founded First Round with Morgan "with a focus on helping founders start their companies," after a run as an entrepreneur himself ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/investor-spotlight-first-round-capital-an-early-uber-backer-that-lives-up-to-its-name)). In 1992 he had co-founded Infonautics, an information services company that went public four years later, then founded Half.com in 1999, a seller of used books, movies and music that was purchased by eBay, and helped create TurnTide, an anti-spam startup quickly acquired by Symantec ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/investor-spotlight-first-round-capital-an-early-uber-backer-that-lives-up-to-its-name)). Morgan retired from the firm in 2018 and became chairman at B Capital Group, the venture firm founded by Facebook co-founder Eduardo Saverin ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/investor-spotlight-first-round-capital-an-early-uber-backer-that-lives-up-to-its-name)).

The firm's name describes its investment philosophy: it backs startups at the earliest possible stages and helps build them into companies that can generate large returns ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/investor-spotlight-first-round-capital-an-early-uber-backer-that-lives-up-to-its-name)). PitchBook ranked it the sixth most active seed investor in the United States, with 180 seed-stage investments over the preceding decade-plus ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/investor-spotlight-first-round-capital-an-early-uber-backer-that-lives-up-to-its-name)). Its initial investments typically ranged from $500,000 to $750,000, and it reserved about 75% of its capital for follow-on fundings rather than spending everything on first checks ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/investor-spotlight-first-round-capital-an-early-uber-backer-that-lives-up-to-its-name)). Through a programme called Pitch Assist it helped portfolio companies raise more than $500 million in follow-on capital ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/investor-spotlight-first-round-capital-an-early-uber-backer-that-lives-up-to-its-name)).

The partnership behind that model included Rob Hayes, who joined in 2006 and led the firm's investment in Uber; Hayley Barna, the firm's first female partner and a Birchbox co-founder; Chris Fralic, who joined as a partner in 2006; Bill Trenchard, who became a partner in 2012; and Brett Berson, who started at the firm as a college intern and was named partner in 2016 ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/investor-spotlight-first-round-capital-an-early-uber-backer-that-lives-up-to-its-name)). The firm also seeded student investing directly, putting $2 million behind the first Dorm Room Fund, which invests in entrepreneurial college students ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/first-round-capital-targets-200m-for-seventh-flagship-fund/)). Geographically it was a US-centric investor: PitchBook data showed 392 investments in California, more than 58% of its US deals, with New York second at 171 deals and Pennsylvania fourth at 23 ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/investor-spotlight-first-round-capital-an-early-uber-backer-that-lives-up-to-its-name)).

## Funds, exits and Alt

First Round's fund sizes rose steadily as seed rounds themselves grew larger. Its third fund closed on $130 million in 2010 ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/investor-spotlight-first-round-capital-an-early-uber-backer-that-lives-up-to-its-name)). Fund 6 was announced in September 2016 at $175 million, and the Fund VII filing of October 2018 targeted $209,392,283 plus a $10,607,717 sidecar — about $220 million in total and the largest flagship fund the firm had raised to that point ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/first-round-capital-targets-200m-for-seventh-flagship-fund/)). Crunchbase News noted the firm was on a two-year tick-tock capital-raising rhythm, and that larger seed and early-stage rounds required larger checks even if the firm made no more investments per fund than before ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/first-round-capital-targets-200m-for-seventh-flagship-fund/)).

The Uber bet remains the firm's signature story. As the first venture backer of the ride-hailing company, First Round gave Uber its first office — the company worked out of a conference room at the firm until it outgrew the space ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/investor-spotlight-first-round-capital-an-early-uber-backer-that-lives-up-to-its-name)). PitchBook put Uber's first round of venture funding at $1.5 million at a $5.5 million valuation, while Crunchbase News separately reported that First Round led a $1.25 million angel round in Uber in 2010; the two figures are attributed separately here because the sources do not reconcile them ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/investor-spotlight-first-round-capital-an-early-uber-backer-that-lives-up-to-its-name); [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/first-round-capital-targets-200m-for-seventh-flagship-fund/)). Other early stakes from the same era included Boxed, Flexport, Warby Parker and Roblox, plus a Series A investment in car-insurance company Metromile and a $5 million early round in home-wifi company Eero ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/first-round-capital-targets-200m-for-seventh-flagship-fund/)). In 2018 the firm saw Ring acquired by Amazon for $1 billion and Birchbox picked up by Viking Global Investors for $15 million ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/first-round-capital-targets-200m-for-seventh-flagship-fund/)).

The seed-first habit continued into the collectibles boom: in the span of a few months in early 2021, Alt wrapped a seed round led by First Round and a Series A led by Seven Seven Six, raising more than $31 million combined for its trading-card marketplace ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/03/11/sports-trading-card-platform-alt-launches-with-31-million-in-funding-and-plenty-of-market-hype/)). Crunchbase News corroborated the shape of the deal, listing First Round among the Series A investors alongside SV Angel and BoxGroup ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/startups/alt-raises-31m-to-buy-and-sell-sports-cards/)).
