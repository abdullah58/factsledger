## Overview

Blue J Legal — known as Blue J — is a Toronto-based legal-tech company founded in 2015 by University of Toronto tax law professor Benjamin Alarie (the Osler Chair of Business Law) with fellow law professors Anthony Niblett and Albert Yoon ([University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/u-t-startup-blue-j-legal-raises-167-million-globe-and-mail)). It builds a generative-AI tax research platform that gives citation-backed answers to complex tax questions, serving more than 3,500 organizations including KPMG UK, several Fortune 500 companies and the Canada Revenue Agency ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/how-ai-tax-startup-blue-j-torched-its-entire-business-model-for-chatgpt-and)). The name should not be confused with BlueJ, an unrelated free Java development environment for beginners ([bluej.org](https://www.bluej.org/)).

## The bet that torched the business model

Blue J's first decade was built on supervised machine learning: predictive models that forecast judicial outcomes on specific tax issues ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/how-ai-tax-startup-blue-j-torched-its-entire-business-model-for-chatgpt-and)). It worked — until it didn't. Customers loved the tool when it applied to their problem and abandoned it when it didn't; revenue plateaued around $2 million a year with about 200 customers by 2021 ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/how-ai-tax-startup-blue-j-torched-its-entire-business-model-for-chatgpt-and)). In the winter of 2022, as ChatGPT mesmerized the tech world, Alarie made a high-stakes call: abandon nearly a decade of proprietary technology and rebuild the company on large language models ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/how-ai-tax-startup-blue-j-torched-its-entire-business-model-for-chatgpt-and)).

The August 2023 relaunch was, in Alarie's own word, "super janky" — 90-second response times, half the answers flawed, an NPS of 20 ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/how-ai-tax-startup-blue-j-torched-its-entire-business-model-for-chatgpt-and)). Relentless iteration changed that: proprietary content licensing (Tax Analysts' Tax Notes, IBFD covering 220+ jurisdictions), tax experts led by former IRS Chief Counsel branch chief Susan Massey, and an early-access partnership with OpenAI that trades real-world feedback for cutting-edge models ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/how-ai-tax-startup-blue-j-torched-its-entire-business-model-for-chatgpt-and)). By 2025 the platform was processing over 3 million tax queries, response times were measured in seconds, NPS sat in the mid-80s, and revenue had multiplied roughly twelve-fold ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/how-ai-tax-startup-blue-j-torched-its-entire-business-model-for-chatgpt-and)).

## The $122 million bet on vertical AI

In August 2025 Blue J raised US$122 million (CAD$167 million) in a Series D led by Sapphire Ventures and Oak HC/FT, valuing the company above $300 million, per the Globe and Mail's reporting on Canadian securities filings ([University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/u-t-startup-blue-j-legal-raises-167-million-globe-and-mail)). Total capital raised passed $133 million, and the company was signing 10 to 15 new customers daily while retaining revenue at best-in-class levels — 99%+ gross retention, 130% net ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/how-ai-tax-startup-blue-j-torched-its-entire-business-model-for-chatgpt-and)). VentureBeat framed the raise as a market consensus: Blue J was the breakout winner in AI tax research ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/how-ai-tax-startup-blue-j-torched-its-entire-business-model-for-chatgpt-and)).
