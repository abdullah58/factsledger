## Overview

Grupo Sura — formally Grupo de Inversiones Suramericana S.A. — is the Medellín-based financial holding that grew out of an insurer founded on 12 December 1944 by 36 companies and 151 investors, and became the center of one of Latin America's most distinctive corporate structures: the cross-shareholding fortress of the Grupo Empresarial Antioqueño (GEA) ([El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/negocios/sura-cumple-80-anos-cual-es-su-historia-PA26044218)). Today it is a financial-services holding built on insurer Suramericana and pensions-and-asset manager SURA Asset Management — and, since 26 July 2025, a pure financial player: the historic *desenroque* ended some 46 years of cross-shareholdings with Grupo Argos, distributing over COP 10.8 billones in Sura shares directly to Argos shareholders ([Valora Analitik](https://www.valoraanalitik.com/un-ano-despues-del-desenroque-los-accionistas-de-grupo-argos-han-visto-crecer-hasta-56-el-valor-de-su-patrimonio-esto-opina-el-mercado/?amp); [Semana](https://www.semana.com/economia/empresas/articulo/superintendencia-financiera-autorizo-la-transaccion-con-la-que-accionistas-de-grupo-argos-recibiran-108-billones-de-pesos-en-acciones-del-grupo-sura/202544/)).

## From insurer to the "enroque": 1944–1997

The company was born to solve a local problem: Medellín's young industries needed someone to insure their assets. Engineer Carlos Gutiérrez and lawyer Bernardo Echeverri drew up the statutes in late 1944; Gutiérrez became the first president, and the first policy went to steelmaker Talleres Robledo while the first claim was paid to Textiles Coltejer ([El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/negocios/sura-cumple-80-anos-cual-es-su-historia-PA26044218)). Within a year Suramericana listed on the stock market and opened branches in Bogotá and Barranquilla; by 1947 it had a life-insurance subsidiary, by 1958 a capitalization company, and by 1960 a thousand employees ([El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/negocios/sura-cumple-80-anos-cual-es-su-historia-PA26044218)). Along the way it seeded institutions that outgrew it — the Banco Industrial Colombiano that became Bancolombia, and the Corporación Financiera Industrial Agrícola that became Corporación Financiera Suramericana — and created what is now Fundación Sura ([El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/negocios/sura-cumple-80-anos-cual-es-su-historia-PA26044218)).

Two 1970s legacies still define the company's identity. The first was commercial: the "Tigre de Suramericana," born in 1972 when a popular song — Eugenio García Cueto's "Que me coma el tigre" — inspired a sales-incentive campaign whose black-and-white tiger, drawn with simple strokes, taught accident prevention with humor, becoming one of Colombian advertising's great icons ([El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/negocios/sura-cumple-80-anos-cual-es-su-historia-PA26044218)). The second was structural: after hostile takeover attempts against listed Antioquian companies, Suramericana joined a defensive movement in which regional firms bought stakes in one another — the *enroque accionario* — that created the Grupo Empresarial Antioqueño, a web of more than 100 companies with significant cross-shareholdings, anchored by Suramericana, Argos, and Nutresa ([El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/negocios/sura-cumple-80-anos-cual-es-su-historia-PA26044218); [Reuters](https://d2233.cms.socastsrm.com/2022/01/06/colombias-grupo-argos-will-not-participate-in-grupo-sura-purchase-offer/)). In 1997 the structure formalized: a spin-off separated the insurance operation from the investment portfolio, creating Suramericana de Inversiones — today's Grupo Sura — as the holding company ([El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/negocios/sura-cumple-80-anos-cual-es-su-historia-PA26044218)).

The 21st century brought the regional expansion. In 2011 Grupo Sura created SURA Asset Management to run the Latin American insurance, pension, and investment assets acquired from Dutch group ING — described by El Colombiano as the largest overseas transaction by a Colombian company at the time — and in 2015 its Suramericana subsidiary bought RSA Insurance Group's Latin American operation, building an insurance platform across nine countries ([El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/negocios/sura-cumple-80-anos-cual-es-su-historia-PA26044218)). By its 80th anniversary the portfolio combined SURA Asset Management (pensions, savings, investment, and asset management across Chile, Colombia, Mexico, Peru, and others), Suramericana insurance operations in Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Mexico, and Panama, plus 46.2% of Bancolombia and 34% of Grupo Argos ([El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/negocios/sura-cumple-80-anos-cual-es-su-historia-PA26044218)).

## The Gilinski siege of 2021–2022

The cross-shareholding fortress that had protected the GEA for decades became its vulnerability when markets priced the complexity at a discount. In November 2021, Grupo Gilinski — via Nugil SAS — launched a $1.78–$2.22 billion bid for 50.1% to 62.625% of food company Nutresa at $7.71 per share; weeks later it moved on Sura itself, offering $952 million to $1.19 billion at $8.01 per share for 25.34% to 31.68% of Colombia's largest holding company, prompting the regulator to suspend trading in Sura shares ([Reuters](https://mnacritique.mergersindia.com/news/grupo-gilinski-launches-public-offer-for-minority-stake-in-grupo-sura/)). Sura — made up of insurer Suramericana and pension manager Sura Asset Management, with 20%-to-50% stakes in Bancolombia, Argos, and Nutresa — was, in Reuters' words, "the jewel in the GEA's crown" ([Reuters](https://mnacritique.mergersindia.com/news/grupo-gilinski-launches-public-offer-for-minority-stake-in-grupo-sura/); [Reuters](https://d2233.cms.socastsrm.com/2022/01/06/colombias-grupo-argos-will-not-participate-in-grupo-sura-purchase-offer/)).

The entanglements were total: Sura owned 35.7% of Nutresa while Nutresa held about 13% of Sura, per Refinitiv data cited by Reuters ([Reuters](https://mnacritique.mergersindia.com/news/grupo-gilinski-launches-public-offer-for-minority-stake-in-grupo-sura/)). In January 2022 Grupo Argos refused to take part in the Sura bid, calling the $8.01 price "considerably inferior" to the company's fundamental value and citing a JP Morgan study; it pledged measures to close the gap between fundamental value and market price, including partnerships or international listings ([Reuters](https://d2233.cms.socastsrm.com/2022/01/06/colombias-grupo-argos-will-not-participate-in-grupo-sura-purchase-offer/)). Reuters noted the irony plainly: the cross-ownership among the GEA's hundred-plus companies had "until recently, helped protect the conglomerate's assets from hostile takeovers" — and the market had found the way in ([Reuters](https://d2233.cms.socastsrm.com/2022/01/06/colombias-grupo-argos-will-not-participate-in-grupo-sura-purchase-offer/)).

## The desenroque: unwinding 46 years

The endgame began at the end of 2024, when Sura and Argos signed a spin-off-by-absorption agreement, approved by their shareholder assemblies in March 2025 and cleared by Colombia's Superintendencia Financiera in June ([El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/economia/empresas/asi-sera-el-intercambio-accionario-definitivo-entre-los-grupos-argos-y-sura-que-le-pondra-fin-al-proceso-de-escision-entre-las-dos-organizaciones-3474492); [Semana](https://www.semana.com/economia/empresas/articulo/superintendencia-financiera-autorizo-la-transaccion-con-la-que-accionistas-de-grupo-argos-recibiran-108-billones-de-pesos-en-acciones-del-grupo-sura/202544/)). On 26 July 2025 the operation materialized: Argos shareholders received 0.2359 Sura shares per Argos share (shares worth COP 10.8 billones in total), while Sura shareholders received 0.7234 Argos shares per Sura share — and the two groups ceased being shareholders of each other, eliminating the cross-holding and simplifying the control structure ([El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/economia/empresas/asi-sera-el-intercambio-accionario-definitivo-entre-los-grupos-argos-y-sura-que-le-pondra-fin-al-proceso-de-escision-entre-las-dos-organizaciones-3474492); [Valora Analitik](https://www.valoraanalitik.com/un-ano-despues-del-desenroque-los-accionistas-de-grupo-argos-han-visto-crecer-hasta-56-el-valor-de-su-patrimonio-esto-opina-el-mercado/?amp)). Cementos Argos became a direct subsidiary of Grupo Argos, now focused on infrastructure and construction materials, while Grupo Sura concentrated on financial and insurance services ([El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/economia/empresas/asi-sera-el-intercambio-accionario-definitivo-entre-los-grupos-argos-y-sura-que-le-pondra-fin-al-proceso-de-escision-entre-las-dos-organizaciones-3474492)).

A year later, the market's verdict was measurable: counting the Sura shares received plus dividends and the increased economic rights in Argos (over 20%), an original Argos shareholder's combined wealth had risen about 46% for ordinary shares and 56% for preferred — before dividends — even though Argos's standalone share price had fallen ([Valora Analitik](https://www.valoraanalitik.com/un-ano-despues-del-desenroque-los-accionistas-de-grupo-argos-han-visto-crecer-hasta-56-el-valor-de-su-patrimonio-esto-opina-el-mercado/?amp)). The 2026 dividends told the same story: Argos approved $750 per share and Sura $2,000, a combined increase of nearly 78% over what the Argos shareholder previously received, with Sura targeting $3,000 per share by 2028 ([Valora Analitik](https://www.valoraanalitik.com/un-ano-despues-del-desenroque-los-accionistas-de-grupo-argos-han-visto-crecer-hasta-56-el-valor-de-su-patrimonio-esto-opina-el-mercado/?amp)). Analysts credited the deal with unlocking value hidden inside the cross-holdings — "it ended up freeing value for shareholders," as Davivienda Corredores' Katherine Ortiz put it — while noting that Argos's next phase, its ACE 1.0 strategy, must now close the remaining gap between market price and fundamental value on its own ([Valora Analitik](https://www.valoraanalitik.com/un-ano-despues-del-desenroque-los-accionistas-de-grupo-argos-han-visto-crecer-hasta-56-el-valor-de-su-patrimonio-esto-opina-el-mercado/?amp)).

## What this profile does not claim

The founding anecdotes (the 1972 tiger campaign, the first policy and claim) are reported by El Colombiano partly as the company's own account; structural facts are the newspaper's journalism, but this profile does not vouch for marketing-colored details. The claim that the 2011 ING deal was the largest overseas transaction by a Colombian company comes from El Colombiano and was not independently ranked here. Later 2022 Gilinski offers, beyond the January 2022 Argos refusal verified from Reuters, are not asserted. The 46.2% Bancolombia and 34% Argos stakes are December-2024 figures and do not reflect the post-desenroque structure. Client and employee totals appear only in company-issued anniversary material and are omitted. This profile deliberately does not repeat the company's own characterizations of its corporate-governance record.
