## Overview

The Chicago Sun-Times is [Chicago](/places/chicago/)'s daily tabloid newspaper, created by the February 1948 merger of the Chicago Sun and the Chicago Daily Times. Owned since January 2022 by the nonprofit Chicago Public Media, owner of public radio station WBEZ, its journalists have won eight [Pulitzer Prizes](/events/pulitzer-prize/) ([The Pulitzer Prizes](https://pulitzer.org/cms/sites/default/files/content/the_pulitzer_prizes_2020_winners_and_finalistsupdatedversion.pdf)), and its ownership has changed hands repeatedly — from the Marshall Field family to Rupert Murdoch, Conrad Black's Hollinger, bankruptcy, and a $1 sale in 2017 ([Associated Press](https://tvnewscheck.com/journalism/article/public-radio-owner-buys-sun-times-in-big-chicago-media-deal/); [MediaShift](https://mediashift.org/2017/07/will-way-sun-times-won/)).

The modern paper's lineage is claimed back to 1844: the Encyclopedia of Chicago calls it "in a sense the city's oldest daily", continuing the Chicago Evening Journal, published from 1844 until 1929, when it was re-launched as the Daily Illustrated Times ([Chicago History Museum](https://libguides.chicagohistory.org/c.php?g=1431164&p=10672595)). It is the city's tabloid rival to the Chicago Tribune, built on street-level reporting, photography, sports and investigations — and since 2022 it has been the test case for whether a legacy metro daily can survive as a nonprofit newsroom ([Associated Press](https://tvnewscheck.com/journalism/article/public-radio-owner-buys-sun-times-in-big-chicago-media-deal/)).

## From the Field family to the $1 sale

Marshall Field III founded the Chicago Sun in 1941 as a morning paper, its first issue appearing on 4 December, and bought the Chicago Daily Times in 1947; the two merged in February 1948, when the Sun's circulation was 340,487 and the Times' 471,137 ([Chicagology](http://chicagology.com/newspapers/suntimes/)). Field Enterprises, the family company, owned the paper until 1984 and also owned WFLD channel 32 from its launch in 1966 ([Chicagology](http://chicagology.com/newspapers/suntimes/)). In 1984 the family sold to Rupert Murdoch's News Corporation for about $100 million; Murdoch sold it on in 1986 to publisher Robert Page and other investors for $145 million ([MediaShift](https://mediashift.org/2017/07/will-way-sun-times-won/)). In March 1994 American Publishing, part of Conrad Black's Hollinger group, bought it for approximately $180 million, when its daily circulation of 535,000 made it Chicago's number two newspaper and the ninth-largest metropolitan daily in the United States ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/hollinger-international-inc-0)).

## Eight Pulitzer Prizes

The Sun-Times' journalists have won eight Pulitzer Prizes, five of them in the 1970s ([The Pulitzer Prizes](https://pulitzer.org/cms/sites/default/files/content/the_pulitzer_prizes_2020_winners_and_finalistsupdatedversion.pdf)). The run opened with Thomas Fitzpatrick's general reporting in 1970 and Jack Dykinga's feature photography in 1971, followed by Ron Powers for criticism in 1973 and Art Petacque and Hugh Hough in 1974 for uncovering evidence that reopened the investigation into the 1966 murder of Valerie Percy ([The Pulitzer Prizes](https://pulitzer.org/cms/sites/default/files/content/the_pulitzer_prizes_2020_winners_and_finalistsupdatedversion.pdf)). Roger Ebert won for criticism in 1975 — the first film critic ever to receive a Pulitzer — after becoming the paper's chief film critic in 1967, a post he held for more than 40 years ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Roger-Ebert?source=NEWS1210)). John H. White won for feature photography in 1982, cited for consistently excellent work on a variety of subjects, and Jack Higgins for editorial cartooning in 1989 ([The Pulitzer Prizes](https://pulitzer.org/cms/sites/default/files/content/the_pulitzer_prizes_2020_winners_and_finalistsupdatedversion.pdf)).

The most recent win, in 2011, came after a 22-year gap: Frank Main spent four months following two Chicago homicide detectives trying to solve a gang-related murder, and the series revisited a violent April 2008 weekend in which 40 shootings — seven fatal — led to only one case going to trial ([Editor and Publisher](https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/2011-pulitzer-prize-winners,142714)). The Pulitzer board cited the trio "for their immersive documentation of violence in Chicago neighborhoods, probing the lives of victims, criminals and detectives as a widespread code of silence impedes solutions" ([The Pulitzer Prizes](https://pulitzer.org/cms/sites/default/files/content/the_pulitzer_prizes_2020_winners_and_finalistsupdatedversion.pdf)). The entry itself almost never arrived: it had to be postmarked 1 February, on the night of a blizzard, and Main's editor flagged down a taxi to a closed UPS facility to get it stamped ([Editor and Publisher](https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/2011-pulitzer-prize-winners,142714)).

## The nonprofit conversion

By the 2010s the ownership churn had become a survival story: Sun-Times Media filed for Chapter 11 in March 2009, and an investment group led by James C. Tyree bought it out of bankruptcy later that year for $5 million in cash and the assumption of $20 million in debts; Tyree died in 2011 ([Associated Press](https://tvnewscheck.com/uncategorized/article/investment-group-buying-chicago-sun-times/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/164725/None)). Wrapports LLC, led by Michael Ferro Jr., bought the company that December for more than $20 million ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/164725/None)). Ferro chaired Wrapports until early 2016, when he donated his 40 percent stake to a community trust ([MediaShift](https://mediashift.org/2017/07/will-way-sun-times-won/)). In July 2017 a group led by former alderman Edwin Eisendrath and the Chicago Federation of Labor bought the paper for $1 with an $11.2 million operating pledge, after the Justice Department's antitrust division blocked a sale to Tronc, owner of the Chicago Tribune ([MediaShift](https://mediashift.org/2017/07/will-way-sun-times-won/)). Control shifted again in March 2019 to Sun-Times Investment Holdings LLC, backed by Michael Sacks and Rocky Wirtz ([Robert Feder](https://robertfeder.com/2019/03/28/new-investors-sacks-wirtz-fortify-sun-times-ownership-group/)).

On 31 January 2022 Chicago Public Media completed its acquisition of the paper, backed by $61 million in philanthropic pledges over five years, and the Sun-Times became a nonprofit subsidiary with its own newsroom alongside WBEZ, the city's [NPR](/organizations/npr/) affiliate ([Associated Press](https://tvnewscheck.com/journalism/article/public-radio-owner-buys-sun-times-in-big-chicago-media-deal/)). The combined organization reported about 200 journalists across both newsrooms, a website audience up about 70 percent year over year and email subscribers up from about 350,000 to about 650,000 ([Northwestern Medill Local News Initiative](https://localnewsinitiative.northwestern.edu/projects/state-of-local-news/2023/case-studies/chicago-public-media/)). Melissa Bell, who co-founded Vox.com and once worked on digital strategy at [The Washington Post](/organizations/washington-post/), took over as CEO in October 2024; about 35 journalists departed in her first year, and the two newsrooms have since merged into one under editor-in-chief Kimbriell Kelly — described as the largest nonprofit newsroom in the country ([Northwestern Medill Local News Initiative](https://localnewsinitiative.northwestern.edu/posts/2026/10/01/local-news-podcast-melissa-bell/)).

## FAQ

### Who owns the Chicago Sun-Times?

Chicago Public Media, the nonprofit parent of public radio station WBEZ, has owned the Sun-Times since 31 January 2022, when it completed the acquisition and converted the paper to nonprofit ownership. The paper operates as an independent subsidiary with its own newsroom, sharing content with WBEZ across platforms.

### When was the Chicago Sun-Times founded?

The modern paper dates to February 1948, when the Chicago Sun — founded by Marshall Field III, first issue 4 December 1941 — merged with the Chicago Daily Times. The paper traces its lineage further back, to the Chicago Evening Journal of 1844, which is why it is sometimes called the city's oldest daily.

### How many Pulitzer Prizes has the Sun-Times won?

Eight, according to the Pulitzer Prizes' own record: Thomas Fitzpatrick (1970), Jack Dykinga (1971), Ron Powers (1973), Art Petacque and Hugh Hough (1974), Roger Ebert (1975), John H. White (1982), Jack Higgins (1989), and Frank Main, Mark Konkol and John J. Kim (2011).

### Is the Chicago Sun-Times a nonprofit newspaper?

Yes. Since the January 2022 acquisition by Chicago Public Media it has operated as a nonprofit, funded by philanthropy, memberships, sponsorships and print subscriptions. The $61 million in pledges backing the deal ran over five years, and the paper converted from a subscription model to a membership model.

### What is the Sun-Times' relationship to WBEZ?

Both are owned by Chicago Public Media: WBEZ 91.5 FM is Chicago's NPR member station and the Sun-Times is its newspaper subsidiary. They long kept separate newsrooms while sharing content; under CEO Melissa Bell the two newsrooms merged into one, led by editor-in-chief Kimbriell Kelly.
