## Overview

ABET is the nonprofit quality-assurance organization that accredits individual
postsecondary programs in engineering, computing, engineering technology, and
applied and natural science — not the universities that house them. As of
September 2026 it accredits 4,863 programs at 950 colleges and universities in
42 countries and areas ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/friday/lifetstyle/a-new-academic-achievement-for-liwa-university-1.500668203)).
Pronounced "A-bet", it has operated exclusively under its acronym since 2005;
its former name, the Accreditation Board for Engineering and Technology,
survives only as the corporate name used when required by law
([ABET](https://www.abet.org/about-abet/history/)).

## What it is known for

ABET is best known as the specialized accreditor whose seal tells students and
employers that a specific degree program meets profession-set standards. Its
voluntary peer-review process is conducted by teams of skilled professionals
from industry, academia, and government who examine program curricula, faculty,
facilities, and institutional support, working from criteria developed by
technical professionals from ABET's member societies
([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/friday/lifetstyle/a-new-academic-achievement-for-liwa-university-1.500668203)).
The organization is itself certified to ISO 9001 for quality management
([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/friday/lifetstyle/a-new-academic-achievement-for-liwa-university-1.500668203)).

## What ABET accreditation actually covers: programs, not institutions

This is the distinction most coverage blurs. ABET accredits **programs**, not
institutions: specifically, postsecondary, degree-granting programs offered by
regionally accredited institutions in the United States and nationally
accredited institutions outside the United States. It explicitly does not
accredit certification, training, or doctoral programs
([ABET](https://www.abet.org/accreditation/what-is-accreditation/what-programs-does-abet-accredit/)).
A university must already hold institutional accreditation before any of its
programs can apply for ABET review — so a school can be fully regionally
accredited while its specific engineering or computing program lacks ABET
accreditation ([LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/what-is-abet-accreditation-and-why-does-it-matter/)).

The work is divided among four commissions, and — a detail generic explainers
routinely miss — each covers different degree levels: ANSAC covers applied and
natural science (associate, bachelor's, master's); CAC covers computing
(associate, bachelor's, master's); EAC covers engineering (bachelor's and
master's only); and ETAC covers engineering technology (associate and
bachelor's only)
([ABET](https://www.abet.org/accreditation/what-is-accreditation/what-programs-does-abet-accredit/)).

Engineering and engineering technology are reviewed under separate criteria by
separate commissions, and the difference is substantive: engineering programs
emphasize theory and conceptual design with higher-level mathematics including
multiple semesters of calculus, while engineering technology programs emphasize
application and implementation with algebra, trigonometry, and applied calculus.
The career stakes differ too — bachelor-level engineering graduates are
eligible to become registered Professional Engineers in all U.S. states and
territories (via the FE and PE examinations plus documented work experience),
whereas engineering technology graduates are eligible in many, but not all,
states
([ABET](https://www.abet.org/accreditation/what-is-accreditation/what-programs-does-abet-accredit/)).
Accreditation is not permanent: a clean review earns a six-year "Next General
Review" cycle, while findings can trigger interim reports or show-cause actions
requiring correction, with denial or withdrawal as the final sanction
([LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/what-is-abet-accreditation-and-why-does-it-matter/)).

## History

ABET was founded in 1932 as the Engineers' Council for Professional Development
(ECPD), an engineering professional body dedicated to the education,
accreditation, regulation, and professional development of engineering
professionals and students in the United States
([ABET](https://www.abet.org/about-abet/history/)). Seven engineering societies
founded it and set its original direction — [ASCE](/organizations/asce/), [AIME](/organizations/aime/), [ASME](/organizations/asme/), the [AIEE](/organizations/aiee/) (now IEEE), the SPEE (now [ASEE](/organizations/asee/)), the [AIChE](/organizations/aiche/), and the National Council of State Boards
of Engineering Examiners (now [NCEES](/organizations/ncees/)) — and it was first headquartered in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) before relocating to [Baltimore, Maryland](/places/baltimore/), in 1996
([ABET](https://www.abet.org/about-abet/history/)).

The milestones tell a story of expanding scope. ECPD evaluated its first
engineering degree programs in 1936, began evaluating engineering technology
programs ten years later, and by 1947 had accredited 580 undergraduate
engineering programs at 133 institutions
([ABET](https://www.abet.org/about-abet/history/)). Renamed the Accreditation
Board for Engineering and Technology in 1980, ABET helped establish the
[Computing Sciences Accreditation Board](/organizations/csab/) (now CSAB) in 1985 ahead of the computer
science boom; CSAB merged into ABET in the early 2000s as one of its largest
member societies, with more than 300 accredited programs
([ABET](https://www.abet.org/about-abet/history/)). In 2005 the organization
began operating exclusively under the acronym ABET, reflecting a scope grown
well beyond engineering and technology into computing and the applied and
natural sciences ([ABET](https://www.abet.org/about-abet/history/)).

## Global reach

From a U.S. professional body, ABET has grown into a global accreditor:
4,863 programs at 950 colleges and universities in 42 countries and areas, a
figure reported in September 2026
([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/friday/lifetstyle/a-new-academic-achievement-for-liwa-university-1.500668203)).
Its degrees carry weight internationally through mutual recognition agreements:
ABET was a founding signatory of the [1989 Washington Accord](/events/washington-accord/), under which
engineering degrees accredited by any signatory are recognized by all other
signatories, and it has been a signatory of the parallel [Sydney Accord](/events/sydney-accord/) for
engineering technology since 2009
([LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/what-is-abet-accreditation-and-why-does-it-matter/)).

## Governance

ABET is governed by the professions it serves. Its activities are driven by 34
member societies that provide experts and set the standards for the
accreditation process ([ABET](https://www.abet.org/about-abet/history/)). The
accreditation criteria are developed by technical professionals from those
member societies and focus on what students experience and learn
([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/friday/lifetstyle/a-new-academic-achievement-for-liwa-university-1.500668203)),
and each of the four commissions makes the final accreditation decision for
the programs it oversees
([LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/what-is-abet-accreditation-and-why-does-it-matter/)).
