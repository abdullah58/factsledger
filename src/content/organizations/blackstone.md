## Overview

Blackstone Inc. is the world's largest alternative asset manager, an American investment firm based in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) that manages $1.27 trillion for pension funds, insurers and wealthy individuals ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/blackstone-beats-estimates-strong-dealmaking-activity-2026-01-29/)). Founded in 1985 by Stephen A. Schwarzman and Peter G. Peterson with $400,000 of their own money, it grew from a small mergers-and-acquisitions boutique into a public company listed on the New York Stock Exchange as BX, spanning private equity, real estate, credit, infrastructure and hedge funds ([Blackstone](https://www.blackstone.com/insights/article/my-personal-history-chapter-13/); [Blackstone](https://www.blackstone.com/news/press/the-blackstone-group-prices-4-133-billion-initial-public-offering/)).

This is Blackstone Inc. the investment firm — not BlackRock, the separate asset manager that grew out of a business Blackstone itself launched in 1988, and not the Blackstone kitchen and griddle brand ([Blackstone](https://www.blackstone.com/insights/article/my-personal-history-chapter-13/)). The name comes from its founders: "Schwarz" is German for black, and "Petro", from Peterson's family name Petropoulos, means stone ([Blackstone](https://www.blackstone.com/insights/article/my-personal-history-chapter-13/)). Its story is one of scale built deal by deal — and of the political scrutiny that scale now attracts in the [United States](/places/united-states/) housing market.

## From a $400,000 boutique to the largest alternative manager

Schwarzman and Peterson had worked together at Lehman Brothers, where Peterson had been chief executive and Schwarzman ran global mergers and acquisitions, before opening their own firm on 1 October 1985 with $200,000 each ([Blackstone](https://www.blackstone.com/insights/article/my-personal-history-chapter-13/)). They planned three pillars: a boutique advisory firm with no conflicts between clients, a leveraged-buyout fund, and new ventures led by an exceptional person. The third pillar produced the liquid-asset business Larry Fink built from 1988, which eventually left the firm and became BlackRock, now far larger than its parent ([Blackstone](https://www.blackstone.com/insights/article/my-personal-history-chapter-13/)). Early going was hard: the first buyout fund aimed to raise $1 billion and gathered only $175 million in the United States before Japanese investors rescued the raise ([Blackstone](https://www.blackstone.com/insights/article/my-personal-history-chapter-13/)). The firm went public on 21 June 2007, pricing 133,333,334 units at $31.00 to raise $4.133 billion, with trading starting the next day under the symbol BX ([Blackstone](https://www.blackstone.com/news/press/the-blackstone-group-prices-4-133-billion-initial-public-offering/)).

## The deal machine: Equity Office, Hilton and Invitation Homes

Blackstone's defining year was 2007. In February, Equity Office Properties shareholders approved its $23 billion cash offer — $39 billion including assumed debt — making it the largest private-equity buyout ever at the time, after rival bidder Vornado Realty Trust withdrew ([Property Week](https://www.propertyweek.com/news/blackstone-scoops-equity-office-in-record-buyout)). In July it agreed a $26 billion all-cash leveraged buyout of Hilton, financed with $20.5 billion of debt and $5.6 billion of equity and orchestrated by Jonathan Gray, who installed Christopher Nassetta as chief executive ([Hospitality Investor](https://www.hospitalityinvestor.com/investment/how-private-equity-giant-blackstone-became-real-estate-monolith)). Bought weeks before the financial crisis, Hilton was restructured, returned to the public markets in December 2013 and fully exited in 2018 with a netted profit of about $14 billion ([Hospitality Investor](https://www.hospitalityinvestor.com/investment/how-private-equity-giant-blackstone-became-real-estate-monolith)). Gray, who joined Blackstone in 1992 and had led real estate since 2005, was promoted to president and chief operating officer in February 2018 ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/commercial-real-estate/jon-gray-promoted-to-president-coo-at-blackstone-84945)).

## Scale, data centres and the housing backlash

The crisis that nearly sank Hilton also created Blackstone's most controversial business. From 2012 it bought 30,000+ foreclosed homes for about $10 billion, spent $2 billion more fixing them up, and housed them in Invitation Homes, taken public in 2017 and exited in 2019 — Gray telling the [Wall Street Journal](/organizations/wall-street-journal/) that "we created a company from scratch" ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blackstone-gets-back-into-the-single-family-rental-game/)). It then returned to the asset class: in May 2024 it took Tricon Residential private for $3.5 billion, acquiring about 38,000 Sun Belt rental homes, with [Wells Fargo](/organizations/wells-fargo/) among its advisers on the deal ([Blackstone](https://www.blackstone.com/news/press/blackstone-real-estate-completes-privatization-of-tricon/)). Meanwhile its data-centre bet paid off: infrastructure valuations rose 8.4% in late 2025, driven by QTS, bought for about $10 billion in 2021 — showing the AI infrastructure build-out, not the old buyout business, now drives growth ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/blackstone-beats-estimates-strong-dealmaking-activity-2026-01-29/)). But housing draws fire: in March 2026 [Elizabeth Warren](/people/elizabeth-warren/) wrote to Blackstone and 13 other landlords probing rental practices, noting Tricon had faced major litigation over alleged fair-housing and habitability failures ([US Senate Committee on Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs](https://www.banking.senate.gov/newsroom/minority/warren-probes-biggest-corporate-landlords-on-predatory-rental-practices-across-housing-sector)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Blackstone?** Blackstone Inc. is the world's largest alternative asset manager, based in New York City and listed on the New York Stock Exchange as BX ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/blackstone-beats-estimates-strong-dealmaking-activity-2026-01-29/)). It manages $1.27 trillion across private equity, real estate, credit, infrastructure and hedge funds, investing mainly for pension funds, insurers and wealthy individuals ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/blackstone-beats-estimates-strong-dealmaking-activity-2026-01-29/)).

**Is Blackstone the same as BlackRock?** No. BlackRock grew out of a liquid-asset business Blackstone launched in 1988 by bringing in Larry Fink; that business later left Blackstone and became a separate, larger asset manager ([Blackstone](https://www.blackstone.com/insights/article/my-personal-history-chapter-13/)). The two firms are independent competitors, and Blackstone is also unrelated to the Blackstone griddle brand.

**Who founded Blackstone and who runs it now?** Stephen A. Schwarzman and Peter G. Peterson founded it in 1985 with $400,000, having worked together at Lehman Brothers ([Blackstone](https://www.blackstone.com/insights/article/my-personal-history-chapter-13/)). Schwarzman remains chairman and chief executive, while Jonathan Gray, who joined in 1992, has been president and chief operating officer since February 2018 ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/commercial-real-estate/jon-gray-promoted-to-president-coo-at-blackstone-84945)).

**What happened with BREIT redemptions?** BREIT, Blackstone's non-traded real estate trust launched in 2017, caps redemptions at 2% of net asset value a month and 5% a quarter ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/capital-markets/blackstone-starwood-nontraded-reits-redemptions-withdrawals-116747)). In November 2022 requests exceeded those caps, so it met only about 43% of them — $1.3 billion — the first limits in the fund's history ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/capital-markets/blackstone-starwood-nontraded-reits-redemptions-withdrawals-116747)).

**Why is Blackstone criticised over housing?** After the 2008 crisis it bought tens of thousands of foreclosed homes for Invitation Homes, and in 2024 it bought Tricon's roughly 38,000 rental homes ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blackstone-gets-back-into-the-single-family-rental-game/); [Blackstone](https://www.blackstone.com/news/press/blackstone-real-estate-completes-privatization-of-tricon/)). Critics say institutional buying pushes up prices; in March 2026 Senator Elizabeth Warren probed Blackstone and other landlords over rental practices and alleged maintenance failures ([US Senate Committee on Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs](https://www.banking.senate.gov/newsroom/minority/warren-probes-biggest-corporate-landlords-on-predatory-rental-practices-across-housing-sector)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not settle how much Blackstone made exiting Invitation Homes: one fully-read source reports about $2 billion in profit, another says about $7 billion, and the two figures are not reconciled here ([Hospitality Investor](https://www.hospitalityinvestor.com/investment/how-private-equity-giant-blackstone-became-real-estate-monolith); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blackstone-gets-back-into-the-single-family-rental-game/)). It does not present Senator Warren's 2026 landlord probe as a finding of wrongdoing — the letters request data, and no outcome was verified ([US Senate Committee on Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs](https://www.banking.senate.gov/newsroom/minority/warren-probes-biggest-corporate-landlords-on-predatory-rental-practices-across-housing-sector)). It does not state an employee count or S&P 500 membership, neither of which was verified from a fully-read source. Founding details such as the $200,000 each founder contributed rest on Schwarzman's own memoir and are presented as his account ([Blackstone](https://www.blackstone.com/insights/article/my-personal-history-chapter-13/)). Finally, it does not claim Blackstone still owns Invitation Homes: it exited in 2019, and its current single-family rental exposure runs through Tricon ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blackstone-gets-back-into-the-single-family-rental-game/); [Blackstone](https://www.blackstone.com/news/press/blackstone-real-estate-completes-privatization-of-tricon/)).
