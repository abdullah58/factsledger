## Overview

**The A.V. Club** is the American pop-culture publication — reviews, interviews, and features on film, TV, music, books, and games — that began in 1993 in the back pages of the satirical newspaper *The Onion* and has published online since 1996 ([The A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/about)). Unlike its parent, it was never satirical ([The A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/about)); after three decades online, G/O Media sold it to Paste Media in March 2024, ending its corporate link to *The Onion* ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/go-media-sells-av-club-the-takeout/)).

The publication's criticism travels: its 2012 review of [The Bunny Game](/creative-works/the-bunny-game/) called the film "brutal" and "repellent" but "far from artless, or pointless" — a verdict quoted ever since in writing about the film ([The A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/the-bunny-game-1798173832)).

## The Onion's straight-faced sibling

The A.V. Club's identity is inseparable from its origin. It began in the back pages of *The Onion* in 1993, when that paper was a weekly in Madison, Wisconsin; by the mid-1990s it was its own section — "a publication-within-the-publication dedicated to following pop culture down its various side and back roads" — and avclub.com became a distinct URL in 2005, with its articles appearing in *The Onion* until the original print run ended in 2013 ([The A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/about)). The name is "audiovisual," after the school AV clubs of "geeks who actually knew how to run the filmstrip and film projectors" — self-deprecation the site says it grew into, taking the "club" seriously through its commenting community, which it calls "a major part of what helped the site stake out a larger place in the pop culture conversation" ([The A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/about)). The March 2024 sale to Paste Media — with most staff carried over and two not, per G/O CEO Jim Spanfeller's memo — was framed as a rescue into a culture-publication portfolio rather than a consolidation ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/go-media-sells-av-club-the-takeout/)).

## A review desk with a long memory

What the site does best is sustained, staff-written criticism with institutional memory — three books carry the banner: *The Tenacity of the Cockroach* (2002), the list-obsessed *Inventory* (2009), and Nathan Rabin's *My Year of Flops* (2010) ([The A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/about)). Its current masthead lists Danette Chavez as editor-in-chief, Jacob Oller as film editor, and a small staff of critics and writers "spread across the continental United States" ([The A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/about)). That continuity is the point of the Bunny Game review: Noel Murray's 2012 write-up judged the film shot-by-shot — stark black-and-white photography, extreme angles, jumpy editing achieving "the quality of a nightmare" — before concluding that "after its first 20 minutes, *The Bunny Game* has done just about all it's going to do" ([The A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/the-bunny-game-1798173832)). For a film whose notoriety is mostly anecdotal, that kind of close, on-the-record criticism is exactly the durable record an outlaw reputation needs.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is The A.V. Club part of The Onion?**
Not anymore. It began in The Onion's back pages in 1993 and published online alongside it from 1996, but it was always the non-satirical sibling — real reviews, interviews, and news. G/O Media sold it to Paste Media in March 2024, and the sale "completely separated" it from The Onion, which G/O sold separately.

**Who owns The A.V. Club now?**
Paste Media has owned it since March 2024, buying it from G/O Media as part of that company's sell-off of its editorial properties (which also included Deadspin, Lifehacker, Jezebel, and Splinter). Paste offered positions to most of the A.V. Club staff; two staffers did not go over, according to G/O CEO Jim Spanfeller's memo.

**Why is it called "The A.V. Club"?**
"A.V." stands for "audiovisual" — a reference to the audiovisual clubs of American high schools, the geeks who ran the filmstrip and film projectors. The site describes the name as originally self-deprecating, but says it grew to take the "club" part seriously through its commenting community.

**What did Noel Murray write about The Bunny Game?**
In his 2012 A.V. Club review, Murray called Adam Rehmeier's film "brutal" and "repellent" but "far from artless, or pointless," praising its stark black-and-white photography, extreme angles, and jumpy editing for achieving "the quality of a nightmare" — while arguing the film had done everything it would do after its first 20 minutes.
