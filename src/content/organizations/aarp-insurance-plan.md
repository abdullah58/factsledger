## Overview

The AARP Insurance Plan, also called the AARP Health Trust, is a grantor trust established in 1958 by an Agreement and Declaration of Trust that holds the master group insurance policies behind AARP-branded insurance products ([AARP's audited financial statements](https://www.presentationnation.com/api/assets/companies/aarp/presentations/aarp-consolidated-financial-statements-2024-annual-report-2024/original.pdf)). It is not an insurance company and it does not underwrite policies: member premiums land in the trust's depository accounts and are remitted to third-party carriers — including UnitedHealthcare — net of royalties owed to AARP, Inc., as AARP's own Grant Thornton-audited financial statements show ([AARP's audited financial statements](https://www.presentationnation.com/api/assets/companies/aarp/presentations/aarp-consolidated-financial-statements-2024-annual-report-2024/original.pdf)).

The Plan is the legal vehicle underneath [AARP](/organizations/aarp/)'s insurance business. Agreements involving AARP, Inc., [AARP Services](/organizations/aarp-services/), United Healthcare Corporation, MetLife and Genworth make group insurance available to members, while billing and the issuance of policy certificates remain the carriers' responsibility; AARP accounts for the premium flows as agency transactions rather than revenue ([AARP's audited financial statements](https://www.presentationnation.com/api/assets/companies/aarp/presentations/aarp-consolidated-financial-statements-2024-annual-report-2024/original.pdf)). The trust's depository accounts collect premiums that members pay directly, and in 2024 the Plan processed $13.1 billion in such payments ([AARP's audited financial statements](https://www.presentationnation.com/api/assets/companies/aarp/presentations/aarp-consolidated-financial-statements-2024-annual-report-2024/original.pdf)). Since a July 2024 restatement of the trust agreement, the Plan is administered by a Board of Trustees appointed jointly by AARP's Board of Directors and by a participating group health insurance provider ([AARP's audited financial statements](https://www.presentationnation.com/api/assets/companies/aarp/presentations/aarp-consolidated-financial-statements-2024-annual-report-2024/original.pdf)).

## How the money moves through the trust

When a member buys an AARP-branded plan, the premium goes to the Plan's depository accounts first. Under the carrier agreements, the trust remits collections to the third-party carriers within contractually specified periods, net of contractual royalties due to AARP, Inc., which AARP books as royalty revenue ([AARP's audited financial statements](https://www.presentationnation.com/api/assets/companies/aarp/presentations/aarp-consolidated-financial-statements-2024-annual-report-2024/original.pdf)). Because the trust acts only as a conduit, accounting rules treat the flows as agency transactions: the billions pass through without being recorded as AARP revenue or expenses ([AARP's audited financial statements](https://www.presentationnation.com/api/assets/companies/aarp/presentations/aarp-consolidated-financial-statements-2024-annual-report-2024/original.pdf)). Under the restructured agreement the Plan now remits all collections to the group health insurance provider daily, a change the statements say sharply reduced the "insurance premiums payable" balance on AARP's books ([AARP's audited financial statements](https://www.presentationnation.com/api/assets/companies/aarp/presentations/aarp-consolidated-financial-statements-2024-annual-report-2024/original.pdf)). The holding period is not dead time: premiums sitting in depository accounts generated $69.9 million in net investment income in 2024 alone and $106.8 million in 2023 — evidence that the trust's premium float is itself an income engine, not just a pass-through ([AARP's audited financial statements](https://www.presentationnation.com/api/assets/companies/aarp/presentations/aarp-consolidated-financial-statements-2024-annual-report-2024/original.pdf)).

## The 2024 restructuring and the royalty economics

In 2024 AARP restructured its royalty agreement with a group health insurance provider: instead of periodic royalties, AARP took a one-time $9.062 billion advance in exchange for an exclusive license to use AARP's trade name and intellectual property in marketing a health insurance program ([AARP's audited financial statements](https://www.presentationnation.com/api/assets/companies/aarp/presentations/aarp-consolidated-financial-statements-2024-annual-report-2024/original.pdf)). Booked in investments and deferred revenue, the payment is amortized straight-line over an estimated 12-year license life, with $8.727 billion still unamortized at year-end ([AARP's audited financial statements](https://www.presentationnation.com/api/assets/companies/aarp/presentations/aarp-consolidated-financial-statements-2024-annual-report-2024/original.pdf)). The audited statements put the trust's economics in hard numbers ([AARP's audited financial statements](https://www.presentationnation.com/api/assets/companies/aarp/presentations/aarp-consolidated-financial-statements-2024-annual-report-2024/original.pdf)):

| Measure | 2024 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|
| Member premiums processed by the Plan | $13.1 billion | $12.1 billion |
| Total royalty revenue | $1.204 billion | $1.134 billion |
| Royalties from health products and services | $962 million | $906 million |
| Membership dues | $288.8 million | $289.2 million |

The trust supplied 34% of AARP's total royalty revenue in 2024 (63% in 2023), and the advance alone was more than 4.6 times AARP's total operating revenue for 2024 ([AARP's audited financial statements](https://www.presentationnation.com/api/assets/companies/aarp/presentations/aarp-consolidated-financial-statements-2024-annual-report-2024/original.pdf)).

## Not an insurer — and why the royalties are disputed

The plan documents are explicit: AARP Medicare Supplement Insurance Plans are insured by [UnitedHealthcare](/organizations/unitedhealthcare/) Insurance Company, which pays AARP royalties for the use of its intellectual property, and "AARP and its affiliates are not insurers" ([AARP Medicare Supplement plan guide](https://1library.net/article/guide-aarp-medicare-supplement-insurance-portfolio-plans.yj85286q)). The master policy (Form GRP79171) is issued in the [District of Columbia](/places/district-of-columbia/) to the trust's trustees; members receive certificates from UnitedHealthcare, and the trust forwards collected premiums to the carrier with a royalty portion directed to AARP ([AARP Medicare Supplement plan guide](https://1library.net/article/guide-aarp-medicare-supplement-insurance-portfolio-plans.yj85286q)). Critics attack the mechanism itself. A proposed California class action against AARP and [UnitedHealth Group](/organizations/unitedhealth-group/) alleges the payments are not royalties at all but a 4.95% insurance commission that AARP — which the complaint says is not licensed as an insurance agent — may not legally collect; that is an allegation, not a finding ([ClassAction.org](https://www.classaction.org/news/class-action-claims-medicare-supplement-insurance-commissions-paid-to-aarp-by-unitedhealth-mischaracterized-as-royalties)). A separate line of criticism argues that a percentage-of-premium royalty gives AARP a financial incentive to favor higher Medigap premiums — stated here as the critics' argument, not as established fact ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/op-eds/4648693/aarp-membership-dues-unitedhealth-group/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the AARP Insurance Plan an insurance company?**
No. It is a grantor trust — the AARP Health Trust — that holds master group insurance policies and channels member premiums to the actual insurers. AARP's own plan documents state that "AARP and its affiliates are not insurers"; the underwriter of the Medicare Supplement plans is UnitedHealthcare Insurance Company ([AARP Medicare Supplement plan guide](https://1library.net/article/guide-aarp-medicare-supplement-insurance-portfolio-plans.yj85286q)).

**Who actually underwrites AARP-branded Medicare Supplement plans?**
UnitedHealthcare Insurance Company underwrites them and issues the certificates of insurance. AARP endorses the plans, and its trust collects premiums before remitting them to UnitedHealthcare. In exchange, UnitedHealthcare pays AARP royalties for using the AARP name and intellectual property, and the fees support AARP's general purposes ([AARP Medicare Supplement plan guide](https://1library.net/article/guide-aarp-medicare-supplement-insurance-portfolio-plans.yj85286q)).

**What was the $9.062 billion payment in 2024?**
A one-time royalty advance from a group health insurance provider, in exchange for an exclusive license to use AARP's trade name and other intellectual property to market a health insurance program. AARP booked it as deferred revenue, amortizing it over an estimated 12-year license life, with $8.727 billion still unrecognized at year-end ([AARP's audited financial statements](https://www.presentationnation.com/api/assets/companies/aarp/presentations/aarp-consolidated-financial-statements-2024-annual-report-2024/original.pdf)).

**How much money moves through the trust each year?**
The trust processed $13.1 billion in member premium payments in 2024 and $12.1 billion in 2023. Premiums held in its depository accounts before remittance generated $69.9 million in net investment income in 2024 — a float that itself earns money ([AARP's audited financial statements](https://www.presentationnation.com/api/assets/companies/aarp/presentations/aarp-consolidated-financial-statements-2024-annual-report-2024/original.pdf)).
