## Overview

**Dassault Systèmes is the French software company that turned an aircraft maker's in-house drafting tool into the industrial world's design platform: founded in 1981 from Dassault Aviation's CATIA team, it sells 3D design, simulation and product-lifecycle software — CATIA, SOLIDWORKS and the 3DEXPERIENCE platform — to more than 390,000 customers across 12 industries, and in 2019 it spent $5.8 billion on Medidata to carry that virtual-twin idea from airplanes and cars into clinical trials.** ([FundingUniverse](https://FundingUniverse.com/company-histories/dassault-syst%C3%A8mes-s-a-history/)) One disambiguation matters before anything else: Dassault Systèmes does not manufacture aircraft — its sister company, Dassault Aviation, designs and builds military aircraft, business jets and space systems, while Dassault Systèmes provides the software used across aerospace and defence, one of the 12 industries it serves. ([Dassault Systèmes](https://www.3ds.com/about/company/facts-and-faqs))

## CATIA, IBM, and the bargain that built the company

Dassault Systèmes grew out of aerospace's search for drafting tools that could handle the increasing complexity of aviation design. ([FundingUniverse](https://FundingUniverse.com/company-histories/dassault-syst%C3%A8mes-s-a-history/)) In the late 1970s, Dassault's own programmers built the predecessor to CATIA (Computer-Aided Three Dimensional Interactive Application), the first to bring three-dimensional modeling to the design process — a third plane on the screen that eliminated many errors in translating a 2D drawing into a physical model and reduced the expense of building models and prototypes at each step. ([FundingUniverse](https://FundingUniverse.com/company-histories/dassault-syst%C3%A8mes-s-a-history/))

In 1981, Avions Marcel Dassault created Dassault Systèmes, transferring its 15-member development staff and the CATIA program to the new subsidiary. ([FundingUniverse](https://FundingUniverse.com/company-histories/dassault-syst%C3%A8mes-s-a-history/)) Instead of building its own sales force, the new company struck an agreement with IBM — then at the height of its dominance — to market, distribute and support CATIA, with IBM receiving about 50 percent of CATIA sales revenue in exchange for bringing the product to its worldwide customer base. ([FundingUniverse](https://FundingUniverse.com/company-histories/dassault-syst%C3%A8mes-s-a-history/)) Demand was immediate, and CATIA spread from aviation into automobiles, consumer goods and shipbuilding. ([FundingUniverse](https://FundingUniverse.com/company-histories/dassault-syst%C3%A8mes-s-a-history/))

The product evolved in public stages: the initial commercial version in 1982 added 3D design, surface modeling and tool programming to existing 2D drafting programs; Version 2, launched commercially in 1985, added drafting and robotics functions; Version 3, in 1988, was among the first to include computer-assisted engineering functions. ([FundingUniverse](https://FundingUniverse.com/company-histories/dassault-syst%C3%A8mes-s-a-history/)) By the 1990s, the company's software was used to develop seven of every ten new airplanes and four of every ten new cars worldwide, and CATIA had designed products as varied as Chrysler's Voyager minivans and the Boeing 777, the Falcon 2000 business jet and the Rafale fighter. ([FundingUniverse](https://FundingUniverse.com/company-histories/dassault-syst%C3%A8mes-s-a-history/)) In 1992, IBM transferred future development of CADAM — Lockheed's pioneering 2D drafting program, which IBM had bought — to Dassault Systèmes, taking a 10 percent share of the company; the first integrated CATIA-CADAM version appeared in 1993. ([FundingUniverse](https://FundingUniverse.com/company-histories/dassault-syst%C3%A8mes-s-a-history/)) The company went public in 1996 on the Paris and NASDAQ exchanges, with the 17.5 percent on offer oversubscribed about 35 times. ([FundingUniverse](https://FundingUniverse.com/company-histories/dassault-syst%C3%A8mes-s-a-history/))

## SolidWorks, 3DEXPERIENCE, and the mainstream market

The 1996 IPO funded the deal that changed Dassault's shape. On June 24, 1997, Dassault Systèmes signed an agreement to acquire SolidWorks Corporation in a stock transaction valued at approximately $310 million — 4.85 million Dassault shares for 100 percent of SolidWorks. ([Dassault Systèmes](https://www.3ds.com/newsroom/press-releases/dassault-systemes-signs-definitive-agreement-acquire-solidworks)) SolidWorks, founded in 1993, had created the first Windows-native mechanical design system, bringing production solid modeling to the engineer's desktop; it had over 6,000 installed seats serving over 3,000 customers and was to continue as a separate subsidiary under founder Jon Hirschtick, based in Concord, Massachusetts. ([Dassault Systèmes](https://www.3ds.com/newsroom/press-releases/dassault-systemes-signs-definitive-agreement-acquire-solidworks)) The logic was segmentation: Dassault's process-centric CATIA-CADAM line served major industrial customers, while SolidWorks served the mainstream, design-centric market on Windows PCs — a market the company's own history says the acquisition opened up, alongside Deneb's digital-manufacturing software, acquired at the end of 1997. ([FundingUniverse](https://FundingUniverse.com/company-histories/dassault-syst%C3%A8mes-s-a-history/))

CATIA remained the company's first brand — originating from technology developed at Dassault Aviation before Dassault Systèmes was founded in 1981 — within a portfolio that grew to 12 brands, including SOLIDWORKS, ENOVIA, SIMULIA, DELMIA, BIOVIA, CENTRIC and MEDIDATA. ([Dassault Systèmes](https://www.3ds.com/about/company/facts-and-faqs)) In 2012, the company introduced the 3DEXPERIENCE platform, providing virtual twins to navigate, evaluate and holistically experiment with an idea in the virtual world before making it reality, pitched at an "experience economy" where experiences matter more than products themselves. ([Dassault Systèmes](https://www.3ds.com/about/company/innovation)) Its own timeline marks the steps: the Boeing 777 virtual twin in 1989, end-to-end 3D product lifecycle management in 1999, the 3DEXPERIENCE platform in 2012, the virtual twin of humans in 2020, and the 3D UNIV+RSES generative-economy vision in 2025. ([Dassault Systèmes](https://www.3ds.com/about/company/innovation))

## Medidata: from things to life

The largest deal in Dassault's history to that point moved it decisively into life sciences. In June 2019, Dassault agreed to acquire Medidata, a clinical-trial technology company, in an all-cash deal valued at $5.8 billion. ([MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2019/06/french-tech-company-dassault-makes-5-8b-acquisition-of-medidata/)) Medidata, then 20 years old and public since 2009, had a cloud platform giving researchers tools to design, manage and collect data from clinical trials, with $636 million in revenue the previous year from 1,300 customers spanning pharma companies, biotech startups, contract research organizations and medical centers. ([MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2019/06/french-tech-company-dassault-makes-5-8b-acquisition-of-medidata/)) The deal was not Dassault's first life-sciences step: in 2014 it had bought Accelrys for $750 million and used its data and lab-management technologies to launch the science-focused BIOVIA brand. ([MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2019/06/french-tech-company-dassault-makes-5-8b-acquisition-of-medidata/)) The Medidata acquisition completed on October 29, 2019, at $92.25 per share in cash, and Medidata ceased NASDAQ trading on October 28, becoming a Dassault Systèmes brand powered by the 3DEXPERIENCE platform. ([Dassault Systèmes](https://www.3ds.com/newsroom/press-releases/dassault-systemes-completes-acquisition-medidata-opening-new-world-virtual-twin-experiences-healthcare))

In competitive terms, Dassault sits in the same simulation-and-design arena as [Altair Engineering](/organizations/altair-engineering/): Altair founder and CEO [James Scapa](/people/james-scapa/) named Dassault Systèmes among Altair's main competitors in 2017, alongside [Siemens](/organizations/siemens/), [Ansys](/organizations/ansys/) and [MSC Software](/organizations/msc-software/). ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/11/01/altair-engineering-soars-41-in-public-debut/1024)) At the scale the company itself reports — 25,000 employees across 188 sites, more than 390,000 customers in 12 industries, and €6.24 billion in 2025 revenue, led by CEO and Chairman Pascal Daloz from Vélizy-Villacoublay outside Paris — Dassault is the independent incumbent in that arena, still standing apart from the consolidation that absorbed two of its rivals. ([Dassault Systèmes](https://www.3ds.com/about/company/facts-and-faqs))
