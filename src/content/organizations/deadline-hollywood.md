## Overview

**Deadline Hollywood** — commonly *Deadline* or *Deadline.com* — is the American entertainment-industry news site founded in 2006 by Nikki Finke as *Deadline Hollywood Daily*, a 24/7 blog extending her *LA Weekly* column, and owned by Penske Media Corporation since its 2009 sale ([ET Online](https://www.etonline.com/nikki-finke-journalist-and-deadline-founder-dead-at-68-192347); [TheWrap](http://www.thewrap.com/nikki-finke-sells-dhd-14m-3852/)). It is the trade outlet whose reporting documented the *Michael* (2026) biopic's delay chain — April 18, 2025, to October 3, 2025, to April 24, 2026 — and carried the reported $25 million settlement figure behind the film's reshoots ([eurweb](https://eurweb.com/michael-jackson-biopic-april-2026/)).

Deadline's significance is structural: it was the blog that proved Hollywood journalism no longer needed print, then watched its owner buy the century-old trade it had disrupted.

## A one-woman 24/7 operation that outran the trades

Finke came to the blog with a reporter's résumé — Associated Press foreign correspondent, *Newsweek* correspondent, *Los Angeles Times* staff writer, editor and columnist for the *[New York Observer](/organizations/new-york-observer/)* and *New York Magazine* — before starting her *Deadline Hollywood* column in *LA Weekly* in 2002 and launching the site as its online version around 2006 ([TheWrap](http://www.thewrap.com/nikki-finke-sells-dhd-14m-3852/)). The site was, by her own account and ET's, a 24/7 operation dedicated to her firsthand, no-nonsense accounts of the entertainment business ([ET Online](https://www.etonline.com/nikki-finke-journalist-and-deadline-founder-dead-at-68-192347)). Her style was the brand: take-no-prisoners reporting, "live-snarking" of award shows, and a standing warning to readers — "come for the cynicism… stay for the subversion" — that made her, in TheWrap's 2009 assessment, a firebrand whose frequent scoops were must-reads for industry insiders even as her methods drew charges of favoritism ([TheWrap](http://www.thewrap.com/nikki-finke-sells-dhd-14m-3852/); [ET Online](https://www.etonline.com/nikki-finke-journalist-and-deadline-founder-dead-at-68-192347)). She landed on *Forbes*' Most Powerful Women list ([ET Online](https://www.etonline.com/nikki-finke-journalist-and-deadline-founder-dead-at-68-192347)).

## The $14 million sale — and the blog that bought the trade

In 2009, Jay Penske's Mail.com Media Corporation — the company that became Penske Media Corporation — bought DeadlineHollywoodDaily.com for a reported $14 million, paid out over several years, with Finke taking the title of general manager, editor-in-chief and founder and staying on under a five-year employment contract ([TheWrap](http://www.thewrap.com/nikki-finke-sells-dhd-14m-3852/); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/nikki-finke-owner-penske-media-buys-hollywood-trade-variety-58041/)). That $14 million bought a site with roughly 200,000 monthly uniques, per Quantcast — about $70 per monthly reader, next to HuffPost's $90 million valuation on 8–10 million uniques — which implies the price was set on influence, not audience: the great majority of those readers were Hollywood insiders ([TheWrap](http://www.thewrap.com/nikki-finke-sells-dhd-14m-3852/)). Three years later the inversion completed: Penske bought 107-year-old [Variety](/organizations/variety/), a deal Finke herself had pushed for, with Reed Elsevier's $40 million asking price settling at a believed ~$25 million with seller financing ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/nikki-finke-owner-penske-media-buys-hollywood-trade-variety-58041/)). The financial backdrop made the symbolism concrete: Variety's annual revenue had roughly halved from ~$90 million in the 1990s to ~$45 million by 2012, while digital rivals Deadline and TheWrap took its market share ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/nikki-finke-owner-penske-media-buys-hollywood-trade-variety-58041/)). Finke left Deadline in 2013, launched HollywoodDementia.com in 2015, and died in October 2022 at 68 ([ET Online](https://www.etonline.com/nikki-finke-journalist-and-deadline-founder-dead-at-68-192347)).

## Deadline and the *Michael* delay chain

Deadline's continuing trade function is visible in its *Michael* coverage. In July 2025 it reported the film's move to a global April 24, 2026 release — the third delay, from April 18, 2025, to October 3, 2025 — driven by a 3.5-hour-plus initial cut and the legal fallout of the 1993 settlement with Jordan Chandler, whom the film's script had originally depicted ([eurweb](https://eurweb.com/michael-jackson-biopic-april-2026/)). Deadline's reporting carried the settlement figure at a reported $25 million, including the clause barring depiction of the Chandlers in any film — the clause whose late discovery forced the reshoots ([eurweb](https://eurweb.com/michael-jackson-biopic-april-2026/)). The same reporting relayed a production source's quote to [PEOPLE](/organizations/people-magazine/) that the film was "not in total chaos" and that reshoots were scheduled for March — a dating that sits in tension with the 22-day June 2025 reshoot reported by Variety, an unresolved conflict this profile flags rather than resolves ([eurweb](https://eurweb.com/michael-jackson-biopic-april-2026/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who founded Deadline Hollywood?**
Nikki Finke, a former AP foreign correspondent, Newsweek correspondent, LA Times staff writer, and NY Observer/NY Magazine editor-columnist. She started the Deadline Hollywood column in LA Weekly in 2002, launched Deadline Hollywood Daily as its 24/7 online version in 2006, and remained editor-in-chief through the 2009 sale until leaving in 2013.

**Who owns Deadline?**
Penske Media Corporation, which bought the site in 2009 when it was still Mail.com Media Corporation, headed by chairman and CEO Jay Penske. The purchase price was reported at $14 million paid over several years, plus a five-year employment contract for Finke.

**How did Deadline relate to Variety?**
Deadline disrupted Variety — then, in 2012, owner Penske Media bought the 107-year-old trade for a believed ~$25 million, a deal Finke herself had pushed for. NPR later called it the story of a blog buying Hollywood's oldest trade paper; TheWrap noted Reed Elsevier's $40 million ask settled with seller financing.

**What did Deadline report about the *Michael* biopic?**
Deadline reported the film's three-date delay chain (April 18, 2025 → October 3, 2025 → April 24, 2026), driven by a 3.5-hour initial cut and the settlement-clause fallout, and carried the reported $25 million settlement figure with Jordan Chandler's family — including the clause barring depiction of the Chandlers in any film.
