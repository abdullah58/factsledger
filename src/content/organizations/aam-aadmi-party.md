## Overview

**The Aam Aadmi Party (AAP; "Common Man's Party")** is an [Indian](/places/india/) political party founded on 26 November 2012 out of the 2011 anti-corruption movement. It governed Delhi from 2015 to 2025, won Punjab in 2022, gained national-party status in April 2023, and lost Delhi in February 2025 — leaving Punjab, under Chief Minister Bhagwant Mann, as its only state government while its founder Arvind Kejriwal faced jail in the Delhi excise-policy case ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/?p=1503142865); [Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/elections/punjab-assembly-election/2022-punjab-election-aap-s-bhagwant-mann-to-be-sworn-in-as-cm-on-march-16-101646995227774.html); [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/elections/delhi-assembly/delhi-election-result-2025-live-updates-bjp-aap-congress/article69193873.ece/amp/)).

AAP is the most electorally successful product of India's 2011 anti-corruption agitation: within three years of its founding it ran the capital, within ten it was a recognised national party, and within thirteen it had lost the capital again. Its story is a test of whether a protest movement can govern — and of how far welfare delivery can carry a party once its leaders face corruption charges.

## From protest movement to party, 2011–2013

On 5 April 2011, activist Anna Hazare began an indefinite fast at Jantar Mantar in Delhi, demanding a stronger anti-corruption Lokpal bill; the India Against Corruption movement drew thousands of supporters ([Bhaskar English](https://www.bhaskarenglish.in/national/news/aap-journey-from-india-against-corruption-movement-to-national-party-status-delhi-assembly-elections-arvind-kejriwal-134412594.html)). The movement split over a basic question: Hazare wanted it to stay politically unaligned, while Kejriwal and his allies concluded that change had to come from inside the system. After opinion polls and internal debate, the party was formally formed on 26 November 2012, born out of the Hazare movement ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/?p=1503142865); [Bhaskar English](https://www.bhaskarenglish.in/national/news/aap-journey-from-india-against-corruption-movement-to-national-party-status-delhi-assembly-elections-arvind-kejriwal-134412594.html)). The name — Aam Aadmi Party, the common man's party — and the broom symbol were chosen to signal cleaning up corruption; the party also kept the movement's cap, reworded with the party name and the slogan "We Want Swaraj" ([Bhaskar English](https://www.bhaskarenglish.in/national/news/aap-journey-from-india-against-corruption-movement-to-national-party-status-delhi-assembly-elections-arvind-kejriwal-134412594.html)).

AAP contested its first Delhi Assembly election in 2013, targeting Chief Minister Sheila Dikshit's 15-year tenure, promising mohalla sabhas (neighbourhood assemblies), free water and electricity, and public consultation ([Bhaskar English](https://www.bhaskarenglish.in/national/news/aap-journey-from-india-against-corruption-movement-to-national-party-status-delhi-assembly-elections-arvind-kejriwal-134412594.html)). It won 28 seats, emerged as the second-largest party, and formed a minority government with Congress support, with Kejriwal as Chief Minister ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/?p=1503142865)). The government resigned after 49 days when it could not pass the Jan Lokpal Bill, drawing criticism for abandoning power ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/?p=1503142865); [Bhaskar English](https://www.bhaskarenglish.in/national/news/aap-journey-from-india-against-corruption-movement-to-national-party-status-delhi-assembly-elections-arvind-kejriwal-134412594.html)). In the 2014 [Lok Sabha](/organizations/lok-sabha/) election the party fielded over 400 candidates but won only four seats, all in Punjab; Kejriwal himself lost to [Narendra Modi](/people/narendra-modi/) in Varanasi ([Bhaskar English](https://www.bhaskarenglish.in/national/news/aap-journey-from-india-against-corruption-movement-to-national-party-status-delhi-assembly-elections-arvind-kejriwal-134412594.html)).

## The Delhi decade, Punjab, and national-party status

AAP bounced back in the 2015 Delhi election, winning 67 of the 70 seats and leaving the BJP with three and Congress with none — though internal conflict soon led to the expulsion of founding figures Yogendra Yadav and Prashant Bhushan ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/?p=1503142865); [Bhaskar English](https://www.bhaskarenglish.in/national/news/aap-journey-from-india-against-corruption-movement-to-national-party-status-delhi-assembly-elections-arvind-kejriwal-134412594.html)). It repeated the feat in 2020, winning 62 seats ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/?p=1503142865)). The party's pitch, the "Delhi Model of Governance" (also called the "Kejriwal Model"), rested on free electricity and water with a cap, free bus travel for women, and heavily promoted government schools — policies critics labelled giveaways and the party defended as welfare ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/?p=1503142865)).

Expansion beyond Delhi took a decade. After finishing second in Punjab in 2017 and winning just one Lok Sabha seat in 2019 (Mann in Punjab), AAP stormed to power in Punjab in 2022, winning 92 of the 117 Assembly seats; Mann was sworn in as Chief Minister on 16 March at Khatkar Kalan, the ancestral village of freedom fighter Bhagat Singh, forming the party's first government outside the national capital ([Bhaskar English](https://www.bhaskarenglish.in/national/news/aap-journey-from-india-against-corruption-movement-to-national-party-status-delhi-assembly-elections-arvind-kejriwal-134412594.html); [Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/elections/punjab-assembly-election/2022-punjab-election-aap-s-bhagwant-mann-to-be-sworn-in-as-cm-on-march-16-101646995227774.html)). Later in 2022 it won five Gujarat Assembly seats and emerged as the single largest party in the [Municipal Corporation of Delhi](/organizations/municipal-corporation-of-delhi/) elections, ending 15 years of BJP rule in the civic body ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/?p=1503142865)). On 10 April 2023 the Election Commission granted AAP national-party status based on its electoral performances in Delhi, [Goa](/places/goa/), Punjab and Gujarat, while withdrawing national status from the Trinamool Congress, the Nationalist Congress Party and the Communist Party of India ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/news/politics/election-commission-grants-national-party-status-to-aap-trinamool-ncp-cpi-lose-tag-10391101.html)). In Parliament, the party's small bloc still made tactical waves: in the August 2018 [Deputy Chairman election](/events/2020-deputy-chairman-rajya-sabha-election/), Kejriwal ordered a boycott while party leader Sanjay Singh publicly questioned the opposition's approach to building support ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/news/asia/india/nda-s-harivansh-narayan-singh-elected-as-rajya-sabha-deputy-chairman-1.2264029)).

## Legal troubles and the 2025 Delhi defeat

The excise-policy case engulfed the party's leadership from 2023. The Enforcement Directorate arrested Kejriwal on 21 March 2024 in a money-laundering probe into alleged irregularities in Delhi's now-cancelled excise policy; he received interim bail on 10 May to campaign in the Lok Sabha election, surrendered on 2 June, and was arrested by the Central Bureau of Investigation on 26 June in a linked corruption case ([ThePrint (PTI)](https://theprint.in/india/sc-grants-bail-to-kejriwal-in-excise-policy-corruption-case/2265865/)). On 13 September 2024 the Supreme Court granted him bail in the CBI case, saying prolonged incarceration amounts to unjust deprivation of liberty; one judge questioned the timing of the CBI arrest and said the agency must dispel the notion of being a "caged parrot" ([ThePrint (PTI)](https://theprint.in/india/sc-grants-bail-to-kejriwal-in-excise-policy-corruption-case/2265865/)). Four days later Kejriwal resigned as Delhi Chief Minister, saying he would return only with a "certificate of honesty" from voters; Atishi, who had held 14 portfolios including finance and education while he was jailed, succeeded him as Delhi's third woman Chief Minister ([CNBC TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/india/politics/aap-leader-arvind-kejriwal-resigns-as-delhi-chief-minister-19477797.htm)).

The voters' answer came on 8 February 2025. The BJP won 48 of the 70 Delhi seats, AAP fell to 22, and Congress drew a blank for a third straight time; Kejriwal lost his New Delhi seat to the BJP's Parvesh Verma by 4,089 votes, though outgoing Chief Minister Atishi held Kalkaji by 3,521 ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/elections/delhi-assembly/delhi-election-result-2025-live-updates-bjp-aap-congress/article69193873.ece/amp/)). The headline seat rout hid a close vote: AAP's vote share was 43.57% against the BJP's 45.56%, down from 53.57% in 2020 and 54.5% in 2015 — a gap of under two points that still translated into a 48–22 seat split, showing how first-past-the-post amplifies small swings ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/elections/delhi-assembly/delhi-election-result-2025-live-updates-bjp-aap-congress/article69193873.ece/amp/)). The same data showed AAP holding 48.5% of the vote in the poorest constituencies against the BJP's 52.6% in the richest, suggesting the party's welfare coalition endured at the bottom even as it lost the middle ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/elections/delhi-assembly/delhi-election-result-2025-live-updates-bjp-aap-congress/article69193873.ece/amp/)). In Punjab, by contrast, the party held on: in March 2026 Mann marked four years of AAP government, claiming its pre-poll guarantees — 300 units of free electricity, daytime farm power, 881 Aam Aadmi Clinics, 65,000 government jobs — had been fulfilled ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/chandigarh/punjab-cm-bhagwant-mann-aap-government-four-years-achievements-10585717/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was the Aam Aadmi Party founded?**
The AAP was formally formed on 26 November 2012, after Arvind Kejriwal and his allies split from Anna Hazare's India Against Corruption movement over entering electoral politics. It grew out of the 2011 campaign for a stronger Jan Lokpal Bill.

**What is the Delhi Model of Governance?**
The Delhi Model, as the party describes it, rests on free electricity and water with a cap, free bus travel for women, and investment in government schools. Critics called the schemes giveaways; the party defended them as welfare for citizens.

**How did AAP become a national party?**
On 10 April 2023 the Election Commission recognised AAP as a national party based on its electoral performances in Delhi, Goa, Punjab and Gujarat. At the same time it withdrew national status from the Trinamool Congress, the NCP and the CPI.

**Why did AAP lose Delhi in 2025?**
The BJP won 48 of 70 seats to AAP's 22, and Kejriwal lost his own New Delhi seat by 4,089 votes. The vote gap was narrow — 45.56% to 43.57% — but first-past-the-post turned it into a rout, and the defeat followed the excise-policy arrests of the party's top leaders.

**What happened to Arvind Kejriwal in the excise-policy case?**
The Enforcement Directorate arrested him on 21 March 2024; the CBI arrested him on 26 June in a linked case. The Supreme Court granted him bail on 13 September 2024. He resigned as Chief Minister on 17 September, and the cases were still pending in the sources read.
