## Overview

The Hollywood Reporter (THR) is an American entertainment trade publication founded in 1930 by William R. "Billy" Wilkerson as Hollywood's first daily entertainment trade newspaper ([USC Libraries](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/c8765nrc)). Today it is a weekly print magazine and continuously updated website covering film, television and the entertainment business in the [United States](/places/united-states/), headquartered in Los Angeles and operated by [Penske Media Corporation](/organizations/penske-media-corporation/) through the PMRC joint venture, with Billboard as its sister publication ([BAFTA](https://www.bafta.org/partners/the-hollywood-reporter/); [Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)).

That shape — one of the two great Hollywood trades, now sharing an owner with its rival — is the product of ninety years of reinvention. THR spent its first eight decades as a daily newspaper, was rebuilt as a glossy weekly in 2010, and passed through Nielsen, Prometheus Global Media, Guggenheim Partners, Todd Boehly's Eldridge Industries, Valence Media and [MRC](/organizations/mrc/) before landing in the PMRC venture that also holds [Variety](/organizations/variety/) ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/variety-parent-penske-media-to-take-over-hollywood-reporter-billboard-in-joint-venture-with-mrc/)). Its founder's role in the Hollywood blacklist, publicly apologised for by his son in 2012, remains part of the record ([NPR](https://www.mtpr.org/2012-11-25/hollywood-paper-apologizes-for-47-blacklist-support?_amp=true)).

## Founding and the Wilkerson era

Wilkerson founded THR in 1930 as the industry's first daily entertainment trade newspaper, and he ran it with a deliberately provocative edge ([USC Libraries](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/c8765nrc)). The University of Southern California's archive notes that he "often used caustic articles and gossip to generate publicity and got noticed by the studio bosses in New York", and that some studio lots tried to ban the paper ([USC Libraries](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/c8765nrc)). His front-page "Tradeviews" column was his personal instrument for settling scores with the studio system.

From the late 1930s, Wilkerson used the publication to push the view that the industry was a communist stronghold, opposing the Screen Writers Guild, which he called the "Red Beachhead" ([USC Libraries](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/c8765nrc)). His column carried the first industry names allegedly associated with communism, including Dalton Trumbo and Howard Koch, on what became the Hollywood blacklist, also known as "Billy's list" ([USC Libraries](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/c8765nrc)). Wilkerson died in 1962, and fifty years later his son published a formal apology in THR, telling NPR his father had "lit the match" ([NPR](https://www.mtpr.org/2012-11-25/hollywood-paper-apologizes-for-47-blacklist-support?_amp=true)).

## The 2010 reinvention: from daily to glossy weekly

By 2009 the daily was in visible decline. THR sat inside Nielsen's Business Media division, up for sale since mid-2008 with 41 other trade papers; by November 2009 it had suffered "huge fall-offs in revenue, readership and even online followers" ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/hollywood-reporter-set-be-sold-whos-whos-finkelstein-9945/)). Nielsen itself had been bought in 2006 by six private equity firms ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/hollywood-reporter-set-be-sold-whos-whos-finkelstein-9945/)). By March 2011 the parent was Prometheus Global Media, led by Richard Beckman ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/performance-marketing/hollywood-reporter-prometheus-global-media-richard-beckman-new-york-post-denial/)).

Beckman recruited Janice Min, formerly editor-in-chief of Us Weekly, to rebuild the paper; Min later said of the old daily: "The only thing we kept was the name" ([LA Weekly](https://www.laweekly.com/janice-min-the-hollywood-reporters-editor/)). The New York Times reported in September 2010 that THR would become a weekly magazine, its daily print edition replaced by a daily PDF ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2010/hollywood-reporter-to-become-a-weekly-magazine/)). Min built a photo and art department, relaunched the website, and added coverage of the industry's social life ([LA Weekly](https://www.laweekly.com/janice-min-the-hollywood-reporters-editor/)). The new THR launched in November 2010, and by February 2011 its website drew 3.5 million unique visitors, up 868 percent year over year per comScore — proof the reinvention had worked ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/performance-marketing/hollywood-reporter-prometheus-global-media-richard-beckman-new-york-post-denial/)).

## Ownership carousel: Guggenheim, Eldridge, Valence and PMRC

The relaunch fixed the product but not the ownership. In 2015, Guggenheim Partners spun off THR, Billboard, Adweek, [Dick Clark Productions](/organizations/dick-clark-productions/) and Mediabistro to its president Todd Boehly, who took a controlling interest ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/variety-parent-penske-media-to-take-over-hollywood-reporter-billboard-in-joint-venture-with-mrc/)). In February 2018 Boehly's Eldridge Industries folded the Billboard-Hollywood Reporter Media Group, Media Rights Capital and Dick Clark Productions into Valence Media, led by MRC co-founders [Asif Satchu](/people/asif-satchu/) and [Modi Wiczyk](/people/modi-wiczyk/), with Boehly as chairman ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/313954/thr-billboard-owner-consolidates-properties-i.html)). Valence was rebranded as MRC in 2020 ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/variety-parent-penske-media-to-take-over-hollywood-reporter-billboard-in-joint-venture-with-mrc/)).

The losses behind the churn were severe: in April 2020 the parent, still called Valence Media, cut staff to save $9–10 million in headcount costs, with losses estimated at about $18 million a year ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/variety-parent-penske-media-to-take-over-hollywood-reporter-billboard-in-joint-venture-with-mrc/)). That September, Penske Media Corporation and MRC announced PMRC: PMC took day-to-day operations of THR, Billboard and [Vibe](/organizations/vibe/), while MRC developed film, TV and live-event projects from the brands' stories and IP ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/356161/penske-media-mrc-to-jointly-own-magazines-includ.html)). In August 2022, MRC separated its media assets from Eldridge; Eldridge kept Dick Clark Productions and the Penske Media joint venture that includes THR, Billboard and Variety ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/mrc-eldridge-todd-boehly-pmrc-reorg/)).

## THR and Billboard: sister publications

THR and Billboard have travelled together for most of the last two decades. Both sat in Nielsen's Business Media division and were put up for sale together in 2008–2009 ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/hollywood-reporter-set-be-sold-whos-whos-finkelstein-9945/)). Both were rebuilt under Prometheus — THR as a glossy weekly, Billboard with ad revenue up 48 percent in 2010 — and both moved together through the Guggenheim spin-off, the Valence merger and the PMRC venture ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/performance-marketing/hollywood-reporter-prometheus-global-media-richard-beckman-new-york-post-denial/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/313954/thr-billboard-owner-consolidates-properties-i.html)). The two brands were formally grouped as the Billboard-Hollywood Reporter Media Group, whose president John Amato became CEO of the division inside Valence ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/313954/thr-billboard-owner-consolidates-properties-i.html)).

The sister relationship is commercial as well as corporate. Beckman's March 2011 email announced the first televised [Billboard Music Awards](/events/billboard-music-awards/) in partnership with ABC, on 22 May 2011, as part of the same growth programme that revived THR's website ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/performance-marketing/hollywood-reporter-prometheus-global-media-richard-beckman-new-york-post-denial/)). Under PMRC the pairing deepened: MRC's arm of the venture developed film, television and live-event projects from all six PMRC brands' stories and intellectual property, with Dick Clark Productions — producer of the Billboard Music Awards and the American Music Awards — as its production engine ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/356161/penske-media-mrc-to-jointly-own-magazines-includ.html)).

## What THR publishes today

THR today is a weekly magazine and a continuously updated website covering film, television and the entertainment business, including its intersections with fashion, finance, law, technology, lifestyle and politics ([BAFTA](https://www.bafta.org/partners/the-hollywood-reporter/)). Beyond news and reviews, it runs the industry's ranking and networking machinery: its annual Power 100 Women in Entertainment breakfast, held in Los Angeles on 5 December 2018, celebrated the women behind the year's most influential moments ([SAG-AFTRA magazine](http://digital.copcomm.com/i/1114369-spring-2019/22)). The same programme runs a mentorship scheme, with Big Brothers Big Sisters of Greater Los Angeles, pairing girls with women working in the industry ([SAG-AFTRA magazine](http://digital.copcomm.com/i/1114369-spring-2019/22)).

The top job has turned over repeatedly in recent years. Maer Roshan, formerly editor-in-chief of Los Angeles Magazine, was hired as co-editor-in-chief in August 2023 alongside Nekesa Mumbi Moody, with Penske Media chairman [Jay Penske](/people/jay-penske/) calling the pair a "powerful duo" ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/maer-roshan-named-the-hollywood-reporter-co-editor-in-chief/)). Moody left in November 2024; her replacement Shirley Halperin departed for [Rolling Stone](/organizations/rolling-stone/) in July 2025, leaving Roshan as sole editor-in-chief ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/shirley-halperin-exits-hollywood-reporter-rolling-stone-maer-roshan-eic/)). The churn mirrors the wider trade-press squeeze: TheWrap reported years of rolling layoffs, including the summer-2024 exit of longtime television editor Lesley Goldberg ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/shirley-halperin-exits-hollywood-reporter-rolling-stone-maer-roshan-eic/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who founded The Hollywood Reporter?**
William R. "Billy" Wilkerson founded THR in 1930 as the industry's first daily entertainment trade newspaper, according to the University of Southern California's archive of the paper's bound volumes ([USC Libraries](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/c8765nrc)). He was its owner, publisher and editor during the blacklist years, and died in 1962.

**Who owns The Hollywood Reporter now?**
THR is operated by Penske Media Corporation through PMRC, a joint venture with MRC announced in September 2020, with PMC running day-to-day operations ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)). After the 2022 Eldridge–MRC split, Eldridge retained the Penske Media joint venture, which also holds Variety and Billboard ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/mrc-eldridge-todd-boehly-pmrc-reorg/)).

**How is The Hollywood Reporter related to Billboard?**
They are sister publications that have shared owners since the Nielsen era, travelling together through Prometheus, Guggenheim, Eldridge, Valence and PMRC ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/313954/thr-billboard-owner-consolidates-properties-i.html)). Both now sit in the PMRC joint venture, and Dick Clark Productions turns their brands into live events ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)).

**When did THR stop being a daily newspaper?**
In November 2010, under Prometheus Global Media, THR relaunched as a glossy weekly magazine with a revamped website, replacing the daily print edition with a daily PDF ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2010/hollywood-reporter-to-become-a-weekly-magazine/)). Janice Min, recruited from Us Weekly by CEO Richard Beckman, led the reinvention ([LA Weekly](https://www.laweekly.com/janice-min-the-hollywood-reporters-editor/)).

**What was THR's role in the Hollywood blacklist?**
Founder Billy Wilkerson's "Tradeviews" column named film workers allegedly linked to communism, including Dalton Trumbo and Howard Koch, on what became the blacklist ([USC Libraries](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/c8765nrc)). In 2012 his son published a formal apology in THR, saying his father had "lit the match" ([NPR](https://www.mtpr.org/2012-11-25/hollywood-paper-apologizes-for-47-blacklist-support?_amp=true)).
