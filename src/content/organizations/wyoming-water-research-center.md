## The institute that was shut down

For thirty-three years, Wyoming's share of America's federal water-research system ran through one unit at the University of Wyoming: the Wyoming Water Resources Center (WWRC), the designated state participant in the National Institutes for Water Resources program since its 1965 inception. In 1998, the center was closed.

The aftermath was close to a worst case. In late 1999 a replacement — the Wyoming Water Research Program (WRP) — was initiated to coordinate Wyoming's NIWR participation, but it was, in UW's own words, "completely reformulated compared to the State's NIWR program as it existed previously, since 1964, within the Wyoming Water Resources Center." That same fall, Wyoming's NIWR program was placed on probationary status after a mandated five-year federal evaluation covering 1992–1997, putting its annual funding eligibility at risk.

The rebuild was deliberately lean: director Larry Pochop (professor of civil engineering), appointed in October 1999, housed the program in the Civil and Architectural Engineering department reporting to the Vice President for Research, and employed students for administrative work rather than creating permanent admin posts — minimizing overhead while maximizing research and training funds. A Priority and Selection Committee drawn from state and federal water agencies solicited research needs, selected projects and monitored progress; faculty competed for funding through peer-reviewed proposals approved by NIWR. A strategic plan was submitted to the USGS in June 2000, an implementation report that December, and in March 2001 Wyoming received notice of removal from probation. Legislative action beginning July 2002 then created the Office of Water Programs to administer the WRP and provide UW's advisor to the Wyoming Water Development Commission.

The WRP's early research portfolio (through February 2003) shows what the reformulated program funded: hydrologic impacts of irrigation efficiency, erosion-potential modeling, septic-system fate in the field, drought prediction, a Wyoming Climate Atlas, and real-time E. coli monitoring in surface waters.

The closed center's paper trail survives in the University of Wyoming Archives: the Wyoming Water Resources Center Records, cataloged through the Orbis Cascade Alliance's Archives West service, hold water-resources research-institute files on groundwater allocations, instream flows and snow hydrology from the 1960s onward, subject files ranging from acid deposition to the Ogallala Aquifer, and administrative records from the 1960s through 1998 — including files on the center's creation and its closure.
