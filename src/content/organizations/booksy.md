## Overview

Booksy is a beauty and wellness appointment-booking platform founded in 2014 by Polish entrepreneurs Stefan Batory (CEO) and Konrad Howard, launched first in Poland and later headquartered in San Francisco ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)). It works as a two-sided product: consumers use the app to find and book appointments with local salons, barbers, nail bars and spas, while businesses run bookings, payments and customer management through the accompanying Booksy Biz app — a software-as-a-service toolkit rather than a pure marketplace ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/), [tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2017/03/03/booksy-series-a/)). By January 2021 it operated in the US — its largest market — the UK, Poland, Spain, Brazil and South Africa, with a reported 13 million consumers on the app ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)).

## Serial founders from Poland's taxi wars

Booksy's founders were not first-timers. Before Booksy, Batory and Howard built iTaxi into the number-one taxi-hailing app in Poland, and earlier still Batory had taken his first software company, Eo Networks, public on the local stock exchange ([Growth Business](https://growthbusiness.co.uk/booksy-raises-3-4m-globalise-mobile-first-bookings-platform-15582/), [tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2017/03/03/booksy-series-a/)). That repeat-founder background mattered: the pair had already won a brutal mobile-first, two-sided market in Poland before turning to beauty — a sector where, Batory argued, more than 90% of appointments were still being processed by phone and email, producing no-shows and wasted back-and-forth ([Growth Business](https://growthbusiness.co.uk/booksy-raises-3-4m-globalise-mobile-first-bookings-platform-15582/)).

## Merchant-first SaaS against the marketplace model

The structural idea that differentiated Booksy from rivals like Treatwell was its business model. Online booking marketplaces typically drive traffic to their own sites and charge merchants a fee per booking generated; Booksy instead charged businesses a transparent monthly subscription sized to the business, letting merchants keep control of their customer relationships — the "merchant-first approach" ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2017/03/03/booksy-series-a/), [Growth Business](https://growthbusiness.co.uk/booksy-raises-3-4m-globalise-mobile-first-bookings-platform-15582/)). The toolkit covered calendars, marketing automation, CRM, inventory, point-of-sale, staff commissions and reporting, with in-app payments added later ([Growth Business](https://growthbusiness.co.uk/booksy-raises-3-4m-globalise-mobile-first-bookings-platform-15582/)). Investors bought the thesis across four rounds in six years: an $800,000 round in 2015 (Müller Medien, Piton Capital, Inovo), a $4.2 million Series A in 2017 led by OpenOcean, a $13.2 million Series B in 2018 led by Piton, and a $28.5 million round in September 2019 co-led by Piton, Enern, Industry Ventures and XG Ventures ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2021/01/29/booksy-slots-in-70m/)).

## The acquisition rollup and the $70M war chest

Booksy grew internationally by buying local competitors rather than just out-marketing them. It acquired its Polish rival Lavito in 2018, and in December 2020 it merged with Versum — another leading Polish competitor — a deal that carried Booksy into Mexico ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)). TechCrunch's Mike Butcher noted that Booksy had "cracked the international expansion game ahead of most competitors," which tended to stay local — naming Treatwell, Styleseat, Vagaro and Mindbody as the locally-rooted rivals — and framed the next phase as using its funding "to roll-up other local players" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)).

The January 2021 $70 million Series C, led by Cat Rock Capital with Sprints Capital participating alongside OpenOcean, Piton, VNV Global, Enern and angels including Kai Hansen and Zach Coelius, took total capital raised to $119 million and was explicitly earmarked for North American expansion, new verticals and further acquisitions ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)). Even amid pandemic lockdowns battering the beauty industry, the round closed on the back of what the company reported as a 1,500% revenue increase over the prior three years ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)).

## The numbers the company pitched

Booksy's investor pitch rested on operational metrics it reported to the press. The company told TechCrunch that 38% of its customers ended up booking after hours and that appointment frequency rose 20% once a business adopted the app — while its launch announcement claimed service providers could save the equivalent of 12 working days a year on bookings, payments and sales management ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)). These are Booksy's own figures as reported by the press, not independently audited numbers, but they were the empirical core of the "merchant-first" argument: more bookings outside working hours, more visits per customer, less time on the phone ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)). Batory had argued from the start that the addressable market was enormous — a global beauty services industry worth more than $150 billion, with 4 to 6 million outlets — and that over 90% of appointments were still being processed through traditional routes like phone and email ([Growth Business](https://growthbusiness.co.uk/booksy-raises-3-4m-globalise-mobile-first-bookings-platform-15582/)).
