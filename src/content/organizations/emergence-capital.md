## Overview

Emergence Capital is an early-stage venture capital firm based in [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/), California, in the [United States](/places/united-states/), that invests almost exclusively in enterprise software companies. Founded in 2003, it was the first venture capital firm committed to the thesis that enterprise software would move to the cloud ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2015/02/11/1130357/0/en/Emergence-Capital-Leading-Enterprise-Cloud-Venture-Capital-Firm-Raises-Oversubscribed-335-Million-Fourth-Fund.html)). That bet produced a strong early-stage track record: early investments in [Salesforce](/organizations/salesforce/), Veeva Systems, Box, Yammer and Zoom, followed by later bets on companies including [Blend](/organizations/blend/), Gusto and Bill.com. The firm makes only five to seven new investments a year and holds its winners long after they go public ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2018/05/21/1509306/0/en/Emergence-Capital-Announces-New-435m-Fund-Dedicated-to-Early-Investments-in-Companies-That-Change-the-Way-the-World-Works.html)).

Each fund ran roughly a third larger than the last; the 2021 pair — nearly $1 billion at once — broke two decades of deliberate restraint.

## Founding and the enterprise-cloud thesis

Emergence was founded in 2003, when enterprise software still overwhelmingly ran on customers' own servers and the idea that businesses would rent core applications over the internet was, in the venture community's view, "completely misunderstood," in the firm's own telling ([Emergence Capital](https://www.emcap.com/thoughts/why-we-only-raised-a-usd250-million-fund)). Jason Green and Gordon Ritter are named as co-founders in the firm's fund announcements, with Brian Jacobs and Kevin Spain as fellow general partners of the early funds ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2015/02/11/1130357/0/en/Emergence-Capital-Leading-Enterprise-Cloud-Venture-Capital-Firm-Raises-Oversubscribed-335-Million-Fourth-Fund.html)). The founders set up in San Mateo rather than on Sand Hill Road, to be closer to the software community they wanted to back ([Emergence Capital](https://www.emcap.com/thoughts/why-we-only-raised-a-usd250-million-fund)). The firm's 2015 announcement states the founding claim plainly: Emergence was "the first venture capital firm committed to the thesis that enterprise software would move to the cloud" ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2015/02/11/1130357/0/en/Emergence-Capital-Leading-Enterprise-Cloud-Venture-Capital-Firm-Raises-Oversubscribed-335-Million-Fourth-Fund.html)).

That thesis was not a slogan. The firm's first investments were in companies selling software as a service to businesses — Salesforce, SuccessFactors, Yammer — at a time when most venture firms still treated enterprise software as a niche. By 2015, the companies Emergence had funded were collectively worth more than $50 billion, and by 2018 nine of its early-stage investments had grown past $1 billion in value each ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2015/02/11/1130357/0/en/Emergence-Capital-Leading-Enterprise-Cloud-Venture-Capital-Firm-Raises-Oversubscribed-335-Million-Fourth-Fund.html); [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2018/05/21/1509306/0/en/Emergence-Capital-Announces-New-435m-Fund-Dedicated-to-Early-Investments-in-Companies-That-Change-the-Way-the-World-Works.html)).

## Fund-by-fund growth

Emergence's fundraising history is unusually disciplined for a firm with its track record. Its second fund totalled $200 million, backed by limited partners including CalPERS and the University of Michigan ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/emergence-finishes-raising-second-fund-at-200m)). For its third fund, raised in 2012, the firm capped itself at $250 million and explained why in public: focus. "We think everyone should have capital constraints," the firm wrote. "It forces us to be clear about our priorities and truly selective about the investments we make" ([Emergence Capital](https://www.emcap.com/thoughts/why-we-only-raised-a-usd250-million-fund); [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2015/02/11/1130357/0/en/Emergence-Capital-Leading-Enterprise-Cloud-Venture-Capital-Firm-Raises-Oversubscribed-335-Million-Fourth-Fund.html)). Fund IV, closed in 2015, was immediately oversubscribed and capped at $335 million, taking the firm to nearly $1 billion under management across four funds ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2015/02/11/1130357/0/en/Emergence-Capital-Leading-Enterprise-Cloud-Venture-Capital-Firm-Raises-Oversubscribed-335-Million-Fourth-Fund.html)).

Then, in 2021, the pattern broke: Emergence closed its sixth early-stage fund at $575 million and, for the first time, raised an opportunity fund alongside it, closing that with $375 million — nearly $1 billion in a single round ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/05/05/emergence-known-for-its-enterprise-bets-just-rounded-up-nearly-1-billion-across-two-new-funds/)). [TechCrunch](/organizations/techcrunch/) called it "a significant shift away from standard operating procedure" for a firm that had until then "maintained a conservative approach when it comes to fundraising" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/05/05/emergence-known-for-its-enterprise-bets-just-rounded-up-nearly-1-billion-across-two-new-funds/)). The raise also marked a generational handover: co-founder Jason Green stepped back, and the new funds were to be managed by co-founder Gordon Ritter and general partners Kevin Spain, Santi Subotovsky, Joe Floyd and Jake Saper — each of whom had joined Emergence as a junior investment professional — alongside principal Carlotta "Lotti" Siniscalco, who joined in 2018 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/05/05/emergence-known-for-its-enterprise-bets-just-rounded-up-nearly-1-billion-across-two-new-funds/)).

## The portfolio: early bets that defined the firm

Three investments explain Emergence's reputation better than any fund size. The first was Salesforce. Salesforce went public in 2004 at about $1 billion; by July 2020 it was worth about $170 billion on $13 billion of revenue — and Emergence had held on rather than distributing its shares at the IPO ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/07/15/emergences-jason-green-thinks-some-of-the-tech-backlash-is-justified-but-the-b2b-opportunities-still-outweigh-the-challenges/)). The second was Veeva Systems. Emergence was Veeva's only institutional investor, Veeva chief executive Peter Gassner said, and Gordon Ritter was still chairman of Veeva's board five years after its IPO ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2015/02/11/1130357/0/en/Emergence-Capital-Leading-Enterprise-Cloud-Venture-Capital-Firm-Raises-Oversubscribed-335-Million-Fourth-Fund.html); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/07/15/emergences-jason-green-thinks-some-of-the-tech-backlash-is-justified-but-the-b2b-opportunities-still-outweigh-the-challenges/)). The third was Zoom. Emergence led Zoom's $30 million Series C in 2015, and Zoom founder Eric Yuan later called Emergence "our first Silicon Valley-based institutional investor", crediting it with helping Zoom grow "from a small startup to over 1,000 employees" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/05/05/emergence-known-for-its-enterprise-bets-just-rounded-up-nearly-1-billion-across-two-new-funds/); [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2018/05/21/1509306/0/en/Emergence-Capital-Announces-New-435m-Fund-Dedicated-to-Early-Investments-in-Companies-That-Change-the-Way-the-World-Works.html)).

The pattern repeated across the portfolio. Emergence made the first venture capital investment in ServiceMax, the field-service software company, in 2008; GE Digital bought ServiceMax for $1 billion in 2016 ([Diginomica](https://diginomica.com/emergence-capitals-jason-green-on-todays-big-growth-sectors)). Its other early bets included Box, Yammer (acquired by Microsoft), SuccessFactors (acquired by SAP), Intacct (acquired by Sage), SteelBrick, SalesLoft and the messaging platform Zinc, formerly Cotap ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2015/02/11/1130357/0/en/Emergence-Capital-Leading-Enterprise-Cloud-Venture-Capital-Firm-Raises-Oversubscribed-335-Million-Fourth-Fund.html); [Diginomica](https://diginomica.com/emergence-capitals-jason-green-on-todays-big-growth-sectors); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/07/15/emergences-jason-green-thinks-some-of-the-tech-backlash-is-justified-but-the-b2b-opportunities-still-outweigh-the-challenges/)). What links them is a customer, not a sector label: each sold software that was a must-have part of somebody's working day. "We'd be better off investing in companies where the technology, the software, was an important part of your job," Green said in 2020 — "it's not a nice-to-have, it's a must-have" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/07/15/emergences-jason-green-thinks-some-of-the-tech-backlash-is-justified-but-the-b2b-opportunities-still-outweigh-the-challenges/)).

## The Blend connection

Blend, the cloud-based lending software provider, is one of Emergence's later enterprise bets and the reason this profile sits in the FactsLedger entity graph. Emergence named Blend among its investments when it announced Fund V in 2018, and it was already a backer before 2019, alongside Greylock, [8VC](/organizations/8vc/), [Lightspeed Venture Partners](/organizations/lightspeed-venture-partners/), [Founders Fund](/organizations/founders-fund/), [Formation 8](/organizations/formation-8/), Nyca Partners, Andreessen Horowitz and Peter Thiel ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2018/05/21/1509306/0/en/Emergence-Capital-Announces-New-435m-Fund-Dedicated-to-Early-Investments-in-Companies-That-Change-the-Way-the-World-Works.html); [Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/blend-labs-lands-on-nyse-in-360m-ipo/)). When Blend raised its $75 million Series F in August 2020, Emergence participated alongside Canapi Ventures, [Temasek](/organizations/temasek/), General Atlantic, 8VC and [Greylock Partners](/organizations/greylock/), at a valuation of $1.7 billion ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/blend-labs-lands-on-nyse-in-360m-ipo/)). Blend floated on the New York Stock Exchange in July 2021 in a $360 million IPO ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/blend-labs-lands-on-nyse-in-360m-ipo/)). The investment fits the firm's thesis: cloud software reworking a core business process — how lenders process loans.

## Thesis evolution: from industry cloud to AI coaching networks

Emergence's investment thesis has moved in stages, each one a refinement of the original cloud bet rather than a replacement. The first stage was software as a service itself. The second was the industry cloud: software tailored to one vertical market, pioneered by Veeva in life sciences, which Green said the firm saw as repeatable in fields as diverse as real estate, automotive and travel ([Diginomica](https://diginomica.com/emergence-capitals-jason-green-on-todays-big-growth-sectors)). The third was the deskless workforce — technology for field and remote workers who do not sit at desks — a thesis that grew directly out of the ServiceMax experience, on the logic that field workers outnumber desk workers ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2018/05/21/1509306/0/en/Emergence-Capital-Announces-New-435m-Fund-Dedicated-to-Early-Investments-in-Companies-That-Change-the-Way-the-World-Works.html); [Diginomica](https://diginomica.com/emergence-capitals-jason-green-on-todays-big-growth-sectors)).

The fourth stage, announced with Fund V in 2018, was Coaching Networks: companies that use machine learning to understand how the most productive workers behave and help the whole network learn from them, with early investments including Textio, Chorus.ai, Mya, Guru and Drishti ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2018/05/21/1509306/0/en/Emergence-Capital-Announces-New-435m-Fund-Dedicated-to-Early-Investments-in-Companies-That-Change-the-Way-the-World-Works.html)). By 2025, the firm's stated conviction areas, as described by general partner Joe Floyd to the NVCA, were Industry Cloud, Deskless Workforce, Deep Collaboration and AI Coaching Networks, with recent bets including Together.AI, StackBlitz (creators of Bolt.new) and Arcee.AI ([NVCA](https://nvca.org/member-spotlight-emergence-capital/)).

## How Emergence invests: small, hands-on, long holds

Emergence's method is as distinctive as its thesis. It makes only a handful of investments a year — five to seven on average — so the partners can work closely with each founding team, including on recruiting and go-to-market strategy ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2018/05/21/1509306/0/en/Emergence-Capital-Announces-New-435m-Fund-Dedicated-to-Early-Investments-in-Companies-That-Change-the-Way-the-World-Works.html); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/07/15/emergences-jason-green-thinks-some-of-the-tech-backlash-is-justified-but-the-b2b-opportunities-still-outweigh-the-challenges/)). Sourcing has shifted from warm introductions to a "data-driven, thesis-driven outbound" model, and about a third of its investments historically sit outside the Bay Area ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/05/05/emergence-known-for-its-enterprise-bets-just-rounded-up-nearly-1-billion-across-two-new-funds/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/07/15/emergences-jason-green-thinks-some-of-the-tech-backlash-is-justified-but-the-b2b-opportunities-still-outweigh-the-challenges/)).

The most unusual part is what happens after a portfolio company goes public. The old venture convention was to distribute shares to limited partners at the IPO and move on; Emergence decided to hold significant positions and board seats for as long as it felt it could add value — in some cases, in Floyd's words to the NVCA, "for decades" ([NVCA](https://nvca.org/member-spotlight-emergence-capital/)). Green's reasoning was arithmetic as much as loyalty: had the firm distributed its Salesforce shares at the 2004 IPO, investors would have missed the roughly 170-fold rise that followed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/07/15/emergences-jason-green-thinks-some-of-the-tech-backlash-is-justified-but-the-b2b-opportunities-still-outweigh-the-challenges/)). It is a strategy only a firm with a small portfolio can run.

The context is a funding market that moved away from Emergence's end of it: PwC MoneyTree data cited by the firm in 2018 showed that since 2008, 80 percent of the increase in US software investment had gone to Series B rounds and beyond, leaving early-stage funding comparatively scarce ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2018/05/21/1509306/0/en/Emergence-Capital-Announces-New-435m-Fund-Dedicated-to-Early-Investments-in-Companies-That-Change-the-Way-the-World-Works.html)). That gap is the opening Emergence has occupied since 2003.

## What this profile does not claim

It does not pin down the date the firm moved its base from San Mateo to San Francisco; it states San Francisco as the current base on the NVCA's 2025 spotlight and the firm's own site footer, and San Mateo as the founding and early base on sources from 2015 through 2021 ([NVCA](https://nvca.org/member-spotlight-emergence-capital/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/05/05/emergence-known-for-its-enterprise-bets-just-rounded-up-nearly-1-billion-across-two-new-funds/)). It does not state the reported 300-fold return on the Veeva investment, which rests on a single second-hand report ([Diginomica](https://diginomica.com/emergence-capitals-jason-green-on-todays-big-growth-sectors)). It does not give a close year for Fund II or a total assets-under-management figure, because no fully-read source verified either ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/emergence-finishes-raising-second-fund-at-200m)).
