## Overview

Formation 8 was an American venture capital firm founded in 2011 by Joe Lonsdale, Brian Koo and Jim Kim, which raised two funds totalling $948 million, backed Oculus VR and [Blend](/organizations/blend/) early, and disbanded in November 2015 when its partners split over investment strategy ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/04/de-formation-8/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/04/18/formation-8/)). Its short life produced two fast venture exits, a hardware spinout, and three successor firms run by its own founders.

The firm's idea was geographic: plug Silicon Valley startups into Asian conglomerates that could become their biggest customers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/04/18/formation-8/)). Lonsdale, a [Palantir](/organizations/palantir/) co-founder, Koo, a scion of the family behind Korean tech giant LG, and Kim, a longtime venture investor, built the firm around that bridge, with local teams in Korea, China and Singapore ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/04/de-formation-8/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/04/18/formation-8/)). Fortune called it the "hottest" venture firm to emerge since [Andreessen Horowitz](/organizations/andreessen-horowitz/) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/01/16/joe-lonsdale-moves-forward-with-8vcs-new-fund-and-more/)). Four years later it was gone, yet its longest-lived bet, Blend, did not reach the public markets until 2021, six years after the firm itself had wound down ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1855747/000119312521194971/d162671ds1.htm)).

## The Asia-bridge thesis

Formation 8 wanted to return venture capital to what Lonsdale called its roots: backing solutions to hard technology problems that could change the world ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/04/18/formation-8/)). It hunted "smart enterprise" startups, companies using data to help enormous organisations in government, finance, healthcare and business services make sense of vast information flows, alongside energy companies that could transform the global energy value chain ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/04/18/formation-8/)). The pitch to founders was not just money but distribution: its network and local offices in Asia could win a portfolio company huge deals with conglomerates in the region ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/04/18/formation-8/)). Lonsdale put the logic bluntly, saying that a company trying to transform a big industry while ignoring Asia was "making a mistake" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/04/18/formation-8/)). The three founders' backgrounds were chosen to make that bridge credible: Lonsdale had worked at [PayPal](/organizations/paypal/) and co-founded Palantir, Koo came from Harbor Pacific Capital, and Kim from GE and Khosla Ventures, while [Stanford University](/universities/stanford-university/) classmate ties bound Lonsdale and Koo ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/04/18/formation-8/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/01/16/joe-lonsdale-moves-forward-with-8vcs-new-fund-and-more/)).

## Two funds, two fast exits

The first fund closed at $448 million in April 2013, after an initial $200 million close the previous year, with limited partners including founders of PayPal, Palantir, Yahoo and Yammer plus Asian conglomerates and New York institutions ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/04/18/formation-8/)). The odd number was deliberate, a play on the firm's name: four hundred for-ty 8 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/04/18/formation-8/)). Within eighteen months the fund had delivered two exits that made the firm's reputation: [Facebook](/organizations/facebook/) bought Oculus VR, whose early rounds Lonsdale and Koo had joined, for $2 billion, and [Salesforce](/organizations/salesforce/) bought RelateIQ for $390 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/12/03/formation-8-500-million-fund/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/01/16/joe-lonsdale-moves-forward-with-8vcs-new-fund-and-more/)). A second fund of $500 million closed in December 2014 to invest from seed to late stage, by which time the team had grown from eight people to about thirty, with staff in Seoul, Singapore, Shanghai and Beijing ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/12/03/formation-8-500-million-fund/)). By the wind-down the portfolio held more than 100 companies, including Wish, Radius, LearnSprout, Leeo and Yello Mobile, which alone made 61 mobile-startup acquisitions in 2014 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/04/de-formation-8/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/12/03/formation-8-500-million-fund/)).

## The Blend stake: the bet that outlived the firm

Formation 8's most durable investment was the one that took longest to pay off. When Blend filed to go public, its S-1 registration statement showed entities affiliated with Formation 8 holding 48,661,537 Class A shares, or 8.4% of the class before the offering, held of record by Formation8 Partners Fund I, L.P. ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1855747/000119312521194971/d162671ds1.htm)). The filing names the fund's managing members as James Kim, Brian Koo and Joe Lonsdale, the firm's three founders ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1855747/000119312521194971/d162671ds1.htm)). Formation 8 also sat among the holders of registration and voting-agreement rights alongside [Coatue](/organizations/coatue/), [General Atlantic](/organizations/general-atlantic/), [Greylock](/organizations/greylock/), [Lightspeed](/organizations/lightspeed-venture-partners/), Ossa and [Tiger Global](/organizations/tiger-global/) ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1855747/000119312521194971/d162671ds1.htm)). It was a first-fund position, held for roughly eight years, in a company founded in 2012 by [Nima Ghamsari](/people/nima-ghamsari/) to digitise mortgage and banking origination ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1855747/000119312521194971/d162671ds1.htm)). Blend completed its listing in July 2021, selling 20,000,000 shares at $18.00 each for $360 million, with underwriters holding a 30-day option on 3,000,000 more shares ([Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/news-and-events/news/2021/07/07_20-blend-completes-360-million-ipo)).

## The 2015 breakup

On 4 November 2015, TechCrunch reported that Formation 8 had sent portfolio companies a memo announcing it would not raise a third fund and was winding down ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/04/de-formation-8/)). The memo's own explanation was plain: the partners recognised they had "different investment styles" and were exploring different avenues in venture capital ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/04/de-formation-8/)). The split followed the firm's founding geography: Lonsdale and Kim planned to focus on investments in the [United States](/places/united-states/), while Koo would focus on Asia ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/04/de-formation-8/)). The memo promised founders the firm would keep managing current investments and follow-on rounds from "meaningful reserves" still held in Fund II, and that the partners would stay actively involved with their companies ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/04/de-formation-8/)). The firm confirmed the story on its own website ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/04/de-formation-8/)). Two earlier moves foreshadowed the split: a hardware fund raised in 2015 closed at $125 million as Eclipse Ventures Fund, and Reuters had reported in June 2015 that the firm was raising another $400 million for an Asia growth co-investment vehicle ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/04/de-formation-8/)).

## Three successor firms

The breakup scattered the team rather than ending its investing. Lonsdale's new firm, originally called 8 Partners and soon renamed [8VC](/organizations/8vc/), raised a debut fund of $425 million plus a separate $50 million seed fund, and fifteen of Formation 8's twenty-five employees moved across with him ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/01/16/joe-lonsdale-moves-forward-with-8vcs-new-fund-and-more/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/04/de-formation-8/)). Its mantra, "The world is broken; let's fix it," carried over Formation 8's taste for hard problems, including the "smart enterprise" thesis of using large data sets to solve non-linear problems in banking, healthcare and intelligence ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/01/16/joe-lonsdale-moves-forward-with-8vcs-new-fund-and-more/)). Koo founded Formation Group, which stayed closest to the original mission of bridging Asian ecosystems and Silicon Valley, while Kim co-founded Builders VC, built around getting sales and marketing staff involved early at nascent companies ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/01/16/joe-lonsdale-moves-forward-with-8vcs-new-fund-and-more/)). The diaspora shows how a four-year firm can shape a decade: its people, its thesis and even its portfolio, including the Color Genomics Series A it backed in spring 2015 and Lonsdale's first venture check, to Wish, all continued under new names ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/01/16/joe-lonsdale-moves-forward-with-8vcs-new-fund-and-more/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What was Formation 8?** Formation 8 was an American venture capital firm founded in 2011 by Joe Lonsdale, Brian Koo and Jim Kim to back industry-transforming startups and connect them with Asian conglomerates ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/04/18/formation-8/)). It raised two funds, $448 million and $500 million, before winding down in November 2015 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/04/de-formation-8/)).

**Why did Formation 8 shut down?** The partners' own memo said they had "different investment styles" and were exploring different avenues in venture capital, so they would not raise a third fund ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/04/de-formation-8/)). Lonsdale and Kim planned to focus on US investments while Koo focused on Asia ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/04/de-formation-8/)).

**What were Formation 8's biggest wins?** Facebook bought Oculus VR for $2 billion and Salesforce bought RelateIQ for $390 million, both in 2014, out of the first fund ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/12/03/formation-8-500-million-fund/)). Its longest-lived bet was Blend, in which its first fund still held 8.4% at the 2021 IPO ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1855747/000119312521194971/d162671ds1.htm)).

**What happened to the founders after Formation 8?** Lonsdale founded 8VC, taking fifteen of the firm's twenty-five employees, and its debut fund closed at $425 million plus a $50 million seed fund, while Koo founded Formation Group and Kim co-founded Builders VC ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/01/16/joe-lonsdale-moves-forward-with-8vcs-new-fund-and-more/)). The hardware team spun out separately as Eclipse Ventures ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/04/de-formation-8/)).
