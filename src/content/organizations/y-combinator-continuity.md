## Overview

Y Combinator Continuity was [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/)'s later-stage investment fund: a $700 million vehicle launched in October 2015 to keep backing YC alumni long after Demo Day, run by former Twitter chief operating officer Ali Rowghani ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/y-combinator-raises-700m-to-keep-funding-yc-startups-as-they-mature)). It invested in two ways — exercising YC's pro-rata rights in follow-on rounds and leading or joining growth rounds — and it was wound down in March 2023, when YC president Garry Tan called late-stage investing a distraction from the accelerator's core mission ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/changes-at-yc)).

The fund existed because YC's own success had created a problem. By 2015 the accelerator was about to fund its 1,000th company, and many of those companies were scaling years after graduation while still turning to YC for advice ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/yc-continuity-fund/)). The seed cheque bought YC a small stake and a relationship; it bought no say in what happened next. Continuity was the answer: a dedicated pool of capital that let YC stay a financing partner for its winners instead of watching them outgrow it. Its backers, according to the Wall Street Journal, included [Stanford University](/universities/stanford-university/) and Willett Advisors, the firm managing Michael Bloomberg's investments ([Inc](https://www.inc.com/business-insider/y-combinator-700-million-fund.html)).

## The model: pro rata first, growth bets second

Continuity invested capital in two distinct ways, and the distinction mattered. First, it exercised YC's pro-rata rights in follow-on rounds, starting at Series A — programmatically, in every YC company raising at a valuation below $300 million, and more selectively above that line ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/q-and-a-with-yc-continuitys-ali-rowghani)). Doing it for every company was deliberate: YC's president Sam Altman argued that picking only some companies would create signalling problems for the ones it skipped, and deprive them of additional advisers ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/yc-continuity-fund/)). Second, on a purely elective basis, the fund would consider leading or participating in later-stage growth rounds, taking board seats where it made sense for founders but not expecting to in every investment ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/q-and-a-with-yc-continuitys-ali-rowghani)).

Just as important was what Continuity would not do. It would invest only in companies that had been through the YC program — never outside the portfolio — and it would not lead seed or traditional Series A rounds, so as not to compete with the early-stage venture firms YC depended on ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/yc-continuity-fund/)). Rowghani, who had spent fifteen years at Pixar and Twitter learning to manage rapid growth and scale, said the idea had come from founders themselves, who wanted YC to remain a financing partner as they grew ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/q-and-a-with-yc-continuitys-ali-rowghani)). The pitch was that Continuity would be a founder-friendly, very long-term investor in a market where most players were not ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/yc-continuity-fund/)).

## Breaking its own rule: Convoy and the $1 billion question

For its first two years Continuity kept to its brief, but in July 2017 it broke its founding rule in public. It led a $62 million investment in Convoy, a Seattle trucking network — the first time YC had taken a stake in a later-stage company it had not previously funded ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/07/25/in-a-first-deal-of-its-kind-convoy-lands-62-million-led-by-ycs-continuity-fund/)). The round, which brought Convoy's total raised to $80 million, came out of a meet-and-greet event run by Greylock Partners and YC, where Convoy's founders met Rowghani and Anu Hariharan, who by then jointly led the fund ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/07/25/in-a-first-deal-of-its-kind-convoy-lands-62-million-led-by-ycs-continuity-fund/)). The deal showed what Continuity had become: not just a pro-rata machine, but a growth investor willing to pick winners outside its own alumni.

The same week, Axios reported that YC was raising up to $1 billion for a new vehicle that would go further — merging early- and late-stage investing into one fund and dropping the requirement that later-stage companies be YC alumni ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/07/21/y-combinator-is-reportedly-raising-a-1-billion-fund/)). YC had not confirmed the fundraising when the report ran. Five months later YC added a different kind of growth support: a free Growth Program for companies with 50 to 100 employees and strong product-market fit, run twice a year for fifteen companies at a time, taking no equity ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/12/14/y-combinator-is-zeroing-in-on-bigger-breakaway-companies-with-a-new-growth-stage-program/)). The program addressed a complaint YC kept hearing — that its network thinned out as companies grew, because so few ever reached growth stage ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/12/14/y-combinator-is-zeroing-in-on-bigger-breakaway-companies-with-a-new-growth-stage-program/)).

## The Brex bet

Continuity's best-known investment came in 2018, in a company that embodied the fund's purpose. [Brex](/organizations/brex/), founded in [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) in 2017 by [Henrique Dubugras](/people/henrique-dubugras/) and [Pedro Franceschi](/people/pedro-franceschi/), was a YC winter 2017 alumnus reimagining the corporate credit card for startups ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/brex-launches-credit-card-for-startups-raises-57-million-from-y-combinator-paypal-founders-others)). In 2018 it closed a $50 million Series B from Y Combinator Continuity, PayPal founders [Peter Thiel](/people/peter-thiel/) and Max Levchin, Yuri Milner, Ribbit Capital and former Visa chief executive Carl Pascarella, bringing its total funding to $57 million ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/brex-launches-credit-card-for-startups-raises-57-million-from-y-combinator-paypal-founders-others)). By its public launch Brex had already signed up some 1,000 customers, including Flexport, Algolia and [Affirm](/organizations/affirm/) ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/brex-launches-credit-card-for-startups-raises-57-million-from-y-combinator-paypal-founders-others)).

The round mattered to Continuity beyond the cheque: partner Anu Hariharan joined Brex's board of directors alongside Ribbit Capital's Meyer Malka ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/06/19/brex-picks-up-57m-to-build-an-easy-credit-card-for-startups/)). That was the elective, board-seat side of the model working as designed — Continuity backing a fast-scaling alumnus at growth stage and staying close to it. Brex went on to become one of YC's biggest alumni outcomes, and Hariharan was still on its board when Continuity's successor fund was announced years later ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/14/former-yc-continuity-head-targets-350-million-growth-stage-fund/)). The Brex profile on this site tells that story in full; Continuity's part of it is the 2018 round and the board seat.

## The 2023 wind-down — and the afterlife

Continuity ended abruptly, and from the top. Garry Tan, who became YC's president and chief executive in January 2023, wrote on 13 March that late-stage investing had turned out to be "so different from early stage that we found it to be a distraction from our core mission", and that YC would decrease the amount of it ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/changes-at-yc)). Seventeen teammates on the late-stage investing team — roughly 20% of YC's workforce — lost their roles the same day ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/13/y-combinator-cuts-nearly-20-of-staff-scales-back-growth-stage-investments/)). The timing, days after the collapse of Silicon Valley Bank, invited an obvious reading; YC told TechCrunch the bank's failure was not a factor and that the shift had been planned "well before" it, even as more than 30% of YC startups were exposed to the bank ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/13/y-combinator-cuts-nearly-20-of-staff-scales-back-growth-stage-investments/)).

What is striking is how little of Continuity actually died. Within two months Hariharan had finalised plans for Avra, a new fund targeting about $350 million and running an eight-week program for post-Series A startups that some described as "YC for growth" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/14/former-yc-continuity-head-targets-350-million-growth-stage-fund/)). Its first cohort included Runway, Supabase, Roboflow and Hex Technologies, with founders including DoorDash's Tony Xu and Rippling's Parker Conrad as mentors ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/14/former-yc-continuity-head-targets-350-million-growth-stage-fund/)). Then in October 2024 Rowghani launched Maxq, a seed-stage firm targeting $250 million for its debut fund ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/02/former-head-of-ycs-continuity-fund-ali-rowghani-launches-new-seed-firm/)). The fund was gone after eight years, but its model — and both of its leaders — carried on under new names. That is the sense in which Continuity outlived itself: YC decided late-stage investing distracted from its mission, while the people who had done it for YC decided the work itself was worth rebuilding elsewhere.

## What this profile does not claim

- This profile does not present Continuity as an independent firm: it was a fund inside Y Combinator, investing only in YC companies at launch, and it had no separate headquarters, public reporting or standalone track record of its own.
- It does not state that Tan "shut down" the fund in his own words — his March 2023 memo says YC would "decrease" late-stage investing; "wound down" is this profile's summary of the outcome, which later TechCrunch reporting describes as a shutdown.
- It does not assert that Continuity "led" the Brex Series B, because the announcement saying so was not read in full; it states only the investment and the board seat, both supported by fully-read tier2 sources.
- It does not state that the up-to-$1-billion vehicle reported in July 2017 ever closed, or at what size; that was not verified from a fully-read source.
- It states no investment count, return or performance figure for the fund; PitchBook tallies were not read in full and are not used.
- The Brex Series B is dated to 2018 only; VentureBeat dates the close to March 2018, and the month is not confirmed by a second fully-read source.
