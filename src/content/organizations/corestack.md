## Overview

CoreStack is a Bellevue, Washington–based enterprise software company that sells an AI-powered cloud-governance platform spanning CNAPP (cloud-native application protection), SecOps, FinOps, and CloudOps — in practice, software that helps large companies control the cost, security, and regulatory compliance of their cloud infrastructure across multiple providers ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/corestack-50-million-raised-to-help-enterprises-manage-and-govern-multi-cloud-environments/)). The company was founded in 2016, with Ezhilarasan Natarajan as founder and CEO, and operates with engineering and business operations in India alongside its Bellevue headquarters ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/corestack/funding/); [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/corestack-50-million-raised-to-help-enterprises-manage-and-govern-multi-cloud-environments/)).

Unlike most of its press coverage — which consists of company-issued funding announcements — this profile is built entirely from what independent outlets reported: a verifiable funding history from seed through Series B, a 2025 growth financing, and the metrics the company put on the record with independent reporters.

## Funding history: seed to Series B

CoreStack's Indian entity is registered as CoreStack India Private Limited, and the company's founding year is independently given as 2016 ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/corestack/funding/)). Its equity funding record, tracked round by round by Inc42, runs: a $3.21 million seed round led by Z5 Capital in October 2018; an $8.5 million Series A led by Dallas Venture Capital in March 2020; a $4 million round involving Iron Pillar in December 2020; and a $30 million Series B led by Avatar Growth Capital in November 2021 — for a total of $45.71 million across four rounds ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/corestack/funding/)).

The Series B was covered by India's Business Standard, which reported that the round brought total funding to $45 million following the March 2020 Series A, with Dallas Venture Capital, Iron Pillar, and angel investors continuing alongside Avatar ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/companies/corestack-closes-30-million-funding-round-led-by-avatar-growth-capital-121112301345_1.html)). At that point the company said it was governing more than $1 billion in combined cloud consumption for over 100 enterprise customers across financial services, healthcare, and retail, and described partnerships with [Amazon Web Services](/organizations/amazon-web-services/), Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/companies/corestack-closes-30-million-funding-round-led-by-avatar-growth-capital-121112301345_1.html)).

## From cloud infrastructure to autonomous governance

CoreStack began focused on simplifying cloud infrastructure for businesses and broadened over time into full multi-cloud governance ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/corestack/funding/)). By the time of the Post Road financing, the company described its offering in "agentic" terms: AI capabilities that continuously enforce governance intent across cloud, SaaS, and security operations rather than merely reporting on violations ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/corestack-50-million-raised-to-help-enterprises-manage-and-govern-multi-cloud-environments/)). The financing was explicitly framed around growth into the public sector as well — the company cited new enterprise and government customers that had increased average deal sizes and expansion revenue, and Post Road's Gardner Horan praised the platform's combination of multi-cloud governance, security, and financial-operations automation as "critical to digital transformation" ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/corestack-50-million-raised-to-help-enterprises-manage-and-govern-multi-cloud-environments/)). Founder and CEO Ezhilarasan Natarajan said the financing reinforced "the strength of CoreStack's business fundamentals, market momentum, and AI-driven product vision" ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/corestack-50-million-raised-to-help-enterprises-manage-and-govern-multi-cloud-environments/)).

## The Post Road financing and the agentic-governance pitch

In October 2025, CoreStack secured a $50 million growth financing led by Post Road Group, an alternative investment firm — structured as funded debt rather than equity, designed to optimize the company's capital structure while funding product innovation, market expansion, and M&A without diluting ownership ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/corestack-50-million-raised-to-help-enterprises-manage-and-govern-multi-cloud-environments/)). Reporting at the time put the company's customer base at more than 780 organizations globally, with year-over-year ARR growth above 30% and "billions of dollars" in managed cloud spend; the platform enforces compliance against more than 2,000 industry standards across AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, Oracle, and hybrid, edge, and private clouds ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/corestack-50-million-raised-to-help-enterprises-manage-and-govern-multi-cloud-environments/)).

The financing announcement marked a product repositioning as well: CoreStack described new "agentic AI" capabilities in its FinOps+ product and a CNAPP product called Graphion, and cited client outcomes including up to 40% reductions in cloud waste, 50% improvements in compliance posture, and thousands of blocked potential cybersecurity threats ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/corestack-50-million-raised-to-help-enterprises-manage-and-govern-multi-cloud-environments/)). The company said the capital would go toward R&D, global go-to-market efforts, hiring, and acquisitions, positioning CoreStack as an AI-powered governance and security platform for enterprises and public institutions ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/corestack-50-million-raised-to-help-enterprises-manage-and-govern-multi-cloud-environments/)).
