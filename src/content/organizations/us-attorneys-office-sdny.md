## Overview

The most famous federal prosecutor's office in America has a pipeline problem — in the best sense. Its alumni include senators, congressmen, New York City mayors, a governor, the Secretary of War, the Secretary of Homeland Security, the Secretary of State, U.S. attorneys general, a Supreme Court justice, ambassadors, and federal judges ([[U.S. Department of Justice](/organizations/us-department-of-justice/)](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny)). And in 2026, the pipeline reached its endpoint: the sitting Attorney General of the United States, [Todd Blanche](/people/todd-blanche/), started his legal career in this office as a paralegal, working there while attending Brooklyn Law School at night, before spending eight years as one of its prosecutors and co-chief of its Violent Crimes Unit and White Plains Division ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/dag/bio/deputy-attorney-general-todd-blanche)). The office Blanche left in 2014 now answers, through the Justice Department, to a man who once carried its files.

The U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York covers eight New York counties — Manhattan and the Bronx plus Dutchess, Orange, Putnam, Rockland, Sullivan, and Westchester — and handles terrorism, white-collar crime, cybercrime, mortgage fraud, public corruption, gang violence, organized crime, narcotics, and civil rights cases ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny)). The department calls it "one of the nation's premier legal institutions" ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny)). Its nickname tells the other half of the story: the "Sovereign District of New York," an office with an unusual degree of independence from Justice Department headquarters ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-picks-jay-clayton-serve-attorney-southern-district-new-york-2024-11-14/?outputType=chromeless)).

## The independence is structural, not reputational

A 2019 University of Oxford case study asked why this particular office keeps producing both elite lawyers and political firestorms, and its answer was organizational. The office was founded by the Judiciary Act of 1789 — the U.S. Attorney position predates the Department of Justice itself, which was only created in 1870 — and its court was the first to sit under the U.S. Constitution, preceding the Supreme Court: America's "mother court" ([University of Oxford, Blavatnik School of Government](https://www.bsg.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-01/A%20model%20public-service%20organisation%20-%20Blavatnik%20School%20of%20Government%20Case%20Centre%20on%20Public%20Leadership.pdf)). That age matters less than the culture it protects: ruthlessly selective hiring from elite law schools, young attorneys given real responsibility early, and a "do the right thing" norm enforced at every level of the hierarchy ([University of Oxford, Blavatnik School of Government](https://www.bsg.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-01/A%20model%20public-service%20organisation%20-%20Blavatnik%20School%20of%20Government%20Case%20Centre%20on%20Public%20Leadership.pdf)).

The independence is the point, and the office's own leaders describe it as a discipline. Robert Fiske, U.S. Attorney from 1976 to 1980, said the hallmark of his tenure was "total independence from politics" — noting that in other districts, hiring decisions could not be made without a senator's approval ([University of Oxford, Blavatnik School of Government](https://www.bsg.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-01/A%20model%20public-service%20organisation%20-%20Blavatnik%20School%20of%20Government%20Case%20Centre%20on%20Public%20Leadership.pdf)). Judge Jed Rakoff, a former prosecutor in the office, described "a tradition of independence... that has often led to tension with the Justice Department" ([University of Oxford, Blavatnik School of Government](https://www.bsg.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-01/A%20model%20public-service%20organisation%20-%20Blavatnik%20School%20of%20Government%20Case%20Centre%20on%20Public%20Leadership.pdf)). Mary Jo White — the first and to date only woman to hold the job, from 1993 to 2002 — said the culture of independence passes from one U.S. Attorney to the next, and that she threatened to resign four times during her career, three of them to keep Washington out of Southern District cases ([University of Oxford, Blavatnik School of Government](https://www.bsg.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-01/A%20model%20public-service%20organisation%20-%20Blavatnik%20School%20of%20Government%20Case%20Centre%20on%20Public%20Leadership.pdf)). Michael Garcia, who held the office from 2005 to 2008, added the caveat that matters: the culture is bigger than any one person and cannot be imposed from the top ([University of Oxford, Blavatnik School of Government](https://www.bsg.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-01/A%20model%20public-service%20organisation%20-%20Blavatnik%20School%20of%20Government%20Case%20Centre%20on%20Public%20Leadership.pdf)).

That independence has been tested by presidents of both parties. Geoffrey Berman was fired in 2020 after refusing to step aside — and his office's investigation of Rudy Giuliani's Ukraine dealings ended in 2022 with no charges filed ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-picks-jay-clayton-serve-attorney-southern-district-new-york-2024-11-14/?outputType=chromeless)).

## Eight counties, two divisions, $12 billion

The office's scale matches its reputation. With a staff of 220 as of 2018, it was one of the largest district offices in the country, split into civil and criminal divisions; the criminal docket alone spans white-collar crime, domestic and international terrorism, cybercrime, international and traditional organized crime including narcotics and drug trafficking, public corruption, and immigration fraud ([University of Oxford, Blavatnik School of Government](https://www.bsg.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-01/A%20model%20public-service%20organisation%20-%20Blavatnik%20School%20of%20Government%20Case%20Centre%20on%20Public%20Leadership.pdf)).

The financial record of the Preet Bharara years shows what that docket converts into. Between January 1, 2014 and December 31, 2015, the office recovered more than $12 billion: over $8.6 billion in forfeitures — the most any U.S. Attorney's Office had forfeited in a two-year period — plus more than $3 billion in civil actions and $1.3 billion in restitution, criminal fines, and special assessments ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/us-attorney-s-office-southern-district-new-york-recovers-nearly-12-billion-forfeitures)). The cases read like a catalog of modern financial enforcement. BNP Paribas pleaded guilty in July 2014 to conspiring to violate the International Emergency Economic Powers Act and the Trading with the Enemy Act by moving more than $8.8 billion through the U.S. financial system for Sudanese, Iranian, and Cuban entities under American sanctions — and was sentenced in May 2015 to forfeit $8.8336 billion ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/us-attorney-s-office-southern-district-new-york-recovers-nearly-12-billion-forfeitures)). JPMorgan Chase forfeited $1.7 billion in January 2014 for Bank Secrecy Act violations tied to the Bernard Madoff Ponzi scheme; Toyota paid $1.2 billion in March 2014 — the largest criminal penalty the Justice Department had ever imposed on an automotive company — for concealing safety defects; General Motors forfeited $900 million in September 2015; Commerzbank $300 million in March 2015; SAC Capital forfeited $284 million in 2014 as part of roughly $1.2 billion in total penalties for insider trading ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/us-attorney-s-office-southern-district-new-york-recovers-nearly-12-billion-forfeitures)). The office also seized the Silk Road's assets — 29,655 Bitcoins forfeited in January 2014, auctioned for $18.74 million — and extracted a $5.15 billion environmental settlement from the Tronox bankruptcy litigation, with more than $1.8 billion going to the United States ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/us-attorney-s-office-southern-district-new-york-recovers-nearly-12-billion-forfeitures)).

## The Williams years and the Clayton nomination

Under Damian Williams, the office's recent docket mixed Wall Street and City Hall. It secured the conviction of FTX founder Sam Bankman-Fried, the conviction of Archegos founder Sung Kook "Bill" Hwang, the bribery conviction of Senator Bob Menendez, and brought a criminal charge against New York City Mayor Eric Adams ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-picks-jay-clayton-serve-attorney-southern-district-new-york-2024-11-14/?outputType=chromeless)). Then came a nomination that broke the office's mold: on November 14, 2024, Jay Clayton — the former Securities and Exchange Commission chair, a corporate lawyer with no criminal-law experience — was tapped as U.S. Attorney ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-picks-jay-clayton-serve-attorney-southern-district-new-york-2024-11-14/?outputType=chromeless)).

## From paralegal to Attorney General

The Blanche arc remains the office's most striking personnel story, and it is the one the office's own website documents. Todd Blanche was born August 6, 1974 in Denver, earned his bachelor's from American University in 1996, and took his law degree from Brooklyn Law School in 2003, where he edited the Law Review ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/dag/bio/deputy-attorney-general-todd-blanche)). He joined the Southern District as a paralegal while attending law school at night, then served eight years as a federal prosecutor, rising to co-chief of the Violent Crimes Unit and co-chief of the White Plains Division before leaving in 2014 ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/dag/bio/deputy-attorney-general-todd-blanche)). Partnerships at WilmerHale and Cadwalader followed, then the return to government: Deputy Attorney General in 2025, acting Attorney General in 2026, and confirmation on August 8, 2026 as the 88th Attorney General of the United States ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/dag/bio/deputy-attorney-general-todd-blanche)). The Sovereign District's independence tradition now coexists with an unusual fact: the Attorney General above it is one of its own.
