## Overview

American Thymele Theatre is a New York-based nonprofit touring theatre company founded in
1993 by actor and director Stephen Diacrussi, who serves as its producing artistic
director ([Playbill](https://playbill.com/article/american-thymele-theatres-free-production-of-medea-begins-july-12-in-nyc-com-180813)).
Its stated mission is to preserve and promote Hellenic culture in America by staging plays
with Greek themes free of charge to the public, and it is chartered by the State of New
York as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit
([American Thymele Theatre](https://americanthymeletheatre.yolasite.com/about-us.php)).
Before it became known for its Euripides project, the company spent its first fifteen
years as a repertory touring company specializing in rarely staged Greek comedies — a
chapter of its history that competing reference results barely mention
([American Thymele Theatre](https://americanthymeletheatre.yolasite.com/about-us.php)).

## The repertory years: comedy festivals and touring

The company's initial production was George Kelly's *The Show-Off*, presented as a
bilingual adaptation at the Kraine Theater in New York
([American Thymele Theatre](https://americanthymeletheatre.yolasite.com/about-us.php)).
In 1995 it inaugurated the Angelos Vlachos Comedy Festival, a ten-year series devoted to
the 19th-century Athenian playwright, opening with Vlachos's *The Grocer's Daughter* at
the Frederick Loewe Theatre
([American Thymele Theatre](https://americanthymeletheatre.yolasite.com/about-us.php)).
That production toured to Boston and Philadelphia in 1996 and to New Jersey and
Connecticut in 1997, and the company later returned to Philadelphia to premiere two more
Vlachos comedies, *My Sober Husband* and *Mr. Floret's Wife*, which prompted further
touring ([American Thymele Theatre](https://americanthymeletheatre.yolasite.com/about-us.php)).
*The Grocer's Daughter* received its English-language premiere in Baltimore in 1999,
followed by extended New York tours that included a dinner-theatre engagement in 2000
([American Thymele Theatre](https://americanthymeletheatre.yolasite.com/about-us.php)).

In 2001 the company mounted a revival of Maxwell Anderson's *Barefoot in Athens* at the
Olympic Theatre, marking the play's 50th anniversary since its Broadway debut, as part of
the "2001: Year of Socrates" festivities; the run was extended into the following season
with daytime performances for New York City public schools
([American Thymele Theatre](https://americanthymeletheatre.yolasite.com/about-us.php)).
The company says the Cultural Olympiad Committee chose its production of *The Grocer's
Daughter* for presentation as part of the 2004 Olympic Games in Athens
([American Thymele Theatre](https://americanthymeletheatre.yolasite.com/about-us.php)).
It premiered *The Needy Barrister* in St. Louis, Missouri, paired with *The Grocer's
Daughter* — by then a touring favorite — took that repertory to Phoenix, Arizona, and,
back in New York, staged daytime performances of Nikos Kazantzakis's *Kouros* for city
public schools
([American Thymele Theatre](https://americanthymeletheatre.yolasite.com/about-us.php)).

## The New York Euripides Summer Festival (2009-2023)

In 2009 the company launched the New York Euripides Summer Festival, an annual free
series with the goal of staging all of Euripides' extant plays in chronological order
([TornosNews.gr](https://www.tornosnews.gr/en/greek-news/culture/49025-ny-euripides-free-summer-theater-festival-presents-bacchae-beginning-june-26.html)).
Early documented productions include *Rhesus* (2009) and *Alcestis* (2010), followed by
*Medea* (2011), directed by Nichole Hamilton with Danijela Popovic in the title role,
playing the East River Park Amphitheatre on July 12-13, the Naumburg Bandshell on July
14 and the Chernuchin Theatre on July 17
([Playbill](https://playbill.com/article/american-thymele-theatres-free-production-of-medea-begins-july-12-in-nyc-com-180813)).
*Children of Hercules* (2012) was directed by Diacrussi with a 25-person cast at the East
River Park Amphitheatre, the Naumburg Bandshell in Central Park and Theatre 500 in the
Times Square Arts Center
([Playbill](https://playbill.com/article/euripides-summer-festival-offers-free-outdoor-performances-of-children-of-hercules-beginning-aug-7-com-196391)).
*Hippolytus* (2013), the festival's fifth year, was directed by Diacrussi with a
22-person cast across the East River Park Amphitheatre, the Naumburg Bandshell and
Theatre 777
([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/article/American-Thymele-Theatre-to-Present-HIPPOLYTUS-in-Outdoor-NYC-Venues-723-27-20130722)).
The chronology continued with *Andromache* (2014), *Hecuba* (2015), the satyr play
*Cyclops* (2016), *The Madness of Hercules* (2017) — the first presented in a
contemporary staging — *Suppliants* (2018), and *Iphigenia among the Taurians* and
*Daughters of Troy* (2019)
([American Thymele Theatre](https://americanthymeletheatre.yolasite.com/about-us.php)).

During the COVID-19 pandemic the festival moved to digital productions: *Helen*
(screened at Columbia University) and *Electra* in 2020, *Phoenician Maidens* and
*Iphigenia at Aulis* in 2021, and *Ion* and *Orestes* in 2022
([American Thymele Theatre](https://americanthymeletheatre.yolasite.com/about-us.php);
[TornosNews.gr](https://www.tornosnews.gr/en/greek-news/culture/49025-ny-euripides-free-summer-theater-festival-presents-bacchae-beginning-june-26.html)).
The festival returned to live performance in 2023 with *Bacchae*, directed by Lorca
Peress with a cast of 16 headed by Diacrussi, playing the Actors' Temple Theatre, the AMT
Theater and the Marilyn Monroe Theatre from June 26 to 30 — the closing production of
the festival's 14-year run, coinciding with the company's 30th anniversary year
([TornosNews.gr](https://www.tornosnews.gr/en/greek-news/culture/49025-ny-euripides-free-summer-theater-festival-presents-bacchae-beginning-june-26.html);
[BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/off-off-broadway/article/BACCHAE-Comes-to-New-York-Euripides-Free-Summer-Theater-Festival-This-Month-20230609)).

Before the pandemic, festival productions premiered each summer at the East River Park
Amphitheatre — a venue whose open-air classics tradition stretched back to the 1950s
before its demolition in late 2021 — and extended to the Naumburg Bandshell in Central
Park, the Richard Rodgers Amphitheater, the Jackie Robinson Bandshell, the Almira
Kennedy Coursey Amphitheater, and off-Broadway houses including Theatre 500, the 777
Theatre and the Stage II Theatre
([American Thymele Theatre](https://americanthymeletheatre.yolasite.com/about-us.php)).
The productions were fully staged with ancient-style costumes, scenery and music
([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/article/American-Thymele-Theatre-to-Present-HIPPOLYTUS-in-Outdoor-NYC-Venues-723-27-20130722)).

## Beyond Euripides: readings, grants and institutional afterlife

Alongside the festival, the company kept up parallel programming strands. It revived its
19th-century Athenian one-act comedy series, with the Federation of Hellenic Societies
sponsoring staged readings of *Not Qualified* and *A Servant Is Sought* in 2016 and of
*Mourning Does Not Become Her* at the Bridge Theatre in 2018
([American Thymele Theatre](https://americanthymeletheatre.yolasite.com/about-us.php)).
Its Spring Readings series workshopped new classical-themed plays: Emory Wilson's *The
Father of Comedy* (2020), *A Season in Sparta* (2021) and *Hit-List: Cicero* (2022)
([American Thymele Theatre](https://americanthymeletheatre.yolasite.com/about-us.php)).
It presented *The Petrakis Universe* for the New York Public Library under a grant from
the [Stavros S. Niarchos Foundation](/organizations/stavros-s-niarchos-foundation/), which led to an encore staging at the Martin E.
Segal Theatre Center
([American Thymele Theatre](https://americanthymeletheatre.yolasite.com/about-us.php)).
In 2021 it joined Leadership 100 as an honorary member of the sponsoring organizations
for the bicentennial of the 1821 Greek Revolution
([American Thymele Theatre](https://americanthymeletheatre.yolasite.com/about-us.php)).

The work has found an institutional afterlife beyond the stage: Brown University
purchased the company's filmed 2013 *Hippolytus* for curricular use in 2022, and the
digital *Helen* was screened at Columbia University
([American Thymele Theatre](https://americanthymeletheatre.yolasite.com/about-us.php)).
According to the company's biography carried by BroadwayWorld, Diacrussi is a character
actor whose stage credits include *The Marriage Proposal*, *The Clouds* and *The
General's Daughter* as well as the long-running off-Broadway *Socrates, Socrates* at the
Actors' Playhouse; his film credits include *In the Name of the Father* and *Meet the
Parents*, and his television credits include *Law & Order: SVU* and *The Sopranos*
([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/off-off-broadway/article/BACCHAE-Comes-to-New-York-Euripides-Free-Summer-Theater-Festival-This-Month-20230609)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim independent confirmation of the 2004 Cultural Olympiad
selection: that account comes from the company itself and its press materials, and no
independent contemporaneous report of the Athens presentation was verified in this
research pass. It does not adjudicate whether Euripides' surviving corpus numbers 18 or
19 plays — the authorship of *Rhesus* is disputed among scholars — and instead follows
the company's own 19-title festival chronology, which includes the satyr play *Cyclops*.
It does not treat the National Herald's 30th-anniversary retrospective or Time Out New
York's *Children of Hercules* listing as sources: both were cited in an earlier version
of this profile but were unreachable during the October 2026 rewrite pass, so they were
dropped and every fact they supported is covered by the sources cited below. Finally, it
does not present Stephen Diacrussi's acting credits as independently reviewed work;
they come from a company-supplied biography published by BroadwayWorld.
