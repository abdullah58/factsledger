## Overview

Fifth Wall is a Los Angeles venture capital firm founded in 2016 by Brendan Wallace and Brad Greiwe that invests exclusively in technology for the real estate industry — so-called proptech — and raises its funds from the industry's largest landlords, owners and developers, who then become customers of the startups it backs ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/02/a-new-venture-firm-focused-on-real-estate-has-raised-212-million-from-real-estate-giants/)). The name alludes to the fifth "disruptive" wall the firm provides in addition to the four physical walls of a building ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/entrepreneur/fifth-wall-ventures-raises-212-million-fund-to-invest-in-real-estate)).

This profile covers that firm — Fifth Wall Ventures, based in Los Angeles in the [United States](/places/united-states/) — not the unrelated "5th Wall" phygital-infrastructure company, and not the interior-design term for a ceiling ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/entrepreneur/fifth-wall-ventures-raises-212-million-fund-to-invest-in-real-estate)). In under a decade it grew from a $212 million debut fund to about $3.2 billion in capital under management, closing the largest proptech fund ever at $866 million in December 2022 — then, in January 2026, it cut staff and suspended fundraising as higher interest rates and a shift in US climate policy squeezed both of its markets ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/proptech/investment-in-proptech-sees-biggest-boost-yet-with-866m-fund-closing-116816); [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/proptech/proptech-climate-vc-heavyweight-fifth-wall-slashes-staff-halts-fundraising-132833)). The scale of its climate bet explains the vulnerability: real estate produces about 40% of global greenhouse gas emissions across a building's life cycle, and its $500 million Climate Fund was raised against a decarbonization funding gap the firm put at $10.5 trillion ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/london/capital-markets/carbon-sink-113889)).

## The model: landlords as investors

Wallace and Greiwe built Fifth Wall on an inversion of the usual venture model. Instead of raising from pension funds and family offices, they went to the largest owners, operators and developers of real estate and told them they needed a strategy for the technology that would change their business — a pitch institutions had first rejected, saying real estate technology "was not a thing" ([Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2026/10/fifth-wall-brendan-wallace-investment/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/02/a-new-venture-firm-focused-on-real-estate-has-raised-212-million-from-real-estate-giants/)). The logic was distribution: new real estate technology is not hard to build, Wallace argued, but selling it to the incumbents who dictate outcomes is — so the fund's limited partners double as its portfolio companies' customers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/02/a-new-venture-firm-focused-on-real-estate-has-raised-212-million-from-real-estate-giants/)). Wallace worked in real estate investment banking at Goldman Sachs and real estate private equity at [Blackstone](/organizations/blackstone/), where he worked on the 2007 buyout of Sam Zell's Equity Office Properties Trust, a $39 billion deal ([Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2026/10/fifth-wall-brendan-wallace-investment/)). Greiwe, after UBS, Tishman Speyer and Starwood Capital, co-founded [Invitation Homes](/organizations/invitation-homes/), where he was chief technology officer early on ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/02/a-new-venture-firm-focused-on-real-estate-has-raised-212-million-from-real-estate-giants/)).

## Funds and growth: from $212 million to $3.2 billion

Fifth Wall's first fund closed in May 2017 at $212 million, raised from nine large US real estate companies including CBRE, [Equity Residential](/organizations/equity-residential/), Hines, Lowe's, Host Hotels & Resorts, Lennar, Macerich and Prologis ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/02/a-new-venture-firm-focused-on-real-estate-has-raised-212-million-from-real-estate-giants/)). Within a year it had put $62 million into seven startups, its biggest bet the home-buying platform Opendoor ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/02/a-new-venture-firm-focused-on-real-estate-has-raised-212-million-from-real-estate-giants/)). Fund II, closed in July 2019 at $503 million, more than doubled the first fund and drew over 50 corporate strategic investors from 11 countries — including British Land, Gecina and [Related Companies](/organizations/related-companies/) — taking total assets under management past $1 billion ([PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fifth-wall-closes-new-503-million-fund-the-largest-real-estate-venture-capital-fund-raised-to-date-300885325.html)). The firm then added a climate arm: its inaugural Climate Fund, launched in 2021 with a $200 million target, closed in July 2022 at $500 million, the largest private fund formed to decarbonize real estate ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/london/capital-markets/carbon-sink-113889)). Five months later, Fund III closed on 9 December 2022 at $866 million, backed by Annaly Capital Management and Koch Real Estate Investments ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/proptech/investment-in-proptech-sees-biggest-boost-yet-with-866m-fund-closing-116816); [Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/12/13/proptechs-polish-your-pitch-decks-fifth-wall-closes-record-866m-fund/)).

## Peak, retrenchment and the next bet

The $866 million Fund III close was reported by *[The Wall Street Journal](/organizations/wall-street-journal/)* and brought capital under management to about $3.2 billion, with more than 110 limited partners ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/proptech/investment-in-proptech-sees-biggest-boost-yet-with-866m-fund-closing-116816)). The returns behind that scale were real: Wallace says the firm put $82 million into Opendoor between September 2016 and March 2019 and sold at the IPO at a multiple of over four times, and its early fintech bets Blend Labs and Hippo both went public in 2021 ([Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2026/10/fifth-wall-brendan-wallace-investment/)). The exits kept coming — ServiceTitan went public in December 2024 at a $9 billion market capitalisation, and a month later CBRE bought Industrious for $400 million ([Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/power-series/player/brad-greiwe-brendan-wallace-jeremy-fox-and-g-m-nicholas-vik/)). But the market turned: proptech venture funding fell 42% in 2023, and the climate arm faced the Trump administration's rollback of climate policies ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/proptech/proptech-climate-vc-heavyweight-fifth-wall-slashes-staff-halts-fundraising-132833)). In January 2026 the firm cut staff — it did not say how many — and suspended fundraising, a year after launching REACT and Fifth Wall Securities ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/proptech/proptech-climate-vc-heavyweight-fifth-wall-slashes-staff-halts-fundraising-132833); [Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/power-series/player/brad-greiwe-brendan-wallace-jeremy-fox-and-g-m-nicholas-vik/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Fifth Wall?** Fifth Wall is a Los Angeles venture capital firm founded in 2016 by Brendan Wallace and Brad Greiwe that invests in technology for the real estate industry, known as proptech, and in climate technology that decarbonizes buildings ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/02/a-new-venture-firm-focused-on-real-estate-has-raised-212-million-from-real-estate-giants/); [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/london/capital-markets/carbon-sink-113889)). It is a Certified B Corporation ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/12/13/proptechs-polish-your-pitch-decks-fifth-wall-closes-record-866m-fund/)).

**Why is it called Fifth Wall?** The name alludes to the fifth "disruptive" wall the firm provides in addition to the four physical walls of a building — the layer of technology it argues buildings need on top of their structure ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/entrepreneur/fifth-wall-ventures-raises-212-million-fund-to-invest-in-real-estate)). The firm came out of stealth under that name in May 2017, when it announced the close of its first fund ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/entrepreneur/fifth-wall-ventures-raises-212-million-fund-to-invest-in-real-estate)).

**How big is Fifth Wall?** Its funds totalled about $3.2 billion in capital under management after Fund III closed in December 2022, with more than 110 limited partners ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/proptech/investment-in-proptech-sees-biggest-boost-yet-with-866m-fund-closing-116816)). By October 2026 Wallace put commitments at approximately $3.1 billion from roughly 115 limited partners, and the firm counts more than 100 limited partners across 20 countries ([Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2026/10/fifth-wall-brendan-wallace-investment/); [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/proptech/proptech-climate-vc-heavyweight-fifth-wall-slashes-staff-halts-fundraising-132833)).

**What are Fifth Wall's best-known investments?** Opendoor, the home-buying platform, was its biggest early bet and its largest win, returning over four times its $82 million investment at the IPO, according to Wallace ([Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2026/10/fifth-wall-brendan-wallace-investment/)). Other notable names include Hippo, SmartRent, Industrious, VTS, Lime and ServiceTitan ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/19/real-estate-vc-fifth-wall-is-raising-500m-for-a-new-fund/)).

**Why did Fifth Wall cut staff and halt fundraising in 2026?** The firm cited interest rates and shifting climate policy from the Trump administration, according to Axios reporting carried by Bisnow ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/proptech/proptech-climate-vc-heavyweight-fifth-wall-slashes-staff-halts-fundraising-132833)). Proptech venture funding had already fallen 42% in 2023, and its climate business faced cancelled clean-energy investment as federal incentives were rolled back ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/proptech/proptech-climate-vc-heavyweight-fifth-wall-slashes-staff-halts-fundraising-132833)).
