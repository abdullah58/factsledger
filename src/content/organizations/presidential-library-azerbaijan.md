## Overview

The Presidential Library of the Republic of Azerbaijan is a Baku library serving the presidency, founded in 2003 through the merger of two existing libraries: the Baku Central City Library and the Library of the General Services Department of the Presidential Administration ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/culture/254577.html)). It holds rare books in various languages dating from the 16th to the early 20th centuries, alongside 250 magazines, newspapers, and other periodicals, both Azerbaijani and foreign ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/culture/254577.html)).

Based at Nizami Street 58 in Baku, it combines a traditional library collection with a growing programme of digital projects on Azerbaijani history, literature, and statehood ([Presidential Library](https://preslib.az/en/eprojects/nizamiganjavi)). Its full formal name is the Presidential Library for Presidential Affairs of the Republic of Azerbaijan ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/culture/254577.html)). Its collection focuses on governance subjects — economics, politics, state building, law, philosophy, statistics, history, literature, and linguistics ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/culture/254577.html)). In 2026 it began applying artificial-intelligence technologies in its "Literary Heritage" virtual collection, the first step in a staged plan to extend AI across its digital platforms ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/culture/254577.html)).

## Origins and institutional role

The library was created in 2003 by merging the Baku Central City Library — formerly the Lenin Library — with the Library of the General Services Department of the Presidential Administration, formerly the Library of the House of Political Education ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/culture/254577.html)). That merger joined a city library's holdings with an administration library's holdings into a single presidential institution. The two predecessor names are recorded here exactly as independent reporting gives them, because they explain what the merged library inherited: a general city collection on one side, and a collection built around political education and administration on the other. The profile does not assert anything about a later formal status decree, since no source read in full states one. The library's subject scope — economics, politics, state building, law, philosophy, statistics, history, literature, and linguistics — is consistent with an institution meant to support the presidency's information needs, though the sources do not spell out which readers it serves or under what access rules.

## Collections

The library's distinguishing holdings are its rare books: volumes in various languages dating from the 16th to the early 20th centuries ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/culture/254577.html)). The sources read in full do not give a total count for the rare-book fund, nor a total collection size, so this profile gives neither. Alongside the rare books, the library holds 250 magazines, newspapers, and other periodicals, both Azerbaijani and foreign ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/culture/254577.html)). The subject emphasis of the collection is governance and the humanities: economics, politics, state building, law, philosophy, statistics, history, literature, and linguistics ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/culture/254577.html)). Taken together, the rare books, the periodicals, and the subject list describe a working research collection for the presidency and for readers interested in Azerbaijani statehood, rather than a lending library of general titles — though the sources do not state access or lending rules, and this profile does not infer them.

## Digital projects and AI

The library has built electronic portals, two documented in its own project pages. The "Nizami Ganjavi. A Digital Library" was compiled after 2021 was declared the Year of Nizami Ganjavi by decree of President Ilham Aliyev; it gathers books, articles, and newspaper publications on the poet and thinker Nizami Ganjavi ([Presidential Library](https://preslib.az/en/eprojects/nizamiganjavi)). It holds electronic versions of Nizami's "Khamsa" and a bibliography of his works and writings about him ([Presidential Library](https://preslib.az/en/eprojects/nizamiganjavi)).

The second portal, "Triumph of the Constitution and Sovereignty," covers President Ilham Aliyev's activities during the 44-day Patriotic War and the post-war period in three periods: cessation of occupation (Sep 2020–Dec 2022), restoration of territorial integrity (Dec 2022–Sep 2023), and complete restoration of sovereignty (Sep 2023–Jun 2024) ([Presidential Library](https://preslib.az/en/eprojects/suverenlik)). It also includes presidential laws, decrees, and orders, plus more than 100 books and articles ([Presidential Library](https://preslib.az/en/eprojects/suverenlik)). In 2026 the library began adding AI to its "Literary Heritage" virtual collection, with staged expansion planned to the rare book fund and art collection ([AzerNews](https://www.azernews.az/culture/254577.html)).
