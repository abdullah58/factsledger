## Overview

[The White House](/organizations/the-white-house/) Correspondents' Association is the nonprofit that has represented the White House press corps since 1914 — roughly 900 journalists strong ([Associated Press](https://srnnews.com/white-house-journalists-use-annual-press-dinner-to-celebrate-first-amendment/); [WHCA](https://whca.press/dinners/2018-dinner/)). Its work, in its own description, is to maintain independent news media coverage of the president: advocating for access, handling logistics for the pools of reporters who stay close to the president and travel with him, and providing scholarships to journalism students ([WHCA](https://whca.press/dinners/2018-dinner/)). The association's best-known public face is its annual dinner, traditionally attended by the President and First Lady as well as senior government officials and members of the press corps, with proceeds supporting the association's year-round work, scholarships for aspiring journalists, and awards recognizing excellence in the profession ([WHCA](https://whca.press/dinners/2018-dinner/)).

Twice in a decade, the association found itself in open conflict with a White House over press access — once in defense of a single reporter, and once over the century-old institution of the press pool itself.

## 2018: the Acosta ban

The queue's "2018 ban" is the November 2018 suspension of [CNN](/organizations/cnn/) correspondent Jim Acosta's hard pass. After a combative post-midterms press conference on November 7, 2018, the White House suspended Acosta's credential "until further notice" ([CNN Wire](https://www.tmj4.com/news/national/reporters-condemn-white-houses-decision-to-revoke-cnn-reporters-hard-pass)). The association's response was immediate and unsparing: the WHCA called the decision "out of line" and "unacceptable," declaring, "We urge the White House to immediately reverse this weak and misguided action" ([CNN Wire](https://www.tmj4.com/news/national/reporters-condemn-white-houses-decision-to-revoke-cnn-reporters-hard-pass)).

That year's dinner had already set the tone for an adversarial era. The April 28, 2018 dinner featured comedian Michelle Wolf as entertainer — announced with praise for her "truth-to-power style" by then-president Margaret Talev ([WHCA](https://whca.press/dinners/2018-dinner/)). Talev used her own dinner remarks to stake the association's position: "We reject efforts by anyone, especially our elected leaders, to paint journalism as un-American, to undermine trust between reporter and reader, or to cast doubt on the relevance of facts and truth in the modern age. An attack on any journalist is an attack on us all" ([WHCA](https://whca.press/dinners/2018-dinner/)).

## 2025: the pool seizure

The far bigger rupture came in the second Trump administration. On February 25, 2025, in a break with more than a century of practice, press secretary [Karoline Leavitt](/people/karoline-leavitt/) announced at a news briefing that the WHCA would no longer control the press pool covering the president ([U.S. Press Freedom Tracker](https://pressfreedomtracker.us/all-incidents/white-house-wrests-control-of-presidential-press-pool-from-correspondents/)). "For decades a group of DC-based journalists — the White House Correspondents Association — has dictated which journalists get to ask questions of the President of the United States in these most intimate spaces," Leavitt said. "Not anymore" ([U.S. Press Freedom Tracker](https://pressfreedomtracker.us/all-incidents/white-house-wrests-control-of-presidential-press-pool-from-correspondents/)).

The pool is a 13-member group of reporters and photojournalists who travel with and cover the president's daily activities — traditionally overseen by the WHCA precisely so that access wouldn't be limited to journalists who cover the sitting administration favorably ([U.S. Press Freedom Tracker](https://pressfreedomtracker.us/all-incidents/white-house-wrests-control-of-presidential-press-pool-from-correspondents/)). WHCA president Eugene Daniels warned: "This move tears at the independence of a free press in the United States. It suggests the government will choose the journalists who cover the president. In a free country, leaders must not be able to choose their own press corps" ([U.S. Press Freedom Tracker](https://pressfreedomtracker.us/all-incidents/white-house-wrests-control-of-presidential-press-pool-from-correspondents/)).

The takeover came amid a separate fight with the [Associated Press](/organizations/associated-press/), excluded from events on Air Force One, in the [Oval Office](/places/oval-office/), and other pool-only areas since February 11 over its editorial policy of continuing to refer to the "Gulf of Mexico" ([U.S. Press Freedom Tracker](https://pressfreedomtracker.us/all-incidents/white-house-wrests-control-of-presidential-press-pool-from-correspondents/)). The WHCA had filed a brief supporting the AP's case on February 23 ([U.S. Press Freedom Tracker](https://pressfreedomtracker.us/all-incidents/white-house-wrests-control-of-presidential-press-pool-from-correspondents/)). That very evening, reporters from HuffPost and Reuters were removed from the next day's pool list; their spots went to Newsmax and Blaze Media, two conservative outlets ([U.S. Press Freedom Tracker](https://pressfreedomtracker.us/all-incidents/white-house-wrests-control-of-presidential-press-pool-from-correspondents/)).

On April 15, 2025, the White House eliminated the pool's permanent wire-service position — the slot historically reserved for one of the AP, Bloomberg News, or Reuters — replacing it with a second print slot for which wire outlets were technically eligible ([U.S. Press Freedom Tracker](https://pressfreedomtracker.us/all-incidents/white-house-wrests-control-of-presidential-press-pool-from-correspondents/)). The move landed one day after a court order restoring the AP's access took effect on April 14, following the wire service's April 8 preliminary-injunction win; the AP condemned the change as an attempt to circumvent the court order ([U.S. Press Freedom Tracker](https://pressfreedomtracker.us/all-incidents/white-house-wrests-control-of-presidential-press-pool-from-correspondents/)). Daniels told the Washington Post the changes were simply a means to "retaliate against news organizations for coverage the White House doesn't like" ([U.S. Press Freedom Tracker](https://pressfreedomtracker.us/all-incidents/white-house-wrests-control-of-presidential-press-pool-from-correspondents/)).

Then the wires were cut off in the most visible way possible. On May 12, 2025, the AP, Bloomberg News, and Reuters were left out of the pool departing on Air Force One for the president's trip to Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and the United Arab Emirates — the first time since the press corps started traveling with presidents abroad that wire services had not been on the president's plane ([U.S. Press Freedom Tracker](https://pressfreedomtracker.us/all-incidents/white-house-wrests-control-of-presidential-press-pool-from-correspondents/)). The WHCA called it "continued retaliation for independent editorial decisions" and "a disservice to Americans who need news about their president, especially on foreign trips where anything could happen and the consequences can impact the entire world" ([U.S. Press Freedom Tracker](https://pressfreedomtracker.us/all-incidents/white-house-wrests-control-of-presidential-press-pool-from-correspondents/)).

## The deglamorized dinner

Even the association's signature event bent under the pressure. The April 2025 dinner went ahead with neither the president nor a comedian: entertainer Amber Ruffin was disinvited after saying on a podcast that the administration was "kind of a bunch of murderers," and the evening was recast as a First Amendment celebration under president Eugene Daniels ([Associated Press](https://srnnews.com/white-house-journalists-use-annual-press-dinner-to-celebrate-first-amendment/)). The AP's own account noted, in passing, the institutional damage behind the scenes: the pool power wrested from the WHCA, the AP's lawsuit over event access, the curtailment of wire agencies ([Associated Press](https://srnnews.com/white-house-journalists-use-annual-press-dinner-to-celebrate-first-amendment/)). An organization founded to guarantee that presidents face independent coverage had, in a single spring, lost the machinery it used to do it.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim the AP's access lawsuit was finally resolved — the injunction order was on hold pending the administration's appeal as of the last fully-read source. It does not claim the White House took over briefing-room seat assignments; that was reported as under consideration, not as fact. It does not give a current membership figure beyond the roughly 900 reported by the AP in April 2025. Evaluative judgments about the 2025 pool changes appear only as attributed quotes from the association and others, not as this profile's own findings. The 2018 dinner's entertainer and the 2025 dinner's disinvitation are reported as documented events, without adopting either side's characterization of them.
