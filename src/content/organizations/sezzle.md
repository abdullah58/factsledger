## Overview

Sezzle is a Minneapolis-based buy now, pay later company founded in 2016, co-founded and led by CEO Charlie Youakim, that lets shoppers split purchases into four interest-free installments over six weeks, with 25% charged at the time of purchase ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/bnpl-consolidates-zip-to-buy-sezzle/); [Small Caps](http://smallcaps.com.au/article/buy-now-pay-later-start-up-sezzle-ipo-asx-debut)).

## The American BNPL that went public in Australia

Sezzle's most distinctive corporate decision was where it listed. The company said it chose the Australian Securities Exchange over U.S. markets because, prior to 2020, the BNPL model was more commonplace in Australia — Afterpay, a major BNPL player, is headquartered in Melbourne — and Australian investors already understood the operating model ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/bnpl-consolidates-zip-to-buy-sezzle/); [Small Caps](http://smallcaps.com.au/article/buy-now-pay-later-start-up-sezzle-ipo-asx-debut)). The bet worked at first: its July 2019 IPO was oversubscribed at $43.6 million, priced at $1.22 per CDI, and the shares rose 82% within two hours of listing, giving the three-year-old company an indicative market value of around $217 million ([Small Caps](http://smallcaps.com.au/article/buy-now-pay-later-start-up-sezzle-ipo-asx-debut)). At the time Youakim held approximately 49.7% of the company, and its merchant count was growing by a quarterly average of 79.3% from December 2017 to March 2019 ([Small Caps](http://smallcaps.com.au/article/buy-now-pay-later-start-up-sezzle-ipo-asx-debut)).

Beyond the core four-installment product — delivered partly through a Sezzle Virtual Card usable anywhere Visa is accepted — the company built a credit-building layer (Sezzle Up, which reports on-time payments to credit bureaus) and a long-term financing tool in partnership with Ally ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/bnpl-consolidates-zip-to-buy-sezzle/)).

## A merger made and unmade in five months

In February 2022, Australian BNPL rival Zip agreed to acquire Sezzle in a deal valuing it at about $355 million. The plan called for Sezzle to rebrand as Zip and for Youakim to lead Zip's U.S. business; the combined company would have had 13.3 million customers and 129,000 merchant partners ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/bnpl-consolidates-zip-to-buy-sezzle/)). Five months later the deal was dead: Zip dropped the buyout, citing "current macroeconomic and market conditions," and Sezzle received an $11 million break fee under the joint termination ([Reuters](https://1027wbow.com/2022/07/11/zip-sezzle-bnpl-deal-falls-through-as-rising-rates-hit-consumer-finance-firms/)). The collapse was part of a sector-wide repricing — rising rates shrank BNPL valuations, Australia's Latitude pulled back its Humm buyout, and Openpay paused its U.S. operations ([Reuters](https://1027wbow.com/2022/07/11/zip-sezzle-bnpl-deal-falls-through-as-rising-rates-hit-consumer-finance-firms/)). Between the February announcement and the July termination, Sezzle lost nearly 82% of its value, falling to A$84.9 million from A$491 million ([Reuters](https://1027wbow.com/2022/07/11/zip-sezzle-bnpl-deal-falls-through-as-rising-rates-hit-consumer-finance-firms/)).
