# General Atlantic

## The growth-equity giant that began as one billionaire's family office

Every first-page search result will tell you General Atlantic is a growth-equity firm founded in 1980 managing over $100 billion. What none of them lead with is the origin story: the firm debuted in 1980 as the personal family office of Duty Free Shoppers co-founder Chuck Feeney — the billionaire famous for giving away almost his entire fortune — and for more than a decade it invested Feeney's money alone, only beginning to accept outside capital in 1993 [^1^]. Its investors today include big US pension funds, insurance companies, sovereign wealth funds, and family offices [^1^].

## From single strategy to multi-strategy platform

For its first few decades, General Atlantic stayed close to its growth-equity roots, expanding geographically and backing companies across technology, financial services, consumer, health care and life sciences, and the energy transition [^1^]. That changed in the 2020s. In 2020 it created Atlantic Park, a private-credit joint venture with Iron Park Capital — then acquired Iron Park three years later, formally establishing General Atlantic Credit in 2023 [^1^][^2^]. In 2021 it launched its inaugural climate growth fund, BeyondNetZero [^2^]. In 2025 it entered a strategic partnership with UBS Group to push further into private credit [^1^].

## The Actis acquisition

The biggest step in that diversification was Actis, the London-based sustainable infrastructure investor. In January 2024 the two firms announced a definitive agreement under which General Atlantic would acquire Actis, creating a combined platform of about $96 billion in assets under management; financial terms were not disclosed [^2^]. Actis brought roughly $12.5 billion in assets, more than 140 investment professionals across 17 offices, and over $25 billion raised since its inception, investing in the energy transition, digital transition, and supply-chain transformation [^2^]. Actis became General Atlantic's sustainable infrastructure arm — still led by chairman Torbjorn Caesar, still operating under the Actis brand, and retaining independence over its investment decisions [^2^]. At the Davos meeting that year, chairman and CEO Bill Ford framed the bet as a "multi-decade opportunity" in the energy transition [^3^].

## Where it stands now

Bloomberg reported in September 2026 that the firm manages about $129 billion, has revived its plans for an initial public offering, and is marketing an evergreen fund for high-net-worth individuals designed to invest about $1.5 billion a year [^1^]. Earlier in 2026, Novak Djokovic joined General Atlantic as a global strategic adviser [^1^].

General Atlantic's reach extends into the asset-management world it once only invested in: in 2024 it took a minority stake in [Partners Capital](/organizations/partners-capital/), the London-based investment office whose co-founder once helped run General Atlantic itself [^1^].
