## Overview

Community Investment Management (CIM) is a [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/)-based impact investment firm that provides debt capital to fintech lenders serving small businesses and underserved borrowers. Founded in 2014 and led by co-founder and managing partner Jacob Haar, it manages more than $1 billion in assets and says it has enabled over $37.7 billion of credit to nearly 21.5 million borrowers ([American Fintech Council](https://fintechcouncil.org/press-releases/american-fintech-council-afc-adds-community-investment-management-cim-as-newest-strategic-partner); [Community Investment Management](https://cim-llc.com/assets/CIM-Operating-Principles-2026-07-31.pdf)).

CIM is not the same firm as CIM Group, a separate real-assets management platform — a diversified owner, operator, lender and developer managing over $30 billion of assets — and the two should not be confused despite the shared initials ([Taft Law](https://www.taftlaw.com/news-events/news/taft-advises-cim-real-estate-finance-trust-in-acquisition-of-cim-group-llc/)). CIM's business is private credit, not real estate: it lends to the lenders, supplying the debt facilities that let technology-driven credit providers reach borrowers traditional banks ignore. Its stated aim is to show that responsible lending to overlooked borrowers can be both profitable and large-scale, drawing mainstream capital in ([Community Investment Management](https://cim-llc.com/assets/CIM-Operating-Principles-2026-07-31.pdf)).

## Founding and Jacob Haar

CIM was founded in 2014 in San Francisco, [California](/places/california/), and has been partnering with innovative fintech companies offering responsible and transparent financial solutions since then ([Brigit](https://www.hellobrigit.com/learn/brigit-announces-75m-in-debt-financing-from-social-impact-fund-cim)). The American Fintech Council describes Haar as the firm's co-founder and managing partner, and by December 2024 the firm brought "a decade of experience as a leading voice in responsible lending practices" to its partnership with the council ([American Fintech Council](https://fintechcouncil.org/press-releases/american-fintech-council-afc-adds-community-investment-management-cim-as-newest-strategic-partner)).

Haar came to credit investing through international development rather than banking. A Fulbright Scholar, he first worked in social-impact small-business lending in Azerbaijan, for Save the Children USA's microfinance institution, where small loans went to refugees with no credit score and little collateral ([SOCAP Global](https://socapglobal.com/2021/04/scaling-responsible-innovation-in-lending-with-jacob-haar-of-community-investment-management-episode-62-of-money-meaning/)). After leaving Save the Children he co-founded and managed Minlam Asset Management, a microfinance fund backing debt investment to micro, small and medium enterprise lenders in emerging markets ([SOCAP Global](https://socapglobal.com/2021/04/scaling-responsible-innovation-in-lending-with-jacob-haar-of-community-investment-management-episode-62-of-money-meaning/)). He says he started a credit strategy in 2005 to finance emerging- and frontier-market microfinance and SME lenders, and turned to US fintech as it began reaching underserved borrowers more efficiently ([Sure Shot Entrepreneur](https://podcast.sure.ventures/episodes/tsse69-venture-debt-jacob-cim?t=1m21s)).

That history shapes the firm's pitch: banks must by design be conservative and are rarely the best early partners for an innovating lender, while aggressive hedge funds may not share a founder's vision, so CIM positions itself as a strategic partner aligned on both returns and social impact ([Sure Shot Entrepreneur](https://podcast.sure.ventures/episodes/tsse69-venture-debt-jacob-cim?t=1m21s)). "Lending has the capacity to transform what people can do based on their capabilities and not necessarily based just on the opportunities that they've been dealt," Haar told SOCAP's Money + Meaning podcast ([SOCAP Global](https://socapglobal.com/2021/04/scaling-responsible-innovation-in-lending-with-jacob-haar-of-community-investment-management-episode-62-of-money-meaning/)).

## The model: financing the financiers

CIM does not lend to small businesses or consumers directly. It provides strategic, asset-backed debt financing to technology-enabled lenders — the fintechs that underwrite, monitor and collect the underlying loans — so those lenders can demonstrate and scale responsible products ([Community Investment Management](https://cim-llc.com/assets/CIM-Operating-Principles-2026-07-31.pdf)). The firm says its facilities isolate bankruptcy risk and create substantial downside protection, and that it backs partner lenders early, when mainstream capital is not yet willing to fund them ([Community Investment Management](https://cim-llc.com/assets/CIM-Operating-Principles-2026-07-31.pdf)).

The distinction from venture equity matters to how CIM works with founders. Equity investors take early-stage risk for a share of a big upside; a credit investor is paid a fixed return and must be comfortable it will be repaid even if the business disappoints, so it underwrites downside scenarios and needs the borrower to hold recoverable assets — cash flow, revenue, receivables or contracts ([Sure Shot Entrepreneur](https://podcast.sure.ventures/episodes/tsse69-venture-debt-jacob-cim?t=1m21s)). Haar's argument is that once a fintech has a loan portfolio of any maturity, funding it with equity alone is inefficient, because venture investors expect far higher returns than the portfolio earns — so debt is the right capital for the receivables book, and CIM supplies it ([Sure Shot Entrepreneur](https://podcast.sure.ventures/episodes/tsse69-venture-debt-jacob-cim?t=1m21s)).

CIM says it seeks impact at three levels: among the underlying borrowers, with its fintech partners, and across the lending ecosystem, where proven responsible products are meant to pull mainstream institutions in ([Community Investment Management](https://cim-llc.com/assets/CIM-Operating-Principles-2026-07-31.pdf)). Its impact strategy focuses on three outcomes for users — access to affordable credit, managing volatile cash flow, and growing income — and it screens partners with an impact scorecard that weighs the design, terms and underwriting practices of their lending products, mapping results to the UN Sustainable Development Goals ([Community Investment Management](https://cim-llc.com/assets/CIM-Operating-Principles-2026-07-31.pdf)).

## Scale and track record

CIM's assets under management exceeded $1 billion when it joined the American Fintech Council in December 2024 ([American Fintech Council](https://fintechcouncil.org/press-releases/american-fintech-council-afc-adds-community-investment-management-cim-as-newest-strategic-partner)). Its own disclosure dated July 31, 2026 puts current covered assets at approximately $1.44 billion and states that, to date, it has enabled over $37.7 billion of credit to nearly 21.5 million borrowers in North America and emerging markets — figures the firm reports about itself, not an independent audit ([Community Investment Management](https://cim-llc.com/assets/CIM-Operating-Principles-2026-07-31.pdf)).

The firm's earliest public commitment set the pattern: in June 2015 it announced a Clinton Global Initiative America Commitment to Action to provide $300 million of responsible and transparent financing to 9,500 small businesses in the [United States](/places/united-states/) over two years, investing through technology-driven lenders alongside LendingClub and Accion Venture Lab, with $75 million dedicated to business loans through the LendingClub platform ([Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2015/06/69237-marketplace-lending-firm-community-investment-management-joins-lending-club-announces-300m-financing-towards-9500-u-s-small-businesses/)). Most of the small businesses it funds are owned by women, minorities or military veterans, and the commitment was described as the first significant impact investment in online marketplace lending ([Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2015/06/69237-marketplace-lending-firm-community-investment-management-joins-lending-club-announces-300m-financing-towards-9500-u-s-small-businesses/)).

## The CircleUp relationship

CIM's longest-documented partnership is with [CircleUp](/organizations/circleup/), the consumer-brands investment platform. When CircleUp launched CircleUp Credit Advisors in July 2017 to issue working-capital loans to consumer packaged goods companies, CIM supplied the $20 million credit facility that got the program off the ground ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/07/26/circleup-credit-advisors/)). The loans funded purchase orders, inventory and cash-flow gaps while brands waited on late receivables; CircleUp had already issued 50 and expected about 200 over the year ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/07/26/circleup-credit-advisors/)). Haar said the partnership fit "right into his firm's sweet spot," arguing that most banks are uninterested in lending to small businesses and many available lenders are not transparent, and expected the recycled credit line to fund more than $100 million of cumulative originations ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/07/26/circleup-credit-advisors/)).

The relationship deepened. In 2019 CircleUp Credit Advisors announced it had raised more than $200 million for non-dilutive working-capital financing, with a facility led by Community Investment Management and investors including Michigan Retirement Systems and Pacific Life Insurance Company; by then it had originated over $300 million in loans since the 2017 launch ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/circleup-200-million-funding/)). The same year, Manatt represented CircleUp in the closing of a $150 million financing transaction with CIM, whose proceeds provided additional lending capital, levered returns for CircleUp's new investment fund, and refinanced its existing loan purchase and participation facility with CIM ([Manatt](https://www.Manatt.com/insights/news/2019/manatt-represents-circleup-in-$150-million-financi)).

## Consumer lending, emerging markets, and standards

CIM also funds consumer financial-health products. In April 2021 it agreed to provide $75 million to Brigit, a financial-health app, so members could manage cash flow without incurring overdraft fees or turning to predatory payday lenders — a pressing issue, Haar said, for the more than 100 million Americans living paycheck to paycheck ([Brigit](https://www.hellobrigit.com/learn/brigit-announces-75m-in-debt-financing-from-social-impact-fund-cim)). Brigit, launched in 2019, said it had served over 1 million members and saved them over $250 million in fees ([Brigit](https://www.hellobrigit.com/learn/brigit-announces-75m-in-debt-financing-from-social-impact-fund-cim)). Calvert Impact, an investor in CIM, profiles two more of its fintech partners: Empower, whose cash-advance product helps users avoid overdraft and late fees, and Gynger, a New York platform that finances businesses' software and technology purchases ([Calvert Impact](https://calvertimpact.org/investing/partner/community-investment-management-llc-cim)).

CIM has also moved back into emerging markets, where Haar began his career. In May 2023 Holland & Knight advised CIM on a $100 million structured loan facility to Fairplay, a Mexico City fintech financing small and medium-sized e-commerce companies, which had provided more than $125 million in financing in the Mexican market since 2019 ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/news/pressreleases/2023/05/holland-knight-represents-community-investment-management)). Mexico and Latin America are among the largest technology-finance opportunities, with more than 400 million underserved consumers, according to the announcement ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/news/pressreleases/2023/05/holland-knight-represents-community-investment-management)). Earlier portfolio names include Salary Finance, which partners with employers to offer workers low-cost loans, and Founders First Capital Partners, a fund providing revenue-based financing to diverse-led businesses ([SOCAP Global](https://socapglobal.com/2021/04/scaling-responsible-innovation-in-lending-with-jacob-haar-of-community-investment-management-episode-62-of-money-meaning/)).

CIM pairs its capital with advocacy. It is a founding signatory to the Operating Principles for Impact Management, co-authored the Small Business Borrowers' Bill of Rights, participates in the Responsible Business Lending Coalition, and is active in Impact Capital Managers and the Global Impact Investing Network ([Community Investment Management](https://cim-llc.com/assets/CIM-Operating-Principles-2026-07-31.pdf)). In December 2024 it joined the American Fintech Council; the council's chief executive called CIM "a leading innovator in expanding access to responsible lending within underserved communities" ([American Fintech Council](https://fintechcouncil.org/press-releases/american-fintech-council-afc-adds-community-investment-management-cim-as-newest-strategic-partner)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What does Community Investment Management do?**
CIM is an institutional impact investment manager that provides strategic, asset-backed debt capital to fintech lenders, so those lenders can scale responsible credit products for small businesses and underserved borrowers in the United States and emerging markets ([American Fintech Council](https://fintechcouncil.org/press-releases/american-fintech-council-afc-adds-community-investment-management-cim-as-newest-strategic-partner); [Community Investment Management](https://cim-llc.com/assets/CIM-Operating-Principles-2026-07-31.pdf)).

**Who founded Community Investment Management?**
Jacob Haar is the firm's co-founder and managing partner. He previously worked in microfinance for Save the Children in Azerbaijan and co-founded Minlam Asset Management, a fund backing micro, small and medium enterprise lenders in emerging markets ([SOCAP Global](https://socapglobal.com/2021/04/scaling-responsible-innovation-in-lending-with-jacob-haar-of-community-investment-management-episode-62-of-money-meaning/)).

**How big is Community Investment Management?**
Its assets under management exceeded $1 billion in December 2024, and its own July 2026 disclosure puts covered assets at about $1.44 billion, with over $37.7 billion of credit enabled to nearly 21.5 million borrowers since its founding — the latter figures are company-reported ([American Fintech Council](https://fintechcouncil.org/press-releases/american-fintech-council-afc-adds-community-investment-management-cim-as-newest-strategic-partner); [Community Investment Management](https://cim-llc.com/assets/CIM-Operating-Principles-2026-07-31.pdf)).

**What is CIM's relationship with CircleUp?**
CIM supplied the $20 million credit facility that launched CircleUp Credit Advisors' working-capital loans in 2017, led the financing facility behind its 2019 raise of more than $200 million, and closed a $150 million financing transaction with CircleUp the same year ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/07/26/circleup-credit-advisors/); [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/circleup-200-million-funding/); [Manatt](https://www.Manatt.com/insights/news/2019/manatt-represents-circleup-in-$150-million-financi)).
