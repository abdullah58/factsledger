## Overview

The Palm Beach Post is the daily newspaper of Palm Beach County, Florida, serving the county and parts of the Treasure Coast. Its defining journalistic honor came in 1970, when photographer Dallas Kinney won the [Pulitzer Prize](https://factsledger.com/events/pulitzer-prize/) for Feature Photography for "Migration to Misery," his portfolio of pictures of Florida migrant workers ([The Pulitzer Prizes](http://www.pulitzer.org/prize-winners-by-year/1970)). The paper was owned by Cox Enterprises from 1969 until 2018, when it was sold to GateHouse Media's parent for $49.25 million — and a year later it was folded into Gannett in the $1.4 billion GateHouse–Gannett merger ([Akron Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/2018/03/28/palm-beach-post-daily-news/7339179007/); [Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2019/the-gatehouse-takeover-of-gannett-has-been-finalized/)).

## "Migration to Misery": the 1970 Pulitzer

The series that won the paper's only Pulitzer began with two new hires. Dallas Kinney, described as an immensely talented visual journalist, had joined the Post from the Miami Herald along with his writing partner Kent Pollock; together they produced a series on Florida's migrant laborers called "Migration to Misery," and Kinney's photographs captured the Pulitzer for Feature Photography ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/archive/2006/recalling-the-pulitzer-rush/)). The newsroom that celebrated was strikingly young — the average age of the Post staff was about 26, the managing editor Dave Lawrence was 28 — and the announcement arrived on the same day as the Kent State shootings, whose iconic photograph would win a Pulitzer the following year ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/archive/2006/recalling-the-pulitzer-rush/)).

The sobering coda: while the story and pictures brought some initial changes to the migrants' lives, the Post went back 30 years later and found that things had not changed that much ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/archive/2006/recalling-the-pulitzer-rush/)).

## Cox decades and the 2018 sale

The paper's institutional memory runs through two mid-century names. In 1947, John Holliday Perry bought the publications from the estate of E.R. Bradley, and Charles Francis Coe was named editor-publisher; Coe received the Freedoms Foundation's George Washington Honor Medal on 21 November 1949 ([New York Social Diary](https://www.newyorksocialdiary.com/palm-beach-esq-historical-society-adds-photo-collection/)). Decades later, the Post and Daily News photo morgue — roughly a million images — was donated to the Historical Society of Palm Beach County ([New York Social Diary](https://www.newyorksocialdiary.com/palm-beach-esq-historical-society-adds-photo-collection/)).

Cox Enterprises bought the paper in 1969 and owned it for nearly 50 years ([Akron Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/2018/03/28/palm-beach-post-daily-news/7339179007/)). In March 2018, New Media Investment Group — the parent of GateHouse Media, then owner of more than 140 daily newspapers — agreed to buy the Post and the Palm Beach Daily News for $49.25 million, with closing expected by May ([Akron Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/2018/03/28/palm-beach-post-daily-news/7339179007/)). At the time the flagship's circulation was about 80,000 daily and 102,000 on Sundays, while the Daily News — "The Shiny Sheet," then 120 years old — circulated more than 4,500 daily from October to May ([Akron Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/2018/03/28/palm-beach-post-daily-news/7339179007/)). Cox kept the newspaper's building and seven acres on Dixie Highway and Belvedere, valued at $15.4 million ([Akron Beacon Journal](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/news/2018/03/28/palm-beach-post-daily-news/7339179007/)). Then, in 2019, GateHouse's parent bought Gannett for about $1.4 billion, and the combined company — the largest newspaper group in the country — took the Gannett name, headquartered in McLean, Virginia ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2019/the-gatehouse-takeover-of-gannett-has-been-finalized/)).

## The Post in 2026: Trump-era Palm Beach

In a county that has become the center of Trump-world gravity, the Post's recent signature work has been chronicling the family. On 18 September 2026, the paper published a detailed timeline reconstructing Donald Trump Jr.'s relationship with [Bettina Anderson](/people/bettina-anderson/) through their May 21, 2026 wedding ([The Palm Beach Post](https://www.palmbeachpost.com/story/news/trump/2026/09/18/bettina-trump-wedding-timeline-russian-oligarch-kremlev-bahamas/91805220007/)). Ten days earlier, its roundup of celebrity schoolchildren reported that [Barron Trump](/people/barron-trump/) was entering his junior year at NYU's Stern School of Business in fall 2026 — having started in September 2024 as part of the class of 2028, after graduating from Oxbridge Academy on 17 May 2024 ([The Palm Beach Post](https://www.palmbeachpost.com/story/news/2026/09/08/celebrity-kids-college-tom-brady-moynahan-kai-trump-tiger-woods-florida-barron/91652861007/)).
