## Overview

Canapi Ventures is a Wilmington, North Carolina-based venture capital firm that invests in early- to growth-stage fintech companies, backed by a network of banks — the Canapi Alliance — that also serves as a distribution channel for its portfolio companies ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/01/23/canapi-captures-545m-funds/)). It launched its first venture funds at $545 million in 2020 and closed a $750 million second fund in 2023, backing companies including [Alloy](/organizations/alloy/), Built, and Greenlight ([Business North Carolina](https://businessnc.com/wilmingtons-canapi-raises-750m-in-second-fund/)).

This profile covers the venture firm Canapi Ventures — not Live Oak Bank itself, though the two share founders and a Wilmington campus. Most coverage is fragmented fund announcements and Wilmington local reporting; this profile assembles the arc from its bank-alliance model through both funds to its best-known exit.

## A bank-backed model

Canapi's pitch is that its limited partners are also its customers' customers. When its first funds launched in January 2020, they had raised $545 million from limited partners including more than 35 banks and strategic investors, incorporating 23 of the 100 largest banks in the United States by asset size, plus trade bodies including the American Bankers Association and the Independent Community Bankers Association ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/01/23/canapi-captures-545m-funds/)). The firm invests in early- to growth-stage financial technology developers and gives them distribution leverage through those LPs — banks that want innovative technology and startups that want bank customers ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/01/23/canapi-captures-545m-funds/)). Co-managing partner Gene Ludwig framed it as aligning both sides: "We believe our model is unique in that it aligns the interests of both banks that want to use innovative technologies as well as the companies creating those technologies and represents a true 'win-win' for all parties involved" ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/01/23/canapi-captures-545m-funds/)).

The model held up at the second raise. About 70% of Canapi's funds have come from banks and other financial services companies, with the balance from wealthy individuals, family offices, and other institutions, and nearly 100% of the banks and financial firms that invested in the first fund reupped for the second, joined by about 30 new banks ([Business North Carolina](https://businessnc.com/wilmingtons-canapi-raises-750m-in-second-fund/)). Backers named in trade coverage include the American Bankers Association, Truist Bank, and Fifth Third Bank ([Bank Automation News](https://bankautomationnews.com/allposts/core/fintech-funding-fintech-venture-fund-canapi-raised-750m/)). Managing partner Gene Ludwig summed up the model as connecting "high-quality fintech companies to our extensive network of banks and strategic partners, creating strong symbiotic value in this important ecosystem" ([Bank Automation News](https://bankautomationnews.com/allposts/core/fintech-funding-fintech-venture-fund-canapi-raised-750m/)). The firm's roots run through Wilmington's Live Oak Bank: co-founder and president Neil Underwood co-founded Live Oak Bank in 2008 alongside its chairman and CEO Chip Mahan, and while at the bank co-founded nCino, a cloud-based banking and loan-origination software company that spun out in 2011 and went public in 2020 ([WilmingtonBiz](https://www.wilmingtonbiz.com/banking_and_finance/2026/10/30/canapi_ventures_bets_on_the_future_of_fintech/27978)).

## Two funds, one distribution network

Canapi was already investing before its first funds formally launched: it took part in construction-lending platform Built Technologies' $31 million Series B in April 2019 and was listed as a participant in Greenlight Financial's $16 million Series A in early 2018 ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/01/23/canapi-captures-545m-funds/)). Most of the first fund went into 20 firms, and its investments included Greenlight, Island, Alloy, and Built, with typical checks of $10 million to as much as $50 million ([Business North Carolina](https://businessnc.com/wilmingtons-canapi-raises-750m-in-second-fund/)). Trade coverage of the second fund likewise lists Alloy — the anti-fraud fintech — among Fund I's investments, alongside Thoropass, Posh, and Greenlight ([Bank Automation News](https://bankautomationnews.com/allposts/core/fintech-funding-fintech-venture-fund-canapi-raised-750m/)).

Fund II, closed in December 2023 at $750 million, took total assets under management past $1.4 billion and widened the aperture beyond core fintech into AI governance, cybersecurity, and the intersection of financial services and climate technology, driven by what investors saw as continued demand across AI, lending, payments, fraud, and real estate technology ([Business North Carolina](https://businessnc.com/wilmingtons-canapi-raises-750m-in-second-fund/)). Perhaps its biggest success so far is Finaxct, a banking-software company that Fortune 500 firm Fiserv acquired in 2022 for about $800 million, returning about four times Canapi's investment ([Business North Carolina](https://businessnc.com/wilmingtons-canapi-raises-750m-in-second-fund/)). By October 2026 the firm was nearing the end of deploying Fund II, with about $150 million remaining of the $750 million, having raised approximately $1.4 billion across its two funds ([WilmingtonBiz](https://www.wilmingtonbiz.com/banking_and_finance/2026/10/30/canapi_ventures_bets_on_the_future_of_fintech/27978)). Recent deployment reflects the widened thesis: banking, payments, and wealth technology plus AI, cybersecurity, and DevOps, particularly where banks are spending most to modernize ([WilmingtonBiz](https://www.wilmingtonbiz.com/banking_and_finance/2026/10/30/canapi_ventures_bets_on_the_future_of_fintech/27978)). In April it led a $9 million round for Minneapolis-based Onsetto, a fintech helping banks build commercial-banking platforms, and owns around 30% of the business ([WilmingtonBiz](https://www.wilmingtonbiz.com/banking_and_finance/2026/10/30/canapi_ventures_bets_on_the_future_of_fintech/27978)). In addition to its Wilmington base, the firm has offices in Washington, D.C., [New York City](/places/new-york-city/), and [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) ([Business North Carolina](https://businessnc.com/wilmingtons-canapi-raises-750m-in-second-fund/)).

## What this profile does not claim

- A 2018 launch of "Canapi Inc." as a Live Oak subsidiary appears in a wire release that was not read in full in this pass; the profile therefore dates the venture funds to 2020 and does not assert the earlier corporate lineage.
- The Onsetto round is dated only as "April" in the October 2026 article; no year is asserted for it.
- No third fund has been verified; none is claimed, and deployment figures are as of the October 2026 article, not current to the profile date.
