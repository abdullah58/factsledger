## Overview

Chime is a banking app that says it is definitely not a bank. Its own positioning — "a financial technology company, not a bank" — is the legal and commercial core of the business: Chime provides the app and the brand while partner banks hold the deposits, and it earns its money the old-fashioned fintech way, from interchange fees collected every time a customer swipes a debit card ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/chime-set-long-awaited-market-debut-after-864-million-us-ipo-2025-06-12/), [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/how-banking-app-chime-went-from-broke-to-ipo-billions/493263)). Founded in 2012 by Chris Britt and Ryan King, it targets the roughly 200 million Americans earning $100,000 or less a year with no-fee accounts, early direct-deposit access, and a mobile-first experience ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/chime-set-long-awaited-market-debut-after-864-million-us-ipo-2025-06-12/)).

## A hundred no's

Chime's founding story is a rejection epic. "We founded the company in 2012, and the first, really, five or six years was very difficult," co-founder and original CTO Ryan King told TechCrunch. In early 2016, trying to raise an extension to the Series A, "we pitched 100 investors, maybe more, and got 100 no's" — the company was, in King's word, broke ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/how-banking-app-chime-went-from-broke-to-ipo-billions/493263)). The lifeline came from a single seed investor willing to take a bet: Lauren Kolodny, now a co-founder of Acrew Capital, who stood on the podium ringing the Nasdaq opening bell on IPO day nearly a decade later ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/how-banking-app-chime-went-from-broke-to-ipo-billions/493263)).

What the 100 rejections missed was the account-primary strategy. Two-thirds of Chime's customers now use it as their direct-deposit and primary account relationship, Britt told CNBC — people tend to switch only at life-change moments, and once they switch, they stay ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/how-banking-app-chime-went-from-broke-to-ipo-billions/493263)). Revenue followed: $1.3 billion in 2023, $1.7 billion in 2024, and profitability in the first quarter of 2025, per its SEC filing ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/how-banking-app-chime-went-from-broke-to-ipo-billions/493263)).

## The $25 billion haircut — then the pop

Chime's last private round, in August 2021, valued it at $25 billion after raising $750 million; in total it took $2.65 billion from private investors, including Yuri Milner's DST Global, General Atlantic, and ICONIQ ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/chime-set-long-awaited-market-debut-after-864-million-us-ipo-2025-06-12/)). By the time it filed to go public, the fintech valuation climate had cooled dramatically — the IPO priced at $27 a share, targeting roughly half the 2021 valuation.

The market disagreed with the discount. On June 12, 2025, Chime's shares opened at $43 and surged 59% in their Nasdaq debut under the ticker CHYM, valuing the company at $18.4 billion; the company and selling investors placed 32 million shares to raise $864 million, with Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and J.P. Morgan leading the offering ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/chime-set-long-awaited-market-debut-after-864-million-us-ipo-2025-06-12/)). Analysts called it a potential bellwether for the long-delayed fintech IPO queue — Klarna, Gemini, Cerebras — waiting behind it ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/chime-set-long-awaited-market-debut-after-864-million-us-ipo-2025-06-12/)).
