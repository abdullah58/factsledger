## Overview

The Canada Revenue Agency is the revenue service of the Canadian federal government, and of most provincial and territorial governments, collecting taxes, administering tax law, and delivering benefit programs and tax credits ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/home/record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=31)). In the year to 31 March 2025 it administered $720.8 billion in revenues and pension contributions — $440.4 billion for the federal government, $191.9 billion for provinces, territories and First Nations, and $88.6 billion for the Canada Pension Plan ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report/2024-25-financial-statements/cra-fs-administered-activities.html)).

The agency is younger than its history suggests. Tax collection in [Canada](/places/canada/) dates to Confederation, but the CRA itself was created only in 1999, when Revenue Canada was converted from a government department into an agency, and it took its current name in 2005, after customs was spun off into the Canada Border Services Agency ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/home/record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=31)). It now runs on a scale few Canadians grasp: 53,585 full-time equivalent staff processed more than 33.2 million individual tax returns in 2024-25, 92 per cent of them filed digitally, and issued 19.1 million refunds ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report.html)).

## From customs houses to a modern agency

The lineage starts at Confederation. In 1867 two departments were established: Inland Revenue, which collected excise duties, internal taxes and stamp duties, and Customs, which collected customs duties and canal tolls ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/home/record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=31)). The two merged in 1918 into the Department of Customs and Inland Revenue, renamed the Department of Customs and Excise in 1921, and the Department of National Revenue Act of 1927 renamed it again as the Department of National Revenue — the same act that set up a separate department to collect income tax ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/home/record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=31)). The department became known as Revenue Canada in the 1970s ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/home/record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=31)).

The modern break came in 1999. On 29 April that year Parliament passed the Canada Customs and Revenue Agency Act, creating the Canada Customs and Revenue Agency, and the change from department to agency took effect on 1 November 1999 — intended, the archives record, "to modernize the organization and strengthen its focus on leadership, innovation, and client service" ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/home/record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=31)). The new agency's mandate covered taxation, customs and border protection, but that arrangement lasted only four years: on 12 December 2003 the government created the Canada Border Services Agency for customs operations, and two years later, on 12 December 2005, legislation legally changed the agency's name to the Canada Revenue Agency ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/home/record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=31)).

## What the agency actually does

The CRA's work splits into two very different jobs: taking money in, and paying money out. On the revenue side, the 2024-25 administered statements show where the $720.8 billion came from: individual and trust income tax of $234.4 billion and corporate income tax of $97.0 billion for the federal government, plus $32.1 billion in employment insurance premiums and $22.3 billion in GST ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report/2024-25-financial-statements/cra-fs-administered-activities.html)). The agency also collects on behalf of the provinces — $109.7 billion in provincial personal income tax alone — and administers the Canada Pension Plan's $88.6 billion in contributions ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report/2024-25-financial-statements/cra-fs-administered-activities.html)).

Enforcement is the other half of the intake job. In 2024-25 the agency resolved $86.6 billion of collectible tax debt — 46.9 per cent more than before the pandemic — and individual compliance work produced $8.2 billion in fiscal impact, or $10.75 billion including the verification of COVID-19 individual benefit payments ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report.html)). It also resolved more than 1.3 million delinquent GST/HST returns through its delinquent-filer program ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report.html)).

## The benefits machine

The less visible half of the agency is the money it pays out. The CRA administers 210 federal, provincial and territorial benefit and credit programs, and issued over $56 billion in benefits in 2024-25 ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report.html)). The largest single stream is children's benefits: $28.6 billion in Canada benefit programs for children, followed by the $12.7 billion Canada Carbon Rebate and the $5.2 billion Canada Workers Benefit ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report/2024-25-financial-statements/cra-fs-administered-activities.html)).

The flagship is the Canada Child Benefit, a tax-free monthly payment for families raising children under 18, administered by the CRA and recalculated every July from the previous year's tax return ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/child-family-benefits/canada-child-benefit-overview.html)). For the July 2026 to June 2027 payment period, the maximum is $8,157 a year per child under six and $6,883 a year per child aged six to seventeen, paid in full to families with adjusted family net income under $38,237 and phased down above that ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/child-family-benefits/canada-child-benefit/how-much.html)). The agency also reaches people who never file on their own: more than 18,000 Community Volunteer Income Tax Program volunteers helped 857,540 individuals file returns in 2024-25 ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report.html)).

## Governance, service strain, and the 100-day plan

The CRA answers to Parliament through the Minister of National Revenue — François-Philippe Champagne, who presented the 2024-25 results report ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report.html)). Day-to-day operations sit with the Commissioner, the agency's chief executive; Bob Hamilton held that role as of August 2025, when he signed the administered financial statements ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report/2024-25-financial-statements/cra-fs-administered-activities.html)). A 15-member Board of Management oversees the agency's organization and management — eleven members nominated by the provinces and territories — but it does not administer or enforce legislation and has no access to confidential taxpayer information ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/canada-revenue-agency-structure-operational-framework.html)). The Taxpayers' Ombudsperson, established in 2007, operates independently as a special advisor to the Minister under the Taxpayer Bill of Rights ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/home/record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=31)).

The agency's own reporting is unusually frank about where service falls short. In 2024-25 it met only 20 of its 33 external service-standard targets ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report.html)). Only 18 per cent of telephone enquiries were answered within the 15-minute standard, as call demand outran agent capacity, and only 38 per cent of Disability Tax Credit applications were processed within standard after applications rose 18 per cent on top of the previous year's 30 per cent surge ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report.html)). The minister directed a 100-day Service Improvement Plan to strengthen services, improve access and reduce delays ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report.html)). The same report notes the agency published its first annual report on employee misconduct and wrongdoing in January 2025 ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report.html)).

## The digital turn — and the Blue J connection

The agency is pushing hard toward digital-first service. Beyond the 92 per cent digital filing rate, it invited 2 million people to auto-complete and file their 2024 returns using SimpleFile by Phone, and 1.6 million new users verified their identity through the Document Verification Service instead of waiting for a mailed security code ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report.html)). Its web pages drew nearly 311 million visits in the year ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report.html)).

The most telling detail is who supplies the agency's research tools. [Blue J](/organizations/blue-j/), the [Toronto](/places/toronto/) legal-tech company founded in 2015 by [University of Toronto](/universities/university-of-toronto/) law professors, applies artificial intelligence to tax research — and its platform is now used by major firms and the Canada Revenue Agency itself, according to the Globe and Mail ([University of Toronto News](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/u-t-startup-blue-j-legal-raises-167-million-globe-and-mail)). The regulator that interprets Canada's tax law does part of that interpretation with software built in a university law faculty. In 2024-25 the agency also adopted a generative-AI chatbot to answer general questions from Canadians ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report.html)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What does the Canada Revenue Agency do?** It administers federal tax law and most provincial and territorial tax programs, collects income tax, GST/HST, excise taxes and CPP contributions, and delivers benefit programs such as the Canada Child Benefit ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/home/record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=31)). In 2024-25 it administered $720.8 billion in revenues and issued over $56 billion in benefits ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report.html)).

**When was the CRA created?** The agency was created on 1 November 1999 as the Canada Customs and Revenue Agency, replacing the Department of National Revenue (Revenue Canada), and renamed the Canada Revenue Agency on 12 December 2005 after customs moved to the new Canada Border Services Agency ([Library and Archives Canada](https://recherche-collection-search.bac-lac.gc.ca/eng/home/record?app=fonandcol&IdNumber=31)).

**How much is the Canada Child Benefit?** For July 2026 to June 2027 the maximum is $8,157 a year per child under six and $6,883 a year per child aged six to seventeen, paid in full below $38,237 of adjusted family net income and phased down above it ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/child-family-benefits/canada-child-benefit/how-much.html)). The benefit is tax-free and recalculated every July.

**Does the CRA use AI?** Yes — its platform of choice for tax research is Blue J, the AI tax-research tool built at the University of Toronto, and in 2024-25 the agency also deployed a generative-AI chatbot for general questions ([University of Toronto News](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/u-t-startup-blue-j-legal-raises-167-million-globe-and-mail); [Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/departmental-performance-reports/2024-25-departmental-results-report.html)).

**Who oversees the CRA?** The Minister of National Revenue is accountable to Parliament; a 15-member Board of Management oversees organization and management but cannot enforce legislation or see taxpayer information; and the independent Taxpayers' Ombudsperson, created in 2007, advises the Minister on service to taxpayers ([Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/corporate/about-canada-revenue-agency-cra/canada-revenue-agency-structure-operational-framework.html)).
