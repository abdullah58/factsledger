## Overview

Credit Suisse was a Swiss bank founded in 1856 to finance Switzerland's railways, which grew into one of the country's two global banks — and then collapsed in a fortnight in March 2023, when UBS bought it for CHF 3 billion in a government-engineered rescue ([Reuters](https://985theriver.com/2024/05/31/how-credit-suisse-evolved-until-its-merger-with-ubs/)). Its legal end came later: on 31 May 2024 UBS completed the merger of the two parent banks, Credit Suisse AG was deregistered in Zurich, and the 168-year-old name ceased to exist as a separate entity ([UBS](https://ubs.com/content/news/en/2024/05/31/pbm/_jcr_content/root/contentarea/mainpar/toplevelgrid/col_2/linklistnewlook_copy/actionbutton.1030517182.file/PS9jb250ZW50L2RhbS9hc3NldHMvbmV3cy8yMDI0LzA1LzMxLzIwMjQwNTMxLW1yLXBibS1jb21wbGV0aW9uLWVuLnBkZg==/20240531-mr-pbm-completion-en.pdf)). The most controversial act of the rescue — the write-down of about CHF 16.5 billion of the bank's AT1 bonds — was ruled unlawful by a Swiss court in October 2025, a decision now under appeal ([Federal Administrative Court](https://www.bvger.ch/en/newsroom/media-releases/unlawful-write-off-of-at1-capital-instruments-2385)).

Credit Suisse's fall was not a single bad bet but the end of a decade of scandals and losses: a $5.5 billion hit from the Archegos fund collapse and a $10 billion Greensill fund freeze in 2021, a CHF 7.3 billion loss in 2022, and a run of client withdrawals that no restructuring could stop ([Reuters](https://985theriver.com/2024/05/31/how-credit-suisse-evolved-until-its-merger-with-ubs/); [AFP](https://www.neuerheinischezeitung.de/en/Economy/122470-credit-suisse-posts-biggest-loss-since-2008-financial-crisis.html)).

## A railway bank that became a global bank

Politician and business leader Alfred Escher founded the bank in 1856 as the Schweizerische Kreditanstalt, to finance the expansion of Switzerland's railroad network and promote Swiss industrialisation ([Reuters](https://985theriver.com/2024/05/31/how-credit-suisse-evolved-until-its-merger-with-ubs/)). It moved to its long-time headquarters on Zurich's Paradeplatz in 1876, and the 1977 Chiasso Affair — a money-laundering scandal that produced a historic loss — pushed it toward becoming an international financial group ([Reuters](https://985theriver.com/2024/05/31/how-credit-suisse-evolved-until-its-merger-with-ubs/)). The global build-out came through Wall Street: CS Holding bought a 45% stake in First Boston in 1988, a 1997 reorganisation created Credit Suisse Group and bought the insurer Winterthur, and the group bought Donaldson, Lufkin & Jenrette in 2000 ([Reuters](https://985theriver.com/2024/05/31/how-credit-suisse-evolved-until-its-merger-with-ubs/)).

By the 2000s Credit Suisse was a universal bank spanning Swiss private banking, wealth management and a large investment bank, and it survived the 2007–08 financial crisis without a state bailout — unlike its rival UBS ([Reuters](https://985theriver.com/2024/05/31/how-credit-suisse-evolved-until-its-merger-with-ubs/)). That survival became a point of pride, and then a trap: the investment bank that had been kept large kept producing the losses that would eventually destroy the group. The bank even tested new technology late in its life, joining [Axoni](/organizations/axoni/)'s October 2016 working group with [Citi](/organizations/citigroup/) and [J.P. Morgan](/organizations/jpmorgan-chase/) to test blockchain processing of equity swaps ([The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/dlt-provider-secures-major-bank-funding/)).

## Archegos, Greensill and the loss of confidence

The end began in March 2021, when the U.S. investment fund Archegos Capital imploded and saddled Credit Suisse with a $5.5 billion loss, while in the same month it had to freeze $10 billion in supply-chain finance funds linked to the insolvent financier Greensill Capital, products it had marketed to clients as low-risk ([Reuters](https://985theriver.com/2024/05/31/how-credit-suisse-evolved-until-its-merger-with-ubs/)). One figure is contested: some contemporary reporting put the Archegos charge at $4.7 billion rather than $5.5 billion, and this profile follows the Reuters timeline while flagging the difference. The investment bank had already lost CHF 3.7 billion in 2021, and the scandals kept coming — covert surveillance, executive departures, a chairman resigning over quarantine breaches ([Reuters](https://985theriver.com/2024/05/31/how-credit-suisse-evolved-until-its-merger-with-ubs/); [AFP](https://www.neuerheinischezeitung.de/en/Economy/122470-credit-suisse-posts-biggest-loss-since-2008-financial-crisis.html)).

In October 2022 the bank announced a sweeping plan to refocus on banking for the wealthy, raise CHF 4 billion in new capital and spin out its investment bank under the revived First Boston name, with Saudi National Bank taking a stake of up to 9.9% ([Reuters](https://985theriver.com/2024/05/31/how-credit-suisse-evolved-until-its-merger-with-ubs/)). It did not work: Credit Suisse reported a CHF 7.3 billion net loss for 2022, its biggest since the 2008 crisis, after CHF 110.5 billion of client assets flowed out in the fourth quarter alone ([AFP](https://www.neuerheinischezeitung.de/en/Economy/122470-credit-suisse-posts-biggest-loss-since-2008-financial-crisis.html)). That stat, source and implication is the core of the collapse — a bank can survive bad trades, but not clients withdrawing CHF 110.5 billion in three months.

## The forced sale to UBS and the AT1 reckoning

March 2023 finished the bank in days. Its 2022 annual report disclosed "material weaknesses" in internal controls over financial reporting, and shares fell as much as 30% after Saudi National Bank said regulatory constraints stopped it providing more support ([Reuters](https://985theriver.com/2024/05/31/how-credit-suisse-evolved-until-its-merger-with-ubs/)). The Swiss National Bank extended a $54 billion lifeline, and after frantic negotiations UBS agreed to buy Credit Suisse for CHF 3 billion in stock — about US$3.2 billion, though some accounts give $3.25 billion — and assume up to CHF 5 billion in losses, in a deal engineered by the Swiss authorities ([Reuters](https://985theriver.com/2024/05/31/how-credit-suisse-evolved-until-its-merger-with-ubs/)). UBS completed the takeover in June 2023 ([Reuters](https://985theriver.com/2024/05/31/how-credit-suisse-evolved-until-its-merger-with-ubs/)).

The rescue's most contested element was the fate of bondholders. On 19 March 2023 the regulator FINMA ordered Credit Suisse to write down all its Additional Tier 1 bonds — about CHF 16.5 billion nominal, though most 2023 reporting said CHF 16 billion — to zero, while shareholders still received UBS stock ([FINMA](https://www.finma.ch/en/news/2025/10/20251015-meldung-bvger-at1/); [Federal Administrative Court](https://www.bvger.ch/en/newsroom/media-releases/unlawful-write-off-of-at1-capital-instruments-2385)). On 1 October 2025 the Swiss Federal Administrative Court revoked that decree, holding the contractual viability event had never been triggered — the bank was sufficiently capitalised — and that no sufficient legal basis existed, with the emergency ordinance provision behind it unconstitutional in several respects ([Federal Administrative Court](https://www.bvger.ch/en/newsroom/media-releases/unlawful-write-off-of-at1-capital-instruments-2385)). FINMA will appeal to the Federal Supreme Court, and no remedy has been ordered ([FINMA](https://www.finma.ch/en/news/2025/10/20251015-meldung-bvger-at1/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was Credit Suisse founded?**
In 1856, when Alfred Escher founded the Schweizerische Kreditanstalt to finance Switzerland's railway expansion and industrialisation ([Reuters](https://985theriver.com/2024/05/31/how-credit-suisse-evolved-until-its-merger-with-ubs/)).

**Why did Credit Suisse collapse?**
A decade of scandals and losses destroyed confidence: the $5.5 billion Archegos loss and $10 billion Greensill freeze in 2021, a CHF 7.3 billion loss in 2022, and CHF 110.5 billion of client outflows in the last quarter of 2022 alone ([Reuters](https://985theriver.com/2024/05/31/how-credit-suisse-evolved-until-its-merger-with-ubs/); [AFP](https://www.neuerheinischezeitung.de/en/Economy/122470-credit-suisse-posts-biggest-loss-since-2008-financial-crisis.html)).

**How much did UBS pay for Credit Suisse?**
CHF 3 billion in UBS stock — about US$3.2 billion — plus assuming up to CHF 5 billion in losses, in a government-engineered deal agreed on 19 March 2023 and completed that June ([Reuters](https://985theriver.com/2024/05/31/how-credit-suisse-evolved-until-its-merger-with-ubs/)).

**What happened to Credit Suisse's AT1 bonds?**
FINMA ordered about CHF 16.5 billion of them written down to zero on 19 March 2023. In October 2025 a Swiss court revoked that order, finding it lacked a legal basis; FINMA is appealing and no repayment has been ordered ([Federal Administrative Court](https://www.bvger.ch/en/newsroom/media-releases/unlawful-write-off-of-at1-capital-instruments-2385); [FINMA](https://www.finma.ch/en/news/2025/10/20251015-meldung-bvger-at1/)).

**Does Credit Suisse still exist?**
No, as a separate legal entity. UBS completed the merger of the parent banks on 31 May 2024 and Credit Suisse AG was deregistered in Zurich, with UBS succeeding to all its rights and obligations ([UBS](https://ubs.com/content/news/en/2024/05/31/pbm/_jcr_content/root/contentarea/mainpar/toplevelgrid/col_2/linklistnewlook_copy/actionbutton.1030517182.file/PS9jb250ZW50L2RhbS9hc3NldHMvbmV3cy8yMDI0LzA1LzMxLzIwMjQwNTMxLW1yLXBibS1jb21wbGV0aW9uLWVuLnBkZg==/20240531-mr-pbm-completion-en.pdf)).

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not settle the Archegos loss at one figure: $5.5 billion (Reuters) versus $4.7 billion (other contemporary reporting) are both on record.
- It does not settle the AT1 size at one figure: CHF 16 billion (2023 reporting) versus about CHF 16.5 billion nominal (the 2025 court).
- It does not claim the AT1 write-down has been reversed or bondholders repaid: the court revoked the decree but ordered no remedy, and FINMA is appealing.
- It does not state that the Swiss domestic-bank merger (Credit Suisse (Schweiz) AG into UBS Switzerland AG) completed; only that it was expected in Q3 2024.
- It does not repeat the bank's own pre-collapse claims about its strategy or controls; the "material weaknesses" finding and the outflow figures speak for themselves.
