## Overview

Lynrock Lake Master Fund LP is a private investment fund based in Rye Brook, New York, founded in 2018 by Cynthia Paul, a former Soros Fund Management portfolio manager ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1734341/000101143823000289/form_sc13ga-kaleyra.htm); [MarTech Series](https://martechseries.com/predictive-ai/ai-platforms-machine-learning/yext-appoints-cynthia-paul-to-its-board-of-directors/)). The fund is managed by Lynrock Lake LP, with Lynrock Lake Partners LLC as general partner, and invests across the full capital structure of public and private technology companies ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1734341/000101143823000289/form_sc13ga-kaleyra.htm)). It is best known as the creditor that became an owner: as [CalAmp](/organizations/calamp/)'s principal secured lender, it converted about $229 million of convertible notes into equity through a prepackaged Chapter 11 and took the telematics company private in August 2024 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000095017024067683/camp-20240522.htm); [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000119312524180486/d828287d8k.htm)).

## A fund built on credit, convertibles and equities

The fund's structure is documented in its own SEC filings rather than in press coverage, because Lynrock Lake almost never talks to the media. Lynrock Lake Master Fund LP is the fund itself; Lynrock Lake LP is its investment manager, delegated full voting and investment power over the fund's securities under an investment management agreement; Lynrock Lake Partners LLC is the manager's general partner; and Cynthia Paul is the manager's Chief Investment Officer and Sole Member of the general partner ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1734341/000101143823000289/form_sc13ga-kaleyra.htm)). Both the manager and the general partner are [Delaware](/places/delaware/) entities, and the firm's principal business address is 2 International Drive, Suite 130, Rye Brook, New York ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1734341/000101143823000289/form_sc13ga-kaleyra.htm)). Paul is also the manager's Chief Executive Officer, a title confirmed in the fund's 2026 filings ([SEC](https://investors.yext.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001231919-26-000745/form3-07092026_090716.html)).

Paul's background explains the fund's approach. She joined Soros Fund Management in 2000 and managed a portfolio across corporate credit, convertible and equity securities from 2002 to 2017, then founded Lynrock Lake LP in January 2018 to invest across the full capital structure of public and private companies with a focus on technology ([MarTech Series](https://martechseries.com/predictive-ai/ai-platforms-machine-learning/yext-appoints-cynthia-paul-to-its-board-of-directors/)). She brings more than 24 years of experience as a portfolio manager taking meaningful stakes in publicly traded enterprise software companies, and has served on the boards of numerous public and private companies since 2013 ([MarTech Series](https://martechseries.com/predictive-ai/ai-platforms-machine-learning/yext-appoints-cynthia-paul-to-its-board-of-directors/)). In July 2026 she joined the board of Yext, Inc. as an independent director ([MarTech Series](https://martechseries.com/predictive-ai/ai-platforms-machine-learning/yext-appoints-cynthia-paul-to-its-board-of-directors/)).

The fund's disclosed positions show the convertible-credit playbook at work: it takes large, near-10% positions, often built through convertible notes rather than open-market stock purchases. Its stake in Kaleyra, Inc. sat exactly at the 9.99% beneficial-ownership cap in its convertible indenture, and its Yext position made Paul a director and 10% owner. ## Lender to owner: the CalAmp restructuring

Lynrock's defining deal began as a rescue loan and ended as a takeover. By late 2023 it was already CalAmp's largest creditor, holding a large majority of the company's 2.00% Convertible Senior Notes due August 2025 ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/calamp-announces-completion-of-strategic-financing-with-lynrock-lake-2023-12-18)). On December 18, 2023, CalAmp closed a $45 million strategic financing with Lynrock in the form of a term loan maturing in November 2027, amending the notes to add a security interest; Paul, quoted as the fund's Chief Investment Officer, called Lynrock "a long-standing investor in CalAmp" ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/calamp-announces-completion-of-strategic-financing-with-lynrock-lake-2023-12-18)). The loan bought CalAmp months, not a recovery: within six months the company signed over its equity to its lender.

On May 31, 2024, CalAmp and its wholly owned subsidiaries — CalAmp Wireless Networks Corporation, [LoJack](/organizations/lojack/) Global LLC and [Synovia Solutions LLC](/organizations/synovia-solutions/) — signed a Restructuring Support Agreement with Lynrock, as lender under the secured term loan and holder of the secured notes, to convert approximately $229 million of Convertible Senior Secured Notes into equity, pay allowed unsecured claims in full in cash, cancel all existing equity, and take the company private ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000095017024067683/camp-20240522.htm)). On June 3, 2024, the four companies filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions in the [U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware](/organizations/us-bankruptcy-court-district-of-delaware/) to execute the prepackaged plan ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000095017024067683/camp-20240522.htm)). Trade press at the time described Lynrock plainly as "the company's largest creditor" and reported the $229 million notes-to-equity exchange ([School Transportation News](https://stnonline.com/news/calamp-restructures-debt-via-chapter-11-bankruptcy-going-private/)).

The court confirmed the plan on July 11, 2024, and the terms were stark: all of Lynrock's secured note claims were cancelled in exchange for 100% of the new equity interests of CalAmp, all existing shares were cancelled and deregistered, and the reorganized board would initially consist of Cynthia Paul alone ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000119312524180486/d828287d8k.htm)). The $229 million debt-for-equity swap plus the amended credit agreement — seven-year maturity at the secured overnight financing rate plus 6.75% — turned a distressed lender into the sole owner of a private company ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000119312524180486/d828287d8k.htm)). CalAmp's shares, which had traded on [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/) under CAMP, were delisted on June 12, 2024 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000119312524180486/d828287d8k.htm)). On August 1, 2024, CalAmp announced it was now a private company owned by Lynrock Lake Master Fund LP, with the restructuring eliminating $230 million of debt and Paul chairing the new board; she said Lynrock had "been invested in CalAmp for nearly six years" ([School Transportation News](https://stnonline.com/industry-releases/lynrock-lake-takes-calamp-private/)).
