## Overview

Euclid Opportunities was the fintech investment arm of [ICAP](/organizations/icap/), the London interdealer broker, founded in 2011 by Steve Gibson to back the unglamorous plumbing of capital markets rather than consumer finance ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsxfy5n3z0yhg3r9883k/corner-office/the-fintech-finance-40-steve-gibson)). It was a corporate venture unit with an unusual discipline: it invested only where a startup's problem matched the interests of ICAP's Post Trade Risk and Information division ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsxfy5n3z0yhg3r9883k/corner-office/the-fintech-finance-40-steve-gibson)).

Its best-known investment was [Axoni](/organizations/axoni/), whose $18 million Series A it co-led with [Wells Fargo](/organizations/wells-fargo/) in December 2016 ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2016/12/22/wells-fargo-icap-lead-18-million-series-a-for-blockchain-startup-axoni)).

Euclid's story is really the story of its parent's transformation: ICAP became [NEX Group](/organizations/nex-group/) in 2017, Euclid became NEX Opportunities, and in 2018 CME Group agreed to buy NEX for around $5.4 billion ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/organization-management/alliances-mergers-acquisitions/3558686/nex-ceo-sees-opportunity-for-opportunities-after-cme-deal)). By 2020 both of the unit's leaders had left to found a new fintech investor, Element Ventures ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/01/21/mcfadgen-finds-element-ventures/)).

## A corporate venture arm with a back-office brief

Corporate venture capital was not a new idea when Gibson founded Euclid in 2011, but the initiative was unusual in its focus on middle- and back-office functions — collateral processing, reconciliations, risk analytics — that paralleled the interests of ICAP's post-trade division and were only beginning to attract entrepreneurial attention ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsxfy5n3z0yhg3r9883k/corner-office/the-fintech-finance-40-steve-gibson)). Gibson, who had previously worked at Calypso Technology, Ipreo and Siebel Systems, drew the line explicitly: "We are focused on the capital markets space, where the problems we are solving, such as collateral processing, are very different from issues such as digital payments" ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsxfy5n3z0yhg3r9883k/corner-office/the-fintech-finance-40-steve-gibson)). The discipline was also a sourcing advantage: Euclid sat inside a firm whose own trading desks were the customers for the startups it backed, so diligence came with a built-in pilot user in ICAP itself. Gibson led the unit from late 2010, before its formal founding in 2011, and stayed as its chief executive through the NEX years ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/01/21/mcfadgen-finds-element-ventures/)).

## The portfolio: plumbing, not payments

By mid-October 2017 Euclid's active portfolio held eight companies, and the list reads as a map of post-trade infrastructure: Duco for machine-learning reconciliations, AcadiaSoft as a joint-venture collateral hub, Axoni and Digital Asset Holdings for distributed ledgers, Cloud9 for trading communications, OpenFin for desktop technology, OpenGamma for derivatives risk analytics, and RSRCHXchange for research unbundling under MiFID II ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsxfy5n3z0yhg3r9883k/corner-office/the-fintech-finance-40-steve-gibson)). Two more names appear in other portfolio lists — Abide Financial, a regulatory reporting firm ICAP later bought outright, and ENSO Financial Analytics, acquired outright by ICAP in April 2016 after Euclid invested ([Markets Media](https://www.marketsmedia.com/nex-invests-research-innovator/)). Euclid's method was to invest and then, where the fit was strategic, to absorb: its capital came with an inside track to becoming part of the parent. The stat that captures the model: of the eight companies in the 2017 portfolio, at least two — AcadiaSoft and Axoni — later became central to the London Stock Exchange Group's post-trade build-out, with AcadiaSoft acquired by LSEG in 2023 and Axoni's post-trade technology the subject of an LSEG acquisition announced in September 2024 ([Asset Servicing Times](https://www.assetservicingtimes.com/assetservicesnews/clearingandsettlementarticle.php?article_id=14526&navigationaction=clearingandsettlementnews&newssection=Clearing%20and%20Settlement); [The TRADE](http://www.thetradenews.com/lseg-acquires-axoni-post-trade-technology/)).

## Two renames and a $5.4 billion exit

Euclid's identity changed twice in two years, both times because its parent changed. At the start of 2017 ICAP sold its voice-broking business to Tullett Prebon and renamed the remainder NEX Group; its post-trade division became NEX Optimisation, and Euclid Opportunities became NEX Opportunities, reporting to NEX Optimisation chief executive Ken Pigaga ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsxfy5n3z0yhg3r9883k/corner-office/the-fintech-finance-40-steve-gibson)). A year later, on 29 March 2018, CME Group made a formal offer to acquire NEX in a cash-and-shares deal valuing it at around $5.4 billion (£3.9 billion), centred on EBS, BrokerTec and TriOptima — but the offer documents said nothing about NEX Opportunities, even though it held seed-to-Series-C stakes in Axoni, OpenFin, Cloud9 and OpenGamma ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/organization-management/alliances-mergers-acquisitions/3558686/nex-ceo-sees-opportunity-for-opportunities-after-cme-deal)). NEX chief executive Michael Spencer said the investing practice would continue in the enlarged group. In January 2020 managing director Michael McFadgen left to join Element Ventures, founded in 2019 to invest in enterprise fintech, and Gibson followed him there ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/01/21/mcfadgen-finds-element-ventures/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What was Euclid Opportunities?**
Euclid Opportunities was the fintech investment arm of ICAP, the London interdealer broker, founded in 2011 by Steve Gibson to invest in capital-markets technology, especially middle- and back-office functions like collateral processing and reconciliations ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsxfy5n3z0yhg3r9883k/corner-office/the-fintech-finance-40-steve-gibson)). It invested only where a startup's problem matched the interests of ICAP's post-trade division, which managed risk after trades were done.

**Who founded Euclid Opportunities?**
Steve Gibson, previously of Calypso Technology, Ipreo and Siebel Systems, founded Euclid in 2011 and led it as chief executive through its years as NEX Opportunities ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsxfy5n3z0yhg3r9883k/corner-office/the-fintech-finance-40-steve-gibson); [Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/01/21/mcfadgen-finds-element-ventures/)). Gibson had led the unit from late 2010, before its formal founding, and he was already a partner at Element Ventures when McFadgen joined it in 2020.

**What did Euclid Opportunities invest in?**
Capital-markets fintech: its 2017 portfolio included Duco, AcadiaSoft, Axoni, Digital Asset Holdings, Cloud9 Technologies, OpenFin, OpenGamma and RSRCHXchange, plus Abide Financial and ENSO Financial Analytics ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsxfy5n3z0yhg3r9883k/corner-office/the-fintech-finance-40-steve-gibson); [Markets Media](https://www.marketsmedia.com/nex-invests-research-innovator/)). Abide and ENSO were both later acquired outright by ICAP following Euclid's investments.

**Did Euclid invest in Axoni?**
Yes. Euclid Opportunities co-led Axoni's $18 million Series A with Wells Fargo in December 2016, alongside [Goldman Sachs](/organizations/goldman-sachs/), J.P. Morgan, [Thomson Reuters](/organizations/thomson-reuters/), [Andreessen Horowitz](/organizations/andreessen-horowitz/) and others, taking Axoni's total funding above $20 million ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2016/12/22/wells-fargo-icap-lead-18-million-series-a-for-blockchain-startup-axoni)). Euclid had also brought ICAP and Axoni together for a blockchain proof of technology in February 2016.

**What happened to Euclid Opportunities?**
It was renamed NEX Opportunities when ICAP became NEX Group in 2017, and NEX was acquired by CME Group in 2018 for around $5.4 billion; its leaders, Steve Gibson and Michael McFadgen, left in 2020 to join Element Ventures ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/organization-management/alliances-mergers-acquisitions/3558686/nex-ceo-sees-opportunity-for-opportunities-after-cme-deal); [Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/01/21/mcfadgen-finds-element-ventures/)).
