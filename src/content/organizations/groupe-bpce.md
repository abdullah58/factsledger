## Overview

Groupe BPCE is [France](/places/france/)'s second-largest banking group, a cooperative bancassurance group built on two retail networks — Banque Populaire and Caisse d'Epargne — and the wholesale bank [Natixis](/organizations/natixis/), owned by 9.8 million cooperative shareholders and headquartered in [Paris](/places/paris/), serving 35 million customers.

Formed on 31 July 2009 from the merger of Groupe Caisse d'Epargne and Groupe Banque Populaire — networks whose roots reach back to 1818 and 1878 — it finances over a fifth of the French economy through some 5,600 branches, and runs the wholesale bank Natixis and asset manager Natixis Investment Managers alongside its retail and insurance businesses ([Groupe BPCE](https://www.groupebpce.com/en/the-group/history/); [Natixis URD 2024](https://natixis.groupebpce.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Natixis_URD_2024_VA_.pdf)).

## Two 19th-century networks, one cooperative group

The group's roots are two 19th-century answers to financial exclusion: the first Caisse d'Epargne opened in Paris in 1818 to give people of modest means a safe place to save, and the first Banque Populaire was founded in Angers in 1878 to lend to artisans and shopkeepers shut out of traditional banking ([Groupe BPCE](https://www.groupebpce.com/en/the-group/history/)). The two networks grew into France's second-largest banking group when their central bodies merged on 31 July 2009 to form Groupe BPCE, a union underwritten by €5 billion of French state aid approved by the [European Union](/organizations/european-union/)'s European Commission as the crisis-era rescue of their joint investment bank, Natixis ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=29460b86-5e83-4096-a75c-40d5e3cb7f99)). BPCE is a cooperative, not a listed company: 9.8 million cooperative shareholders own 14 Banques Populaires and 15 Caisses d'Epargne, which in turn own the central body BPCE SA fifty-fifty, bound by a legal solidarity and guarantee system ([Groupe BPCE](https://www.ppr.groupebpce.com/app/uploads/2025/02/bpce-investor-presentation-q4-24-long-5.pdf)).

## The 2021–22 reshaping: Natixis in, CNP out

In 2021 BPCE simplified a group grown tangled. On 9 February it offered €4 a share for the 29.3% of Natixis it did not own; the tender offer ran in June and July, a squeeze-out followed on 21 July 2021, and Natixis was delisted from [Euronext Paris](/organizations/euronext-paris/) the same day, with BPCE at 99.78% by February 2022 ([Federal Reserve](http://federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/resolution-plans/bpce-3g-20220701.pdf)). Months later it unwound its other cross-holding: on 28 October 2021 [La Banque Postale](/organizations/la-banque-postale/) agreed to buy BPCE's 16.1% stake in [CNP Assurances](/organizations/cnp-assurances/) at €21.90 a share, completing the €2.4 billion purchase on 16 December, then offering the same price to minorities in a bid valuing the insurer at about €15 billion ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2021/10/28/639703.htm); [La Banque Postale](https://le-groupe-laposte-recette.cdn.prismic.io/le-groupe-laposte-recette-recette-next/61f28efd-da09-4426-84b7-f25a30e24dd2_20211216_CP+LBP+BPCE++EN.pdf)). The deal was as much a swap as a sale — Natixis Investment Managers bought La Banque Postale's 45% stake in [Ostrum Asset Management](/organizations/ostrum-asset-management/) and 40% in [AEW Europe](/organizations/aew-europe/), completing both in May 2022 to own 100% of each, while the groups extended their industrial partnerships to 2030 ([Groupe BPCE newsroom](https://newsroom-en.groupebpce.fr/download-pdf/627e80c308de164c260fe401)).

## Scale, strategy, and the Generali deal that wasn't

By 2024 the reshaped group was growing again: net banking income of €23.3 billion and net income of €3.5 billion, up 26% on the year, with a CET1 capital ratio of 15.6% and €302 billion of liquidity reserves ([Groupe BPCE](https://echanges.dila.gouv.fr/OPENDATA/AMF/MKW/2025/02/FCMKW116925_20250205.pdf)). Its VISION 2030 plan pushes beyond France — a payments joint venture with BNP Paribas, an equipment-leasing acquisition in SGEF, and the purchase of Belgium's oldest bank, Nagelmackers, agreed in July 2024 and closed in March 2025 ([Groupe BPCE](https://echanges.dila.gouv.fr/OPENDATA/AMF/MKW/2025/02/FCMKW116925_20250205.pdf)). The boldest move failed: in January 2025 BPCE and Generali agreed to combine their asset managers into what would have been Europe's largest by revenue, with over €1.9 trillion under management, but talks collapsed in December 2025, with the two sides saying the conditions for a final agreement were not present after opposition from the Italian government and Generali shareholders Delfin and Caltagirone ([IPE](https://www.ipe.com/news/generali-scraps-plans-to-create-joint-venture-with-natixis-investment-managers/10134257.article)).

Groupe BPCE held a 21.7% share of French customer deposits in the third quarter of 2024, according to Banque de France figures cited in its investor presentation ([Groupe BPCE](https://www.ppr.groupebpce.com/app/uploads/2025/02/bpce-investor-presentation-q4-24-long-5.pdf)); with more than a fifth of the nation's savings, its cooperative ownership is not a quaint detail but a structural fact of French retail banking.

## FAQ

### Is Groupe BPCE a cooperative bank?

Yes. It is owned by 9.8 million cooperative shareholders — the customers of its local banks — rather than by stock-market investors. The 14 Banques Populaires and 15 Caisses d'Epargne own the central body, BPCE SA, fifty-fifty, and the group is bound by a legal solidarity and guarantee system between its affiliated banks.

### What is the difference between Banque Populaire and Caisse d'Epargne?

They are two separate retail banking networks with different origins: Caisse d'Epargne began in 1818 as a savings bank for people of modest means, while Banque Populaire began in 1878 lending to artisans and small businesses. They still operate under their own brands and branch networks within Groupe BPCE.

### Is Natixis part of Groupe BPCE?

Yes. Natixis is Groupe BPCE's wholesale banking subsidiary, handling corporate & investment banking and asset & wealth management. BPCE bought out its minority shareholders at €4 a share in 2021, delisted it from Euronext Paris on 21 July 2021, and owned 99.78% of it by February 2022.

### Does Groupe BPCE still own part of CNP Assurances?

No. BPCE sold its entire 16.1% stake in CNP Assurances to La Banque Postale for €2.4 billion in December 2021 at €21.90 a share. La Banque Postale then bid for the remaining shares, reaching 97.79% of the capital by June 2022 and moving to squeeze out the minorities.
