## Overview

iCapital is a New York-based fintech that builds the technology infrastructure letting wealth managers offer alternative investments — private equity, private credit, hedge funds — alongside traditional holdings ([Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/46278/alternative-investment-platform-icapital-raises-820m)). Founded in 2013, its platform unifies onboarding, document workflows, performance data and regulatory compliance for private markets, structured investments and annuities, serving more than 3,000 wealth management firms and 750 product providers with $945 billion in assets serviced globally ([Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/46278/alternative-investment-platform-icapital-raises-820m)). In 2025 it raised over $820 million at a valuation above $7.5 billion in a round co-led by T. Rowe Price and SurgoCap Partners, with State Street participating and Temasek, UBS and BNY increasing commitments ([Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/46278/alternative-investment-platform-icapital-raises-820m)).

The company's unusual position is that its investors are also its distribution channel: the banks and asset managers whose alternative products flow through iCapital's rails — firms like JPMorgan Chase, Morgan Stanley, BlackRock, Fidelity and Blackstone have backed it — are the institutions the platform was built to serve. Chairman and CEO Lawrence Calcano has framed the opportunity as transforming the investing experience as demand for alternatives, structured investments and annuities accelerates ([Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/46278/alternative-investment-platform-icapital-raises-820m)).

## The valuation arc

iCapital's funding history tracks the wealth channel's embrace of private markets. In July 2021 it raised $440 million led by Temasek at about a $4 billion valuation, with Wells Fargo, Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs Asset Management exiting or participating; that December a $50 million round led by WestCap pushed the valuation past $6 billion, with Apollo, Blackstone, KKR and Carlyle among the backers ([Reuters](https://mix929.com/2021/12/23/fintech-firm-icapital-network-valued-at-over-6-billion-after-latest-funding/)). The 2025 round — over $820 million at more than $7.5 billion — was earmarked for a global acquisition strategy, geographic expansion and technology innovation ([Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/46278/alternative-investment-platform-icapital-raises-820m)).

## Infrastructure-level entrenchment

The October 2026 DTCC investment marks a shift from venture backing to market-infrastructure ownership: the Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation, the post-trade utility at the center of U.S. securities settlement, took an undisclosed stake and placed its head of Wealth & Investment Solutions, Talia Klein, on iCapital's board ([Connect Money](https://www.connectmoney.com/stories/dtcc-takes-stake-in-icapital-to-expand-private-markets-infrastructure/)). The two plan to integrate DTCC's Alternative Investment Product with iCapital's platform across transaction processing, data exchange and reporting — a bid to standardize how alternatives move through the wealth channel ([Connect Money](https://www.connectmoney.com/stories/dtcc-takes-stake-in-icapital-to-expand-private-markets-infrastructure/)). It follows iCapital's collaboration with BlackRock's Aladdin Wealth to unify systems for advisors navigating alternatives ([Connect Money](https://www.connectmoney.com/stories/dtcc-takes-stake-in-icapital-to-expand-private-markets-infrastructure/)). DTCC's chief executive framed it plainly: broader adoption of alternatives depends on making the ecosystem "easier to navigate, more efficient to operate, and highly resilient" ([Connect Money](https://www.connectmoney.com/stories/dtcc-takes-stake-in-icapital-to-expand-private-markets-infrastructure/)).
