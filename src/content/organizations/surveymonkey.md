## Overview

SurveyMonkey is an online survey software company that has changed ownership and identity more than most brands its size — a corporate saga that most coverage of the brand never mentions ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](http://gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/surveymonkey-2014), [Metro US](https://www.metro.us/zendesk-investors-reject-deal/), [Private Equity Wire](https://www.privateequitywire.co.uk/stg-completes-15bn-surveymonkey-take-private-deal/)). It was founded in 1999 by Ryan Finley and bought out in 2009 by Spectrum Equity and Bain Capital ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](http://gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/surveymonkey-2014)). At the corporate level it later became Momentive Global, survived a rejected roughly $4B takeover by Zendesk in 2022, and went private again in 2023 — now under Symphony Technology Group after a $1.5B take-private completed on June 2, 2023, followed by delisting ([Metro US](https://www.metro.us/zendesk-investors-reject-deal/), [Private Equity Wire](https://www.privateequitywire.co.uk/stg-completes-15bn-surveymonkey-take-private-deal/)). The valuation arc is striking: sixteen months after Zendesk investors killed a ~$4B deal, the company sold privately for $1.5B — less than half the implied public-market value ([Metro US](https://www.metro.us/zendesk-investors-reject-deal/), [Private Equity Wire](https://www.privateequitywire.co.uk/stg-completes-15bn-surveymonkey-take-private-deal/)).

## The Goldberg era

Ryan Finley founded SurveyMonkey in 1999, and in 2009 the company was bought out by Spectrum Equity and Bain Capital, with Dave Goldberg becoming chief executive ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](http://gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/surveymonkey-2014)). The Stanford Graduate School of Business documented this era in a 2014 case study, capturing the company at the point it had grown from a Finley side project into a private-equity-backed software business ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](http://gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/surveymonkey-2014)). The 2009 buyout set the template for everything that followed: SurveyMonkey as a company that private capital would repeatedly price, reject, and reprice over the next decade and a half ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](http://gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/surveymonkey-2014)).

## The Momentive interlude

By 2021 the corporate parent had become Momentive Global — Reuters reporting identifies the takeover target as "SurveyMonkey parent Momentive Global," the formulation this profile uses because the specifics of the 2021 renaming were not verified from a fully-read independent source ([Metro US](https://www.metro.us/zendesk-investors-reject-deal/)). In October 2021 Zendesk agreed an all-stock takeover of Momentive Global valued at roughly $4B ([Metro US](https://www.metro.us/zendesk-investors-reject-deal/)). But on February 25, 2022, Zendesk's investors rejected the deal decisively: 91,677,121 votes against versus 9,334,851 for, after activist firm Jana Partners had urged rejection ([Metro US](https://www.metro.us/zendesk-investors-reject-deal/)). The merger agreement was terminated, leaving Momentive independent ([Metro US](https://www.metro.us/zendesk-investors-reject-deal/)). A 10-to-1 shareholder rejection of an already-agreed deal is an unusually emphatic market verdict, and it stranded Momentive as a standalone public company with a takeover price the market had just declared excessive ([Metro US](https://www.metro.us/zendesk-investors-reject-deal/)).

## Going private again

Sixteen months after the failed sale, Symphony Technology Group completed the $1.5B take-private of SurveyMonkey on June 2, 2023, at $9.46 per share, and the company was delisted ([Private Equity Wire](https://www.privateequitywire.co.uk/stg-completes-15bn-surveymonkey-take-private-deal/)). The sequence — a rejected ~$4B public-market deal in February 2022 followed by a $1.5B private deal in June 2023 — closed the loop that the 2009 buyout had opened: SurveyMonkey began and ended the period under private ownership, with the failed Zendesk sale as the pivot between the two ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](http://gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/surveymonkey-2014), [Metro US](https://www.metro.us/zendesk-investors-reject-deal/), [Private Equity Wire](https://www.privateequitywire.co.uk/stg-completes-15bn-surveymonkey-take-private-deal/)).
