## Overview

Tiger Global Management is a [New York City](/places/new-york-city/)-based investment firm founded by Chase Coleman III in March 2001 with a $25 million check from his former boss, Julian Robertson. It invests in public and private technology companies, became the world's most prolific startup investor in 2021, saw its hedge fund lose 52% in 2022, and has since rebuilt under Coleman's direct control, gaining about 24% in 2024 ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/); [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2ekchq8rll8mbbn2glq80/hedge-funds/the-rich-list-the-24th-annual-ranking-of-the-highest-earning-hedge-fund-managers)).

The firm runs two businesses under one roof: a public equity arm that buys and shorts listed stocks, and a private equity arm that backs growth-stage startups. Its stated focus is internet, software, consumer and financial technology companies, mainly in the [United States](/places/united-states/), China and [India](/places/india/) ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/)). Its SEC filing describes the same split — long/short funds, a long-only fund, a crossover fund spanning public and private markets, and closed-end private equity funds — and reports approximately $54.7 billion of net assets under management as of 1 January 2026 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://alpha-maven.com/company/tiger-global-management)).

Tiger Global's best-known connection to this site's graph is [Blend](/organizations/blend/): in January 2021 it co-led Blend's $300 million Series G alongside [Coatue](/organizations/coatue/), nearly doubling the digital lender's valuation to $3.3 billion in five months ([National Mortgage Professional](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/76260/blend-closes-300m-funding-nearly-doubles-valuation)). Blend's technology had facilitated $1.4 trillion in mortgages and consumer loans in 2020, and the round took its total raised to $665 million ([National Mortgage Professional](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/76260/blend-closes-300m-funding-nearly-doubles-valuation)).

## A Tiger Cub is born

Coleman was one of Julian Robertson's "Tiger Cubs" — managers who learned the trade at Robertson's Tiger Management and then launched their own firms with his backing. He founded Tiger Global in 2001 with a $25 million check from Robertson, starting as a hedge fund investing in public equities just as the dot-com bust was unfolding ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/); [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2ekchq8rll8mbbn2glq80/hedge-funds/the-rich-list-the-24th-annual-ranking-of-the-highest-earning-hedge-fund-managers)). The firm found early opportunity in undervalued Chinese public technology stocks, correctly anticipating the growth of internet-enabled retail in China ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/)). In 2003 it created its first fund for private companies, raising $75.8 million ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/)). Scott Shleifer, who joined in 2002 from [Blackstone](/organizations/blackstone/), led the private push and the early Chinese investments, while Lee Fixel joined in 2006 to lead investing in India and left in 2019 to start his own firm, Addition ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/)). Early American bets included Facebook and LinkedIn through secondary financings in 2009 ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/)).

## The crossover model: public and private under one roof

Tiger Global's edge was combining a hedge fund's research on public markets with a venture investor's access to private companies — the "crossover" model. The same team that studied listed technology stocks could back a startup before it went public and keep holding it afterwards. By mid-2021 the private side had grown slightly larger than the public side, at about $35 billion against $30 billion ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/)).

| Business | What it does | Size (mid-2021) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public equity | Long/short and long-only listed stocks | ~$30 billion |
| Private equity | Growth-stage startups, US / China / India | ~$35 billion |

The style was speed and scale, not hands-on guidance: Tiger Global reportedly pounced on deals early, closed them extremely fast, offered sky-high valuations, then stayed hands-off — paying for portfolio companies to use Bain & Co. consultants instead of coaching founders itself ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/)). Its most frequent co-investors over the last decade were [Accel](/organizations/accel/), Coatue, [DST Global](/organizations/dst-global/) and Dragoneer ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/)). The approach paid off for years: the private equity business earned a 26% net internal rate of return over its life, and in 2020 the firm was named the world's top hedge fund manager ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/)).

## The 2021 sprint: the world's most prolific startup investor

In 2021 Tiger Global invested at a pace no venture firm had matched. By early June it had backed 118 companies — ten times its pace in the first five months of 2020 — with 90 of them new to the portfolio, against 69 companies in all of 2020 ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/)). In those five months it led or co-led deals totalling $10.5 billion and joined rounds worth a further $11.5 billion, more than any other firm and well ahead of Sequoia Capital's $4.1 billion ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/)). Its portfolio held 126 unicorns, 48 of them added in 2021, including rounds it led in corporate card issuer [Brex](/organizations/brex/), Blend and creator platform Patreon ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/)). Fund XIV closed in early 2021 at $6.75 billion, then the firm's largest, and it was already raising a new $10 billion fund ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/)). The year's exits were spectacular — 29 portfolio companies went public, including [Coinbase](/organizations/coinbase/) at $86 billion, Nubank at $41 billion, Grab at $40 billion and [Roblox](/organizations/roblox/) at $30 billion ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/startups/tiger-global-vc-hedge-fund-private-equity/)).

## The 2022 collapse: 52% gone in five months

The sprint ended when technology stocks crashed: the Nasdaq Composite fell 23% in 2022 and the S&P 500 dropped 14%. Tiger Global's hedge fund — heavily invested in high-growth names such as Snowflake and Carvana just before the downturn — sank 14.2% in May alone, taking its loss for the year to 52% ([Financial Advisor Magazine](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/tiger-global-tumbles-52---prompting-fee-cut-and-redemption-plan-68150.html); [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/public/tiger-global-warning-losses-tech-selloff/)). By April, the hedge fund's 44% fall combined with losses in the long-only and crossover funds had wiped out about $16 billion ([Financial Advisor Magazine](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/tiger-global-tumbles-52---prompting-fee-cut-and-redemption-plan-68150.html)). The firm cut hedge-fund management fees by 50 basis points through December 2023 and let redeeming clients put illiquid private holdings into separate side-pocket accounts ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/public/tiger-global-warning-losses-tech-selloff/)). In a letter to investors it wrote: "We take very seriously that our recent performance does not live up to the standards we have set for ourselves over the last 21 years" ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/public/tiger-global-warning-losses-tech-selloff/)). The private business held up far better: worth about $64 billion at the end of 2021, it had invested $34 billion and returned about $28 billion since 2003, and was marked down only about 9% in early 2022 ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/startups/tiger-global-vc-hedge-fund-private-equity/)).

## Rebuilding under Coleman

Tiger Global had invested a record $19 billion in the private market across 2021 and 2022, and the correction hit that book too: the $12.7 billion venture fund launched in October 2021 was down 20% on paper, net of fees ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/tiger-globals-private-investment-head-scott-shleifer-steps-down/article67561614.ece/amp/)). In November 2023 Coleman told shareholders that Shleifer would step down as head of private investing in January 2024 and become a senior advisor, with Coleman taking over both businesses and a new investment committee formed for the private unit ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/tiger-globals-private-investment-head-scott-shleifer-steps-down/article67561614.ece/amp/)). The rebuild was deliberately quieter: in 2024 the long-short fund gained 23.8% and the long-only fund 26.3%, with the firm barely trading its largest holdings in a buy-and-hold approach ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2ekchq8rll8mbbn2glq80/hedge-funds/the-rich-list-the-24th-annual-ranking-of-the-highest-earning-hedge-fund-managers)). Five of its seven largest US-listed long positions were Magnificent Seven stocks — [Meta](/organizations/meta/) at more than 16%, plus [Microsoft](/organizations/microsoft/), Alphabet, Nvidia and Amazon — and a new venture fund raised $2.2 billion, far less than the firm had originally sought ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2ekchq8rll8mbbn2glq80/hedge-funds/the-rich-list-the-24th-annual-ranking-of-the-highest-earning-hedge-fund-managers)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Tiger Global?** Tiger Global Management is a New York-based investment firm that invests in public and private technology companies — internet, software, consumer and financial technology — mainly in the United States, China and India ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/)). It runs hedge, long-only, crossover and private equity funds, with about $54.7 billion in net assets at 1 January 2026 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://alpha-maven.com/company/tiger-global-management)).

**Who founded Tiger Global?** Chase Coleman III founded the firm in March 2001 with a $25 million check from Julian Robertson, his former boss at Tiger Management, where Coleman had been one of the "Tiger Cubs" ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/); [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2ekchq8rll8mbbn2glq80/hedge-funds/the-rich-list-the-24th-annual-ranking-of-the-highest-earning-hedge-fund-managers)). Scott Shleifer joined in 2002 and built the private equity business before stepping down in January 2024 ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tiger-global-management-strategy-investing-2021/)).

**What happened to Tiger Global in 2022?** Its hedge fund lost 52% of its value through May 2022 as technology stocks crashed, wiping out about $16 billion by April across the hedge, long-only and crossover funds ([Financial Advisor Magazine](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/tiger-global-tumbles-52---prompting-fee-cut-and-redemption-plan-68150.html)). The firm cut management fees and offered side-pocket accounts for illiquid holdings, while its private equity business was marked down only about 9% ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/public/tiger-global-warning-losses-tech-selloff/)).

**How big is Tiger Global now?** Its SEC Form ADV reports approximately $54.7 billion of net assets under management as of 1 January 2026 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://alpha-maven.com/company/tiger-global-management)). That is below its roughly $65 billion total in mid-2021, but the 2024 gains — 23.8% in the long-short fund and 26.3% in the long-only fund — show the public business recovering ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2ekchq8rll8mbbn2glq80/hedge-funds/the-rich-list-the-24th-annual-ranking-of-the-highest-earning-hedge-fund-managers)).
