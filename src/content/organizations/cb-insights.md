## Overview

CB Insights is a [New York City](/places/new-york-city/)-based market-intelligence company that sells data, research and predictive analytics on private companies to investors, corporations and sales teams. Founded in 2008 by Anand Sanwal and Jonathan Sherry, it began life as ChubbyBrain, grew largely without venture capital, and became known for its Mosaic health scores, its unicorn lists and its irreverent daily newsletter ([Bloomberg](https://www.pilotgrowth.com/vc-intel-provider-cb-insights-to-weigh-800-million-sale/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/08/24/with-1m-in-funding-vc-database-cb-insights-launches-a-data-tool-for-salespeople/)).

The company's pitch is that private companies are hard to understand because the information about them is scattered across millions of unstructured documents — filings, news, websites, earnings calls, social media and patents — and that software, not armies of analysts, should turn that material into structured data ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2020/07/anand-sanwal-cb-insights-b2b-newsletter-data-startups-technology?utm_pageloadtype=scroll)). Its customers are the people who need to judge those companies: venture capitalists, corporate development and strategy teams, bankers, and sales teams hunting their next lead ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/09/cb-insights-raises-10-million-in-series-a-funding/)). By 2021 it reported more than 1,000 clients, mostly enterprises paying on average around $60,000 a year for a team licence ([Axios](https://www.pilotgrowth.com/data-software-company-cb-insights-now-has-nearly-one-million-email-subscribers/)).

## From ChubbyBrain to a data business

Sanwal and Sherry came to the idea from inside the problem. Sanwal had been a vice-president at American Express, co-leading a $50 million innovation fund with Sherry, and the pair had seen first-hand how hard it was to understand private companies as a lender to them ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/09/cb-insights-raises-10-million-in-series-a-funding/)). They were also frustrated with the incumbents that tracked the private markets, Dow Jones and Thomson Reuters, whose offerings they found inadequate ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/09/cb-insights-raises-10-million-in-series-a-funding/)). Sanwal left American Express in 2007 to build a data firm that would apply the kind of statistical, trend-spotting analysis he admired in Nate Silver's election forecasting to private business ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/3061344/w_750)).

The company launched in 2009 under the name ChubbyBrain, a joke that did not survive contact with customers: an investment bank the founders had spent months wooing told them, "We love the product and the data and what you guys are doing. But we can't buy a product called ChubbyBrain" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/08/24/with-1m-in-funding-vc-database-cb-insights-launches-a-data-tool-for-salespeople/); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2020/07/anand-sanwal-cb-insights-b2b-newsletter-data-startups-technology?utm_pageloadtype=scroll)). The rebrand to CB Insights kept the initials "as an homage to our younger, dumber days," Sanwal said ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2020/07/anand-sanwal-cb-insights-b2b-newsletter-data-startups-technology?utm_pageloadtype=scroll)).

For its first six years the company was, in its own phrase, "revenue-funded": it lived off subscriptions and three small grants from the National Science Foundation, totalling $1.15 million, which paid for the Mosaic scoring software ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/09/cb-insights-raises-10-million-in-series-a-funding/); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2020/07/anand-sanwal-cb-insights-b2b-newsletter-data-startups-technology?utm_pageloadtype=scroll)). Mosaic is the company's signature product: an algorithmic health score for a private company, on a scale of 0 to 1,000, built from three sub-scores — Momentum, Market and Money — and from signals including job postings, web traffic, social-media chatter, executive turnover, customer signings, mobile-app data and news sentiment ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/team-blog/mosaic-moneyball-for-startups/)). Behind it sits a machine-learning system the company calls the Cruncher, which crawls millions of unstructured documents every day and extracts structured data from them ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/team-blog/mosaic-moneyball-for-startups/)).

The first outside equity came late and from a customer. In November 2015 CB Insights raised a $10 million Series A from RSTP, the growth-equity firm later known as Pilot Growth Equity, its first equity round and first institutional financing ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/09/cb-insights-raises-10-million-in-series-a-funding/)). RSTP had been a longtime customer that reached out about an investment roughly two years earlier, and its co-founder John DeLoche joined the board ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/09/cb-insights-raises-10-million-in-series-a-funding/)). At the time the company had 61 employees, standard subscriptions of $20,000 to $60,000 a year, and said it was profitable ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/09/cb-insights-raises-10-million-in-series-a-funding/)). By 2020 the team had grown to 310 people, and clients included [Salesforce](/organizations/salesforce/) and Cisco among Fortune 500 companies, plus venture firms such as FirstMark Capital and Sequoia Capital ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2020/07/anand-sanwal-cb-insights-b2b-newsletter-data-startups-technology?utm_pageloadtype=scroll)).

## The newsletter as a growth engine

CB Insights' most unusual asset is not a database but a daily email. The newsletter began in 2010, written in Sanwal's voice — short, conversational, highly visual — and signed off with the words "I love you" ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2020/07/anand-sanwal-cb-insights-b2b-newsletter-data-startups-technology?utm_pageloadtype=scroll)). The sign-off began in 2016 as an experiment to see whether anyone was reading; engagement jumped and it stayed ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2020/07/anand-sanwal-cb-insights-b2b-newsletter-data-startups-technology?utm_pageloadtype=scroll)). Sanwal told Fast Company he had received more than 1,800 replies to "I love you" alone, only three negative, and that omitting it brought about 200 emails asking whether he no longer loved his readers ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/3061344/w_750)).

The growth in miniature: about 1,400 subscribers at the end of 2010, 9,000 in 2012, 51,000 in 2014, 171,000 in the first half of 2016, and more than 640,000 by 2020 ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/3061344/w_750); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2020/07/anand-sanwal-cb-insights-b2b-newsletter-data-startups-technology?utm_pageloadtype=scroll)). By 2021 the flagship newsletter reached more than 750,000 people, with niche newsletters on digital health, consumer retail and financial services adding around 150,000 more — nearly one million subscribers in total ([Axios](https://www.pilotgrowth.com/data-software-company-cb-insights-now-has-nearly-one-million-email-subscribers/)). The newsletter is free, so it earns nothing directly, but Sanwal said about $1 million of revenue in the year to mid-2016 could be attributed to its reach, including one $100,000 deal that began when a subscriber recommended the service to a friend ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/3061344/w_750)). The strategy was deliberate: the firm began investing in content shortly after its 2010 launch as a way to compete with data giants like Thomson Reuters, Dow Jones and S&P, on the reasoning that giving away useful, data-driven research was how a small firm could "punch above our weight" ([Axios](https://www.pilotgrowth.com/data-software-company-cb-insights-now-has-nearly-one-million-email-subscribers/)).

## Acquisitions, rankings and the AI 100

After a decade as a buyer of nothing, it made two acquisitions in two months in 2020. In July it acquired the data assets of Dow Jones VentureSource, the venture-backed-company database, extending its private-company data back to 1983; it also agreed to supply a limited set of research reports and company data to Factiva, Dow Jones's news database ([PR Newswire](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/cb-insights-acquires-venturesource-data-from-dow-jones-significantly-expanding-private-markets-coverage-810198821.html)). In September it acquired Blockdata, a Netherlands-based firm tracking the global blockchain economy, for undisclosed terms; Blockdata stayed standalone with its own team, and CB Insights opened its first international office in Amsterdam ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2020/09/16/cb-insights-buys-blockdata-to-build-out-blockchain-data-offering)).

In 2015 it worked with [The New York Times](/organizations/new-york-times/) on a list of 50 Future Unicorns — private companies it expected to reach $1 billion valuations — generated algorithmically using Mosaic; the company says 48% of those picks became unicorns, and it repeated the exercise with the Times in 2019 ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/team-blog/mosaic-moneyball-for-startups/); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/future-unicorn-startups-billion-dollar-companies/?utm_source=CB+Insights+Newsletter&utm_campaign=ca9763a0d7-WedNL_02_20_2019&utm_medium=email&utm_term=0_9dc0513989-ca9763a0d7-92041965)). The Times has featured the company's data more than a few times, according to Fast Company ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/3061344/w_750)).

Its best-known ranking is the AI 100, an annual list of the 100 most promising private AI companies in the world; the 2026 edition is the tenth, with winners selected from more than 40,000 companies on deal activity, investor strength, hiring momentum and the firm's predictive scores, plus buyer interviews and startup briefings ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/reports/CB-Insights_AI-100-2026.pdf)). The company claims the list carries signal: across five cohorts, 64% of AI 100 winners closed a follow-on equity round, against 31% for comparable AI companies, and did so a median 198 days sooner ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/reports/CB-Insights_AI-100-2026.pdf)). One earlier AI 100 company was [Behavox](/organizations/behavox/), the AI compliance-surveillance firm, whose inclusion is recorded in the sibling Behavox profile on the strength of the Milken Institute's bio of its founder — a source this profile could not re-fetch in this session (see open items).

## A founder steps aside — and a sale is weighed

By 2021 Sanwal told Axios the company was on track for nearly $100 million in annual recurring revenue the following year, with no plans to sell but an IPO not ruled out ([Axios](https://www.pilotgrowth.com/data-software-company-cb-insights-now-has-nearly-one-million-email-subscribers/)). Two years later Bloomberg reported that CB Insights was exploring a potential sale, interviewing investment banks about a process that could seek as much as $800 million — ten times its expected $80 million in annual revenue in 2023 — and that it could appeal to private-equity firms and industry players such as Morningstar and S&P Global ([Bloomberg](https://www.pilotgrowth.com/vc-intel-provider-cb-insights-to-weigh-800-million-sale/)). No final decision had been made, Bloomberg said, and the company could remain independent; all three firms declined to comment ([Bloomberg](https://www.pilotgrowth.com/vc-intel-provider-cb-insights-to-weigh-800-million-sale/)). No sale followed in any source read for this profile. Bloomberg named the company's competitors as Morningstar-owned PitchBook, S&P Capital IQ and [Gartner](/organizations/gartner/), and its customers as [Microsoft](/organizations/microsoft/), [IBM](/organizations/ibm/) and Block Inc. ([Bloomberg](https://www.pilotgrowth.com/vc-intel-provider-cb-insights-to-weigh-800-million-sale/)).

The leadership changed around the same time. After 14 years as chief executive, Sanwal decided to "fire" himself and move to Founder & Executive Chairman, focusing on customer conversations, product, content, partnerships and generative AI — a shift he attributed to ChatGPT's arrival in November 2022, which made a 2017 product vision newly achievable ([CB Insights](https://www.pilotgrowth.com/ceo-change-yeah-really-ive-decided-to-fire-myself-after-14-years-as-ceo-of-cb-insights/)). He promised to introduce the new chief executive the next day and to help onboard them; the successor's name is not given in the announcement and is not claimed here ([CB Insights](https://www.pilotgrowth.com/ceo-change-yeah-really-ive-decided-to-fire-myself-after-14-years-as-ceo-of-cb-insights/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What does CB Insights do?** It sells data, research and predictive analytics on private companies — funding, valuations, hiring, partnerships and market trends — to investors, corporate strategy and development teams, and sales teams, as an annual-subscription platform ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/11/09/cb-insights-raises-10-million-in-series-a-funding/); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2020/07/anand-sanwal-cb-insights-b2b-newsletter-data-startups-technology?utm_pageloadtype=scroll)).

**What is the Mosaic score?** Mosaic is CB Insights' algorithmic health score for a private company, on a 0–1,000 scale, combining three sub-scores — Momentum, Market and Money — built from signals such as hiring, web traffic, news sentiment and financing history ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/team-blog/mosaic-moneyball-for-startups/)).

**What is the AI 100?** The AI 100 is CB Insights' annual ranking of the 100 most promising private AI companies, chosen from more than 40,000 candidates on deal activity, investor strength, hiring and the firm's predictive scores; the 2026 edition was the tenth ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/reports/CB-Insights_AI-100-2026.pdf)).
