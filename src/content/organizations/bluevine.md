## Overview

BlueVine is a Jersey City, New Jersey-based financial technology company founded in 2013 by Israeli entrepreneurs Eyal Lifshitz and Nir Klar, serving approximately 175,000 active small-business customers across the United States with a platform combining business checking, payments, bill pay, invoicing, lending, and financial-management tools ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)). On September 28, 2026, Valley National Bancorp announced it would acquire BlueVine for approximately $340 million, with Lifshitz joining Valley as head of Small Business Banking when the transaction closes ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)).

## From invoice factoring to a digital bank — then into a real one

BlueVine began as a pure lender with a single trick: invoice factoring, issuing cash to small businesses in exchange for their unpaid invoices at a discount, with up to $5 million in working capital available in days ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/bluevine-brings-in-60-million/)). By its 2018 Series E — $60 million led by Menlo Ventures with SVB Capital participating, bringing total financing to $578 million — it had funded more than $900 million in loans to over 10,000 customers, 80% of them repeat borrowers ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/bluevine-brings-in-60-million/)).

The pivot to banking came in 2019: a $102.5 million Series F that valued the company at approximately $700-800 million, paired with the launch of an FDIC-insured business checking account ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)). The deposit franchise that followed became the company's crown jewel — $2.1 billion in digitally sourced deposits by mid-2026, growing at roughly 35% annually since 2023, on total equity raised of about $290 million alongside ~$770 million in credit and debt facilities ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)).

The Valley National deal prices that franchise at less than half the 2019 private valuation — $340 million against $700-800 million — a comedown that reflects both fintech's post-2021 repricing and what the deposits are worth to an acquirer. Valley, a $66 billion-asset bank with 220-plus branches, expects the acquisition to bring $2.1 billion in low-cost digital deposits into its funding base, contribute about 8% to projected 2028 earnings per share, and give it a digital customer-acquisition channel to complement its relationship-banking model ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)). For BlueVine's customers, the prize is access to Valley's branch network and broader services — cash-flow management, credit, insurance, wealth management ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)).
