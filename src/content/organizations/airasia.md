## Overview

AirAsia is the Malaysian low-cost airline group that pioneered budget travel across Southeast
Asia after Tony Fernandes and Kamarudin Meranun bought the debt-laden carrier for a token RM1 in
2001 and relaunched it as a budget airline in 2002
([Channel News Asia](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/asia/airasia-timeline-budget-airline-pioneer-financial-strain-6389446)).
But the most unusual thing about the modern AirAsia is not an aircraft at all: inside its super
app, alongside flight bookings, sits a "transport" category where travelers book trains, buses,
ferries and private transfers from ground-transport partner
[12Go](/organizations/12go/) — a deal the airline's super-app arm struck in 2024, giving 10.6
million monthly app users access to more than 4,000 transport operators across roughly 50,000
routes in 26 Asian countries
([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/airasia-partners-12go-ground-transportation)).
In January 2026 the group completed the consolidation of all AirAsia-branded airlines under
AirAsia X, uniting seven carriers under a single listed aviation platform
([The Edge Malaysia](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/801265)).

## From a token ringgit to 500 million passengers

AirAsia was established in 1993 and began flying in 1996 as a conventional airline, but it
struggled financially and accumulated about RM40 million in debt
([Channel News Asia](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/asia/airasia-timeline-budget-airline-pioneer-financial-strain-6389446)).
The September 2001 purchase by Tune Air, led by Fernandes and Kamarudin, set the stage for the
2002 relaunch on a low-fare, high-utilisation model with direct online — and later SMS — ticket
sales
([Channel News Asia](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/asia/airasia-timeline-budget-airline-pioneer-financial-strain-6389446)).
Expansion was rapid: the group listed on Bursa Malaysia in 2004, the same year it committed to
up to 100 Airbus A320s; the first A320 arrived in 2005 and began a shift to a standardised
Airbus narrowbody fleet that made the model scalable
([Channel News Asia](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/asia/airasia-timeline-budget-airline-pioneer-financial-strain-6389446)).
AirAsia X launched in 2007 to extend the model to medium- and long-haul flying, the Malaysian
operation retired its last Boeing 737 in 2008 to become an all-Airbus carrier, and by that year
the group had flown 50 million passengers
([Channel News Asia](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/asia/airasia-timeline-budget-airline-pioneer-financial-strain-6389446)).

Skytrax named AirAsia the world's best low-cost airline for the first time in 2009; the
100-millionth passenger followed in 2010, the Fly-Thru connecting service in 2011, and a short
European experiment — London and Paris — was abandoned in 2012 on high fuel prices and weak
European demand
([Channel News Asia](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/asia/airasia-timeline-budget-airline-pioneer-financial-strain-6389446)).
The group's worst moment came in December 2014, when AirAsia Indonesia Flight QZ8501 crashed
into the Java Sea en route from Surabaya to Singapore, killing all 162 people aboard
([Channel News Asia](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/asia/airasia-timeline-budget-airline-pioneer-financial-strain-6389446)).
By 2018 the group had carried 500 million passengers since its transformation, but that scale
came with exposure: when COVID-19 shut borders in 2020, the group flew just 29 per cent of its
2019 capacity and posted a RM5.9 billion net loss, and both AirAsia X and then Capital A — the
holding company formerly known as AirAsia Group — entered Malaysia's PN17 financially distressed
classification in 2021 and 2022
([Channel News Asia](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/asia/airasia-timeline-budget-airline-pioneer-financial-strain-6389446)).

## Capital A splits, and AirAsia X becomes the airline

In 2024 Capital A shareholders approved the sale of the aviation businesses — AirAsia Berhad
and AirAsia Aviation Group Limited — to the separately listed AirAsia X, a carve-out designed
to put every AirAsia-branded short-, medium- and long-haul carrier under one aviation group
while leaving Capital A focused on non-airline businesses and on exiting PN17 status
([Channel News Asia](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/asia/airasia-timeline-budget-airline-pioneer-financial-strain-6389446)).
The deal cleared its conditions in October 2025, with Reuters reporting that it would unify
seven airlines under a single banner and that the unified group would be called AirAsia Group,
while Capital A kept its travel and digital operations
([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/malaysias-capital-ordeal-unify-seven-airasia-airlines-nears-end-2025-10-30/)).
The transaction completed in January 2026, settled through the issuance of 2.31 billion new
AirAsia X shares to Capital A and its shareholders alongside AirAsia X's assumption of RM3.8
billion previously owed by Capital A to AirAsia Berhad
([The Edge Malaysia](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/801265)).
In April 2026 AirAsia X proposed renaming itself AirAsia Group Bhd to reflect its new role as
the listed holding company for the wider airline group, pending shareholder approval
([The Edge Malaysia](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/801265)).
The enlarged group began reporting consolidated operating data: for the first quarter ended
March 31, 2026 it carried 18.9 million passengers, up 9 per cent year on year, with capacity at
98 per cent of pre-pandemic levels and load factor steady at 85 per cent
([The Edge Malaysia](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/801265)).

The consolidation was followed almost immediately by a new fuel shock. In March-April 2026,
conflict in the Middle East pushed jet-fuel prices past US$200 a barrel; AirAsia introduced
fuel surcharges and warned some routes could be cut
([Channel News Asia](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/asia/airasia-timeline-budget-airline-pioneer-financial-strain-6389446)).
The group temporarily suspended 21 routes and reduced second-quarter capacity by about 10 per
cent, and its second-quarter results showed a net loss of RM830.5 million, including RM331
million in foreign-exchange losses
([Channel News Asia](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/asia/airasia-timeline-budget-airline-pioneer-financial-strain-6389446)).
By September 2026 the Malaysian government was reportedly asking Malaysia Airlines and Batik
Air whether they could absorb AirAsia's domestic routes if necessary — contingency planning,
not a decision, but a measure of the strain
([Channel News Asia](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/asia/airasia-timeline-budget-airline-pioneer-financial-strain-6389446)).

## Seven airlines and one super app

The unified group operates seven branded carriers: the short-haul AirAsia Malaysia, Thai
AirAsia (FD), Indonesia AirAsia, Philippines AirAsia and AirAsia Cambodia — which began
operations in May 2024 — alongside the medium/long-haul AirAsia X and Thai AirAsia X
([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/malaysias-capital-ordeal-unify-seven-airasia-airlines-nears-end-2025-10-30/);
[Channel News Asia](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/asia/airasia-timeline-budget-airline-pioneer-financial-strain-6389446)).
The Thai pair alone planned 53 routes to 46 cities from Don Mueang for the winter 2026 high
season, with Thai AirAsia X serving four Japanese cities and Thai AirAsia restoring six India
routes in the fourth quarter of 2026
([Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/business/corporate/40071361)).

The non-airline half of the story is the super app. AirAsia built it out in 2021 with the
creation of AirAsia Ride and the acquisition of Indonesian ride-hailing and payments firm
Gojek's Thailand business, added biometric facial recognition later that year, and signed a
five-year agreement with Google Cloud to develop the app's technology
([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/airasia-partners-12go-ground-transportation)).
The 2024 12Go integration — trains, buses, ferries and private transfers bookable where
flights are booked — was presented as making AirAsia the first super app to offer a complete
journey experience at scale; AirAsia's super-app chief executive Amanda Woo said ground
transport was "the final piece of the jigsaw" for the app's users, while 12Go chief executive
Alexey Abolmasov called it the largest third-party inventory deal the company had signed
([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/airasia-partners-12go-ground-transportation)).
The deal's premise was that only about 10 per cent of bus, ferry and train tickets are booked
digitally, leaving a largely offline market to be captured
([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/airasia-partners-12go-ground-transportation)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim a single settled legal name: the aviation holding company is
AirAsia X, which in April 2026 proposed renaming itself AirAsia Group Bhd — a proposal
reported as pending shareholder approval, and the renaming is not treated here as complete.
It does not give a fleet count; trade reports carry varying approximate figures, and this
profile reports consolidated passenger operating figures instead. The 2026 financial
information is fast-moving — September 2026 reports of government contingency planning,
creditor discussions and financing efforts describe an ongoing situation, not a resolved one,
and are deliberately limited to what the sources read for this profile support. The 12Go
partnership year of 2024 follows the independently validated repo record; the announcement
article read in full prints no explicit publication date. Finally, no FactsLedger profiles
exist yet for the group's related entities (Capital A, Thai AirAsia), so this profile links
only to the existing 12Go profile and otherwise carries no outgoing internal links.
