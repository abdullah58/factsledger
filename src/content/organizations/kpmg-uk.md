## Overview

KPMG UK is the [United Kingdom](/places/united-kingdom/) member firm of KPMG International and one of the Big Four professional services firms, organised as a partner-owned limited liability partnership, KPMG LLP, headquartered at 15 Canada Square in Canary Wharf, [London](/places/london/). It provides audit, tax and legal, advisory and deal advisory services, and since 1 October 2024 it has operated as part of a combined UK/Swiss group led by Jon Holt.

The firm is often described simply as "KPMG", but that name is a brand shared by a network of separate firms, not a single company. Each KPMG member firm is a separate legal entity, generally locally owned and managed, and each is responsible for its own obligations and liabilities ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/pt/en/governance.html)). KPMG International Limited, the coordinating entity, is an English private company limited by guarantee that provides no professional services to clients; the network is not a global partnership, a single firm, a multinational corporation or a joint venture ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/pt/en/governance.html)). KPMG firms collectively operate in 138 countries and territories and employ more than 276,000 people ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/about/our-history.html)).

Two different revenue figures circulate for the firm, and they measure different things: UK-only fee income was £3.021 billion in the year to 30 September 2025, up 1% from £2.990 billion ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/)), while the combined UK/Swiss Group reported revenue of £3.6 billion for the same year, its first year of combined reporting ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/01/kpmg-uk-swiss-group-annual-results.html)). The UK firm has 850 partners and 15,889 UK staff excluding partners, and the combined group employs 25,000 people ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/01/kpmg-uk-swiss-group-annual-results.html)).

## From Peat Marwick McLintock to KPMG LLP

The UK firm's current form dates to April 1987, when Peat Marwick and KMG merged worldwide and the UK practice became Peat Marwick McLintock ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/)). KMG itself had been formed in 1979, when Thomson McLintock joined Klynveld Kraayenhof & Co. of the Netherlands, McLintock Main LaFrentz and Deutsche Treuhand-Gesellschaft in a grouping of independent national practices ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/)). The 1987 merger was announced as the largest in the history of the accounting business ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/about/our-history.html)). The UK firm was renamed KPMG Peat Marwick McLintock in early 1990 and became KPMG Peat Marwick on 1 October 1991 ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/)).

The roots run deeper than 1987: the UK's Peat, Marwick, Mitchell & Co helped develop the first-ever accounting standards when financial reporting was heavily criticised in the 1960s and 1970s ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/about/our-history.html)). The modern legal vehicle, KPMG LLP, was incorporated on 22 February 2002 and is registered at Companies House as OC301540 ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/)). It remains partner-owned, with no private equity backing ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/)).

## Leadership and the UK/Swiss merger

Jon Holt has been the firm's chief executive since April 2021, and now serves as Group Chief Executive and UK Senior Partner of the combined UK/Swiss group; his second term runs to September 2029 ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/)). In 2026 Holt ran for the global chairmanship of KPMG International; Gary Wingrove was elected instead and took over globally on 1 October 2026, while Holt remains group chief executive ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/)).

The most significant structural change of recent years is the 2024 combination with KPMG Switzerland. Partners in both countries approved the merger, which took effect on 1 October 2024; the two firms remain separate legal entities within the group ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/01/kpmg-uk-swiss-group-annual-results.html)). Vicki Heard became group managing partner designate on 1 October 2026 and takes full operational responsibility on 1 January 2027, succeeding Chris Hearld — the first woman in the role — while Alan Turner succeeded her as group head of tax and legal services, Callum Licence has led advisory since 1 July 2026, and Cath Burnet leads audit ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/)).

## FY25 results: growth in audit and tax, a weaker advisory market

The UK/Swiss Group's first combined results, for the year ended 30 September 2025, show a firm growing slowly but becoming markedly more profitable through cost control. Revenue was £3.6 billion, up 2% on a pro forma basis; audit grew 5% and tax and legal grew 6%, while advisory fell 3% "against the backdrop of a difficult consulting and deals trading environment" ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/01/kpmg-uk-swiss-group-annual-results.html)). Profit before tax was £576 million, up 14%, and average distributable profit per partner rose 11% to £880,000 ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/01/kpmg-uk-swiss-group-annual-results.html)). The firm credited "careful cost management in response to the economic cycle" and said it would keep focusing on managing costs given lower industry-wide attrition ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/01/kpmg-uk-swiss-group-annual-results.html)).

## Tax technology: the Blue J alliance

KPMG UK's most distinctive technology bet is in tax, where it was an early adopter of [Blue J](/organizations/blue-j/)'s AI. The two firms first linked up in 2022: KPMG uses Blue J's technology to predict tax scenario outcomes with over 90% accuracy for employees to review — for example, whether a worker is employed or self-employed for tax purposes — cutting the time spent searching and analysing tax legislation and case law, and the tool is now available across the firm's 3,000-strong tax and legal function ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2024/02/kpmg-uk-and-blue-j-expand-strategic-alliance-to-introduce-generative-ai-into-tax-tools.html)).

In February 2024 the alliance was expanded with Ask Blue J, a generative-AI product that answers challenging tax research questions in seconds, using OpenAI's GPT models fine-tuned for tax and Blue J's curated database of current tax content ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2024/02/kpmg-uk-and-blue-j-expand-strategic-alliance-to-introduce-generative-ai-into-tax-tools.html)). KPMG has exclusive access to the tool among the Big Four accountancy firms in the UK for the first three years ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2024/02/kpmg-uk-and-blue-j-expand-strategic-alliance-to-introduce-generative-ai-into-tax-tools.html)). The same AI push runs through the rest of the firm: AI is now in the hands of every auditor in the UK through the firm's AI audit platform, advisory teams use AI agents as "digital teammates", and in December 2025 KPMG signed a three-year Workplace AI agreement with Aiimi, while KPMG International became the first Big Four entity to gain ISO 42001 AI management certification ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/01/kpmg-uk-swiss-group-annual-results.html); [Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/)).

## Audit quality and the regulatory record

KPMG UK's recent history cannot be told without its regulatory sanctions, which are set out in the table below. The same regulator that fines the firm also reviews its audit quality each year, and Accountancy Today notes the FRC's annual review has identified KPMG as a market leader in audit quality ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/)) — a present-tense assessment that sits alongside the historical findings below.

| Case | Date | Sanction on the firm |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Silentnight sale | Aug 2021 | £13m fine, severe reprimand, independent reviewer ordered |
| Carillion audits | Oct 2023 | £21m payable (£30m gross), severe reprimands, remedial-action order |
| Carr's Group audit | Jun 2025 | £690,625 for independence breaches |
| N Brown audit | Oct 2025 | £1.25m gross (£710,937.50 payable), severe reprimand |
| John Wood Group audit | Jul 2026 | FRC investigation opened; outcome pending |

The Carillion sanctions are the largest in the table above by amount. KPMG audited the construction and facilities-management group for the financial years 2014, 2015 and 2016, giving unqualified opinions each year, before Carillion went into liquidation in January 2018 ([FRC](https://www.frc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/2023/10/sanctions-against-kpmg-llp-kpmg-audit-plc-and-two-former-partners/)). The FRC found "significant and serious breaches" in every audit investigated: KPMG failed to gather sufficient appropriate audit evidence, failed to exercise adequate professional scepticism, and failed to respond to numerous indicators that Carillion's core operations were loss-making and reliant on short-term, unsustainable measures to support cash flows ([FRC](https://www.frc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/2023/10/sanctions-against-kpmg-llp-kpmg-audit-plc-and-two-former-partners/)). The sanctions totalled £26.5 million reduced to £18.55 million for KPMG LLP, plus £3.5 million reduced to £2.45 million for KPMG Audit Plc — £21 million payable, plus £5,324,365.68 in investigation costs ([FRC](https://www.frc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/2023/10/sanctions-against-kpmg-llp-kpmg-audit-plc-and-two-former-partners/)). Engagement partner Peter Meehan was fined £350,000 after discount and excluded from ICAEW membership for 10 years ([FRC](https://www.frc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/2023/10/sanctions-against-kpmg-llp-kpmg-audit-plc-and-two-former-partners/)). The breaches were found not to be dishonest and mostly not intentional, but the FRC recorded a lack of integrity in Meehan's record of his review of the 2016 audit and four findings of a lack of objectivity ([FRC](https://www.frc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/2023/10/sanctions-against-kpmg-llp-kpmg-audit-plc-and-two-former-partners/)).

Silentnight was a different kind of failure — not an audit, but restructuring advice. Between August 2010 and January 2011, KPMG's then head of Manchester restructuring, David Costley-Wood, advised both Silentnight and the private equity buyer HIG on HIG's proposed acquisition, at a time when their interests conflicted; a tribunal found the firm and Costley-Wood breached the fundamental principles of objectivity and integrity, and that Costley-Wood acted dishonestly in dealings with the Pension Protection Fund and the Pensions Regulator ([FRC](https://www.frc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/2021/08/sanctions-against-kpmg-and-former-partner-in-relation-to-silentnight/)). The tribunal described KPMG's involvement as "deeply troubling" and found Costley-Wood had assisted a strategy designed to drive Silentnight into insolvency so HIG could acquire it without the burden of its pension scheme liabilities ([FRC](https://www.frc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/2021/08/sanctions-against-kpmg-and-former-partner-in-relation-to-silentnight/)). KPMG was fined £13 million and ordered to appoint an independent reviewer to examine why objectivity threats were not identified; Costley-Wood was fined £500,000 and excluded from ICAEW membership for 13 years ([FRC](https://www.frc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/2021/08/sanctions-against-kpmg-and-former-partner-in-relation-to-silentnight/)).

The more recent cases are smaller but show the scrutiny continuing. In June 2025 KPMG was fined £690,625 for independence breaches in its 2021 audit of Carr's Group, and in October 2025 it was fined £1.25 million, reduced to £710,937.50, and severely reprimanded over serious breaches of international auditing standards in its audit of N Brown Group's financial year ended 26 February 2022 — weaknesses in impairment modelling, cash flow forecasts, discount rates and sensitivity analysis — with partner Anthony Sykes fined £51,187.50 after discount ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/)). The FRC declared that the N Brown audit report did not satisfy the required standards, while not questioning the truth or fairness of N Brown's own financial statements ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/)). In July 2026 the FRC opened an investigation into KPMG's audit of John Wood Group's 2023 financial statements ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is KPMG UK?** KPMG UK is the United Kingdom member firm of KPMG International, organised as the partner-owned limited liability partnership KPMG LLP, headquartered at 15 Canada Square, Canary Wharf, London ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/)). It is one of the Big Four professional services firms, providing audit, tax and legal, advisory and deal advisory services, and since October 2024 it has operated within a combined UK/Swiss group ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/01/kpmg-uk-swiss-group-annual-results.html)).

**Is KPMG UK the same as KPMG globally?** No. KPMG is a network of separate legal entities, each locally owned and managed; KPMG International Limited only coordinates the network and provides no client services ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/pt/en/governance.html)). The UK firm is responsible for its own obligations and liabilities, and its accounts — £3.021 billion UK fee income — are separate from the combined £3.6 billion UK/Swiss Group revenue ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/01/kpmg-uk-swiss-group-annual-results.html)).

**How big is KPMG UK?** The UK firm has 850 partners and 15,889 staff excluding partners, and the combined UK/Swiss Group employs 25,000 people ([Accountancy Today](https://www.accountancytoday.co.uk/knowledge-base/kpmg/); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2026/01/kpmg-uk-swiss-group-annual-results.html)). The wider KPMG network operates in 138 countries and territories with more than 276,000 people ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/about/our-history.html)).

**What is KPMG UK's relationship with Blue J?** KPMG UK has used Blue J's AI tax technology since 2022, and in February 2024 the firms launched Ask Blue J, a generative-AI tax research tool to which KPMG has exclusive access among Big Four firms in the UK for three years ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/uk/en/media/press-releases/2024/02/kpmg-uk-and-blue-j-expand-strategic-alliance-to-introduce-generative-ai-into-tax-tools.html)).

**Why was KPMG UK fined over Carillion?** The FRC found its audits of Carillion for 2014–2016 seriously deficient, with inadequate professional scepticism and a failure to respond to signs the business was loss-making, and imposed £21 million in payable sanctions plus costs in October 2023 ([FRC](https://www.frc.org.uk/news-and-events/news/2023/10/sanctions-against-kpmg-llp-kpmg-audit-plc-and-two-former-partners/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not state KPMG's global FY25 revenue: the press figure of $39.8 billion could not be verified from a fully-read source, because the KPMG International release could not be fetched this session, so it is left out rather than cited from a snippet. It does not state a single headcount for the firm, because the sources give different measures by scope — 15,889 UK staff excluding partners, almost 17,000 people in the UK including partners, and 25,000 across the combined UK/Swiss Group — and collapsing them into one number would mislead. It does not state the exact year the firm name shortened to "KPMG", which reference works give as 1999 but no fully-read source confirmed. It does not report 2026 job cuts or the July 2022 £14.4 million FRC fine for misleading documents, neither of which was verified from a fully-read source this session. It does not claim the FRC's John Wood Group investigation has reached any conclusion — it was opened in July 2026 and no outcome has been published. Finally, the "market leader in audit quality" description is Accountancy Today's summary of the FRC's annual review, not this profile's own judgement.
