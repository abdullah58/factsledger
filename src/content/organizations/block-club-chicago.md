## Overview

Block Club Chicago is a nonprofit, reader-funded online newsroom that covers [Chicago](/places/chicago/) neighborhood by neighborhood. Founded in June 2018 by three former DNAinfo Chicago editors after that site's abrupt closure, it assigns reporters to geographic beats across the city's community areas and funds its work through subscriptions, foundation grants, advertising and donations. The site launched on 13 June 2018, founded by Shamus Toomey, Jen Sabella and Stephanie Lulay, editors of DNAinfo Chicago, which billionaire owner Joe Ricketts had shuttered in November 2017 after four years and 11 months ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/business-work/2023/block-club-chicago-fifth-anniversary-dnainfo/)). Its name refers to a type of Chicago neighborhood association where people band together to share information and improve the community, and its stated mission is "to build community through truly ground-level reporting of the city's neighborhoods" ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/business-work/2023/block-club-chicago-fifth-anniversary-dnainfo/)). Block Club is an independent 501(c)(3), journalist-run newsroom promising 24/7 non-partisan coverage ([Editor & Publisher](https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/block-club-chicago-celebrates-five-years-and-counting,242601)). By June 2023 it had almost 20,000 subscribers and a staff of 29, and its neighborhood reporting had covered institutions across the city, including a 2021 feature on the [Copernicus Foundation](/organizations/copernicus-foundation/) marking its 50th anniversary ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/business-work/2023/block-club-chicago-fifth-anniversary-dnainfo/)).

## From DNAinfo's closure to a reader-funded launch

DNAinfo, founded in 2009 by Joe Ricketts, who made his fortune through TD Ameritrade, embedded reporters in Chicago and New York neighborhoods, but Ricketts shut it and the Gothamist network down in November 2017, citing a business model that would not work ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2018/02/dnainfo-chicago-will-be-reborn-as-block-club-chicago-relying-on-blockchain-and-subscriptions-instead-of-billionaires/)). Toomey, Sabella and Lulay — respectively DNAinfo Chicago's former managing editor, deputy editor and senior editor — decided to rebuild the mission as a nonprofit that asked readers to pay ([American Journalism Project](https://theajp.org/american-journalism-project-announces-transformative-grants/)). Their Kickstarter campaign set a $25,000 goal, passed it within hours, raised about $100,000 on the first day and finished at $183,000, which Lulay said broke a Kickstarter record ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/business-work/2023/block-club-chicago-fifth-anniversary-dnainfo/)). Seed support also came from Civil, a blockchain-based journalism marketplace, and the newsroom hired five reporters at launch, four of them from DNAinfo Chicago ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2018/02/dnainfo-chicago-will-be-reborn-as-block-club-chicago-relying-on-blockchain-and-subscriptions-instead-of-billionaires/)). Early subscriptions were priced at $5 a month, later $59 a year or $6 a month ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/business-work/2023/block-club-chicago-fifth-anniversary-dnainfo/)).

## A neighborhood-beat newsroom and its funding model

Block Club organizes coverage by geography, not topic: reporters are assigned to specific neighborhoods, aiming to cover every neighborhood in the city ([American Journalism Project](https://theajp.org/american-journalism-project-announces-transformative-grants/)). By January 2022 it covered 32 neighborhoods, half of them majority Black or Latino on the historically undercovered South and West Sides; by June 2023 that had grown to 45 of Chicago's 77 community areas ([American Journalism Project](https://theajp.org/american-journalism-project-announces-transformative-grants/); [Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/business-work/2023/block-club-chicago-fifth-anniversary-dnainfo/)). Its output spans the website, daily newsletters, the "It's All Good" podcast, the weekly "On The Block" TV show and a COVID-19 hotline ([Robert R. McCormick Foundation](https://www.mccormickfoundation.org/news/mccormick-foundation-announces-7-5m-investment-in-sustainable-fact-based-reporting/)). Funding shifted sharply as grants arrived: a year before June 2023 about 70 percent of funding came from readers; by then only about a third did, with about 60 percent from foundation grants and about 5 percent each from advertising and donations ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/business-work/2023/block-club-chicago-fifth-anniversary-dnainfo/)). That swing shows how fast reader-funded independence can give way to grant dependence — and why Lulay said keeping readers a big share matters ([Editor & Publisher](https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/block-club-chicago-celebrates-five-years-and-counting,242601)).

## Investigations, awards and collaborations

Its best-known investigation, led by Kelly Bauer, exposed Loretto Hospital diverting COVID-19 vaccines from its underserved West Side community to ineligible people with ties to hospital administrators; the reporting prompted the hospital chief executive's ouster and an FBI investigation ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/business-work/2023/block-club-chicago-fifth-anniversary-dnainfo/)). Lighter coverage also traveled widely: a contest to name an alligator found in the Humboldt Park lagoon produced "Chance the Snapper," and "Chicago Is for Lovebirds" T-shirts celebrated Monty and Rose, piping plovers nesting at Montrose Beach ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/business-work/2023/block-club-chicago-fifth-anniversary-dnainfo/)). In May 2023 the newsroom won five Peter J. Lisagor awards from the Chicago Headline Club, a branch of the Society of Professional Journalists, including a report on racial inequity in police traffic stops ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/business-work/2023/block-club-chicago-fifth-anniversary-dnainfo/)). It was also LION Publishers' Publisher of the Year in 2021 and won its 2022 Outstanding Coverage and Revenue Campaign awards ([Editor & Publisher](https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/block-club-chicago-celebrates-five-years-and-counting,242601)). With Borderless Magazine it followed ten Venezuelan migrants bused to Chicago by Texas Governor Greg Abbott in the series "After the Buses" ([Editor & Publisher](https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/block-club-chicago-celebrates-five-years-and-counting,242601)).

## FAQ

### When was Block Club Chicago founded, and by whom?

Block Club Chicago launched on 13 June 2018, founded by Shamus Toomey, Jen Sabella and Stephanie Lulay, three former editors of DNAinfo Chicago ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/business-work/2023/block-club-chicago-fifth-anniversary-dnainfo/)). They started the newsroom after DNAinfo's billionaire owner, Joe Ricketts, abruptly shut the site down in November 2017.

### How is Block Club Chicago funded?

It is an independent 501(c)(3) nonprofit funded by reader subscriptions — $59 a year or $6 a month in 2023 — plus foundation grants, advertising, donations and merchandise ([Editor & Publisher](https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/block-club-chicago-celebrates-five-years-and-counting,242601)). By June 2023 about 60 percent of revenue came from foundation grants and about a third from readers ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/business-work/2023/block-club-chicago-fifth-anniversary-dnainfo/)).

### What does Block Club Chicago cover?

It covers Chicago neighborhood by neighborhood, assigning reporters to geographic beats rather than topical ones, with the stated aim of covering every neighborhood in the city ([American Journalism Project](https://theajp.org/american-journalism-project-announces-transformative-grants/)). By June 2023 it reported covering 45 of the city's 77 community areas ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/business-work/2023/block-club-chicago-fifth-anniversary-dnainfo/)).

### What is Block Club Chicago's best-known investigation?

An investigation led by Kelly Bauer exposed Loretto Hospital diverting COVID-19 vaccines from its underserved West Side community in Chicago to ineligible people with ties to hospital administrators ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/business-work/2023/block-club-chicago-fifth-anniversary-dnainfo/)). The reporting prompted the hospital chief executive's ouster and an FBI investigation.
