## Overview

The Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF) is [France](/places/france/)'s independent financial markets regulator: the public authority that licenses, supervises and, where necessary, sanctions the firms, products and markets of the French financial marketplace, and that rules on whether takeover offers for listed companies may proceed ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/en/amf/our-missions)). Created by the Financial Security Act of 1 August 2003 from the merger of three older regulators, it describes its own tasks in four verbs — regulate, supervise, inform and protect — and its defining feature is a split between the body that prosecutes and the body that punishes ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/en/amf/our-missions); [AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/en/amf/our-organisation/our-governance)).

The AMF is an independent public authority with legal personality and financial autonomy, funded by the industry it supervises rather than by the state budget ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/sites/institutionnel/files/contenu_simple/rapport_annuel/rapport_annuel_amf/Annual%20Report%202003%20-%20Preamble.pdf)). Its reach runs from listed-company disclosures and takeover bids to asset managers, investment advisers and crypto-asset service providers — and, as the 2024 enforcement figures show, it is the most financially punitive securities regulator in Europe.

## Three regulators into one

France reorganised its financial regulation in 2003, replacing a fragmented system with a single entity to handle corporate finance, financial information, investment service providers, collective investment schemes, financial markets and post-trade activities ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/sites/institutionnel/files/contenu_simple/rapport_annuel/rapport_annuel_amf/Annual%20Report%202003%20-%20Preamble.pdf)). The new authority was inaugurated by Francis Mer, then Minister of the Economy, Finance and Industry, on 24 November 2003, under the Financial Security Act of 1 August 2003, and its first chairman was Michel Prada, appointed by decree of the President of the Republic on 21 November 2003 ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/sites/institutionnel/files/contenu_simple/rapport_annuel/rapport_annuel_amf/Annual%20Report%202003%20-%20Preamble.pdf)).

The AMF succeeded to the rights and obligations of all three and took over their inspection, investigation and sanction procedures unchanged, enforcing the COB's regulations and the CMF's general regulations until it adopted its own rulebook ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/sites/institutionnel/files/contenu_simple/rapport_annuel/rapport_annuel_amf/Annual%20Report%202003%20-%20Preamble.pdf)). Its founding law is now codified as Articles L.621-1 to L.621-30 of the Monetary and Financial Code. The merger was presented as making French regulation more transparent and effective, with greater powers and resources and a legally secure disciplinary procedure — a deliberate break from a system in which market regulation, conduct rules and asset-management discipline had sat in separate hands.

## What the AMF does: regulate, supervise, inform and protect

The AMF's remit is to ensure that savings are protected when invested in financial instruments or any other investment offered to the public, that investors receive proper information, and that the markets where financial instruments trade function efficiently — taking financial stability objectives into account ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/en/amf/our-missions)). It is also responsible for the quality of information management companies provide about their strategy for combating climate change ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/en/amf/our-missions)). To carry out these tasks it regulates, authorises, monitors and where necessary inspects, investigates and enforces, and it contributes to regulation at European and international level ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/en/amf/our-missions)).

Its areas of action cover financial markets and market infrastructures, listed companies, financial intermediaries authorised to provide investment services and advice — credit institutions, investment firms, investment management companies and financial investment advisers — collective investment products, and token issuers and digital asset service providers ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/en/amf/our-missions)). Retail investors who run into trouble can turn to the AMF's financial ombudsman for assistance ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/en/amf/our-missions)). What the AMF is not matters too: prudential supervision of banks and insurers — checking whether a firm is financially sound — sits with other authorities, while the AMF is the conduct-and-markets regulator. Its independence is structural: its members are nominated by public authorities, including the President of the Republic, the Conseil d'État, the Court of Cassation, the Cour des Comptes and the governor of the Banque de France ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/sites/institutionnel/files/contenu_simple/rapport_annuel/rapport_annuel_amf/Annual%20Report%202003%20-%20Preamble.pdf)).

## Governance, enforcement — and the CNP Assurances takeover

The AMF is built so that the same hands never both accuse and punish. Its Board (*Collège*), the main decision-making body, has 16 members designated by the public authorities; it adopts the AMF's General Regulation, grants authorisations and approvals, examines inspection and investigation reports, and decides whether to open sanction or injunction proceedings ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/en/amf/our-organisation/our-governance)). The power to impose sanctions belongs to a separate Enforcement Committee of 12 members, which enjoys full decision-making autonomy ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/en/amf/our-organisation/our-governance)). The split was designed to segregate prosecution from discipline, as required by Article 6(1) of the European Convention for the Protection of Human Rights ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/sites/institutionnel/files/contenu_simple/rapport_annuel/rapport_annuel_amf/Annual%20Report%202003%20-%20Preamble.pdf)). The Chair — currently Marie-Anne Barbat-Layani — is appointed by presidential decree for a five-year, non-renewable term, and each year submits the annual report to the President of the Republic and the Presidents of the [Assemblée Nationale](/organizations/assemblee-nationale/) and the Senate ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/en/amf/our-organisation/our-governance)). The Secretary General, Sébastien Raspiller, decides whether to open investigations and inspections and negotiates settlements; six consultative commissions of experts advise the Board.

The enforcement record gives the structure teeth. In 2024 the AMF imposed 42 financial penalties and disciplinary sanctions and entered into nine administrative settlements totalling €29.4 million — almost a third of the entire European Economic Area total of over €100 million, the highest consolidated amounts in Europe ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/en/news-publications/news/publication-annual-esma-report-sanctions-and-measures-2024-amf-imposes-highest-amounts-europe)). Market manipulation alone accounted for €19.1 million, and sanctions under the Market Abuse Regulation made up 70% of the AMF's total — a concentration that shows the authority's enforcement weight falls overwhelmingly on market integrity rather than on paperwork breaches ([AMF](https://www.amf-france.org/en/news-publications/news/publication-annual-esma-report-sanctions-and-measures-2024-amf-imposes-highest-amounts-europe)). The same gatekeeping role appears in takeover offers. [La Banque Postale](/organizations/la-banque-postale/)'s simplified tender offer for [CNP Assurances](/organizations/cnp-assurances/) was declared compliant by the AMF on 26 April 2022 and ran from 2 to 31 May at €20.90 per share ex-dividend (€21.90 cum dividend), a 36.2% premium valuing CNP at about €15 billion ([La Banque Postale](https://echanges.dila.gouv.fr/OPENDATA/AMF/ACT/2022/05/FCACT059857_20220502.pdf)). On 3 June 2022 La Banque Postale announced all conditions were fulfilled, holding 97.79% of the capital and 98.56% of the voting rights, and moved to squeeze out the remaining minorities ([Eurex](https://www.eurex.com/ex-en/rules-regs/corporate-actions/corporate-action-information/CNP-Assurances-S.A.-Takeover-Updated-06-June-2022--2827330)).

## FAQ

### What is the AMF in France?

The Autorité des marchés financiers is France's independent financial markets regulator. Created in 2003 by merging three older bodies, it regulates French financial markets, listed companies, investment firms, asset managers and crypto-asset service providers, and its tasks are to regulate, supervise, inform and protect investors.

### When was the AMF created and from what?

The AMF was created by the Financial Security Act of 1 August 2003 and inaugurated on 24 November 2003. It merged the Commission des Opérations de Bourse (founded 1967), the Conseil des Marchés Financiers (1996) and the Conseil de Discipline de la Gestion Financière (1989) into a single markets regulator.

### Who runs the AMF and who can punish wrongdoers?

The Chair, currently Marie-Anne Barbat-Layani, is appointed by presidential decree for a single five-year term. A 16-member Board decides whether to bring proceedings, but only the separate 12-member Enforcement Committee can impose sanctions — a deliberate split between prosecution and punishment.

### What did the AMF do in the CNP Assurances takeover?

It declared La Banque Postale's simplified tender offer compliant on 26 April 2022, visaing the offer and response documents. The offer ran 2–31 May 2022 at €20.90 per share ex-dividend (€21.90 cum dividend); by 3 June 2022 La Banque Postale held 97.79% of the capital and moved to squeeze out the minorities.

### How tough an enforcer is the AMF?

In 2024 it imposed 42 penalties and disciplinary sanctions plus nine settlements totalling €29.4 million — the highest consolidated amounts of any regulator in the European Economic Area, and almost a third of the EEA total. Market manipulation accounted for €19.1 million of that sum.
