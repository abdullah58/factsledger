## Overview

Most retellings of the UFC's rise treat Zuffa, LLC as a chapter that closed: the Fertitta brothers' vehicle bought a failing promotion for $2 million in 2001, built it into a global sport, sold it for about $4 billion in 2016, and that was that. The undercovered part is that Zuffa never actually went away. After the 2016 sale it stayed on as the company's legal name, and in the 2023 merger with [WWE](/organizations/wwe/) it became TKO Operating Company — the subsidiary that, by [TKO](/organizations/tko-group-holdings/)'s own SEC filings, owns all of the UFC and WWE businesses' assets ([SEC](https://d5bp8bijhhba2.cloudfront.net/pdf/bd29de828343ae895da7c83f452d252cda663dd138e009ad278affb9c20535c4.pdf)). Then, in 2025, TKO dusted the name off for a new boxing promotion ([SportsBusiness Journal](https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Articles/2025/03/05/tko-group-officially-enters-boxing-in-deal-with-saudis/)). The corporate body outlived the brand.

## The $2 million gamble

By 2000 the [Ultimate Fighting Championship](/organizations/ufc/) was on the brink of bankruptcy, its owner Semaphore Entertainment Group drained by cable boycotts and regulatory bans ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/ultimate-fighting-championships-fight-to-the-top-1.149391)). The buyers were unlikely saviors: [Lorenzo Fertitta](/people/lorenzo-fertitta/) and his older brother [Frank Fertitta III](/people/frank-fertitta/), Station Casinos executives with a passion for martial arts, plus their childhood friend [Dana White](/people/dana-white/), who was managing MMA fighters at the time ([Sherdog](https://www.sherdog.com/news/news/Report-UFC-Sells-10-Percent-Stake-to-Abu-Dhabi-Company-22003)). In January 2001 they bought the UFC and all its assets for $2 million and set up a holding company to contain it — Zuffa, LLC ([Sherdog](https://www.sherdog.com/news/news/Report-UFC-Sells-10-Percent-Stake-to-Abu-Dhabi-Company-22003)). The price looked exorbitant then: Lorenzo later recalled that his attorneys told him he was crazy, that he was paying $2 million for "nothing." His answer, in a 2009 Fighters Only interview: "I'm getting the most valuable thing... those three letters: UFC" ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/ultimate-fighting-championships-fight-to-the-top-1.149391)).

The name itself was a wink at the business: Bloomberg's profile of the brothers described Zuffa as "named after the Italian word for fight" — a detail most histories of the company never mention ([Bloomberg via republication](https://filsingergames.proboards.com/thread/11243/article-bloomberg-fertitta-brothers)).

## Turning losses into an empire

For years the gamble looked terrible. By 2004 the company had lost $34 million ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/ultimate-fighting-championships-fight-to-the-top-1.149391)). The rescue was television. The brothers put $10 million of their own money into producing The Ultimate Fighter, a reality competition that aired on Spike TV in 2005 — and the first pay-per-view after the show doubled the UFC's previous buy-rate record, pulling 300,000 buys ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/ultimate-fighting-championships-fight-to-the-top-1.149391)). They also drew on their gaming-industry roots, using contacts from the casino world to stage events at Donald Trump's Atlantic City casino and the MGM Grand and Mandalay Bay in Las Vegas ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/ultimate-fighting-championships-fight-to-the-top-1.149391)).

Flush with momentum, Zuffa started buying rivals: World Extreme Cagefighting and the World Fighting Alliance in 2006, Japan's Pride Fighting Championships for close to $70 million in 2007, and Strikeforce for about $40 million in early 2011 ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/ultimate-fighting-championships-fight-to-the-top-1.149391)). The 2006 pay-per-view revenue of $222.7 million broke a seven-year-old boxing record ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/ultimate-fighting-championships-fight-to-the-top-1.149391)). In August 2011 the company signed a seven-year broadcast deal with Fox reportedly worth $90 million a year; by 2015 the UFC was running 41 events in 32 cities across 13 countries ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/ultimate-fighting-championships-fight-to-the-top-1.149391)).

The growth needed money, and in January 2010 the Fertittas sold a 10% stake to Flash Entertainment, an Abu Dhabi investment vehicle ([Sherdog](https://www.sherdog.com/news/news/Report-UFC-Sells-10-Percent-Stake-to-Abu-Dhabi-Company-22003)). The new cap table: 40.5% each for Lorenzo and Frank, 10% for Flash, 9% for White ([Sherdog](https://www.sherdog.com/news/news/Report-UFC-Sells-10-Percent-Stake-to-Abu-Dhabi-Company-22003)). Behind the glamour, the borrowing was real — data from Moody's, reported via the Las Vegas Review-Journal, showed Zuffa carrying $450 million in debt ([Sherdog](https://www.sherdog.com/news/news/Report-UFC-Sells-10-Percent-Stake-to-Abu-Dhabi-Company-22003)).

## The exits — and the name that wouldn't die

On July 11, 2016, the UFC was sold to a group led by [WME-IMG](/organizations/wme-img/) for about $4 billion — described at the time as the single biggest transaction in sports history ([Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/2651522-for-dana-white-and-fertittas-ufc-sale-leaves-behind-complex-legacy)). The brothers kept passive minority interests, and White stayed on as president ([Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/2651522-for-dana-white-and-fertittas-ufc-sale-leaves-behind-complex-legacy)). Lorenzo's estimate put 2015 revenue at close to $600 million, but the deal's shadow side was already visible: analysts at Bloody Elbow estimated fighters took home only 13.6 to 16.3 percent of revenue from 2011 to 2015, and a class-action antitrust suit was already pending ([Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/2651522-for-dana-white-and-fertittas-ufc-sale-leaves-behind-complex-legacy)).

Endeavor, WME-IMG's parent, took a majority stake in that deal — 50.1% — and in February 2021 agreed to buy out the remaining minority interests and take 100% ownership, lining up at least $1.75 billion to do it ahead of its own IPO ([ESPN](https://www.espn.ph/mma/ufc/story/_/id/31173773/endeavor-already-majority-owner-ufc-agrees-take-full-control-promotion)).

Then came the merger. On September 12, 2023, TKO Group Holdings debuted on the New York Stock Exchange, combining the UFC and WWE businesses at a $21.4 billion valuation — Endeavor holding a 51% controlling interest, WWE shareholders 49%, Ari Emanuel as CEO and White as CEO of UFC ([Sherdog](https://www.sherdog.com/news/news/UFC-WWE-Officially-Merge-Under-Parent-Company-TKO-Group-Holdings-191014)). Buried in the transaction paperwork was the legal-entity mechanic competitors skip: the entity then called "Zuffa Parent, LLC" was renamed TKO Operating Company, LLC. TKO's own 10-K spells it out — "TKO OpCo" is "f/k/a Zuffa Parent LLC," the accounting acquirer and predecessor of TKO, the operating subsidiary that owns all of the UFC and WWE businesses' assets. Zuffa didn't die; it became the house ([SEC](https://d5bp8bijhhba2.cloudfront.net/pdf/bd29de828343ae895da7c83f452d252cda663dd138e009ad278affb9c20535c4.pdf)).

The legal afterlife surfaced again in 2024: when TKO agreed in March to pay $335 million to settle two class-action antitrust suits, the cases were described as having been brought against Zuffa, "the previous entity that owned and operated UFC" ([Sportcal](https://www.sportcal.com/newsletters/tko-agrees-335m-settlement-in-antitrust-case-against-ex-ufc-fighters/)). The settlement averted an April 15 trial that could have put up to $1.6 billion in claimed damages in front of a jury ([Sportcal](https://www.sportcal.com/newsletters/tko-agrees-335m-settlement-in-antitrust-case-against-ex-ufc-fighters/)).

And in 2025 the name itself came back. TKO announced in March that it would enter boxing as managing partner in a venture with Saudi Arabia's Sela and Turki Alalshikh, with White and WWE president Nick Khan tapped to lead — and the venture took the name Zuffa Boxing in June 2025 ([SportsBusiness Journal](https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Articles/2025/03/05/tko-group-officially-enters-boxing-in-deal-with-saudis/)). The revival drew an immediate threat: Frank Warren's Queensberry Promotions, citing its own relationship with Sela, was reported to be preparing a breach-of-contract suit worth up to $1 billion, a claim Sela denied ([MMA Weekly](https://www.mmaweekly.com/news/frank-warren-preparing-1-billion-lawsuit-over-zuffa-boxing)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim the 2016 transaction was a clean, total sale at a single price: the fully-read sources describe it as "about $4 billion" with the Fertittas retaining minority interests, and ESPN reports Endeavor acquired a 50.1% majority stake that year. The precise $4.025 billion figure some accounts cite was not confirmed in a fully read source. This profile does not treat Zuffa as a defunct entity — it became TKO Operating Company, LLC in 2023; claims about what Zuffa, LLC still holds by name beyond the 10-K's asset language are listed as open items, not asserted. This profile does not assert the exact Italian rendering of "Zuffa" beyond Bloomberg's attributed "fight"; variant renderings are parked in open items. This profile does not assert the outcome of Frank Warren's reported $1 billion threat against Zuffa Boxing; the February 2026 report is the last verified status. This profile does not claim the $450 million debt figure as current — it reflects 2010 reporting of Zuffa's obligations at that time.
