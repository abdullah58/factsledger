## Overview

StyleSeat is a [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/)-based online marketplace and business-management platform for independent beauty and wellness professionals, founded in 2011 by Melody McCloskey and Dan Levine. It is often described as the "OpenTable for beauty": clients discover and book stylists, barbers and other professionals, and the professionals get scheduling, payments and marketing tools ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/beauty-hairstyling-scheduling-digital-payments/)). Its distinctive economics are a 50% cut of a new client's first appointment, a referral fee, on top of the tools it sells to professionals ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/beauty-hairstyling-scheduling-digital-payments/)). It raised $40 million by March 2019 ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/beauty-hairstyling-scheduling-digital-payments/)).

## Bootstrapped, then laughed out of boardrooms

The idea came from McCloskey's own frustration finding and booking a stylist ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/entrepreneur/styleseat-helps-200k-beauty-wellness-professionals-grow-their-business)). She had led online distribution at Current TV, where she met co-founder Dan Levine ([Women 2.0](https://women2.com/2014/01/22/20140122female-founded-styleseat-raises-10-2-million-series-financing/)). By her own account she went back and forth on the idea for two years before finally pursuing it, which dates the idea to about 2009, two years before the 2011 launch ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/beauty-hairstyling-scheduling-digital-payments/)).

## How StyleSeat makes money

StyleSeat's model has two parts. Professionals pay for tools — scheduling, client notes, social and email marketing, payments and daily payouts — and StyleSeat takes a 50% cut of a new client's first appointment, a referral fee for bringing the professional a customer they did not have ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/beauty-hairstyling-scheduling-digital-payments/)). The referral fee is the distinctive part: unlike a flat subscription, it ties StyleSeat's revenue to the new business it actually delivers, which is why the company describes itself as a marketplace rather than a software vendor ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/beauty-hairstyling-scheduling-digital-payments/)). It is the opposite of [Booksy](/organizations/booksy/)'s model, which charges salons a flat monthly subscription and keeps the booking revenue in the salon's hands. The company was self-funded for about 18 months, and McCloskey says she was "laughed out of every boardroom" raising for a hair startup in Silicon Valley, where male investors did not understand a female-centred industry ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/beauty-hairstyling-scheduling-digital-payments/), [Women 2.0](https://women2.com/2014/01/22/20140122female-founded-styleseat-raises-10-2-million-series-financing/)). Early angels included Ashton Kutcher, Sophia Bush, Travis Kalanick and Garrett Camp, the [Uber](/organizations/uber/) co-founders ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/beauty-hairstyling-scheduling-digital-payments/)).

The fundraising barrier is the most distinctive verified thread in StyleSeat's story: a female-founded company in a female-dominated industry, raising in a venture market that did not see hair as a technology category. Forbes named StyleSeat one of the top four marketplaces of 2014 ([Women 2.0](https://women2.com/2014/01/22/20140122female-founded-styleseat-raises-10-2-million-series-financing/)).

## Series A, Series B, and the bookings trajectory

StyleSeat's Series A of $10.2 million was announced in January 2014, led by [Lightspeed Venture Partners](/organizations/lightspeed-venture-partners/) with Lowercase Capital participating ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/entrepreneur/styleseat-helps-200k-beauty-wellness-professionals-grow-their-business)). At the time the platform counted more than 200,000 beauty professionals, 2 million clients and $350 million in services booked since 2011, with 5.5 million appointments in 2013 and professionals in about 15,000 US cities; the average professional's revenue rose 68% in the first 15 months, according to the company, and 68% of usage came from mobile ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/entrepreneur/styleseat-helps-200k-beauty-wellness-professionals-grow-their-business), [Women 2.0](https://women2.com/2014/01/22/20140122female-founded-styleseat-raises-10-2-million-series-financing/)).

The Series B followed in July 2015: $25 million led by Fosun Kinzon Capital, with Lightspeed, Cowboy Ventures and Slow Ventures participating ([Fosun RZ Capital](https://www.frzcapital.com/content/details5_224.html)). The bookings trajectory the company disclosed then is the clearest measure of its growth:

| Year | Services booked | Appointments |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2013 | $180m | 5.5m |
| 2014 | $443m | Not stated |
| 2015 (expected) | More than $1.1bn | 23m |

The company had passed 20 million appointments since launch by mid-2015, and said it would use the new money for a big-data experiment, a change to its business model, and expansion into Asia and South America in 2016 with Fosun's help ([Fosun RZ Capital](https://www.frzcapital.com/content/details5_224.html)).

## The BeautyBooked pivot

In January 2016 StyleSeat acquired BeautyBooked, a New York high-end salon and spa booking platform with more than 1,000 marquee salons and a partnership with Allure magazine, to expand from individual professionals into the $21 billion salon-and-spa segment ([Salon Today](https://www.salontoday.com/news/styleseat-acquires-beautybooked)). The deal came after a year of 100% year-on-year growth in 2015, with 23 million appointments booked, more than $2 billion in booked services and 44 million appointments to date ([Salon Today](https://www.salontoday.com/news/styleseat-acquires-beautybooked)).

By March 2019 total funding stood at $40 million ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/beauty-hairstyling-scheduling-digital-payments/)). The board then included former Uber chief executive Travis Kalanick and Minted co-founder Melissa Kim ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/beauty-hairstyling-scheduling-digital-payments/)). StyleSeat's model — a referral fee on new clients rather than a flat subscription — sets it apart from Booksy, which charges salons a monthly subscription; the two are the US and European answers to the same booking problem, built on opposite economics.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is StyleSeat?**
A San Francisco-based marketplace and business platform for independent beauty and wellness professionals, founded in 2011 ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/entrepreneur/styleseat-helps-200k-beauty-wellness-professionals-grow-their-business)).

**How does StyleSeat make money?**
It takes a 50% cut of a new client's first appointment — a referral fee — and sells professionals scheduling, client notes, social and email marketing, payments and daily payouts ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/beauty-hairstyling-scheduling-digital-payments/)).
