## Overview

Craft Ventures is a [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) venture capital firm co-founded in 2017 by [David Sacks](/people/david-sacks/) and Bill Lee, built on the idea that former founders and operators make better early-stage investors. It manages $3.3 billion across six funds and is best known for leading Bird's Series A and backing [ClickUp](/organizations/clickup/), [SpaceX](/organizations/spacex/) and [Reddit](/organizations/reddit/) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/15/david-sackss-craft-ventures-just-closed-its-second-fund-with-500-million/); [Craft Ventures](https://www.craftventures.com/articles/announcing-1-3-billion-for-craft-ventures-iv-and-growth-ii)).

The firm invests in B2B software — above all SaaS and marketplace businesses — from seed stage through growth rounds, and its partners divide the work by the companies they once built themselves: Sacks leads SaaS, StubHub founder Jeff Fluhr leads marketplaces, Lee chases breakthrough technologies and EarthLink founder Sky Dayton covers hard tech ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/15/david-sackss-craft-ventures-just-closed-its-second-fund-with-500-million/); [Craft Ventures](https://www.craftventures.com/articles/announcing-craft-iii-1-1-billion-for-saas-and-marketplaces)). Craft's public profile surged beyond venture circles in December 2024, when President-elect Donald Trump named Sacks his White House AI and Crypto Czar; Sacks stepped down from that role in March 2026 and returned to fundraising, filing for a roughly $1 billion fifth fund that August ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/white-house-ai-czar-sacks-step-down-moves-advisory-role-2026-03-27/); [TechStartups](https://techstartups.com/2026/08/11/david-sacks-craft-ventures-targets-1-billion-for-first-new-fund-since-leaving-white-house/)).

## Founding and the operator thesis

Craft was founded in late 2017 by two serial entrepreneurs with long angel-investing records. Sacks had been the founding COO of [PayPal](/organizations/paypal/) and founded Yammer, the enterprise social network Microsoft bought in 2012 for $1.2 billion — proceeds that helped fund Craft — and later spent under a year as interim CEO of Zenefits after its founder was ousted amid a regulatory scandal ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91246166/david-sacks-trump-ai-crypto-czar-explained); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/15/david-sackss-craft-ventures-just-closed-its-second-fund-with-500-million/)). Lee had sold Remarq to Critical Path for more than $200 million, Fluhr had founded StubHub and sold it to eBay for $310 million, and Dayton had founded the dial-up pioneer EarthLink and the Wi-Fi provider Boingo Wireless ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2019/01/inside-the-mind-of-a-nyc-vc-bryan-rosenblatt-of-craft-ventures/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/15/david-sackss-craft-ventures-just-closed-its-second-fund-with-500-million/)).

The pitch was "founders for founders": all three original general partners had built and sold companies and had angel-invested for two decades in names including Uber, Facebook, Airbnb, Slack, Tesla, SpaceX and Warby Parker, and the firm argued that operating experience — not just a investing track record — was what made founders take its calls ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2019/01/inside-the-mind-of-a-nyc-vc-bryan-rosenblatt-of-craft-ventures/)). Craft also built a platform team unusual for a firm of its size: full-time executives focused on recruiting, marketing and communications, plus operating partners covering talent, PR, an Executive Briefing Center that introduces portfolio companies to Fortune 2000 CIOs and CTOs, performance marketing, legal and governance, government relations and information security ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2019/01/inside-the-mind-of-a-nyc-vc-bryan-rosenblatt-of-craft-ventures/); [Craft Ventures](https://www.craftventures.com/articles/announcing-craft-iii-1-1-billion-for-saas-and-marketplaces)). Sacks is also a co-host of the [All-In podcast](/organizations/all-in-podcast/), which became a prominent part of his public profile ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91246166/david-sacks-trump-ai-crypto-czar-explained)).

At the outset the firm described itself as industry-agnostic, investing at seed, Series A and Series B in whatever fit each partner's experience — Sacks looked for consumer and enterprise startups that could "go viral," Fluhr for marketplaces and e-commerce, Lee for breakthrough technology and Dayton for hard tech ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/15/david-sackss-craft-ventures-just-closed-its-second-fund-with-500-million/)). Blockchain was a major early theme — Harbor, a protocol for issuing crypto securities in compliance with regulations, was among its first announced deals — but by 2019 the firm was already broadening well beyond crypto ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/15/david-sackss-craft-ventures-just-closed-its-second-fund-with-500-million/)). The focus then narrowed to its lasting shape: B2B software, and within it bottom-up SaaS companies whose products spread virally like consumer products, and marketplace businesses with network effects and liquidity-driven moats ([Craft Ventures](https://www.craftventures.com/articles/announcing-craft-iii-1-1-billion-for-saas-and-marketplaces)).

## The funds

Craft grew its capital base in a steady cadence — a $350 million debut fund, $500 million second fund, then paired early-stage and growth funds from 2021 onward:

| Fund | Year | Size | What it marked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Craft Ventures I | 2018 | $350 million | Debut fund |
| Craft Ventures II | 2019 | $500 million | Closed October 2019 after about a year of fundraising |
| Craft Ventures III | 2021 | $612 million | Third early-stage fund; SaaS and marketplaces focus |
| Craft Ventures Growth I | 2021 | $510 million | First growth fund; combined 2021 total $1.12 billion, AUM $2 billion |
| Craft Ventures IV | 2023 | $712 million | Fourth early-stage fund; B2B software in the generative-AI era |
| Craft Ventures Growth II | 2023 | $608 million | Second growth fund; combined 2023 total $1.32 billion, AUM $3.3 billion |

The table's figures rest on [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/15/david-sackss-craft-ventures-just-closed-its-second-fund-with-500-million/) for Funds I and II and the firm's own announcements for 2021 and 2023, corroborated by independent reporting of the same numbers ([Craft Ventures](https://www.craftventures.com/articles/announcing-craft-iii-1-1-billion-for-saas-and-marketplaces); [Craft Ventures](https://www.craftventures.com/articles/announcing-1-3-billion-for-craft-ventures-iv-and-growth-ii); [TechStartups](https://techstartups.com/2026/08/11/david-sacks-craft-ventures-targets-1-billion-for-first-new-fund-since-leaving-white-house/)). Two figures in the wider coverage need care: some secondary sources state the 2021 total as $1.3 billion, but $612 million plus $510 million is $1.12 billion — $1.3 billion is the 2023 total, misattributed to 2021. And the debut fund is dated to 2017 in some sources and to its January 2018 announcement in others; this profile dates it to 2018.

The 2023 raise also changed the firm's shape. With $1.12 billion of AUM across its two growth funds, Craft hired seasoned growth investor Michael Robinson — formerly a lead investor at [Georgian](/organizations/georgian/), where he had led growth-equity investments in software companies — to run the growth practice, and opened a [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) office in the Flatiron District, noting that roughly 20 percent of its portfolio companies were based in New York or had a significant presence there ([Craft Ventures](https://www.craftventures.com/articles/announcing-1-3-billion-for-craft-ventures-iv-and-growth-ii)). The firm later also opened an office in [Austin, Texas](/places/austin-texas/) ([TechStartups](https://techstartups.com/2026/08/11/david-sacks-craft-ventures-targets-1-billion-for-first-new-fund-since-leaving-white-house/)). Its B2B focus even produced a spinout: SaaSGrid, a dashboarding tool the firm built to instrument SaaS businesses, became popular enough to spin out as a standalone company ([Craft Ventures](https://www.craftventures.com/articles/announcing-1-3-billion-for-craft-ventures-iv-and-growth-ii)).

## The portfolio

By January 2019 Craft had already invested in more than 20 companies, including Bird, SpaceX, Harbor, Reddit and Cloud9 ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2019/01/inside-the-mind-of-a-nyc-vc-bryan-rosenblatt-of-craft-ventures/)). The firm later said it had backed more than 30 unicorn companies at an early stage across its angel and fund investing, and named the unicorns in its first two funds as Addepar, Bird, Bitgo, ClickUp, Multicoin, Neuralink, Pipe, Reddit, Sourcegraph and SpaceX ([Craft Ventures](https://www.craftventures.com/articles/announcing-craft-iii-1-1-billion-for-saas-and-marketplaces)).

| Company | What is verified about Craft's involvement |
| --- | --- |
| Bird | Signature early bet; Craft led the $15 million Series A in February 2018. Bird later raised a $275 million Series D at a $2.5 billion valuation |
| ClickUp | Craft led the Series A in June 2020 and joined the $100 million Series B in December 2020, led by Georgian at a reported $1 billion valuation |
| SpaceX | Named among Craft's investments in 2019 and among its Fund I/II unicorns in 2021 |
| Reddit | Named among Craft's investments in 2019 and among its Fund I/II unicorns in 2021 |
| Cloud9 | Esports company; a Bill Lee focus, per TechCrunch's 2019 account |
| Trusted Health | Nursing marketplace; Series A was a Jeff Fluhr deal |
| Superplastic | Art-toy maker; Craft led its $10 million Series A in summer 2019 |
| Affirm | Angel-era investment by the partners, predating the firm — not a fund investment |

The table's rows rest on the sources cited in the surrounding text: Bird, Cloud9, Trusted Health and Superplastic on [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/15/david-sackss-craft-ventures-just-closed-its-second-fund-with-500-million/), ClickUp on [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2020/12/15/productivity-platform-startup-clickup-raises-100m-1b-unicorn-valuation/), SpaceX and Reddit on [AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2019/01/inside-the-mind-of-a-nyc-vc-bryan-rosenblatt-of-craft-ventures/) and the firm's 2021 announcement, and [Affirm](/organizations/affirm/) on [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/15/david-sackss-craft-ventures-just-closed-its-second-fund-with-500-million/) as an angel bet. The SpaceX row carries a caveat: some secondary sources describe Sacks' SpaceX stake as a personal angel investment predating the firm, so the firm-versus-personal attribution is flagged rather than settled here.

Bird shows how the firm operated at its best — and ClickUp shows how it compounded. Craft led Bird's $15 million Series A in February 2018, when the scooter startup was barely months old, and TechCrunch called the bet Sacks' signature investment at the firm ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/15/david-sackss-craft-ventures-just-closed-its-second-fund-with-500-million/)). With ClickUp, Craft led the productivity platform's first outside round in June 2020, then followed into the $100 million Series B six months later, led by Georgian, which valued the company at a reported $1 billion and brought its total raised to $135 million ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2020/12/15/productivity-platform-startup-clickup-raises-100m-1b-unicorn-valuation/)). ClickUp said it had grown revenue 900 percent in the prior year and served more than 200,000 teams, a surge tied to remote work during the [COVID-19 pandemic](/events/covid-19-pandemic/) ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2020/12/15/productivity-platform-startup-clickup-raises-100m-1b-unicorn-valuation/)). Not every early bet aged well: Harbor, the crypto-securities startup Craft announced alongside its own public launch, saw its founders leave to start Internal, which then raised a $5 million seed round led by Craft — the firm backing the founders' second act rather than the first ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/15/david-sackss-craft-ventures-just-closed-its-second-fund-with-500-million/)).

## The White House years — and Craft V

On December 5, 2024, President-elect Donald Trump named Sacks his "White House A.I. & Crypto Czar," saying Sacks would guide policy on artificial intelligence and cryptocurrency and work on a legal framework to give the crypto industry the clarity it had sought ([Inc / Reuters](https://www.inc.com/reuters/trump-appoints-ex-paypal-exec-david-sachs-as-ai-and-crypto-czar/91048792)). Sacks also chaired the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology. The appointment put the firm's holdings under scrutiny: White House ethics disclosures showed that Sacks and Craft divested more than $200 million in cryptocurrency-related positions before or around the start of his government tenure ([TechStartups](https://techstartups.com/2026/08/11/david-sacks-craft-ventures-targets-1-billion-for-first-new-fund-since-leaving-white-house/)).

The tenure was short by design. Sacks served as a special government employee, a status capped at 130 days of work in a 12-month period, and on March 26, 2026 he told Bloomberg Television he had reached the limit and was stepping down as czar, moving to co-chair the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology, where he could advise on a broader range of technology issues ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/white-house-ai-czar-sacks-step-down-moves-advisory-role-2026-03-27/)). Reuters noted at the time that he remained a partner at Craft Ventures, the firm he co-founded in 2017 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/white-house-ai-czar-sacks-step-down-moves-advisory-role-2026-03-27/)).

Five months later Craft was raising again. On August 7, 2026, Craft Ventures V, LP filed a Form D with the SEC — a Delaware limited partnership based at Craft's San Francisco address, with the offering amount listed as indefinite and no capital sold and no investors as of the filing date. The Information reported, citing the filing and people familiar with the effort, that the firm was seeking roughly $1 billion in a single combined vehicle — a departure from its paired early-stage/growth structure — which would push its total capital raised from limited partners past $4 billion ([TechStartups](https://techstartups.com/2026/08/11/david-sacks-craft-ventures-targets-1-billion-for-first-new-fund-since-leaving-white-house/)). The target is reported, not closed: the filing itself discloses no target, close date or strategy, and no final close had been reported as of this profile's publication.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Craft Ventures?** It is a San Francisco venture capital firm founded in 2017 by David Sacks and Bill Lee. It invests in B2B software — SaaS and marketplaces — from seed stage through growth rounds, and manages $3.3 billion across six funds ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/15/david-sackss-craft-ventures-just-closed-its-second-fund-with-500-million/); [Craft Ventures](https://www.craftventures.com/articles/announcing-1-3-billion-for-craft-ventures-iv-and-growth-ii)).

**Who founded Craft Ventures and what is its investment thesis?** Sacks (former PayPal COO and Yammer founder) and Lee (Remarq founder) founded it with Jeff Fluhr (StubHub founder) and Sky Dayton (EarthLink founder) as partners. The thesis is founders backing founders: partners invest in the sectors they once built companies in, and the firm supports portfolio companies with a full-time operating team ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2019/01/inside-the-mind-of-a-nyc-vc-bryan-rosenblatt-of-craft-ventures/); [Craft Ventures](https://www.craftventures.com/articles/announcing-craft-iii-1-1-billion-for-saas-and-marketplaces)).

**Which companies has Craft Ventures backed?** Its best-documented bets are Bird (led the $15 million Series A), ClickUp (led the Series A, joined the $100 million Series B), SpaceX and Reddit, plus Fund I/II unicorns including Addepar, Bitgo, Neuralink, Pipe and Sourcegraph. Affirm, Airbnb and Slack were angel investments by the partners before the firm existed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/15/david-sackss-craft-ventures-just-closed-its-second-fund-with-500-million/); [Craft Ventures](https://www.craftventures.com/articles/announcing-craft-iii-1-1-billion-for-saas-and-marketplaces)).

**What happened to Craft Ventures when David Sacks joined the White House?** Trump named Sacks White House AI and Crypto Czar in December 2024; Sacks and Craft divested more than $200 million in crypto-related holdings, and Sacks stepped down in March 2026 after hitting the 130-day special-government-employee limit, moving to co-chair the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology. In August 2026 Craft filed for a fifth fund with a reported target of roughly $1 billion ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/white-house-ai-czar-sacks-step-down-moves-advisory-role-2026-03-27/); [TechStartups](https://techstartups.com/2026/08/11/david-sacks-craft-ventures-targets-1-billion-for-first-new-fund-since-leaving-white-house/)).
