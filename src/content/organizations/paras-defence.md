# Paras Defence and Space Technologies Limited

## The gap: the Indian partner of a Trump-sons-backed drone company

Most stock-market coverage treats Paras Defence and Space Technologies as a rallying defence scrip. The verified-but-undercovered fact is its corporate role in 2026: on June 30, 2026, the company disclosed to India's stock exchanges that it had entered into an exclusive intellectual property licensing agreement with Tandem Defense LLC, a wholly owned subsidiary of Autonomous Power Corporation — better known as Powerus — for that company's Guardian-1 interceptor technology ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/23/pakistans-new-drone-deal-is-with-trump-backed-firm-also-selling-to-india); [Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/world/trump-s-son-linked-powerus-signed-india-anti-drone-deal-months-before-pakistan-deal-article-14034132.html)).

Powerus is the year-old United States drone company backed by two sons of President Donald Trump: [Donald Trump Jr.](/people/donald-trump-jr/) and Eric Trump are investors through a vehicle called American Ventures, which Al Jazeera reported was expected to hold a 9.9 percent beneficial stake in the combined company after Powerus's planned merger with Nasdaq-listed Aureus Greenway Holdings ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/23/pakistans-new-drone-deal-is-with-trump-backed-firm-also-selling-to-india)). Moneycontrol described the Trump brothers as "notable investors" when Powerus prepared to go public through the merger, and reported that Powerus representatives said the family investors were passive with no operational control ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/world/trump-s-son-linked-powerus-signed-india-anti-drone-deal-months-before-pakistan-deal-article-14034132.html)). Under the licence, Paras received exclusive, non-transferable, non-sublicensable rights to manufacture and commercialise the products in India, with an initial term of 12 months renewable by mutual consent; Powerus is to receive a licence fee plus a share of net profits from sales in India ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/world/trump-s-son-linked-powerus-signed-india-anti-drone-deal-months-before-pakistan-deal-article-14034132.html)). The Guardian-1 is a high-speed, battery-powered counter-drone system designed to combat low-cost aerial threats ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/world/trump-s-son-linked-powerus-signed-india-anti-drone-deal-months-before-pakistan-deal-article-14034132.html)).

The sequencing is what makes the deal notable. The India agreement predates by nearly three months the September 2026 arrangement between Powerus and Pakistan's military: on September 16 a Powerus delegation met Pakistan's army chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir, at Rawalpindi, followed by an announced memorandum of understanding with the [Pakistan Army](/organizations/pakistan-army/) and a "limited procurement order" from Pakistan's Ministry of Defence ([Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/world/trump-s-son-linked-powerus-signed-india-anti-drone-deal-months-before-pakistan-deal-article-14034132.html); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/23/pakistans-new-drone-deal-is-with-trump-backed-firm-also-selling-to-india)). Al Jazeera noted that Paras's India licence does not mean the Indian military has selected the system — any induction would still go through standard government evaluation, and no order had been placed ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/23/pakistans-new-drone-deal-is-with-trump-backed-firm-also-selling-to-india)).

## What the company makes

Paras Defence describes itself as one of the leading "Indigenously Designed, Developed and Manufactured" (IDDM) category private-sector companies in India, engaged in designing, developing, manufacturing, and testing defence and space engineering products ([Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/market-news/stocks/mtar-technologies-paras-defence-asta-microwave-and-others-witness-strong-fii-buying-in-q1-fy-27-shares-soar-up-to-180/article-198888/)). Its operations span two core verticals: Optics & Optronic Systems, and Defence Engineering — the latter covering defence electronics, electromagnetic pulse (EMP) protection solutions, and heavy engineering ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/paras-defence-zooms-163-from-march-low-hits-52-week-high-on-huge-volume-126081800661_1.html)). Broader financial coverage breaks the business into four segments: defence and space optics, defence electronics, heavy engineering, and EMP protection ([Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/market/stock-insights/indias-drone-revolution-why-paras-defence-and-zen-technologies-are-key-stocks-to-watch-in-2026/4150943/)).

One of its distinctive product claims is submarine hardware: the company is the only Indian company in the Asia-Pacific region developing and manufacturing optronic periscopes for submarine applications, per its own press material carried in the market coverage ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/paras-defence-zooms-163-from-march-low-hits-52-week-high-on-huge-volume-126081800661_1.html); [NSE](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/PARAS_06052025111657_PressRelease.pdf)). The Financial Express also credits it with developed counter-drone and anti-drone solutions, positioning it in India's emerging drone and aerial-defence ecosystem ([Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/market/stock-insights/indias-drone-revolution-why-paras-defence-and-zen-technologies-are-key-stocks-to-watch-in-2026/4150943/)). Its own NSE-filed press release claims an AS9100D-certified operation with more than 600 professionals, manufacturing facilities at Nerul and Ambernath in Maharashtra, R&D centres in Bengaluru, and offices in Hyderabad, Chennai, and Delhi ([NSE](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/PARAS_06052025111657_PressRelease.pdf)). That same release lists its claimed marquee clients as DRDO, ISRO, Bharat Electronics, Larsen & Toubro, the Ordnance Factories, and the Ministry of Defence, and claims exports to Israel, Europe, the USA, and South Korea ([NSE](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/PARAS_06052025111657_PressRelease.pdf)).

## From copper trading to defence engineering

The corporate history is more layered than the "40+ years" framing in the company's own material suggests. According to credit-rating agency CARE Ratings, the company was promoted by Sharad Shah and incorporated on June 16, 2009 — initially as Paras Flowform Engineering Limited, engaged mainly in trading copper, copper alloys, and printed circuit boards ([CARE Ratings](https://www.careratings.com/upload/CompanyFiles/PR/Paras%20Defence%20and%20Space%20Technologies%20Limited-07-10-2019.pdf)). It was only from FY15 that the company became purely engaged in manufacturing components for the domestic defence and aeronautical sectors, using the flow-forming process, with its manufacturing unit at Ambernath, Thane ([CARE Ratings](https://www.careratings.com/upload/CompanyFiles/PR/Paras%20Defence%20and%20Space%20Technologies%20Limited-07-10-2019.pdf)). It was renamed Paras Defence and Space Technologies Limited with effect from January 29, 2016, and amalgamated its subsidiary Mechvac India Limited and associate Concept Shapers and Electronics Private Limited ([CARE Ratings](https://www.careratings.com/upload/CompanyFiles/PR/Paras%20Defence%20and%20Space%20Technologies%20Limited-07-10-2019.pdf)). The company went public in September 2021 with an IPO of about ₹171 crore at a price band of ₹165–175, listing on both the NSE and BSE.

## A 2026 stock-market darling — and an expanding footprint

In 2026 the company became a favourite of foreign institutional investors. For the quarter ending June 2026, sales rose 35 percent to ₹118 crore (from ₹87.4 crore a year earlier), while net profit surged more than 38 percent to ₹21.4 crore ([Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/market-news/stocks/mtar-technologies-paras-defence-asta-microwave-and-others-witness-strong-fii-buying-in-q1-fy-27-shares-soar-up-to-180/article-198888/)). FIIs raised their stake from 5.06 percent in the quarter ending March 2026 to 8.29 percent in the quarter ending June 2026, propelling the stock from ₹593 in April to ₹1,294 by the end of June, and on to a fresh record high of ₹1,577 on August 19, 2026 ([Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/market-news/stocks/mtar-technologies-paras-defence-asta-microwave-and-others-witness-strong-fii-buying-in-q1-fy-27-shares-soar-up-to-180/article-198888/)). Business Standard reported a 52-week high of ₹1,527.65 on August 18, a 163 percent rebound from the ₹580 low of March 23, 2026, in the same stretch ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/paras-defence-zooms-163-from-march-low-hits-52-week-high-on-huge-volume-126081800661_1.html)).

The company is also moving up the value chain. It incorporated a new subsidiary, Paras Avionics Private Limited, for avionics systems — manufacturing, testing, repair, and overhauling for defence and aerospace platforms ([Financial Express](https://www.financialexpress.com/market/stock-insights/indias-drone-revolution-why-paras-defence-and-zen-technologies-are-key-stocks-to-watch-in-2026/4150943/)). And on July 22, 2026, Paras Semiconductors signed an MoU with the Government of Madhya Pradesh to establish an advanced semiconductor packaging Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test (OSAT) facility in the state, backed by a proposed investment of ₹6,200 crore ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/paras-defence-zooms-163-from-march-low-hits-52-week-high-on-huge-volume-126081800661_1.html)). The backdrop is a massive strategic push in India's Union Budget FY2026–27: a total defence allocation of ₹7.85 trillion, with more than ₹2.19 trillion for capital acquisition and roughly 75 percent of that — about ₹1.39 trillion — reserved for procurement from domestic defence industries ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/paras-defence-zooms-163-from-march-low-hits-52-week-high-on-huge-volume-126081800661_1.html)).
