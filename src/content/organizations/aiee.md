## Overview

The American Institute of Electrical Engineers (AIEE) was the establishment — the institute of the wire age, founded May 13, 1884, in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/), whose interests were "wire communications and light and power systems" and which "laid the foundations for all work on electrical standards done in the [United States](/places/united-states/)" ([ETHW](https://ethw.org/IEEE_Philadelphia_Section_History)). For half a century it defined the electrical profession. Then radio arrived, the upstart Institute of Radio Engineers outgrew it, electronics blurred every boundary between them — and on January 1, 1963, the two merged as the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers ([IEEE](/organizations/ieee/)). The AIEE name vanished; its lineage did not. It was also one of the seven societies that founded the Engineers' Council for Professional Development, now [ABET](/organizations/abet/), in 1932 ([ABET](https://www.abet.org/about-abet/history/)).

## The 1884 founding: "an organization to define a new profession"

By 1884, "applications for electricity were rapidly increasing, progress in electrical theory and practice was accelerating," and practitioners "began to feel the need for a national forum to exchange ideas and experiences and an organization to define a new profession" ([ETHW](https://ethw.org/IEEE_Philadelphia_Section_History)). That spring a call went out for a meeting to form a national electrical society; after preliminary gatherings, the AIEE was established in New York City on May 13. The impetus came from Philadelphia: the Franklin Institute's International Electrical Exhibition, planned for that fall, and the National Conference of Electricians, which "emphasized the need for a national organization to represent the 'practical men'" — among them Elihu Thomson, Edwin Houston, and Thomas Edison. The standard histories add the most famous names to the founder roll — Nikola Tesla, Edison, Thomson, Houston, Edward Weston — with Western Union president Norvin Green as first president.

The institute's early decades were organizational: a permanent headquarters, contact with a far-flung membership, student outreach, and technical committees for the field's increasing specialization. Standardization began in 1891 with a committee on units and standards; local sections were authorized in 1902, first in Chicago and Ithaca, with Toronto (1903) the first outside the United States. In 1904 the AIEE joined [AIME](/organizations/aime/) and the [American Society of Mechanical Engineers](/organizations/asme/) to found the United Engineering Society ([JPT](https://jpt.spe.org/aime-spes-parent-organization)).

## The wire–radio rivalry

The AIEE's challenger was born May 13, 1912 — exactly 28 years after the AIEE — when two local wireless societies merged to form the Institute of Radio Engineers (IRE). The IRE "grew so rapidly its membership soon surpassed that of the AIEE" ([ETHW](https://ethw.org/IEEE_Philadelphia_Section_History)). In the 1930s the word "electronics" entered the vocabulary; electronics engineers tended to join the IRE, but electron-tube applications spread so widely that "the technical boundaries differentiating the IRE and the AIEE became difficult to distinguish." After World War II the two became "increasingly competitive," with overlap and duplication only partly managed by joint committees.

The profession had seen it coming for decades: as early as 1922 the two organizations discussed merging. "No serious steps were taken until 1961," when both leaderships resolved to end the difficulties through consolidation; the merger plan was approved in 1962 and took effect on January 1, 1963. The new name kept both lineages visible: the Institute of Electrical *and Electronics* Engineers.

## Frequently asked questions

**When was the AIEE founded?**
May 13, 1884, in New York City, after a spring call for a national electrical society. The Franklin Institute's 1884 International Electrical Exhibition in Philadelphia supplied the impetus.

**What happened to the AIEE?**
It merged with the Institute of Radio Engineers (IRE) on January 1, 1963, forming the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE). The AIEE name was retired; its members, standards work, and sections continued inside IEEE.

**Why did the AIEE and IRE merge?**
By the 1930s electronics had blurred the line between the AIEE's wire-and-power world and the IRE's radio world; after WWII the two competed directly with overlapping efforts. Merger talks began as early as 1922 but turned serious only in 1961.

**What was the AIEE's connection to ABET?**
The AIEE was one of seven engineering societies that founded ABET's predecessor — the Engineers' Council for Professional Development — in New York City in 1932.

**Who were the AIEE's notable early figures?**
The standard histories name Tesla, Edison, Elihu Thomson, Edwin Houston, and Edward Weston among the founders, with Western Union president Norvin Green as first president; IEEE's own history emphasizes Thomson, Houston, and Edison as the "practical men" behind the founding.
