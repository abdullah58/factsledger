## A city and a county that became one government

National coverage of the September 2026 ruling put Nashville in a plaintiff list — one city name among jurisdictions in three states ([Associated Press (via 990theanswer)](https://990theanswer.com/news/national/trump-cant-force-election-changes-by-blocking-counterterrorism-funds-to-states-j/ede327cf2d9d71869dd82cc9536b4a4c)). The name on the case caption, though, describes something distinctive: the Metropolitan Government of Nashville and Davidson County, Tennessee — a single government in place of both a city and a county ([Nashville Banner](https://nashvillebanner.com/2026/09/29/nashville-symphony-counterterrorism-funds-christa-pike/)).

The Metropolitan Nashville-Davidson County government was organized in 1963, and when the city and county merged that year they became, in the Tennessee Encyclopedia's telling, the first consolidated city/county government in the United States ([Tennessee Encyclopedia (Tennessee Historical Society)](https://tennesseeencyclopedia.net/entries/nashville-metropolitan-nashville-davidson-county/)). No other plaintiff in the 2026 litigation carried a story like that: Harris and El Paso counties sued as counties, Columbus as a city, and Nashville–Davidson as a government that had dissolved the line between the two six decades earlier ([Associated Press (via 990theanswer)](https://990theanswer.com/news/national/trump-cant-force-election-changes-by-blocking-counterterrorism-funds-to-states-j/ede327cf2d9d71869dd82cc9536b4a4c)).

## The counterterrorism-grant lawsuit

The suit was *Metropolitan Government of Nashville and Davidson County, Tennessee, et al. v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security, et al.*, filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia and heard by Judge Amir H. Ali ([Democracy Docket](https://www.democracydocket.com/cases/fema-national-security-grant-conditional-funding-challenge/)). The complaint was filed on August 17, 2026 ([Democracy Docket](https://www.democracydocket.com/cases/fema-national-security-grant-conditional-funding-challenge/)).

The original plaintiffs were Nashville and Davidson County, Tennessee; Harris County and El Paso County, Texas; and Columbus, Ohio — joined later by Dallas County and Travis County, Texas ([Associated Press (via 990theanswer)](https://990theanswer.com/news/national/trump-cant-force-election-changes-by-blocking-counterterrorism-funds-to-states-j/ede327cf2d9d71869dd82cc9536b4a4c)).

Their target was a set of five election-related conditions that the Federal Emergency Management Agency attached to fiscal year 2026 funding under the Homeland Security Grant Program in its June 2026 Notice of Funding Opportunity ([Nashville Banner](https://nashvillebanner.com/2026/09/29/nashville-symphony-counterterrorism-funds-christa-pike/)). [FEMA](https://factsledger.com/organizations/fema/) said it would withhold 20% of grant funds for states and urban areas until they implemented the changes ([Nashville Banner](https://nashvillebanner.com/2026/09/29/nashville-symphony-counterterrorism-funds-christa-pike/)). The [Associated Press](https://factsledger.com/organizations/associated-press/) described the summer announcement the same way: 20% of grants for states and urban areas withheld pending compliance ([Associated Press (via 990theanswer)](https://990theanswer.com/news/national/trump-cant-force-election-changes-by-blocking-counterterrorism-funds-to-states-j/ede327cf2d9d71869dd82cc9536b4a4c)).

The conditions touched the mechanics of voting itself. According to the Nashville Banner, they included an analog audit of paper ballots and required use of the Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements (SAVE) system to check the citizenship status of voters and poll workers ([Nashville Banner](https://nashvillebanner.com/2026/09/29/nashville-symphony-counterterrorism-funds-christa-pike/)). The Associated Press described the list as covering citizenship verification of voters and election workers, hand-marked paper ballots, and audits ([Associated Press (via 990theanswer)](https://990theanswer.com/news/national/trump-cant-force-election-changes-by-blocking-counterterrorism-funds-to-states-j/ede327cf2d9d71869dd82cc9536b4a4c)). The local governments argued that Congress never conditioned counterterrorism money on how localities run elections ([Democracy Docket](https://www.democracydocket.com/cases/fema-national-security-grant-conditional-funding-challenge/)).

## The ruling

On September 28, 2026, Judge Ali granted summary judgment for the local governments and vacated the policy ([Democracy Docket](https://www.democracydocket.com/cases/fema-national-security-grant-conditional-funding-challenge/)). In a 24-page opinion, Ali wrote that federal agencies lacked the authority from Congress to add the conditions to the grants ([Associated Press (via 990theanswer)](https://990theanswer.com/news/national/trump-cant-force-election-changes-by-blocking-counterterrorism-funds-to-states-j/ede327cf2d9d71869dd82cc9536b4a4c)). As the Nashville Banner reported it: "FEMA's conditions here…go beyond what Congress authorized" ([Nashville Banner](https://nashvillebanner.com/2026/09/29/nashville-symphony-counterterrorism-funds-christa-pike/)).

The reaction from Nashville's legal team was immediate. Tyler Chance Yarbro, the director of law for Nashville and Davidson County, said after the ruling: "The end result is exactly what we wanted" ([Associated Press (via 990theanswer)](https://990theanswer.com/news/national/trump-cant-force-election-changes-by-blocking-counterterrorism-funds-to-states-j/ede327cf2d9d71869dd82cc9536b4a4c)). Local coverage framed it as funding restored — the federal government could not withhold counterterrorism money from states until they changed how they administer elections ([Nashville Banner](https://nashvillebanner.com/2026/09/29/nashville-symphony-counterterrorism-funds-christa-pike/)).

The Metro government's suit was not the only challenge. Twenty-five state attorneys general or governors filed a similar lawsuit, according to the Associated Press ([Associated Press (via 990theanswer)](https://990theanswer.com/news/national/trump-cant-force-election-changes-by-blocking-counterterrorism-funds-to-states-j/ede327cf2d9d71869dd82cc9536b4a4c)).

## A consolidated plaintiff

There is a symmetry in a consolidated government leading this particular challenge. The Homeland Security Grant Program allocates money according to each jurisdiction's vulnerability to terrorist attack ([Democracy Docket](https://www.democracydocket.com/cases/fema-national-security-grant-conditional-funding-challenge/)). A government formed by merging a city and a county — one office, one budget, one legal identity speaking for both — could put its full name on the caption of a suit defending the boundary between counterterrorism funding and election administration ([Nashville Banner](https://nashvillebanner.com/2026/09/29/nashville-symphony-counterterrorism-funds-christa-pike/)). The ruling answered a question of authority: Ali held that FEMA had gone beyond what Congress authorized, and that FEMA never explained how the new conditions were tied to shoring up vulnerabilities to terrorist attacks ([Nashville Banner](https://nashvillebanner.com/2026/09/29/nashville-symphony-counterterrorism-funds-christa-pike/)).
