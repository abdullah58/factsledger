## Overview

BYD Company Limited is a Chinese manufacturer founded in Shenzhen in February 1995 by Wang Chuanfu as a maker of rechargeable batteries, which grew into the world's largest maker of electric vehicles ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/BYD-Co-Ltd)). Its name stands for "Build Your Dreams", and its path was the reverse of almost every other carmaker's: it started with the battery, bought a small car factory in 2002, and built the car business around the cell ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/BYD-Co-Ltd)). In 2024 it reported revenue of 777.1 billion yuan ($107.2 billion), passing Tesla's $97.7 billion for the first time, and sold nearly 4.3 million vehicles ([AFP](https://techxplore.com/news/2025-03-chinese-ev-giant-byd-surpasses.html)).

## A battery company that bought a carmaker

BYD's early batteries were copies of Sony and Sanyo products, analysed and improved, before the company added nickel–metal hydride types and eventually became [China](/places/china/)'s largest producer of rechargeable batteries ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/BYD-Co-Ltd)). In 2002 Wang Chuanfu bought Qinchuan Automobile, a small car factory in Shaanxi, along with a mould factory in Beijing and a research facility in Shanghai, aiming to build a carmaker that kept everything inside its own corporate sphere ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/BYD-Co-Ltd)). BYD Auto was founded in 2003 as a subsidiary, placing its emphasis on electric and hybrid vehicles ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/BYD-Co-Ltd)).

The first mass-produced car was the Flyer, developed by Qinchuan, but the breakthrough was the F3, the first car BYD developed in-house, which debuted in 2005 and sold more than one million in China ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/BYD-Co-Ltd)). The F3DM plug-in hybrid version followed, then the e6 in 2010, the Qin plug-in hybrid in 2012, the Tang SUV in 2015 and the Han sedan in 2020 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/BYD-Co-Ltd)). In 2008 the company received a financial boost when Berkshire Hathaway, owned by Warren Buffett, acquired a 10% stake for $230 million ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/BYD-Co-Ltd)).

## Vertical integration: the moat

BYD's advantage is that it makes almost everything itself. It designs and builds its own batteries, motors, electronics and semiconductors, and in 2020 it formalised that model by creating FinDreams, a subgroup that sells EV components — batteries, powertrains, electronics, vision systems and moulding — to other manufacturers ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/BYD-Co-Ltd)). The centrepiece is the Blade battery, a lithium iron phosphate design debuted in 2020 with improved safety and greater energy density, which in 2024 BorgWarner agreed to use under licence to make battery packs in Europe and elsewhere ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/BYD-Co-Ltd)). BYD Semiconductor, founded in October 2004 with BYD retaining a 72.3% stake, extends the same logic to chips ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/BYD-Co-Ltd)).

That integration is what let BYD make the industry's boldest break with petrol. In March 2022 it stopped production of automobiles powered only by gasoline, with the final model rolling off the line that month, and turned its focus to battery-electric vehicles and plug-in hybrids, while continuing to supply parts and after-sales support to existing petrol-car owners ([BYD](https://en.byd.com/news/byd-discontinues-gas-only-auto-line-to-focus-on-phev-and-pure-electric-tech/)). No other major carmaker had done that. The bet paid off in volume: BYD had already surpassed Tesla in total electrified-vehicle sales (battery-electric plus plug-in hybrid) in 2022, and Britannica records it as the world's largest seller of battery-electric vehicles in 2025 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/BYD-Co-Ltd)).

## 2024: the Tesla crossover

BYD's 2024 results, published in a Shenzhen stock exchange filing in March 2025, mark the moment the challenger passed the incumbent on the scoreboard that matters most to investors. Revenue rose 29% to 777.1 billion yuan ($107.2 billion), beating a Bloomberg forecast of 766 billion yuan and eclipsing Tesla's $97.7 billion, while net profit reached a record 40.3 billion yuan, up 34% ([AFP](https://techxplore.com/news/2025-03-chinese-ev-giant-byd-surpasses.html)). Vehicle sales rose more than 40% to nearly 4.3 million ([AFP](https://techxplore.com/news/2025-03-chinese-ev-giant-byd-surpasses.html)). The comparison needs one precision: 2024 was a revenue crossover and a total-electrified-sales crossover, and BYD became the largest seller of pure battery-electric vehicles in 2025, per Britannica — the profile does not claim BYD outsold Tesla on pure battery-electrics in 2024 itself ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/BYD-Co-Ltd)).

The next technology claim came in March 2025, when BYD unveiled its Super e-Platform battery and charging system, with peak speeds of 1,000 kilowatts and up to 470 kilometres of range after a five-minute charge — against 500 kilowatts for Tesla's Superchargers ([AFP](https://techxplore.com/news/2025-03-chinese-ev-giant-byd-surpasses.html)). The expansion is not unopposed: EU authorities were reported to be investigating whether the Chinese government provided unfair subsidies for BYD's first European factory, in Hungary, and the US has imposed higher blanket tariffs on Chinese imports ([AFP](https://techxplore.com/news/2025-03-chinese-ev-giant-byd-surpasses.html)).

## Beyond cars: buses, rail and chips

BYD is more than a carmaker. It spent 5 billion yuan over five years developing SkyRail, a monorail system for small and midsize cities, and in 2021 Salvador, Brazil selected SkyRail for an urban transit project ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/BYD-Co-Ltd)). Its electric buses include the C10MS, which in 2022 became the first electric double-decker bus to pass durability road testing under US Federal Transit Administration guidelines ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/BYD-Co-Ltd)). The company describes its operations as spanning more than 70 countries and regions, across autos, electronics, new energy and rail transit ([BYD](https://en.byd.com/news/byd-discontinues-gas-only-auto-line-to-focus-on-phev-and-pure-electric-tech/)).

## The Cerence thread: an LLM assistant for global buyers

BYD's link to [Cerence](/organizations/cerence/) is the newest of the four profiles in this batch, and the most forward-looking. On 8 April 2026 Cerence announced an expansion of its longstanding partnership with BYD — the two companies "have partnered for years across BYD's innovative vehicle portfolio" — so that Cerence xUI will power BYD's conversational in-car AI assistant for global customers ([Cerence Inc.](https://stockhouse.com/news/press-releases/2026/04/08/cerence-ai-to-power-intelligent-llm-powered-in-car-experiences-for-byd)). The assistant, powered by CaLLM, Cerence's family of automotive-optimised language models, was to go live in spring 2026 in the ATTO 2 DM-i, followed by other global models, with multi-step conversations and real-time information across regions and languages ([Cerence Inc.](https://stockhouse.com/news/press-releases/2026/04/08/cerence-ai-to-power-intelligent-llm-powered-in-car-experiences-for-byd)). Cerence said more than 525 million cars have shipped with its technology ([Cerence Inc.](https://stockhouse.com/news/press-releases/2026/04/08/cerence-ai-to-power-intelligent-llm-powered-in-car-experiences-for-byd)). For BYD, whose growth now depends on buyers outside China, a single assistant that adapts to each market's language and habits is a genuine piece of infrastructure.
