## Overview

Eventbrite is a [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) self-service ticketing and live-events marketplace founded in 2006 by Kevin Hartz, Julia Hartz and Renaud Visage, which listed on the New York Stock Exchange in September 2018 at a $1.76 billion valuation and was taken private by Milan-based [Bending Spoons](/organizations/bending-spoons/) in March 2026 for approximately $500 million after years of flat revenue ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2018/09/20/eventbrite-proves-just-the-ticket-in-230m-ipo/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/05/what-is-bending-spoons-everything-to-know-about-aols-acquirer/)). Its arc — a tool built for small organizers, a hot IPO, a pandemic collapse, then a cut-price sale to a serial acquirer — is the clearest single-company version of the whole 2018–2026 software cycle.

## A self-service bet on smaller events

Eventbrite's founding idea was to let anyone create, promote and sell tickets to an event of any size, rather than serving only large venues through established ticketing firms ([FounderBrew](https://www.founderbrew.com/stories/eventbrite-co-founders-discuss-walking-away-after-two-decades)). The company charged organisers per ticket sold, and by 2011 it was generating ticket sales in more than 170 countries ([FounderBrew](https://www.founderbrew.com/stories/eventbrite-co-founders-discuss-walking-away-after-two-decades); [Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2018/09/20/eventbrite-proves-just-the-ticket-in-230m-ipo/)). In 2016 Julia Hartz became sole chief executive as Kevin Hartz moved to executive chair, a leadership change that would carry the company through its public years ([FounderBrew](https://www.founderbrew.com/stories/eventbrite-co-founders-discuss-walking-away-after-two-decades)).

Music became the expansion route. Eventbrite acquired European ticketing firm Ticketscript early in 2017, then agreed in June 2017 to buy Ticketfly from Pandora for $200 million, completing the deal that September, with Ticketfly chief Andrew Dreskin taking charge of Eventbrite's music business ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/2017/09/04/eventbrite-completes-acquisition-ticketfly/)). The three platforms together processed nearly 15 million music tickets in the first half of 2017 — yet that was only around 15% of Eventbrite's overall volume, a reminder that its core was always the long tail of smaller events, not the concert industry alone ([Music Ally](https://musically.com/2017/09/04/eventbrite-completes-acquisition-ticketfly/)).

The IPO priced that story richly. Eventbrite sold 10 million shares at $23 each in September 2018, raising $230 million at a $1.76 billion valuation, after having raised about $359 million privately from backers including Tiger Global, Sequoia Capital and T. Rowe Price ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2018/09/20/eventbrite-proves-just-the-ticket-in-230m-ipo/)). Shares jumped to about $37 on debut, briefly valuing the company near $3 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/09/20/eventbrites-ipo-should-encourage-tech-companies-to-get-out-while-they-still-can/amp/)). But the filing showed a company that had never made money: revenue rose from $133 million in 2016 to $201 million in 2017, while losses ran at $40.4 million and then $38.5 million, with a further $15.6 million net loss in the first half of 2018 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/09/20/eventbrites-ipo-should-encourage-tech-companies-to-get-out-while-they-still-can/amp/)). In 2017 it had sold some 203 million tickets; growth, not profit, was what investors were buying ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2018/09/20/eventbrite-proves-just-the-ticket-in-230m-ipo/)).

## The pandemic shock and the flat years

COVID-19 removed the product itself: live gatherings stopped. In the first quarter of 2020 Eventbrite's net revenue fell 39% year over year, from $81.3 million to $49.1 million, and its adjusted EBITDA loss reached $119.6 million, including $113.7 million in charges and reserve increases tied to the pandemic ([TheTicketingBusiness](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2020/05/eventbrite-secures-225m-financing-reports-major-q1-losses/)). Paid tickets fell 82% in May 2020 alone ([FounderBrew](https://www.founderbrew.com/stories/eventbrite-co-founders-discuss-walking-away-after-two-decades)). Survival came from financing rather than trading: a $225 million deal with Francisco Partners, structured as a $125 million term loan with an option for $100 million more ([TheTicketingBusiness](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2020/05/eventbrite-secures-225m-financing-reports-major-q1-losses/)). The one growth line was online events, up more than 2,000% year over year in April 2020 ([TheTicketingBusiness](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2020/05/eventbrite-secures-225m-financing-reports-major-q1-losses/)).

Live events returned, but Eventbrite's growth did not. Audited annual revenue was flat at around $325 million in both 2023 and 2024, with trailing twelve-month revenue of about $295 million at the time of the sale ([The Tech Buzz](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/bending-spoons-buys-eventbrite-for-500m-70-below-ipo-peak?utm_source=www.techbuzz.ai&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=open-deal-wyde-s-eat-causecoin&_bhlid=af05b7f4a2d168404ecfba9ef628cd6d7d20813f)). That flat line is the key number in this profile: a company that had raised roughly $330 million privately and listed at $1.76 billion was, eight years later, a no-growth business with a loyal creator base and a share price to match — $2.48 the Friday before the deal ([The Tech Buzz](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/bending-spoons-buys-eventbrite-for-500m-70-below-ipo-peak?utm_source=www.techbuzz.ai&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=open-deal-wyde-s-eat-causecoin&_bhlid=af05b7f4a2d168404ecfba9ef628cd6d7d20813f)).

## Sold for $500 million: the Bending Spoons ending

On December 2, 2025, Eventbrite agreed to be acquired by Bending Spoons in an all-cash transaction valued at approximately $500 million, with shareholders to receive $4.50 per share — an 83% premium over the 60-day volume-weighted average price at November 28, 2025, according to the company's SEC filing ([SEC filing](https://www.otcmarkets.com/filing/html?id=18977711&guid=4wt-kn4eJqORBih)). The filing said the company would become a private company fully owned by Bending Spoons, would keep operating under its own brand, and that Julia Hartz would remain chief executive through the transition, with no layoff decisions made at signing ([SEC filing](https://www.otcmarkets.com/filing/html?id=18977711&guid=4wt-kn4eJqORBih)). At roughly 1.7 times trailing revenue, the price was a bargain by software standards and a verdict on eight flat years: $4.50 a share against the $23 IPO price ([The Tech Buzz](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/bending-spoons-buys-eventbrite-for-500m-70-below-ipo-peak?utm_source=www.techbuzz.ai&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=open-deal-wyde-s-eat-causecoin&_bhlid=af05b7f4a2d168404ecfba9ef628cd6d7d20813f)).

The deal closed on March 10, 2026, taking Eventbrite private and off the New York Stock Exchange ([Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/bending-spoons-cuts-eventbrite-staff-rolls-out-product/)). What followed matched Bending Spoons' established pattern — buy a popular brand, hold it, and remake it through technology, pricing and headcount changes ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/05/what-is-bending-spoons-everything-to-know-about-aols-acquirer/)). Within weeks, the new owner had cut a large portion of Eventbrite's pre-acquisition workforce, offering laid-off staff what it called a substantial separation package, under new lead Andrea Parodi ([Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/bending-spoons-cuts-eventbrite-staff-rolls-out-product/)). Product work accelerated at the same time: faster event creation, redesigned creator profile pages, higher-resolution event images and clearer confirmation emails, with offline ticket access, check-in windows and multi-slot scanning for overlapping sessions to follow ([Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/bending-spoons-cuts-eventbrite-staff-rolls-out-product/)). Bending Spoons itself said the AOL, Eventbrite and Vimeo acquisitions had added 1,830 full-time-equivalent staff, but that only a few hundred were expected to remain once the transformations were substantially complete later in 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/05/what-is-bending-spoons-everything-to-know-about-aols-acquirer/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not state a single deal premium without qualification: the SEC filing's 83% (60-day average at November 28, 2025) differs from other coverage's 81% (Friday close) and 82% (60-day average at December 1), because the reference dates and measures differ. It does not assert the $505 million prospectus consideration figure, using instead the approximately $500 million deal-announcement value. It does not give a number for the post-acquisition workforce cut, which Reuters reports only as "a large portion," and it does not cover Ticketfly's 2018 security breach, which was not re-verified from a source read in full in this pass. It makes no claim about Eventbrite's product roadmap beyond the updates Bending Spoons had already announced by April 2026, and it omits the founders' personal lives, on which sources conflict.
