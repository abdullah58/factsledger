## Overview

citybiz is a privately held digital business-news network based in [Baltimore](/places/baltimore/), Maryland, that covers local business news — companies, executives, deals and commercial real estate — across 20 major city markets in the [United States](/places/united-states/) ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/citybizco); [Muck Rack](https://muckrack.com/media-outlet/citybiz)). Founded in 2004 by publisher Edwin Warfield as Citybizlist, it pairs original reporting and long-form CEO interviews with a paid channel through which companies publish their own announcements ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/409493/bill-atkinson-interviews-jay-nwachu-president-ceo-of-innovation-works-2-3-2-3/)).

The publisher Edwin Warfield is not the Edwin Warfield who was governor of Maryland in 1904–1908 ([Maryland State Archives](http://2020mdmanual.msa.maryland.gov/msa/mdmanual/01glance/chron/html/chron19.html)). The modern Warfield is a later-generation member of the same Maryland family — his father was Adjutant General of Maryland — and he has spent his whole career in Baltimore business publishing ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/409493/bill-atkinson-interviews-jay-nwachu-president-ceo-of-innovation-works-2-3-2-3/)). citybiz is also a digital-only operation with no print edition: unlike the weekly business journals, it runs a 24-hour, five-day virtual news operation with no legacy print deadlines ([Talking Biz News](http://talkingbiznews.com/1/citybizlist-com-tackles-business-news-on-the-web/)).

## Warfield before citybiz: The Daily Record and the Local Business Network

Warfield's career in local business publishing began in print. He was publisher of Baltimore's *The Daily Record* from 1983 to 1994, turning what he describes as a moribund public-notice newspaper into a local daily business newspaper, and he launched an award-winning business magazine, *Warfields* ([Talking Biz News](http://talkingbiznews.com/1/citybizlist-com-tackles-business-news-on-the-web/); [citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/409493/bill-atkinson-interviews-jay-nwachu-president-ceo-of-innovation-works-2-3-2-3/)). In 1990 he orchestrated a $2 million buyout of the paper's shareholders, with investment-banking advice from Veronis, Suhler & Associates and financing from Maryland National Bank, and in 1994 he sold *The Daily Record* to Dolan Media ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/409493/bill-atkinson-interviews-jay-nwachu-president-ceo-of-innovation-works-2-3-2-3/)).

Warfield was early to the idea that business news belonged online — by his own account, a little too early. In 1994 he moved to Florida and launched his first local business news website, ReviewNet, which was rebranded as The Local Business Network ([Talking Biz News](http://talkingbiznews.com/1/citybizlist-com-tackles-business-news-on-the-web/)). From 1994 to 2002 he ran that company, which raised over $20 million from Bruce Wasserstein's private equity firm U.S. Equity Partners, Times Mirror, RS Investments and Rustic Canyon Partners, and built partnerships with Cox Communications, Tribune, Digital Cities, City Search, Hoovers, The Deal, Bankrate and Netscape ([Talking Biz News](http://talkingbiznews.com/1/citybizlist-com-tackles-business-news-on-the-web/)). The company merged with True Advantage in 2001 before its divestiture and restructuring in 2002 ([Talking Biz News](http://talkingbiznews.com/1/citybizlist-com-tackles-business-news-on-the-web/)). The pattern of that first venture — a virtual newsroom, strategic content, technology and distribution partnerships rather than a printing press — became the template for citybiz ([Talking Biz News](http://talkingbiznews.com/1/citybizlist-com-tackles-business-news-on-the-web/)).

## Founding, funding and growth from 11 to 20 markets

Warfield founded citybiz in 2004 after returning to Baltimore, as a Web 2.0 local business publication built on a virtual newsroom ([Talking Biz News](http://talkingbiznews.com/1/citybizlist-com-tackles-business-news-on-the-web/); [citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/409493/bill-atkinson-interviews-jay-nwachu-president-ceo-of-innovation-works-2-3-2-3/)). He and his wife bootstrapped it with a second mortgage on their house until the first angel round in October 2009; in 2009 and 2010 the company raised $650,000 from friends and users of the site ([Talking Biz News](http://talkingbiznews.com/1/citybizlist-com-tackles-business-news-on-the-web/)). A further round followed: the Fells Point-based CityBizList, Inc. completed a $150,000 funding round on August 14, 2012 ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/startups/startup-md-pitch-to-win-closes-friday-reed-street-productions-beats-buzzfeed-ed-chalfin-on-angel-investing-startup-roundup/)). Those two rounds — $650,000 and $150,000 — are the only fundraising figures verified from sources read in full for this profile.

The network's footprint roughly doubled over its second decade. In March 2011, when Talking Biz News profiled it, Citybizlist ran 11 city sites — Atlanta, Baltimore, Boston, Charlotte/Raleigh, Dallas, Houston, New York, Pittsburgh, Philadelphia, South Florida and Washington, D.C. — drawing more than 200,000 unique visitors a month with a staff of 15: six editors, four reporters and five curators or editorial assistants ([Talking Biz News](http://talkingbiznews.com/1/citybizlist-com-tackles-business-news-on-the-web/)). By May 2023 the company described itself as operating in 20 cities, adding Detroit, Chicago, Indianapolis, Denver, Phoenix, San Diego, Los Angeles, San Francisco, Portland and Seattle to the original eastern footprint ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/409493/bill-atkinson-interviews-jay-nwachu-president-ceo-of-innovation-works-2-3-2-3/)).

## The model: a local Bloomberg with a paid publishing channel

Warfield's pitch for the network, given to Talking Biz News in 2011, was that citybiz would be "a local Bloomberg" — covering companies, their people and deals, and helping readers connect with others passionate about their professional lives — at a moment when daily newspapers' business sections were, in his words, lackluster, and the weekly business journals still ran on print deadlines while citybiz ran a 24-hour, five-day virtual news operation ([Talking Biz News](http://talkingbiznews.com/1/citybizlist-com-tackles-business-news-on-the-web/)). The editorial bet was on local public- and private-company news that other outlets were not covering, delivered with a growing social component ([Talking Biz News](http://talkingbiznews.com/1/citybizlist-com-tackles-business-news-on-the-web/)).

The second half of the model is paid publishing. citybiz gives organizations and their communications agencies a direct channel for publishing company news, executive interviews and thought-leadership content, combining local market reach with national digital distribution, search visibility, email newsletters and LinkedIn promotion ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/citybizco)). Its 2026 publishing pages make the offer concrete: companies with a finished announcement can submit it directly, companies that need help can use a self-service article creator to turn basic information into a publishable article, and one-time placements are sold a la carte — all positioned as a cheaper, more targeted alternative to wire services, with the article then living on as a searchable public record ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/836884/where-to-publish-company-news-online-in-2026-citybiz/)). Submissions go through editorial review before publication ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/836884/where-to-publish-company-news-online-in-2026-citybiz/)). That dual character is also how the outlet is classified externally: Muck Rack lists citybiz as a "Press release/News aggregator" and notes daily email newsletters in some local markets, specialty newsletters for commercial real estate and CEO interviews, and contributor news aimed at business owners, C-level executives, senior managers and directors ([Muck Rack](https://muckrack.com/media-outlet/citybiz)).

The interview program is the editorial flagship. Since 2014 Warfield has conducted over 1,000 interviews of entrepreneurs, visionaries, digital disruptors, angels, venture capitalists, social innovators and CEOs ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/409493/bill-atkinson-interviews-jay-nwachu-president-ceo-of-innovation-works-2-3-2-3/)). Subjects he singled out in a 2023 interview include Daniel Lamarre of Cirque du Soleil, Amy King of the social-purpose homebuilder Pallet, Paul Reed Smith of PRS Guitars, Greg Hobelmann of Ashley Addiction Treatment, James Min of the cardiac-AI startup Cleerly and Stephen Willard of NRX ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/409493/bill-atkinson-interviews-jay-nwachu-president-ceo-of-innovation-works-2-3-2-3/)). One stated mission of the program is highlighting mental-health initiatives — Warfield speaks openly in the same interview about his own bipolar diagnosis, a stroke, an aorta bypass and a transient ischemic attack, and about his father's alcoholism and recovery at Father Martin's Ashley House ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/409493/bill-atkinson-interviews-jay-nwachu-president-ceo-of-innovation-works-2-3-2-3/)).

## Coverage in practice: the CircleUp example

citybiz's day-to-day output shows what the model produces: short, market-tagged business items, many of them company announcements and executive moves, alongside the interview series. A representative example, and the reason citybiz appears in this site's entity graph, is its June 6, 2023 report that [Brightflow AI](/organizations/brightflow-ai/) had acquired [CircleUp](/organizations/circleup/), the [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/), [California](/places/california/) fintech behind the Helio insights engine ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/425249/brightflow-ai-acquires-circleup/?abkw=citybizsanfrancisco)). The article reported the deal price as undisclosed, described Helio's data and machine-learning capabilities being combined with Brightflow's cash-flow intelligence toolkit, and credited CircleUp with supplying over $1 billion in capital to more than 500 consumer companies — including Beyond Meat, Supergoop!, Liquid I.V. and Halo Top — backed by investors including Union Square Ventures, GV, Canaan Partners, QED Advisors, TPG, [Temasek](/organizations/temasek/) and Rose Park Advisors ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/425249/brightflow-ai-acquires-circleup/?abkw=citybizsanfrancisco)). The $1 billion figure and the investor list are the company's and the acquirer's claims as reported by citybiz, not independently audited figures, and this profile uses the article only as evidence of what citybiz covers and how it covers it — not as an independent source for CircleUp's own facts, which the site's CircleUp profile establishes from other sources.

## FAQ

**What is citybiz?**
citybiz is a privately held digital business-news network headquartered in Baltimore, Maryland, covering companies, executives, transactions and commercial real estate across 20 major US city markets through original reporting, CEO interviews and a paid channel for company announcements ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/citybizco); [citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/409493/bill-atkinson-interviews-jay-nwachu-president-ceo-of-innovation-works-2-3-2-3/)).

**Who founded citybiz, and when?**
Publisher Edwin Warfield founded it in Baltimore in 2004, originally as Citybizlist ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/409493/bill-atkinson-interviews-jay-nwachu-president-ceo-of-innovation-works-2-3-2-3/)). Warfield previously ran *The Daily Record* (1983–1994) and the Local Business Network (1994–2002) ([Talking Biz News](http://talkingbiznews.com/1/citybizlist-com-tackles-business-news-on-the-web/)). LinkedIn lists the founding year as 2005, a discrepancy flagged in this profile's open items.

**How does citybiz make money?**
Through a mix of editorial business news and paid publishing: companies and their PR agencies pay to publish announcements, executive interviews and thought-leadership content, using direct submission, a self-service article creator or a la carte placements, with editorial review before publication ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/citybizco); [citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/836884/where-to-publish-company-news-online-in-2026-citybiz/)).

**How big is citybiz?**
It operates in 20 city markets as of 2023, up from 11 in 2011, and LinkedIn lists 11–50 employees ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/409493/bill-atkinson-interviews-jay-nwachu-president-ceo-of-innovation-works-2-3-2-3/); [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/citybizco)). No later traffic, revenue or headcount figures were verified from a fully read source, so none is stated.

**Is citybiz a wire service?**
No. It positions itself against wire services as a more targeted, cheaper alternative: announcements are published as articles on its own city sites rather than syndicated broadly, and are meant to remain searchable over time ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/836884/where-to-publish-company-news-online-in-2026-citybiz/)). Muck Rack classifies it as a press-release and news aggregator ([Muck Rack](https://muckrack.com/media-outlet/citybiz)).
