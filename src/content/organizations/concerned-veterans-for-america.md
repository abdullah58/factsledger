## Overview

The strangest fact about Concerned Veterans for America is who tried to destroy it — and who then defended it. In December 2024, CBS News reported that the 2016 campaign to force Pete Hegseth out as the group's leader had been spearheaded by Jessie Jane Duff, a Marine veteran who was then one of Donald Trump's 2024 campaign executive directors ([CBS News](https://tahrir2day.com/news/2024/12/03/gop-insiders-sought-hegseths-removal-as-leader-of-veterans-group-in-2016/)). Duff had worked inside CVA as a senior military adviser and, according to multiple Republican insiders, complained about Hegseth's frequent public intoxication, poor leadership, and the toxic work climate he fostered — even, two sources said, slighting his National Guard service as merely "part-time" soldiering ([CBS News](https://tahrir2day.com/news/2024/12/03/gop-insiders-sought-hegseths-removal-as-leader-of-veterans-group-in-2016/)). Yet once Trump nominated Hegseth for secretary of defense, Duff flipped: she publicly supported the nomination and defended his opposition to women serving in combat ([CBS News](https://tahrir2day.com/news/2024/12/03/gop-insiders-sought-hegseths-removal-as-leader-of-veterans-group-in-2016/)). One of Hegseth's fiercest private critics became one of his public defenders, and the veterans' group at the center of it kept growing.

Concerned Veterans for America is a conservative veterans advocacy organization founded in 2011 as the nonprofit Vets for Economic Freedom — originally the "Vets for Economic Freedom Trust" — and later renamed CVA ([Military.com](https://military.com/daily-news/2025/02/20/new-concerned-veterans-america-director-lead-groups-effort-expand-priorities.html); [CBS News](https://tahrir2day.com/news/2024/12/03/gop-insiders-sought-hegseths-removal-as-leader-of-veterans-group-in-2016/)). It is a partner organization with Americans for Prosperity, the influential conservative-libertarian group founded by billionaire Charles Koch ([Military.com](https://military.com/daily-news/2025/02/20/new-concerned-veterans-america-director-lead-groups-effort-expand-priorities.html)). For a decade its public identity was narrow — expanded health-care options for veterans and accountability at the Department of Veterans Affairs — but in 2025 it began a deliberate expansion into economic policy, foreign policy, and energy, a transformation that its July 2026 letter to the Senate on defense legislation shows is now complete ([Military.com](https://military.com/daily-news/2025/02/20/new-concerned-veterans-america-director-lead-groups-effort-expand-priorities.html); [Concerned Veterans for America](https://cv4a.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/FY-27-CVA-Senate-NDAA-Amendment-Recs.pdf)).

## The Hegseth years and the messy exit

Hegseth took the helm in July 2012 and quickly made CVA a prominent voice among congressional Republicans, insisting all the while that the goal was a bipartisan consensus on VA reform ([Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/veterans/2016/01/20/conservative-backed-veterans-group-restructures-after-leader-quits/)). The group's 2016 town halls on veterans' issues doubled as campaign events for Republican presidential hopefuls — Senators [Marco Rubio](/people/marco-rubio/), Ted Cruz, and Rand Paul, former Florida governor Jeb Bush, and business leader Carly Fiorina all discussed their veterans and foreign-policy plans at CVA forums in early primary states ([Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/veterans/2016/01/20/conservative-backed-veterans-group-restructures-after-leader-quits/)). Its "Defend Freedom" tour mixed free concerts with volunteer sign-up events, with dates scheduled in Ohio, South Carolina, and Virginia in early 2016 ([Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/veterans/2016/01/20/conservative-backed-veterans-group-restructures-after-leader-quits/)).

Then Hegseth quietly resigned. In January 2016, CVA announced a leadership reorganization just weeks before the first presidential primaries; both Hegseth and the organization called the departure mutual and dismissed rumors of a rift with the group's financial backers ([Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/veterans/2016/01/20/conservative-backed-veterans-group-restructures-after-leader-quits/)). "Sometimes it just makes sense to make a transition," Hegseth said, announcing a book and advocacy work "broader than just VA issues" ([Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/veterans/2016/01/20/conservative-backed-veterans-group-restructures-after-leader-quits/)). Chief Operating Officer Jae Pak assumed the presidency, and Peter Gaytan — a former executive director of the American Legion and executive vice president at the Wounded Warrior Project — took over communications ([Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/veterans/2016/01/20/conservative-backed-veterans-group-restructures-after-leader-quits/)).

The "mutual" framing did not survive the decade. CBS News reported in 2024, citing Republican insiders, that the billionaire donors who funded the group — Charles and David Koch — had forced Hegseth to step down after staff complaints, and that a previously undisclosed seven-page whistleblower report had detailed allegations of sexual misconduct and repeated public intoxication ([CBS News](https://tahrir2day.com/news/2024/12/03/gop-insiders-sought-hegseths-removal-as-leader-of-veterans-group-in-2016/)). The tax filings told their own story: in 2016, Hegseth's last year, CVA raised $15.9 million and spent $16.4 million; the following year expenses fell sharply after "major programs developed in the last fiscal year were paused" ([CBS News](https://tahrir2day.com/news/2024/12/03/gop-insiders-sought-hegseths-removal-as-leader-of-veterans-group-in-2016/)). The filings also showed the group had hired Hegseth's brother Philip while he was still in college ([CBS News](https://tahrir2day.com/news/2024/12/03/gop-insiders-sought-hegseths-removal-as-leader-of-veterans-group-in-2016/)). Hegseth's attorney denied all the allegations, and Hegseth himself declined to answer CBS's questions on Capitol Hill ([CBS News](https://tahrir2day.com/news/2024/12/03/gop-insiders-sought-hegseths-removal-as-leader-of-veterans-group-in-2016/)). Margaret Hoover, the PBS host who had advised Hegseth's earlier nonprofit Vets for Freedom, told CNN he had managed that organization "very poorly" — a group that raised $8.7 million in 2008 but spent more than $9 million, then saw revenue collapse to under $265,000 by 2010 ([CBS News](https://tahrir2day.com/news/2024/12/03/gop-insiders-sought-hegseths-removal-as-leader-of-veterans-group-in-2016/)).

## The alumni pipeline and the 2021 surprise

CVA's people kept surfacing in Washington. When Trump nominated Hegseth for secretary of defense in late 2024, the nomination put the group in the national spotlight; his tenure there drew scrutiny, but he was confirmed in a tie-breaking vote with Vice President JD Vance casting the final "yes" ([Military.com](https://military.com/daily-news/2025/02/20/new-concerned-veterans-america-director-lead-groups-effort-expand-priorities.html)). Other CVA alumni entered the administration too: former senior adviser Darin Selnick became a senior Pentagon adviser, and former executive director Dan Caldwell led the Trump transition team at the Defense Department ([Military.com](https://military.com/daily-news/2025/02/20/new-concerned-veterans-america-director-lead-groups-effort-expand-priorities.html)).

The group's least predictable moment came in 2021. After President Biden announced the U.S. withdrawal from Afghanistan, a diverse coalition of veterans stepped in to give him political cover — and CVA, a Koch-network group, was in it, coordinating closely with the White House National Security Council alongside Common Defense, a longtime Trump antagonist, and the Secure Families Initiative, a nonpartisan group of military spouses ([The New York Times](https://quincyinst.org/2021/07/06/unlikely-coalition-of-veterans-backs-biden-on-ending-afghan-war/)). The American Legion, the nation's largest veteran service organization, backed the withdrawal too, to the surprise of many ([The New York Times](https://quincyinst.org/2021/07/06/unlikely-coalition-of-veterans-backs-biden-on-ending-afghan-war/)). For a group usually filed under "conservative advocacy," standing with a Democratic president against congressional hawks was a genuine departure — and it previewed the broader foreign-policy role CVA was preparing to play.

## The 2025 reinvention and the 2026 policy fingerprint

In early 2025, CVA appointed John Vick as executive director with an explicit mandate to expand the group's influence ([Military.com](https://military.com/daily-news/2025/02/20/new-concerned-veterans-america-director-lead-groups-effort-expand-priorities.html)). Vick's resume bridged both worlds: an enlisted Marine who deployed to Anbar, Iraq, in 2003, a 2011 Naval Academy graduate with a master's from the Naval War College, a current Navy Reserve intelligence officer, a former House Veterans Affairs Committee staffer, and — most recently — the Republican National Committee's director for military and veterans engagement ([Military.com](https://military.com/daily-news/2025/02/20/new-concerned-veterans-america-director-lead-groups-effort-expand-priorities.html)). The group's 2025 legislative priorities kept the veterans issues but added economic reform — entitlement reviews, congressional budget process overhaul, debt reduction, tax-code revision, deregulation — plus a drawdown of the U.S. presence in Europe, a reduced NATO role limited to logistics and support, the removal of all American troops from Iraq and Syria, and expanded domestic energy production ([Military.com](https://military.com/daily-news/2025/02/20/new-concerned-veterans-america-director-lead-groups-effort-expand-priorities.html)).

A July 23, 2026 letter from CVA strategic director John Byrnes to the Senate shows how far that expansion has gone ([Concerned Veterans for America](https://cv4a.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/FY-27-CVA-Senate-NDAA-Amendment-Recs.pdf)). Writing on behalf of the group and its "thousands of activists," the letter takes positions on fiscal 2027 defense authorization amendments that read like a full foreign-policy platform: yes to war-powers reform strengthening Congress's constitutional role in declaring war; no to amendments limiting the president's ability to redeploy troops, on the ground that wealthy NATO allies "are capable of shouldering a greater portion of their regional defense burden"; yes to an Iran War Powers Resolution barring unauthorized operations against Iran; yes to repealing Syria sanctions imposed on the now-defunct Assad regime; and no to a mechanism moving frozen Russian sovereign assets into a Ukraine support fund, because it would "further lock the United States into an open-ended conflict" and allies should "take full ownership of Ukraine's defense" ([Concerned Veterans for America](https://cv4a.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/FY-27-CVA-Senate-NDAA-Amendment-Recs.pdf)).

The letter's domestic positions are equally pointed: support for requiring warrants before intelligence agencies access Americans' communications incidentally collected under foreign surveillance programs; support for a "right to repair" provision letting the government sue defense contractors that withhold technical data; opposition to collective-bargaining carve-outs at national-security agencies, backing Trump's executive order excluding them; and opposition to a U.S.–Israel technology-integration framework, on the stated ground that the Defense Intelligence Agency had raised its counterintelligence threat assessment of Israel from "High" to "Critical" after revelations of spying on senior U.S. national-security officials ([Concerned Veterans for America](https://cv4a.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/FY-27-CVA-Senate-NDAA-Amendment-Recs.pdf)). It also urges retaining requirements that NATO allies report progress toward spending 3.5 percent of GDP on defense and creating incentives for a clean Pentagon audit ([Concerned Veterans for America](https://cv4a.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/FY-27-CVA-Senate-NDAA-Amendment-Recs.pdf)). Whatever CVA was in 2016 — a Koch-backed pressure group on VA reform — by 2026 it is something rarer: a veterans organization lobbying as a full-spectrum policy shop.
