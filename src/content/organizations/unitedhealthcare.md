## Overview

UnitedHealthcare is the insurance arm of [UnitedHealth Group](/organizations/unitedhealth-group/) and the largest provider of Medicare Advantage plans in the [United States](/places/united-states/), covering 49.8 million members with $344.9 billion in 2025 revenue ([Associated Press](https://biztimes.biz/unitedhealthcares-ceo-is-shot-and-killed-outside-a-new-york-city-hotel-ap-source-says/); [Becker's Payer Issues](https://www.beckerspayer.com/financial/unitedhealths-2025-profit-dips-to-12-1b/)). Every AARP-branded Medicare plan is a UnitedHealthcare policy sold under an exclusive license to the AARP name — a license AARP's own financial statements show was secured in 2024 with a $9.062 billion advance ([Juniper Research Group](http://www.juniperresearchgroup.com/post/7233/the-9000000000-question-how-can-anyone-trust-aarp)).

UnitedHealthcare is not the same company as its parent. UnitedHealth Group is the holding company; its two operating arms are UnitedHealthcare, the insurer, and Optum, the health-services business. The insurer is based in Minnetonka, Minnesota, and manages coverage for employers and state- and federally funded Medicaid programs alongside its Medicare book ([Associated Press](https://biztimes.biz/unitedhealthcares-ceo-is-shot-and-killed-outside-a-new-york-city-hotel-ap-source-says/)). This profile covers its scale, the AARP licensing business most reference entries never mention, and the federal investigations surrounding it.

## The insurance engine of UnitedHealth Group

UnitedHealth Group reported $447.6 billion in 2025 revenue, but nearly all of it flows through two operating businesses: UnitedHealthcare, the insurer, and Optum, the health-services arm ([Becker's Payer Issues](https://www.beckerspayer.com/financial/unitedhealths-2025-profit-dips-to-12-1b/)). UnitedHealthcare alone took in $344.9 billion, up 16% year over year, while its operating margin compressed to 2.7% from 5.2% amid Medicare funding reductions and rising medical costs. Its 49.8 million members split across 29.7 million commercial, 8.4 million Medicare Advantage, 7.4 million Medicaid, 4.3 million Medicare supplement, and 2.8 million stand-alone Part D enrollees ([Becker's Payer Issues](https://www.beckerspayer.com/financial/unitedhealths-2025-profit-dips-to-12-1b/)). Optum added $270.6 billion of its own, spanning care delivery (Optum Health, $102 billion), pharmacy benefits (Optum Rx, $154.7 billion), and data and technology (Optum Insight, $19.4 billion) ([Becker's Payer Issues](https://www.beckerspayer.com/financial/unitedhealths-2025-profit-dips-to-12-1b/)). At that scale, the $9.062 billion AARP advance equals roughly ten days of UnitedHealthcare's revenue ([Becker's Payer Issues](https://www.beckerspayer.com/financial/unitedhealths-2025-profit-dips-to-12-1b/); [Juniper Research Group](http://www.juniperresearchgroup.com/post/7233/the-9000000000-question-how-can-anyone-trust-aarp)).

## The AARP brand-licensing business

The plan documents for "AARP Medicare Advantage from UHC" are explicit: "Plans are insured through UnitedHealthcare Insurance Company or one of its affiliated companies," which "pays royalty fees to [AARP](/organizations/aarp/) for the use of its intellectual property," while "AARP and its affiliates are not insurers" ([aarpmedicareplans.com](https://www.aarpmedicareplans.com/alphadog/AAUT27HP0414390_000)). In 2024 the two sides restructured that royalty arrangement, and AARP's financial statements recorded an advance of $9.062 billion — an exclusive license for UnitedHealth's insurance unit to use AARP's trade name and intellectual property in marketing health insurance, to be recognized as income over 12 years ([Juniper Research Group](http://www.juniperresearchgroup.com/post/7233/the-9000000000-question-how-can-anyone-trust-aarp); [Wendell Potter / HEALTH CARE un-covered](https://hc4us.org/unitedhealth-and-aarp-shake-hands-on-9-billion-deal-health-care-un-covered/)). That implies about $755 million a year in royalties — versus roughly $289 million in 2024 membership dues, less than half of one year's implied royalties ([Juniper Research Group](http://www.juniperresearchgroup.com/post/7233/the-9000000000-question-how-can-anyone-trust-aarp)). AARP defends the deal, saying the royalty strengthens its long-term capacity and that [AARP Services Inc.](/organizations/aarp-services/), which manages the agreement, has no input on its policymaking ([HEALTH CARE un-covered](https://hc4us.org/unitedhealth-and-aarp-shake-hands-on-9-billion-deal-health-care-un-covered/)).

## Investigations and scrutiny

In February 2025, the Wall Street Journal reported that the [Justice Department](/organizations/us-department-of-justice/) had launched a civil fraud investigation into UnitedHealth Group's Medicare billing practices — how diagnoses triggering extra payments to its Medicare Advantage plans were recorded, including at physician groups the company owns ([Wall Street Journal](https://archive.org/download/gov.uscourts.mnd.215359/gov.uscourts.mnd.215359.157.19.pdf)). DOJ lawyers from the U.S. Attorney's Office for Minnesota and the Civil Division in [Washington, D.C.](/places/district-of-columbia/) had interviewed providers by January 31, 2025, and the HHS Office of Inspector General — part of the [U.S. executive branch](/organizations/us-executive-branch/) — was also involved ([Wall Street Journal](https://archive.org/download/gov.uscourts.mnd.215359/gov.uscourts.mnd.215359.157.19.pdf)). The Journal separately reported that the DOJ was investigating possible criminal Medicare fraud, and that a Senate committee found United used aggressive tactics to collect lucrative MA diagnoses ([Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/health/healthcare/texas-ag-paxton-launches-investigation-into-unitedhealth-group-bc6ce142)). In 2026, Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton opened a parallel state probe over alleged delayed hospitalizations and care denials ([Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/health/healthcare/texas-ag-paxton-launches-investigation-into-unitedhealth-group-bc6ce142)). The company says its practices improve diagnosis accuracy and perform well in Medicare audits ([Wall Street Journal](https://archive.org/download/gov.uscourts.mnd.215359/gov.uscourts.mnd.215359.157.19.pdf)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is UnitedHealthcare the same as UnitedHealth Group?**
No. UnitedHealthcare is the insurance operating unit — "the insurance arm" — of the parent holding company UnitedHealth Group, whose other arm is the health-services business Optum; it reports its own earnings separately ([Associated Press](https://biztimes.biz/unitedhealthcares-ceo-is-shot-and-killed-outside-a-new-york-city-hotel-ap-source-says/); [Becker's Payer Issues](https://www.beckerspayer.com/financial/unitedhealths-2025-profit-dips-to-12-1b/)).

**Is AARP an insurance company?**
No. AARP-branded Medicare Advantage and Medigap plans are insured through UnitedHealthcare Insurance Company or its affiliates, which pay AARP royalties for the use of its intellectual property; "AARP and its affiliates are not insurers" ([aarpmedicareplans.com](https://www.aarpmedicareplans.com/alphadog/AAUT27HP0414390_000)).

**How big is UnitedHealthcare?**
It reported $344.9 billion in 2025 revenue and 49.8 million medical members, including 8.4 million Medicare Advantage and 7.4 million Medicaid enrollees — the largest MA book in the United States ([Becker's Payer Issues](https://www.beckerspayer.com/financial/unitedhealths-2025-profit-dips-to-12-1b/); [Associated Press](https://biztimes.biz/unitedhealthcares-ceo-is-shot-and-killed-outside-a-new-york-city-hotel-ap-source-says/)).

**Why is the Justice Department investigating UnitedHealth?**
The Wall Street Journal reported a civil fraud probe into how UnitedHealth records diagnoses that trigger extra Medicare Advantage payments, and separately reported a possible criminal Medicare-fraud investigation; the DOJ has not commented and the company disputes the reporting ([Wall Street Journal](https://archive.org/download/gov.uscourts.mnd.215359/gov.uscourts.mnd.215359.157.19.pdf); [Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/health/healthcare/texas-ag-paxton-launches-investigation-into-unitedhealth-group-bc6ce142)).

**What happened to UnitedHealthcare's CEO in 2024?**
Brian Thompson, who had led UnitedHealthcare for more than three years and worked at the company since 2004, was killed on December 4, 2024, in what investigators described as a targeted shooting outside a Manhattan hotel during the company's investor conference ([Associated Press](https://biztimes.biz/unitedhealthcares-ceo-is-shot-and-killed-outside-a-new-york-city-hotel-ap-source-says/)).
