## Overview

The Association of Mature American Citizens (AMAC) is a [United States](/places/united-states/)-based conservative advocacy organization for Americans age 50 and older, founded in 2007 by insurance-agency owner Dan Weber as an explicitly conservative alternative to [AARP](/organizations/aarp/) ([InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/for-profit/the-association-of-mature-american-citizens/)). Weber's daughter Rebecca Weber now runs it as CEO, keeping the group family-owned; it operates a 501(c)(4) lobbying arm, AMAC Action, alongside a 501(c)(3) education arm, the AMAC Foundation, and claims more than 2 million members nationwide ([AMAC](https://amac.us/about); [InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/for-profit/the-association-of-mature-american-citizens/)). Notably, AMAC's self-reported membership has barely moved since 2020 even as its national profile as AARP's loudest critic has grown. This profile is about the seniors advocacy group only — it is not AARP, and it is unrelated to any other organization using the "AMAC" acronym.

## Founded on frustration

Dan Weber was not a political operative. He ran the Hometown Insurance Agency in Bohemia, New York, for more than 30 years before founding AMAC in 2007, driven by concerns about high taxes and growing government and by the conviction that older Americans needed more effective conservative advocacy in Washington ([InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/for-profit/the-association-of-mature-american-citizens/)). Mark Hemingway reported in The Federalist that Weber had himself been an AARP member, frustrated by what he saw as the organization's politically liberal direction ([The Federalist](https://amac.us/newsline/politics/newsline/politics/in-case-you-missed-it-the-association-of-mature-american-citizens-has-quietly-become-a-conservative-powerhouse-the-federalist/)). The group sums up its founding in one line: "For many years, there was only one 50-plus organization. Now, you have a choice" ([AMAC](https://amac.us/about)). AMAC remains family-owned and family-run: the founder's daughter Rebecca Weber serves as CEO, David Weber as chief operating officer and chief marketing officer, headquartered in Bohemia with offices in Florida and New Mexico ([AMAC](https://amac.us/about/leadership)). InfluenceWatch's 2020 profile already called Weber "the late Dan Weber," confirming leadership had passed to the next generation by then ([InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/for-profit/the-association-of-mature-american-citizens/)).

## The business behind the advocacy

AMAC's revenue model looks more like a benefits-and-media business than a pure advocacy group. Income comes from membership fees — $17 a year, spouse included free — plus advertising revenue from AMAC Advantage, and AMAC operates a for-profit arm in the insurance market while saying it returns all proceeds to the membership organization ([AMAC](https://amac.us/about); [InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/for-profit/the-association-of-mature-american-citizens/)). Crucially, AMAC is compensated when members choose insurance coverage through an affiliated carrier, with sales representatives typically employed or contracted by a Medicare health-plan carrier, prescription-drug-plan provider, or insurance carrier ([InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/for-profit/the-association-of-mature-american-citizens/)). Members get a Medicare advisory call center (the Senior Resources Network), Social Security guidance through the AMAC Foundation, financial planning via RoseMark Advisors, a bimonthly magazine AMAC says reaches 1.7 million households, and an Ambassador volunteer program ([InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/for-profit/the-association-of-mature-american-citizens/); [AMAC](https://amac.us/about/leadership)). AMAC claimed 2.1 million members as of 2020 ([InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/for-profit/the-association-of-mature-american-citizens/)); its own site says "over 2 million" today ([AMAC](https://amac.us/about)) — six years of rising national prominence have produced essentially no growth in the organization's own published membership figures.

## The 2025 premium fight — data even AARP cited

AMAC Newsline reprinted a Daily Signal column by Heritage Foundation health-policy expert Edmund F. Haislmaier reporting that the national average monthly premium for standalone Medicare Part D plans rose 57% over plan years 2022-2025, while average plan choice per state fell from 29 in 2021 to 14 in 2025 ([AMAC](https://amac.us/newsline/politics/newsline/politics/biden-harris-admins-medicare-shock-for-seniors/)). The column describes a Biden-Harris administration "demonstration project" that spent more than $5 billion to cap 2025 premium increases at $35 a month, with the Congressional Budget Office estimating the demo would cost taxpayers $7 billion the next year and run two more years. It names CVS Aetna (dropping two of its three plans for 2025), UnitedHealthcare (three plans down to two), and Mutual of Omaha (leaving the program entirely). The postscript: those same 57% figures later appeared in the 2025 premium-fight coverage of AARP itself — the conservative group's ideological rival, citing data carried on AMAC's own site, as documented in this site's own AARP profile.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is AMAC the same as AARP?**
No. AMAC was founded in 2007 explicitly as a conservative alternative to AARP, which was founded in 1958 by Dr. Ethel Percy Andrus. AMAC is far smaller (about 2 million claimed members versus AARP's reported nearly 38 million), family-owned, headquartered in Bohemia, New York — and says it does not hide its politics.

**Who runs AMAC?**
The founder's family. CEO Rebecca Weber is Dan Weber's daughter; David Weber serves as chief operating officer and chief marketing officer, both with roots in the family-owned Hometown Insurance Agency. Matthew Racanelli is chief financial officer and Dave Kane chief technology officer. The group describes itself as family-owned and member-first.

**What does AMAC membership cost and include?**
Membership starts at $17 a year with a spouse included free, plus multi-year discounts. Members get AMAC Newsline access, the bimonthly AMAC Magazine, free Medicare and Social Security advisory services, participation in AMAC Action advocacy, and discounts on restaurants, retail, travel, and insurance.

**How is AMAC funded?**
Primarily through membership fees and advertising in AMAC Advantage, plus a for-profit insurance arm whose proceeds the group says it returns to the membership organization. Notably, AMAC also earns compensation when members buy insurance through affiliated carriers — a funding mechanism worth understanding alongside its advocacy positions.

**What is AMAC Action?**
AMAC Action is the group's 501(c)(4) advocacy arm, which lobbies on Capitol Hill and locally — the counterpart to the 501(c)(3) AMAC Foundation, which handles education like Social Security advisory services and, by law, does not engage in direct lobbying.

It does not assert the "conservative alternative to AARP" label as an objective fact; that is AMAC's self-description, while the independent characterization used here is "right-leaning" (InfluenceWatch). It does not give a date for Dan Weber's death, which could not be verified. It draws nothing from the Wall Street Journal (2014), New York Times (2012), Sun-Sentinel (2013), or PolitiFact (2012) coverage of AMAC, none of which was read in full. The Part D premium figures are attributed to a Daily Signal/Heritage Foundation columnist's reprinted piece, not to AMAC's own research — and AMAC itself disclaims columnists' views. Finally, it does not claim that AMAC's insurance-carrier compensation shaped any specific advocacy position; the funding mechanism is documented, the causal inference is not made.
