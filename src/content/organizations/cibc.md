## Overview

The Canadian Imperial Bank of Commerce — CIBC — is one of Canada's "Big Six" banks, headquartered at Commerce Court in Toronto and listed on the Toronto and New York stock exchanges under the ticker CM ([CIBC](https://www.cibc.com/content/dam/about_cibc/investor_relations/pdfs/annual_reports/1999-2009/2006-annual-info-form-en.pdf)). It was formed in 1961 by the amalgamation of The Canadian Bank of Commerce and the Imperial Bank of Canada, and its roots run back further: the Bank of Commerce traces to an 1858 incorporation, and the Imperial Bank to 1875 ([CIBC](https://www.cibc.com/content/dam/about_cibc/investor_relations/pdfs/annual_reports/1999-2009/2006-annual-info-form-en.pdf)). In fiscal 2025, the year ended October 31, 2025, CIBC reported total revenue of C$29,133 million and net income of C$8,454 million on total assets of C$1,116,938 million, serving 15 million clients with about 50,000 employees ([CIBC](https://www.cibc.com/content/dam/cibc-public-assets/about-cibc/investor-relations/pdfs/quarterly-results/2025/annual-hlts-2025-en.pdf)).

CIBC's connection to this site's loop is narrow and specific, and it matters to state it precisely. The bank itself did not invest in [Booksy](/organizations/booksy/). Its specialist arm, CIBC Innovation Banking, provided growth capital to Booksy in a deal announced on October 3, 2024, for an amount that was not disclosed ([Business Wire](http://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241003502568/en/cibc-innovation-banking-provides-growth-capital-to-booksy/)). This profile is therefore two things at once: a profile of a very large Canadian bank, and a careful separation of that bank from the innovation-banking unit that actually dealt with Booksy.

## Two banks, one merger

CIBC's history is a merger story, and the bank's own filings tell it plainly. The Canadian Bank of Commerce was originally incorporated as Bank of Canada by special act of the legislature of the Province of Canada in 1858; the name was subsequently changed to The Canadian Bank of Commerce, and it opened for business under that name in 1867 ([CIBC](https://www.cibc.com/content/dam/about_cibc/investor_relations/pdfs/annual_reports/1999-2009/2006-annual-info-form-en.pdf)). The Imperial Bank of Canada was incorporated in 1875 by special act of the Parliament of Canada and commenced operations in that year ([CIBC](https://www.cibc.com/content/dam/about_cibc/investor_relations/pdfs/annual_reports/1999-2009/2006-annual-info-form-en.pdf)). In 1961 the two amalgamated to form the Canadian Imperial Bank of Commerce ([CIBC](https://www.cibc.com/content/dam/about_cibc/investor_relations/pdfs/annual_reports/1999-2009/2006-annual-info-form-en.pdf)). The registered and head office is Commerce Court, Toronto, Ontario ([CIBC](https://www.cibc.com/content/dam/about_cibc/investor_relations/pdfs/annual_reports/1999-2009/2006-annual-info-form-en.pdf)).

One date in that history is worth flagging rather than smoothing over. The bank's own Annual Information Form says the Imperial Bank of Canada was incorporated in 1875; other sources, including Wikipedia, say it was founded in 1873. The discrepancy is small — incorporation versus founding, or a two-year difference in the record — but it is unresolved in the sources read, so this profile follows the bank's own filing and records the 1873 figure as an open item rather than choosing between them.

## The bank in fiscal 2025

CIBC organises itself into four strategic business units: Canadian Personal and Business Banking; Canadian Commercial Banking and Wealth Management; U.S. Commercial Banking and Wealth Management; and Capital Markets ([CIBC](https://www.cibc.com/content/dam/cibc-public-assets/about-cibc/investor-relations/pdfs/quarterly-results/2025/annual-hlts-2025-en.pdf)). It serves 15 million personal banking, business, public-sector and institutional clients in Canada, the United States and around the world, with about 50,000 employees ([CIBC](https://www.cibc.com/content/dam/cibc-public-assets/about-cibc/investor-relations/pdfs/quarterly-results/2025/annual-hlts-2025-en.pdf)). The structure is the familiar shape of a Canadian Big Six bank: a large domestic retail and wealth franchise, a growing US commercial and wealth business, and a capital-markets arm that has become the engine of earnings growth.

Fiscal 2025 was, by the bank's own numbers, a record year. Total revenue was C$29,133 million, up from C$25,606 million in 2024; net income was C$8,454 million, up from C$7,154 million; total assets reached C$1,116,938 million; deposits were C$808,124 million; and loans and acceptances, net of allowance for credit losses, were C$589,504 million ([CIBC](https://www.cibc.com/content/dam/cibc-public-assets/about-cibc/investor-relations/pdfs/quarterly-results/2025/annual-hlts-2025-en.pdf)). Assets under administration stood at C$3,998,199 million and assets under management at C$430,982 million; the common equity Tier 1 ratio was 13.3%; and market capitalisation was C$107,682 million ([CIBC](https://www.cibc.com/content/dam/cibc-public-assets/about-cibc/investor-relations/pdfs/quarterly-results/2025/annual-hlts-2025-en.pdf)). The bank employed 49,824 full-time equivalent employees at year-end ([CIBC](https://www.cibc.com/content/dam/cibc-public-assets/about-cibc/investor-relations/pdfs/quarterly-results/2025/annual-hlts-2025-en.pdf)).

The assets-under-administration figure needs a note, because it is easy to misread. It includes C$3,117.4 billion under a 50/50 joint venture between CIBC and the Bank of New York Mellon — the full contract amount of the venture, not assets wholly owned by CIBC. Stating the figure without that qualification would overstate what the bank itself administers, so the qualification stays attached to the number wherever it appears.

The fourth quarter closed the year strongly. Revenue was C$7,576 million, up 14% year over year; reported net income was C$2,180 million, up 16%; and adjusted diluted earnings per share were C$2.21, against an average analyst estimate of C$2.08 compiled by LSEG ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/canadas-td-bank-quarterly-profit-rises-interest-income-boost-2025-12-04/)). Capital Markets net income rose 40% for the full year, and by 58% in the quarter itself ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/canadas-td-bank-quarterly-profit-rises-interest-income-boost-2025-12-04/)). The board raised the quarterly dividend from C$0.97 to C$1.07 per share ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/cibc-profits-rise-on-broad-gains-including-from-increased-u-s-exposure/)).

## A record year for the Big Six — and a new chief executive

CIBC's 2025 results were part of a sector-wide story. All of Canada's Big Six banks — Royal Bank of Canada, TD, Bank of Montreal, CIBC, Scotiabank and National Bank — wrapped up fiscal 2025 with earnings that exceeded expectations, boosted by their capital-markets businesses, which benefited from a rebound in dealmaking and higher trading revenue, despite a sluggish Canadian economy and tariff uncertainty from US trade policy ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/canadas-td-bank-quarterly-profit-rises-interest-income-boost-2025-12-04/)). The banks largely benefited from high-margin, fee-based investment banking, thanks to a strong mergers-and-acquisitions market supported by mega deals and private-equity activity ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/canadas-td-bank-quarterly-profit-rises-interest-income-boost-2025-12-04/)).

CIBC's share price reflected the year: its stock gained 34% in 2025 to the point of the December report, against about 26% for the broader Toronto Stock Exchange ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/canadas-td-bank-quarterly-profit-rises-interest-income-boost-2025-12-04/)). The quarter also marked a leadership transition. The fourth-quarter results were the last under Victor Dodig, who retired as president and chief executive officer at the end of the quarter, and the first reported under Harry Culham, who stepped into the role on November 1, 2025 ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/cibc-profits-rise-on-broad-gains-including-from-increased-u-s-exposure/)). Culham signalled no big changes on his first analyst call as chief executive: "Our strategy remains consistent. It is working, and we are committed to delivering on what we set out to achieve" ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/cibc-profits-rise-on-broad-gains-including-from-increased-u-s-exposure/)).

The US push is a central part of that strategy. CIBC's capital-markets revenue rose 32% in the quarter, in part because of the push south, and the United States now accounts for roughly 34–35% of capital-markets revenue — roughly double what it was five years ago ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/cibc-profits-rise-on-broad-gains-including-from-increased-u-s-exposure/)). Canadian wealth-management revenue was up 18% in the quarter from a year earlier ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/cibc-profits-rise-on-broad-gains-including-from-increased-u-s-exposure/)). The caution in the results was credit: provisions for potentially bad loans rose to C$605 million in the quarter, up from C$419 million a year earlier, though the bank said it expected impaired provisions to remain broadly stable entering fiscal 2026 ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/cibc-profits-rise-on-broad-gains-including-from-increased-u-s-exposure/)).

## CIBC Innovation Banking and Booksy

The Booksy connection runs through CIBC Innovation Banking, a specialist unit that delivers strategic advice, cash management and funding to innovation companies across North America, the United Kingdom and select European countries, from startup to IPO and beyond ([Business Wire](http://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241003502568/en/cibc-innovation-banking-provides-growth-capital-to-booksy/)). On October 3, 2024 it announced that it had provided growth capital to Booksy, the mobile-first booking platform connecting beauty, wellness and health professionals with local customers ([Business Wire](http://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241003502568/en/cibc-innovation-banking-provides-growth-capital-to-booksy/)). Booksy said it would use the financing to accelerate new customer acquisition, marketplace expansion and platform enhancements ([Business Wire](http://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241003502568/en/cibc-innovation-banking-provides-growth-capital-to-booksy/)).

The amount was not disclosed, and this profile does not state or imply one. That matters because the Booksy profile itself mentions the CIBC financing only in its does-not-claim section, as a first-party-only report — the caution that originally put this entity on the watch list. The independent sourcing now exists: the Business Wire release is the bank's own announcement, and the financing is real, but its size remains undisclosed, and the profile records exactly that.

Andrew Schwartz, a managing director at CIBC Innovation Banking, said at the time that Booksy's management team had "built a mission-critical SaaS-platform for beauty providers to run their business on and have reached an impressive scale with over 130,000 providers and 38+ million consumers worldwide" — Booksy's own company claims, reported by the bank ([Business Wire](http://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241003502568/en/cibc-innovation-banking-provides-growth-capital-to-booksy/)). Booksy's chief financial officer, Kristina Salen, said the partnership would allow Booksy "to continue building on our momentum in the United States and throughout Europe" ([Business Wire](http://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241003502568/en/cibc-innovation-banking-provides-growth-capital-to-booksy/)). The unit's offices include Atlanta, Austin, Boston, Chicago, Denver, Durham, London, Menlo Park, Montreal, New York, Reston, Seattle, San Francisco, Toronto and Vancouver ([Business Wire](http://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241003502568/en/cibc-innovation-banking-provides-growth-capital-to-booksy/)).
