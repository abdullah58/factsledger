## Overview

Smiths News PLC is the United Kingdom's largest wholesaler of newspapers and magazines, a Swindon-headquartered
public company listed on the London Stock Exchange under the ticker SNWS
([Investegate](http://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/smiths-news--snws/contract-renewal-with-the-guardian/9481408)).
The Companies House record identifies the legal entity as SMITHS NEWS PLC, company number 05195191, an active
public limited company with its registered office at Rowan House, Cherry Orchard North, Kembrey Park, Swindon
([Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/05195191)). The company
describes itself as the UK's largest news wholesaler and a leading provider of early-morning, end-to-end supply
chain solutions, and states that it delivers to over 22,000 customers across England and Wales on a daily basis
([Investegate](http://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/smiths-news--snws/contract-renewal-with-the-guardian/9481408)).
Chief executive is Jonathan (Jon) Bunting; the March 2026 regulatory announcement names Richard Clay as chief
financial officer
([Investegate](http://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/smiths-news--snws/contract-renewal-with-the-guardian/9481408)).

## Corporate history

The modern company is legally distinct from W.H. Smith the retailer, though the two share a history. According
to the Companies House record, the company was incorporated on 2 August 2004 as Brightway Services Limited and
renamed Smiths News PLC in June 2006
([Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/05195191)). It became an
independent company in September 2006 when W.H. Smith's news-distribution business was separated from the retail
operation ([The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/smith-news-rebrands-to-connect-group/355584.article);
[Better Retailing](https://www.betterretailing.com/smiths-news-plc-to-be-renamed-and-rebranded-as-connect/)).
The company's management invokes a brand heritage "going back over 200 years" to the W.H. Smith news-vending
business, a claim reported in 2014 by The Grocer quoting the then Smiths News managing director
([The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/smith-news-rebrands-to-connect-group/355584.article)).

In April 2014 the company changed its name to Connect Group PLC, trading under the new name from 23 April 2014,
as part of a strategy to become a more diversified specialist distribution business
([London Stock Exchange Regulatory News](https://www.lse.co.uk/rns/change-of-name-u9vibbfegrwiiwh.html)). The
rebrand created three divisions — Connect News and Media, Connect Books (combining Bertrams, Dawson Books and
Wordery) and Connect Education and Care — with a stated ambition to derive half of operating profit from outside
newspaper and magazine wholesaling by 2016
([The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/smith-news-rebrands-to-connect-group/355584.article)).

The diversification push led to the November 2014 agreement to acquire Tuffnells Parcels Express, a next-day
business-to-business delivery service, for £113.4 million, partly funded by a £55 million rights issue; chief
executive Mark Cashmore called it "significantly earnings enhancing" and said it accelerated the diversification
strategy ([Alliance News](https://shareprices.com/news/connect-group-launches-rights-issue-to-buy-tuffnells-parcels-alliss-uhirbvw8i1vg3yu/)).
The deal did not work out as planned. In April 2020 Connect Group announced the proposed sale of Tuffnells to
Palm Bidco for £15 million of deferred consideration, saying group ownership afforded "no material synergies"
and that the business had become a significant drag on profit and cash flow; Tuffnells had reported an adjusted
operating loss of £14.1 million on revenues of £164.4 million for the year to August 2019
([London Stock Exchange Regulatory News](https://www.lse.co.uk/rns/disposal-of-tuffnells-trading-and-chairman-update-8g7oc4yx13z8hw1.html)).
The buyer was backed by turnaround investors brought together by Broad Oak Support Services, and the group also
agreed a £10.5 million term loan facility to support Tuffnells' turnaround
([London Stock Exchange Regulatory News](https://www.lse.co.uk/rns/disposal-of-tuffnells-trading-and-chairman-update-8g7oc4yx13z8hw1.html)).

In November 2020 the company changed its name back from Connect Group PLC to Smiths News PLC, with chief
executive Jonathan Bunting saying the move signalled "our confidence in the core news and magazine business and
its ability to deliver value to all our stakeholders"
([Alliance News](https://www.lse.co.uk/news/in-brief-connect-group-changes-name-to-smiths-news-vt4p2f4qw6qj4pk.html));
the Companies House record confirms the Connect Group name period ended on 2 November 2020
([Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/05195191)). Tuffnells
itself later entered administration in June 2023 ([Pensions Age](https://www.pensionsage.com/pa/TPR-issues-warning-notice-to-Smiths-News-over-section-75-debt.php)).

## Operations and publisher contracts

Smiths News sits between publishers and retailers in the UK news supply chain, and its recent history has been
defined by a rolling programme of long-term publisher contract renewals. In December 2024 it secured a new
contract with Reach PLC — publisher of the Daily Mirror, Sunday Mirror, Daily Express, Daily Star and OK!
magazine among more than 120 media brands — covering all of its current UK distribution territories through to
2029 and representing about £160 million of revenue a year at then-current market values; the company said it
had thereby secured long-term contracts with 91% of its newspaper and magazine revenues
([Alliance News](https://shareprices.com/news/smiths-news-seals-new-reach-contract-worth-gbp160-million-per-year-fgqyujxjpws6ozl/)).
In March 2026 it renewed its contract with Guardian News and Media through to 2031, completing the last of the
material contracts in its 2024–2026 renewal round and taking long-term coverage to over 96% of newspaper and
magazine revenues through to 2029
([Investegate](http://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/smiths-news--snws/contract-renewal-with-the-guardian/9481408)).

The largest single development came in June 2026, when Smiths News signed a 10-year exclusive wholesale
distribution agreement with News UK and Ireland covering The Sun, The Times and The Sunday Times across Great
Britain from July 2027 through to 2037. The contract is worth an extra £125 million a year in revenue, requires
Smiths News to expand its national footprint (with one-off implementation costs expected in FY2027), and
commits the wholesaler to freezing delivery service charges for retail customers for the life of the contract
([Business Live](https://www.business-live.co.uk/retail-consumer/owner-sunday-times-signs-10-34134706)). Bunting
described the deal as deepening a relationship spanning more than 50 years and called it a "significant
milestone" in securing a sustainable future for print news distribution
([Business Live](https://www.business-live.co.uk/retail-consumer/owner-sunday-times-signs-10-34134706)).

Beyond print, the company's stated growth initiatives build on its warehousing, reverse-logistics and
early-morning final-mile capabilities, including a waste recycling collection service, the delivery of books and
home entertainment, and expanded final-mile services
([Investegate](http://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/smiths-news--snws/contract-renewal-with-the-guardian/9481408)).

## Financial performance

For the year to August 2025 (FY2025), Smiths News reported revenue of £1,064 million, down 3.6% on the previous
year, reflecting the long-term volume decline in newspapers and magazines; adjusted operating profit was £39.1
million and profit after tax rose to £27.0 million from £24.7 million, with earnings per share of 11.1p against
10.3p a year earlier
([Business Live](https://www.business-live.co.uk/retail-consumer/newspaper-wholesaler-smiths-news-looks-32806417)).
Revenues from new verticals grew 16%, the company reported 93% of revenues secured to 2029, and free cash flow
improved to £36.1 million from £23.0 million, helped by a £5.4 million recovery from the administrators of
McColl's Retail Group
([Business Live](https://www.business-live.co.uk/retail-consumer/newspaper-wholesaler-smiths-news-looks-32806417)).
Bunting called it a "strong financial and operational performance" and said a three-year internal investment
programme to boost service and efficiency was advancing on schedule
([Business Live](https://www.business-live.co.uk/retail-consumer/newspaper-wholesaler-smiths-news-looks-32806417)).

## Pension-regulator warning

In February 2026 Smiths News disclosed that the UK Pensions Regulator had issued it a warning notice and was
considering a financial support direction against the company in relation to the Tuffnells Parcels Express
Pension Scheme, a power that can impose funding obligations without any finding of wrongdoing
([Pensions Age](https://www.pensionsage.com/pa/TPR-issues-warning-notice-to-Smiths-News-over-section-75-debt.php);
[Business Live](https://www.business-live.co.uk/retail-consumer/owner-sunday-times-signs-10-34134706)). The
regulator estimated the scheme's section 75 debt — the maximum it could seek in aggregate from all targeted
parties — at £3,467,000. Smiths News owned Tuffnells, the scheme's sponsoring employer, from December 2014 to
May 2020; the board said it was reviewing the notice with advisers and maintained that it had acted reasonably
as parent and had been an overall net contributor of funding to Tuffnells
([Pensions Age](https://www.pensionsage.com/pa/TPR-issues-warning-notice-to-Smiths-News-over-section-75-debt.php)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not treat the history of W.H. Smith the retailer as the corporate history of Smiths News PLC;
the two have been separate companies since the 2006 separation, and the profile keeps the 1792-era lineage as
company-invoked brand heritage rather than an independently verified founding date. It does not present the
company-stated scale figure (over 22,000 daily customers) or market-position descriptions as independently
measured current metrics. It does not assert the exact calendar day of the 2006 demerger, which the sources
read here confirm at month precision only. It does not take a position on the unresolved Pensions Regulator
warning notice, and it flags — rather than silently resolving — the one press report that misstates the end of
the Tuffnells ownership period as 2023 rather than 2020.
