## Overview

Pollstar is a trade publication for the concert and live-music industry, founded in Fresno, California, in the [United States](/places/united-states/) in 1981 as a verified box-office database and owned by Oak View Group since 2017 ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/25/pollstar-at-40-celebrating-four-decades-of-innovation-change/); [The Ticketing Business](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2017/07/pollstar-bought-venue-management-firm-oak-view-group/)). It publishes touring charts, the annual Pollstar Awards and the Pollstar Live! conference, and its Top 100 Tours ranking is the live business's standard measure of who grossed what ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/12/14/a-helluva-year-end-talking-top-tours-small-venues/)).

Pollstar is a separate trade from Billboard, and the difference defines it: where Billboard's history runs through song charts — its first "hit parade" appeared in 1934 — Pollstar's runs through verified concert box-office figures, a job its founders say no rival trade did properly ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/); [Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/25/pollstar-at-40-celebrating-four-decades-of-innovation-change/)). It serves the whole business — talent buyers, agents and venues — and its data operation grew from a dial-up account and a Radio Shack TRS-80 to the cloud ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/27/qs-with-the-garys-pollstar-founders-take-stock-of-a-legacy-of-live/)).

## A newsletter built on verified box office

Gary Bongiovanni was a booking agent for the Canadian rock band The Guess Who, and Gary Smith was a concert promoter who had worked many of the band's California shows, when the two came together in 1981 to establish Pollstar ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/27/qs-with-the-garys-pollstar-founders-take-stock-of-a-legacy-of-live/)). Bongiovanni was then working out of a Fresno doctor's office, where he shared space — and a mini-computer — with a pathologist ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/25/pollstar-at-40-celebrating-four-decades-of-innovation-change/)). Bongiovanni had an idea for a business that would supply accurate box-office reports and agency information to concert promoters, under the name Promoters On-Line Listings — POLL — and the pair incorporated in 1981, though they did not really publish until 1982 ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/25/pollstar-at-40-celebrating-four-decades-of-innovation-change/); [Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/27/qs-with-the-garys-pollstar-founders-take-stock-of-a-legacy-of-live/)). With investment from friends including major promoters Bill Graham of Bill Graham Presents and Brian Murphy of Avalon Attractions, a newsletter was developed and mailed to a small group of subscribers, carrying tour itineraries, artist availabilities and contact information ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/25/pollstar-at-40-celebrating-four-decades-of-innovation-change/)).

The founding discipline was verification. Bongiovanni and Smith built the first electronic database of its kind to store and update box-office data, persuaded promoters to report their results subject to checks, and refused advertising at the start so no advertiser could influence coverage ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/25/pollstar-at-40-celebrating-four-decades-of-innovation-change/)). Early subscribership was about 200, growing past 300 within a year, and by the 1990s, the founders say, basically everybody in the business supported Pollstar ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/25/pollstar-at-40-celebrating-four-decades-of-innovation-change/); [Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/27/qs-with-the-garys-pollstar-founders-take-stock-of-a-legacy-of-live/)). Rival trades of the day — Performance, Amusement Business and Billboard — reported only small amounts of such information, usually for major tours and rarely vetted, and two of them did not survive Pollstar's entry into the market ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/25/pollstar-at-40-celebrating-four-decades-of-innovation-change/)). Pollstar went online as ConcertNet in 1995, later becoming PollstarOnline.com for subscribers alongside Pollstar.com, and shifted to feeding the web first and assembling the print magazine second ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/27/qs-with-the-garys-pollstar-founders-take-stock-of-a-legacy-of-live/)).

## Awards, consortium and conference

The first Pollstar Awards were decided by readers and printed in the newsletter, but the ceremony became a live event in 1990, when Ed Micone, then a vice-president of Radio City Entertainment, offered Radio City Music Hall as a venue ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/25/pollstar-at-40-celebrating-four-decades-of-innovation-change/)). Micone wanted the awards to help Radio City book more contemporary acts, calling the partnership a leap of faith on both sides ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/25/pollstar-at-40-celebrating-four-decades-of-innovation-change/)). The first in-person awards took place on 16 January 1990 at Radio City Music Hall in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/), hosted by Penn Jillette with the Rockettes performing, before an audience of only about 200 to 250 people filling the first few rows. Micone later called the night a success for both Pollstar and Radio City Music Hall ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/25/pollstar-at-40-celebrating-four-decades-of-innovation-change/)). The awards continue annually; Prince made a surprise appearance at the 2005 ceremony to collect Major Tour of the Year ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/25/pollstar-at-40-celebrating-four-decades-of-innovation-change/)).

Within a decade, the pair had built something larger than a magazine. They formed the Concert Industry Consortium, a limited partnership capped at 35 members, whose partners included Bill Graham Presents, John Scher at Metropolitan, Ron Delsener and Irving Azoff, alongside two other major agencies, two other major promoters and record labels Warner Bros. and Columbia ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/27/qs-with-the-garys-pollstar-founders-take-stock-of-a-legacy-of-live/)). The consortium's annual conference began in 1994 at the Century Plaza Hotel in Los Angeles ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/25/pollstar-at-40-celebrating-four-decades-of-innovation-change/)). The partnership dissolved under a sunset provision in 2008 and the conference was rebranded Pollstar Live!, which now draws about 2,500 industry peers a year and is helmed by Ray Waddell, Oak View Group's president of media and conferences ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/25/pollstar-at-40-celebrating-four-decades-of-innovation-change/)). Its keynote tradition continued under the new ownership: [Nicki Minaj](/people/nicki-minaj/) gave the Pollstar Live! 2020 keynote, interviewed on stage by her manager Irving Azoff in a standing-room-only ballroom, talking touring, new music and the late Kobe Bryant ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/02/06/pollstar-live-2020-keynote-nicki-minaj-hints-at-friday-track-drop-talks-touring-sheds-tears-with-irving-azoff/)).

## The Oak View Group sale and the objectivity question

After 35 years of independent operation, Pollstar was sold in 2017 to Oak View Group, founded in November 2015 by Tim Leiweke, the former AEG chief executive, and Irving Azoff, head of Azoff MSG Entertainment ([The Ticketing Business](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2017/07/pollstar-bought-venue-management-firm-oak-view-group/); [IQ Magazine](https://www.iqmagazine.com/?p=25398)). Azoff had been an early Pollstar supporter and investor who called it his "go to" when planning a tour ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/25/pollstar-at-40-celebrating-four-decades-of-innovation-change/)). The deal closed on 30 June 2017 and was announced on 12 July at OVG's VenuesNow conference in Los Angeles, covering the weekly print publication, Pollstar.com, Pollstar Pro and the Pollstar Live! conference ([The Ticketing Business](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2017/07/pollstar-bought-venue-management-firm-oak-view-group/)). The sale also put Pollstar under the same roof as its main competitor, Venues Today, which OVG already owned ([The Ticketing Business](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2017/07/pollstar-bought-venue-management-firm-oak-view-group/)). Bongiovanni and Smith stayed in their leadership roles, and Leiweke called Pollstar "the voice of record for the live music and ticketing industries for over three decades" ([The Ticketing Business](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2017/07/pollstar-bought-venue-management-firm-oak-view-group/)).

The sale immediately raised a question the founders had spent 35 years trying to pre-empt: could a trade publication owned by active industry players stay neutral? OVG already owned Venues Today, Pollstar's main competitor, plus VenuesNow and SportTechie, and Azoff was both an early Pollstar investor and a party to the venue business ([The Ticketing Business](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2017/07/pollstar-bought-venue-management-firm-oak-view-group/)). Within two weeks, AEG — locked in a turf war with MSG, whose entertainment arm backed OVG — said it had concerns about whether Pollstar could retain "objectivity" and considered stopping its venues from reporting data to Pollstar and halting advertising, a step [Variety](/organizations/variety/) reported could mar the credibility of the publication's charts ([The Ticketing Business](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2017/07/aeg-considers-shunning-pollstar-msg-row/)). AEG's statement put it bluntly: "A conflict of interest clearly arises when individuals with vested interests in our industry own and control … influential trade publications" ([The Ticketing Business](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2017/07/aeg-considers-shunning-pollstar-msg-row/)). Bongiovanni replied to Variety that "Pollstar has always tried to speak to and for all facets of the concert industry, and that has not changed" ([The Ticketing Business](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2017/07/aeg-considers-shunning-pollstar-msg-row/)).

A year later the founding era closed: in June 2018 Pollstar moved its headquarters from Fresno to Los Angeles after 37 years, Bongiovanni retired that month and Smith continued in an advisory capacity ([IQ Magazine](https://www.iqmagazine.com/?p=25398)). Smith retired in 2019 after 38 years with the publication, and died at home in Fresno on 20 January 2024, aged 77 ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2024/01/22/gary-smith-pollstar-co-founder-and-concert-industry-fixture-for-six-decades-dies-at-77/)).

## What the 2025 numbers say about touring

Pollstar's year-end charts remain the scoreboard the industry quotes, and the 2025 edition told a two-speed story about a maturing market. The Top 100 Tours worldwide grossed $8.9 billion from 67 million tickets sold, down 6.1% and 3.7% respectively from 2024's record $9.5 billion and 70 million tickets — yet still 60.8% above 2019, the last full year before the pandemic ([TicketNews](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/12/beyonce-finishes-2025-as-highest-grossing-touring-artist/)). The headline dip masked stronger per-show economics: the Top 100 averaged $2.5 million and 19,000 tickets per show, against $2.3 million and 17,000 in 2024 and $1.3 million and 13,397 in 2019 ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/12/14/a-helluva-year-end-talking-top-tours-small-venues/)). Pollstar's own verdict was that in 2025, for the global arena and stadium business, "down is up" ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/12/14/a-helluva-year-end-talking-top-tours-small-venues/)). The top 10 spanned hip-hop, R&B, rock and pop, with no country act among them — George Strait, the highest country act, ranked 33rd — and Pollstar called hip-hop and R&B "super healthy right now" ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/12/14/a-helluva-year-end-talking-top-tours-small-venues/)).

Beyoncé's Cowboy Carter Tour led the year with more than $407 million from about 1.6 million tickets across just 32 shows at nine stadiums, the shortest tour ever to pass $400 million ([TicketNews](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/12/beyonce-finishes-2025-as-highest-grossing-touring-artist/)). Coldplay's Music of the Spheres tour, running since 2022, had grossed $1,522,708,583 from 13,138,644 tickets — an all-time record for a single tour — across 223 shows reported to Pollstar ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/12/14/a-helluva-year-end-talking-top-tours-small-venues/)). That record implies a touring business now dominated by a handful of stadium acts whose individual runs rival the industry's annual grosses of a generation ago.

The growth was not evenly shared: international reporting rose to 24,383 shows in 2025, up 4.3% from 23,380 in 2024, worth $7.6 billion, up 8.64% from $7 billion, while North America's Top 100 artists grossed $5.8 billion, down 6.6% from $6.2 billion ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/12/14/a-helluva-year-end-talking-top-tours-small-venues/); [TicketNews](https://www.ticketnews.com/2025/12/beyonce-finishes-2025-as-highest-grossing-touring-artist/)). Small venues contracted: rooms of 750 capacity or lower averaged 278 tickets per show in the third quarter of 2025, 3.5% fewer than in 2024 and 7% fewer than in 2023, even as their average ticket price rose 11.1% to $34.74 ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/12/14/a-helluva-year-end-talking-top-tours-small-venues/)). Pollstar's data therefore documents a split: record stadium economics at the top, a club sector under pressure at the bottom.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who owns Pollstar?** Oak View Group has owned Pollstar since 2017, when the company founded by Tim Leiweke and Irving Azoff bought the publication from its founders, Gary Bongiovanni and Gary Smith. The deal covered the weekly print publication, Pollstar.com, Pollstar Pro and the Pollstar Live! conference ([The Ticketing Business](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2017/07/pollstar-bought-venue-management-firm-oak-view-group/)).

**When was Pollstar founded?** Pollstar was established in Fresno, California, in 1981, when Gary Bongiovanni incorporated Promoters On-Line Listings (POLL) with Gary Smith; the newsletter was first published in 1982 and later became a magazine. Bongiovanni was a booking agent and Smith a concert promoter, and the two had first worked together around tours by The Guess Who ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/27/qs-with-the-garys-pollstar-founders-take-stock-of-a-legacy-of-live/)).

**What is Pollstar known for?** It is known for verified box-office data and touring charts — above all the Top 100 Tours ranking — plus the annual Pollstar Awards and the Pollstar Live! industry conference. Its charts are built from box-office reports that promoters submit and Pollstar verifies, a discipline that set it apart from rival trades ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/12/14/a-helluva-year-end-talking-top-tours-small-venues/); [Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/25/pollstar-at-40-celebrating-four-decades-of-innovation-change/)).

**Where is Pollstar based?** Pollstar is based in Los Angeles, California, under owner Oak View Group, after moving its headquarters there from Fresno in 2018, ending 37 years in the Central Valley city where it was founded. The move brought the publication to its owner's Los Angeles base ([IQ Magazine](https://www.iqmagazine.com/?p=25398); [The Ticketing Business](https://www.theticketingbusiness.com/2017/07/pollstar-bought-venue-management-firm-oak-view-group/)).

**What are the Pollstar Awards?** The Pollstar Awards are the concert industry's annual awards, first decided by readers in the newsletter and first presented live on 16 January 1990 at Radio City Music Hall in New York City, hosted by Penn Jillette. The awards continue annually; Prince made a surprise appearance at the 2005 ceremony to collect Major Tour of the Year ([Pollstar News](https://news.pollstar.com/2022/07/25/pollstar-at-40-celebrating-four-decades-of-innovation-change/)).
