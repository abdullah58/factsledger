## Overview

McDonald's Corporation is the world's largest fast-food chain by revenue: a Chicago-based company with 45,356 restaurants in more than 100 countries at the end of 2025, about 95 percent of them run by franchisees, and $26.885 billion in revenue and $8.563 billion in net income in 2025 ([McDonald's FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390826000035/mcd1231202510k.pdf)). It began as a single drive-in opened by brothers Richard and Maurice McDonald in San Bernardino, [California](/places/california/), on May 15, 1940, and was turned into a global franchise system by salesman Ray Kroc, who opened his first McDonald's in Des Plaines, Illinois, on April 15, 1955, and bought the brothers out for $2.7 million in 1961 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)).

The company is as much a real-estate business as a restaurant business. It generally owns or leases the land and buildings of its franchised restaurants and earns rent, royalties and fees from franchisees, on top of sales at the restaurants it operates itself — a model that produced $139.4 billion in systemwide sales in 2025, far more than the $26.885 billion it records as its own revenue ([McDonald's FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390826000035/mcd1231202510k.pdf)). Its stock trades on the New York Stock Exchange as MCD, and Chris Kempczinski is its chairman and chief executive ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)).

## From a San Bernardino drive-in to a global chain

The McDonald brothers opened their first restaurant, McDonald's Bar-B-Q, as a typical drive-in with carhop service. In 1948 they shut it for three months and reopened as a self-service drive-in built around the Speedee Service System: a limited menu, hamburgers cooked ahead and warmed under heat lamps, and a self-service counter that eliminated waiters — food produced in quantity at low prices, with the 15-cent hamburger as the staple ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)). The format worked, and the brothers began franchising it, selling 14 franchises of which 10 became operating restaurants ([McDonald's Corporation](https://corporate.mcdonalds.com/corpmcd/our-company/who-we-are/our-history.html)).

Ray Kroc entered in 1954. A distributor of Multimixer milkshake machines, he was intrigued that the brothers' single restaurant needed eight of them, visited San Bernardino, and became their franchise agent. In April 1955 he launched McDonald's Systems, Inc. — later McDonald's Corporation — in Des Plaines, Illinois, opening the first McDonald's east of the Mississippi River ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)). He bought the brothers out in 1961, set strict standards for food preparation and cleaning, and founded Hamburger University that same year to train operators in consistent methods ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)). The chain went public in 1965, listed on the New York Stock Exchange the following year, opened its first restaurant outside the [United States](/places/united-states/) in Richmond, British Columbia, in 1967, and passed 1,000 restaurants in 1968 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)).

Global expansion followed through the 1970s — Japan, Australia, Germany and the Netherlands in 1971, France in 1972, the United Kingdom in 1974 — reaching 6,000 restaurants worldwide by 1980, including 1,000 outside the United States ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)). The symbolic peak came on January 31, 1990, when the first Moscow restaurant opened in Pushkin Square and served more than 30,000 customers on its opening day ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)). China followed in 1990 and India in 1996, where menus use chicken and vegetarian items instead of beef or pork ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)). The first McCafé opened in Melbourne, Australia, in 1993 ([McDonald's Corporation](https://corporate.mcdonalds.com/corpmcd/our-company/who-we-are/our-history.html)).

## The business model: a landlord that sells burgers

McDonald's describes itself as primarily a franchisor, and the numbers show why. Of its 45,356 restaurants at year-end 2025, about 95 percent were franchised; the company directly operated only about 2,000 ([McDonald's FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390826000035/mcd1231202510k.pdf)). Its franchised revenues of $16.548 billion in 2025 — rent and royalties based on a percentage of franchisee sales, plus initial fees — were nearly double its $9.690 billion in sales from company-operated restaurants ([McDonald's FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390826000035/mcd1231202510k.pdf)). The franchise model is the point: the company's own filing says it is "designed to generate stable and predictable revenue, which is largely a function of franchisee sales" ([McDonald's FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390826000035/mcd1231202510k.pdf)).

The real-estate side is what makes the model distinctive. Under a conventional franchise, McDonald's generally owns or secures a long-term lease on the land and building, while the franchisee pays for equipment, signs, seating and decor; franchise agreements generally run for 20 years ([McDonald's FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390826000035/mcd1231202510k.pdf)). As of December 31, 2025, the company owned approximately 56 percent of the land and 80 percent of the buildings for restaurants in its consolidated markets ([McDonald's FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390826000035/mcd1231202510k.pdf)). That ownership is the source of the often-repeated description of McDonald's as a real-estate company that happens to sell hamburgers — a description Britannica states directly: "McDonald's is both a fast-food chain and a real estate company" ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)).

The scale of the system is large in human terms as well: over 150,000 company employees worldwide at year-end 2025, about 70 percent of them outside the United States, plus over two million people working in franchised restaurants ([McDonald's FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390826000035/mcd1231202510k.pdf)). The company is also a major enterprise-software customer: TechCrunch's coverage of [ClickUp](/organizations/clickup/)'s $400 million Series C in October 2021 named McDonald's among ClickUp's large enterprise customers, alongside [Google](/organizations/google/) and [Netflix](/organizations/netflix/) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2224795)).

## The menu: invented by franchisees

McDonald's started with a deliberately tiny menu — hamburgers, fries and milkshakes — and most of its famous items were invented not at headquarters but by franchisees solving local problems ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)). The Filet-O-Fish (1965) was created by Cincinnati franchisee Lou Groen to attract customers who avoided meat on Fridays, particularly during Lent; the Big Mac (1968) by Pittsburgh franchisee Jim Delligatti; the Egg McMuffin (1975) by California franchisee Herb Peterson, which carried the chain into breakfast ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)). The Happy Meal arrived in 1979, Chicken McNuggets in 1983, and the first drive-through window in 1975, at a restaurant in Sierra Vista, Arizona ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)). Ronald McDonald, introduced in 1963, became the mascot; the original mascot had been Speedee, a smiling chef representing the Speedee Service System ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)).

Beyond its own restaurants, McDonald's tried diversification in the late 1990s and early 2000s — investing in Chipotle Mexican Grill in 1998 and acquiring Donatos Pizza (1999), Boston Market (2000) and Aroma Cafe (1999), plus an interest in Pret a Manger in 2001 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)). Slowing sales produced its first quarterly loss in 2002, and the company sold off the other chains, withdrawing completely by 2008 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)).

## Controversies and setbacks

McDonald's has been a focal point for criticism of fast food for decades, and the record is mixed rather than one-sided. In the early 2000s, lawsuits in the United States alleged that McDonald's food had caused health problems; none succeeded ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)). The 2004 documentary *Super Size Me*, in which filmmaker Morgan Spurlock ate only McDonald's food for a month, brought the criticism to a wide audience; the company responded by expanding healthier menu options, eliminating supersize portions, and by 2008 switching to cooking oil free of trans fat in its U.S. and Canadian restaurants ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)). It also faced campaigns over wages, including the Fight for $15 movement that began in 2012, and scrutiny of the environmental impact of beef production and packaging waste ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)).

The best-known lawsuit is the 1994 hot-coffee case. Stella Liebeck, 79, suffered severe burns — requiring skin grafting and hospitalization — from coffee served at a McDonald's in New Mexico. A jury found McDonald's liable for selling a dangerously hot product and awarded $200,000 in compensatory damages, reduced to $160,000 for her 20 percent fault, plus $2.7 million in punitive damages ([Cornell Law Institute](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/Liebeck_v_McDonalds_Restaurants_1994)). The trial court suggested reducing the punitive award to $480,000, and the case settled confidentially before a final judgment ([Cornell Law Institute](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/Liebeck_v_McDonalds_Restaurants_1994)). The case became a flashpoint in the American tort-reform debate — cited by critics as frivolous litigation, and by others as a misunderstood case in which the coffee was served at nearly 190°F and the plaintiff had first offered to settle for less than her medical costs ([Cornell Law Institute](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/Liebeck_v_McDonalds_Restaurants_1994)).

In 2022, McDonald's withdrew from Russia following the invasion of Ukraine, ending more than three decades of operations there and selling its roughly 850 Russian restaurants to a local owner, who reopened them under a different name ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)). It was the first time the company had exited a major market entirely. Then, in September–October 2024, an *E. coli* O157:H7 outbreak linked to slivered onions on Quarter Pounders sickened 104 people in 14 states, hospitalizing 34 and killing one; the CDC declared the outbreak over on December 3, 2024 ([CIDRAP](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/e-coli/feds-say-probe-e-coli-outbreak-tied-mcdonalds-over)). The supplier, Taylor Farms, recalled its yellow onions, McDonald's temporarily pulled Quarter Pounders in the affected states, and beef patties tested negative for *E. coli* ([CIDRAP](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/e-coli/feds-say-probe-e-coli-outbreak-tied-mcdonalds-over)). The company then invested over $100 million in recovery — about $65 million to support the hardest-hit franchisees and roughly $35 million on marketing to bring customers back — and restored Quarter Pounders to all menus ([Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/mcdonalds-100m-investment-help-franchisees-recover-ecoli-outbreak/733175/)).

## Recent developments

The recovery held. In 2025 the company opened 2,275 restaurants and delivered systemwide sales of nearly $140 billion, up 5.5 percent in constant currency, and its filing targets 50,000 restaurants by the end of 2027 — which it says would be the fastest period of unit growth in company history ([McDonald's FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390826000035/mcd1231202510k.pdf)). By the end of 2025, delivery was available from nearly 41,000 restaurants worldwide, and the chain continued expanding self-service kiosks, mobile ordering and loyalty programs ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)).

The most recent results, for the third quarter of 2026, showed global comparable sales up 1.5 percent — the U.S. down 0.2 percent on falling guest counts, offset by growth in international markets — with systemwide sales over $38 billion for the quarter and diluted earnings per share of $3.51 ([McDonald's Corporation](https://corporate.mcdonalds.com/content/dam/sites/corp/nfl/pdf/MCD%20Q326%20-%20Exhibit%2099.1.pdf)). Loyalty is now a major sales channel: systemwide sales to loyalty members reached $41 billion over the trailing twelve months across 70 loyalty markets ([McDonald's Corporation](https://corporate.mcdonalds.com/content/dam/sites/corp/nfl/pdf/MCD%20Q326%20-%20Exhibit%2099.1.pdf)). The company also continues its charitable work through Ronald McDonald House Charities, founded in 1987; in 2022, customers and franchisees donated over $182 million to the charity, which provided 2.2 million overnight stays for families near hospitals ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was McDonald's founded?** The first McDonald's restaurant opened on May 15, 1940, in San Bernardino, California, founded by brothers Richard and Maurice McDonald. The corporation itself dates to April 15, 1955, when Ray Kroc opened his first franchised restaurant in Des Plaines, Illinois ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/McDonalds)).

**How many McDonald's restaurants are there?** 45,356 at the end of 2025, in more than 100 countries, with about 95 percent operated by franchisees. The United States has the most, with 13,706 ([McDonald's FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390826000035/mcd1231202510k.pdf)).

**How does McDonald's make money?** Mostly from franchising, not from selling food itself. It collects rent, royalties and fees from franchisees — $16.548 billion in 2025 — plus sales at the roughly 2,000 restaurants it operates directly. It also owns about 56 percent of the land and 80 percent of the buildings in its consolidated markets ([McDonald's FY2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390826000035/mcd1231202510k.pdf)).

**What happened in the 2024 E. coli outbreak?** Slivered onions on Quarter Pounders were the likely source of an *E. coli* outbreak that sickened 104 people in 14 states, hospitalizing 34 and killing one. The CDC declared it over on December 3, 2024, and McDonald's invested over $100 million in franchisee support and marketing to recover ([CIDRAP](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/e-coli/feds-say-probe-e-coli-outbreak-tied-mcdonalds-over); [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/mcdonalds-100m-investment-help-franchisees-recover-ecoli-outbreak/733175/)).

**Is McDonald's a ClickUp customer?** Yes. TechCrunch's October 2021 coverage of ClickUp's $400 million Series C named McDonald's among ClickUp's large enterprise customers, alongside Google and Netflix ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2224795)).
