## Overview

Zacks Investment Research is a [Chicago](/places/chicago/)-based independent investment research firm founded in 1978, best known for the Zacks Rank — a quantitative system that rates stocks from #1 (Strong Buy) to #5 (Strong Sell) based on how analysts are revising their earnings estimates ([Wharton](http://news.wharton.upenn.edu/press-releases/2012/12/the-wharton-school-and-zacks-investment-research-provide-zacks-data-via-wrds)). It sells research, market data and quantitative models to institutional investment managers in the [United States](/places/united-states/) and Canada, supplies earnings-forecast data to academic researchers through the Wharton School's WRDS service, and publishes market news and stock analysis for individual investors on zacks.com ([Wharton](http://news.wharton.upenn.edu/press-releases/2012/12/the-wharton-school-and-zacks-investment-research-provide-zacks-data-via-wrds); [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/trading/research-firms/zacks-investment-research)). The firm is unusual among research providers in that its product is a single empirical finding, made in 1979 and still its core: earnings estimate revisions are the most powerful force affecting stock prices.

## The 1979 finding and the Rank

Len Zacks founded the firm in 1978 to compile and analyze brokerage research for institutional and individual investors ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/upload_education/zrank.pdf)). In the 1970s he had been head of quantitative research for a major brokerage firm, holding a PhD from MIT, and his testing led to a finding he published in the Financial Analysts Journal in 1979, in an article titled "EPS Forecasts — Accuracy Is Not Enough": earnings estimate revisions are the most powerful force impacting stock prices ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/upload_education/zrank.pdf); [Wharton](http://news.wharton.upenn.edu/press-releases/2012/12/the-wharton-school-and-zacks-investment-research-provide-zacks-data-via-wrds)). The Zacks Rank was built on that finding. It is a quantitative model that uses trends in earnings estimate revisions and earnings surprises to classify stocks into five groups, from #1 Strong Buy to #5 Strong Sell, recalculated every night across roughly 4,400 stocks for which analyst estimates are available ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/upload_education/zrank.pdf)).

The Rank is deliberately scarce at the top: only the top 5% of stocks — about 220 — receive the #1 designation at any time, and about the same number receive a #5, which the company contrasts with brokerage recommendations that skew heavily toward buys ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/upload_education/zrank.pdf)). Four factors drive the score: Agreement, the extent to which analysts are revising estimates in the same direction; Magnitude, the size of recent changes in the consensus estimate; Upside, the gap between the most accurate estimate and the consensus; and Surprise, whether the company's last reported earnings beat or missed estimates ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/upload_education/zrank.pdf)).

## Two businesses: data vendor and publisher

Zacks' institutional business is data, not opinions. The Wharton School describes it as a leading provider of research, market data and quantitative models to institutional investment management firms in the US and Canada for over 30 years, and the premier source of analysts' earnings forecasts ([Wharton](http://news.wharton.upenn.edu/press-releases/2012/12/the-wharton-school-and-zacks-investment-research-provide-zacks-data-via-wrds)). Its database feeds estimates, ratings, earnings report data, fundamental data and institutional holdings for US and Canadian traded equities, and since 2012 the Wharton Research Data Services platform has offered the Zacks database to its 30,000 academic users in 30 countries ([Wharton](http://news.wharton.upenn.edu/press-releases/2012/12/the-wharton-school-and-zacks-investment-research-provide-zacks-data-via-wrds)).

The retail side is analyst coverage and publishing. A staff of 70 analysts provides continuous coverage of about 1,200 US and Canadian traded stocks, including the S&P 500, while a further 3,000 stocks are covered by the rating system alone ([Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/trading/research-firms/zacks-investment-research)). Alongside the short-term Zacks Rank, the firm publishes the Zacks Recommendation — Outperform, Neutral or Underperform — aimed at a 6- to 12-month horizon, with analysts allowed to override the model where valuation, industry conditions or management effectiveness argue otherwise ([Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/trading/research-firms/zacks-investment-research)). The Zacks Investment Committee has maintained the Focus List, a model portfolio of 50 long-term recommendations selected by Director of Research Sheraz Mian, since 1996 ([Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/trading/research-firms/zacks-investment-research)). And Zacks is itself a financial-news publisher: its market-news desk covers deals such as [Capital One](/organizations/capital-one/)'s completion of its $5.15 billion acquisition of [Brex](/organizations/brex/) in April 2026, reporting the terms — about $2.56 billion in cash plus about 10.7 million shares, $950 million in costs, nearly 80% goodwill — and noting that Capital One carried a Zacks Rank #3 (Hold) at the time ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2897536/capital-one-completes-brex-buyout-boosts-digital-payments-strategy)).

## The +28% claim, and what it does not prove

The number Zacks is most quoted for is its own: a portfolio of #1 Rank stocks has generated an average annual return of +28% since 1988, against +9% for the S&P 500 over the same period, according to the company's guide ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/upload_education/zrank.pdf)). The guide's year-by-year table runs from 1988 to 2010 and shows a +28.07% annual average for #1 Rank stocks versus +9.65% for the S&P 500 — including 2008, when the #1 portfolio fell 41.13% against the index's 37.00% decline ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/upload_education/zrank.pdf)). The figure is repeated on Wharton's vendor page, but that page is Zacks-supplied copy, so it is not independent corroboration: the honest statement is that Zacks reports +28% since 1988, and no independent audit of the claim was found in any source read for this profile.

That caveat matters because of what Zacks is used for on this site. Zacks' reporting is cited as a tier2 source for the Capital One–Brex completion figures — and for the [Discover Financial](/organizations/discover-financial/) acquisition that remade Capital One into the largest US card issuer by loan volume, the figures come from Virginia Business and Capital One itself, not from Zacks. The firm's research product and its journalism are separate things: the Rank is a model whose track record is self-reported, while its news reporting of deal terms is ordinary financial journalism, checked against the same filings and releases other outlets use.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the Zacks Rank?**
A quantitative model that classifies about 4,400 stocks into five groups, #1 Strong Buy to #5 Strong Sell, based on four factors in earnings estimate revisions: Agreement, Magnitude, Upside and Surprise, recalculated nightly ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/upload_education/zrank.pdf)).

**Who founded Zacks?**
Len Zacks, a MIT PhD who had been head of quantitative research at a major brokerage firm; he founded Zacks Investment Research in 1978 in Chicago ([Wharton](http://news.wharton.upenn.edu/press-releases/2012/12/the-wharton-school-and-zacks-investment-research-provide-zacks-data-via-wrds)).

**What did Len Zacks discover?**
That earnings estimate revisions are the most powerful force affecting stock prices, published in the Financial Analysts Journal in 1979 as "EPS Forecasts — Accuracy Is Not Enough" ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/upload_education/zrank.pdf)).

**How many stocks does Zacks cover?**
About 4,400 stocks carry a Zacks Rank; 70 analysts cover about 1,200 US and Canadian stocks in depth, including the S&P 500, and a further 3,000 are covered by the rating system alone ([Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/trading/research-firms/zacks-investment-research)).

**Does the Zacks Rank really return 28% a year?**
That is Zacks' own reported figure for a #1 Rank portfolio since 1988, against about 9% for the S&P 500; no independent audit of the claim was found, so this profile states it as a company claim, not a verified fact ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/upload_education/zrank.pdf)).

This profile does not present the +28% performance figure as independently verified: it is company-reported, repeated in Zacks-supplied vendor copy, and no independent audit was found.
