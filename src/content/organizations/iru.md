## Overview

Iru is a private IT and security software company led by chief executive and co-founder Adam Pettit — and it is not, despite what a casual search suggests, a new startup ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/10/22/kandji-rebrands-iru-launches-ai-powered-unified-security-platform/)). Iru is the October 2025 rebrand of Kandji Inc., the Apple device-management company founded in 2018 that raised $288 million in disclosed funding before relaunching under the new name ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2024/07/17/kandji-raises-100m-850m-valuation-enhance-apple-endpoint-management-solutions/)). Page-one search results for "Iru" are dominated by company-issued award announcements; the independently verifiable story is the Kandji lineage and the rebrand.

## From Kandji to a $288 million funding arc

Kandji was founded in 2018 as a device-management and endpoint-security platform for Apple products, designed to make it easier to deploy hardened devices, update software, resolve vulnerabilities and prevent problems across device fleets ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2024/07/17/kandji-raises-100m-850m-valuation-enhance-apple-endpoint-management-solutions/)). It launched in October 2019 with a zero-touch management product built around roughly 200 one-click compliance policy frameworks ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/10/28/kandji-hauls-in-21m-series-a-as-apple-device-management-flourishes-during-pandemic/)). In October 2020, as pandemic-era remote work drove demand for remote device management, the company announced a $21 million Series A led by Greycroft, with participation from Okta Ventures, B Capital Group and existing investor First Round Capital, bringing its total raised to $28.4 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/10/28/kandji-hauls-in-21m-series-a-as-apple-device-management-flourishes-during-pandemic/)).

Growth accelerated sharply. A $60 million Series B followed in April 2021, and that November Kandji raised a $100 million Series C on an $800 million valuation led by Tiger Global, with total funding passing $188 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/18/kandji-pulls-in-100m-series-c-on-800m-valuation-up-10x-since-last-year/)). By then more than 1,000 companies were using the platform, headcount had grown from 40 to over 250 in a year, and the company had opened a London office alongside its San Diego base while operating remote-first ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/18/kandji-pulls-in-100m-series-c-on-800m-valuation-up-10x-since-last-year/)). In July 2024 Kandji raised another $100 million — $50 million in Series D equity financing plus $50 million in go-to-market investment — led by General Catalyst at an $850 million valuation, with General Catalyst's Deep Nishar joining the board ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2024/07/17/kandji-raises-100m-850m-valuation-enhance-apple-endpoint-management-solutions/)). That round brought total disclosed funding to $288 million, with the company reporting 600%-plus growth in annual recurring revenue and fourfold customer growth across more than 40 industries, including Rackspace, Allbirds, Canva and Notion ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2024/07/17/kandji-raises-100m-850m-valuation-enhance-apple-endpoint-management-solutions/)).

## The rebrand: collapsing the stack

On October 22, 2025, Kandji announced it had changed its name to Iru and launched an AI-powered IT and security platform unifying identity and access, endpoint security and management, and compliance automation in a single system ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/10/22/kandji-rebrands-iru-launches-ai-powered-unified-security-platform/)). The rebrand marked a strategic expansion: the company was no longer only about Apple device management but offered mobile-device-management capabilities for Windows and Android as well, so enterprises could manage mixed fleets from one console ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4077093/kandji-becomes-iru-opens-mdm-for-windows-and-android.html)). Pettit told Computerworld the company remained committed to Apple management while responding to customers who had been asking for Windows support ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4077093/kandji-becomes-iru-opens-mdm-for-windows-and-android.html)).

The new platform is organized around what the company calls the Iru Context Model — a unified context layer building a living map across users, apps, devices, posture, policy and events — with Iru AI turning that context into insights, actions and audit-ready evidence ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/10/22/kandji-rebrands-iru-launches-ai-powered-unified-security-platform/)). It consists of six integrated products: Workforce Identity (passwordless single sign-on with hardware-backed passkeys), Endpoint Management, Endpoint Detection and Response, Vulnerability Management, Compliance Automation, and a Trust Center portal for sharing certifications and security posture ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/10/22/kandji-rebrands-iru-launches-ai-powered-unified-security-platform/)). Existing Kandji customers were to transition to the Iru experience with their configurations intact ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4077093/kandji-becomes-iru-opens-mdm-for-windows-and-android.html)).
