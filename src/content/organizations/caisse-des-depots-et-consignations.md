## Overview

The Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations (CDC) is [France](/places/france/)'s public financial institution, created by the law of 28 April 1816 to safeguard public funds and restore trust after the fall of Napoleon ([Caisse des Dépôts](https://www.caissedesdepots.fr/en/know-us/our-model/our-history)). It is not a bank: it is a special public institution under the supervision of the French Parliament that protects regulated savings, funds social housing and local development, manages pension schemes, and invests long-term as the French state's financial arm ([Caisse des Dépôts](https://www.caissedesdepots.fr/sites/cdc.fr/files/2025-09/Financial%20report%202024.pdf)).

Since 2020 it has also sat at the top of France's public financial group, holding 66% of [La Poste](/organizations/la-poste/), whose banking arm [La Banque Postale](/organizations/la-banque-postale/) owns [CNP Assurances](/organizations/cnp-assurances/) outright ([Moody's](https://www.caissedesdepots.fr/sites/cdc.fr/files/2025-12/Moody's%20Credit_Opinion-Caisse-Des-Depots-et-30%20October%202025.pdf)).

## A guardian of public money, supervised by Parliament

The CDC was born from distrust. After the collapse of the Napoleonic empire, savers had lost faith in French institutions, so in April 1816 the government created a new body to manage private funds and placed it under the protection and supervision of Parliament, with restoring public faith as its primary objective ([Caisse des Dépôts](https://www.caissedesdepots.fr/en/know-us/our-model/our-history)). Parliamentary oversight remains its defining feature: its financial report calls it the only financial institution in Europe whose independence is protected by parliament, through a 16-member Supervisory Board including five parliamentarians, reporting to Parliament at least once a year ([Caisse des Dépôts](https://www.caissedesdepots.fr/sites/cdc.fr/files/2025-09/Financial%20report%202024.pdf)). Its chairman and chief executive is appointed for five years by decree of the President of the Republic, after review by Parliament's finance committees, and is personally responsible for the entrusted funds ([Caisse des Dépôts](https://www.caissedesdepots.fr/sites/cdc.fr/files/2025-09/Financial%20report%202024.pdf)). The 2019 PACTE Law strengthened the board further, giving it deliberative power, and submitted the CDC to the direct supervision of the banking supervisor ACPR, although it is not a credit institution ([Moody's](https://www.caissedesdepots.fr/sites/cdc.fr/files/2025-12/Moody's%20Credit_Opinion-Caisse-Des-Depots-et-30%20October%202025.pdf)).

## Turning savings into housing and local investment

Its Savings Funds centralise and manage around 60% of the French population's regulated savings — the Livret A, LDDS and LEP passbooks — and transform them into very long-term loans of up to 80 years for social housing, urban policy and local authority projects ([Caisse des Dépôts](https://www.caissedesdepots.fr/sites/cdc.fr/files/2025-09/Financial%20report%202024.pdf)). That housing role is old: the CDC made its first housing loans from its own funds in 1905, took over the government's housing loan programme in 1908, and was allowed from 1921 to back low-income housing loans with savings funds ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/caisse-des-depots-et-consignations)). In 2024 the Savings Funds agreed €28.1 billion in loans, including €20.8 billion for social housing and urban planning ([Caisse des Dépôts](https://www.caissedesdepots.fr/sites/cdc.fr/files/2025-09/Financial%20report%202024.pdf)) — a scale that makes the passbook savings of ordinary French savers the direct funding line for the country's social housing. Beyond housing, its Social Policy division supports people through pensions, vocational training, disability, old age and health, working for 63,000 public employers and managing more than 70 funds ([Caisse des Dépôts](https://www.caissedesdepots.fr/sites/cdc.fr/files/2025-09/Financial%20report%202024.pdf)).

## The top of France's public financial group

The CDC is also a major shareholder. Its Strategic Investment division manages stakes in some 20 companies where it is the reference shareholder, spanning finance, real estate, infrastructure and services — among them Bpifrance, SFIL, Euronext, Euroclear, Icade, Compagnie des Alpes, Transdev, RTE, GRTgaz and Suez ([Caisse des Dépôts](https://www.caissedesdepots.fr/sites/cdc.fr/files/2025-09/Financial%20report%202024.pdf)). The decisive reorganisation came on 4 March 2020, when the creation of a large public financial pole was completed: the CDC became majority shareholder of La Poste with 66% of its capital, the French State holding the remaining 34%, and La Banque Postale became majority shareholder of CNP Assurances with 62.1% ([La Banque Postale](https://www.labanquepostale.com/content/dam/lbp/documents/communiques-de-presse/en/2020/CP-creation-large-public-financial-pole-2020.pdf)). The competition authority had cleared both moves without conditions in December 2019, as a government-initiated public bancassurance pole ([Autorité de la concurrence](https://www.autoritedelaconcurrence.fr/en/print/pdf/node/6782)). Moody's rates the CDC Aa3 with a negative outlook, aligned with the Government of France ([Moody's](https://www.caissedesdepots.fr/sites/cdc.fr/files/2025-12/Moody's%20Credit_Opinion-Caisse-Des-Depots-et-30%20October%202025.pdf)). At 31 December 2024 the group held €1,034.4 billion in total consolidated assets and earned €3.0 billion of attributable net profit ([Caisse des Dépôts](https://www.caissedesdepots.fr/sites/cdc.fr/files/2025-09/Financial%20report%202024.pdf)).

## FAQ

### What is the Caisse des Dépôts?

It is France's public financial institution, created in 1816 to safeguard public funds. It protects regulated savings, funds social housing and local development, manages pension schemes, and invests long-term on behalf of the French state, under the supervision of Parliament rather than as a commercial bank.

### Is the Caisse des Dépôts a bank?

No. It is a special public institution with a sui generis legal status dating from the 1816 law. It is not a credit institution under EU law and is not within the scope of European Banking Supervision, although the 2019 PACTE Law placed it under the direct supervision of France's banking supervisor, the ACPR.

### What does the Caisse des Dépôts own?

Its largest holding is 66% of La Poste, with the French State holding 34%. Through La Poste it ultimately controls La Banque Postale and CNP Assurances, and it also holds stakes in Bpifrance, SFIL, Euronext, Euroclear, Icade, Compagnie des Alpes, Transdev, RTE and Suez, among others.

### How big is the Caisse des Dépôts?

At 31 December 2024 the group held €1,034.4 billion in total consolidated assets and reported €3.0 billion of attributable net profit. Its Savings Funds agreed €28.1 billion in loans during 2024, and its Asset Management division managed more than €300 billion.
