## Overview

nutpods is an American plant-based coffee creamer brand, the consumer brand of Green Grass Foods, Inc. of Bellevue, Washington. Founded in 2013 by Madeline Haydon, it grew from a $32,000 Kickstarter campaign into the top-selling plant-based creamer on [Amazon](/organizations/amazon/), reaching around 15,000 US stores before its acquisition by Kroger-backed investment group MPearlRock in January 2024 ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-sells-to-kroger-backed-mpearlrock/); [BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-ceo-haydon-on-acquisition-were-just-a-bigger-player-now/)).

The brand name is styled lowercase by the company, and the founder's surname is Haydon — not "Hayden", as some podcast summaries render it. Haydon had no consumer-packaged-goods experience when she started it: she had worked as a recruitment director for a blood donation nonprofit after 14 years in the medical field, and holds a BA in English from the University of Washington and an MBA from Seattle University ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-ceo-haydon-on-acquisition-were-just-a-bigger-player-now/); [UW Magazine](https://magazine.washington.edu/feature/madeline-haydon-finds-a-yummier-way-to-drink-healthy-coffee/)).

## Founding and the Kickstarter start

Haydon founded nutpods in 2013 after finding no non-dairy creamer that offered the texture and flavor of dairy creamers without sugar and artificial ingredients. Working from her kitchen, she spent months developing a formula — a blend of almond milk and coconut cream that would not overpower coffee — and in late 2013 took it to Kickstarter, raising $32,000 to convert the kitchen recipe into a commercial formulation ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-ceo-haydon-on-acquisition-were-just-a-bigger-player-now/); [UW Magazine](https://magazine.washington.edu/feature/madeline-haydon-finds-a-yummier-way-to-drink-healthy-coffee/)).

The brand launched in May 2015, with cartons sold on Amazon.com and in 10 Seattle-area PCC Natural Markets, in three flavors — Original, French Vanilla and Hazelnut ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2015/nutpods-closes-first-funding-round-on-circleup-enters-haggen-stores-in-washington-and-oregon); [CLA Meridian Capital](https://meridianib.com/press-releases/nutpods-raises-series-b-growth-equity-raise-circleup/?wpv_view_count=14831-TCPID12272&wpv_paged=2)). The product was positioned as the first unsweetened, dairy-free alternative to half-and-half, certified vegan, OU Kosher and gluten-free, Non-GMO Project Verified and carrageenan-free ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2015/nutpods-closes-first-funding-round-on-circleup-enters-haggen-stores-in-washington-and-oregon)). On Amazon it became the #1 new release and a Best Seller in its category, and by the autumn of 2015 it had entered 37 Haggen stores across Washington and Oregon, with further placements along the West Coast ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2015/nutpods-closes-first-funding-round-on-circleup-enters-haggen-stores-in-washington-and-oregon)).

## The Amazon-first route to market

nutpods' defining strategic choice was to build the brand online before chasing grocery shelves. Launching on Amazon let a tiny team sell nationally without paying slotting fees or waiting for retail resets, and the reviews and sales data that came back shaped the product and the pitch to retailers ([Food Business News](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/15600-food-entrepreneur-the-amazon-effect)). Amazon sales grew 5,500% between the 2015 launch and 2019, and the brand collected more than 10,000 reviews with a 4.5-star average — feedback Haydon said investors read closely when she later raised capital ([Food Business News](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/15600-food-entrepreneur-the-amazon-effect)).

The online-first model produced a breakout year in 2019: nutpods was named Amazon's Small Business of the Year, selected from more than 1,300 nominees, and placed No. 13 overall — No. 2 among food and beverage companies — on the Inc. 5000 list, with three-year growth of 11,623% and $19.1 million in 2018 revenue, achieved with just 24 employees ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2019/amazon-awards-shine-spotlight-third-party-sellers-including-bellevue-based-startup-nutpods/); [Food Business News](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/15600-food-entrepreneur-the-amazon-effect)). Haydon was also named Ernst & Young's Entrepreneur of the Year for the Pacific Northwest in 2019, and [Forbes](/organizations/forbes/) recognized her in its 2017 coverage of women leading the future of food; Inc. later named her to its Female Founders 200 ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2019/amazon-awards-shine-spotlight-third-party-sellers-including-bellevue-based-startup-nutpods/); [Seattle University Magazine](https://cms.seattleu.edu/newsroom/seattle-university-magazine/winter-2026-articles/a-trailblazer-in-small-business-entrepreneurship.php)).

Scale came without a large headcount. Haydon ran the company lean — Amazon fulfillment, a small staff and a third-party warehouse — and told UW Magazine in 2023 that the team was 33 people with more than $100 million in annual sales ([UW Magazine](https://magazine.washington.edu/feature/madeline-haydon-finds-a-yummier-way-to-drink-healthy-coffee/)). By the January 2024 acquisition the team had grown to 36 full-time employees ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-ceo-haydon-on-acquisition-were-just-a-bigger-player-now/)).

## Funding: CircleUp, VMG, and the road to 15,000 stores

nutpods raised money in stages, and the funding story has one distinction worth keeping straight: the 2015 raise and the later CircleUp investment were different things. The first funding round closed in the summer of 2015 on [CircleUp](/organizations/circleup/)'s investment platform — then a crowdfunding marketplace — oversubscribing in under a month, with the money earmarked for production, distribution expansion and marketing ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2015/nutpods-closes-first-funding-round-on-circleup-enters-haggen-stores-in-washington-and-oregon)). CircleUp Growth Partners, the platform's venture fund, made its initial equity investment separately, in December 2016, and later led a Series B growth-equity raise for the company ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2019/nutpods-secures-investment-from-vmg-partners/); [CLA Meridian Capital](https://meridianib.com/press-releases/nutpods-raises-series-b-growth-equity-raise-circleup/?wpv_view_count=14831-TCPID12272&wpv_paged=2)).

The July 2019 round with VMG Partners was the turning point. Haydon said she had resisted raising money for years — "it took me many years to let go of the need to validate our brand by raising money" — and chose VMG partly for its record with women-founded businesses ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2019/nutpods-secures-investment-from-vmg-partners/)). The company had grown 250% over the prior year, had expanded into refrigerated products and added Caramel as a fourth core flavor, and planned to spend the money on brand awareness through digital marketing rather than on its own factory or television advertising ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2019/nutpods-secures-investment-from-vmg-partners/)). VMG's Wayne Wu said the firm backed nutpods because of its "highly focused strategy around trying to be the largest player in non-dairy coffee creamers" ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2019/nutpods-secures-investment-from-vmg-partners/)). According to BevNET citing Crunchbase, the brand raised at least $35.1 million across three funding rounds in total ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-ceo-haydon-on-acquisition-were-just-a-bigger-player-now/)).

Retail followed the online proof. Kroger became the first nationwide chain to carry nutpods, in 2018, and by the January 2024 acquisition the brand was sold in around 15,000 stores nationwide — including Kroger, Costco, Walmart, Albertsons, Sprouts, Publix, Target and Whole Foods — while remaining the top-selling plant-based creamer in the natural retail channel, according to the brand ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-ceo-haydon-on-acquisition-were-just-a-bigger-player-now/); [Just Food](https://www.just-food.com/news/mpearlrock-acquires-us-non-dairy-creamer-brand-nutpods/)). During the four-year VMG partnership nutpods more than tripled net sales, according to MPearlRock ([Just Food](https://www.just-food.com/news/mpearlrock-acquires-us-non-dairy-creamer-brand-nutpods/)).

## Product expansion beyond the creamer

The original shelf-stable cartons were only the starting point. nutpods moved into refrigerated creamers, then in May 2021 launched a naturally sweetened creamer line that still contained zero grams of sugar per serving; after nine months on the market those two SKUs accounted for 15% of nutpods' refrigerated offerings at Whole Foods Market ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2022/plant-based-creamer-nutpods-brings-zero-sugar-innovation-to-the-milk-aisle)). An oat cinnamon swirl creamer followed in 2021, and in March 2022 the company launched zero-sugar Barista oatmilks in Original and Cinnamon Dolce — 32 oz cartons formulated to foam and froth for at-home espresso drinks, with zero grams of sugar and 70 calories per 8 oz serving ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2022/plant-based-creamer-nutpods-brings-zero-sugar-innovation-to-the-milk-aisle)). By 2024 the range had expanded further into ready-to-drink products and brand collaborations, including one with Chamberlain Coffee ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-sells-to-kroger-backed-mpearlrock/)).

The through-line was the same positioning Haydon started with: zero-sugar, plant-based products for coffee drinkers who wanted a rich, creamy cup without sweeteners or additives, including vegans and followers of paleo, Whole30, gluten-free and keto diets ([Food Business News](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/15600-food-entrepreneur-the-amazon-effect); [BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2015/nutpods-closes-first-funding-round-on-circleup-enters-haggen-stores-in-washington-and-oregon)). That positioning put a small Bellevue brand head-to-head with the category's giants: by 2024 Haydon said nutpods had become competitive with leading non-dairy brands such as So Delicious and International Delight ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-ceo-haydon-on-acquisition-were-just-a-bigger-player-now/)).

## The MPearlRock acquisition and Haydon's departure

On January 16, 2024, MPearlRock announced it had acquired nutpods for undisclosed terms ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-sells-to-kroger-backed-mpearlrock/); [Just Food](https://www.just-food.com/news/mpearlrock-acquires-us-non-dairy-creamer-brand-nutpods/)). MPearlRock itself had launched only the previous week, as a partnership between PearlRock Partners — the venture capital arm of The Kroger Co. — and New York asset manager MidOcean Partners, headed by former Keurig CEO Brian Kelley and aimed at high-growth emerging consumer packaged goods brands with $30 million to $100 million in annual revenue ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-sells-to-kroger-backed-mpearlrock/); [Just Food](https://www.just-food.com/news/mpearlrock-acquires-us-non-dairy-creamer-brand-nutpods/)). Kroger had been watching the brand since becoming its first national retail chain in 2018, and Haydon said the retailer had seen strong shelf metrics before the deal ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-ceo-haydon-on-acquisition-were-just-a-bigger-player-now/)).

Haydon confirmed at the time that she would stay as chief executive and a board director for the "foreseeable future", and framed the sale as a bigger platform rather than an exit: "I didn't pop out of nowhere, we're just a bigger player now" ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-ceo-haydon-on-acquisition-were-just-a-bigger-player-now/)). Her stated priorities under the new owner were to go deeper in existing stores, then to enter foodservice — cafes and restaurants, a channel shelved when the pandemic closed it in 2020 — and eventually the club channel, alongside new pack sizes from travel-size to multi-serve ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-ceo-haydon-on-acquisition-were-just-a-bigger-player-now/)). She also used the moment to call for more support for woman-owned brands, noting that "females have a harder time accessing capital on the private equity and on the venture side" ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-ceo-haydon-on-acquisition-were-just-a-bigger-player-now/)).

The stay proved short. Later in 2024 Haydon stepped down as chief executive, and Seattle University Magazine describes her as founder and former CEO, now focused on mentoring female founders and entrepreneurs of color, serving on private-company boards, and funding the Madeline Haydon Endowed Scholarship at Seattle University ([Seattle University Magazine](https://cms.seattleu.edu/newsroom/seattle-university-magazine/winter-2026-articles/a-trailblazer-in-small-business-entrepreneurship.php)). She had begun giving back earlier, creating the Madeline Haydon Award for Entrepreneurship at Seattle University in 2021, after placing third in the same business-plan competition with nutpods in 2015 ([Seattle University Magazine](https://cms.seattleu.edu/newsroom/seattle-university-magazine/winter-2026-articles/a-trailblazer-in-small-business-entrepreneurship.php)). Her successor as chief executive was not named in any source read for this profile.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who founded nutpods?** Madeline Haydon founded nutpods in 2013. She developed the almond-and-coconut creamer recipe in her kitchen, raised $32,000 on Kickstarter, and launched the brand in May 2015 on Amazon and in Seattle-area PCC Natural Markets ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-ceo-haydon-on-acquisition-were-just-a-bigger-player-now/)).

**Who owns nutpods now?** MPearlRock acquired nutpods on January 16, 2024, for undisclosed terms. MPearlRock is a partnership between PearlRock Partners, the venture capital arm of The Kroger Co., and MidOcean Partners, headed by former Keurig CEO Brian Kelley ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-sells-to-kroger-backed-mpearlrock/)).

**How much funding did nutpods raise before the acquisition?** According to BevNET citing Crunchbase, the brand raised at least $35.1 million across three funding rounds, with investors including CircleUp Growth Partners, VMG Partners and Sidekick Fund ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2024/nutpods-ceo-haydon-on-acquisition-were-just-a-bigger-player-now/)).

**Where is nutpods headquartered?** In Bellevue, Washington, according to the acquisition announcements, GeekWire and UW Magazine. Some company releases describe it as based in Seattle; this profile uses Bellevue and flags the discrepancy ([Just Food](https://www.just-food.com/news/mpearlrock-acquires-us-non-dairy-creamer-brand-nutpods/)).

**What does nutpods sell?** Dairy-free, plant-based coffee creamers — originally an unsweetened almond-and-coconut blend, later joined by refrigerated creamers, zero-sugar sweetened creamers, oat creamers and zero-sugar Barista oat milks, plus ready-to-drink products ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2022/plant-based-creamer-nutpods-brings-zero-sugar-innovation-to-the-milk-aisle)).
