## Overview

The Great Public Schools (GPS) Association of Queensland has staged an interschool rugby competition since 1918 — but not always in the same code: the member schools played rugby league through most of the 1920s and only reverted to rugby union in 1928 ([GPS 100](https://gps100.org.au/schools/churchie/)). Founded in 1918 with rugby among its original sports alongside rowing and swimming, GPS rugby marked its centenary in 2018, and its governing body today describes itself as Australia's leading school sports association ([GPS 100](https://gps100.org.au/); [GPS Queensland](https://gpsqld.org.au/)). Nine schools contest the premiership: Anglican Church Grammar School (Churchie), Brisbane Boys' College, Brisbane Grammar School, Brisbane State High School, Ipswich Grammar School, St Joseph's College, Gregory Terrace, [St Joseph's Nudgee College](/schools/st-josephs-nudgee-college/), The Southport School and Toowoomba Grammar School ([GPS Queensland](https://gpsqld.org.au/)).

## A century of competition

Churchie, one of the association's founding schools, records that it entered its first competitive team in the B-grade All Schools' competition in 1920 — when rugby league was the code being played — and that the GPS schools did not play rugby union until 1928 ([GPS 100](https://gps100.org.au/schools/churchie/)). Churchie won its first GPS rugby union championship in 1938 and has since collected 18 titles, including back-to-back premierships in 1950–51, 1962–63 and 2014–15 ([GPS 100](https://gps100.org.au/schools/churchie/)). The association celebrated the hundredth anniversary of its foundation with a year-long "GPS 100" program in 2018, launched at St Joseph's Nudgee College before 150 past and present students, staff and guests drawn from all nine member schools ([GPS 100](https://gps100.org.au/)). That centenary season was fiercely contested: the Gold Coast Bulletin covered The Southport School's push for an 11th GPS premiership against the backdrop of the competition's roughly hundred-year history, while The Courier-Mail reported on the premiership race involving Churchie and the other member schools ([Gold Coast Bulletin](https://www.goldcoastbulletin.com.au); [The Courier-Mail](https://www.couriermail.com.au/sport/rugby/gps-rugby-gregory-terrace-must-overcome-history-to-topple-rivals/news-story/81ba2d252b256b81cedc3d98b03f6519)).

## The Nudgee era

No school has shaped the modern competition like St Joseph's Nudgee College. In September 2026 the association confirmed Nudgee as GPS First XV premiers for a fifth consecutive year — the school's 47th GPS rugby title ([GPS Queensland](https://gpsqld.org.au/)). Rugby is a Term 3 sport in the GPS calendar, contested alongside basketball and chess, and the First XV premiership is the code's senior schoolboy prize, played out each season between the nine schools ([GPS Queensland](https://gpsqld.org.au/)).

## A pipeline into representative rugby — and a contest with league

The competition's wider significance lies in its function as a talent pipeline: a 2023 [ABC News](/organizations/abc-news/) report on the state of Australian rugby examined GPS rugby's role in feeding the representative and professional ranks, and the pressure it faces from rugby league clubs recruiting players out of the same schools ([ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2023-06-14/australian-rugby-union-wallabies-wallaroos-grassroots-schools/102464748)). The report's title stated the stakes plainly: if Australian rugby wants to thrive, it needs to win the fight for talent at school level ([ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2023-06-14/australian-rugby-union-wallabies-wallaroos-grassroots-schools/102464748)). The alumni record puts names to that pipeline — Nudgee produced the Wallaby Mark Loane, and Gregory Terrace the Wallaby great Michael Lynagh — in a 2018 survey that treated the GPS schools as the benchmark for Queensland's schoolboy rugby nurseries ([Westside News](https://ashgroveclassof1968.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/Marist-Brothers-Ashgrove_-Is-this-Queensland%E2%80%99s-greatest-rugby-union-schoolboy-college-_-Quest-News.pdf)). That survey's framing question — whether Queensland's greatest rugby school might lie outside the GPS system at all — itself testifies to the system's standing: the GPS is the standard every other nursery is measured against ([Westside News](https://ashgroveclassof1968.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/Marist-Brothers-Ashgrove_-Is-this-Queensland%E2%80%99s-greatest-rugby-union-schoolboy-college-_-Quest-News.pdf)).
