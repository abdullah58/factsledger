## Overview

Agence France-Presse (AFP) is a French international news agency headquartered in [Paris](/places/paris/), and the oldest news agency in the world — founded as Agence Havas in 1835, from the Bureau Havas created in 1832, and reborn under its current name in August 1944, when journalists of the French Resistance seized its offices in liberated Paris ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Agence-France-Presse)). It is one of the three largest news agencies in the world, alongside the [Associated Press](/organizations/associated-press/) and [Reuters](/organizations/reuters/), and France's largest news agency ([New World Encyclopedia](http://Www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Agence_France_Presse)). What makes AFP unusual is its legal form: a government-chartered public corporation whose 1957 statute forbids it from falling under the control of any ideological, political or economic group — independence written into law rather than left to custom. Its wire reporting still moves world news, including the November 2025 Tokyo court ruling against [Cloudflare](/organizations/cloudflare/) over manga piracy, carried by [The Straits Times](/organizations/the-straits-times/).

## The first news agency

Charles-Louis Havas, a Parisian translator and advertising agent, created Bureau Havas in 1832, translating reports from foreign papers and distributing them to Paris and provincial newspapers; in 1835 the bureau became Agence Havas, "the world's first true news agency" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Agence-France-Presse)). The agency pioneered the collection and dissemination of news as a commodity, adopting the carrier pigeon, then the railroad and the telegraph — establishing the first telegraph service in France in 1845 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Agence-France-Presse); [New World Encyclopedia](http://Www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Agence_France_Presse)). Between 1852 and 1919 it worked in close collaboration with the advertising firm Correspondance Générale Havas, and by the late 1800s its staff correspondents were stationed in many world capitals ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Agence-France-Presse)).

Two of Havas's employees went on to found the agencies that would become AFP's great rivals: Paul Julius Reuter set up Reuters in London and Bernhard Wolff the Wolff Agency in Berlin ([New World Encyclopedia](http://Www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Agence_France_Presse)). Havas's sons, who succeeded him in 1852, signed agreements giving each agency an exclusive reporting zone in different parts of Europe — an arrangement that lasted until the 1930s, when short-wave wireless cut communications costs ([New World Encyclopedia](http://Www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Agence_France_Presse)).

## War, rebirth, and the 1957 statute

The German occupation of France suppressed Agence Havas in 1940, and many of its personnel were active in the underground ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Agence-France-Presse)). On 20 August 1944, as Allied forces moved on Paris, a group of journalists in the French Resistance seized the offices of the French Information Office and issued the first news dispatch from the liberated city under the name of Agence France-Presse ([New World Encyclopedia](http://Www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Agence_France_Presse)). The postwar French government gave AFP the assets of Agence Havas, including the Paris building that became its headquarters, and AFP quickly joined Reuters, TASS and the US agencies AP and UPI as one of the world's leading news agencies ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Agence-France-Presse)).

Established after the war as a state enterprise, AFP spent the postwar years building its network of international correspondents — one of them the first Western journalist to report the death of Joseph Stalin, on 6 March 1953 ([New World Encyclopedia](http://Www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Agence_France_Presse)). Keen to shake off its semi-official status, the agency won its independence by law: on 10 January 1957 the French parliament passed a statute establishing AFP as a commercial business independent of the French government ([New World Encyclopedia](http://Www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Agence_France_Presse)). Article 2 of the statute states: "The Agency shall be independent of all political, economic, philosophical or religious influence" ([New World Encyclopedia](http://Www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Agence_France_Presse)). AFP is administered by a chief executive and a board of 15 members — eight press representatives, two AFP personnel, two from government-owned radio and television, and three from the government — and the board elects the CEO for a renewable three-year term ([New World Encyclopedia](http://Www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Agence_France_Presse)). A council is charged with ensuring the agency operates according to its statutes, which mandate absolute independence and neutrality ([New World Encyclopedia](http://Www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Agence_France_Presse)). The French government remains the agency's primary client, buying subscriptions for its services — an indirect subsidy, since the statutes prohibit direct government subsidies ([New World Encyclopedia](http://Www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Agence_France_Presse)).

In 1982 AFP began decentralising editorial decision-making, setting up the first of its autonomous regional centres in Hong Kong; today its regional centres are in Washington, Hong Kong, Nicosia and Montevideo, with bureaus in 110 countries ([New World Encyclopedia](http://Www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Agence_France_Presse)). It transmits news in French, English, Arabic, Spanish, German, Portuguese and Russian, and runs a photo service and specialised reports, several concerned with African matters ([New World Encyclopedia](http://Www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Agence_France_Presse); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Agence-France-Presse)). In September 2007 the AFP Foundation was launched to promote journalism standards worldwide, training journalists in developing countries and hosting debates on press freedom and ethics ([New World Encyclopedia](http://Www.newworldencyclopedia.org/entry/Agence_France_Presse)).

## AFP versus the platforms — and AFP on Cloudflare

AFP has become a leading combatant in the fight over paying for news online. On 2 August 2023 it applied to a Paris court to compel X, the platform formerly known as Twitter, to hand over data needed "for assessing the remuneration owed to AFP" under the European Union's neighbouring-rights rules, after what the agency called a "clear refusal" to discuss payment ([Associated Press](https://techxplore.com/news/2023-08-agence-france-presse-pursues-copyright-case.pdf)). France was the first EU country to adopt the rules into national law, in 2019, and AFP described itself as "a leading advocate for the adoption of neighboring rights for the press" ([Associated Press](https://techxplore.com/news/2023-08-agence-france-presse-pursues-copyright-case.pdf)). Elon Musk called the case "bizarre" on X ([Associated Press](https://techxplore.com/news/2023-08-agence-france-presse-pursues-copyright-case.pdf)).

The agency's day-to-day work is less visible but more consequential: its wire copy is the first draft of world news in thousands of newsrooms. One example is its reporting on Cloudflare's November 2025 legal defeat in [Tokyo](/places/tokyo/): the [Tokyo District Court](/organizations/tokyo-district-court/) ordered Cloudflare to pay 500 million yen (S$4.2 million) to four major Japanese publishers — [Kodansha](/organizations/kodansha/), [Shueisha](/organizations/shueisha/), [Shogakukan](/organizations/shogakukan/) and [Kadokawa](/organizations/kadokawa/) — over servers for manga piracy sites, in a suit filed in 2022 ([Red Hot Singapore](https://www.redhot.sg/japan-court-orders-cloudflare-to-pay-4-2m-over-manga-piracy-606438.html)). The dispatch, carried by The Straits Times, reported the publishers' claim that Cloudflare provided a server for "two massive manga piracy sites that distribute over 4,000 manga titles without permission and rack up 300 million views a month", and that Cloudflare planned to appeal ([Red Hot Singapore](https://www.redhot.sg/japan-court-orders-cloudflare-to-pay-4-2m-over-manga-piracy-606438.html)).

## FAQ

**What is Agence France-Presse?** AFP is France's international news agency, headquartered in Paris and the oldest news agency in the world. It supplies text, photo, video, audio and graphics to media outlets worldwide in seven languages.

**When was AFP founded?** Its roots go to Bureau Havas, created by Charles-Louis Havas in 1832; it became Agence Havas in 1835 and took the name Agence France-Presse on 20 August 1944, during the liberation of Paris.

**Is AFP independent of the French government?** By law, yes. The statute of 10 January 1957 makes AFP a commercial business independent of the government and forbids it from falling under any ideological, political or economic control, though the government remains its largest subscription client.

**How big is AFP?** It is one of the three largest news agencies in the world, with Reuters and the Associated Press, with regional centres in Washington, Hong Kong, Nicosia and Montevideo and bureaus in 110 countries. Larger staff figures circulate but were not verified for this profile.

**Why did AFP sue X (Twitter)?** In August 2023 AFP asked a Paris court to force X to disclose data for assessing payment under EU neighbouring-rights rules, after what AFP called a "clear refusal" to negotiate over sharing its copyrighted content.
