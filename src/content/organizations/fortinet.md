## Overview

Fortinet is a Sunnyvale, California cybersecurity company whose products — headlined by the FortiGate firewall line — serve roughly 70% of the Fortune 100 and governments worldwide ([Cyber Magazine](https://cybermagazine.com/articles/ken-xie-a-masterclass-in-cybersecurity-excellence)). It went public on Nasdaq in 2009 at roughly an $800 million valuation and, as of 2024, was valued at approximately $50 billion ([Cyber Magazine](https://cybermagazine.com/articles/ken-xie-a-masterclass-in-cybersecurity-excellence)). What distinguishes it in a sector of churned founders and acquired startups is continuity: it was founded in 2000 by Ken Xie and his brother Michael Xie, and Ken has been CEO the entire time since ([Cyber Magazine](https://cybermagazine.com/articles/ken-xie-a-masterclass-in-cybersecurity-excellence)).

## Three companies, one problem

Ken Xie did not stumble into network security — he spent a career iterating on a single insight. In 1993, while at Stanford, he founded Systems Integration Solutions (SIS), a software-firewall company; by 1996 he had concluded that software firewalls running on PCs and servers would never keep up, and in a Palo Alto garage he built the first ASIC-based firewall/VPN appliance ([Cyber Magazine](https://cybermagazine.com/articles/ken-xie-a-masterclass-in-cybersecurity-excellence), [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2018/03/01/fortinets-founder-sees-network-security-bigger-picture-accelerate18/)).

That insight became his second company, NetScreen Technologies, founded in 1996 with Yan Ke and Feng Deng to build dedicated hardware systems for high-performance firewalls and VPNs ([Cyber Magazine](https://cybermagazine.com/articles/ken-xie-a-masterclass-in-cybersecurity-excellence)). NetScreen succeeded so thoroughly that Juniper Networks bought it for $4 billion ([Cyber Magazine](https://cybermagazine.com/articles/ken-xie-a-masterclass-in-cybersecurity-excellence)). Xie then started Fortinet in 2000 on a bigger thesis: that security could not remain a bolt-on point product, and had to be embedded in end-to-end computing and networking infrastructure ([Cyber Magazine](https://cybermagazine.com/articles/ken-xie-a-masterclass-in-cybersecurity-excellence)). "Each time we try to do better," Xie has said of the three ventures ([Cyber Magazine](https://cybermagazine.com/articles/ken-xie-a-masterclass-in-cybersecurity-excellence)).

## The founder who never left

Most acquired-startup founders move on; Xie built the competitor instead. As attack surfaces exploded — "data no longer sits inside a company... they go to the mobile device, they go to the cloud, they go to the internet of things" — Fortinet positioned its Security Fabric architecture around securing the entire infrastructure rather than individual points ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2018/03/01/fortinets-founder-sees-network-security-bigger-picture-accelerate18/)).

Under Xie's continuous leadership the company expanded through acquisitions including Next DLP and Lacework, pushed into cloud and AI-powered services with the FortiGate 200G series, and kept its hardware-plus-software balance while rivals like CrowdStrike and Palo Alto Networks chased cloud-only narratives ([Cyber Magazine](https://cybermagazine.com/articles/ken-xie-a-masterclass-in-cybersecurity-excellence)). A founder-led company across a quarter-century in cybersecurity is rare; at Fortinet it is the operating model ([Cyber Magazine](https://cybermagazine.com/articles/ken-xie-a-masterclass-in-cybersecurity-excellence)).
