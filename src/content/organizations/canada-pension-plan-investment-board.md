## Overview

The Canada Pension Plan Investment Board — operating as CPP Investments — is the professional investment manager that invests the Canada Pension Plan Fund on behalf of more than 22 million contributors and beneficiaries ([CPP Investments](https://www.cppinvestments.com/contact-us/)). Created by an Act of Parliament in 1997 and deliberately set apart from government — the Act states the Board "is not an agent of Her Majesty" — it invests globally for maximum return without undue risk of loss, and at 31 March 2026 the Fund totalled C$793.3 billion in net assets ([Government of Canada](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-8.3/page-1.html?txthl=on); [CPP Investments](https://www.cppinvestments.com/newsroom/cpp-investments-net-assets-total-793-3-billion-at-2026-fiscal-year-end/)).

Since it began investing in 1999 it has earned C$549 billion in cumulative net income, and its 10-year annualised net return stands at 8.8% ([CPP Investments](https://www.cppinvestments.com/newsroom/cpp-investments-net-assets-total-793-3-billion-at-2026-fiscal-year-end/)). Its relationship with [Axoni](/organizations/axoni/) is often misdescribed: CPPIB was a participant in Axoni's 2017 pilot of blockchain infrastructure for equity swaps, not an investor in the company ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/industry/working-group-tests-blockchain-system-for-equity-swaps/)).

## A corporation designed to be at arm's length

The Board's design is written into the statute that created it. The 1997 Act establishes the corporation and declares it is not an agent of Her Majesty, its directors, officers and employees are not part of the federal public administration, and a 12-director board manages it ([Government of Canada](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-8.3/page-1.html?txthl=on)). Section 5 states its objects plainly — to help the CPP meet its obligations, to manage transferred amounts in the best interests of contributors and beneficiaries, and to invest for maximum return without undue risk of loss ([Government of Canada](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-8.3/page-1.html?txthl=on)). The Fund is built for the long term: because the CPP serves multiple generations, the organisation judges performance over extended periods rather than single years, and it measures itself against benchmark portfolios built from public-market indexes ([CPP Investments](https://www.cppinvestments.com/newsroom/cpp-investments-net-assets-total-793-3-billion-at-2026-fiscal-year-end/)).

## Two accounts, one fund

The Fund is really two accounts with different designs. The base CPP account — C$712.9 billion at March 2026 — holds the original plan's money, while the additional CPP account — C$80.4 billion — holds the enhanced CPP contributions that began in 2019, with a different funding profile and a lower market-risk target ([CPP Investments](https://www.cppinvestments.com/newsroom/cpp-investments-net-assets-total-793-3-billion-at-2026-fiscal-year-end/)). The split shows up in performance: in fiscal 2026 the base account returned 8.0% against 5.4% for the additional account, and the additional account is expected to keep growing much faster because its net contributions are larger ([CPP Investments](https://www.cppinvestments.com/newsroom/cpp-investments-net-assets-total-793-3-billion-at-2026-fiscal-year-end/)). Geographically the Fund is mostly invested outside Canada — 47% in the United States, 12% in Canada, 19% in Europe, 17% in Asia-Pacific and 5% in Latin America at March 2025 — though its Canadian investments still totalled C$119.2 billion a year later ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/research-and-markets/cpp-investments-earned-9-3-net-return-in-latest-fiscal-year/); [CPP Investments](https://www.cppinvestments.com/newsroom/cpp-investments-net-assets-total-793-3-billion-at-2026-fiscal-year-end/)).

## The Axoni pilot — and a correction

CPPIB's link to Axoni runs through a working group, not a cap table. In November 2017 Axoni and eleven firms completed a months-long pilot of peer-to-peer blockchain infrastructure for equity swaps on Axoni's AxCore software, with CPPIB alongside [Goldman Sachs](/organizations/goldman-sachs/), [J.P. Morgan](/organizations/jpmorgan-chase/), [Citi](/organizations/citigroup/) and [Credit Suisse](/organizations/credit-suisse/) ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/industry/working-group-tests-blockchain-system-for-equity-swaps/)). The aim was fewer valuation disputes, less reconciliation and real-time data access ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/industry/working-group-tests-blockchain-system-for-equity-swaps/)).

CPPIB does not appear on Axoni's 2018 Series B investor list, which names HSBC, Goldman Sachs, Andreessen Horowitz, Citi, Coatue, Franklin Templeton, J.P. Morgan, Wells Fargo and Y Combinator — so calling it an Axoni investor would be wrong. The Fund's record carries a tension: in fiscal 2026 it earned C$56.9 billion yet lagged its benchmark by 5.4 points, because concentrated technology stocks drove the benchmark ([CPP Investments](https://www.cppinvestments.com/newsroom/cpp-investments-net-assets-total-793-3-billion-at-2026-fiscal-year-end/)). The implication is that the Fund trades single-year upside for resilience — the logic behind its 8.8% ten-year return and the proposed contribution-rate cut from 9.9% to 9.5% ([CPP Investments](https://www.cppinvestments.com/newsroom/cpp-investments-net-assets-total-793-3-billion-at-2026-fiscal-year-end/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is CPP Investments?**
CPP Investments is the operating name of the Canada Pension Plan Investment Board, created by Parliament in 1997 to invest the Canada Pension Plan Fund for more than 22 million contributors and beneficiaries ([Government of Canada](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-8.3/page-1.html?txthl=on); [CPP Investments](https://www.cppinvestments.com/contact-us/)). It began investing in 1999 and has since earned C$549 billion in cumulative net income ([CPP Investments](https://www.cppinvestments.com/newsroom/cpp-investments-net-assets-total-793-3-billion-at-2026-fiscal-year-end/)).

**Is CPP Investments part of the Government of Canada?**
No. The 1997 Act establishes it as a corporation that is expressly not an agent of Her Majesty, and its directors, officers and employees are not part of the federal public administration ([Government of Canada](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-8.3/page-1.html?txthl=on)). A 12-director board manages it, under a legislated mandate to invest for maximum return without undue risk of loss ([Government of Canada](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-8.3/page-1.html?txthl=on)).

**How big is the CPP Fund?**
At 31 March 2026 the Fund totalled C$793.3 billion in net assets, after C$56.9 billion of net income in fiscal 2026 and a 7.8% net return ([CPP Investments](https://www.cppinvestments.com/newsroom/cpp-investments-net-assets-total-793-3-billion-at-2026-fiscal-year-end/)). That was up from C$714.4 billion a year earlier, with the base CPP account at C$712.9 billion and the additional CPP account at C$80.4 billion ([CPP Investments](https://www.cppinvestments.com/newsroom/cpp-investments-net-assets-total-793-3-billion-at-2026-fiscal-year-end/)).

**Did CPPIB invest in Axoni?**
No — that is a common misstatement. CPPIB took part in Axoni's 2017 pilot of blockchain infrastructure for equity swaps, alongside Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan, Citi and Credit Suisse, but it does not appear on Axoni's 2018 Series B investor list ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/industry/working-group-tests-blockchain-system-for-equity-swaps/)).

**Why did CPP Investments drop its net-zero pledge?**
When releasing its fiscal 2025 annual report it dropped the net-zero-by-2050 commitment, citing recent Canadian legal developments around how net-zero claims are interpreted, while saying the substance of its climate work was unchanged ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/research-and-markets/cpp-investments-earned-9-3-net-return-in-latest-fiscal-year/)). Chief executive John Graham said sustainability still matters for a long-horizon investor ([Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/research-and-markets/cpp-investments-earned-9-3-net-return-in-latest-fiscal-year/)).
