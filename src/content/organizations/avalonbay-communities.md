## Overview

AvalonBay Communities, Inc. was an American apartment real estate investment trust that developed, owned and operated upscale apartment communities in coastal U.S. markets, and it ceased to exist as a standalone company on August 17, 2026, when it merged with [Equity Residential](/organizations/equity-residential/) to form Vivmark Residential. At the start of 2026 it owned or held interests in 292 operating communities containing 88,768 apartment homes ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/915912/000110465926039983/tm262436d2_ars.pdf); [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://investors.avalonbay.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001104659-26-097833/tm2623381d1_8k.htm)).

This profile covers that company — a Maryland corporation headquartered in Arlington, Virginia, whose shares traded on the New York Stock Exchange as AVB and which belonged to the S&P 500 — not the AvalonBay brand names that survive inside Vivmark ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://investors.avalonbay.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001104659-26-064784/0001104659-26-064784.pdf)). Its story runs from a San Jose developer founded in 1978, through a 1998 merger that created the second-largest apartment REIT in the [United States](/places/united-states/), to a 2026 merger of equals that ended its independent existence after 28 years — a deal Commercial Observer called the largest public REIT merger ever, at roughly $71 billion of enterprise value ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/avalonbay-communities-inc); [Commercial Observer](https://www.commercialobserver.com/2026/08/equity-residential-avalonbay-communities-inside-71b-mega-multifamily-merger/)). The new name, Vivmark Residential, was announced in late July 2026, and the successor company reported more than 185,000 apartment homes in its first quarterly results, winning an S&P Global Ratings upgrade to 'A' the day after the merger closed ([Commercial Observer](https://www.commercialobserver.com/2026/08/equity-residential-avalonbay-communities-inside-71b-mega-multifamily-merger/); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261104847582/en/Vivmark-Residential-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results-and-Provides-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2026-Guidance)).

## Two predecessor REITs, one bicoastal company

AvalonBay was born from two companies created in the early-1990s REIT boom. Avalon Properties grew out of the Trammell Crow Residential Mid-Atlantic and Northeast groups, led by Richard L. Michaux as chairman and chief executive and Chuck Berman as president and chief operating officer; its August 24, 1993 IPO raised $400 million on a portfolio of 22 communities and 7,044 homes ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/avalonbay-communities-inc)). The other half came from the West Coast: Bay Apartment Communities was formed as a REIT in 1994, assuming the assets of San Jose-based Greenbriar Development Company, founded in 1978 by Gilbert "Mike" Meyer, and its IPO raised more than $200 million ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/avalonbay-communities-inc)). Avalon kept expanding first, paying $196 million in 1997 for Trammell Crow Residential-Midwest properties in Chicago, Minneapolis, St. Louis, Cincinnati and Indianapolis ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/avalonbay-communities-inc)). Then, in 1998, the two REITs merged in a $2 billion deal, and the combined company — renamed AvalonBay Communities, with 40,506 units — instantly became the country's second-largest apartment REIT, behind only Equity Residential ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/avalonbay-communities-inc)).

## Scale, brands and the development engine

AvalonBay's strategy was to own luxury apartments where building is hardest: New England, the [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) metropolitan area, the Mid-Atlantic, the Pacific Northwest and Northern and Southern California, with newer expansion regions in Raleigh-Durham, Charlotte, Southeast Florida, Dallas, Austin and Denver ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/915912/000110465926039983/tm262436d2_ars.pdf)). It sold that strategy through four brands — Avalon for upscale living, AVA for smaller transit-served apartments aimed partly at roommates, eaves by Avalon for the cost-conscious suburban "value" segment, and Kanso, a moderate-price, low-amenity, largely self-service product ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/915912/000110465926039983/tm262436d2_ars.pdf)). Development, not just acquisition, was its engine: in 2025 it acquired 12 communities for $842.0 million, sold nine for $811.7 million, completed four developments and started construction on 11 more, ending the year with 24 developments under construction ([AvalonBay Communities](https://investors.avalonbay.com/_assets/_69c2703b54c379c8196355b13e781a57/avalonbay/db/2294/21791/earnings_release/Q4+2025+Earnings+Release.pdf)). Core FFO per share rose 2.1% to $11.24 in 2025, while same-store residential revenue grew 2.5% and net operating income 1.9% ([AvalonBay Communities](https://investors.avalonbay.com/_assets/_69c2703b54c379c8196355b13e781a57/avalonbay/db/2294/21791/earnings_release/Q4+2025+Earnings+Release.pdf)) — steady cash-earnings growth in a soft rental year, which let the company enter 2026 from operating strength and return nearly $1.5 billion to shareholders ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/915912/000110465926039983/tm262436d2_ars.pdf)).

## The 2026 merger into Vivmark Residential

The end came fast. On May 20, 2026 AvalonBay and Equity Residential signed an all-stock merger of equals under which each AvalonBay share converted into 2.793 shares of the combined company, leaving former AvalonBay holders with about 51.2% and Equity Residential holders with about 48.8% ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://investors.avalonbay.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001104659-26-064784/0001104659-26-064784.pdf)). The deal promised a pro forma equity market capitalisation of about $52 billion and an enterprise value of about $69 billion, with more than 180,000 rental apartments and $175 million of gross synergies, with Stephen E. Sterrett chairing the board and AvalonBay chief executive Benjamin W. Schall as chief executive ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://investors.avalonbay.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001104659-26-064784/0001104659-26-064784.pdf)). Goldman Sachs led AvalonBay's advisers, with [Wells Fargo](/organizations/wells-fargo/) also advising ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://investors.avalonbay.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001104659-26-064784/0001104659-26-064784.pdf)). The logic was shared scale: roughly 95% of the two portfolios overlapped, mainly in New York, San Francisco, Boston and Washington, D.C. ([Commercial Observer](https://www.commercialobserver.com/2026/08/equity-residential-avalonbay-communities-inside-71b-mega-multifamily-merger/)). The merger closed on August 17, 2026; AvalonBay's shares were delisted that morning, Vivmark began trading as VMRK the next day, and expected annual net run-rate operating synergies were later raised to $135 million by March 2028 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://investors.avalonbay.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001104659-26-097833/tm2623381d1_8k.htm); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261104847582/en/Vivmark-Residential-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results-and-Provides-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2026-Guidance)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is AvalonBay still a public company?** No. AvalonBay Communities ceased to exist on August 17, 2026, when it merged into a subsidiary of Equity Residential, which then renamed itself Vivmark Residential ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://investors.avalonbay.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001104659-26-097833/tm2623381d1_8k.htm)). Its AVB shares were removed from New York Stock Exchange trading that morning, and Vivmark shares began trading as VMRK on August 18, 2026 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://investors.avalonbay.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001104659-26-097833/tm2623381d1_8k.htm)).

**What did AvalonBay shareholders receive in the merger?** Each AvalonBay share converted into 2.793 shares of the combined company, plus cash for any fractional share ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://investors.avalonbay.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001104659-26-064784/0001104659-26-064784.pdf)). Former AvalonBay shareholders owned about 51.2% of Vivmark Residential and former Equity Residential shareholders about 48.8%, on a fully diluted basis ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://investors.avalonbay.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001104659-26-064784/0001104659-26-064784.pdf)).

**What brands did AvalonBay operate?** Four: Avalon for upscale apartment living with high-end amenities; AVA for smaller apartments in high-energy, transit-served neighbourhoods, many designed for roommate living; eaves by Avalon for the cost-conscious suburban value segment; and Kanso, a moderate-price, low-amenity, largely self-service product using technology and smart access ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/915912/000110465926039983/tm262436d2_ars.pdf)).

**What was the Archstone deal?** In 2012, AvalonBay and Equity Residential jointly bought the Archstone apartment portfolio from Tishman Speyer and Lehman Brothers Holdings for $16 billion, splitting it 60-40: Equity Residential took 23,110 units and AvalonBay took 22,222 ([Commercial Observer](https://www.commercialobserver.com/2026/08/equity-residential-avalonbay-communities-inside-71b-mega-multifamily-merger/)). The two companies had worked together successfully on that purchase, which analysts later cited as a seed of the 2026 merger ([Commercial Observer](https://www.commercialobserver.com/2026/08/equity-residential-avalonbay-communities-inside-71b-mega-multifamily-merger/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not date AvalonBay's founding to 1978 without qualification: Greenbriar Development Company was founded in 1978, but Bay Apartment Communities was formed as a REIT in 1994 and AvalonBay itself only in the 1998 merger, and a shorthand that says Meyer "formed Bay Apartment Communities" in 1978 is flagged rather than adopted ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/avalonbay-communities-inc); [Commercial Observer](https://www.commercialobserver.com/2026/08/equity-residential-avalonbay-communities-inside-71b-mega-multifamily-merger/)). It does not merge the merger's enterprise-value figures into one number: about $69 billion pro forma at announcement and roughly $71 billion as described at closing come from different dates and bases ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://investors.avalonbay.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001104659-26-064784/0001104659-26-064784.pdf); [Commercial Observer](https://www.commercialobserver.com/2026/08/equity-residential-avalonbay-communities-inside-71b-mega-multifamily-merger/)). It does not treat the $175 million gross synergy figure and the later $135 million net run-rate figure as the same measure ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://investors.avalonbay.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001104659-26-064784/0001104659-26-064784.pdf); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261104847582/en/Vivmark-Residential-Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Results-and-Provides-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2026-Guidance)). It does not date the headquarters move from Alexandria to Arlington, Virginia, which was not verified ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/avalonbay-communities-inc); [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://investors.avalonbay.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001104659-26-064784/0001104659-26-064784.pdf)). It does not state the date of the shareholder votes approving the merger, which was not verified from a source read in full here. Finally, it does not claim the Avalon, AVA, eaves by Avalon or Kanso brands disappeared with the company — the brands were operating assets of the combined Vivmark Residential, and this profile covers only the former standalone company ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/915912/000110465926039983/tm262436d2_ars.pdf)).
