## Overview

Mindbody is a software company for the wellness industry — booking and business-management tools for fitness studios, gyms, salons and spas, plus a consumer marketplace for finding and booking classes and appointments. It was founded in 2000 from co-founder Rick Stollmeyer's garage in San Luis Obispo, California, went public on the NASDAQ in June 2015, and was taken private by Vista Equity Partners for about $1.9 billion in early 2019 ([SEC Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1458962/000119312518357270/d656877dex991.htm)). In October 2021 it acquired ClassPass, the fitness-subscription marketplace, in an all-stock deal ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/13/mindbody-acquires-classpass-in-all-stock-deal-and-secures-500-million-investment/?ref=laborcapital.co)). This is the wellness-software company (NASDAQ: MB, 2015–2019), not the Mindbody Green media company and not any generic "mind body" wellness brand.

## From a garage to the cloud

Blake Beltram had written software for boutique fitness studios when Rick Stollmeyer — a former Navy nuclear submarine officer who had studied integrated technology management at Cal Poly — joined him, and the two "ended up founding Mindbody from my garage in 2000", with a consumer marketplace vision from the start ([StrengthPortal](http://strengthportal.com/blog/interview-with-mindbody-ceo-rick-stollmeyer/)). The early product was desktop software: OmSoft, and PilatesSoft for Pilates studios ([StrengthPortal](http://strengthportal.com/blog/interview-with-mindbody-ceo-rick-stollmeyer/)).

The product evolved in a clear sequence. Multi-location data synchronisation arrived in 2002, the first web scheduling tool in 2003, and in early 2004 the team decided to rebuild as cloud software — inspired by QuickBooks Online — with a beta in the fourth quarter of 2004 and the commercial release of Mindbody Online in February 2005 ([StrengthPortal](http://strengthportal.com/blog/interview-with-mindbody-ceo-rick-stollmeyer/)). The move from desktop to cloud is the founding technical decision of the company: it turned a tool sold to individual studios into a platform that could hold the whole boutique-fitness market.

## Public, then private: the IPO and the Vista sale

Mindbody went public in June 2015, and the IPO brought in over $100 million in capital ([MarketRealist](https://marketrealist.com/p/is-mindbody-publicly-traded/)). The stock fell almost immediately — down 17% in the first weeks — and Stollmeyer later said he was "waking up in the middle of the night asking, 'Why the hell did I take this company public?'" ([MarketRealist](https://marketrealist.com/p/is-mindbody-publicly-traded/)). Within weeks of the listing, Stollmeyer bought $186,000 of the company's stock at the low price to minimise losses ([MarketRealist](https://marketrealist.com/p/is-mindbody-publicly-traded/)). TechCrunch had described the company as "a bit like the OpenTable of the fitness world" — a booking layer for an industry that had never had one ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/13/mindbody-acquires-classpass-in-all-stock-deal-and-secures-500-million-investment/?ref=laborcapital.co)).

By 2018 the company was spending heavily to grow. Revenue reached $182.6 million in 2017, with about 1,500 employees worldwide, and in 2018 it bought FitMetrix for $15.3 million and Booker Software for $150 million; in the third quarter of 2018 the net loss widened to $17.2 million, from $3.6 million a year earlier, on revenue of $63.8 million, up 37% ([CFO.com](https://www.cfo.com/news/mindbody-to-be-sold-to-vista-equity-for-19b/658308/)). CFO.com's reporting ties the two together: the acquisitions pushed the company "further and further away from the black" ([CFO.com](https://www.cfo.com/news/mindbody-to-be-sold-to-vista-equity-for-19b/658308/)).

The sale followed weeks later. On 24 December 2018 Mindbody signed a definitive agreement under which Vista Equity Partners would acquire all outstanding shares for $36.50 per share in cash, about $1.9 billion in total — a 68% premium to the unaffected closing price on 21 December 2018 — with unanimous board approval, a 30-day go-shop period and an expected close in the first quarter of 2019 ([SEC Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1458962/000119312518357270/d656877dex991.htm)). Qatalyst advised Mindbody on finance and Cooley on law; Kirkland & Ellis advised Vista ([SEC Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1458962/000119312518357270/d656877dex991.htm)). The acquisition closed in early 2019 and Mindbody was taken private ([CFO.com](https://www.cfo.com/news/mindbody-to-be-sold-to-vista-equity-for-19b/658308/)).

## The ClassPass roll-up

On 13 October 2021 Mindbody acquired ClassPass, the subscription-based fitness marketplace founded in 2013 and most recently valued at $1 billion, in an all-stock deal with the terms undisclosed, alongside a $500 million investment led by Sixth Street ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/13/mindbody-acquires-classpass-in-all-stock-deal-and-secures-500-million-investment/?ref=laborcapital.co), [Pacific Coast Business Times](https://www.pacbiztimes.com/2021/10/13/mindbody-to-buy-billion-dollar-fitness-company-fueling-talk-of-new-ipo/)). Then-chief executive Josh McCarter described the deal as all stock, with the terms undisclosed ([MarketRealist](https://marketrealist.com/p/is-mindbody-publicly-traded/)). ClassPass kept its brand and there was no forced migration of its studios to Mindbody's software; about one-third of ClassPass studios used no booking software at all, which was part of the point — the deal bought Mindbody a distribution channel into studios it did not already serve ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/13/mindbody-acquires-classpass-in-all-stock-deal-and-secures-500-million-investment/?ref=laborcapital.co)). ClassPass chief executive Fritz Lanman became Mindbody's president ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/13/mindbody-acquires-classpass-in-all-stock-deal-and-secures-500-million-investment/?ref=laborcapital.co)).

Bloomberg had reported merger talks since May 2021 and the possibility of an IPO after closing; Stollmeyer, then executive chairman, said the company was not discussing an IPO "at the moment" and could remain private indefinitely ([Pacific Coast Business Times](https://www.pacbiztimes.com/2021/10/13/mindbody-to-buy-billion-dollar-fitness-company-fueling-talk-of-new-ipo/)). The arc is the profile's central thread: a garage startup that became a cloud platform, went public, found the public market hostile, was bought out at a large premium, and then used private ownership to absorb the largest marketplace in its own market.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Mindbody publicly traded?**
No. It went public in June 2015 and was taken private by Vista Equity Partners for about $1.9 billion in early 2019 ([SEC Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1458962/000119312518357270/d656877dex991.htm)).

**Who owns Mindbody?**
Vista Equity Partners, which bought all outstanding shares at $36.50 each, a 68% premium ([SEC Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1458962/000119312518357270/d656877dex991.htm)).

**What does Mindbody do?**
It sells booking and business-management software to fitness, wellness, beauty and spa businesses, and runs a consumer marketplace for finding and booking classes and appointments ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/13/mindbody-acquires-classpass-in-all-stock-deal-and-secures-500-million-investment/?ref=laborcapital.co)).

**What is Mindbody's relationship with ClassPass?**
Mindbody acquired ClassPass on 13 October 2021 in an all-stock deal; ClassPass kept its brand and its studios were not forced onto Mindbody's software ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/13/mindbody-acquires-classpass-in-all-stock-deal-and-secures-500-million-investment/?ref=laborcapital.co)).

**Is Mindbody the same as Mindbody Green?**
No. Mindbody is the wellness-software company (NASDAQ: MB, 2015–2019); Mindbody Green is a separate media company. The spelling in SEC filings is "MINDBODY"; current branding uses "Mindbody".
