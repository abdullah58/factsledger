## Overview

TikTok is a short-video platform owned by ByteDance, the Beijing-based company that launched it in [China](/places/china/) as Douyin in September 2016 and internationally as TikTok in 2017. It became the global app most people recognise after ByteDance folded the lip-sync app Musical.ly into it on 2 August 2018, keeping the TikTok name.

The app's centrepiece is the "For You" feed, a personalised stream chosen by a proprietary recommendation algorithm that ByteDance owns and develops in China ([Supreme Court](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/24-656)). The platform has accumulated more than 170 million users in the [United States](/places/united-states/) and more than one billion worldwide, the US Supreme Court recorded in January 2025, and in 2023 its American users uploaded more than 5.5 billion videos, viewed over 13 trillion times ([Supreme Court](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/24-656)). Since 22 January 2026 its American business has been run by TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC, a majority-American company in which ByteDance keeps a 19.9% stake ([Associated Press](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-01-tiktok-american-entity.html)). TikTok is also one of the companies reported as a client of [Brex](/organizations/brex/).

## From Douyin to a global app

ByteDance built Douyin for China first. Its original head, Kelly Zhang, described it as a music short-video community for young people, designed around full-screen, high-definition video, with music acting as a filter that made clips more expressive ([Pandaily](https://archive.pandaily.com/creator-of-douyin-tiktok-how-we-created-a-product-with-a-billion-views-a-day-in-18-months-part-i/)). The app launched in September 2016 under the name A.me and took the Douyin name that December; its slogan later changed, in March 2018, to "record your beautiful life" ([Pandaily](https://archive.pandaily.com/creator-of-douyin-tiktok-how-we-created-a-product-with-a-billion-views-a-day-in-18-months-part-i/)). ByteDance launched TikTok as the separate international version in 2017, and the following November it bought Musical.ly, the lip-sync app with about 60 million users, most of them in the US, for a reported $800 million to $1 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/11/09/chinas-toutiao-is-buying-musical-ly-in-a-deal-worth-800m-1b/?ref=pennyfractions.ghost.io)).

On 2 August 2018 ByteDance merged Musical.ly into TikTok, moving its accounts, videos and settings into the TikTok app; Musical.ly had about 100 million users and TikTok claimed 500 million monthly active users outside China, while Douyin stayed separate in China ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/08/02/musically-tiktok/)). Growth brought regulators with it: in February 2019 the company paid $5.7 million to settle US Federal Trade Commission allegations that Musical.ly had illegally collected children's personal information, then the largest children's-privacy penalty the agency had obtained ([Federal Trade Commission](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2019/02/video-social-networking-app-musically-agrees-settle-ftc)). In June 2020 India — TikTok's biggest overseas market, with more than 200 million monthly users there — banned the app along with 58 other Chinese apps on national-security grounds ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/06/29/india-bans-tiktok-dozens-of-other-chinese-apps/)).

## The American divestiture: a sale that licenses the algorithm

The American fight began in August 2020, when President Trump ordered ByteDance to divest its US TikTok interests; courts blocked the move, and two years of negotiations on a security agreement ended without a deal ([Supreme Court](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/24-656)). Congress then passed the Protecting Americans from Foreign Adversary Controlled Applications Act, signed on 24 April 2024, which made it unlawful to distribute, maintain or update the app in the US unless its American operation was severed from Chinese control; the prohibitions took effect on 19 January 2025 ([Supreme Court](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/24-656)). Two days earlier the Supreme Court unanimously upheld the law, holding that it did not violate the First Amendment ([Supreme Court](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/24-656)). The app went dark for American users for several hours around the deadline, then returned as executive orders paused enforcement through 2025 while a sale was negotiated ([Associated Press](https://www.wvlt.tv/2025/12/18/tiktok-signs-deal-sell-us-unit-american-investors-including-oracle-silver-lake/)).

The sale that emerged is not a clean break. Binding agreements signed in December 2025 created TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC, which took over the US business on 22 January 2026 under chief executive Adam Presser and a seven-member, majority-American board that includes TikTok chief executive Shou Zi Chew ([Associated Press](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-01-tiktok-american-entity.html)). American user data sits in a system run by Oracle, and the recommendation algorithm is to be retrained on US user data ([Associated Press](https://www.wvlt.tv/2025/12/18/tiktok-signs-deal-sell-us-unit-american-investors-including-oracle-silver-lake/)). But ByteDance would license the algorithm to the new entity, and the 2024 law bars any cooperation with it over a content recommendation algorithm — so, as the Associated Press put it, it is unclear how ByteDance's continued involvement will play out ([Associated Press](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-01-tiktok-american-entity.html)).

Brussels is a second front: on 6 November 2026 the European Commission preliminarily found that TikTok's For You feed breaches the [European Union](/organizations/european-union/)'s Digital Services Act, because the recommender system can pull users into "rabbit holes" of repeated content about sadness, isolation or body image, and the company's safeguards were judged ineffective ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-preliminarily-finds-tiktoks-you-feed-breach-digital-services-act-harmful-rabbit-holes)). The Commission says the feed's design must change — for example, by relying more on users' explicit preferences and disabling viewing time as a recommendation signal ([EU Reporter](https://www.eureporter.co/politics/european-commission/2026/11/10/commission-preliminarily-finds-tiktoks-for-you-feed-in-breach-of-digital-services-act-for-harmful-rabbit-holes-effect/)). The findings are preliminary, not a decision, and a confirmed breach could bring a fine of up to 6% of worldwide annual turnover ([EU Reporter](https://www.eureporter.co/politics/european-commission/2026/11/10/commission-preliminarily-finds-tiktoks-for-you-feed-in-breach-of-digital-services-act-for-harmful-rabbit-holes-effect/)).

## Commerce and the creator economy

TikTok stopped being only a video feed in September 2023, when TikTok Shop fully launched in the United States with a Shop Tab marketplace, affiliate videos that let creators earn commissions on products they feature, and a logistics arm, Fulfilled by TikTok, that stores and ships merchants' goods ([Associated Press](https://biztimes.biz/tiktok-shop-launches-in-the-u-s-as-the-company-bets-big-on-e-commerce/)). The company pointed to trends such as #TikTokMadeMeBuyIt, in which users' recommendations sent products flying off shelves, and said more than 150 million Americans then used the app ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-tiktok-shop?lang=en)). The model copies Douyin, where ByteDance already ran a thriving social marketplace ([Associated Press](https://biztimes.biz/tiktok-shop-launches-in-the-u-s-as-the-company-bets-big-on-e-commerce/)). TikTok has also become a news source: about 43% of US adults under 30 regularly get news from TikTok, more than from any other social app including YouTube, Facebook and Instagram, according to a Pew Research Center report published in autumn 2025 ([Associated Press](https://www.wvlt.tv/2025/12/18/tiktok-signs-deal-sell-us-unit-american-investors-including-oracle-silver-lake/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who owns TikTok?**
ByteDance owns TikTok globally. Since 22 January 2026 its US business has been run by TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC, in which Oracle, Silver Lake and MGX hold 15% each and ByteDance keeps 19.9% ([Associated Press](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-01-tiktok-american-entity.html)). The recommendation algorithm itself is licensed from ByteDance, not transferred.

**When was TikTok launched?**
The product began as Douyin in China in September 2016 and launched internationally as TikTok in 2017; the global app most people recognise dates from 2 August 2018, when ByteDance merged Musical.ly into TikTok ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/08/02/musically-tiktok/)). All three dates describe different stages of the same product.

**Is TikTok banned in the United States?**
No. A 2024 law required ByteDance to divest its US business or face removal from app stores; the Supreme Court upheld it in January 2025 and the app went dark for several hours ([Supreme Court](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/24-656)). A joint venture took over the US business in January 2026, though whether that satisfies the law is still questioned.

**What is TikTok Shop?**
TikTok's e-commerce feature, fully launched in the US in September 2023. It puts shoppable videos and live streams in the For You feed, lets creators earn commissions through an affiliate programme, and offers Fulfilled by TikTok logistics for merchants ([Associated Press](https://biztimes.biz/tiktok-shop-launches-in-the-u-s-as-the-company-bets-big-on-e-commerce/)).

**Why is the EU investigating TikTok?**
Under the Digital Services Act, the European Commission preliminarily found in November 2026 that the For You feed's "rabbit hole effect" breaches the rules, because it can repeatedly serve content about sadness, isolation and body image ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-preliminarily-finds-tiktoks-you-feed-breach-digital-services-act-harmful-rabbit-holes)). The findings are preliminary and TikTok may reply before any decision.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not settle TikTok's launch year to a single date, because the sources disagree: the Supreme Court says the platform launched in 2017, while TechCrunch says it was created in 2016 and Pandaily dates Douyin to September 2016. It treats 2016 as the product origin and 2017 as the international launch. It does not state a single US user count, because the Supreme Court and AP put it at 170 million-plus while AP's January 2026 report says more than 200 million Americans, with no explanation of the difference in the sources read. It does not state a current global user figure: third-party aggregators range from 1.12 billion to 1.94 billion for 2025 under incompatible definitions. It does not state that the January 2026 joint venture satisfies the 2024 law — the law bars algorithm cooperation with ByteDance, yet the deal licenses the algorithm from ByteDance, and AP itself says it is unclear how that plays out. It does not state a reported US$10 billion payment to the Trump administration or a ~$14 billion valuation of the US entity, neither of which was verified from a fully-read tier1 or tier2 source. It does not state the September 25, 2025 executive order's qualified-divestiture determination as a dated fact, for the same reason. The Musical.ly price is a reported range, never a disclosed figure, and the exact Douyin launch day is not stated because only Wikipedia-derived pages give it.
