## Overview

South Park Cafe was a French bistro at 108 S. Park St in [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/)'s South Park neighborhood, founded in 1985 and a fixture of the SoMa dining scene for three decades. After closing in 2017, it was bought and relaunched in September 2019 by [Brex](/organizations/brex/)'s 23-year-old co-CEOs, then closed again in March 2020 as [COVID-19](/events/covid-19-pandemic/) arrived, and had still not reopened by 2026.

The café's story is really two stories sharing one address. For 32 years it was a neighbourhood French bistro, opened by a coffee merchant and kept for most of its life by a chef-owner who had cooked in France. Then, for about six months, it became one of the strangest footnotes in startup history: a beloved restaurant bought by two 23-year-olds who had never run one, as a marketing experiment for a fintech company valued at $2.6 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/); [InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/food/the-5-best-restaurants-that-opened-in-the-bay-area-this-september)). The experiment ended when the pandemic emptied the city, and the room has stayed dark ever since ([The Dissent](https://thedissentsf.com/article/the-last-table-at-south-park)).

## The original café, 1985–2017

Bob Voorhees, a coffee merchant, opened the upscale South Park Cafe in June 1985, brought into the restaurant business by his partner Catherine Allswang; a later partner, Katherine Hagen, also helped open the neighbouring Ristorante Ecco at 101 South Park ([FoundSF](https://www.foundsf.org/index.php?title=THE_REAL_SOUTH_PARK)). The café embodied the neighbourhood's story, as the design press later put it: a park modelled on a London square, ringed by a curved line of buildings, with a restaurant that had been there since the area's 1980s revival ([Hospitality Snapshots](https://hospitalitysnapshots.com/projects/9216/brex-lounge-south-park-cafe/)).

The face of the café for most of its life was Ward Little, who had held the kitchen since 1993 — a chef-owner who had toured and cooked in France and kept the menu deliberately old-fashioned ([tablehopper](https://www.tablehopper.com/regular/south-park-cafe/)). It was a homey little French bistro built around two dishes regulars ordered again and again: the pig salad, slow-cooked pork confit with green apple and shallots in mustard dressing, and steak frites, a grilled Angus New York steak with red wine sauce, alongside a $32 prix-fixe dinner and a full bar ([tablehopper](https://www.tablehopper.com/regular/south-park-cafe/)). Lunch service came and went with the neighbourhood's fortunes — table service vanished after the dot-com bust, lunch disappeared entirely for a time, then returned as counter ordering as the area picked up again ([tablehopper](https://www.tablehopper.com/regular/south-park-cafe/)). After more than 30 years, the original café shuttered in 2017, following the owners' retirement ([InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/food/the-5-best-restaurants-that-opened-in-the-bay-area-this-september)).

## Brex's revival, 2019–2020

The revival came from an unlikely direction. In 2019, Brex's then-23-year-old co-CEOs, Henrique Dubugras and Pedro Franceschi — Brazilian entrepreneurs who had dropped out of [Stanford University](/universities/stanford-university/) as freshmen to join [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/)'s winter 2017 batch and found Brex — bought the beloved café, despite never having run a restaurant ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)). Their idea was that Brex cardmembers would dine downstairs before heading upstairs to the company's exclusive members-only lounge, the Oval Room, which sat directly above the café ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)). The relaunched café, listed among the Bay Area's notable September 2019 openings, served gluten-free fried chicken with collard greens, McFarland Spring cured trout with olive oil and crème fraîche, and steak frites with béarnaise aioli ([InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/food/the-5-best-restaurants-that-opened-in-the-bay-area-this-september)).

Gensler redesigned the 3,000 sq ft space to share a design language with the lounge upstairs, with oval detailing as a nod to the park's form, an open street frontage, inset oval millwork, a continuous bar, custom seating made in the Mission district, custom floor tiles, repurposed light fixtures and the original storefront windows; upstairs, furniture organised the casual lounge and private event space ([Hospitality Snapshots](https://hospitalitysnapshots.com/projects/9216/brex-lounge-south-park-cafe/)). The café doubled as a co-working space as well as a real restaurant serving lunch and dinner, with 20 to 30 employees at a time when Brex itself had only about 50 ([The Spl.it](https://www.thespl.it/p/the-ai-native-gtm-playbook-sam-blond)). Brex still owned the café in March 2020, when the company was best known for its startup corporate cards and backed by Kleiner Perkins, Y Combinator and [Peter Thiel](/people/peter-thiel/) ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/startups/brex-buys-three-san-francisco-startups-to-complement-its-product-line/)).

## Closure in March 2020

The revival lasted about six months. In the week of 12 March 2020, an employee of South Park Cafe tested positive for COVID-19; TechCrunch described the café at the time as "a popular hub for techies and venture capitalists run by the credit card startup Brex," a few hundred feet from high-profile venture firms including Kleiner Perkins ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/03/12/doing-deals-through-zoom-these-investors-have-some-tips/)). The café pulled its shutters in March 2020 ([The Dissent](https://thedissentsf.com/article/the-last-table-at-south-park)). COVID-19 then shut down most of San Francisco for over a year — timing that TechCrunch, looking back in 2026, called "spectacularly lousy" for a restaurant bet made in 2019 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)).

Brex's own people later filed the café under lessons learned. Sam Blond, Brex's chief revenue officer from 2018 to 2022, recalled it as one of the company's creative marketing experiments that was not successful: "Running a restaurant is hard. The lesson isn't necessarily, don't open up the restaurant. The lesson is, you should be trying a bunch of stuff," he said, adding that the team could tell the restaurant "wasn't driving the impact" ([The Spl.it](https://www.thespl.it/p/the-ai-native-gtm-playbook-sam-blond)). By July 2026, Caffe Centro at 102 S Park St was the only restaurant South Park had left, according to Mission Local, and South Park Cafe stood as one of the park's closures ([The Dissent](https://thedissentsf.com/article/the-last-table-at-south-park)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who bought South Park Cafe in 2019?**
Brex's co-CEOs, Henrique Dubugras and Pedro Franceschi, bought the café in 2019, when both were 23 and had never run a restaurant. They planned for Brex cardmembers to dine downstairs before heading up to the members-only lounge above the café ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)).

**When did the original South Park Cafe close, and why?**
The original café closed in 2017, after more than 30 years, when its owners retired. It had opened in 1985 under Bob Voorhees and spent most of its life as a French bistro run by chef-owner Ward Little, who had held the kitchen since 1993 ([InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/food/the-5-best-restaurants-that-opened-in-the-bay-area-this-september); [tablehopper](https://www.tablehopper.com/regular/south-park-cafe/)).

**What was the café like under Brex?**
Gensler redesigned the 3,000 sq ft space with oval detailing echoing the park, built by local artisans, with a members' lounge and private event space upstairs. It served lunch and dinner — gluten-free fried chicken, cured trout, steak frites — and doubled as a co-working space with 20 to 30 staff ([Hospitality Snapshots](https://hospitalitysnapshots.com/projects/9216/brex-lounge-south-park-cafe/); [InsideHook](https://www.insidehook.com/food/the-5-best-restaurants-that-opened-in-the-bay-area-this-september); [The Spl.it](https://www.thespl.it/p/the-ai-native-gtm-playbook-sam-blond)).

**Why did South Park Cafe close in 2020?**
In the week of 12 March 2020 an employee tested positive for COVID-19, and the café pulled its shutters that month. COVID-19 then shut down most of San Francisco for over a year, and the short-lived revival never recovered. Whether the positive test itself triggered the closure was not established by the sources read in full ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/03/12/doing-deals-through-zoom-these-investors-have-some-tips/); [The Dissent](https://thedissentsf.com/article/the-last-table-at-south-park)).

**Did South Park Cafe ever reopen?**
There is no record of it reopening. By July 2026, Caffe Centro at 102 S Park St was the only restaurant left in South Park, according to Mission Local, and South Park Cafe was remembered as one of the park's closures, its shutters pulled in March 2020 ([The Dissent](https://thedissentsf.com/article/the-last-table-at-south-park)).

Finally, this is the San Francisco café at 108 S. Park St: not Casa Bonita in Colorado, bought by the South Park television creators, and not the same-named cafés in Darlington, England, or Dandenong, Australia.
