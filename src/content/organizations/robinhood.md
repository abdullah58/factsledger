## Overview

Robinhood Markets, Inc. is a US online brokerage that made stock trading commission-free for ordinary investors and, in doing so, forced the whole American discount-brokerage industry to drop its own commissions. Founded in 2013 by Vlad Tenev and Baiju Bhatt, it listed on the [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/) as HOOD in July 2021, months after it restricted GameStop buying in January 2021 and paid record regulatory penalties ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/robinhoods-ipo-fizzles-it-faces-regulatory-scrutiny)). By the end of 2024 it reported 25.2 million funded customers and $193 billion in assets under custody ([Robinhood Markets, Inc.](https://investors.robinhood.com/node/12776/pdf)). Robinhood is also a name that turns up in other fintech stories on this site: it is among the reported client names in coverage of Capital One's acquisition of [Brex](/organizations/brex/).

## Founding and the zero-commission bet

Tenev and Bhatt, friends from college who had previously built companies in algorithmic trading and investment-bank software, devised Robinhood while working on Wall Street in 2011, after seeing the Occupy Wall Street protests first-hand ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/09/23/robinhood-stock-app/)). They brought the company out of stealth in April 2013 with a simple pitch: replace costly offices and marketing with lean software and a mobile app, and take the commission out of stock trading altogether ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/09/23/robinhood-stock-app/)). At the time, established brokers charged $7 to $10 per trade, a fee that made small investments uneconomic; four-fifths of Robinhood's beta users were under 30 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/09/23/robinhood-stock-app/)).

The money followed quickly. Robinhood raised $3 million in December 2013 from [Index Ventures](/organizations/index-ventures/) and [Andreessen Horowitz](/organizations/andreessen-horowitz/), then a $13 million Series A led by Index Ventures in September 2014, by which time half a million people had joined its waitlist ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/09/23/robinhood-stock-app/)). The app launched in 2015, and the model worked: within four years the large incumbents had surrendered, with Charles Schwab, TD Ameritrade, E*Trade, Fidelity and Interactive Brokers all eliminating online stock-trading commissions in 2019 ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/robinhoods-ipo-fizzles-it-faces-regulatory-scrutiny)). Robinhood kept expanding the menu, adding options trading in December 2017 and zero-fee Bitcoin and Ethereum trading in February 2018, initially in five states, with price tracking for 16 coins ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/01/25/free-cryptocurrency-trading-app/)). By that point it had 3 million users, had raised $176 million, and was valued at $1.3 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/01/25/free-cryptocurrency-trading-app/)).

## How Robinhood makes money, and what regulators found

Robinhood charges no commission, so it earns money elsewhere: payment for order flow, interest on customer cash and margin lending, and its Gold subscription. Payment for order flow means market makers pay Robinhood to execute its customers' orders, and the practice was so central that four market makers accounted for 59% of revenue in the first quarter of 2021, with Citadel Securities alone at 27% and market-maker revenue at 81% of the total ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/robinhoods-ipo-fizzles-it-faces-regulatory-scrutiny)). The Gold subscription, which offered margin borrowing and extra data, cost $6 to $200 a month in 2018 and $5 a month by 2021, and Gold subscribers reached 2.6 million by the end of 2024 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/01/25/free-cryptocurrency-trading-app/); [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/robinhoods-ipo-fizzles-it-faces-regulatory-scrutiny); [Robinhood Markets, Inc.](https://investors.robinhood.com/node/12776/pdf)).

The model drew two record penalties in the space of six months. The SEC found that between 2015 and late 2018 Robinhood misled customers about payment for order flow being its largest revenue source, while its unusually high payment rates left customers with execution prices inferior to other brokers' — depriving them of $34.1 million in aggregate even after the savings from not paying commission ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2020-321)). Robinhood settled without admitting or denying the findings, paying a $65 million penalty in December 2020 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2020-321)). Then, in June 2021, FINRA ordered about $70 million in penalties — a $57 million fine plus about $12.6 million in restitution, then the largest penalty it had ever imposed — over misleading customer communications, options approvals granted by algorithm with little oversight, and the March 2020 outages that locked customers out during historic volatility ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/robinhoods-ipo-fizzles-it-faces-regulatory-scrutiny)).

## GameStop, the IPO, and scale to 2024

The defining episode came on January 28, 2021, when Robinhood restricted buying in GameStop and other heavily shorted stocks. Its clearinghouse, the National Securities Clearing Corporation, had demanded an additional $3 billion in collateral — ten times the requirement three days earlier — and Robinhood, which had launched its own clearing broker in 2018, bore that demand in full with no external clearing broker to share it ([Reuters](https://mix929.com/2021/02/19/analysis-robinhood-ceos-u-s-lawmaker-grilling-spotlights-brokers-hidden-risks/)). Restricting trading cut the requirement to $737 million, and the company raised $3.4 billion from investors to cover future calls ([Reuters](https://mix929.com/2021/02/19/analysis-robinhood-ceos-u-s-lawmaker-grilling-spotlights-brokers-hidden-risks/)). At the February 18, 2021 House Financial Services Committee hearing, chief executive Vlad Tenev insisted the move was dictated by collateral rules, not by hedge funds, but conceded under questioning that Robinhood had not had the $3 billion ([Reuters](https://mix929.com/2021/02/19/analysis-robinhood-ceos-u-s-lawmaker-grilling-spotlights-brokers-hidden-risks/)). The self-clearing detail matters: it is the structural reason a fast-growing broker could be stopped by its own plumbing, and larger brokers with deeper pockets did not restrict buying ([Reuters](https://mix929.com/2021/02/19/analysis-robinhood-ceos-u-s-lawmaker-grilling-spotlights-brokers-hidden-risks/)).

Robinhood listed anyway, six months later. It priced shares at $38 on July 28, 2021, valuing the company at about $32 billion, and debuted on the Nasdaq as HOOD on July 29, falling on its first day after setting aside about a third of the offering for its own users through IPO Access ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/robinhoods-ipo-fizzles-it-faces-regulatory-scrutiny)). Growth continued regardless: funded accounts stood at 18 million at March 31, 2021, up from 12.5 million at the end of 2020, and first-quarter 2021 revenue was $522 million ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/robinhoods-ipo-fizzles-it-faces-regulatory-scrutiny)). By full-year 2024 the company reported $2.95 billion in revenue and $1.41 billion in net income, with 25.2 million funded customers and $193 billion in assets under custody ([Robinhood Markets, Inc.](https://investors.robinhood.com/node/12776/pdf)). The revenue mix had also flipped: in the fourth quarter of 2024, crypto revenue of $358 million exceeded options ($222 million) and equities ($61 million) combined, so the brokerage that began by disrupting stock commissions now earns most from crypto and options trading ([Robinhood Markets, Inc.](https://investors.robinhood.com/node/12776/pdf)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who founded Robinhood, and when?**
Vlad Tenev and Baiju Bhatt founded Robinhood in 2013, bringing it out of stealth in April of that year after building algorithmic-trading software for financial institutions ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/09/23/robinhood-stock-app/)).

**How does Robinhood make money if trading is free?**
Mainly through payment for order flow — market makers pay to execute customer orders — plus interest on cash and margin lending and its Gold subscription ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/robinhoods-ipo-fizzles-it-faces-regulatory-scrutiny)).

**Why did Robinhood restrict GameStop trading in January 2021?**
Its clearinghouse demanded an additional $3 billion in collateral on January 28, 2021, and restricting buying cut that to $737 million; Tenev told Congress the move was dictated by collateral rules, not hedge funds ([Reuters](https://mix929.com/2021/02/19/analysis-robinhood-ceos-u-s-lawmaker-grilling-spotlights-brokers-hidden-risks/)).

**What penalties has Robinhood paid to regulators?**
A $65 million SEC settlement in December 2020 over undisclosed payment for order flow, and about $70 million ordered by FINRA in June 2021 over misleading communications, options approvals and outages ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2020-321); [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/robinhoods-ipo-fizzles-it-faces-regulatory-scrutiny)).

**How big is Robinhood now?**
For full-year 2024 it reported $2.95 billion in revenue, $1.41 billion in net income, 25.2 million funded customers, 2.6 million Gold subscribers and $193 billion in assets under custody ([Robinhood Markets, Inc.](https://investors.robinhood.com/node/12776/pdf)).
