## Overview

NEX Group plc was a London-based financial-markets technology company that ran electronic trading and post-trade businesses for banks and other financial institutions ([The Global Treasurer](https://www.theglobaltreasurer.com/2016/05/16/icap-to-become-nex-group/)). It existed under that name for barely two years: created from [ICAP](/organizations/icap/) plc in 2016, it was bought by CME Group for £3.9 billion in November 2018, and the NEX name was retired on completion ([The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/cme-completes-3-9-billion-nex-group-acquisition/)). Its trading platforms, BrokerTec and EBS, still sit at the centre of CME's fixed-income and foreign-exchange offering.

The company's roots ran much deeper than its short life as NEX. Michael Spencer founded the interdealer brokerage that became ICAP in 1986, and in the early 2000s ICAP bought the electronic-trading platforms BrokerTec and EBS, combining them into EBS BrokerTec ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsy43mwbyfe6ngkd36dc/portfolio/the-2016-tech-50-michael-spencer)). By 2016 the electronic businesses were the growth engine: 79% of ICAP's operating profit came from the activities that would become NEX ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsy43mwbyfe6ngkd36dc/portfolio/the-2016-tech-50-michael-spencer)).

## From Intercapital to ICAP to NEX

Spencer built his brokerage from a specialist in interest-rate swaps into one of London's largest interdealer brokers, then spent the 2000s buying the electronic venues that would define its second act ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsy43mwbyfe6ngkd36dc/portfolio/the-2016-tech-50-michael-spencer)). BrokerTec became a leader in US Treasurys trading and EBS a dominant foreign-exchange platform, while post-trade businesses such as TriOptima, which ICAP had funded as a startup in 2002, grew alongside them ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/organization-management/alliances-mergers-acquisitions/3558686/nex-ceo-sees-opportunity-for-opportunities-after-cme-deal)). In June 2016 EBS BrokerTec signed a three-year, $65 million deal to supply technology to the China Foreign Exchange Trade System, a project Spencer described as a significant platform for the renminbi's future ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsy43mwbyfe6ngkd36dc/portfolio/the-2016-tech-50-michael-spencer)). In 2016 ICAP sold its global hybrid voice-broking business to rival Tullett Prebon, the ICAP brand transferred with the sale, and the remaining electronic-markets and post-trade business was renamed NEX Group plc ([The Global Treasurer](https://www.theglobaltreasurer.com/2016/05/16/icap-to-become-nex-group/)). The new company was headquartered in London with about 1,800 employees worldwide ([Financial Adviser Magazine](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/vc-deal-of-the-day-axioni-raises-18m-series-a-46944.html?print)).

## The CME acquisition and the brand's slow disappearance

CME Group's formal offer of 29 March 2018 was £10 per share — 500p in cash plus 0.0444 CME shares — valuing NEX at £3.9 billion, about $5.4 billion ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/organization-management/alliances-mergers-acquisitions/3558686/nex-ceo-sees-opportunity-for-opportunities-after-cme-deal)). The rationale centred on EBS in foreign exchange, BrokerTec in US Treasurys, and post-trade businesses including TriOptima ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/organization-management/alliances-mergers-acquisitions/3558686/nex-ceo-sees-opportunity-for-opportunities-after-cme-deal)). CME completed the deal on 2 November 2018 after clearance from the UK Competition and Markets Authority, retired the NEX name, and kept BrokerTec, EBS, Traiana and TriOptima running as sub-brands ([The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/cme-completes-3-9-billion-nex-group-acquisition/)). Spencer joined CME's board as a special adviser ([The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/cme-completes-3-9-billion-nex-group-acquisition/)).

| Business unit | What it did | Fate after the CME deal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BrokerTec | US Treasurys trading | Integrated into CME Globex |
| EBS | Foreign-exchange trading | Integrated into CME Globex |
| TriOptima | Post-trade risk mitigation | Continued as a separate entity |
| Traiana | Trade processing | Continued as a separate entity |
| NEX Exchange | UK growth-company stock market | Sold to Aquis Exchange, July 2019 |
| Enso Financial Analytics | Portfolio analytics | Sold to HazelTree, 2019 |
| Abide Financial / NEX Regulatory Reporting | Regulatory reporting | Wound down by CME, 2020 |

The pattern is the acquisition in miniature: CME kept what fitted its markets business and sold or shuttered the rest ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/data-management/7665481/the-nex-brand-slowly-disappears-and-market-data-fights-ai-integrations)). NEX Exchange went to Aquis Exchange in July 2019 for £1 million plus about £2.7 million of working capital, Enso Financial Analytics was sold to HazelTree, and in 2020 CME moved to wind down Abide Financial and NEX Regulatory Reporting after finding no buyers ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/data-management/7665481/the-nex-brand-slowly-disappears-and-market-data-fights-ai-integrations)).

## The venture arm: Euclid Opportunities

NEX's least-known business may have been its most forward-looking: a venture arm that backed early-stage fintech startups. ICAP's fintech investment business, [Euclid Opportunities](/organizations/euclid-opportunities/), was renamed NEX Opportunities after the 2016 split, and it invested from seed rounds through Series C in firms including RSRCHXChange, OpenFin, [Axoni](/organizations/axoni/), Cloud9, Enso and OpenGamma ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/organization-management/alliances-mergers-acquisitions/3558686/nex-ceo-sees-opportunity-for-opportunities-after-cme-deal)). Its most consequential bet was Axoni: in December 2016 Euclid co-led the blockchain firm's $18 million Series A with Wells Fargo, alongside Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan, Thomson Reuters, Andreessen Horowitz, FinTech Collective, F-Prime Capital Partners and Digital Currency Group ([Financial Adviser Magazine](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/vc-deal-of-the-day-axioni-raises-18m-series-a-46944.html?print)). Spencer defended the practice when the CME deal was announced, saying the opportunities project started "about five or six years ago has been very effective for us" and noting that ICAP had funded TriOptima as an idea in 2002 ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/organization-management/alliances-mergers-acquisitions/3558686/nex-ceo-sees-opportunity-for-opportunities-after-cme-deal)). The venture arm was not mentioned in CME's offer documents, and its later fate inside CME was not covered in the sources read here.

## Frequently asked questions

**What was NEX Group?**
NEX Group plc was a London-based financial-markets technology company formed from ICAP plc in 2016, running electronic trading platforms (BrokerTec, EBS) and post-trade businesses (TriOptima, Traiana) for financial institutions ([The Global Treasurer](https://www.theglobaltreasurer.com/2016/05/16/icap-to-become-nex-group/)). CME Group bought it for £3.9 billion in November 2018 and retired the name ([The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/cme-completes-3-9-billion-nex-group-acquisition/)).

**Why did ICAP become NEX Group?**
ICAP sold its global hybrid voice-broking business to Tullett Prebon in 2016, and the ICAP brand transferred with that sale ([The Global Treasurer](https://www.theglobaltreasurer.com/2016/05/16/icap-to-become-nex-group/)). The remaining electronic-markets and post-trade business needed a new name, so it became NEX Group plc, with London as its headquarters ([The Global Treasurer](https://www.theglobaltreasurer.com/2016/05/16/icap-to-become-nex-group/)).

**What happened to NEX's businesses after CME bought it?**
BrokerTec and EBS were integrated into CME Globex, while Traiana and TriOptima continued as separate post-trade entities ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/data-management/7665481/the-nex-brand-slowly-disappears-and-market-data-fights-ai-integrations)). NEX Exchange was sold to Aquis Exchange in 2019, Enso was sold to HazelTree, and the Abide and NEX Regulatory Reporting businesses were wound down in 2020 ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/data-management/7665481/the-nex-brand-slowly-disappears-and-market-data-fights-ai-integrations)).

**What was NEX's connection to Axoni?**
NEX's venture arm, Euclid Opportunities (renamed NEX Opportunities), co-led Axoni's $18 million Series A in December 2016 with Wells Fargo ([Financial Adviser Magazine](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/vc-deal-of-the-day-axioni-raises-18m-series-a-46944.html?print)). Axoni's Veris network later became the subject of a 2024 acquisition agreement by the London Stock Exchange Group, expected to complete in the fourth quarter ([The TRADE](http://www.thetradenews.com/lseg-acquires-axoni-post-trade-technology/)).
