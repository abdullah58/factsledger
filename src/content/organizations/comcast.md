Comcast Corporation (Nasdaq: CMCSA) is the Philadelphia-based telecommunications and media conglomerate founded in 1963 as a 1,200-subscriber cable system in Tupelo, Mississippi ([Ad Age](https://adage.com/article/media/robers/299137/)). It grew through acquisitions — AT&T Broadband, NBCUniversal, Sky — into a $123.7-billion-revenue company, the nation's largest cable and broadband provider ([Comcast](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250130280894/en/Comcast-Reports-4th-Quarter-2024-Results); [Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/comcast-elevates-michael-cavanagh-to-co-ceo-in-face-of-competition/)). It is led by co-CEOs Brian L. Roberts and Mike Cavanagh, with Roberts as chairman ([Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/comcast-elevates-michael-cavanagh-to-co-ceo-in-face-of-competition/)). It is now unwinding that empire: cable networks went to Versant in 2026, with NBCUniversal and Sky slated to separate by mid-2027 ([Cord Cutters News](https://cordcuttersnews.com/comcast-is-breaking-apart-as-nbcuniversal-becomes-independent-we-explain-exactly-what-is-happening/)).

## From a Mississippi cable system to a media empire

In 1963, Ralph J. Roberts co-founded American Cable Systems in Tupelo, Mississippi, as a 1,200-subscriber service, later renaming it Comcast ([Ad Age](https://adage.com/article/media/robers/299137/)). Ralph Roberts was chairman for 18 years until 2002, when he became chairman emeritus and his son Brian took over as chairman and chief executive officer ([Ad Age](https://adage.com/article/media/robers/299137/)). The company expanded north to Pennsylvania in 1971 and held its initial public offering the following year, when paying for TV was still a novel concept ([Ad Age](https://adage.com/article/media/robers/299137/)). The name itself was coined at the 1969 incorporation: Ralph Roberts combined "communications" and "broadcasting" into "Comcast," as Brian Roberts recounted in 2002 Senate testimony ([Comcast](https://corporate.comcast.com/news-information/news-feed/comcast-president-brian-l-roberts-testifies-about-benefits-of-comcast-att-broadband-merger-before-us-senate-subcommittee)).

Three acquisitions defined the company's shape — each a turning point, each recorded with its price:

| Acquisition | Year | Price paid | What it brought Comcast |
|---|---|---|---|
| AT&T Broadband | 2002 | $58.7 billion | National cable scale; the merger announced in December 2001 created a ~22-million-subscriber company present in 17 of the 20 largest U.S. metros ([Ad Age](https://adage.com/article/media/robers/299137/); [Comcast](https://corporate.comcast.com/news-information/news-feed/comcast-president-brian-l-roberts-testifies-about-benefits-of-comcast-att-broadband-merger-before-us-senate-subcommittee)) |
| NBCUniversal | 2011–2013 | ~$30.5 billion across two deals with General Electric (the final 49% cost $16.7 billion) | The NBC and Telemundo broadcast networks, Universal Pictures, Universal theme parks, and the cable portfolio ([Ad Age](https://adage.com/article/media/robers/299137/); [AP](https://phys.org/news/2013-02-comcast-tv-167b-nbcu.html)) |
| Sky | 2018 | £30.6 billion (~$40 billion), £17.28 per share | 23 million subscribers across the UK, Ireland, Germany, Austria and Italy, after outbidding 21st Century Fox ([SportsBusiness Journal](https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Daily/Issues/2018/09/24/Technology/comcast-acquires-european-broadcaster-sky-after-losing-fox-to-disney/)) |

Comcast completed the NBCUniversal takeover on February 12, 2013, buying General Electric's remaining 49% for $16.7 billion several years ahead of schedule, on CEO Brian Roberts's judgment that the stake would only get more expensive — a bet that paid off, since the deal valued NBCUniversal at $34 billion, about 13% more than two years earlier ([AP](https://phys.org/news/2013-02-comcast-tv-167b-nbcu.html)). The one big prize that got away: in February 2014 Comcast agreed to buy Time Warner Cable for $45.2 billion, combining the two largest U.S. cable companies, but dropped the bid that April, and Charter Communications acquired Time Warner Cable instead ([Ad Age](https://adage.com/article/media/robers/299137/)).

## What Comcast is today — and what it is becoming

Comcast's 2024 results tell the scale of the machine it built. Full-year revenue was $123,731 million, up 1.8%, with net income attributable to Comcast of $16,192 million and adjusted EBITDA of $38,069 million ([Comcast](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250130280894/en/Comcast-Reports-4th-Quarter-2024-Results)). It reports in two segments — Connectivity & Platforms (Xfinity broadband, wireless and video; Comcast Business; international connectivity) and Content & Experiences (Media including Peacock, Studios, and Theme Parks) — and the fourth-quarter 2024 split shows where the profit lives:

| Content & Experiences | $12,078M | $1,491M |
| — of which Media | $7,222M | $298M |

([Comcast](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250130280894/en/Comcast-Reports-4th-Quarter-2024-Results))

The customer footprint: 51.6 million total Connectivity & Platforms customer relationships, including 31.8 million domestic broadband customers, 12.5 million domestic video customers and 7.8 million domestic wireless lines ([Comcast](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250130280894/en/Comcast-Reports-4th-Quarter-2024-Results)). Peacock grew revenue 46% to $4.9 billion, the studios ranked second worldwide at the box office, and the new Universal Epic Universe park was scheduled to open on May 22, 2025 ([Comcast](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250130280894/en/Comcast-Reports-4th-Quarter-2024-Results)). In September 2025 the company announced that Mike Cavanagh — president since 2022, who joined from JPMorgan Chase in 2015 — would become co-CEO alongside Brian Roberts in January 2026, the first time a non-family member has held the top job ([Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/comcast-elevates-michael-cavanagh-to-co-ceo-in-face-of-competition/)); Roberts, who has led Comcast since 2002, remains chairman and co-CEO ([Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/comcast-elevates-michael-cavanagh-to-co-ceo-in-face-of-competition/)).

That handover coincides with the dismantling of the empire the company spent six decades assembling. In November 2024 Comcast announced it would spin off its NBCUniversal cable networks — USA Network, CNBC, [MSNBC](/organizations/msnbc/), Oxygen, [E!](/organizations/e-network/), SYFY and Golf Channel, plus Fandango, Rotten Tomatoes, GolfNow and SportsEngine — into a tax-free, publicly traded "SpinCo" of roughly $7 billion in annual revenue reaching about 70 million U.S. households ([Comcast](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/comcast-announces-intention-create-leading-independent-media-business-through-spin)). The separation completed in January 2026 as Versant Media Group, per the specialist outlet Cord Cutters News ([Cord Cutters News](https://cordcuttersnews.com/comcast-is-breaking-apart-as-nbcuniversal-becomes-independent-we-explain-exactly-what-is-happening/)). Then, in late June 2026, Comcast announced the larger break: NBCUniversal and Sky would be separated into their own standalone public media company around mid-2027, with Comcast retaining up to 19.9% for up to a year — Cavanagh slated to lead the new NBCUniversal, former CFO Michael Angelakis the remaining connectivity company, Roberts staying involved in both ([Cord Cutters News](https://cordcuttersnews.com/comcast-is-breaking-apart-as-nbcuniversal-becomes-independent-we-explain-exactly-what-is-happening/)). The vertical bet that began in 2011 — own the pipes and the programming — is being unwound.

## Two episodes the record keeps: the courtroom and the scorecard

In April 2010, Comcast won the landmark regulatory case that carries its name. The U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit ruled that the Federal Communications Commission lacked the authority to order Comcast to stop throttling peer-to-peer traffic, overturning the FCC's August 2008 order against the company ([PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/511955/article-3978.html)). The case began with reports in late 2007 that Comcast was slowing BitTorrent traffic — which the company first denied, then defended as congestion management — and ended with Judge David Tatel writing that the FCC lacked "any statutorily mandated responsibility" to enforce network-neutrality rules ([PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/511955/article-3978.html)).

Twelve years later, Comcast appeared in a very different ranking. Accountable.US's September 2022 American Democracy Scorecard graded the top 100 Fortune 500 companies on 14 criteria — pro-democracy statements, political-spending disclosure transparency, and PAC donations to lawmakers who opposed voting-rights legislation, backed objections to certifying the 2020 election, or opposed the January 6 commission ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression?campaign_date=01062024&position=3)). Two-thirds of the companies received failing marks; AT&T was the lowest scorer, followed by [Verizon](/organizations/verizon/), and Charter Communications and Comcast were also among the 10 lowest scorers — making telecommunications the scorecard's lowest-ranked industry ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression?campaign_date=01062024&position=3)). None of the telecommunications companies responded to [Capital & Main](/organizations/capital-and-main/)'s request for comment before publication ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression?campaign_date=01062024&position=3)).

## FAQ

**What does Comcast own?**
Comcast owns Xfinity, Comcast Business and Sky on the connectivity side, and NBC, Telemundo, Universal Pictures, Peacock, Bravo and the Universal theme parks on the content side. In January 2026 it spun its cable networks — including CNBC, USA and E! — into Versant Media Group, and it plans to separate NBCUniversal and Sky by mid-2027.

**Who is in charge of Comcast?**
Brian L. Roberts, son of founder Ralph J. Roberts, has led Comcast since 2002; in January 2026 he began sharing the chief executive role with Mike Cavanagh, president since 2022 and a former JPMorgan Chase executive. Cavanagh is the first non-family member to hold Comcast's top job, and Roberts remains chairman and co-CEO.

**Is CNBC still owned by Comcast?**
No. In November 2024 Comcast announced it would spin off its NBCUniversal cable networks — including CNBC, MSNBC, USA, Oxygen, E!, SYFY and Golf Channel — into a new public company; separation completed in January 2026 as Versant Media Group, where CNBC now sits. The earlier Comcast → NBCUniversal → CNBC chain is confirmed in Comcast's November 2024 announcement.

**When was Comcast founded and where is it headquartered?**
Comcast traces its origin to 1963, when Ralph J. Roberts co-founded American Cable Systems — a 1,200-subscriber cable service in Tupelo, Mississippi. It was incorporated as Comcast Corporation in 1969, with Roberts coining the name from "communications" and "broadcasting." The company is headquartered in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania.

**Why did Accountable.US give Comcast a failing grade?**
Accountable.US's September 2022 American Democracy Scorecard graded the top 100 Fortune 500 companies on 14 criteria covering pro-democracy statements and political spending. Comcast landed among the 10 lowest scorers, alongside AT&T, Verizon and Charter Communications, making telecom the worst-ranked industry; two-thirds of the companies graded received failing marks.
