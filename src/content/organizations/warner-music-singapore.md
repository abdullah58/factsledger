## Overview

In July 2015, Warner Music Singapore did something no other major-label office in the country
had yet dared to do: it stopped bringing in physical CDs for sale and distribution in
Singapore altogether
([Retail News Asia](https://retailnews.asia/warner-music-singapore-to-stop-distributing-cds)).
Retailers were informed only about two weeks before the story broke that August, and the
Singapore office of one of the world's three major labels simply exited the physical
supply chain — years before streaming made the CD irrelevant everywhere else. HMV Singapore
told reporters it would have to import Warner CDs itself, while Sony titles could still be
supplied locally through the company Sony had engaged, MM2; a veteran record retailer, Ngiam
Kwang Hwa of Rock Records, explained the arithmetic plainly — freight, GST, warehousing and
distribution costs against falling sales volumes — while Warner itself declined to comment
([Retail News Asia](https://retailnews.asia/warner-music-singapore-to-stop-distributing-cds)).

That early, unsentimental pivot to digital is the clearest statement of what Warner Music
Singapore is: the Singapore recorded-music arm of Warner Music Group, run as a regional hub
rather than a manufacturing outpost. Corporate records show the company, Warner Music
Singapore Pte Ltd, was incorporated on 31 March 1978 under UEN 197800692E and was formerly
known as WEA Records, the old Warner-Elektra-Atlantic international imprint
([RecordOwl](https://recordowl.com/company/warner-music-singapore-pte-ltd)). Its registered
activity — distributing records, music videos and CDs while managing artists — has barely
changed on paper, but its registered office moved in June 2026 from The Central on Eu Tong
Sen Street to Marina One West Tower at 9 Straits View, a filing that quietly confirms the
office is still very much alive
([RecordOwl](https://recordowl.com/company/warner-music-singapore-pte-ltd)).

## The decade before the streaming era

The 2015 CD exit was not a bluff: in the year before it, the office had released albums by
Stevie Nicks, Ed Sheeran, Jolin Tsai and Singapore's own JJ Lin, alongside local signings
such as singer Reuby and hip-hop duo SleeQ
([Retail News Asia](https://retailnews.asia/warner-music-singapore-to-stop-distributing-cds)).
Universal Music kept bringing in CDs — its Singapore physical sales had fallen 8 to 9 per
cent in the first half of that year — and Rock Records' Ngiam warned that Warner's move could
be the start of an industry-wide collapse of the distribution system
([Retail News Asia](https://retailnews.asia/warner-music-singapore-to-stop-distributing-cds)).
A public servant quoted in the piece summed up the consumer mood: he had not bought a CD in
more than a decade, buying individual songs on iTunes and streaming on Spotify and YouTube
instead ([Retail News Asia](https://retailnews.asia/warner-music-singapore-to-stop-distributing-cds)).

## Leadership: from Nasser to Ang

Warner Music Singapore was led from 2015 to 2020 by Simon Jerome Nasser, who had joined
Warner Music Group back in 2004 and was credited with discovering and developing domestic and
international talent across Singapore and South-East Asia
([Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/universal-music-singapore-ramps-up-sg-presence-names-new-md)).
Nasser later crossed to the rival camp, becoming managing director of Universal Music
Singapore and head of live for South-East Asia, while chairing three industry bodies — RIAS,
Music Rights Singapore and RIPS
([Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/universal-music-singapore-ramps-up-sg-presence-names-new-md)).

On 21 July 2020 Warner Music Group named Gerald Ang as his successor, effective immediately,
reporting to Simon Robson, President of Warner Music Asia
([Warner Music Group](https://www.wmg.com/news/gerald-ang-named-md-warner-music-singapore-34891)).
Ang was an unusual hire for a label: a marketer who had co-founded agency Frontal
Productions, worked at Green Dot, NCS, BlackBerry and General Electric, then ran marketing
for Singtel's fintech and mobile financial services divisions, growing brand awareness by 20
per cent in just over a year
([Warner Music Group](https://www.wmg.com/news/gerald-ang-named-md-warner-music-singapore-34891)).
His stated brief was to drive the domestic business by expanding Warner Music Singapore's
Chinese roster, with Singapore positioned as a key regional hub and gateway to South-East
Asia — a market Robson called increasingly influential on global streaming platforms
([Warner Music Group](https://www.wmg.com/news/gerald-ang-named-md-warner-music-singapore-34891)).
Robson pointed to the office's record with Singaporean Chinese talent — JJ Lin, Stephanie
Sun, and more recently Nathan Hartono and Jasmine Sokko — as the standard Ang was expected
to continue
([Warner Music Group](https://www.wmg.com/news/gerald-ang-named-md-warner-music-singapore-34891)).

Two of those signings show how the label built its local catalogue. JJ Lin, one of
Singapore's biggest Mandopop exports, signed with Warner Music at a Beijing press conference
on 8 August 2011, calling it a new start after eight years with Ocean Butterflies; the event
drew Warner Music Asia Pacific president Lachie Rutherford and Warner China president Sam
Chen, with welcome messages from labelmates Jolin Tsai, Khalil Fong and F.I.R.
([China Daily](http://www.chinadaily.com.cn/entertainment/2011-08/09/content_13080802.htm)).
A decade later the label was signing a different kind of Singaporean act: electronic
singer-songwriter Jasmine Sokko, whose stage name comes from the Finnish word for "blind",
broke out in 2016 with the self-penned single "1057" — its video shot around Arab Street —
and then signed with Warner Music Singapore, debuting on the label in 2018 with "Hurt" and
releasing her most successful song, "Tired", the following year
([The Business Times](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/lifestyle/bt-luxe/the-sound-of-independence)).

## The Cross Ratio distribution pact

The most consequential deal of Ang's tenure came in April 2023, when Warner Music Singapore
signed an exclusive worldwide distribution agreement with
[Cross Ratio Entertainment](/organizations/cross-ratio-entertainment/), described by Warner
itself as Singapore's largest independent record label
([Warner Music Group](https://www.wmg.com/news/warner-music-singapore-and-cross-ratio-entertainment-sign-global-distribution-deal)).
The pact went beyond distribution: the two labels agreed to collaborate on new releases,
catalogues, brand partnerships, synchronisation deals and marketing events, and to identify
and "upstream" promising artists together — with Singapore explicitly framed as the regional
hub from which both would export artists across Asia and beyond
([Warner Music Group](https://www.wmg.com/news/warner-music-singapore-and-cross-ratio-entertainment-sign-global-distribution-deal)).
Cross Ratio's founder Dean Augustine said the indie trusted Warner's regional and global
expertise to put homegrown artists "on the map"; Ang answered that the deal deepened
Warner's commitment to connecting artists with fans in South-East Asia and Greater China and
strengthened its position in C-Pop
([Warner Music Group](https://www.wmg.com/news/warner-music-singapore-and-cross-ratio-entertainment-sign-global-distribution-deal)).

## A Singapore node in Warner's Asia map

Since 2025, Warner Music Singapore has sat inside a restructured regional chain. Dan Rosen
was promoted to President of Warner Music Australasia and SE Asia in November 2025, with his
remit expanded over the recorded-music businesses of Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines,
Singapore, Thailand and Vietnam, reporting to Lo Ting-Fai, President of Warner Music APAC
([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/warner-music-forms-cross-border-artist-development-platform-gliide-with-thailands-gmm-music/)).
In 2026 Warner and Thailand's GMM Music launched GLIIDE, a cross-border artist-development
platform inside their GMM Global joint venture designed to build Asian pop acts for global
audiences from their very first release — its first signing was the artist SVRN — and the
ANZSEA promotions that December included a Singapore-based International Artist Strategy
Lead for SE Asia, Lisan Yee
([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/warner-music-forms-cross-border-artist-development-platform-gliide-with-thailands-gmm-music/)).
Corporate trademark filings under the company's name — "music street" from 1998 and
"playmusic" from 2005 — hint at the local sublabels the Singapore office has run along the
way ([RecordOwl](https://recordowl.com/company/warner-music-singapore-pte-ltd)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim an exact founding story for the label brand beyond the verified
1978 company incorporation. It does not assert that Warner Music Singapore acquired Music
Street or Play Music in 2005: that claim appears only in unverified lists, and only the
underlying trademark filings are verified. It makes no claim about the office's current
complete roster or its commercial performance. Warner Music Singapore is not Warner Bros.
Discovery — a separate media conglomerate profiled elsewhere — and this profile claims no
relationship between the two.
