## Overview

Stereogum is an [American](/places/united-states/) music website founded in January 2002 by Scott Lapatine, who still runs it as editor-in-chief. It began as one of the first MP3 blogs, sharing downloadable songs by little-known bands, and grew into a daily music publication known for spotting acts early, for Tom Breihan's Hot 100 column The Number Ones, and for surviving as an independent, reader-funded site after its founder bought it back in 2020 ([Clash](https://www.clashmusic.com/news/the-national-sharon-van-etten-ben-gibbard-contribute-to-save-stereogum-compilation/); [Hachette](https://www.hachette.co.uk/titles/tom-breihan/the-number-ones/9780306826535/); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/next-management-partners-acquires-spin-media/)).

The name comes from Air, the French electronic duo: Stereogum is named after their song "Radio Number 1", from the album *10,000 Hz Legend*, as the band's Jean-Benoît Dunckel confirmed in a Stereogum interview ([Stereogum](https://stereogum.com/1823047/qa-airs-jean-benoit-dunckel-on-the-future-of-the-band-working-with-sofia-coppola-and-inspiring-the-name-stereogum/interviews)). The site covers a broad sweep of music — indie rock, pop, metal, hip-hop and jazz — through news, reviews, interviews and recurring columns such as Album Of The Week and Band To Watch, the latter spotlighting artists just before they break into the mainstream ([MaxiJournal](https://maxijournal.com/independent-music-blogs/)). Lapatine has said it became the most popular standalone music website in America according to Comscore, and that it publishes 365 days a year with a very small staff ([Stereogum](https://stereogum.com/2478838/stereogum-relaunch/tcb)).

## From MP3 blog to independent publication

Stereogum started in January 2002, when Lapatine set it up as a LiveJournal site at the height of the MP3-blog era, posting downloadable songs alongside writing about new bands ([Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/blog-it-how-the-web-changed-music-criticism/)). It followed Pitchfork into the form, with an even stronger emphasis on sharing MP3 samples, and was one of the first wave of music blogs built around that habit ([The History of the Web](https://thehistoryoftheweb.com/the-online-world-of-high-taste-and-rock-music/); [Clash](https://www.clashmusic.com/news/the-national-sharon-van-etten-ben-gibbard-contribute-to-save-stereogum-compilation/)). Its early reputation rested on spotting talent first: it touted Vampire Weekend and Cold War Kids early in their careers, before either found commercial success, and Vampire Weekend was later described as one of the handful of bands readers first encountered on a pre-Spin Stereogum ([Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/blog-it-how-the-web-changed-music-criticism/); [Consequence](https://consequence.net/2019/05/did-vampire-weekend-win-the-indie-rock-age/)).

Ownership changed hands repeatedly. Bob Pittman's Pilot Group bought the site in 2006, and it later passed to Buzz Media, which became SpinMedia ([Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2020/01/16/valence-media-selling-spin-and-stereogum/); [BrooklynVegan](https://www.brooklynvegan.com/spin-vibe-stereogum-sold-to-billboardhollywood-reporter/)). In December 2016 the Hollywood Reporter–Billboard Media Group acquired SpinMedia's music assets, including Stereogum, Spin and [Vibe](/organizations/vibe/) ([Billboard](https://stereogum.com/2070280/billboard-the-hollywood-reporter-media-group-selling-stereogum-to-founder-scott-lapatine/news); [BrooklynVegan](https://www.brooklynvegan.com/spin-vibe-stereogum-sold-to-billboardhollywood-reporter/)). Lapatine bought it back in January 2020, when Valence Media sold Spin to Next Management Partners and agreed to sell Stereogum to its own leadership, with Lapatine becoming chief executive ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/next-management-partners-acquires-spin-media/)).

## The Number Ones and the Billboard connection

Tom Breihan began The Number Ones in early 2018, a column reviewing, analysing and giving historical context to every number-one single in the history of the Billboard Hot 100, in chronological order ([Hachette](https://www.hachette.co.uk/titles/tom-breihan/the-number-ones/9780306826535/)). The column grew into a book, *The Number Ones*, which was named one of Pitchfork's Best Music Books of 2022 ([Hachette](https://www.hachette.co.uk/titles/tom-breihan/the-number-ones/9780306826535/)). The chart connection also runs the other way: Stereogum's site carries Billboard's own chart reporting, including the March 2017 report that [Nicki Minaj](/people/nicki-minaj/) had passed [Aretha Franklin](/people/aretha-franklin/) for the most Hot 100 appearances among women, reaching 76 entries against Franklin's 73 on the chart dated 1 April 2017 ([Billboard](https://stereogum.com/1930785/nicki-minaj-breaks-record-for-most-hot-100-hits-by-a-female-artist/news)). Minaj's first Hot 100 entry had come as a featured artist on [Lil Wayne](/people/lil-wayne/)'s "Knockout" in February 2010, and Franklin had held the women's record for nearly 40 years ([Billboard](https://stereogum.com/1930785/nicki-minaj-breaks-record-for-most-hot-100-hits-by-a-female-artist/news)). The Alternative Number Ones, a subscriber column, now alternates weeks with the main column on Mondays ([Stereogum](https://stereogum.com/2478838/stereogum-relaunch/tcb)).

## Reader funding, rescue and relaunch

After retaking control in 2020, Lapatine diversified revenue that had come entirely from advertising, adding a newsletter, a merch shop, affiliate links, live events and a paid subscription programme ([Stereogum](https://stereogum.com/2478838/stereogum-relaunch/tcb)). The pandemic then threatened the newly independent site: Stereogum predicted its income would fall by 50%, and it turned to readers with Save Stereogum, an Indiegogo campaign built around a 55-track compilation of 2000s covers ([Clash](https://www.clashmusic.com/news/the-national-sharon-van-etten-ben-gibbard-contribute-to-save-stereogum-compilation/)). Readers gave more than $370,000, and the compilation reached number 11 on a Billboard album chart — a reader-funded rescue that also became a charting record, proving the audience would pay directly to keep the site alive ([Loud and Quiet](https://www.loudandquiet.com/short/the-unexpected-joys-of-the-save-stereogum-00s-compilation/?utm_source=dlvr.it&utm_medium=facebook&amp)).

Five years later the economics forced a bigger change. Lapatine said Google's pivot to AI search had cut Stereogum's ad revenue by 70%, with Facebook and X deprioritising links as well, so the site relaunched on 10 November 2025 on Alley's Lede platform with a metered paywall and membership at $6, $11 and $99 a month ([Stereogum](https://stereogum.com/2478838/stereogum-relaunch/tcb)). Paying members get a Discord community, members-only articles and commenting, while The Number Ones stays free; the site still refuses generative AI and has never taken money to write about an artist ([Stereogum](https://stereogum.com/2478838/stereogum-relaunch/tcb)). The output behind the paywall is substantial: the site published over 120 longform artist interviews in 2024, and in 2025 interviewed acts ranging from Agriculture to Raekwon to Lady Gaga ([Stereogum](https://stereogum.com/2478838/stereogum-relaunch/tcb)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who founded Stereogum?**

Scott Lapatine founded Stereogum in January 2002, starting it as a LiveJournal site in the early MP3-blog era ([Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/blog-it-how-the-web-changed-music-criticism/)). He has run it as editor-in-chief for most of its life, including after buying it back in 2020, when he also became chief executive ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/next-management-partners-acquires-spin-media/)).

**Who owns Stereogum now?**

Scott Lapatine owns it. He bought the site back from the Billboard–The Hollywood Reporter Media Group in January 2020, after it had passed through the Pilot Group, Buzz Media/SpinMedia and the Billboard group, making it independent again for the first time since 2006 ([Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2020/01/16/valence-media-selling-spin-and-stereogum/)).

**What is The Number Ones?**

It is Tom Breihan's column reviewing every number-one single in the history of the Billboard Hot 100, in chronological order, begun in early 2018 ([Hachette](https://www.hachette.co.uk/titles/tom-breihan/the-number-ones/9780306826535/)). It grew into a book of the same name, named one of Pitchfork's Best Music Books of 2022 ([Hachette](https://www.hachette.co.uk/titles/tom-breihan/the-number-ones/9780306826535/)).

**What was Save Stereogum?**

A 2020 Indiegogo campaign to keep the newly independent site alive after COVID-19 hit its advertising income ([Clash](https://www.clashmusic.com/news/the-national-sharon-van-etten-ben-gibbard-contribute-to-save-stereogum-compilation/)). Donors received a 55-track compilation of 2000s covers by artists including The National and Sharon Van Etten; the campaign raised over $370,000 and the album reached number 11 on a Billboard album chart ([Loud and Quiet](https://www.loudandquiet.com/short/the-unexpected-joys-of-the-save-stereogum-00s-compilation/?utm_source=dlvr.it&utm_medium=facebook&amp)).

**Why did Stereogum add a paywall in 2025?**

Because advertising collapsed: founder Scott Lapatine said Google's shift to AI search cut ad revenue by 70%, after Facebook and X had already reduced link traffic ([Stereogum](https://stereogum.com/2478838/stereogum-relaunch/tcb)). The November 2025 relaunch rebuilt the site around paid membership — $6, $11 or $99 a month — with members-only articles and commenting for paying readers ([Stereogum](https://stereogum.com/2478838/stereogum-relaunch/tcb)).
