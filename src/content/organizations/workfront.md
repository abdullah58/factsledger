## Overview

Workfront was a Lehi, Utah-based enterprise work-management software company that [Adobe](/organizations/adobe/) acquired for $1.5 billion in 2020 — and one of the least "startup-like" startups Adobe ever bought. It was founded in 2001 as AtTask, rebranded to Workfront in January 2015, and by the time the deal was announced it had more than 3,000 customers, one million users, over $200 million in annual revenue, roughly 1,000 employees, and was cash-flow positive ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/innovationNews/idUSKBN27P305/?il=0), [Enterprise Times](https://www.enterprisetimes.co.uk/2020/08/13/a-conversation-with-workfront/)). Today the product is sold as Adobe Workfront, the work-management layer of Adobe Experience Cloud ([Talk Commerce](https://talk-commerce.com/blog/adobe-hands-the-ceo-job-to-the-executive-who-bought-semrush)).

## Nineteen years before the deal: AtTask becomes Workfront

Workfront's pre-history is longer than most acquisition targets get credit for. The company began in 2001 as AtTask, a work-management software provider ([ProjectManagement.com](https://www.projectmanagement.com/articles/289511/AtTask-Renamed-Workfront)). In January 2015 it renamed itself Workfront, with then-CEO Eric Morgan saying the company had simply "outgrown the AtTask name" and needed one that "conveyed the enterprise power of the solutions we deliver" ([ProjectManagement.com](https://www.projectmanagement.com/articles/289511/AtTask-Renamed-Workfront), [Tech Monitor](https://www.techmonitor.ai/technology/attast-becomes-workfront-4495110)). Even then it was no minnow: over 500 employees, more than 1,000 clients, a 42% three-year compound annual growth rate, and recognition as a leader in Gartner's 2014 Magic Quadrant for cloud-based IT project and portfolio management ([ProjectManagement.com](https://www.projectmanagement.com/articles/289511/AtTask-Renamed-Workfront)).

Leadership turned over in 2016, when Alex Shootman — previously president of Eloqua through its 2012 IPO and $900 million sale to Oracle, and then president of Apptio — became Workfront's President and CEO ([Pulse2](http://pulse2.com/pagerduty-appoints-alkami-ceo-alex-shootman-to-board-of-directors/)). The funding history is notable for how little of it was primary capital: Workfront raised about $375 million over its lifetime, but $280 million of that was secondary money from a March 2019 share purchase in which Susquehanna International Group, W Capital Partners, and AB Private Credit bought out earlier backers; total primary funding since 2001 was roughly $95 million from firms including JMI Equity, Greenspring, OpenView Venture Partners, and Atlas Peak ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/11/09/adobe-acquires-marketing-workflow-startup-workfront-for-1-5b/512), [Global Corporate Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/11/10/adobe-to-administer-1-5bn-workfront/)).

By August 2020 — three months before the acquisition — Workfront's scale was public record in an independent interview with its EMEA managing director: approximately 3,000 customers (about 600 in EMEA), more than $200 million in revenue with consistent year-over-year growth, cash-flow positivity, and around 1,000 employees across Lehi, Basingstoke (UK), and development offices in Armenia ([Enterprise Times](https://www.enterprisetimes.co.uk/2020/08/13/a-conversation-with-workfront/)). Its biggest accounts were worth over $1 million in annual recurring revenue each, with tens of thousands of users ([Enterprise Times](https://www.enterprisetimes.co.uk/2020/08/13/a-conversation-with-workfront/)). The product line by then included Fusion, a codeless integration platform, native integrations with Salesforce and Adobe, and partnerships with AWS and Google ([Enterprise Times](https://www.enterprisetimes.co.uk/2020/08/13/a-conversation-with-workfront/)).

## The $1.5 billion deal and what came after

On November 9, 2020 — after Bloomberg first reported the sale — Adobe announced a definitive agreement to acquire Workfront for $1.5 billion, "subject to customary purchase price adjustments," with the close expected in the first quarter of Adobe's fiscal 2021 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/11/09/adobe-acquires-marketing-workflow-startup-workfront-for-1-5b/512), [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/innovationNews/idUSKBN27P305/?il=0)). The rationale was bluntly pandemic-shaped: with marketing teams distributed by COVID-19, Adobe wanted work-management tooling inside its Experience Cloud to own the full lifecycle "from ideation to activation," in the words of Digital Experience chief Anil Chakravarthy ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/adobe-buying-workfront-for-1-billion)). The two companies were already longstanding partners with APIs connecting Workfront to Adobe Creative Cloud and Experience Cloud, and over 1,000 shared customers — including Home Depot, T-Mobile, Deloitte, Under Armour, Nordstrom, and Prudential Financial ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/innovationNews/idUSKBN27P305/?il=0), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/11/09/adobe-acquires-marketing-workflow-startup-workfront-for-1-5b/512)). Shootman was to continue leading the team, reporting to Chakravarthy ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/innovationNews/idUSKBN27P305/?il=0)).

TechCrunch put the deal in a broader context: Workfront was Adobe's third big Experience Cloud acquisition in two years, after Magento ($1.68 billion) and Marketo ($4.75 billion) in 2018 — nearly $8 billion spent as Adobe battled Salesforce, SAP, and Oracle in marketing automation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/11/09/adobe-acquires-marketing-workflow-startup-workfront-for-1-5b/512)).

The deal closed in December 2020, and reality set in more slowly than the announcements suggested. Six months later, Securian Financial — a Workfront customer since January 2017 — was still working through the first integrations: a Creative Cloud connector letting marketers accept tasks inside Illustrator, and an Adobe DAM connector for adding metadata and assets ([MarTech](https://martech.org/securian-financial-sees-opportunities-in-the-adobe-workfront-integrations/)). Its marketing operations manager described the value as theoretically visible but not yet captured — "it's early days" — and worried that the integration work risked pulling marketers away from strategic work onto administrative load ([MarTech](https://martech.org/securian-financial-sees-opportunities-in-the-adobe-workfront-integrations/)).

Six years on, Workfront's footprint inside Adobe is settled: Adobe folded it into Experience Cloud as work management for marketing teams, where it has stayed an enterprise-tier product ([Talk Commerce](https://talk-commerce.com/blog/adobe-hands-the-ceo-job-to-the-executive-who-bought-semrush)). Shootman went on to become CEO of Alkami Technology, and joined PagerDuty's board in July 2026, with his Workfront tenure described as running from 2016 "until Adobe acquired the company for $1.5 billion in 2020" ([Pulse2](http://pulse2.com/pagerduty-appoints-alkami-ceo-alex-shootman-to-board-of-directors/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not state the exact closing date of the acquisition, because the only source for a specific day is Adobe's own announcement, which was excluded under the no-first-party rule; it states only that the deal closed in December 2020. It does not name Workfront's original founder, since no independently read source verified that fact. Employee counts are stated as dated snapshots from each source, not as a single continuous figure. The $375 million lifetime-funding figure is Crunchbase's, relayed by TechCrunch; only the $280M secondary and $95M primary components were independently reconstructed by Global Corporate Venturing. Post-close integration progress is described only through the single customer account (Securian Financial) that was independently reported — it does not claim that account represents all customers' experience.
