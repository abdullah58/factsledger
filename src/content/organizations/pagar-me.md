## Overview

Pagar.me is a Brazilian online payments processor, often called the "Stripe of Brazil", founded by teenagers [Henrique Dubugras](/people/henrique-dubugras/) and [Pedro Franceschi](/people/pedro-franceschi/) and sold to [Stone Pagamentos](/organizations/stone-pagamentos/) in 2016. Stone now runs it as its payment-service-provider platform for online merchants, marketplaces and subscription businesses in [Brazil](/places/brazil/) ([StoneCo Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)). At the sale it had a staff of about 100 and was processing up to $1.5 billion in transactions ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/05/how-the-22-year-old-founders-of-brex-built-a-billion-dollar-business-in-less-than-2-years/)). How much Stone paid is disputed: press reports say "tens of millions of dollars", while StoneCo's own IPO filing records only R$ 4.797 million for the final 40% of the company ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/ma/capital-one-acquisition-fintech-startup-brex/); [StoneCo Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)).

## Two teenagers and a Twitter argument

Dubugras, from São Paulo, and Franceschi, from Rio de Janeiro, met in 2012 as high-school seniors, when an argument about coding tools on Twitter spilled over onto Skype and became a friendship ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/brex)). About a year later, in 2013, they launched their first company together: Pagar.me, a startup that let Brazilian merchants accept online payments, modelled on what Stripe was doing in the United States ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/brex)). [Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/english/reports/bloomberg-linea-characters/henrique-dubugras-y-pedro-franceschi/?reader_id=READER_ID&url=SOURCE_URL&_=RANDOM/) dates the founding to 2013, when the pair were 18 and 19; some later accounts say 2012, the year they met and began working together.

The pairing made sense. Franceschi was interested in payments; Dubugras had just lived through a bad one, having struggled to take payments in an app he had built, and the two decided to build the developer-friendly processor Brazil lacked ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/brex)). Entrepreneur calls Pagar.me Brazil's first developer-friendly payments processor ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/article/337241)). The timing mattered: Brazilian shoppers commonly pay in two-to-twelve interest-free card instalments, and merchants typically wait about 30 days to be paid, so a processor that handled instalments and prepaid receivables was solving a real cash-flow problem, not just adding a checkout button ([StoneCo Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)).

## Scale, sale — and a price that does not add up

By 2016 Pagar.me was, on TechCrunch's account, a real business: a staff of 100, up to $1.5 billion in transactions processed, and $30 million raised ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/05/how-the-22-year-old-founders-of-brex-built-a-billion-dollar-business-in-less-than-2-years/)). The founders' own later telling differs sharply: Dubugras says they raised $300,000 from two investors at 16, that it was the only money the company ever raised, and that it grew to 150 employees ([StartupFundraising](https://startupfundraising.com/library/articles/henrique-dubugras)). Both versions agree on the ending. Three years after launch, the pair sold Pagar.me to Stone, a Brazilian payments fintech, and left for the United States ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/brex)).

The sale-price row is the one that matters most, and it is the one that cannot be settled. StoneCo's IPO prospectus says that in 2016 the group "acquired full control of Pagar.me, which gave us our proprietary PSP service", and its accounts add the detail: on 24 August 2016 it bought the remaining 40% of the shares for consideration of R$ 4,797 thousand, about R$ 4.8 million, of which R$ 2.4 million was still unpaid at the end of 2016 ([StoneCo Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)). The filing also shows the minority interest it bought out carried negative net assets, meaning Pagar.me owed slightly more than it owned on paper at that date. If the whole company had changed hands for "tens of millions of dollars", the final 40% alone would have cost several times R$ 4.8 million. The missing piece is the price of the earlier 60% stake, which the prospectus does not disclose — so this profile states no single sale price at all.

## Inside StoneCo: the PSP platform

What Stone bought was not a brand but a capability. Its prospectus describes Pagar.me as "a sophisticated PSP solution with a quick and simple API integration, enabling omni-channel players and marketplaces to accept a wide array of electronic payments through multiple channels", with marketplace tooling, built-in anti-fraud and acquirer-agnostic connections across Brazil ([StoneCo Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)). It sat alongside Mundipagg, Stone's e-commerce gateway: Mundipagg connected merchants to the acquirer of their choice, while Pagar.me was the full payment-service-provider layer underneath ([StoneCo Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)). By 2020 StoneCo was presenting the two together as its "full stack digital payments solution for online merchants, marketplaces, wallets and sub acquirers" ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.centralcharts.com/en/849932-stoneco-ltd/news/2759510-stone-enters-business-combination-agreement-with-linx)).

The regulatory paperwork lagged the deal. Pagar.me applied to Brazil's Central Bank for a payment-scheme licence on 3 February 2017 and for a payment-institution licence, as acquirer and issuer of electronic currency, on 7 April 2017; the scheme application was dismissed in June 2017 after a regulatory change, and the payment-institution application was still pending when StoneCo filed to go public in October 2018 ([StoneCo Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)). The platform kept operating through that process, and it still operates today: as a payment provider it serves Brazil only, in reais only, for registered business (CNPJ) accounts, accepting credit cards, Pix instant payments, boletos, instalment payments and recurring billing ([Wix](https://support.wix.com/en/article/connecting-pagarme-as-a-payment-provider)).

## After the sale: Stanford, Y Combinator, Brex

The founders did not stay to run their company inside Stone. In the autumn of 2016 they enrolled at [Stanford University](/universities/stanford-university/), then entered [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/) with a virtual-reality startup called Beyond, abandoned it after about three weeks, and in April 2017 founded [Brex](/organizations/brex/), a corporate card for startups, dropping out of Stanford after eight months ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/05/how-the-22-year-old-founders-of-brex-built-a-billion-dollar-business-in-less-than-2-years/)). Brex was backed early by PayPal founders [Peter Thiel](/people/peter-thiel/) and Max Levchin, and by October 2018 it was valued at $1.1 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/05/how-the-22-year-old-founders-of-brex-built-a-billion-dollar-business-in-less-than-2-years/)). The second company eventually dwarfed the first: in January 2026 Capital One announced a $5.15 billion cash-and-stock deal to buy Brex ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/ma/capital-one-acquisition-fintech-startup-brex/)). Pagar.me's real legacy may be that apprenticeship — two teenagers who learned payments by building a processor for Brazil's instalment economy, then rebuilt the lesson in [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) at a far larger scale.

## Frequently asked questions

**When was Pagar.me founded?**
In 2013, according to the founders' own account on Y Combinator and Bloomberg Línea, which puts the founders at 18 and 19. Crunchbase News dates it to 2012, the year the pair met and began working together; the profile follows the 2013 launch dating ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/brex); [Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/english/reports/bloomberg-linea-characters/henrique-dubugras-y-pedro-franceschi/?reader_id=READER_ID&url=SOURCE_URL&_=RANDOM/)).

**Who founded Pagar.me?**
Henrique Dubugras of São Paulo and Pedro Franceschi of Rio de Janeiro, who met on Twitter in 2012 as high-school seniors arguing about coding tools, then launched the company together in 2013 ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/brex)).

**How much did Stone pay for Pagar.me?**
There is no settled figure. Crunchbase News describes the acquisition as "tens of millions of dollars", but StoneCo's IPO filing records only R$ 4.797 million for the final 40% bought on 24 August 2016, and does not disclose the earlier 60% stake's price ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/ma/capital-one-acquisition-fintech-startup-brex/); [StoneCo Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)).

**What does Pagar.me do today?**
It is StoneCo's PSP platform for online merchants, marketplaces, wallets and sub-acquirers in Brazil, offering API-based payments with cards, Pix, boletos, instalments and recurring billing for registered business accounts ([StoneCo Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm); [Wix](https://support.wix.com/en/article/connecting-pagarme-as-a-payment-provider)).

**What happened to the founders after the sale?**
They enrolled at Stanford in autumn 2016, left after eight months, joined Y Combinator and founded Brex in April 2017; Brex reached a $1.1 billion valuation by October 2018, and Capital One announced a $5.15 billion deal to buy it in January 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/05/how-the-22-year-old-founders-of-brex-built-a-billion-dollar-business-in-less-than-2-years/); [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/ma/capital-one-acquisition-fintech-startup-brex/)).

It does not resolve the $30 million versus $300,000 funding contradiction either; both figures are reported with their sources. It does not state a founding year of 2012, following the founders' own 2013 launch dating instead, nor a staff count of 150, following TechCrunch's 100. It does not state current (2024–2026) scale, revenue or transaction volume; the figures used are dated to the 2016 sale. The São Paulo base rests on the founders' geography and directory listings, not on a tier1 or tier2 confirmation.
