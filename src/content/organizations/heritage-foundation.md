## Overview

The Heritage Foundation is an American conservative public-policy research organisation, or think tank, based in Washington, D.C., whose mission is "to formulate and promote conservative public policies based on the principles of free enterprise, limited government, individual freedom, traditional American values, and a strong national defense" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Heritage-Foundation)). Founded in 1973 by two congressional aides, Edwin Feulner and Paul Weyrich, it provides research and policy recommendations to presidential administrations, Congress, news media, and academic communities ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Heritage-Foundation)). Its influence has run through a single recurring device — the *Mandate for Leadership* handbook, handed to incoming conservative administrations — and in July 2026 it added a new device, a Legal Action Center that litigates rather than merely publishes, with a [Colorado](/places/colorado/) school district's book-ban defence as its first client ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Heritage-Foundation); [Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/colorado/2026/07/21/heritage-foundation-alliance-defending-freedom-lawyers-join-book-ban-lawsuit/)).

## Founding and the Mandate model

Heritage was founded in 1973, and it flourished in the 1980s during the presidency of Republican Ronald Reagan, who used its handbook *Mandate for Leadership: Principles to Limit Government, Expand Freedom, and Strengthen America* (1981) as a source of guidance for his administration ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Heritage-Foundation)). The backstory, as E&E News by POLITICO tells it, is that Heritage had been urging presidents to roll back regulations and consolidate agencies for decades, and that in 1980 it drafted an extensive policy agenda in the hope that a conservative would replace then-President Jimmy Carter ([E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/the-first-version-of-project-2025/)). The think tank then offered its proposals to President-elect Reagan's transition team leader, Edwin Meese, and the guide, published in January 1981, was a hit inside Reagan's brain trust: the *Mandate for Leadership* was handed out during Reagan's first Cabinet meeting ([E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/the-first-version-of-project-2025/)).

The Reagan-era *Mandate* became the first in a long series of regularly published handbooks, most of which recommended government policies and political strategies to incoming conservative administrations ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Heritage-Foundation)). Heritage also built standing research products alongside the handbooks: it founded the *Index of Economic Freedom* in 1995 as a way for countries to measure progress in economic freedom, then in its 14th edition and co-published with the *Wall Street Journal* ([Heritage](https://Www.Heritage.org/node/9272/print-display)). Kevin D. Roberts, president of Heritage and its lobbying arm Heritage Action since taking the helm in 2021, previously served as chief executive of the Texas Public Policy Foundation and as president of Wyoming Catholic College ([Heritage](https://www.heritage.org/staff/kevin-d-roberts-phd)).

## Project 2025

In 2023 the foundation released *Mandate for Leadership: The Conservative Promise 2025*, which contained numerous policy and legislative proposals as well as recommendations aimed at restructuring the federal government's executive branch ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Heritage-Foundation)). That volume serves as the central document of Project 2025, a detailed and wide-ranging plan of action for the next administration should a conservative candidate win the presidential election of 2024, and the project also encompasses a database of ideologically vetted candidates for political appointment and a training program to enable potential appointees to operate successfully from the start of a conservative administration ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Heritage-Foundation)). Critics of Project 2025 have argued that the policy and structural changes called for in its *Mandate* would create an authoritarian and Christian nationalist state by massively expanding presidential power and aggressively promoting conservative Christian values, a characterisation reported here as critics' argument, not as this profile's finding ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Heritage-Foundation)).

In 2023, as former president Donald Trump campaigned for the 2024 Republican presidential nomination, the project came to be regarded as the likely blueprint of a second Trump administration, while supporters of President Joe Biden publicised some of its more extreme proposals — including, as Britannica lists them, reversing the federal government's longstanding approval of abortion pills, eliminating the Department of Education, and placing the Department of Justice under direct presidential control ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Heritage-Foundation)). Fearing a loss of support among moderate and independent voters, Trump's campaign sought to distance itself from the project, and Trump himself eventually disavowed it, claiming he knew nothing about Project 2025 or the people involved in its creation — a claim Britannica characterises as false, noting that many of the project's recommendations reflect policies Trump pursued as president or promises he made during his 2024 campaign, and that many of its contributors, organisers, and promoters are Trump supporters or former Trump administration officials ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Heritage-Foundation)). E&E News likewise records that Heritage and its policy road map became flashpoints in the 2024 presidential contest as Democrats seized upon Project 2025 to criticise Republican policies ([E&E News](https://www.eenews.net/articles/the-first-version-of-project-2025/)).

## From blueprints to briefs: the Legal Action Center

On July 8, 2026, Heritage launched the Legal Action Center, which it said will advocate for liberty across all areas of public-interest litigation, and named Justin Butterfield as its director ([Heritage](https://www.heritage.org/press/the-heritage-foundation-launches-legal-action-center-names-justin-butterfield-director)). Drawing on Heritage's legal and policy expertise, the new litigation centre will fight for conservative principles on issues such as freedom of speech, religious liberty, election integrity, the administrative state, the right to life, medical freedom, and parental rights ([Heritage](https://www.heritage.org/press/the-heritage-foundation-launches-legal-action-center-names-justin-butterfield-director)). Roger Severino, Heritage's vice president for economic and domestic policy, framed the move in Heritage's own terms: "Heritage's strength is getting conservative policies enacted, and we have added a vital tool for enshrining those policy wins in court precedents that will last for generations" ([Heritage](https://www.heritage.org/press/the-heritage-foundation-launches-legal-action-center-names-justin-butterfield-director)). Butterfield had previously served as senior advisor for HIPAA and senior advisor for Conscience and Religious Freedom in the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, and Lea Patterson joined as senior litigation counsel, having served on the litigation team for *Carson v. Makin*, a 2022 Supreme Court decision on school choice and religious freedom ([Heritage](https://www.heritage.org/press/the-heritage-foundation-launches-legal-action-center-names-justin-butterfield-director)).

The centre's first case arrived within weeks, and it ties Heritage directly to the FactsLedger source article for this round. Lawyers from Heritage and [Alliance Defending Freedom](/organizations/alliance-defending-freedom/) filed paperwork in July 2026 to join the legal team representing the Elizabeth School District, which was sued after removing books from its school libraries, about five months before a December jury trial was set to begin in federal district court ([Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/colorado/2026/07/21/heritage-foundation-alliance-defending-freedom-lawyers-join-book-ban-lawsuit/)). A statement provided by a district spokesman said the district is the first client of Heritage's new Legal Action Center, which was created for cases like the library book lawsuit, and quoted the district as saying it had assembled its legal team "to mount a vigorous, well-resourced defense of its policies on the merits" ([Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/colorado/2026/07/21/heritage-foundation-alliance-defending-freedom-lawyers-join-book-ban-lawsuit/)). The suit on the other side was brought in December 2024 by the [American Civil Liberties Union](/organizations/aclu/) of Colorado on behalf of two students, a chapter of the [NAACP](/organizations/naacp/), and an authors group, alleging that the removals violate students' First Amendment rights to access information and ideas ([Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/colorado/2026/07/21/heritage-foundation-alliance-defending-freedom-lawyers-join-book-ban-lawsuit/)). Heritage's Legal Action Center described its goals on social media as defending "free speech, religious liberty, election integrity, parental rights, medical freedom, the right to life, and the constitutional principles that safeguard liberty" ([Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/colorado/2026/07/21/heritage-foundation-alliance-defending-freedom-lawyers-join-book-ban-lawsuit/)). Where the 1981 *Mandate* handed a president a book of policies, the 2026 centre hands a school district lawyers — the same institution, moving from advising government to defending it in court.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim that Heritage wrote any administration's policy wholesale; it reports that its *Mandate* served as guidance and blueprint, and that supporters and critics characterise its influence differently. It does not repeat Project 2025 implementation-rate claims from 2026 commentary, because none appeared in a source read in full for this profile. It does not treat the district spokesman's "first client" statement as a Heritage announcement: the attribution is preserved, and a Heritage announcement naming Elizabeth was not read for this profile. It does not report an outcome for the Elizabeth case, only the July 2026 expansion of the defence team. And it does not repeat first-party promotional figures from Heritage's staff pages, such as revenue growth, which are not needed to establish the organisation's identity or history.
