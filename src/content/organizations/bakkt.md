## Overview

Bakkt is a U.S. cryptocurrency infrastructure company launched in 2018 with backing from Intercontinental Exchange — the operator of the New York Stock Exchange — as an institutional-grade platform for crypto custody and physically-settled bitcoin futures ([The Block](https://www.tradingview.com/news/the_block:a5685dfe7094b:0-bakkt-stock-rallies-20-amid-plans-to-acquire-a-stablecoin-infra-firm-founded-by-bakkt-s-co-ceo/)). In October 2021 it went public on the NYSE by merging with special-purpose acquisition company VPC Impact Acquisition Holdings — choosing the SPAC route where rival [Coinbase](/organizations/coinbase/) had chosen a direct Nasdaq listing — with ICE holding about 68% economic interest and a minority voting interest in the combined company ([Forkast](https://forkast.news/crypto-trading-platform-bakkt-goes-public/)).

## From bitcoin futures to consumer crypto — and back to infrastructure

Bakkt's first act was institutional: custody in hard wallets for large investors, evolving into a marketplace for bitcoin futures trading ([Forkast](https://forkast.news/crypto-trading-platform-bakkt-goes-public/)). Its second act was consumer: a mobile app for spending bitcoin on everyday goods, a [Visa](/organizations/visa/) debit card, and a Google partnership letting cardholders spend bitcoin anywhere accepting Google Pay while Bakkt built analytics on Google Cloud ([Forkast](https://forkast.news/crypto-trading-platform-bakkt-goes-public/)).

The third act, beginning in 2025, was a deliberate unbuilding of the second. Bakkt pivoted to what The Block describes as a "B2B2C" turnkey operator — infrastructure for traditional institutions entering crypto rather than a consumer brand. It sold its loyalty rewards business, acquired money-transmitter licenses covering all 50 U.S. states to support crypto trading, transfers, and settlement, launched a bitcoin treasury financed through a public offering, and brought crypto veteran Mike Alfred onto its board ([The Block](https://www.tradingview.com/news/the_block:a5685dfe7094b:0-bakkt-stock-rallies-20-amid-plans-to-acquire-a-stablecoin-infra-firm-founded-by-bakkt-s-co-ceo/)).

## The CEO's own company — and the committee that approved buying it

The pivot's centerpiece was a related-party transaction. In March 2025 Bakkt named Akshay Naheta — founder of stablecoin-infrastructure provider Distributed Technologies Research (DTR) — as co-CEO alongside a commercial partnership between the firms; Naheta later became sole CEO ([The Block](https://www.tradingview.com/news/the_block:a5685dfe7094b:0-bakkt-stock-rallies-20-amid-plans-to-acquire-a-stablecoin-infra-firm-founded-by-bakkt-s-co-ceo/)). Bakkt then agreed to acquire DTR outright, issuing over 9 million Class A shares to DTR's shareholders — a deal in which the buyer was acquiring its own chief executive's company. An independent special board committee of directors Colleen Brown and Mike Alfred approved the transaction, and ICE — by then holding about 31% of Bakkt's Class A shares — agreed to vote for it ([The Block](https://www.tradingview.com/news/the_block:a5685dfe7094b:0-bakkt-stock-rallies-20-amid-plans-to-acquire-a-stablecoin-infra-firm-founded-by-bakkt-s-co-ceo/)). Naheta framed it as completing "the transformation of the company into a unified global financial infrastructure platform" for stablecoin settlement, payments, and banking use cases ([The Block](https://www.tradingview.com/news/the_block:a5685dfe7094b:0-bakkt-stock-rallies-20-amid-plans-to-acquire-a-stablecoin-infra-firm-founded-by-bakkt-s-co-ceo/)). In January 2026 the company simplified its name from Bakkt Holdings, Inc. to Bakkt, Inc. ([The Block](https://www.tradingview.com/news/the_block:a5685dfe7094b:0-bakkt-stock-rallies-20-amid-plans-to-acquire-a-stablecoin-infra-firm-founded-by-bakkt-s-co-ceo/)).
