## Overview

The Department of Housing and Urban Development opened its doors with a civil-rights first. When President Lyndon B. Johnson elevated the housing agencies to cabinet rank in 1966, he named Robert C. Weaver as the department's first secretary — making Weaver the first African-American cabinet secretary in American history ([Congressional Record](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-144/issue-106/senate-section/article/S9530-1)). Weaver, a Harvard-trained economist who had advised three presidents and served in Franklin Roosevelt's informal "Black Cabinet," had been tapped by President Kennedy in 1961 to head the Housing and Home Finance Agency — then "the highest Federal post ever occupied by an African-American" ([Congressional Record](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-144/issue-106/senate-section/article/S9530-1)). In 1998, Congress named the department's headquarters at 451 Seventh Street SW the Robert C. Weaver Federal Building ([Congressional Record](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-144/issue-106/senate-section/article/S9530-1)). Weaver wrote four books on urban affairs and later served as president of Baruch College; he died in July 1997 ([Congressional Record](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-144/issue-106/senate-section/article/S9530-1)).

The department itself was created on September 9, 1965, "to allow the federal government to tackle urban problems including substandard and deteriorating housing in a coordinated manner" ([HUD](https://archives.hud.gov/hud50/hud50.hud.gov/hud_history_timeline/index.html)). It was an initiative begun under President John F. Kennedy and completed by Johnson, consolidating five independent federal housing and community development agencies: the Federal Housing Administration (1934), the Public Housing Administration (1937), the Federal National Mortgage Association — Fannie Mae (1934), the Urban Renewal Administration (1949), and the Community Facilities Administration ([HUD](https://archives.hud.gov/hud50/hud50.hud.gov/hud_history_timeline/index.html)).

## The scale of the mission

The numbers HUD claims for its programs are among the largest in domestic policy. Since 1934, the FHA and HUD have insured over 44 million home mortgages and approximately 50,000 multifamily project mortgages ([HUD](https://archives.hud.gov/hud50/hud50.hud.gov/hud_history_timeline/index.html)). In a twenty-year span, the department provided housing assistance to more than 35 million individuals through public housing, Housing Choice Vouchers (Section 8), project-based rental assistance and supportive-housing programs ([HUD](https://archives.hud.gov/hud50/hud50.hud.gov/hud_history_timeline/index.html)). The Community Development Block Grant program, running since 1974, has awarded more than $144 billion to state and local governments, and since the 1987 Stewart B. McKinney Homeless Assistance Act, HUD has directed more than $14 billion to programs combating homelessness ([HUD](https://archives.hud.gov/hud50/hud50.hud.gov/hud_history_timeline/index.html)). The HOME Investment Partnerships Program, a federal block grant to states and localities, created roughly 1.2 million affordable housing units ([HUD](https://archives.hud.gov/hud50/hud50.hud.gov/hud_history_timeline/index.html)).

## Two secretaries, two eras

The cabinet post Weaver created has been held by figures who later became nationally prominent. [Andrew Cuomo](/people/andrew-cuomo/) was the nation's 11th secretary of Housing and Urban Development, unanimously confirmed by the Senate and serving from January 29, 1997 to January 20, 2001 — the full second Clinton term ([HUD](https://archives.hud.gov/secretaries/cuomo/cuomo.cfm)).

The Clinton-era department also employed a future political strategist: [Adrienne Elrod](/people/adrienne-elrod/) began her political career in the Clinton administration, serving in both the White House Office of Intergovernmental Affairs and the Department of Housing and Urban Development, according to her professional biography ([Leading Authorities](https://www.leadingauthorities.com/speakers/adrienne-elrod/download-profile)).

The 19th secretary is Scott Turner, confirmed by the Senate on February 5, 2025. A former nine-season NFL player — with Washington, San Diego and Denver — and Texas state legislator (2013–2017), Turner had previously served as Executive Director of the White House Opportunity and Revitalization Council ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/119/meeting/house/118872/witnesses/HHRG-119-BA00-Bio-TurnerS-20260121.pdf)).
