## Overview

Marqeta is the card-issuing platform that fintech rode into the embedded-finance era — and whose fortunes were made, and complicated, by a single customer. Founded in Oakland, California in 2010 by PropertyBridge co-founder Jason Gardner, Marqeta built a programmable issuer-processing platform that lets companies issue physical and virtual cards through open APIs rather than legacy bank processors ([Bloomberg via Bank Automation News](https://bankautomationnews.com/allposts/payments/payments-processor-marqeta-climbs-after-1-23-billion-ipo/)). Its defining relationship was with Block's Square and Cash App: Square accounted for 70% of Marqeta's net revenue in 2020, according to the company's SEC filings cited at the IPO ([Bloomberg via Bank Automation News](https://bankautomationnews.com/allposts/payments/payments-processor-marqeta-climbs-after-1-23-billion-ipo/)). By the second quarter of 2026, that figure had fallen to 41% — down five percentage points from a year earlier — as Marqeta posted its second consecutive quarter of GAAP profitability and pushed into stablecoin-backed cards and European money movement ([MarketBeat via Sharewise](https://www.sharewise.com/us/news_articles/Marqeta_Q2_Earnings_Call_Highlights_MarketBeat_20260805_0304)).

## From $4.3B private valuation to a $16B debut

By 2020 Marqeta had already become the infrastructure behind much of consumer fintech's card boom. Reuters reported that August that the Oakland company — then backed by Goldman Sachs and [Visa](/organizations/visa/) and serving Square, Uber and Instacart — was hiring banks for an IPO after raising $150 million at a $4.3 billion valuation; revenue had doubled in 2019 to around $300 million, and more than 140 million cards had been issued through its platform ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-marqeta-ipoexclusive/exclusive-goldman-sachs-backed-payments-startupmarqeta-prepares-for-ipo-idUSKBN24A37L/)). The listing came in June 2021: 45.5 million shares priced at $27, raising $1.23 billion and giving Marqeta a market value of about $16 billion on debut, with Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan leading the sale ([Bloomberg via Bank Automation News](https://bankautomationnews.com/allposts/payments/payments-processor-marqeta-climbs-after-1-23-billion-ipo/)). The customer roster named at the time — Uber, DoorDash, Instacart — plus a JPMorgan relationship that put Marqeta behind commercial-card use in Apple Wallet, showed a company embedded in Big Tech's money movement, but the SEC filings told the concentrated truth: one customer, Square, was 70% of net revenue ([Bloomberg via Bank Automation News](https://bankautomationnews.com/allposts/payments/payments-processor-marqeta-climbs-after-1-23-billion-ipo/)).

## Diversification, slowly: 2025–2026

Marqeta's post-IPO years were defined by the long attempt to grow out from under Block. In February 2025 the company made two moves at once: it agreed to acquire TransactPay, a UK/European e-money institution and BIN sponsor live in 25 countries across 16 currencies and a principal member of [Mastercard](/organizations/mastercard/) and Visa, and it parted ways with CEO Simon Khalaf after two years, installing CFO Mike Milotich as interim CEO ([Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/45578/marqeta-to-buy-transactpay-for-european-push-makes-ceo-change)). The TransactPay deal closed in 2025 and became the backbone of Marqeta's European push: a partnership with Banking Circle extended account and money-movement capabilities into 30 additional European countries, and Expensify used the new rails to take its corporate card offering from the US into the UK and EU ([MarketBeat via Sharewise](https://www.sharewise.com/us/news_articles/Marqeta_Q2_Earnings_Call_Highlights_MarketBeat_20260805_0304)).

By mid-2026 the diversification was measurable but incomplete. In Q2 2026 Marqeta processed $120 billion in total processing volume, up 32% year over year — its fourth straight quarter above 30% TPV growth — with net revenue of $176 million and gross profit of $122 million, both up 17%, and $8 million in GAAP net income, the second consecutive GAAP-profitable quarter ([MarketBeat via Sharewise](https://www.sharewise.com/us/news_articles/Marqeta_Q2_Earnings_Call_Highlights_MarketBeat_20260805_0304)). The same call disclosed the other side of the story: non-Block TPV was growing more than twice as fast as Block TPV, Block had fallen to 41% of net revenue, and new Cash App card issuance had begun declining around mid-June — roughly 10% below trend, with management expecting "little to no new issuance" by year-end as Block diversified providers ([MarketBeat via Sharewise](https://www.sharewise.com/us/news_articles/Marqeta_Q2_Earnings_Call_Highlights_MarketBeat_20260805_0304)). Milotich framed provider diversification as common practice among large customers and noted Block was still growing across Cash App, Square and [Afterpay](/organizations/afterpay/) ([MarketBeat via Sharewise](https://www.sharewise.com/us/news_articles/Marqeta_Q2_Earnings_Call_Highlights_MarketBeat_20260805_0304)).

## The 2026 pivot: stablecoins, credit, and a reverse split

Marqeta's 2026 strategy reaches beyond cards. In September 2026 it partnered with BVNK, the stablecoin infrastructure company, to let customers embed US-dollar stablecoin capabilities into wallets, cards and financial products — users transact in digital dollars at millions of merchants on standard card rails while BVNK handles stablecoin movement and Marqeta keeps issuance, acceptance and bank and network relationships ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/cryptocurrency/2026/marqeta-turns-to-bvnk-for-stablecoin-backed-card-capabilities/)). The deal has a network-level dimension: Mastercard is one of Marqeta's major network partners and acquired BVNK earlier in 2026, and all three companies back the Open USD stablecoin standard ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/cryptocurrency/2026/marqeta-turns-to-bvnk-for-stablecoin-backed-card-capabilities/)). The Q2 call had already disclosed a parallel partnership with zerohash, plus expansion into non-card rails — ACH, real-time payments, push-to-card and wires in the US, UK and EU — and a Real-Time Decisioning fraud product whose gross profit grew more than 80% in the first half, incorporating transaction data from partners including [Adyen](/organizations/adyen/), Riskified and Signifyd ([MarketBeat via Sharewise](https://www.sharewise.com/us/news_articles/Marqeta_Q2_Earnings_Call_Highlights_MarketBeat_20260805_0304)).

The quarter also marked a financial reset: a 1-for-4 reverse stock split took effect June 30 (GAAP EPS of $0.07 reflects it), the board authorized a new $150 million share repurchase on August 3, and the company ended the quarter with $700 million in cash and short-term investments ([MarketBeat via Sharewise](https://www.sharewise.com/us/news_articles/Marqeta_Q2_Earnings_Call_Highlights_MarketBeat_20260805_0304)). Management guided to slower second-half growth — 12–13% full-year net revenue growth and GAAP net income in the high-$20 million range — citing the Cash App issuance shift, a large renewal's timing, and tougher comparisons in lending and buy-now-pay-later, where a BNPL customer was load-balancing single-use virtual-card volume among providers ([MarketBeat via Sharewise](https://www.sharewise.com/us/news_articles/Marqeta_Q2_Earnings_Call_Highlights_MarketBeat_20260805_0304)). Three credit programs are slated to launch over the coming quarters: a revolving consumer co-brand, a secured credit-builder with BNPL functionality, and a commercial charge card ([MarketBeat via Sharewise](https://www.sharewise.com/us/news_articles/Marqeta_Q2_Earnings_Call_Highlights_MarketBeat_20260805_0304)).
