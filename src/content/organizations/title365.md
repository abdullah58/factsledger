## Overview

Title365 is a national title insurance and settlement services provider in the [United States](/places/united-states/), founded in 2009 to give mortgage lenders and servicers a centralized, technology-driven way to handle title, escrow and default services. It does not underwrite policies itself: as a title insurance agency it searches titles and places policies with third-party underwriters, while providing escrow, closing and settlement services on real estate transactions and title services on home-equity loans ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/06/09/covius-inks-agreement-to-acquire-blends-title365-business/)). In sixteen years it changed hands four times — bought for $36 million, sold for about $422 million, then sold again to Covius — a valuation swing that tracks the American mortgage cycle more closely than any strategy document ([ALTA](https://www.alta.org/news-and-publications/news/20250612-Covius-to-Acquire-Title365-from-Blend); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-breaks-into-title-insurance-with-latest-acquisition/)).

Covius completed the integration of Title365 into its settlement-services business in 2026 ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/covius-completes-integration-of-title365-operations/)).

## Founding and the centralized-title model

Title365 was founded in 2009 by a group of settlement-service veterans who bet that title work could be centralized: one national operation doing title, escrow and default fulfillment for lenders and servicers, instead of a patchwork of local offices ([ALTA](https://www.alta.org/news-and-publications/news/20210318-Blend-to-Acquire-Title365-from-Mr-Cooper-Group-in-500M-Deal)). Its holding company was Experience 1, Inc., and by 2012 it was headquartered in Newport Beach, California, with offices in California, Florida, Texas and Arizona, under chief executive Michael Tafoya ([Greater Phoenix In Business Magazine](https://inbusinessphx.com/just-in/title365s-expansion-continues-into-arizona)). That year it expanded into Arizona with three full-service offices in Maricopa County — Tempe, Gilbert and Phoenix — led by industry veteran Anthony Nalbone ([Greater Phoenix In Business Magazine](https://inbusinessphx.com/just-in/title365s-expansion-continues-into-arizona)). Its pitch was speed and transparency: a title order platform with Ordertrac, which let every party follow an order from start to finish with real-time updates by email or text message ([Greater Phoenix In Business Magazine](https://inbusinessphx.com/just-in/title365s-expansion-continues-into-arizona)).

## The Nationstar years and the Stewart split

In 2014, Solutionstar Holdings LLC — an indirect wholly-owned subsidiary of Nationstar Mortgage Holdings — purchased Title365 for $36 million, folding it into the servicing giant's orbit as a division of Xome, its real estate services company ([ALTA](https://www.alta.org/news-and-publications/news/20250612-Covius-to-Acquire-Title365-from-Blend); [Houlihan Lokey](https://hl.com/about-us/transactions/houlihan-lokey-advises-mr-cooper-group/)). HousingWire dates the same purchase to 2015, a discrepancy this profile flags rather than resolves ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-breaks-into-title-insurance-with-latest-acquisition/)). Nationstar later rebranded as [Mr. Cooper Group](/organizations/mr-cooper-group/), and Title365 stayed inside that group for seven years. In 2017 the company was split in two: Stewart Information Services acquired Title365's retail branch division, which operated primarily in Southern California, leaving the centralized national operation — the part that served lenders and servicers — under Mr. Cooper ([ALTA](https://www.alta.org/news-and-publications/news/20210318-Blend-to-Acquire-Title365-from-Mr-Cooper-Group-in-500M-Deal)). The split defined what Title365 would become: not a consumer-facing branch network, but a behind-the-scenes fulfillment engine for the mortgage industry.

## Blend's $422 million bet

In March 2021, digital lending software provider Blend announced it would acquire Title365 from Mr. Cooper Group for approximately $422 million, reflecting an enterprise value of $500 million, with Mr. Cooper retaining a 9.9% ownership interest; the deal closed on 30 June 2021 ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-breaks-into-title-insurance-with-latest-acquisition/); [Houlihan Lokey](https://hl.com/about-us/transactions/houlihan-lokey-advises-mr-cooper-group/)). Mr. Cooper expected an after-tax gain of about $350 million, and its chief executive Jay Bray said the sale let the company focus on servicing and originations ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-breaks-into-title-insurance-with-latest-acquisition/)). Blend's president [Tim Mayopoulos](/people/timothy-mayopoulos/), a former chief executive of [Fannie Mae](/organizations/fannie-mae/), said combining Blend's technology with Title365's operational expertise would "minimize costs and inefficiencies by deeply integrating title and settlement into the loan process" ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-breaks-into-title-insurance-with-latest-acquisition/)). Blend financed the deal with a $225 million term loan and $25 million in revolving credit, and Title365 president Kristen Estrella continued to lead the business across its US and India offices ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/06/09/covius-inks-agreement-to-acquire-blends-title365-business/); [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-breaks-into-title-insurance-with-latest-acquisition/)).

## Boom, bust and the debt

The timing looked perfect, then turned fast. In the 2020 refinance boom Title365 had generated $212 million in revenue and counted six of the top 12 US mortgage lenders as clients, and Blend went public in July 2021, shortly after the acquisition closed ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/06/09/covius-inks-agreement-to-acquire-blends-title365-business/); [Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/04/01/mortgage-tech-provider-blend-pulls-back-on-hiring-eyes-title365-layoffs/)). The deal helped lift Blend's 2021 revenue by 144% to $234.5 million, and in the fourth quarter title insurance was its single biggest revenue source, generating 55% of the $81 million it reported ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/04/01/mortgage-tech-provider-blend-pulls-back-on-hiring-eyes-title365-layoffs/)). But operating expenses rose 129% to $313.2 million, Blend posted a $169.1 million net loss for 2021, and the acquisition had added 587 employees in [Chennai](/places/chennai/), India, taking Blend's headcount to 2,276 ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/04/01/mortgage-tech-provider-blend-pulls-back-on-hiring-eyes-title365-layoffs/)).

Rising mortgage rates crushed refinancing, and by April 2022 Blend was pulling back "very hard" on hiring and contemplating layoffs at Title365, whose legacy business was heavily refinance-driven ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/04/01/mortgage-tech-provider-blend-pulls-back-on-hiring-eyes-title365-layoffs/)). The acquisition debt lingered longer than the boom: in 2023 Blend amended its terms, extending maturity to 30 June 2027 if certain conditions were met; they were not, and in 2024 Blend raised $150 million from Haveli Investments to pay the debt off, giving Haveli a board seat and convertible preferred stock ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/06/09/covius-inks-agreement-to-acquire-blends-title365-business/)).

## The Covius sale and integration

In May 2025 Blend entered exclusive negotiations to sell Title365, and on 9 June 2025 Covius Services LLC signed a definitive agreement to acquire Title365 Holding Co., with terms undisclosed and closing subject to third-party consents and regulatory approvals ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/06/09/covius-inks-agreement-to-acquire-blends-title365-business/)). Covius chairman and chief executive Rob Clements said Title365 would "further expand and scale Covius' presence in the origination title space and complement our strength in loss mitigation and default title" ([ALTA](https://www.alta.org/news-and-publications/news/20250612-Covius-to-Acquire-Title365-from-Blend)). Title365 was licensed in 43 states and served top-10 lenders and servicers, capital-market participants and home-equity originators, with products covering first and second mortgage origination and default title ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-sells-title365-to-covius-services/)). The sale also showed Blend's dependence on its former owner: Mr. Cooper accounted for 17% of Blend's 2024 revenue, including 47% of Title365's revenue — $21.6 million — just as Rocket Companies was acquiring Mr. Cooper ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-sells-title365-to-covius-services/)). President Kirby Hulbert and his team joined Covius' settlement-services team, while Title365 kept serving Blend customers ([ALTA](https://www.alta.org/news-and-publications/news/20250612-Covius-to-Acquire-Title365-from-Blend)). Covius completed the integration of Title365 into Covius Settlement Services in 2026, offering origination, home-equity and default title through Covius Connect's APIs, and Blend officially exited the title business ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/covius-completes-integration-of-title365-operations/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Title365?** Title365 is a national title insurance and settlement services provider founded in the United States in 2009 ([ALTA](https://www.alta.org/news-and-publications/news/20250612-Covius-to-Acquire-Title365-from-Blend)). As a title insurance agency it searches titles and places policies with third-party underwriters, and it provides escrow, closing and settlement services plus title services on home-equity loans ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/06/09/covius-inks-agreement-to-acquire-blends-title365-business/)).

**Who owns Title365 now?** Covius Services acquired Title365 from Blend under a definitive agreement signed 9 June 2025 and completed the integration of its operations into Covius Settlement Services in 2026, while Title365 continues to provide integrated title and closing services for Blend customers ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/covius-completes-integration-of-title365-operations/)). Financial terms of the Covius deal were not disclosed ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/06/09/covius-inks-agreement-to-acquire-blends-title365-business/)).

**How much did Blend pay for Title365?** About $422 million in 2021, reflecting a $500 million enterprise value, with Mr. Cooper retaining a 9.9% ownership interest ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-breaks-into-title-insurance-with-latest-acquisition/)). Houlihan Lokey, Mr. Cooper's advisor, puts the cash consideration at $450 million — the difference appears to be $31 million in adjustments ([Houlihan Lokey](https://hl.com/about-us/transactions/houlihan-lokey-advises-mr-cooper-group/)).

**Why did Blend sell Title365?** Blend said it was pursuing a "software-first" business model, with chief executive [Nima Ghamsari](/people/nima-ghamsari/) saying the sale lets Blend "sharpen our focus on becoming the leading software platform for financial services" ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/06/09/covius-inks-agreement-to-acquire-blends-title365-business/)). Title365's revenue had fallen 59% from $113.6 million in 2022 to $46.3 million in 2024 as refinancing collapsed ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/06/09/covius-inks-agreement-to-acquire-blends-title365-business/)).
