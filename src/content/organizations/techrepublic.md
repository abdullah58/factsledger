## Overview

TechRepublic is an online trade publication and social community for IT professionals, offering technology news, advice, best practices and peer-to-peer help to the people who decide what technology their organisations buy and run ([ASBPE](https://azbeeawards.secure-platform.com/a/gallery/rounds/35/details/10548)). Founded in Louisville, Kentucky, in 1997 by Tom Cottingham and Kim Spalding, it launched as a website in May 1999, passed through [Gartner](/organizations/gartner/) and CNET, and has been owned by the Nashville B2B media company TechnologyAdvice since August 2021 ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/forums/discussions/william-ziff-grandfather-of-tr/); [PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/technologyadvice-acquires-techrepublic-from-red-ventures-expanding-global-capabilities-and-audiences-810862944.html)).

TechRepublic is a business-technology publication, not a consumer one — a distinction its history makes clear. It is not ZDNet, although the two are often confused: both ended up inside CNET around the same time, but as separate acquisitions, and TechRepublic's own staff history stresses the point ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/forums/discussions/william-ziff-grandfather-of-tr/)). Nor is it CNET itself, the consumer-tech site that owned it for twenty years, or TechnologyAdvice, the parent company that owns it now. And despite the shared CNET lineage, later changes to CNET's ownership do not apply to it: TechRepublic had already left the CNET portfolio for TechnologyAdvice in 2021 ([PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/technologyadvice-acquires-techrepublic-from-red-ventures-expanding-global-capabilities-and-audiences-810862944.html)).

## From Louisville newsletters to the web

TechRepublic began as a newsletter business, not a website. Cottingham was co-founder and president of The Cobb Group, a Louisville-based publisher of technology-focused newsletters, and Spalding was its vice president of finance; New York-based Ziff-Davis Publishing had acquired The Cobb Group in 1991 ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/forums/discussions/william-ziff-grandfather-of-tr/)). Cottingham left The Cobb Group in 1996 and, with Spalding, began operating Narrow Cast Concepts, a travel-industry publisher. When Ziff-Davis decided in 1998 to relocate the former Cobb Group to Rochester, New York — and very few Louisville employees accepted the move — Cottingham saw an opening: he refocused Narrow Cast Concepts around IT newsletters, brought on several former Cobb Group co-workers, and Narrow Cast Concepts became TechRepublic, launching TechRepublic.com in May 1999 ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/forums/discussions/william-ziff-grandfather-of-tr/)).

The young site changed hands quickly. Stamford, Connecticut-based IT consulting firm Gartner Inc. acquired TechRepublic in March 2000, according to the staff history; the same year, CNET Networks acquired ZDNet, the online arm of Ziff-Davis Publishing ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/forums/discussions/william-ziff-grandfather-of-tr/)). A year later, in April 2001, CNET announced it would acquire TechRepublic from Gartner for $23 million ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/cnet-networks-inc)). The deal added 1.5 million registered IT professionals to CNET Networks' base of 3.4 million professionals — a combined reach that, according to Nielsen/NetRatings, represented 66 percent of the IT professional market ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/cnet-networks-inc)).

CNET held TechRepublic for two decades, through the company's own transformations: CNET Networks became part of CBS Interactive, and the CNET Media Group later passed to Red Ventures, which owned TechRepublic immediately before the 2021 sale ([ASBPE](https://azbeeawards.secure-platform.com/a/gallery/rounds/35/details/10548); [PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/technologyadvice-acquires-techrepublic-from-red-ventures-expanding-global-capabilities-and-audiences-810862944.html)). The prices of those two parent-company deals — CBS's acquisition of CNET Networks and Red Ventures' purchase of the CNET Media Group — were not verified from sources read in full for this profile and are not stated here.

The 2021 sale moved TechRepublic to TechnologyAdvice, a Nashville-based full-service B2B media company. Announced on August 9, 2021, the deal included a global team of technology and media professionals who would augment TechnologyAdvice's growing teams in the [United States](/places/united-states/), the UK, Singapore and Australia ([PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/technologyadvice-acquires-techrepublic-from-red-ventures-expanding-global-capabilities-and-audiences-810862944.html)). TechnologyAdvice founder and CEO Rob Bellenfant said the acquisition would let the company's 600-plus technology clients "more intelligently engage with over 10 million technology users every month," and framed it as the step that gave TechnologyAdvice "a truly global operation" ([Greater Nashville Technology Council](https://technologycouncil.com/technologyadvice-acquires-techrepublic-from-red-ventures-expanding-global-capabilities-and-audiences/)). The move capped eighteen months of expansion for TechnologyAdvice that had begun in early 2020 with its acquisition of QuinStreet's B2B media business unit ([PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/technologyadvice-acquires-techrepublic-from-red-ventures-expanding-global-capabilities-and-audiences-810862944.html)). TechnologyAdvice's other brands include eWeek, Datamation, Developer.com and Webopedia ([Greater Nashville Technology Council](https://technologycouncil.com/technologyadvice-acquires-techrepublic-from-red-ventures-expanding-global-capabilities-and-audiences/)).

## What TechRepublic publishes and who reads it

TechRepublic describes itself as a multiplatform publication that "helps professionals make smart decisions about technology," with over 10 million readers in over 150 countries — a scale that made it, in its own 2018 award entry, "one of the world's most widely read publications on B2B technology since its launch in May 1999" ([ASBPE](https://azbeeawards.secure-platform.com/a/gallery/rounds/35/details/10548)). Its users run from entrepreneurs and small business owners to software engineers and CIOs, and the site serves them with news, tips, best practices, case studies and peer-to-peer advice ([ASBPE](https://azbeeawards.secure-platform.com/a/gallery/rounds/35/details/10548)). By the time of the 2021 sale, its web, video and audio content reached a community of more than 18 million registered users ([PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/technologyadvice-acquires-techrepublic-from-red-ventures-expanding-global-capabilities-and-audiences-810862944.html)).

The audience figures have moved with the years and the metric: 1.5 million registered users at the 2001 CNET deal, over 10 million readers in over 150 countries in 2018, more than 18 million registered users in 2021, and 3.9 million unique visitors a month as of January 2022 ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/cnet-networks-inc); [ASBPE](https://azbeeawards.secure-platform.com/a/gallery/rounds/35/details/10548); [PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/technologyadvice-acquires-techrepublic-from-red-ventures-expanding-global-capabilities-and-audiences-810862944.html); [Communiqué PR](https://www.communiquepr.com/publication-spotlight-techrepublic/)). The citation-magnet figure is the 2021 one: 18 million registered users, plus 10 million technology users a month for TechnologyAdvice's clients — a large, registered, professional audience is exactly what a B2B media buyer pays for, and it explains why the site changed hands as a strategic asset rather than a distressed one.

Its editorial model is organised around its readers' jobs. TechRepublic runs a family of virtual communities called "republics" that organise coverage by job function, mixing expert niche content with peer-to-peer advice, and it has offered international editions in the United Kingdom, Australia and Japan ([Communiqué PR](https://www.communiquepr.com/publication-spotlight-techrepublic/)). Its readers include CEOs, CIOs, CFOs, CMOs, IT directors, IT managers and IT professionals — anyone whose job requires making decisions about technology ([Communiqué PR](https://www.communiquepr.com/publication-spotlight-techrepublic/)). It publishes several newsletters, including Developer Essentials, Innovation and Executive Briefing, covering cloud, data analytics, AI, 5G, cybersecurity, Microsoft and Apple — and it does not publish contributed content ([Communiqué PR](https://www.communiquepr.com/publication-spotlight-techrepublic/)).

The formats go beyond news. Content spans daily features, tips and tutorials, long-form reports, explainer videos, multimedia slideshows and social updates, all aimed at helping users "make great technology decisions that can improve their organizations" ([ASBPE](https://azbeeawards.secure-platform.com/a/gallery/rounds/35/details/10548)). In 2017 the newsroom quadrupled the amount of video it created each week, produced nearly 100 "cheat sheets" and "smart person's guides" on the most important new technologies and products, and published investigative reports on Tesla's move into self-driving cars, the escalation of cyber warfare, the state of women in computer science, and the battle between Google Fiber and AT&T over gigabit internet ([ASBPE](https://azbeeawards.secure-platform.com/a/gallery/rounds/35/details/10548)). That work was recognised in 2018, when TechRepublic won Website of the Year at the Azbee Awards of Excellence, awarded to Global Editor in Chief Jason Hiner, Managing Editor Bill Detwiler and Senior Product Manager Dan Kamas, with CBS Interactive listed as the publishing company ([ASBPE](https://azbeeawards.secure-platform.com/a/gallery/rounds/35/details/10548)).

TechRepublic is still a working source in current technology reporting, not just a historical name. FactsLedger's [ClickUp](/organizations/clickup/) profile cites TechRepublic's reporting on ClickUp's 2026 staff cuts and AI restructuring — including its sceptical note that cutting headcount lowers costs quickly but does not by itself demonstrate that AI improved work quality, and its reporting that one growth operations manager oversaw 37 AI agents himself. A publication founded as a Louisville newsletter business in 1997 remains, nearly three decades later, a cited authority on how technology changes work.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is TechRepublic?** It is an online trade publication and community for IT professionals, providing technology news, advice, best practices and peer-to-peer help to IT decision-makers, from software engineers to CIOs ([ASBPE](https://azbeeawards.secure-platform.com/a/gallery/rounds/35/details/10548)).

**Who founded TechRepublic and when?** Tom Cottingham and Kim Spalding founded it in Louisville, Kentucky, in 1997, growing out of the Narrow Cast Concepts newsletter business; the TechRepublic.com website launched in May 1999 ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/forums/discussions/william-ziff-grandfather-of-tr/)).

**Who owns TechRepublic now?** TechnologyAdvice, a Nashville-based B2B media company, has owned it since August 9, 2021, when it purchased the brand from Red Ventures and CNET Media Group ([PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/technologyadvice-acquires-techrepublic-from-red-ventures-expanding-global-capabilities-and-audiences-810862944.html)).

**How big is TechRepublic's audience?** At the 2021 sale it had more than 18 million registered users, and TechnologyAdvice said its clients could reach over 10 million technology users a month through the combined audience ([PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/technologyadvice-acquires-techrepublic-from-red-ventures-expanding-global-capabilities-and-audiences-810862944.html)).

**Is TechRepublic the same as ZDNet or CNET?** No. ZDNet was a separate CNET acquisition from the same era, CNET was TechRepublic's owner from 2001 to 2021, and TechRepublic left the CNET portfolio for TechnologyAdvice in 2021 ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/forums/discussions/william-ziff-grandfather-of-tr/); [PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/technologyadvice-acquires-techrepublic-from-red-ventures-expanding-global-capabilities-and-audiences-810862944.html)).
