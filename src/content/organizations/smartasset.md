## Overview

SmartAsset is a New York-based financial technology company that turned personal-finance content into a lead-generation marketplace: it publishes financial guides, calculators and interactive tools that attract consumers, then sells those consumers — as leads — to financial advisors ([WealthManagement.com](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/financial-technology/smartasset-reaches-unicorn-status)). Founded in 2011 by CEO Michael Carvin, it raised $110 million in a 2021 Series D led by TTV Capital at a valuation over $1 billion, with Citi Ventures, New York Life Ventures, Javelin Venture Partners, Contour and others participating, bringing total funding past $161 million ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/corporates-assist-smartasset-in-110m/)).

The model is a two-sided funnel. Consumers arrive through SmartAsset's content; an in-house concierge team contacts them by phone, text and email to validate their information and intent, then matches them with up to three advisors via a "Live Connection" phone introduction led by a SmartAsset operator ([WealthManagement.com](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/financial-technology/smartasset-reaches-unicorn-status)). The company says its average matched investor is about 57 years old with $890,000 in investable assets, and claims it generates $1.5 billion in new closed assets under management per month ([WealthManagement.com](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/financial-technology/smartasset-reaches-unicorn-status)).

## The contested economics of buying customers

SmartAsset's unicorn status rests on advisors willing to pay for introductions — and advisor opinion is split. LPL Financial added the service to its vendor platform in 2021, and some advisors credit it with doubling their firms; Procyon Partners co-founder Jeff Farrar, whose firm manages over $1 billion, said closing just a handful of leads makes it cost-effective, though he restricts his filters to $1 million-plus prospects to screen out weaker ones ([WealthManagement.com](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/financial-technology/smartasset-reaches-unicorn-status)).

Others describe paying for strangers. "You end up paying for leads that know nothing about you, so retentions and closing rates are way lower," one advisor told WealthManagement.com, saying his own content marketing worked better for his under-40 clientele ([WealthManagement.com](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/financial-technology/smartasset-reaches-unicorn-status)). Another said prospects arrive "worn thin" — SmartAsset's own verification gauntlet of outbound calls and emails exhausts potential clients before the advisor ever speaks to them, "a factor [that] is not well published" ([WealthManagement.com](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/financial-technology/smartasset-reaches-unicorn-status)). Carvin's response: a significant share of prospects never get matched at all if they don't meet SmartAsset's standards, and the firm claims "negative churn," with successful advisors upselling faster than unsuccessful ones downsell ([WealthManagement.com](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/financial-technology/smartasset-reaches-unicorn-status)).
