## Overview

The Daily Stormer was an American neo-Nazi commentary and message-board website founded on July 4, 2013, by Andrew Anglin as a faster-paced replacement for his previous site, Total Fascism, taking its name from Der Stürmer, the Nazi tabloid newspaper of Julius Streicher ([Southern Poverty Law Center](https://starrindissent.com/2017/02/12/look-deep-enough-youll-find-this/)). It became the most visited website of the American radical right, was cut off by [Cloudflare](/organizations/cloudflare/) and a string of other internet providers in August 2017 after the deadly Unite the Right rally in Charlottesville, Virginia, and was ordered by a federal judge in 2019 to pay $14 million to a woman its readers had harassed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/08/16/digital-ocean-and-cloudflare-ditch-neo-nazi-client-the-daily-stormer/?ncid=rss); [NPR](https://www.wcmu.org/national-world-news/2019-07-17/federal-judge-awards-jewish-woman-14-million-in-case-against-neo-nazi-trolls)). After about thirteen years online, Anglin announced in 2026 that the site would close ([The Unz Review](https://www.unz.com/announcement/andrew-anglin-archives/)).

## Founding and rise

Anglin, who grew up in Worthington, Ohio, had run Total Fascism as a home for his own long-form essays on fascism, race, and antisemitic conspiracy theories; he judged it too slow and too long-winded for a younger audience and built The Daily Stormer around short, punchy, image-heavy stories that relied heavily on quoted material with exaggerated headlines ([Southern Poverty Law Center](https://starrindissent.com/2017/02/12/look-deep-enough-youll-find-this/)). The style borrowed openly from chan culture — memes, irony, and provocation — and it worked: the Southern Poverty Law Center (SPLC) documented that by July 2016, four months before Donald Trump's election win, The Daily Stormer had become the most popular English-language website of the radical right, eclipsing Stormfront, which had held that position since the early days of the internet ([Southern Poverty Law Center](https://starrindissent.com/2017/02/12/look-deep-enough-youll-find-this/)). The site was not only a publication. In 2016 Anglin moved it into the real world, building a "Troll Army" of local activist chapters that followed his lead in coordinated online harassment; the SPLC confirmed 31 distinct chapters were active by late 2016, and Anglin urged followers to prepare for a coming race war ([Southern Poverty Law Center](https://starrindissent.com/2017/02/12/look-deep-enough-youll-find-this/); [PhillyVoice](https://www.phillyvoice.com/splc-seven-hate-groups-operated-philly-last-year/)). The SPLC lists the site as a neo-Nazi group, and PhillyVoice's account of the SPLC's 2016 hate-group census describes it the same way ([PhillyVoice](https://www.phillyvoice.com/splc-seven-hate-groups-operated-philly-last-year/)).

## Charlottesville and the deplatforming

The site used its platform to help organize the violent white supremacist demonstration in Charlottesville on August 11–12, 2017, and afterwards celebrated the killing of counter-protester Heather Heyer, who died after a far-right supporter drove his car into a crowd of counter-protesters ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/08/16/digital-ocean-and-cloudflare-ditch-neo-nazi-client-the-daily-stormer/?ncid=rss)). The post mocking Heyer triggered a cascade of terminations within days. GoDaddy and Google were among the first to cut ties; the site briefly came back online through a new domain host before going down again ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/08/16/digital-ocean-and-cloudflare-ditch-neo-nazi-client-the-daily-stormer/?ncid=rss)). Cloud hosting platform DigitalOcean terminated the site and the pro-hate-speech crowdfunding platform Hatreon on August 16, 2017, saying its team had "investigated and determined this site violates our TOS by inciting violence or hate crimes" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/08/16/digital-ocean-and-cloudflare-ditch-neo-nazi-client-the-daily-stormer/?ncid=rss)). DigitalOcean later confirmed the site had first become a customer on December 22, 2016, drew abuse complaints the same day and the next, and had its server disabled on December 23, 2016 — meaning the August 2017 cutoff concerned a reappearance through a reseller ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/08/16/digital-ocean-and-cloudflare-ditch-neo-nazi-client-the-daily-stormer/?ncid=rss)).

Cloudflare's termination, also on August 16, 2017, was the most consequential, because the company had been providing the denial-of-service protection that kept the site online: Cloudflare stopped proxying its traffic, stopped answering DNS requests for its domains, and said it had "taken measures to ensure that they cannot sign up for Cloudflare's services ever again" ([Cloudflare Blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/why-we-terminated-daily-stormer/?ref=our.status.im)). Chief executive [Matthew Prince](/people/matthew-prince/) wrote that the tipping point was not the site's content as such — the company had "followed the law and remained content neutral as a network" for years — but the site's claim that Cloudflare secretly supported its ideology: "We could not remain neutral after these claims of secret support by Cloudflare" ([Cloudflare Blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/why-we-terminated-daily-stormer/?ref=our.status.im)). Prince called the decision arbitrary and warned that it set a dangerous precedent: with a handful of giant networks handling a large share of internet traffic — Cloudflare then handled around 10 percent of internet requests — content decisions by infrastructure companies would replace due process with vigilante justice by hackers launching DDoS attacks ([Cloudflare Blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/why-we-terminated-daily-stormer/?ref=our.status.im)). SendGrid and Zoho were among the other providers that cut ties that week ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/08/16/digital-ocean-and-cloudflare-ditch-neo-nazi-client-the-daily-stormer/?ncid=rss)). The deplatforming did not end the site: Cloudflare's own 2019 account notes that it "quickly came back online using a Cloudflare competitor" and that by 2019 its operators "bragged that they have more readers than ever" ([Cloudflare Blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/terminating-service-for-8chan/)).

## Legal consequences and closure

The site's harassment tactics, rather than its articles, produced its largest legal liability. The SPLC sued Anglin on behalf of Tanya Gersh, a Jewish real estate agent in Whitefish, Montana, alleging he had directed followers to target her and her family after a dispute involving the mother of white nationalist Richard Spencer ([NPR](https://www.wcmu.org/national-world-news/2019-07-17/federal-judge-awards-jewish-woman-14-million-in-case-against-neo-nazi-trolls)). Anglin instructed readers to publish Gersh's personal information and harass her with antisemitic messages, death threats, and repeated calls to her home; the judge ruled the conduct crossed from protected speech into threats to personal safety ([NPR](https://www.wcmu.org/national-world-news/2019-07-17/federal-judge-awards-jewish-woman-14-million-in-case-against-neo-nazi-trolls)). In July 2019 a federal judge ordered Anglin to pay Gersh $14 million in damages; Anglin refused to appear in court, his whereabouts were unknown, and whether the money would ever be paid was uncertain ([NPR](https://www.wcmu.org/national-world-news/2019-07-17/federal-judge-awards-jewish-woman-14-million-in-case-against-neo-nazi-trolls)). The site limped on for years after the deplatforming, and in 2026 Anglin announced it would close, publishing a post titled "The End of the Daily Stormer" ([The Unz Review](https://www.unz.com/announcement/andrew-anglin-archives/)). The account of the closure, which quotes Anglin's post, says the site had become financially nonviable after nine years of operating without access to mainstream internet infrastructure ([The Unz Review](https://www.unz.com/announcement/andrew-anglin-archives/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What was The Daily Stormer?**
An American neo-Nazi commentary and message-board website founded by Andrew Anglin on July 4, 2013, named after the Nazi tabloid Der Stürmer, and described by the Southern Poverty Law Center as the most popular English-language website of the radical right by 2016 ([Southern Poverty Law Center](https://starrindissent.com/2017/02/12/look-deep-enough-youll-find-this/)).

**Why did Cloudflare terminate The Daily Stormer?**
Cloudflare ended the site's service on August 16, 2017, after the Charlottesville rally. CEO Matthew Prince said the tipping point was the site's claim that Cloudflare secretly supported its ideology, which made the company's content-neutral stance untenable ([Cloudflare Blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/why-we-terminated-daily-stormer/?ref=our.status.im)).

**Did the 2017 deplatforming shut the site down?**
No. GoDaddy, Google, DigitalOcean, and Cloudflare all cut ties within days, but the site returned online through other providers, and by 2019 its operators claimed more readers than ever ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/08/16/digital-ocean-and-cloudflare-ditch-neo-nazi-client-the-daily-stormer/?ncid=rss); [Cloudflare Blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/terminating-service-for-8chan/)).

**What was the $14 million judgment about?**
A federal judge ordered founder Andrew Anglin to pay $14 million to Tanya Gersh, a Montana real estate agent, after he directed followers to harass her and her family with threats and antisemitic messages; Anglin refused to appear in court ([NPR](https://www.wcmu.org/national-world-news/2019-07-17/federal-judge-awards-jewish-woman-14-million-in-case-against-neo-nazi-trolls)).

**Is The Daily Stormer still online?**
Anglin announced in 2026 that the site would close after about thirteen years, citing cost and the effects of deplatforming — but the site's exact final status was not independently verified from a fully-read source this pass ([The Unz Review](https://www.unz.com/announcement/andrew-anglin-archives/)).
