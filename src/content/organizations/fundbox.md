## Overview

Fundbox is a San Francisco-based financial technology company founded in 2013 by Israeli entrepreneurs Eyal Shinar, Yuval Ariav and Tomer Michaeli, with its development center in Tel Aviv ([Haaretz](https://canary.haaretz.com/israel-news/business/2015-09-11/ty-article/.premium/kutcher-bezos-firm-invest-in-fundbox/0000017f-da75-d42c-afff-dff7322e0000)). It began as a direct lender advancing payment on small businesses' unpaid invoices, reached a $1.1 billion valuation with a $100 million Series D in November 2021 — and then spent the following years rebuilding itself into something different: embedded capital infrastructure, credit delivered inside the software platforms small businesses already use ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/30/fundbox-raises-100m/150); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/b2b-payments/2025/vaya-co-founders-join-fundbox-to-help-expand-embedded-lending-offerings/)).

## Invoice financing, Israeli-founded

Shinar had been a partner at the venture firm Battery Ventures before founding Fundbox ([Haaretz](https://canary.haaretz.com/israel-news/business/2015-09-11/ty-article/.premium/kutcher-bezos-firm-invest-in-fundbox/0000017f-da75-d42c-afff-dff7322e0000)). The original product was invoice financing with an algorithmic twist: beginning in August 2013, Fundbox offered credit lines to small businesses based on their receivables, charging a computer-generated commission calibrated to the risk of each cash advance ([Haaretz](https://canary.haaretz.com/israel-news/business/2015-09-11/ty-article/.premium/kutcher-bezos-firm-invest-in-fundbox/0000017f-da75-d42c-afff-dff7322e0000)). The model attracted notable early backers: a $50 million round in September 2015 led by Spark Growth Capital brought in Ashton Kutcher, Jeff Bezos' Bezos Expeditions and talent manager Guy Oseary, following a $40 million round that March and taking total investment to $107.5 million ([Haaretz](https://canary.haaretz.com/israel-news/business/2015-09-11/ty-article/.premium/kutcher-bezos-firm-invest-in-fundbox/0000017f-da75-d42c-afff-dff7322e0000)).

## The unicorn round and the AI underwriting story

The November 2021 Series D — $100 million led by the Healthcare of Ontario Pension Plan — valued Fundbox at $1.1 billion and brought total equity raised to $410 million since 2013 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/30/fundbox-raises-100m/150)). CEO Prashant Fuloria told TechCrunch the company, boosted by pandemic-era demand, had crossed a $100 million annual revenue run rate — "well and truly beyond" that, though not yet profitable — had tripled its new-customer acquisition rate in 2021, and had connected with over 325,000 small businesses while transacting more than $2.5 billion in working capital ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/30/fundbox-raises-100m/150)).

The company's differentiator, Fuloria said, was its focus on business-to-business SMBs — suppliers invoicing clients like restaurants and waiting to be paid — combined with an AI-driven underwriting model that mapped what he called a "business graph": businesses as nodes, their transactions as connections, generating risk signals for each B2B transaction ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/30/fundbox-raises-100m/150)). More than 99 percent of credit decisions were fully automated, he said, and the company offered lines of credit up to $150,000 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/30/fundbox-raises-100m/150)). Around the same time Fundbox expanded beyond lending into payments and membership offerings, launching Flex Pay to give business owners more ways to fund expenses ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/30/fundbox-raises-100m/150)).

## The embedded-capital pivot

The pivot's clearest public marker came in May 2022, when [Visa](/organizations/visa/) announced a partnership with Fundbox to strengthen what it already called an "embedded working capital platform" ([IBS Intelligence](https://ibsintelligence.com/ibsi-news/visa-partners-with-fundbox-to-enable-new-payment-capabilities-for-small-businesses/)). The first product was the Fundbox Flex Visa Debit Card, issued by Pathward, N.A., bringing Flex Pay's spend-management to card-accepting merchants; the companies also planned small-business buy-now-pay-later products and instant fund disbursement via Visa Direct ([IBS Intelligence](https://ibsintelligence.com/ibsi-news/visa-partners-with-fundbox-to-enable-new-payment-capabilities-for-small-businesses/)). Fundbox reported Flex Pay payment volume growing more than 80 percent quarter-over-quarter and said its annual revenue run-rate had passed $160 million ([IBS Intelligence](https://ibsintelligence.com/ibsi-news/visa-partners-with-fundbox-to-enable-new-payment-capabilities-for-small-businesses/)).

The embedded strategy deepened in the following years. By mid-2025 Fundbox's credit was being embedded through partners including Autobooks (Autobooks Capital), Cantaloupe (Cantaloupe Capital) and Nav, which presented Fundbox's pre-approved offers to its own SMB users ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/b2b-payments/2025/vaya-co-founders-join-fundbox-to-help-expand-embedded-lending-offerings/)). On June 23, 2025, the founding team of embedded-lending platform Vaya Technologies — co-founders Ankit Singh and Soham Sen — joined Fundbox in product and engineering leadership roles to accelerate the development of new embedded credit products, a move the company framed as strengthening its embedded-fintech and vertical-SaaS expertise ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/b2b-payments/2025/vaya-co-founders-join-fundbox-to-help-expand-embedded-lending-offerings/)).
