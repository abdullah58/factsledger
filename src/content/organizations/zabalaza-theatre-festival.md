## Overview

The Zabalaza Theatre Festival is the Baxter Theatre Centre's theatre-development
programme in Cape Town, launched in 2011 to develop theatre practitioners from in
and around the Western Cape in partnership with the provincial Department of
Cultural Affairs and Sports
([Baxter Theatre Centre](https://baxter.uct.ac.za/zabalaza-theatre-festival);
[YOMZANSI](https://www.yomzansi.com/2012/10/08/applications-now-open-for-2013-zabalaza-theatre-festival/)).
The full name, *Zabalaza Intsika eBaxter Theatre*, is isiXhosa — "zabalaza" meaning
to strive — loosely translated as "striving towards being a pillar of the Baxter
Theatre Centre and striving towards success"
([YOMZANSI](https://www.yomzansi.com/2012/10/08/applications-now-open-for-2013-zabalaza-theatre-festival/)).
It is a different entity from Zabalaza Books, the publishing project whose
materials are hosted on the website of the Zabalaza Anarchist Communist Front
(ZACF) — "zabalaza" being an indigenous word for "struggle" in Zulu and Xhosa
([ZACF constitution](https://Theanarchistlibrary.org/library/zabalaza-constitution-of-the-zabalaza-anarchist-communist-front-zacf.pdf)).

## Founding and name

Contemporary 2012 reporting describes the festival as launched "with a new team,
name, vision and integrated approach" since its inception in 2011, quickly
establishing itself as "one of the premier and vital development theatre platforms
in the country"
([YOMZANSI](https://www.yomzansi.com/2012/10/08/applications-now-open-for-2013-zabalaza-theatre-festival/)).
The Baxter's own later account says the Centre launched the festival in 2011
"with a new name, managing team, vision, format and programme designed to
showcase new and upcoming talent that may otherwise not be seen on professional
stages" ([BroadwayWorld South Africa](http://www.broadwayworld.com/south-africa/article/Applications-Now-Open-for-Baxters-2015-Zabalaza-Theatre-Festival-20140902)).
Theatre writer Robyn Sassen, in her tribute to Zoleka Helesi, described Zabalaza
as an iteration of the earlier Ikhwezi Theatre Festival — an account not
independently corroborated by a second source
([Robyn Sassen](http://robynsassenmyview.com/2020/12/17/cape-theatres-gentle-lion-rip-mazo/)).

At the 2025 15th-anniversary edition, the festival formally honoured its founder
members — the late Zoleka Helesi, Thami Mbongo and Bongile Mantsai
([BroadwayWorld South Africa](https://www.Broadwayworld.com/south-africa/article/Baxter-Zabalaza-Theatre-Festival-Celebrates-15-Years-This-Month-20250304)).
Helesi, fondly known as MaZo, had joined the Baxter in 2007, served as the
festival's co-founder and coordinator from its inception, and died of cancer on
11 December 2020, aged 48; she was also a performer, remembered for roles
including Christine in Yael Farber's *Mies Julie*
([Robyn Sassen](http://robynsassenmyview.com/2020/12/17/cape-theatres-gentle-lion-rip-mazo/)).
Mbongo was artistic director in the festival's early years
([YOMZANSI](https://www.yomzansi.com/2012/10/08/applications-now-open-for-2013-zabalaza-theatre-festival/)).
Since Mbongo's era, the festival has been led by curator and artistic director
Mdu Kweyama, working with coordinator Bulelani Mabutyana, administrator Zanele
Tlali and audience-development lead Nontsikelelo Maboza
([Weekend Argus](https://weekendargus.co.za/news/2026-03-24-16th-baxter-zabalaza-theatre-festival-who-took-home-the-awards/);
[Baxter Theatre Centre](https://baxter.uct.ac.za/zabalaza-theatre-festival)).

## Format and development mission

The Baxter describes Zabalaza as a development programme that gives community
practitioners performance platforms "so that they can realise their creative
concepts," through community mini-festivals, script readings, showcases and the
annual March festival
([Baxter Theatre Centre](https://baxter.uct.ac.za/zabalaza-theatre-festival)).
Its stated aims include developing new South African work, running an ongoing
skills programme (stage management, script-writing and devising, directing,
acting, arts administration, theatre design, marketing), identifying raw talent
in community groups, building new theatre audiences, and providing
mentorship and internships to writers, directors, stage managers and actors
([Baxter Theatre Centre](https://baxter.uct.ac.za/zabalaza-theatre-festival)).
Applicants audition at their own rehearsal spaces, receive detailed feedback,
and are revisited to see whether they have worked on the notes; selected
productions then play mini-festivals in communities including Robertson,
Kraaifontein, Stellenbosch, Khayelitsha, Langa, Delft, Nyanga East and Athlone
— "we strongly believe in taking the theatre to the people"
([Baxter Theatre Centre](https://baxter.uct.ac.za/zabalaza-theatre-festival)).

The main festival takes over the Baxter every March, with community groups
presenting on a professional platform — around 50 new works annually, many in
indigenous languages. All door takings go straight to the participants, and
Golden Arrow Bus Services provides audience transport at R10 a trip
([Baxter Theatre Centre](https://baxter.uct.ac.za/zabalaza-theatre-festival)).
Baxter CEO Lara Foot introduced the Zabalaza Challenge in 2018, inviting the
public to buy bulk tickets (R30 each, later R40) for those who cannot afford
them, with the proceeds likewise returning to the artists
([UCT Humanities](https://humanities.uct.ac.za/articles/2021-03-16-book-your-zabalaza-tickets-now);
[BroadwayWorld South Africa](https://www.Broadwayworld.com/south-africa/article/Baxter-Zabalaza-Theatre-Festival-Celebrates-15-Years-This-Month-20250304)).
The festival also runs a bursary programme with City Varsity and ACT Cape
Town, and its "Finest of the Fest" award sends winning productions through a
mentorship process before a two-week professional season at the Baxter
([Baxter Theatre Centre](https://baxter.uct.ac.za/zabalaza-theatre-festival)).

## Editions and winners

The second festival, in 2012, staged 19 productions across main and fringe
programmes over two weeks in March
([YOMZANSI](https://www.yomzansi.com/2012/10/08/applications-now-open-for-2013-zabalaza-theatre-festival/)).
The Baxter's own winners roll records Best of Zabalaza productions from
*Uhlazo* by Loyiso Damoyi (2011) through *Worst of Both Worlds* (2012),
*Skierlik* by Phillip Dikotla (2013, later winner of the Olive Schreiner
Prize), *The Champion* by Khayalethu Anthony (2014, later winner of the Fresh
Award for Writing at the 2017 Cape Town Fringe), *Fruit* (2015), *TIP-ex*
(2016), *Boy Ntulikazi* (2017) and *Onweer* (2018)
([Baxter Theatre Centre](https://baxter.uct.ac.za/zabalaza-theatre-festival)).

The 11th annual festival ran 19-27 March 2021 under COVID Level 1 restrictions,
with 21 productions and 200 artists, and was dedicated to Helesi's memory; the
2020 edition had "just managed to finish" before the hard lockdown
([UCT Humanities](https://humanities.uct.ac.za/articles/2021-03-16-book-your-zabalaza-tickets-now)).
The 13th annual festival ran 24-31 March 2023 with 37 productions and 189
participants; its awards, held in the Baxter's Golden Arrow Theatre the day
after the festival closed, named *Oorwinnings Reis*, *Tiro's Toughloop
Testimony* and *Lamentations* as Finest of the Festival, with *Tiro's
Toughloop Testimony* also taking Best Script
([BroadwayWorld South Africa](https://www.broadwayworld.com/south-africa/article/Feature-THE-2023-ZABALAZA-THEATRE-AWARDS-at-the-Baxter-Theatre-Centre-Reflect-Passion-Comradery-and-Talent-20230404)).
The script was subsequently published in paperback by Junkets Publisher in
2024 ([Clarke's Bookshop](https://clarkesbooks.co.za/products/tiros-toughloop-testimony-winner-of-the-best-script-baxter-zabalaza-theatre-festival-2023?view=quick)).
Independent arts press notes the festival's wider industry impact: audience
attendance grew from 5,300 in 2022 to 6,800 in 2023 with revenue up R5,000
returned directly to artists, the four 2021 Best of Zabalaza winners were all
nominated for the Fleur du Cap best-new-director award in 2022 (Siphenathi
Siqwayi winning for *Ganga Nyoko! Inzima Nyoko!*), and 2022 winner *Hostel
Lights* by Sohail Booise was nominated for best direction at the 2023 Fleur du
Cap awards ([Theatre Scene Cape Town](https://www.theatrescenecpt.co.za/post/press-applications-now-open-for-2024-baxter-zabalaza-theatre-festival)).

The 2024 Best Production winner was *Invisible Scars*, written and directed by
Themba Baleni — a two-hander about gender-based violence and mental health
that played its Finest of the Fest season at the Baxter Studio from 9 to 19
October 2024 ([WeekendSpecial](https://weekendspecial.co.za/limited-season-of-zabalaza-winner-invisible-scars/)).
For its 15th anniversary in 2025, the festival shifted format: for the first
time the Baxter itself curated the edition, commissioning 15 productions under
the theme "Make Your Mark" (14-22 March) and pairing the makers with mentors
including Yael Farber, Lara Foot, Adrian Kohler, Mandla Mbothwe and Nico
Scheepers ([BroadwayWorld South Africa](https://www.Broadwayworld.com/south-africa/article/Baxter-Zabalaza-Theatre-Festival-Celebrates-15-Years-This-Month-20250304)).

The 16th annual awards ceremony took place on 21 March 2026. *Kinnes van
Somers* (directed by Tamsyn Alexander and Hanro Cloete) and *Until I Arrive*
(by Lindokuhle Melaphi) were joint recipients of the Best Production/Finest of
the Fest award; Mnqobi Molefe (*When Black Men Disappear*) and Yolanda Ntanyana
(*Bring Light*) won Best Actor and Best Actress; and Andiswa Anthony received
the Zoleka Helesi Award for a female artist of outstanding talent
([Weekend Argus](https://weekendargus.co.za/news/2026-03-24-16th-baxter-zabalaza-theatre-festival-who-took-home-the-awards/)).
The two winning productions then received their Finest of the Fest professional
seasons at the Baxter Studio — *Until I Arrive* from 29 September to 3 October
2026 and *Kinnes van Somers* from 6 to 10 October 2026 — under the creative
supervision of director Sylvaine Strike
([Cape Town This Month](https://capetownthismonth.co.za/two-zabalaza-finest-of-the-fest-winners-head-to-the-baxter/)).

## Current status

The festival is active and preparing its next edition: the Baxter's official
festival page lists applications for the 17th Zabalaza Theatre Festival as
open, closing 26 June 2026, with a programme for 2027 in preparation
([Baxter Theatre Centre](https://baxter.uct.ac.za/zabalaza-theatre-festival)).
Its funders are the Western Cape Department of Cultural Affairs and Sports,
the Oppenheimer Memorial Trust Fund, the Makwande Empowerment Trust, the City
of Cape Town and the HCI Foundation
([Weekend Argus](https://weekendargus.co.za/news/2026-03-24-16th-baxter-zabalaza-theatre-festival-who-took-home-the-awards/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not state the winners of the 2025 commissioned edition's
Best of the Fest awards, since they were not identified in this research. It
does not present the Ikhwezi Theatre Festival predecessor claim as settled
fact — it rests on Robyn Sassen's single account and is attributed as such. It
does not assign precise founding titles to Thami Mbongo and Bongile Mantsai
beyond what the sources document, and it makes no claim about the festival's
current staffing, budget or programme beyond what the cited sources state.
