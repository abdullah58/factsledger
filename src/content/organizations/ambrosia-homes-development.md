## Overview

Ambrosia Homes Development (Ambrosia Homes, Inc.) is a small [Chicago](/places/chicago/) developer led by president Tim Pomaville that specialises in reviving historic buildings on the city's Northwest Side. It is best known for a nine-year effort to turn the 1906 [Jefferson Park](/places/jefferson-park/) firehouse into a brewery and apartments — a project that survived a $300,000 rival bid, a lawsuit, and the brewery's exit — and for proposing Chicago's first Metra-oriented condo development in Edgebrook ([Streetsblog Chicago](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2016/04/20/chicagos-first-metra-oriented-development-proposed-in-edgebrook); [Preservation Chicago](https://www.preservationchicago.org/potential-win-jefferson-park-firehouse-seeks-commercial-tenant-after-renovation-2/)).

The company works slowly and locally: it spent years in the city's Negotiated Sales Process before buying the firehouse, reshaped its plans after neighbourhood and preservation groups objected, and still owns the building rather than flipping it. Its portfolio, as the public record shows it, is small — the Jefferson Park firehouse, a second Jefferson Park rehab called the Lero building, and one earlier transit-oriented condo proposal in Edgebrook whose outcome is unverified ([Preservation Chicago](https://www.preservationchicago.org/sign-the-petition-to-support-the-adaptive-reuse-the-jefferson-park-firehouse/); [Preservation Chicago](https://www.preservationchicago.org/potential-win-jefferson-park-firehouse-seeks-commercial-tenant-after-renovation-2/)).

## Bicycle Flats of Edgebrook

Ambrosia's first appearance in the Chicago press came in April 2016, when it proposed "Bicycle Flats of Edgebrook", a four-storey condominium building at 5306 West Devon Avenue, about a quarter-mile east of the Edgebrook station on Metra's Union Pacific-North Line and a short ride from the North Branch Trail. It was pitched as the city's first Metra-oriented development, more than 11 miles from Daley Plaza, where almost all transit-oriented building had clustered near CTA stations ([Streetsblog Chicago](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2016/04/20/chicagos-first-metra-oriented-development-proposed-in-edgebrook)).

The building would have held seven two-bedroom condos and one ground-floor live/work space, with only three car-parking spots against 16 indoor bike spaces, and Ambrosia looked into paying the city to install a Divvy bike-share station outside. It needed 41st Ward Alderman Anthony Napolitano to approve a zoning change from B3-1 to B2-3, and Pomaville argued it would appeal to buyers who walk or cycle to work and bring foot traffic to Devon's vacant storefronts ([Streetsblog Chicago](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2016/04/20/chicagos-first-metra-oriented-development-proposed-in-edgebrook)). No later source found confirms whether Bicycle Flats was built.

## The $10 sale and the lawsuit

The firehouse is Ambrosia's defining project. The 1906 two-storey brick building at 4841 N. Lipps Avenue had sat vacant for years after serving as the 45th Ward sanitation office, and Ambrosia began working on its reuse with Lake Effect Brewing in 2016, unveiling a $2.4 million plan at a packed 2018 meeting at the [Copernicus Center](/organizations/copernicus-center/) and cutting back a floor in 2019 after preservation groups objected ([Preservation Chicago](https://www.preservationchicago.org/sign-the-petition-to-support-the-adaptive-reuse-the-jefferson-park-firehouse/)).

In July 2020 the city's Community Development Commission agreed to sell the firehouse to an affiliate of Ambrosia Homes Development, and the sale then became a public fight. City officials defended selling it effectively for $10 and turning down a $300,000 offer from the nearby [Copernicus Foundation](/organizations/copernicus-foundation/), saying the bid showed no evidence of financing, plan or timetable; Ambrosia, led by Pomaville, agreed to pay remediation costs estimated at almost the building's $208,000 appraised value — which the city said explained the $10 figure ([Chicago Sun-Times](https://myemail.constantcontact.com/September-2020-Month-in-Review-Newsletter-Preservation-Chicago--Love-Your-City-Fiercely--Pullman-Renovation--Emmett-Till-Landmar.html?soid=1101248905774&aid=-JD8EzBFINc)). In November 2020 the Foundation sued the [City of Chicago](/organizations/city-of-chicago/) over the sale, with its attorney [Robert Fioretti](/people/robert-fioretti/) saying: "Sometimes, things go on behind the scenes. We are advocating for an open, transparent and fair process" ([Chicago Sun-Times](https://chicago.suntimes.com/2020/11/11/21561394/chicago-firehouse-sale-copernicus-foundation-lawsuit-jefferson-park)).

## After the brewery walked away

The sale went ahead anyway: in January 2021 the City Council agreed to sell the firehouse to Ambrosia for $1, with nine loft apartments planned above and a $200,000 lead-paint cleanup estimate from Pomaville ([Preservation Chicago](https://www.preservationchicago.org/newsletters/2021-01/)). But the brewery did not wait. After six years of planning, Lake Effect Brewing pulled out in 2022 — its Montrose Avenue lease was ending and the property had needed a 2020 zoning change and a liquor-ban lift in 2021 — and opened instead in Avondale ([Preservation Chicago](https://www.preservationchicago.org/potential-win-jefferson-park-firehouse-seeks-commercial-tenant-after-renovation-2/)).

By February 2024 Pomaville was showing the firehouse and the Lero building at 4762 N. Milwaukee Avenue to prospective tenants, saying the firehouse would never be sold. In August 2025 construction finally began on converting the second floor into four apartments of about 900 square feet each, after structural issues ruled out the extra floors; the 4,000-square-foot ground floor was offered for lease ([Preservation Chicago](https://www.preservationchicago.org/win-after-long-delay-construction-begins-on-jefferson-park-firehouse-second-floor-residential-adaptive-reuse/)).

## FAQ

### What is Ambrosia Homes Development?

Ambrosia Homes Development, also called Ambrosia Homes, Inc., is a small Chicago developer led by president Tim Pomaville and based on the city's Northwest Side. It specialises in rehabilitating historic buildings, most notably the 1906 Jefferson Park firehouse, and it proposed the Bicycle Flats condo project in Edgebrook in 2016.

### Why did the Jefferson Park firehouse sale cause a dispute?

The city agreed to sell the vacant 1906 firehouse to an Ambrosia affiliate effectively for $10, while rejecting a $300,000 offer from the nearby Copernicus Foundation. The city said the Foundation's bid lacked financing evidence, a clear plan and a timetable, and noted Ambrosia would pay about $208,000 in remediation costs.

### Did the Copernicus Foundation sue over the firehouse?

Yes. In November 2020 the Foundation sued the City of Chicago after the city agreed to sell the firehouse to Ambrosia Homes Development for $10, rejecting its $300,000 offer. The sale went ahead anyway, with the City Council approving it in January 2021, but no court record of the lawsuit's outcome was verified.

### What happened to the brewery planned for the firehouse?

Lake Effect Brewing Company planned a ground-floor taproom and kitchen there from 2016, but pulled out in 2022 after six years of delays in buying and rezoning the property. It opened instead in Avondale, and Ambrosia began seeking another commercial tenant for the ground floor.

### What is happening at the firehouse now?

Construction began in August 2025 on converting the second floor into four apartments of about 900 square feet each, after structural issues ruled out adding a floor. The 4,000-square-foot ground floor is for lease to one or two commercial tenants, and Ambrosia says it will not sell the building.
