## Overview

SVB Capital was the venture-capital investment platform of SVB Financial Group, founded in 1999 as a division of the group and built into a multi-strategy investor in the technology and life-sciences "innovation economy," with about $10 billion of assets under management across venture capital fund of funds, direct funds, and private credit funds ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/719739/000119312524133296/d802412dex991.htm)). It was not a bank and never took deposits, which is why it survived the March 2023 failure of Silicon Valley Bank intact — and it was sold to affiliates of Pinegrove Capital Partners on September 26, 2024, after which its strategies were absorbed into Pinegrove under the Pinegrove name ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/719739/000119312524230139/d883468dex991.htm)).

## A fund of funds built inside SVB

SVB Capital's model was to invest in other people's funds as well as directly in companies. Its own description, repeated across its releases, calls it a multi-strategy platform investing in fund managers and private technology and life science companies around the world, positioned to reach sought-after opportunities in start-ups and venture funds ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/719739/000119312524133296/d802412dex991.htm)). Co-founder Aaron Gershenberg, who joined SVB in 1999, built the platform's fund products for institutional investors seeking access to the innovation economy, spanning fund investments, direct and co-investments, credit, and secondaries ([Antler](https://www.Antler.co/blog/aaron-gershenberg-joins-antler-board)). By 2024 it managed about $10 billion for 750 limited partner investors, according to Reuters reporting at the time of the sale ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/partnerships/2024/pinegrove-capital-partners-affiliated-entity-to-buy-svb-capital/)). Antler's biography of Gershenberg puts the platform's inception-to-date track record at over 20% IRR, a figure that comes from a third-party biography rather than an audited filing ([Antler](https://www.Antler.co/blog/aaron-gershenberg-joins-antler-board)).

Its investing reached beyond venture funds into operating companies. In 2018, SVB Capital joined as a new investor in [BlueVine](/organizations/bluevine/)'s $60 million Series E, a round led by [Menlo Ventures](/organizations/menlo-ventures/) that brought the small-business lender's total financing to $578 million ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/bluevine-brings-in-60-million/)). BlueVine, founded in 2013, had made its name in invoice factoring — advancing cash against small businesses' unpaid invoices — and used the round to expand its products and research team ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/bluevine-brings-in-60-million/)).

## The bank failed; the investment platform did not

The distinction between SVB Capital and Silicon Valley Bank matters, because the two are routinely conflated. Silicon Valley Bank was a commercial bank: on March 10, 2023, it was closed by the California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation, which appointed the [FDIC](/organizations/fdic/) as receiver, after $42 billion in deposits left the bank in a single day on March 9 ([FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/index.php/news/speeches/2023/spmar2723.html)). The bank held $209 billion in assets at year-end 2022, and First-Citizens Bank & Trust Company later acquired its bridge bank ([FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/index.php/news/speeches/2023/spmar2723.html)).

SVB Capital, by contrast, was an investment manager, not a deposit-taker. When its parent, SVB Financial Group, filed a voluntary Chapter 11 petition on March 17, 2023, in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, SVB Capital's funds and general partner entities were not included in the filing: they were separate legal entities that continued operating in the ordinary course, with access to subscription credit facilities and investor commitments ([ABL Advisor](https://www.abladvisor.com/articles/36131/svb-financial-group-files-chapter-11)). The parent's roughly $3.3 billion of funded unsecured debt had no claim against SVB Capital or SVB Securities, and the filing stated that SVB Financial Group was no longer affiliated with Silicon Valley Bank, N.A. or its private banking business ([ABL Advisor](https://www.abladvisor.com/articles/36131/svb-financial-group-files-chapter-11)).

## Retain, then sell: the 2024 reversal

The bankruptcy process first pointed toward keeping SVB Capital. On January 9, 2024, SVB Financial Group and its creditors determined after a review of strategic alternatives that retaining the business would create superior value, and SVB Capital created a new operating committee of three longtime executives — co-founder Aaron Gershenberg, Sulu Mamdani, and Beau Laskey — to run it ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/svb-capital-announces-svb-financial-group-will-retain-ownership-of-the-business-and)). Gershenberg, who had served as a Senior Advisor since January 2023, returned as a Managing Partner under that plan ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/svb-capital-announces-svb-financial-group-will-retain-ownership-of-the-business-and)).

Four months later the decision was reversed. On May 2, 2024, SVB Financial Group announced a definitive agreement under which a newly created entity affiliated with Pinegrove Capital Partners, backed by permanent capital from Brookfield Asset Management and Sequoia Heritage, would acquire SVB Capital for a combination of cash and other economic consideration ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/719739/000119312524133296/d802412dex991.htm)). Pinegrove, a capital-solutions partner for the venture ecosystem focused on secondary liquidity for general and limited partners, and SVB Capital were to operate independently, each led by its existing management team ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/719739/000119312524133296/d802412dex991.htm)). The deal needed Bankruptcy Court and regulatory approval, with a buyer-protection hearing sought for May 16, 2024 and a sale-approval hearing requested for June 5, 2024 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/719739/000119312524133296/d802412dex991.htm)). The sale completed on September 26, 2024 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/719739/000119312524230139/d883468dex991.htm)).

The SVB Capital name did not survive long as a standalone brand. By January 2026, Pinegrove described its venture fund primaries, co-investment and credit strategies as "(formerly known as SVB Capital)" ([Le Lézard](https://www.lelezard.com/en/news-22088325.html)). In September 2026, Pinegrove Venture Partners closed its Strategic Investors Fund XII at $1.5 billion, significantly oversubscribed, building on what it called a 26-year track record — a span that reaches back to SVB Capital's 1999 founding — with Gershenberg now a Managing Partner of Pinegrove Venture Partners ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20260910la45101/pinegrove-closes-15-billion-oversubscribed-strategic-investors-fund-xii)). Pinegrove's platform by then managed over $15 billion across venture fund primaries and co-investments, venture debt and private credit, and venture and growth secondaries ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20260910la45101/pinegrove-closes-15-billion-oversubscribed-strategic-investors-fund-xii)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim a sale price: a $340 million figure circulates in one trade-blog report citing unnamed bankruptcy documents, while the official releases state only "cash and other economic consideration," so no price is asserted. It does not claim SVB Capital was a bank, took deposits, or failed — the March 2023 failure was Silicon Valley Bank's, and SVB Capital's funds were legally separate and excluded from the parent's Chapter 11 filing. The over-20% inception-to-date IRR figure is from a third-party biography of Aaron Gershenberg, not an audited filing. No post-sale assets-under-management figure for SVB Capital as a standalone business was verified; the ~$10 billion figure is as stated in 2024. Whether HRTG Partners, named as a Pinegrove backer in 2026 materials, is the same entity as Sequoia Heritage, named in 2024, was not verified and is not asserted.
