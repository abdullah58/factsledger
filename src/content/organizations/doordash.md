## Overview

DoorDash is the largest food-delivery platform in the [United States](/places/united-states/), a [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/)-based local-commerce company founded in 2013 by four [Stanford University](/universities/stanford-university/) students. It connects consumers, restaurants and other merchants, and the independent drivers it calls Dashers, and in 2024 it handled 2.58 billion orders worth $80.2 billion in Marketplace gross order value ([DoorDash](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/doordash-releases-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024-financial-results-2025-02-11)). It began as a website delivering food from a couple dozen Palo Alto restaurants, and it has since grown, by acquisition and by moving beyond restaurants, into a company operating in more than 40 countries with brands including DoorDash, Deliveroo, SevenRooms and Wolt ([DoorDash](https://ir.doordash.com/news/news-details/2026/DoorDash-to-Acquire-Instacart-to-Help-Local-Stores-Win/default.aspx)).

The scale is what sets DoorDash apart from the rivals it started beside. Bloomberg Second Measure put DoorDash at 67% of US meal-delivery sales in March 2024, against 23% for Uber Eats, with Grubhub far behind ([Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/technology/which-restaurant-delivery-app-best-consumers-weigh)). The Associated Press calls DoorDash the largest US food delivery platform ([Associated Press](https://techxplore.com/news/2025-10-doordash-billion-acquisition-uk-deliveroo.html)). By October 2026 the company said it had facilitated more than 10 billion consumer orders since its founding, driven more than $230 billion in sales for merchants, and helped Dashers earn more than $80 billion through the platform ([DoorDash](https://ir.doordash.com/news/news-details/2026/DoorDash-to-Acquire-Instacart-to-Help-Local-Stores-Win/default.aspx)). It is also one of the public companies named as a customer of [Brex](/organizations/brex/).

## Founding: a Stanford class project that became a logistics company

Tony Xu, Andy Fang, Stanley Tang and Evan Moore met at Stanford, where Xu and Moore were business school students and Fang and Tang were computer science undergraduates, and the idea grew out of a class project in which they interviewed small Bay Area businesses about their problems ([The Courier](https://courier.fyi/case-studies/the-doordash-success-story-four-stanford-students-a-palo-alto-macaroon-shop-and-the-suburban-bet-nobody-thought-would-work/)). A Palo Alto bakery owner showed them a binder of delivery orders she could never fulfil, because she had no drivers, and that gap became the business ([Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/hospitality/how-ceo-tony-xu-led-doordash-to-delivery-dominance-f36b5e7c)). They built PaloAltoDelivery.com in January 2013, a bare website with PDF menus for eight local restaurants, and drove the first deliveries themselves ([The Courier](https://courier.fyi/case-studies/the-doordash-success-story-four-stanford-students-a-palo-alto-macaroon-shop-and-the-suburban-bet-nobody-thought-would-work/)). The Journal dates the PaloAltoDelivery.com launch to 2012, while the company's own account says it started in 2013; this profile follows 2013.

The team joined [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/)'s Summer 2013 batch and in September 2013 raised a $2.4 million seed round led by Khosla Ventures, then delivering from 70 restaurants in Palo Alto and Mountain View for $6 a delivery ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/09/30/door-dash-raises-2-4m/)). Co-founder Andy Fang told TechCrunch at the time, "We view ourselves as a logistics company," a framing that explains everything DoorDash did next ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/09/30/door-dash-raises-2-4m/)). The early test came three months after launch, when a Stanford home football game flooded the system with orders and every delivery ran late; Xu says the founders refunded every order at a cost of about 40% of their remaining runway, then stayed up all night baking cookies and delivering apology notes by 5AM ([DoorDash](https://about.doordash.com/en-au/news/doordash-celebrates-its-10th-dashiversary)). Between March 2015 and March 2018 the company spent about a thousand days struggling to raise money while peers raised 50 to 100 times more capital, and Xu describes it as fourth place in its category and last in capital ([DoorDash](https://about.doordash.com/en-au/news/doordash-celebrates-its-10th-dashiversary)). Evan Moore, the fourth co-founder, left after about 17 months ([The Courier](https://courier.fyi/case-studies/the-doordash-success-story-four-stanford-students-a-palo-alto-macaroon-shop-and-the-suburban-bet-nobody-thought-would-work/)).

## Beyond restaurants: DashMart, grocery and the suburban bet

DoorDash's defining strategic choice was to chase the suburbs while Grubhub and Uber Eats fought over dense cities, on the reasoning that suburban restaurants had almost no delivery infrastructure and suburban customers had few alternatives ([Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/hospitality/how-ceo-tony-xu-led-doordash-to-delivery-dominance-f36b5e7c)). It wooed restaurants with investments from Sequoia Capital and other venture firms and with exclusive deals that put their menus only on DoorDash, a tactic that became central as it moved from independent restaurants into chains such as Cheesecake Factory ([Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/hospitality/how-ceo-tony-xu-led-doordash-to-delivery-dominance-f36b5e7c)). The bet paid off in the pandemic: when COVID-19 arrived in March 2020, order volume doubled almost overnight, and DoorDash cut its commissions by 50% while onboarding tens of thousands of merchants whose dining rooms had shut ([DoorDash](https://about.doordash.com/en-au/news/doordash-celebrates-its-10th-dashiversary)).

The company used the same logistics network to move beyond meals. In August 2020 it launched DashMart, a virtual convenience store carrying about 2,000 grocery, household and restaurant items from dark warehouses, with delivery in as little as 30 minutes, starting in eight cities including Chicago, Dallas and Phoenix ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/doordash-launches-new-online-convenience-store-dashmart/583047/?fbclid=IwAR2roVQI12mSDqVS2FWraVuriaJThEKsNSVV23rF0HXMkw6GgnxmsUhtDjk)). DashMart mattered because DoorDash owned and curated the inventory itself, becoming a retailer rather than only a fulfilment service for other stores ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/doordash-launches-new-online-convenience-store-dashmart/583047/?fbclid=IwAR2roVQI12mSDqVS2FWraVuriaJThEKsNSVV23rF0HXMkw6GgnxmsUhtDjk)). Grocery followed: the company struck a deal with Kroger in 2022, and by December 2024 more than 25% of its monthly active users ordered from at least one of its new verticals categories, up from more than 20% a year earlier ([Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/hospitality/how-ceo-tony-xu-led-doordash-to-delivery-dominance-f36b5e7c); [DoorDash](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/doordash-releases-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024-financial-results-2025-02-11)). A DashPass subscription had grown to more than 22 million DashPass and Wolt+ members by the end of 2024 ([DoorDash](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/doordash-releases-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024-financial-results-2025-02-11)).

## Going public and going global

DoorDash filed to go public in February 2020, just before the pandemic surge, and priced its IPO at $102.00 per share on December 8, 2020, offering 33 million Class A shares and raising about $3.4 billion ([Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/hospitality/how-ceo-tony-xu-led-doordash-to-delivery-dominance-f36b5e7c); [Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/print/v2/content/184997/Wilson-Sonsini-Advises-DoorDash-on-its-IPO.pdf)). The shares began trading on the [New York Stock Exchange](/organizations/new-york-stock-exchange/) on December 9, 2020 under the symbol DASH, and shot up to $182 on the first day ([Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/hospitality/how-ceo-tony-xu-led-doordash-to-delivery-dominance-f36b5e7c); [Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/print/v2/content/184997/Wilson-Sonsini-Advises-DoorDash-on-its-IPO.pdf)). The 2024 results then marked a turn from growth at a loss to profit: revenue grew 24% to $10.72 billion, and the company generated its first full year of positive GAAP net income, $123 million, with $1.9 billion of Adjusted EBITDA ([DoorDash](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/doordash-releases-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024-financial-results-2025-02-11)). That profit, on $80.2 billion of Marketplace gross order value, is the stat that matters most for the business model: the take rate implied by $10.72 billion of revenue on $80.2 billion of orders is about 13%, and the company kept growing while turning that thin slice into a first profitable year.

Growth after the IPO came largely by acquisition, and the sequence shows a company buying its way into every market it had not built itself:

The Wolt deal, agreed in November 2021 and closed on May 31, 2022 in an all-stock transaction, gave DoorDash its international platform ([SEC](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001792789/85d02598-94b6-48ea-a3c7-165d58e1cefd.pdf)). The Deliveroo deal, agreed in May 2025 for $3.9 billion in cash at 180 pence per share and approved by a British court, closed on October 2, 2025 and took DoorDash to 45 markets worldwide, 30 of them in Europe ([Associated Press](https://techxplore.com/news/2025-10-doordash-billion-acquisition-uk-deliveroo.html)).

## The Instacart deal, six years in the making

The freshest chapter in the DoorDash story is the one most reference pages do not yet cover. On October 29, 2026 DoorDash announced a definitive agreement to acquire Instacart (Maplebear Inc.) for $65.00 per share, an equity value of about $17 billion, at least 75% in cash, a 26% premium to Instacart's October 22 closing price ([DoorDash](https://ir.doordash.com/news/news-details/2026/DoorDash-to-Acquire-Instacart-to-Help-Local-Stores-Win/default.aspx)). The deal is expected to close in late 2027 or the first half of 2028, subject to Instacart shareholder approval and antitrust clearance in the United States, Canada and Israel, and the two companies will operate separately until then ([DoorDash](https://ir.doordash.com/news/news-details/2026/DoorDash-to-Acquire-Instacart-to-Help-Local-Stores-Win/default.aspx)). Xu called it the company's biggest deal in its 13-year history ([Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/hospitality/how-ceo-tony-xu-led-doordash-to-delivery-dominance-f36b5e7c)).

What makes the deal more than a headline is the history behind it. DoorDash looked at buying Instacart in 2021, when Instacart was the dominant third-party grocery-delivery service, and did not pursue it, striking a grocery deal with Kroger instead in 2022 ([Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/hospitality/how-ceo-tony-xu-led-doordash-to-delivery-dominance-f36b5e7c)). Five years later it is buying the grocery capability outright: Instacart brings more than 2,200 retail banners representing nearly 100,000 stores, plus the enterprise technology grocers use to run their own e-commerce, while DoorDash brings the delivery network ([DoorDash](https://ir.doordash.com/news/news-details/2026/DoorDash-to-Acquire-Instacart-to-Help-Local-Stores-Win/default.aspx)). The companies say DashPass and Instacart+ members would eventually pay one membership fee instead of two, saving an estimated $100 million a year, and that Instacart's brand, app and enterprise products would be kept ([DoorDash](https://ir.doordash.com/news/news-details/2026/DoorDash-to-Acquire-Instacart-to-Help-Local-Stores-Win/default.aspx)). The risk is regulatory: the Journal reports the deal could face scrutiny given both parties' shares of third-party grocery delivery, and more than 80% of grocery shopping is still done in stores ([Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/hospitality/how-ceo-tony-xu-led-doordash-to-delivery-dominance-f36b5e7c)). The deal also lands in a soft year for the stock, down 20% in 2026 and on pace for its worst year since 2022, with a market value of about $78 billion ([Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/hospitality/how-ceo-tony-xu-led-doordash-to-delivery-dominance-f36b5e7c)).

## The Dasher question

DoorDash's model rests on about nine million Dashers, independent drivers who choose when to work and which orders to take ([Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/hospitality/how-ceo-tony-xu-led-doordash-to-delivery-dominance-f36b5e7c)). Whether those drivers are employees or independent contractors has been the company's longest-running legal fight, and California has been the centre of it. California voters passed Proposition 22 in November 2020, a ballot measure that classifies app-based drivers who meet its conditions as independent contractors rather than employees, which means they are not covered by California's workers' compensation laws ([Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/print/pdf/node/6660)). Drivers and unions challenged the measure as unconstitutional, and on July 25, 2024 the California Supreme Court unanimously upheld it, holding that the initiative did not conflict with the state constitution's grant of workers' compensation power to the legislature ([Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/print/pdf/node/6660)). The ruling lets DoorDash and its peers keep classifying California drivers as contractors, while leaving open whether the legislature can extend workers' compensation to app-based drivers in future.

Pay practices have drawn separate scrutiny. States and cities went after the company's pay practices, with DoorDash settling multimillion-dollar lawsuits, and Chicago accused the company of posting restaurant menus on DoorDash without the proprietors' permission and luring in customers with deceptively low fees that later grew ([Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/hospitality/how-ceo-tony-xu-led-doordash-to-delivery-dominance-f36b5e7c)). DoorDash settled the Chicago lawsuit in 2025 and said it no longer participated in the business practices at issue ([Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/hospitality/how-ceo-tony-xu-led-doordash-to-delivery-dominance-f36b5e7c)). The classification question is not settled everywhere: the Supreme Court decision answered only the California constitutional challenge, and the Instacart announcement itself promises Dashers and Shoppers "more ways to earn," a sign the company knows driver earnings remain the argument it has to keep winning ([DoorDash](https://ir.doordash.com/news/news-details/2026/DoorDash-to-Acquire-Instacart-to-Help-Local-Stores-Win/default.aspx)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was DoorDash founded?**
DoorDash was founded in 2013, when its four Stanford founders launched PaloAltoDelivery.com in Palo Alto and joined Y Combinator's Summer 2013 batch, renaming the company DoorDash that year ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/09/30/door-dash-raises-2-4m/); [The Courier](https://courier.fyi/case-studies/the-doordash-success-story-four-stanford-students-a-palo-alto-macaroon-shop-and-the-suburban-bet-nobody-thought-would-work/)). The Wall Street Journal dates the PaloAltoDelivery.com launch to 2012, a discrepancy this profile flags openly.

**Who founded DoorDash?**
Tony Xu, Andy Fang, Stanley Tang and Evan Moore founded DoorDash in 2013 after meeting at Stanford ([The Courier](https://courier.fyi/case-studies/the-doordash-success-story-four-stanford-students-a-palo-alto-macaroon-shop-and-the-suburban-bet-nobody-thought-would-work/)). Xu remains chief executive, Fang is a co-founder named in the earliest coverage, and Moore left the company after about 17 months.

**How big is DoorDash?**
In 2024 DoorDash handled 2.58 billion orders worth $80.2 billion, with revenue of $10.72 billion and more than 42 million monthly active users ([DoorDash](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/doordash-releases-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024-financial-results-2025-02-11)). By October 2026 it operated in more than 40 countries and counted about nine million Dashers ([DoorDash](https://ir.doordash.com/news/news-details/2026/DoorDash-to-Acquire-Instacart-to-Help-Local-Stores-Win/default.aspx)).

**Is DoorDash profitable?**
Yes, since 2024, when it recorded its first full year of positive GAAP net income at $123 million, after years of losses, with Adjusted EBITDA of $1.9 billion ([DoorDash](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/doordash-releases-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024-financial-results-2025-02-11)). The profit came on thin margins: $10.72 billion of revenue on $80.2 billion of orders.

**Will DoorDash own Instacart?**
Not yet. DoorDash announced an agreement on October 29, 2026 to acquire Instacart for about $17 billion, but the deal is expected to close only in late 2027 or the first half of 2028, subject to shareholder and regulatory approval ([DoorDash](https://ir.doordash.com/news/news-details/2026/DoorDash-to-Acquire-Instacart-to-Help-Local-Stores-Win/default.aspx)). Until then the two companies operate separately.
