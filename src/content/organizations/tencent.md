## Overview

Tencent is a Chinese multinational technology conglomerate headquartered in Shenzhen, founded in 1998 by Ma Huateng — known as "Pony" Ma — and the company behind WeChat, QQ, and some of the world's most-played video games. It grew from an instant-messaging startup into a conglomerate spanning social platforms, games, advertising, payments and cloud, with an investment portfolio reaching from Riot Games to a 5% stake in [Afterpay](/organizations/afterpay/). ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Ma-Huateng); [The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/stocks/what-does-tencent-own/))

Ma earned a computer science degree at Shenzhen University in 1993 and worked in research and development for China Motion Telecom Development Ltd. before founding Tencent in 1998 with several friends ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Ma-Huateng)). A year later the company launched the internet-based QQ service, then called OICQ, which soon became one of China's most popular instant-messaging platforms, and in June 2004 it raised nearly $200 million in a Hong Kong listing after gaining the backing of two overseas venture-capital firms ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Ma-Huateng)).

## From QQ to WeChat

Tencent launched Weixin/WeChat in 2011, and it evolved from a mobile messaging app into a super app spanning messaging, payments through Weixin Pay/WeChat Pay, and other financial services ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/stocks/what-does-tencent-own/)). Britannica put WeChat above 1 billion users as of December 2023; the Fool reports more than 1.4 billion combined monthly active users for Weixin and WeChat in 2026 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Ma-Huateng); [The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/stocks/what-does-tencent-own/)).

## Games and the investor's playbook

Tencent launched Tencent Games in 2003 ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/stocks/what-does-tencent-own/)). Its most complete conquest was Riot Games, maker of League of Legends: Tencent bought a majority stake in February 2011 and then purchased the remaining equity in 2015, having owned around 93% beforehand ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/12/17/tencent-takes-full-control-of-league-of-legends-creator-riot-games/)). Elsewhere it prefers influence to control. It made a strategic investment in Epic Games, maker of Fortnite, in 2012 and holds an estimated 40% stake; it is the largest shareholder of Singapore-based Sea Limited with less than 20% of its shares and less than 10% of its voting power, after trimming about 2.5% of its holding for around $3 billion in 2022 ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/stocks/what-does-tencent-own/)).

The same pattern runs through fintech and social media. Tencent and partners founded WeBank, China's first digital bank, in 2014, with Tencent holding a 30% stake and WeBank serving more than 440 million individual clients as of October 2026 ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/stocks/what-does-tencent-own/)). It bought a 12% stake in Snap in 2017 and is now Snap's largest shareholder at over 16%, and its $180 million 2018 investment in Brazil's Nu Holdings grew to nearly 150 million shares worth about $2 billion by mid-2025, around 4% of the company ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/stocks/what-does-tencent-own/)).

In May 2020 Tencent became a "substantial shareholder" of Afterpay with a 5% stake valued at approximately A$390 million; chief strategy officer James Mitchell said Tencent operates China's leading digital payment service, and outside China it has actively invested in pioneering fintech companies ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/fintech-investments/2020/tencent-becomes-substantial-shareholder-of-afterpay/)). It was a minority bet on a payments model, not a takeover. Tencent's own largest shareholder is South African media company Naspers, which owns roughly a quarter of Tencent through its affiliate Prosus ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/stocks/what-does-tencent-own/)).

## The 2021 regulatory reset

Tencent's scale made it a target when Beijing moved against its internet giants. In July 2021 anti-monopoly regulators fined companies in 22 cases at 500,000 yuan (about $75,000) each for acquisitions that might improperly increase market power; the violators included five companies owned by Tencent, alongside six owned by Alibaba ([Associated Press](https://techxplore.com/news/2021-07-china-fines-internet-giants-anti-monopoly.amp)). Weeks later, China banned minors from playing online games for more than three hours a week — 8 p.m. to 9 p.m. on Fridays, weekends and public holidays from September 1 — down from a 2019 rule allowing an hour and a half a day ([Associated Press](https://techxplore.com/news/2021-08-china-limits-children-hours-online.amp)). The rule hit Tencent, whose Honor of Kings game was hugely popular, and Tencent had already announced August curbs: an hour a day for minors, two hours on holidays, and no in-game purchases for children under 12 ([Associated Press](https://techxplore.com/news/2021-08-china-limits-children-hours-online.amp)). By the announcement, Tencent's market capitalisation of $573 billion was down more than $300 billion from its February peak ([Associated Press](https://techxplore.com/news/2021-08-china-limits-children-hours-online.amp)).

## Scale today: WeChat as commerce and AI infrastructure

Full-year revenue was RMB660.3 billion, up 8%: gaming RMB197.7 billion (about 30%), advertising RMB121.4 billion (up 20%), and fintech and enterprise services RMB212 billion (up 4%) ([Jiemian Global](https://en.jiemian.com/article/12498630.html)). WeChat Shop's gross merchandise value nearly doubled in 2024 and order volume surged 125% ([Jiemian Global](https://en.jiemian.com/article/12498630.html)). Tencent is also spending heavily on artificial intelligence, with capital expenditure up 221% year on year to RMB76.7 billion in 2024, largely on graphics processors, while chairman and CEO Pony Ma described a dual strategy embracing both Tencent's in-house Hunyuan model and the open-source DeepSeek model as complementary ([Jiemian Global](https://en.jiemian.com/article/12498630.html)). Its AI chatbot Yuanbao saw daily active users surge 20-fold from February to March, per the company's earnings-call account ([Jiemian Global](https://en.jiemian.com/article/12498630.html)).
