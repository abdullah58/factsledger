## Overview

LoJack is a stolen-vehicle-recovery brand founded in 1986 by William Reagan, a former Medfield, Massachusetts police commissioner, on a system he patented in 1979: a hidden radio transceiver in the vehicle that police tracking computers could locate after a theft was reported ([TWICE](https://www.twice.com/news/lojack-founder-william-reagan-78-passes-away-52236)). [CalAmp](/organizations/calamp/) acquired LoJack Corporation in 2016 for approximately $134 million, sold the US stolen-vehicle-recovery business to Spireon in 2021 while keeping LoJack International, and in 2024 the LoJack legal entity, LoJack Global LLC, filed for Chapter 11 alongside CalAmp ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/355777/000119312516447680/d112853dex991.htm); [Auto Remarketing](https://www.autoremarketing.com/bhph/spireon-acquires-calamp-stolen-vehicle-solution/); [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000095017024067683/camp-20240522.htm)).

## A radio signal police cars could hear

Reagan's idea was shaped by police work: the LoJack name was coined as the antithesis of "hijack", and the system was founded in 1986 specifically to keep officers safe during the pursuit and recovery of stolen vehicles ([TWICE](https://www.twice.com/news/lojack-founder-william-reagan-78-passes-away-52236)). A small, silent transponder was hidden in the vehicle and registered against its VIN; once a theft was reported to police, the unit was remotely activated and began transmitting on a dedicated tracking frequency set aside by the Federal Communications Commission, so that tracking computers in police cars within a few miles could home in on its direction and distance ([TWICE](https://www.twice.com/news/lojack-founder-william-reagan-78-passes-away-52236)).

The system spread through law-enforcement partnerships rather than advertising. By 2013 it operated in 28 states and the District of Columbia and in more than 30 countries across North America, South America, Europe and Africa, with more than 1,800 US law-enforcement agencies carrying LoJack tracking computers in their vehicles ([TWICE](https://www.twice.com/news/lojack-founder-william-reagan-78-passes-away-52236)). The acquisition announcement in 2016 described LoJack as a company that had "helped more than nine million people protect their vehicles in the event of theft over the past 25+ years" ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/355777/000119312516447680/d112853dex991.htm)). Reagan himself served as the company's inaugural chairman and CEO, and died on July 1, 2013, aged 78 ([TWICE](https://www.twice.com/news/lojack-founder-william-reagan-78-passes-away-52236)).

## The CalAmp years: acquisition, split, bankruptcy

On February 1, 2016, CalAmp — then listed on [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/) as CAMP — and LoJack Corporation (Nasdaq: LOJN) announced a definitive agreement under which CalAmp would acquire all outstanding LoJack shares for $6.45 each in cash, a deal valued at approximately $134 million and funded from cash on hand ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/355777/000119312516447680/d112853dex991.htm)). The combination was pitched as a connected-car leader: CalAmp expected consolidated revenue above $400 million in its fiscal 2017, with LoJack contributing about $10 million in Adjusted EBITDA, and the LoJack brand, dealer relationships and law-enforcement network folded into CalAmp's telematics portfolio ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/355777/000119312516447680/d112853dex991.htm)).

Five years later the US business changed hands again. On March 16, 2021, Spireon acquired the LoJack US Stolen Vehicle Recovery business from CalAmp; CalAmp said it would retain and expand LoJack International, which operates as a subscription-based software business, and keep ownership of the LoJack patents and trademarks ([Auto Remarketing](https://www.autoremarketing.com/bhph/spireon-acquires-calamp-stolen-vehicle-solution/)). CalAmp's chief executive said the deal followed "our previous plans to wind down the LoJack U.S. business", and Spireon said LoJack users would join its nearly 4 million active subscribers from over 20,000 customers ([Auto Remarketing](https://www.autoremarketing.com/bhph/spireon-acquires-calamp-stolen-vehicle-solution/)).

## The 2024 bankruptcy

The end of CalAmp's run as a public company pulled LoJack's legal entity with it. On May 31, 2024, CalAmp and its wholly-owned subsidiaries — CalAmp Wireless Networks, LoJack Global LLC and [Synovia Solutions](/organizations/synovia-solutions/) — signed a restructuring support agreement with [Lynrock Lake Master Fund](/organizations/lynrock-lake-master-fund/) to convert approximately $229 million of convertible senior secured notes into equity and take the company private, as disclosed in a filing with the [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](/organizations/us-securities-and-exchange-commission/) ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000095017024067683/camp-20240522.htm)). On June 3, 2024, the same four companies filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions in the [U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware](/organizations/us-bankruptcy-court-district-of-delaware/), in [Delaware](/places/delaware/), continuing to operate as debtors in possession ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000095017024067683/camp-20240522.htm)).

Today the LoJack name lives in two places: the US stolen-vehicle-recovery business now runs on Spireon's GPS-based connected-vehicle platform, while CalAmp retains LoJack International and the LoJack patents and trademarks ([Auto Remarketing](https://www.autoremarketing.com/bhph/spireon-acquires-calamp-stolen-vehicle-solution/)). The radio-based system that defined the brand for three decades has given way to cellular and GPS tracking — a transition the 2021 deal was already built around, when Spireon's pitch was GPS-based connected-vehicle technology rather than the legacy radio network ([Auto Remarketing](https://www.autoremarketing.com/bhph/spireon-acquires-calamp-stolen-vehicle-solution/)).

## What this profile does not claim

- This profile does not assert that the US LoJack radio network was shut down on a specific date in 2026: that claim circulates from a single low-tier source and could not be corroborated by any tier1 or tier2 source.
- It does not state a precise closing date or final purchase price for the 2016 acquisition beyond the announced ~$134 million, because the SEC 10-Q material carrying those details was not read in full for this profile.
- It does not give current recovery totals or subscriber counts for LoJack after 2016; no independent source was found for them.
- LoJack Global LLC is the CalAmp subsidiary that filed for Chapter 11 in 2024; it is not the same thing as the LoJack brand as operated in the [United States](/places/united-states/) today by Spireon, and this profile is about the LoJack business and brand, not any other LoJack-named entity.
