## Overview

Anchorage Digital is a San Francisco-based institutional digital-asset platform — offering custody, trading, staking, governance and financing for institutions — co-founded by CEO Nathan McCauley and President Diogo Mónica ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/investment-tracker/2021/anchorage-closes-350m-funding-deal-at-3b-valuation/)). On January 13, 2021, the U.S. Office of the Comptroller of the Currency granted Anchorage conditional approval for a national trust charter, creating what was reported as the first federally chartered digital-asset bank in U.S. history ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/there-is-now-a-federally-chartered-digital-asset-bank-in-the-u.s.-2021-01-13)). Because it holds a national *trust* charter rather than a full commercial-bank charter, assets it holds do not need to be insured by the FDIC — while the charter simultaneously put the company under direct federal supervision, a fact that would shape the next four years ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/there-is-now-a-federally-chartered-digital-asset-bank-in-the-u.s.-2021-01-13)).

## The charter and the capital

The charter made Anchorage a regulatory first, and investors followed quickly. The company — described at the time as a startup backed by [Visa](/organizations/visa/) — offered custody for institutional crypto traders alongside real-time trading and U.S.-dollar loans and lines of credit collateralized by digital assets ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/there-is-now-a-federally-chartered-digital-asset-bank-in-the-u.s.-2021-01-13)). In February 2021 it closed an $80 million Series C led by Singapore sovereign wealth fund GIC, with Andreessen Horowitz, Blockchain Capital, Lux and Indico participating ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/59543/anchorage-armed-with-banking-charter-raises-80-million-series-c?amp=1)).

That December, Anchorage closed a $350 million Series D led by KKR — the private-equity firm's first direct equity investment in a digital-asset company, made through its Next Generation Technology Growth Fund II — valuing Anchorage at over $3 billion ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/investment-tracker/2021/anchorage-closes-350m-funding-deal-at-3b-valuation/)). The round drew Goldman Sachs, Andreessen Horowitz, Blockchain Capital, GIC, Kraken, Thoma Bravo, Wellington and others, with the new capital earmarked for crypto-infrastructure products aimed at global financial firms and fintech clients ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/investment-tracker/2021/anchorage-closes-350m-funding-deal-at-3b-valuation/)).

## The consent order — and its termination

The charter's supervisory bite came on April 21, 2022, when the OCC and Anchorage agreed to a consent order (AA-ENF-2022-7) in which the regulator said the bank had failed to meet Bank Secrecy Act requirements for its internal controls ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2022/04/21/occ-found-anchorage-failed-to-maintain-proper-aml-rules)). Anchorage neither admitted nor denied the findings. The order found that, as of 2021, the bank had failed to adopt an adequate BSA/AML compliance program — specifically deficient internal controls for customer due diligence, procedures for monitoring suspicious activity, and BSA officer, staff and training ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2022/04/21/occ-found-anchorage-failed-to-maintain-proper-aml-rules)). It required Anchorage to create a compliance committee, a majority of whose members could not be Anchorage employees, filing progress reports with the OCC every 30 days, and to hire an executive to oversee Bank Secrecy Act compliance ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2022/04/21/occ-found-anchorage-failed-to-maintain-proper-aml-rules)). Then-acting Comptroller Michael Hsu said the OCC held all nationally chartered banks to the same standards "whether they engage in traditional or novel activities" ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/cryptocurrency/2025/occ-lifts-consent-order-against-crypto-focused-anchorage-digital-bank/)).

Three years later, the order was lifted. On August 18, 2025, the OCC issued order AA-ENF-2025-44 terminating the 2022 consent order against Anchorage Digital Bank, National Association of Sioux Falls, South Dakota, finding that "the safety and soundness of the Bank and its compliance with laws and regulations does not require the continued existence of the Order" ([OCC](https://occ.gov/static/enforcement-actions/eaAA-ENF-2025-44.pdf?ref=dc.finance)). The termination, announced August 21, 2025, closed a three-year enforcement chapter for the first federally chartered crypto-focused bank ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/cryptocurrency/2025/occ-lifts-consent-order-against-crypto-focused-anchorage-digital-bank/)). Co-founder and CEO Nathan McCauley framed the arc as proof that "crypto and federal oversight are not mutually exclusive — and can in fact be stronger working in tandem," saying the company had spent the intervening period — 1,681 days since the charter — building compliance infrastructure under annual OCC examinations ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/cryptocurrency/2025/occ-lifts-consent-order-against-crypto-focused-anchorage-digital-bank/)). The charter remains in active use: in September 2026 the bank added custody support for assets on Etherlink, an EVM-compatible layer-2 network — including xU3O8, a token representing physical uranium — for its institutional clients ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/anchorage-digital-brings-tokenized-uranium-custody-to-federally-chartered-crypto-bank)).

## What this profile does not claim

- This profile describes Anchorage as the *first* federally chartered digital-asset bank — firmly established. Whether it remains the *only* one is attributed to the dates of the cited coverage; later charter applications by other crypto firms were reported but not independently verified here.
- The profile does not reproduce the founders' prior-employment history or name institutional clients, since those details come from company material rather than independent sources.
- The OCC's reasons for terminating the 2022 order are reported exactly as stated in the termination order; the profile does not interpret whether the decision reflected remediated compliance, a changed enforcement posture, or both.
- This profile covers Anchorage Digital, the San Francisco institutional crypto platform and its OCC-chartered bank — not any similarly named entity.
