## Overview

The Home Depot, Inc. is the world's largest home-improvement retailer, an Atlanta-based public company (NYSE: HD) founded in 1978 by Bernie Marcus and Arthur Blank ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/usa250/2026/04/15/home-depot-iconic-brand-history-diy-retail/88960160007/)). It runs 2,359 stores plus 1,250+ distribution branches, employs 470,000+ people, and reported $164.7 billion in fiscal 2025 sales ([Home Depot](https://corporate.homedepot.com/news/earnings/home-depot-announces-fourth-quarter-2025-earnings)). A September 2022 Accountable.US scorecard named it the top corporate donor to federal lawmakers who voted to overturn the 2020 presidential election ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression?utm_source=postup&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=democracy&position=4&partner=newsletter&campaign_date=04262024)).

The company sits in both the Dow Jones Industrial Average and the S&P 500, and Ted Decker serves as chair, president and CEO ([Home Depot](https://corporate.homedepot.com/news/earnings/home-depot-announces-fourth-quarter-2025-earnings)).

## Origins and scale

Home Depot's premise was deliberately disruptive: warehouse-sized stores carrying vast selection at low prices, staffed by people who could teach customers to use the merchandise ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/usa250/2026/04/15/home-depot-iconic-brand-history-diy-retail/88960160007/)). The first two stores opened in 1979, pioneering the big-box format in home-improvement retail — cavernous aisles of lumber, drywall, tools, and appliances that dwarfed the locally owned hardware stores of the era ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/usa250/2026/04/15/home-depot-iconic-brand-history-diy-retail/88960160007/)).

The company grew alongside the suburban homeownership boom of the 1980s and 1990s, then institutionalized do-it-yourself culture through in-store workshops, instructional guides, and online tutorials ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/usa250/2026/04/15/home-depot-iconic-brand-history-diy-retail/88960160007/)). Today its company-reported footprint spans all 50 states, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, Guam, all ten Canadian provinces, and Mexico ([Home Depot](https://corporate.homedepot.com/news/earnings/home-depot-announces-fourth-quarter-2025-earnings)). It is also a major force in disaster response, routinely pre-positioning plywood, generators, and water pumps ahead of hurricanes and wildfires ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/usa250/2026/04/15/home-depot-iconic-brand-history-diy-retail/88960160007/)).

## The pro-contractor pivot: the SRS acquisition

In March 2024 Home Depot announced, and on June 18, 2024 completed, the acquisition of SRS Distribution — a specialty-trade distributor serving professional roofers, landscapers, and pool contractors — for an enterprise value of approximately $18.25 billion ([Home Depot](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/home-depot-completes-acquisition-srs-distribution-2024-06-18)). SRS, founded in 2008 and headquartered in McKinney, Texas, brought more than 760 locations across 47 states under a family of local brands ([Home Depot](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/home-depot-completes-acquisition-srs-distribution-2024-06-18)). Trade publication Digital Commerce 360 called it Home Depot's largest acquisition ever ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2024/03/28/home-depot-srs-distribution-acquisition/)).

The strategic logic was explicit: the deal raised Home Depot's total addressable market to approximately $1 trillion, an increase of about $50 billion, by accelerating growth with the residential professional customer ([Home Depot](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/home-depot-completes-acquisition-srs-distribution-2024-06-18)). SRS brought a 2,500-plus professional sales force, a 4,000-plus truck fleet, and jobsite delivery capabilities aimed at larger contractors' complex, delivery-intensive orders ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2024/03/28/home-depot-srs-distribution-acquisition/)). In short, the retailer was betting its future less on weekend DIYers and more on professional contractors and B2B digital commerce ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2024/03/28/home-depot-srs-distribution-acquisition/)).

## Political spending and the Democracy Scorecard

In September 2022 — days after President Joe Biden's speech warning that American democracy faced an existential threat — Accountable.US unveiled its "American Democracy Scorecard," grading the top 100 Fortune 500 companies on 14 criteria ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression?utm_source=postup&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=democracy&position=4&partner=newsletter&campaign_date=04262024)). The criteria covered pro-democracy statements, political-spending disclosure transparency, and whether companies' PACs had donated to lawmakers who opposed voting-rights legislation, backed objections to certifying the 2020 election, or opposed creating a January 6 investigation committee ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression?utm_source=postup&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=democracy&position=4&partner=newsletter&campaign_date=04262024)).

Two-thirds of the companies received failing marks; AT&T was the lowest scorer, followed by Verizon, with the remaining worst-ten companies being Energy Transfer, Charter Communications, Comcast, Boeing, ExxonMobil, Lockheed Martin, UnitedHealth Group, and Pfizer — and telecommunications was the worst-ranked industry ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression?utm_source=postup&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=democracy&position=4&partner=newsletter&campaign_date=04262024)). Home Depot emerged as the top corporate donor to federal lawmakers who voted to overturn the 2020 election, contributing over $350,000 to that group — a figure attributed to the scorecard's campaign-finance analysis, presented as reported and not independently audited ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression?utm_source=postup&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=democracy&position=4&partner=newsletter&campaign_date=04262024)). The scorecard also found that 33 of the 50 companies that had paused donations to election objectors after January 6 had quietly resumed them ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression?utm_source=postup&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=democracy&position=4&partner=newsletter&campaign_date=04262024)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who founded Home Depot?**
Home Depot was founded in Atlanta in 1978 by Bernie Marcus and Arthur Blank, and its first two stores opened in 1979. Their warehouse-sized stores with vast selection, low prices, and knowledgeable staff pioneered the big-box format in home-improvement retail ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/usa250/2026/04/15/home-depot-iconic-brand-history-diy-retail/88960160007/)).

**How big is Home Depot today?**
At the end of fiscal 2025 the company reported 2,359 retail stores plus 1,250+ SRS distribution locations across the U.S., D.C., Puerto Rico, the U.S. Virgin Islands, Guam, ten Canadian provinces, and Mexico, with over 470,000 associates and fiscal 2025 sales of $164.7 billion ([Home Depot](https://corporate.homedepot.com/news/earnings/home-depot-announces-fourth-quarter-2025-earnings)).

**What was the SRS Distribution acquisition?**
Home Depot completed its approximately $18.25 billion acquisition of SRS Distribution on June 18, 2024 — the company's largest acquisition ever. SRS, based in McKinney, Texas, supplies professional roofers, landscapers, and pool contractors through 760+ branches ([Home Depot](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/home-depot-completes-acquisition-srs-distribution-2024-06-18); [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2024/03/28/home-depot-srs-distribution-acquisition/)).

**What did the 2022 Democracy Scorecard say about Home Depot?**
Accountable.US's September 2022 American Democracy Scorecard graded the top 100 Fortune 500 companies on 14 democracy criteria. Reporting by Capital & Main/Fast Company identified Home Depot as the top corporate donor to federal lawmakers who voted to overturn the 2020 election — over $350,000, presented as reported, not independently audited ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression?utm_source=postup&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=democracy&position=4&partner=newsletter&campaign_date=04262024)).

**Who leads Home Depot?**
Ted Decker is chair, president and CEO of The Home Depot, as confirmed in the company's earnings and acquisition announcements ([Home Depot](https://corporate.homedepot.com/news/earnings/home-depot-announces-fourth-quarter-2025-earnings)). Decker has publicly framed the company's pro-contractor business as the engine of its next growth chapter, notably praising the SRS Distribution acquisition as an excellent fit for the retailer ([Home Depot](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/home-depot-completes-acquisition-srs-distribution-2024-06-18)).
