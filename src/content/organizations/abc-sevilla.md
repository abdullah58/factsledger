## Overview

ABC Sevilla is the [Seville](/places/seville/) edition of ABC, [Spain's](/places/spain/) conservative, monarchist daily newspaper, founded in the Andalusian capital on 12 October 1929 by Juan Ignacio Luca de Tena ([Francisco Baena Sánchez, Universidad de Sevilla](https://core.ac.uk/download/157757400.pdf)). It shares its Madrid parent's editorial line but has its own newsroom and local pages — and famously, during the Spanish Civil War the two editions backed opposite sides ([Extradigital](https://www.extradigital.es/con-90-anos-abc-de-sevilla-goza-de-buena-salud-gracias-a-su-talante-liberal/)). It is not the American [ABC News](/organizations/abc-news/): the Spanish ABC is an unrelated newspaper, founded in [Madrid](/places/madrid/) in 1903 and owned by the Vocento group.

The Seville edition was born for a world fair. It first appeared on 12 October 1929, the year of the Ibero-American Exposition in Seville, at the instigation of Juan Ignacio Luca de Tena, fulfilling a wish of his father Torcuato — a Sevillian and founder of the Madrid ABC — who had planned a paper for his home city but died in 1929 before launching it ([Francisco Baena Sánchez, Universidad de Sevilla](https://core.ac.uk/download/157757400.pdf); [Extradigital](https://www.extradigital.es/con-90-anos-abc-de-sevilla-goza-de-buena-salud-gracias-a-su-talante-liberal/)). Its formula was twofold: the Madrid issue's national and international content, reprinted identically, plus local Andalusian news expanded "with the greatest care" ([Francisco Baena Sánchez, Universidad de Sevilla](https://core.ac.uk/download/157757400.pdf)). It is still published daily from Seville, directed since 1 September 2022 by Alberto García Reyes ([Dos Hermanas Diario Digital](https://www.doshermanasdiariodigital.com/2022/11/alberto-garcia-reyes-pregonero-del-50.html)).

## A Seville paper, born for an exposition

The first ABC de Sevilla was a physical statement as much as an editorial one. Its premises stood in the Huerta de la Salud, on about 2,600 square metres, with five linotype machines and a Koenig & Bauer double-reel rotary press driven by a 60-horsepower motor and capable of 25,000 copies an hour ([Francisco Baena Sánchez, Universidad de Sevilla](https://core.ac.uk/download/157757400.pdf)). Copies travelled by train and bus from its Calle Muñoz Olive office across the province and Andalusia, reaching Huelva, Cádiz, part of Córdoba and points in Jaén, Málaga and Granada ([Francisco Baena Sánchez, Universidad de Sevilla](https://core.ac.uk/download/157757400.pdf)).

Its first director, Juan Carretero Luca de Tena, previously of El Noticiero Sevillano, stayed in post until 1937 ([Francisco Baena Sánchez, Universidad de Sevilla](https://core.ac.uk/download/157757400.pdf)). From the first issue its line was fixed: monarchist, Catholic, conservative and right-wing, defending a unitary Spanish nationalism and hostile to Catalan and Basque nationalism ([Francisco Baena Sánchez, Universidad de Sevilla](https://core.ac.uk/download/157757400.pdf)). In a crowded Seville market — El Liberal, El Correo de Andalucía, La Unión and El Noticiero Sevillano — ABC took the monarchist readership, and El Noticiero Sevillano disappeared in 1933 ([Francisco Baena Sánchez, Universidad de Sevilla](https://core.ac.uk/download/157757400.pdf)).

The government suspended it for 23 days in 1931, and after the failed Sanjurjo coup attempt of August 1932 silenced it for 112 days, from 10 August to 30 November, while its Seville premises suffered a fire that month ([Francisco Baena Sánchez, Universidad de Sevilla](https://core.ac.uk/download/157757400.pdf)). Yet the business held: in 1931 the Andalusian edition averaged 31,032 copies a day over 289 publishing days, 651,660 more than in 1930, and by 1933 circulation had risen 8.38% on the year before ([Francisco Baena Sánchez, Universidad de Sevilla](https://core.ac.uk/download/157757400.pdf)). Prensa Española's 1935 annual report called it the largest-circulation paper in Andalusia, and in 1936 it counted 3,500 subscribers ([Francisco Baena Sánchez, Universidad de Sevilla](https://core.ac.uk/download/157757400.pdf)).

## Two ABCs: the Civil War split

When the war broke out in 1936, the Madrid edition fell under Republican control while the Seville edition backed the Nationalist side, and the two papers printed war bulletins claiming opposite victories ([Extradigital](https://www.extradigital.es/con-90-anos-abc-de-sevilla-goza-de-buena-salud-gracias-a-su-talante-liberal/)). The Luca de Tena family, expropriated in the capital, fled to Seville and took control of the Seville edition there ([Extradigital](https://www.extradigital.es/con-90-anos-abc-de-sevilla-goza-de-buena-salud-gracias-a-su-talante-liberal/)). During the war the Seville edition became practically the only daily circulating across all of Nationalist-controlled Spain ([Francisco Baena Sánchez, Universidad de Sevilla](https://core.ac.uk/download/157757400.pdf)).

Juan Ignacio Luca de Tena, who had resigned as director in March 1936, acted as the link for General Emilio Mola and financed the purchase of the Dragon Rapide aircraft — "Operación Ernestina" — that flew General Franco from the Canary Islands to North Africa to start the uprising ([Francisco Baena Sánchez, Universidad de Sevilla](https://core.ac.uk/download/157757400.pdf)). The Seville paper was a protagonist of the history it covered: during 1931–1936 it acted as an instrument of political agitation against the young democracy, a defiance the republican president Manuel Azaña summed up as, "either the Republic finishes off ABC, or ABC finishes off the Republic" ([Francisco Baena Sánchez, Universidad de Sevilla](https://core.ac.uk/download/157757400.pdf)).

## The Marginales and the paper's literary voice

If the Civil War is ABC Sevilla's most dramatic chapter, its most beloved is a joke column. The "Marginales" — short, unsigned one-liners in the manner of Ramón Gómez de la Serna's greguerías — first appeared on 4 February 1962, created after a conversation between director Joaquín Carlos López-Lozano and the writer Joaquín Romero Murube ([Ámbitos, Universidad de Sevilla](https://revistascientificas.us.es/index.php/Ambitos/article/download/9668/8471/29678)). Manuel Ferrand chose the name; after Benigno González and Antonio Burgos, Julio Martínez Velasco took it over on 30 June 1968 and kept it until his retirement in January 1986 ([Ámbitos, Universidad de Sevilla](https://revistascientificas.us.es/index.php/Ambitos/article/download/9668/8471/29678)). Unsigned at first, the column began to carry Martínez Velasco's name in 1977, when director Nicolás Salas proposed it; over eighteen years more than 60,000 Marginales saw print ([Ámbitos, Universidad de Sevilla](https://revistascientificas.us.es/index.php/Ambitos/article/download/9668/8471/29678)).

Its celebrated page three published José María Pemán, Julián Marías, Fernando Arrabal, Rafael Laffón, Sánchez Ferlosio and Camilo José Cela, among others, and was described as the most prestigious space in the Spanish daily press ([Ámbitos, Universidad de Sevilla](https://revistascientificas.us.es/index.php/Ambitos/article/download/9668/8471/29678)). Marking the 75th anniversary in 2004, Fernando Iwasaki argued that the history of twentieth-century Seville could never be written without the memories in ABC Sevilla's pages, summing up its legacy as information, creation and opinion ([Fernando Iwasaki, Universidad de Sevilla](https://idus.us.es/bitstream/handle/11441/72086/Pages%20from%20segunda%20versi%c3%b3n%20actas%20abc-6.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y)). Three books grew directly from its columns: Joaquín Romero Murube's "Los cielos que perdimos" (1964), Juan Sierra González's "Sevilla en su cielo" (1984) and Manuel Ferrand's "Las campanas perdidas" (1987) ([Fernando Iwasaki, Universidad de Sevilla](https://idus.us.es/bitstream/handle/11441/72086/Pages%20from%20segunda%20versi%c3%b3n%20actas%20abc-6.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y)).

## ABC Sevilla today: digital, Andalusian, still family-owned

Álvaro Ybarra Pacheco, who directed ABC de Sevilla from July 2000 after joining as an editorial assistant in 1982, launching its economics section in 1987 and serving as editor-in-chief and deputy director, led a broad remodelling of the newsroom and its digital transformation ([Europa Press](https://www.vanguardiadesevilla.com/texto-diario/mostrar/1655291/alvaro-ybarra-pacheco-recibe-galardon-periodistico-fundacion-alberto-jimenez-becerril)). By the 90th anniversary in 2019 the newsroom was bimedia — website and social media first, print after — and 70% of the paper's revenue came from digital advertising ([Extradigital](https://www.extradigital.es/con-90-anos-abc-de-sevilla-goza-de-buena-salud-gracias-a-su-talante-liberal/)). It also runs specialist digital verticals — Gourmet for restaurants, Tecnológica for technology and Bulevar 21 for leisure — while the group publishes ABC de Córdoba, La Voz de Cádiz and a Jerez edition ([Extradigital](https://www.extradigital.es/con-90-anos-abc-de-sevilla-goza-de-buena-salud-gracias-a-su-talante-liberal/)).

Ybarra described its line as a Christian humanism with a liberal temper, and noted that its highest distinction, the Premio Mariano de Cavia, bears the name of a republican left-winger who never wrote for ABC ([Extradigital](https://www.extradigital.es/con-90-anos-abc-de-sevilla-goza-de-buena-salud-gracias-a-su-talante-liberal/)). The Seville edition was the first newspaper to carry the Andalusian flag on its front page, and Ybarra credited Antonio Burgos and Nicolás Salas with bringing its conservative readership towards the new autonomous institutions ([Extradigital](https://www.extradigital.es/con-90-anos-abc-de-sevilla-goza-de-buena-salud-gracias-a-su-talante-liberal/)).

Since 1 September 2022 the paper has been directed by Alberto García Reyes, who joined ABC de Sevilla in 1999, served as local editor-in-chief, and won the Premio Joaquín Romero Murube in 2010 and the Premio Vocento in 2012 ([Dos Hermanas Diario Digital](https://www.doshermanasdiariodigital.com/2022/11/alberto-garcia-reyes-pregonero-del-50.html)). Its local reporting still reaches into Seville's social sector: it covered a €100,000 donation to the deafblind association [APASCIDE](/organizations/asociacion-espanola-de-familias-de-personas-con-sordoceguera-apascide/) in 2017 ([ABC Sevilla](https://www.abc.es/sevilla/pasionensevilla/noticias-semana-santa-sevilla/sevi-la-vera-cruz-vuelve-conceder-100-000-euros-apascide-118981-1509662011-201711030014_noticia.html)). Vocento had by 2019 put paywalls on Ideal and Sur, its other Andalusian papers, but Seville — where ABC leads the market — was to be among the last ([Extradigital](https://www.extradigital.es/con-90-anos-abc-de-sevilla-goza-de-buena-salud-gracias-a-su-talante-liberal/)).
