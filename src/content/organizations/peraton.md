## Overview

Peraton is an American national-security technology company owned by private-equity firm Veritas Capital — and it was effectively built, not born. In 2021, Veritas assembled Peraton through two mega-deals: the $7.1 billion all-cash acquisition of government-services provider Perspecta and the $3.4 billion purchase of Northrop Grumman's IT and mission support services business ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/veritas-purchases-perspecta-for-7-billion-in-cash); [GovCon Wire](https://www.govconwire.com/article/veritas-strengthens-peraton-with-perspecta-acquisition)).

## Two deals, one platform

The Perspecta deal, announced January 27, 2021, priced the Chantilly-based contractor at $29.35 per share — a premium of nearly 50% over its unaffected stock price before sale reports surfaced, and a 12% premium to the prior day's close ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/veritas-purchases-perspecta-for-7-billion-in-cash)). Veritas was already a long-time investor, holding about 14.5% of Perspecta's shares, and Peraton — then a smaller Veritas portfolio company — was designated the acquiring entity ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/veritas-purchases-perspecta-for-7-billion-in-cash)).

Weeks later, in February 2021, Peraton completed the $3.4 billion acquisition of Northrop Grumman's IT and mission support business ([GovCon Wire](https://www.govconwire.com/article/veritas-strengthens-peraton-with-perspecta-acquisition)). When the Perspecta deal closed on May 6, 2021, the combined company operated under the Peraton brand, led by chairman, president and CEO Stu Shea — with 22,000 employees, a backlog of about $24.4 billion, and approximately $7 billion in annual revenue ([GovCon Wire](https://www.govconwire.com/article/veritas-strengthens-peraton-with-perspecta-acquisition)).

## The maturing incumbent: wins and losses

Five years on, Peraton behaves like the entrenched incumbent Veritas set out to build. In August 2026 it won a potential 10-year, $953 million recompete of the Defense Information Systems Agency's Capacity Services Communications contract, covering communications infrastructure for data centers and hybrid-cloud environments across DISA and non-DISA sites worldwide ([Washington Technology](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/08/peraton-wins-290m-disa-comms-infrastructure-recompete/415482/?oref=wt-skybox-post)). Notably, Peraton inherited the program through the Perspecta combination — Perspecta had bought longtime incumbent Knight Point Systems in 2019, and DISA had already obligated $573 million in task-order volume against the current contract ([Washington Technology](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/08/peraton-wins-290m-disa-comms-infrastructure-recompete/415482/?oref=wt-skybox-post)).

Incumbency cuts both ways. In September 2025, Peraton lost a Government Accountability Office protest seeking to regain a $1.5 billion U.S. Strategic Command IT services task order it had held as incumbent; General Dynamics IT won the work supporting classified and unclassified networks, and the GAO rejected Peraton's challenges to the evaluation as well as its organizational-conflict-of-interest allegation ([Washington Technology](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2025/09/peraton-loses-gao-protest-over-15b-stratcom-it-contract/408202/)).
