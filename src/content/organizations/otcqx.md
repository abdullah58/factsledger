## Overview

OTCQX is the top tier of the OTC Markets — the "Best Market," with more stringent financial and reporting requirements than the tiers below it ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/o/over-the-countermarket.asp)). Three distinctions matter before anything else: OTCQX is not a company and not an exchange — it is a market tier operated by OTC Markets Group Inc. (which itself trades as OTCQX: OTCM); its companies are quoted over the counter through dealer networks on SEC-regulated OTC Link ATSs, not listed on a national securities exchange; and it sits atop four tiers — OTCQX, then the OTCQB Venture Market, the OTCID Basic Market, and the Pink Limited Market ([OTC Markets Group](https://markets.financialcontent.com/pentictonherald/article/gnwcq-2026-1-15-otc-markets-group-announces-the-2026-otcqx-best-50); [Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/o/over-the-countermarket.asp)). The tier's most visible role: giving established foreign companies — Deutsche Telekom, Roche, AXA, BNP Paribas, Fortescue, Nordea all appeared in the 2026 OTCQX Best 50 — efficient access to U.S. investors without a [NYSE](/organizations/new-york-stock-exchange/) or Nasdaq listing ([OTC Markets Group](https://markets.financialcontent.com/pentictonherald/article/gnwcq-2026-1-15-otc-markets-group-announces-the-2026-otcqx-best-50)).

## What it takes to be on OTCQX

The April 2026 U.S. eligibility standards read like a lite exchange listing rulebook. Issuers need PCAOB-audited GAAP financials, a transfer agent in the Verified Shares program, at least two independent directors, an audit committee of at least two (majority independent), annual shareholder meetings with reports available 15 days prior, and no bankruptcy or reorganization proceedings ([OTC Markets Group](https://www.otcmarkets.com/files/Eligibility_Requirements_OTCQX_US.pdf)). The quantitative floors: a $0.25 bid price to qualify ($0.10 to stay), $25 million market capitalization to qualify ($10 million to stay), 100 beneficial shareholders each holding at least 100 shares, and public float worth at least $5 million meeting distribution criteria ([OTC Markets Group](https://www.otcmarkets.com/files/Eligibility_Requirements_OTCQX_US.pdf)). Shells and penny stocks are excluded — issuers must meet a penny-stock exemption via net tangible assets, revenue, or a $5.00 bid-price test ([securitieslawyer101](https://www.securitieslawyer101.com/2025/10/13/comparing-otcqx-otcqb-and-otcid/)). The step down is visible in the tiers below: OTCQB, the "Venture Market" for earlier-stage companies, asks only a $0.01 bid, about 50 shareholders, and 10% float; OTCID is the entry-level disclosure tier ([securitieslawyer101](https://www.securitieslawyer101.com/2025/10/13/comparing-otcqx-otcqb-and-otcid/); [Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/o/over-the-countermarket.asp)).

## How the tier changed in 2025–2026

OTC Markets has been tightening the velvet rope. In March 2026 it published proposed OTCQX rule amendments (Release No. 22): companies missing the revenue or net-tangible-asset penny-stock tests could conditionally qualify on a $5.00 bid until their next annual report; every company would need the $5 million public-float floor; the shareholder minimum rose from 50 to 100; and the market-cap floors rose to $25 million initial / $10 million ongoing — scheduled to take effect April 6, 2026, with existing companies given until April 2027 to comply ([OTC Markets Group](https://www.otcmarkets.com/files/OTCQX%20Rules%20Release%20No%2022.pdf)). The updated U.S. eligibility requirements followed in April 2026 ([OTC Markets Group](https://www.otcmarkets.com/files/Eligibility_Requirements_OTCQX_US.pdf)). The lineage runs back over a century: the Pink Sheets were literally pink paper on which bid and ask prices for unlisted securities were printed and circulated, going electronic in the late 1990s before becoming today's OTC Markets Group ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/o/over-the-countermarket.asp)).

A concrete case: [First Acceptance Corporation](/organizations/acceptance-insurance/) — the Nashville non-standard auto insurer — announced in March 2018 that it would voluntarily delist from the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE: FAC) effective on or about April 9, 2018, "to allow its common stock to trade through the OTC market"; it now trades on OTCQX as FACO ([First Acceptance](https://www.advfn.com/stock-market/USOTC/FACO/stock-news/76977389/first-acceptance-corporation-announces-intent-to-v)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is OTCQX a stock exchange?**
No. It is the top tier of the OTC Markets, operated by OTC Markets Group Inc. Companies on OTCQX are quoted over the counter through dealer networks, not listed on a national securities exchange like the NYSE or Nasdaq.

**What is the difference between OTCQX, OTCQB, and OTCID?**
OTCQX is the premium tier for established companies with the strictest financial, governance, and disclosure standards. OTCQB ("Venture Market") serves earlier-stage companies with lighter requirements. OTCID is the entry-level disclosure-based tier. All three sit above the Pink Limited Market.

**Who operates OTCQX?**
OTC Markets Group Inc. (ticker OTCM, itself traded on OTCQX), which operates regulated markets for 12,000 U.S. and international securities through SEC-regulated OTC Link ATSs.

**What does a company need to join OTCQX?**
As of April 2026: PCAOB-audited financials, independent directors and an audit committee, annual shareholder meetings, no bankruptcy, penny-stock exemption, a $0.25+ bid, $25M+ market cap, 100+ shareholders, and $5M+ public float.
