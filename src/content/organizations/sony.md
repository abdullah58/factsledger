## Overview

Sony Group Corporation is a Japanese multinational conglomerate headquartered in Tokyo, [Japan](/places/japan/), founded in 1946 as Tokyo Tsushin Kogyo by Masaru Ibuka and Akio Morita ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Sony?ref=thinkml.ai)). It makes PlayStation consoles and games, films, music and consumer electronics — and its image sensors sit inside many of the world's flagship phones: Sony held about 54% of the global image-sensor market in 2025 ([The Elec](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=14744)). In the fiscal year ended 31 March 2026 it reported sales of ¥12.48 trillion and operating income of ¥1.45 trillion from its continuing operations ([TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/news/company-announcements/sony-group-posts-higher-fy2026-operating-profit-as-financial-unit-spin-off-triggers-accounting-loss)).

The company began in war-devastated Tokyo with 190,000 yen of capital, took its name from the Latin *sonus* ("sound") in January 1958, and became a holding company called Sony Group Corporation in April 2021 ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/sony-corporation); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Sony?ref=thinkml.ai)). Since 1 April 2025 it has been led by President and CEO Hiroki Totoki, with former CEO Kenichiro Yoshida remaining as chairman ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/sony-group-says-president-totoki-to-add-ceo-role-from-april/)). It is not the American Sony Pictures alone, nor only an electronics maker: games, music, film and image sensors now matter at least as much as the televisions and radios that built the brand.

## From a Tokyo radio shop to a global brand

Ibuka and Morita had met during World War II as engineers designing heat-seeking missiles for the Imperial Japanese Army, and they worked together for the next 40 years in what has been called one of "business history's most productive and intriguing relationships" ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Sony?ref=thinkml.ai)). Their first consumer product was an electric rice cooker, which sold poorly, but the young firm did steady business repairing radios and other electrical equipment, including work for the broadcaster NHK that had to be approved by the U.S. army of occupation ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Sony?ref=thinkml.ai)). In 1950 it introduced the first Japanese-designed tape recorder, and in 1952 Ibuka visited the United States to make the initial contacts for licensing the transistor from Bell Laboratories; Morita signed the deal with Western Electric the next year ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Sony?ref=thinkml.ai)).

The transistor licence was the turning point. Although Texas Instruments was first to market with its Regency transistor radio in 1955, it was Sony's TR-63, an inexpensive shirt-pocket-sized all-transistor radio released in 1957, that caught consumers' attention and brought the brand international recognition ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Sony?ref=thinkml.ai)). Sony Corporation of America followed in 1960, and when the company opened its Fifth Avenue store in 1962 it unfurled the first Japanese flag flown in the United States since the start of World War II ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Sony?ref=thinkml.ai)). At the 1964 New York World's Fair Sony introduced the MD-5, the first all-transistor desktop calculator, and in 1968 it shipped its first Trinitron colour television, a set that earned Sony a Technical Emmy ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Sony?ref=thinkml.ai); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/sony-corporation)).

Sony's format wars taught it hard lessons. It introduced the first colour video cassette recorder in 1971 and the Betamax VCR in 1975, but Betamax — though widely considered the best VCR technology ever developed — cost more than its rival VHS, and as studios and video stores turned to VHS, Sony finally introduced its own VHS in 1988 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Sony?ref=thinkml.ai)). The lesson stuck: the first compact disc player, which emerged in 1982 from a development agreement with Philips, used a format standard agreed by a wide range of companies, and the first camcorder followed in 1983 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Sony?ref=thinkml.ai)). In 1979 the Walkman portable tape player hit the streets — Morita insisted on developing a device that could only play and not record, saying he would resign if it was not a success — and it became an international sensation that eventually sold hundreds of millions of units ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Sony?ref=thinkml.ai)).

By the late 1980s Sony wanted to own entertainment content as well as the hardware that played it. It bought CBS Records Group, including Columbia Records, from CBS Inc. in 1988, acquiring the world's largest record company, and the next year it purchased Columbia Pictures Entertainment — the largest acquisition to that time of an American company by a Japanese firm, which ignited controversy in the United States ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Sony?ref=thinkml.ai)). The early 1990s were difficult: the Japanese economy entered a decade-long recession, Ibuka and Morita suffered strokes in 1992 and 1993, Morita retired in 1994 and died in 1999, and Sony declared its first loss, more than $200 million, in 1993 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Sony?ref=thinkml.ai)). Yet the same decade produced the PlayStation, introduced in Japan in 1994 and contributing more than 10% of yearly revenues by 2002, plus the VAIO computer line in 1997 and the AIBO robot dog in 1999 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Sony?ref=thinkml.ai)). Howard Stringer became chairman and CEO in 2005, the first non-Japanese to head the parent company, and Kazuo Hirai succeeded him in 2012; in 2013 Sony sold its U.S. headquarters in New York City for more than $1 billion ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Sony?ref=thinkml.ai)).

## The business today: games, entertainment and sensors

Sony today is an entertainment and technology group whose largest business is games. In the fiscal year ended 31 March 2025, its Game & Network Services division — the PlayStation business — generated sales of ¥4.67 trillion, up 9%, and operating income of ¥414.8 billion, up 43%, accounting for 36% of group revenue ([GamesMarket](https://www.gamesmarket.global/financial-results-sonys-playstation-division-recorded-strong-growth-but-the-outlook-remains-cautious-1a5ecfa83784106c362f1205c86bc396/)). It sold 18.5 million PlayStation 5 consoles in that year, taking cumulative PS5 sales to 77.8 million since launch, and sold 303.3 million full games, 76% of them digital, to 124 million monthly active PlayStation Network users ([GamesMarket](https://www.gamesmarket.global/financial-results-sonys-playstation-division-recorded-strong-growth-but-the-outlook-remains-cautious-1a5ecfa83784106c362f1205c86bc396/)). By the time the PlayStation 5 launched in 2020, Sony had become the largest manufacturer of video game consoles and the largest video game publisher in the world ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/sony-corporation)). It strengthened that position by acquiring the studio Bungie for $3.6 billion in 2022, and in 2024 Sony Pictures Entertainment acquired the dine-in cinema chain Alamo Drafthouse ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/sony-group-says-president-totoki-to-add-ceo-role-from-april/); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/sony-corporation)).

The quietest part of Sony's business is one of its most dominant. Sony held a 54% share of global CMOS image sensor revenue in 2025, far ahead of OmniVision at 13% and Samsung at 12.8%, in a market worth $25.3 billion ([The Elec](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=14744)). [Apple](/organizations/apple/) previously sourced all the image sensors for its iPhones from Sony, which is why the sensor division's fortunes now track the premium smartphone cycle as closely as Sony's own electronics do ([The Elec](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=14744)). The division's strength is the quiet counterpart to the PlayStation story: the company that once licensed transistor technology from Bell Laboratories now supplies the image sensors inside its rivals' phones.

In October 2025 Sony completed a partial spin-off of its financial services business, Sony Financial Group Inc., keeping a 16.40% stake and turning the unit from a consolidated subsidiary into an equity-method affiliate ([TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/news/company-announcements/sony-completes-spin-off-of-financial-services-business)). The move sharpened Sony's focus on entertainment, technology and imaging, and it carried an accounting cost: the spin-off produced a ¥1.38 trillion reclassification loss in discontinued operations, leaving a consolidated net loss of ¥302.5 billion for the year ended 31 March 2026 even as continuing operations earned ¥1.06 trillion ([TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/news/company-announcements/sony-group-posts-higher-fy2026-operating-profit-as-financial-unit-spin-off-triggers-accounting-loss)). The spin-off had been planned since May 2025, when Sony said it would keep a stake of under 20% and absorb an estimated ¥100 billion hit to operating profit from U.S. tariffs ([Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/SONY-RESULTS-b6cc9cdd-8996-4daf-8edb-406aa9f24aad/)).

## The $15 million JPEG licence

One episode in Sony's history rarely appears in company overviews, and it runs through the FactsLedger profile of [Asure Software](/organizations/asure-software/). In July 2002, Forgent Networks of Austin, Texas — the company now known as Asure Software — announced that it held the sole and exclusive right to license all claims under U.S. Patent 4,698,672 that implement JPEG, in every field of use except satellite broadcasting ([InternetNews](https://www.internetnews.com/it-management/jpeg-patent-becoming-a-battleground/)). The patent, titled "coding system for reducing redundancy," had been filed in 1986 by [Compression Labs](/organizations/compression-labs/), a company Forgent acquired in 1997 ([InternetNews](https://www.internetnews.com/it-management/jpeg-patent-becoming-a-battleground/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/offbeat/2004/04/23/jpeg-patent-holder-renews-royalty-offensive/799817)). Forgent claimed the patent covered the run-length encoding used in JPEG compression — the format behind nearly every digital photograph ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/offbeat/2004/04/23/jpeg-patent-holder-renews-royalty-offensive/799817)).

Sony paid Forgent $15 million for the licensing rights, according to SEC documents ([InternetNews](https://www.internetnews.com/it-management/jpeg-patent-becoming-a-battleground/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/offbeat/2004/04/23/jpeg-patent-holder-renews-royalty-offensive/799817)). The payment alarmed the imaging industry: the JPEG committee's standards body, [ISO](/organizations/iso/), threatened to withdraw its blessing from the format, since ISO only endorses royalty-free standards ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/offbeat/2004/04/23/jpeg-patent-holder-renews-royalty-offensive/799817)). By April 2004 Forgent claimed about $90 million in licensing royalties from the patent and sued 30 companies that had refused its terms, including [JVC](/organizations/jvc/), Matsushita ([Panasonic](/organizations/panasonic/)), [Ricoh](/organizations/ricoh/), [Fuji](/organizations/fujifilm/), [Kodak](/organizations/kodak/), [IBM](/organizations/ibm/), [Dell](/organizations/dell/), Apple, [HP](/organizations/hp/), [Toshiba](/organizations/toshiba/) and [Adobe](/organizations/adobe/) ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/offbeat/2004/04/23/jpeg-patent-holder-renews-royalty-offensive/799817)). The patent had only about two years left before expiry at that point, twenty years after its filing ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/offbeat/2004/04/23/jpeg-patent-holder-renews-royalty-offensive/799817)). Sony's $15 million bought certainty at the moment the dispute was hottest — and, as the Asure Software profile records, Forgent went on to collect more than $110 million from over 60 companies before the patent expired ([Asure Software](https://factsledger.com/organizations/asure-software/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Sony Group Corporation?**

Its businesses span games, music, film and television, consumer electronics and image sensors ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Sony?ref=thinkml.ai); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/sony-corporation)).

**When was Sony founded and who founded it?**

**What are Sony's main businesses today?**

**What happened to Sony's financial services business?**
Sony completed a partial spin-off of Sony Financial Group Inc. on 1 October 2025, keeping a 16.40% stake. The unit is now an equity-method affiliate rather than a consolidated subsidiary, sharpening Sony's focus on entertainment, technology and imaging ([TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/news/company-announcements/sony-completes-spin-off-of-financial-services-business)).
