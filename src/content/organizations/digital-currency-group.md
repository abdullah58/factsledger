## Overview

Digital Currency Group (DCG) is an American crypto investment firm founded in 2015 by Barry Silbert, an early Bitcoin investor who had sold his private-shares trading platform SecondMarket to Nasdaq that year ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/sponsored/special-projects/top-people-in-crypto-and-blockchain/2022/barry-slbert/)). Based in Stamford, Connecticut after moving from [New York City](/places/new-york-city/), it built, bought and invested in blockchain companies — at its peak backing more than 200 crypto startups and owning Grayscale, Genesis, CoinDesk, Foundry and Luno ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/sponsored/special-projects/top-people-in-crypto-and-blockchain/2022/barry-slbert/)). Its story since is a rise and a reckoning: a $10 billion valuation in 2021, then the bankruptcy of its lending arm Genesis in 2023 and a New York fraud lawsuit naming Silbert himself.

DCG survived that reckoning in a smaller form. Its best-known asset, Grayscale's Bitcoin Trust, became a spot Bitcoin ETF in January 2024, while Genesis was restructured and its creditors repaid in kind under a court-confirmed plan that DCG itself opposed ([Cleary Gottlieb](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/news-and-insights/news-listing/genesis-completes-debt-restructuring)). CoinDesk, the media arm, was sold to Bullish in November 2023 ([Bullish](https://www.bullish.com/us/news-insights/bullish-global-acquires-coindesk-from-digital-currency-group)).

## Building a crypto conglomerate

Silbert's path into crypto began before DCG existed. He first bought Bitcoin in 2012 and launched Grayscale and its Bitcoin Investment Trust in 2013 — the first publicly quoted security deriving its value from Bitcoin's price ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/sponsored/special-projects/top-people-in-crypto-and-blockchain/2022/barry-slbert/)). When Nasdaq bought SecondMarket in 2015, Silbert kept Genesis and Grayscale and combined them with his personal seed investments to form DCG, a holding company meant to build and fund the whole crypto ecosystem at once ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/sponsored/special-projects/top-people-in-crypto-and-blockchain/2022/barry-slbert/)).

The model worked while crypto boomed. DCG invested in more than 200 crypto startups across 15 countries, including Coinbase, Circle, Kraken, Ripple Labs, BitGo and BitPay, and added CoinDesk in 2016, Foundry for Bitcoin mining and the Luno exchange by the end of 2020 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/sponsored/special-projects/top-people-in-crypto-and-blockchain/2022/barry-slbert/)). In November 2021 it sold $700 million of shares to SoftBank and Alphabet's venture arm, valuing the company at $10 billion, and announced a move of its headquarters from New York City to Stamford, Connecticut, bringing hundreds of jobs to the region ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/sponsored/special-projects/top-people-in-crypto-and-blockchain/2022/barry-slbert/)). It also joined the $18 million Series A of blockchain firm [Axoni](/organizations/axoni/) in 2016 alongside [Thomson Reuters](/organizations/thomson-reuters/) and [Goldman Sachs](/organizations/goldman-sachs/) ([Financial Adviser Magazine](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/vc-deal-of-the-day-axioni-raises-18m-series-a-46944.html?print)).

## The Genesis collapse and the New York lawsuit

The conglomerate's weak point was Genesis, its lending arm. When crypto hedge fund Three Arrows Capital defaulted in June 2022, Genesis lost heavily, and after the FTX collapse that November it suspended withdrawals, leaving investors unable to redeem their assets ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/ny-attorney-general-sues-gemini-genesis-and-dcg-for-defrauding-crypto-investors-of-over-1b/204)). Genesis filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in January 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/ny-attorney-general-sues-gemini-genesis-and-dcg-for-defrauding-crypto-investors-of-over-1b/204)). Days earlier, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission had charged Genesis and the Gemini exchange over the unregistered offer of securities through Gemini Earn, the lending programme Genesis ran with Gemini ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/ny-attorney-general-sues-gemini-genesis-and-dcg-for-defrauding-crypto-investors-of-over-1b/204)).

In October 2023 New York Attorney General Letitia James sued Gemini, Genesis and DCG, alleging they defrauded more than 230,000 investors of over $1 billion, and personally charging Silbert and former Genesis chief Michael Moro with hiding $1.1 billion in losses ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/ny-attorney-general-sues-gemini-genesis-and-dcg-for-defrauding-crypto-investors-of-over-1b/204)). The suit alleged Gemini called Earn a "low-risk investment" while Genesis' loans were undersecured and concentrated in Sam Bankman-Fried's Alameda, and that losses from Three Arrows Capital and Babel Finance topped $1.1 billion ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/ny-attorney-general-sues-gemini-genesis-and-dcg-for-defrauding-crypto-investors-of-over-1b/204)). DCG said it would fight the claims, called them "baseless", and said it had always conducted business lawfully ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/ny-attorney-general-sues-gemini-genesis-and-dcg-for-defrauding-crypto-investors-of-over-1b/204)). These are allegations, not findings, and the profile states them as such.

## What survived: Grayscale, Foundry, Luno — and a smaller DCG

The bankruptcy court, not DCG, decided how Genesis ended. Its Chapter 11 plan was confirmed on 21 May 2024 over DCG's objection — DCG had argued post-petition crypto appreciation should go to it as shareholder — and the restructuring completed on 2 August 2024 with about $4 billion distributed to creditors, plus about $2.2 billion to Gemini Earn users in May–June 2024 ([Cleary Gottlieb](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/news-and-insights/news-listing/genesis-completes-debt-restructuring)). Bitcoin creditors received 51% of their holdings in kind (166% of petition-date value) and dollar creditors 100% ([Cleary Gottlieb](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/news-and-insights/news-listing/genesis-completes-debt-restructuring)). The plan also settled a $3 billion NYAG claim — with the attorney general waiving penalties and sending recoveries to a victims' fund — and extracted more than $630 million in loan repayments from DCG ([Cleary Gottlieb](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/news-and-insights/news-listing/genesis-completes-debt-restructuring)).

Meanwhile the surviving businesses found their footing. On 10 January 2024 the SEC approved converting Grayscale's Bitcoin Trust into a spot Bitcoin ETF on NYSE Arca, trading from the next day, making it the world's largest spot Bitcoin ETF at launch with 3.16% of all Bitcoin in circulation ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1588489/000095017024003686/gbtc_uplisting_email.htm)). DCG sold CoinDesk to Bullish on 20 November 2023 for undisclosed terms ([Bullish](https://www.bullish.com/us/news-insights/bullish-global-acquires-coindesk-from-digital-currency-group)). For 2023, excluding Genesis, DCG reported $749 million in revenue and $275 million in EBITDA, and an independent valuation put it at $4.4 billion — down from the $10 billion peak, but double the $2.2 billion of a year earlier ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2024/02/12/digital-currency-group-reports-q4-revenue-of-210m-up-59-yy-full-text)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who founded Digital Currency Group?**
Barry Silbert founded DCG in 2015 after selling SecondMarket to Nasdaq. He had bought Bitcoin in 2012 and launched Grayscale and its Bitcoin trust in 2013 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/sponsored/special-projects/top-people-in-crypto-and-blockchain/2022/barry-slbert/)).

**What happened to Genesis?**
Genesis, DCG's lending arm, suspended withdrawals in November 2022 and filed for Chapter 11 in January 2023 after losses on Three Arrows Capital and other borrowers. Its court-confirmed plan repaid creditors in kind, distributing about $4 billion by August 2024 ([Cleary Gottlieb](https://www.clearygottlieb.com/news-and-insights/news-listing/genesis-completes-debt-restructuring)).

**Was DCG found guilty of fraud?**
No finding is stated in any source read for this profile. New York's attorney general sued DCG, Genesis and Gemini in October 2023 alleging a fraud of over $1 billion; DCG called the claims baseless and said it would fight them ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/ny-attorney-general-sues-gemini-genesis-and-dcg-for-defrauding-crypto-investors-of-over-1b/204)).

**What is DCG worth now?**
An independent 409A valuation put DCG at $4.4 billion at 31 December 2023, up from $2.2 billion a year earlier but well below the $10 billion valuation from its November 2021 secondary share sale ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2024/02/12/digital-currency-group-reports-q4-revenue-of-210m-up-59-yy-full-text)).

**Does DCG still own CoinDesk?**
No. Bullish Global acquired CoinDesk from DCG on 20 November 2023, for undisclosed terms, and CoinDesk operates as an independent subsidiary of Bullish ([Bullish](https://www.bullish.com/us/news-insights/bullish-global-acquires-coindesk-from-digital-currency-group)).
