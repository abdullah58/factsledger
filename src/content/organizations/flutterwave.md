## Overview

Flutterwave is the African payments fintech that raised more venture money than any startup on the continent — and then spent the next two years proving it could survive the scrutiny that money attracted. Founded in 2016 in Lagos and San Francisco by Olugbenga "GB" Agboola, Adeleke Adekoya and Iyinoluwa Aboyeji, it built a single API letting businesses collect and disburse payments across Africa's fragmented banking, card and mobile-money systems ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2022/04/16/flutterwave-iyin-aboyeji-startup-ceo-accusation/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/02/16/african-fintech-flutterwave-triples-valuation-to-over-3b-after-250m-series-d/)). In March 2021 it raised $170 million at a valuation above $1 billion — unicorn status — and eleven months later a $250 million Series D tripled that to over $3 billion, making it the most valuable African startup and bringing its total raise to $475 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/02/16/african-fintech-flutterwave-triples-valuation-to-over-3b-after-250m-series-d/)). At the peak it claimed 200 million transactions worth over $16 billion, 900,000 businesses on the platform, 150 currencies, and infrastructure across 34 African countries ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/02/16/african-fintech-flutterwave-triples-valuation-to-over-3b-after-250m-series-d/)). But the same scale made it a target: Kenya's anti-money-laundering agency froze $52.5 million in its accounts, Nigerian fraudsters repeatedly breached its systems, and its own founding story came under public attack ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/flutterwaves-accounts-frozen-kenya-over-money-laundering-claims-2022-07-07/); [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2024/05/16/exclusive-flutterwave-loses-%e2%82%a611-billion-in-security-breach/)).

## The fundraising machine

Flutterwave's rise was a venture-capital story as much as a payments story. The $170 million Series C in March 2021, from Tiger Global and Avenir, came when the company had processed 140 million transactions worth over $9 billion for 290,000 businesses ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/02/16/african-fintech-flutterwave-triples-valuation-to-over-3b-after-250m-series-d/)). The February 2022 Series D, led by B Capital with Alta Park, Whale Rock and Lux Capital participating and existing backers Avenir, Tiger Global, Glynn Capital, Green Visor and [Salesforce](/organizations/salesforce/) Ventures doubling down, valued it above $3 billion — ahead of SoftBank-backed OPay's $2 billion and FTX-backed Chipper Cash ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/02/16/african-fintech-flutterwave-triples-valuation-to-over-3b-after-250m-series-d/)). The capital funded an aggressive product expansion: the Barter consumer app, Flutterwave Store for small-business e-commerce (launched April 2020), the Send remittance app (December 2021), and the acquisition of creator platform Disha — its first public deal, for an undisclosed six-figure sum ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/02/16/african-fintech-flutterwave-triples-valuation-to-over-3b-after-250m-series-d/)). Its enterprise client list — Booking.com, Flywire, Uber — and partnerships with PayPal, [Visa](/organizations/visa/), Discover and Worldpay FIS were meant to signal it was infrastructure, not just an app ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/02/16/african-fintech-flutterwave-triples-valuation-to-over-3b-after-250m-series-d/)).

The founding itself was messier than the fundraising decks suggested. TechCabal obtained Flutterwave's US certificate of incorporation, prepared by Aboyeji on 2 May 2016, and established the three co-founders — Agboola, Adekoya and Aboyeji, who had met at Andela ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2022/04/16/flutterwave-iyin-aboyeji-startup-ceo-accusation/)). Aboyeji served as first CEO until resigning on 12 October 2018 after clashing with Agboola — "there can't be two captains of this ship" — with Agboola, then chief product officer, taking over ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2022/04/16/flutterwave-iyin-aboyeji-startup-ceo-accusation/)). In 2022 Aboyeji publicly rebutted allegations that Agboola had created a fictitious "Greg" co-founder to hold extra shares and had used "Greg" as an alias when dealing with banks as an Access Bank employee; he also disclosed that the US SEC had investigated whether Flutterwave's early investors were properly accredited, an inquiry closed by an SEC letter dated 28 September 2017 ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2022/04/16/flutterwave-iyin-aboyeji-startup-ceo-accusation/)). Aboyeji said he fully exited the company at the $1 billion round ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2022/04/16/flutterwave-iyin-aboyeji-startup-ceo-accusation/)).

## Trial by regulator: Kenya

The stress test began in July 2022, when Kenya's Assets Recovery Agency obtained a High Court order freezing $52.5 million across 62 accounts linked to Flutterwave and six recipient companies, alleging the fintech operated without a Central Bank of Kenya licence and that its accounts were conduits for money laundering, with some transactions raising card-fraud suspicions ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/flutterwaves-accounts-frozen-kenya-over-money-laundering-claims-2022-07-07/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/07/07/flutterwave-denies-money-laundering-and-fraud-allegations-following-accounts-freeze-by-kenyan-agency/)). Flutterwave called the claims "entirely false," said its anti-money-laundering practices were regularly audited by a Big Four firm, and alleged a witch hunt ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/07/07/flutterwave-denies-money-laundering-and-fraud-allegations-following-accounts-freeze-by-kenyan-agency/)). The saga dragged for sixteen months: another $3.3 million was frozen in September 2022; the first case was withdrawn in February 2023 and the $52.5 million released; in June 2023, 45 more accounts were frozen in connection with the collapsed 86FB/86Z Ponzi scheme; in July 2023 a judge blocked the agency from withdrawing its second case mid-argument; and finally, in November 2023, the court allowed the withdrawal — ruling that further investigation showed Flutterwave had engaged in no illegal activity, condemning the agency's "negligent, reckless, careless and rash" investigation, and making the ARA director and investigator personally liable for future civil claims rather than the Kenyan state ([Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/news/flutterwave-cleared-in-kenya/)). Along the way, the Central Bank of Kenya had separately warned that Flutterwave was operating without a remittance licence — though by September 2023 the company said it had received first-name approval from the CBK and announced plans to invest $50 million in Kenya once licensed ([Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/news/flutterwave-cleared-in-kenya/)).

## Breaches at home: Nigeria

While Kenya played out, Nigeria produced a different kind of crisis: repeated unauthorised transfers through Flutterwave's own systems. In February 2023, ₦2.9 billion was diverted to 107 bank accounts; in March 2023, ₦550 million reached 107 accounts across 27 banks; and in October 2023, about ₦19 billion ($24 million) was illegally transferred through unauthorised POS-merchant transactions to some 6,000 account holders across 35 banks, according to court documents seen by TechCabal ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2024/05/16/exclusive-flutterwave-loses-%e2%82%a611-billion-in-security-breach/)). Then in April 2024, perpetrators diverted a reported ₦11 billion (about $7 million; one insider put it at ₦20 billion) to several accounts over four days, keeping deposits below fraud-check thresholds ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2024/05/16/exclusive-flutterwave-loses-%e2%82%a611-billion-in-security-breach/)). Flutterwave confirmed the April incident but said "no customer funds were lost or compromised," reported the matter to law enforcement, and in February 2024 secured a Mareva injunction letting it recover funds and assets from identified account holders ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2024/05/16/exclusive-flutterwave-loses-%e2%82%a611-billion-in-security-breach/)). The incidents fed the same narrative the Kenya case had started: a company scaling faster than its controls could cover.

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not claim the Kenyan cases found any wrongdoing by Flutterwave — the courts withdrew the ARA's cases, and the judge explicitly rebuked the agency's investigation ([Techpoint](https://techpoint.africa/news/flutterwave-cleared-in-kenya/)).
- It does not endorse the 2022 founder-identity allegations; it reports Aboyeji's on-the-record account and Flutterwave's dismissal of the claims as recycled and false ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2022/04/16/flutterwave-iyin-aboyeji-startup-ceo-accusation/)).
- The Nigerian breach amounts come from financial-industry insiders cited by TechCabal and are disputed by the company; the profile attributes them rather than stating them as established fact ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2024/05/16/exclusive-flutterwave-loses-%e2%82%a611-billion-in-security-breach/)).
- It does not cover Flutterwave's post-2024 trajectory (Mono acquisition, IPO plans, reported Ripple/Series E moves) because those were not verified against fully-read, citable independent sources in this research pass.
