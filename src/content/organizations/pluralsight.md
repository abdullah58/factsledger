## Overview

Pluralsight is a technology workforce-development company — it sells enterprises online courses and analytics for building software-engineering skills through its Skills and Flow product lines ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-pluralsight-m-a-vistaequity/vista-equity-inks-3-5-billion-deal-to-acquire-pluralsight-idUSKBN28O05B/)). The Utah company went public in 2018, was taken private by Vista Equity Partners in 2021 in a deal worth roughly $3.5–3.8 billion, and then became one of the most visible casualties of the 2020–21 leveraged-buyout wave: by May 2024 Vista had written its entire equity investment to zero, and the company's lenders took ownership ([Reuters](https://wqxc.com/2024/07/02/exclusive-vista-equity-in-talks-to-hand-over-pluralsight-to-creditors-sources-say/), [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/private-equity/oaktree-ares-write-off-pluralsight-equity-18-months-after-deal)).

## The buyout investors fought over

On December 13, 2020, Vista Equity agreed to acquire Pluralsight for $3.5 billion in cash — $20.26 per share, a roughly 25% premium to the 30-day volume-weighted average price — on a bet that pandemic-supercharged demand for skilled software engineers would persist ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-pluralsight-m-a-vistaequity/vista-equity-inks-3-5-billion-deal-to-acquire-pluralsight-idUSKBN28O05B/)). But investor groups said the initial bid was not sufficient and complained about how Pluralsight had run its sale process, forcing Vista to raise its offer to $22.50 a share — an enterprise value of about $3.8 billion — before the deal could proceed in 2021 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/technology/pluralsight-shares-rise-7-after-vista-equity-sweetens-buyout-offer-idUSKBN2B01DQ/)). Vista then merged Pluralsight with cloud-learning company Cloud Guru, which it had acquired for $1.6 billion, and saddled the combined company with more than $1.5 billion of debt from private-credit "shadow banks" rather than traditional banks, via a "recurring revenue term loan" ([Reuters](https://wqxc.com/2024/07/02/exclusive-vista-equity-in-talks-to-hand-over-pluralsight-to-creditors-sources-say/)).

## Marked to zero in a single quarter

The unraveling was fast. A customer slowdown — enterprises laying off IT staff — plus rising interest rates squeezed a debt structure built for a low-rate, high-growth world ([Reuters](https://wqxc.com/2024/07/02/exclusive-vista-equity-in-talks-to-hand-over-pluralsight-to-creditors-sources-say/)). Vista injected $75 million in the first quarter of 2023 and got lenders to loosen covenants, but its own fund marks tell the story: Pluralsight's equity value slid from $2.3 billion in Q1 2023 to $1.5 billion in Q4 2023, and then to zero in the first quarter of 2024 — a $1.5 billion writedown in a single quarter ([Reuters](https://wqxc.com/2024/07/02/exclusive-vista-equity-in-talks-to-hand-over-pluralsight-to-creditors-sources-say/)). Along the way, Vista let Pluralsight borrow $162.5 million from its private equity fund and, in May 2024, moved some of Pluralsight's intellectual property into a new subsidiary — partly outside lenders' collateral — and borrowed against it to fund an interest payment to those same lenders ([Reuters](https://wqxc.com/2024/07/02/exclusive-vista-equity-in-talks-to-hand-over-pluralsight-to-creditors-sources-say/)). Co-founder Aaron Skonnard stepped down as CEO in April 2024, replaced by Chris Walters ([Reuters](https://wqxc.com/2024/07/02/exclusive-vista-equity-in-talks-to-hand-over-pluralsight-to-creditors-sources-say/)).

## The 2020 logic — and the 2023 warning signs

The buyout's logic was pandemic-era: edtech was booming, Pluralsight's acquisition was "one of the largest enterprise buys of the year," and investors saw a sector where companies could "move fluidly between privately held and publicly held companies" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/12/15/vistas-3-5b-purchase-of-pluralsight-signals-a-maturing-edtech-market/)). At $3.5 billion, Vista was paying a healthy bump over Pluralsight's then-market valuation of $2.97 billion — but the company's stock had been "stagnant in recent years" and never recovered its pre-pandemic highs after an August 2019 earnings report, which "may have painted a target on its back for the right acquirer" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/12/15/vistas-3-5b-purchase-of-pluralsight-signals-a-maturing-edtech-market/)).

The warning signs arrived in 2023. In the first quarter, Vista asked lenders to loosen covenants on Pluralsight's debt so the company could keep meeting its obligations, and injected $75 million of equity to ease lenders' concerns about the cash position ([Reuters](https://wqxc.com/2024/07/02/exclusive-vista-equity-in-talks-to-hand-over-pluralsight-to-creditors-sources-say/)). By the third quarter of 2023, Vista was reporting revenue declines to its own fund investors. In April 2024, its fourth-quarter update claimed Pluralsight had "shifted from unprofitable to a sustainably profitable business," pointing to a 28.5% profitability margin — while warning in the same breath of "liquidity challenges" from "capital structure constraints" ([Reuters](https://wqxc.com/2024/07/02/exclusive-vista-equity-in-talks-to-hand-over-pluralsight-to-creditors-sources-say/)). A month later, the equity was marked to zero.

## The lenders' turn — and their losses too

By July 2024, Reuters reported that Vista was in talks to cede control to its creditors — Blue Owl Capital, Ares Management, Goldman Sachs' asset management unit, Benefit Street, BlackRock, Oaktree and Golub Capital — through a debt-to-equity conversion, set to become "the first major debt restructuring of a company that borrowed from shadow banks" ([Reuters](https://wqxc.com/2024/07/02/exclusive-vista-equity-in-talks-to-hand-over-pluralsight-to-creditors-sources-say/)). The takeover did complete: in September 2026, [Bloomberg](/organizations/bloomberg/) Law reported that "nearly 18 months after lenders to Pluralsight took over the educational-software business," Oaktree and Ares had marked their own equity stakes to zero, attributing the unraveling to higher interest rates, increased competition and softening demand ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/private-equity/oaktree-ares-write-off-pluralsight-equity-18-months-after-deal)). The buyer's equity went to zero — and then the lenders' equity did too.
