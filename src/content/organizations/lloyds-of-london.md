## Overview

Lloyd's of London is an insurance and reinsurance market in London — not an insurance company. That distinction is the single most important fact about it: "Lloyd's is not an insurance company: policyholders are insured at Lloyd's, not by Lloyd's," as the market's own written evidence to a House of Lords inquiry states ([UK Parliament](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/58203/pdf/)). It is a Society of Members incorporated under the Lloyd's Acts 1871–1982, a corporate body created by Parliament that operates as a marketplace where competing financial backers, grouped into syndicates, pool and spread risk — with capital partially mutualised across the market through the Central Fund ([UK Parliament](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/58203/pdf/); [AM Best](https://news.ambest.com/PR/PressContent.aspx?altsrc=146&refnum=36398)).

## A market, not a company

All insurance business at Lloyd's is underwritten by its members, organised into syndicates and run by managing agents ([UK Parliament](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/58203/pdf/)). The Lloyd's Market Association counted 52 managing agents, 94 active syndicates (including special-purpose vehicles), and four members' agents acting for third-party capital in its 2024–25 submission to the Civil Justice Council ([Judiciary of England and Wales](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Arabella-Rammage-Lloyds-Market-Association.pdf)). The members are a mix of corporations and private individuals — the latter traditionally known as "Names." Individuals have underwritten at Lloyd's since 1688; companies were admitted from 1994, Scottish limited partnerships from 1997, and limited liability partnerships from 2007, and no new individual members have been admitted since 2003 ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2008/9780110834504/pdfs/ukdsiem_9780110834504_en.pdf)).

Most business is written on a subscription basis: risks are shared between more than one syndicate rather than taken in full by a single underwriter ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2008/9780110834504/pdfs/ukdsiem_9780110834504_en.pdf)). In 2023 the market earned £52.1 billion in gross premiums, with business reaching more than 200 countries ([Judiciary of England and Wales](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Arabella-Rammage-Lloyds-Market-Association.pdf)). For scale of growth: in 2007 gross written premiums were £16.3 billion with a £3.8 billion pre-tax profit ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2008/9780110834504/pdfs/ukdsiem_9780110834504_en.pdf)). The market specialises in complex, large, high-severity/low-frequency risks — non-marine property and casualty, marine, energy and aviation ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2008/9780110834504/pdfs/ukdsiem_9780110834504_en.pdf)).

## The statutory framework

The Society of Lloyd's is incorporated under Lloyd's Act 1871, but the current statutory framework for governance is set out in Lloyd's Act 1982 ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2008/9780110834504/pdfs/ukdsiem_9780110834504_en.pdf)). The 1982 Act provides for a governing Council with powers to regulate and direct the business of insurance at Lloyd's, including making byelaws and regulations with direct legal effect on those operating in the market ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2008/9780110834504/pdfs/ukdsiem_9780110834504_en.pdf)). The Council delegates most day-to-day oversight to the Franchise Board, created in 2003 ([Gresham College](https://www.gresham.ac.uk/watch-now/london-insurance-and-reinsurance-market)).

Since 1 December 2001 the Society has been authorised under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2008/9780110834504/pdfs/ukdsiem_9780110834504_en.pdf)). Managing agents are "dual regulated" firms under the Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) and the [Financial Conduct Authority](/organizations/financial-conduct-authority/) (FCA), while members' agents are regulated by the FCA alone ([Judiciary of England and Wales](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Arabella-Rammage-Lloyds-Market-Association.pdf)). Layered on top of that, the Corporation of Lloyd's operates its own framework of bye-laws and regulations — industry evidence describes it as "a statutory regulator" in its own right — and acts as a monitoring body and provider of services to the syndicates it governs ([Judiciary of England and Wales](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Arabella-Rammage-Lloyds-Market-Association.pdf); [Gresham College](https://www.gresham.ac.uk/watch-now/london-insurance-and-reinsurance-market)).

The financial backstop is the "chain of security": claims are met first from syndicate-level assets, then from members' Funds at Lloyd's, and finally from central assets including the Central Fund, which pools capital across the whole market ([ainvest.com](https://www.ainvest.com/news/average-lloyd-syndicate-top-tier-rating-2609/)). The chain is not marketing language — AM Best treats the Lloyd's market rating as the floor for every syndicate rating precisely because the Central Fund partially mutualises capital at the market level, so a weak syndicate cannot sink below the collective ([AM Best](https://news.ambest.com/PR/PressContent.aspx?altsrc=146&refnum=36398)).

## From coffee house to Lime Street

Edward Lloyd's Coffee House is first mentioned in 1688; his establishment became a meeting place for merchants and insurers, and Lloyd built an information system on shipping movements for them, starting the bulletin Lloyd's News in 1696, the precursor of Lloyd's List ([Gresham College](https://www.gresham.ac.uk/watch-now/london-insurance-and-reinsurance-market)). On 13 December 1771, seventy-nine underwriters subscribed £100 each and formed a committee — the institutional seed of the modern market ([Gresham College](https://www.gresham.ac.uk/watch-now/london-insurance-and-reinsurance-market)). Until 1994, all business was accepted by syndicates of individuals with unlimited personal liability ([Gresham College](https://www.gresham.ac.uk/watch-now/london-insurance-and-reinsurance-market)). The market's reputation in the United States was established by its response to the 1906 San Francisco earthquake, when Lloyd's underwriters paid claims while many American companies failed ([Gresham College](https://www.gresham.ac.uk/watch-now/london-insurance-and-reinsurance-market)).

One persistent confusion deserves a direct answer: Lloyd's of London, the insurance market, is a different entity from Lloyds Banking Group, one of the UK's Big 4 high-street banks ([ACT Wiki](https://wiki.treasurers.org/wiki/Lloyd%27s_of_London); [UK Parliament](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/58203/pdf/)). They share a name root and a city; they share nothing else.

Related: Lloyd's submitted the written evidence cited here to a [UK Parliament](/organizations/uk-parliament/) House of Lords inquiry into commercial insurance and reinsurance regulation.

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not give a single current syndicate count: dated sources cite 89 (Lloyd's own Lords evidence) and 94 including SPVs (LMA, 2024–25). The figure moves with the market.
- It does not quote current chain-of-security pound values; those figures were not verified from fully-read sources.
- It does not cover the 1980s–90s losses and litigation in detail (asbestos and the Names crisis), which would need dedicated sourcing.
- It does not assert anything about famous one-off policies (insured vocal cords, legs); those stories are widely repeated but were not verified here.
- The 1906 San Francisco earthquake account reflects the market's own telling via a practitioner-historian; independent claims-payment records were not examined.
