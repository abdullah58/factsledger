## Overview

Tech Nation is the [United Kingdom](/places/united-kingdom/)'s scale-up network for technology companies: a government-backed organisation that lost its core state funding in early 2023, announced it would close, was acquired by the Founders Forum Group within weeks, and relaunched later that year — and in 2026 it still runs its flagship Future Fifty programme and remains the Home Office's endorsing body for Global Talent visas in digital technology ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/global-talent-digital-technology); [BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/tech-nation-unveils-2026-future-fifty-cohort/)).

The organisation was established in November 2017, when Tech City UK and Tech North were consolidated into a single national network, primarily funded by the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport (DCMS) ([The National Archives](https://beta.nationalarchives.gov.uk/catalogue/id/C17204907/)). Its work was boosting UK startups through growth programmes, training in digital entrepreneurship skills, support with Tier 1 visas for overseas employees, and research on the state of the UK digital landscape ([The National Archives](https://beta.nationalarchives.gov.uk/catalogue/id/C17204907/)). Some coverage dates the organisation to 2011, tracing its lineage to Tech City UK's origins; the 2017 merger date is the one the official record gives, and the difference is flagged at the end of this profile ([CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/574445/tech-nation-to-close-as-uk-government-pulls-key-funding.html)).

By the time it closed, Tech Nation claimed to have supported more than 5,000 companies, processed over 6,000 Global Talent Visa applications and contributed £600 million of gross value added to the UK economy since 2014 — the organisation's own figures as reported at the time, not audited results ([CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/574445/tech-nation-to-close-as-uk-government-pulls-key-funding.html)). The funding model behind them was fragile: government grants accounted for approximately 75% of its income ([TechMonitor](https://www.techmonitor.ai/digital-economy/tech-nation-shuts-down-leaves-question-mark-over-administration-of-visa-scheme)).

## From Tech City UK to a national network

Tech Nation was formed from the merger of Tech City UK and Tech North, and it built a network of conferences, sandboxes, panels, research, analysis and a digital academy to help entrepreneurs move their companies beyond small start-ups into viable businesses ([TechMonitor](https://www.techmonitor.ai/digital-economy/tech-nation-shuts-down-leaves-question-mark-over-administration-of-visa-scheme)). Its focus was deliberately on scale-ups rather than brand-new startups, on the argument that scale-ups create 90% of UK tech jobs ([TechMonitor](https://www.techmonitor.ai/digital-economy/tech-nation-shuts-down-leaves-question-mark-over-administration-of-visa-scheme)).

The record it built up was striking. Tech Nation said that while 80% of start-ups fail within their first two to five years, more than 95% of the start-ups on its accelerator programmes went on to scale, and that more than a third of all tech unicorns and decacorns created in the UK had graduated from a Tech Nation programme, collectively raising over £28 billion in venture capital and capital markets ([TechMonitor](https://www.techmonitor.ai/digital-economy/tech-nation-shuts-down-leaves-question-mark-over-administration-of-visa-scheme)). Its alumni included Monzo, [Revolut](/organizations/revolut/), Depop, Just Eat, Ocado, Skyscanner and Deliveroo ([TechMonitor](https://www.techmonitor.ai/digital-economy/tech-nation-shuts-down-leaves-question-mark-over-administration-of-visa-scheme)). Founding chief executive Gerard Grech put it more simply: "Many of Britain's most successful tech companies, from Monzo to Deliveroo, and from Skyscanner to Darktrace, have passed through one or more of Tech Nation's growth programs" ([CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/574445/tech-nation-to-close-as-uk-government-pulls-key-funding.html)). The chair, Stephen Kelly, called Tech Nation "the masterclass and finishing school for UK scale-up founders" ([TechMonitor](https://www.techmonitor.ai/digital-economy/tech-nation-shuts-down-leaves-question-mark-over-administration-of-visa-scheme)).

Its best-known programme, Future Fifty, was established in 2013 to champion and support British tech businesses as they scale up nationally and internationally ([FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/new-wave-of-uk-banking-pioneers-takes-centre-stage-in-tech-nations-future-fifty-programme-for-2019/)). It took about 25 late-stage companies a year onto a roughly two-year programme, looking for firms with at least £5 million in revenues or a Series B round raised, and 50% year-on-year growth ([Beauhurst](https://www.beauhurst.com/blog/future-fifty-empowering-future-unicorns/)). Support came through C-suite peer roundtables, masterclasses led by global experts, ministerial roundtables, and a dedicated visa team ([Beauhurst](https://www.beauhurst.com/blog/future-fifty-empowering-future-unicorns/)).

## The 2023 closure — and the rescue that followed

The break came on 20 January 2023, when the government announced that the £12.09 million Digital Growth Grant would go to Barclays Eagle Labs instead of Tech Nation, following an open competition and rigorous assessment ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/barclays-eagle-labs-chosen-to-help-turbocharge-next-generation-of-uk-tech-stars)). The two-year grant was to run from April 2023, funding activity until March 2025, with combined government and Eagle Labs investment intended to benefit more than 22,000 businesses, at least 80% of them based outside [London](/places/london/) ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/barclays-eagle-labs-chosen-to-help-turbocharge-next-generation-of-uk-tech-stars)). The same announcement stressed that Barclays Eagle Labs would not take over any administration of visa schemes ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/barclays-eagle-labs-chosen-to-help-turbocharge-next-generation-of-uk-tech-stars)).

Eleven days later, on 31 January 2023, Tech Nation announced it would close its doors from 31 March 2023, when its DCMS contract concluded ([CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/574445/tech-nation-to-close-as-uk-government-pulls-key-funding.html)). "With this foundation removed, Tech Nation's remaining activities are not viable on a standalone basis," it said, adding that it had "exhaustively explored whether Tech Nation could continue without core government grant funding, but has concluded after extensive consultation that this is not an option" ([TechMonitor](https://www.techmonitor.ai/digital-economy/tech-nation-shuts-down-leaves-question-mark-over-administration-of-visa-scheme)). It began a redundancy consultation for all permanent employees and invited expressions of interest from organisations that might take its portfolio of assets forward ([CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/574445/tech-nation-to-close-as-uk-government-pulls-key-funding.html)). More than 400 entrepreneurs signed an open letter calling on the government to rethink the decision ([CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/574445/tech-nation-to-close-as-uk-government-pulls-key-funding.html)). Tech Nation ceased operations on 31 March 2023, with Barclays Eagle Labs taking over the DCMS-contracted work from 1 April ([The National Archives](https://beta.nationalarchives.gov.uk/catalogue/id/C17204907/); [TechMonitor](https://www.techmonitor.ai/digital-economy/tech-nation-shuts-down-leaves-question-mark-over-administration-of-visa-scheme)).

The closure lasted less than a month as a final outcome. In April 2023, Tech Nation was acquired by Founders Forum Group, a global community of businesses supporting entrepreneurs ([BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/tech-nation-acquired-by-founders-forum/)). The group planned to revive several Tech Nation programmes and tech-sector reports, and to manage its Global Talent visa scheme ([BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/tech-nation-acquired-by-founders-forum/)). Founding CEO Gerard Grech and board director Sarah Wood joined the Founders Forum Group's Tech Nation Advisory Board, though some of Tech Nation's almost 50 staff had already moved on ([BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/tech-nation-acquired-by-founders-forum/)). Brent Hoberman, the group's co-founder and chair, said Tech Nation "has positioned the UK as a world-class innovation hub" ([BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/tech-nation-acquired-by-founders-forum/)).

The relaunch came in October 2023 at Birmingham Tech Week, with Founders Forum Group chief executive Carolyn Dawson saying the relaunched organisation was "here to collaborate, not compete" with Barclays Eagle Labs ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2023/09/26/relaunched-tech-nation-to-collaborate-not-compete-with-barclays-says-boss/)). The new model was different in kind: starved of its £12 million government grant, Tech Nation would be funded through commercial support and partnerships, with a team of eight against the roughly 100 people it had employed while receiving the Digital Growth Grant ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2023/09/26/relaunched-tech-nation-to-collaborate-not-compete-with-barclays-says-boss/)). Grech stayed on in an advisory role ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2023/09/26/relaunched-tech-nation-to-collaborate-not-compete-with-barclays-says-boss/)).

## Future Fifty, the other programmes, and the visa

The clearest proof that the rescue worked is Future Fifty, which Founders Forum Group relaunched in March 2024 and which has since fast-tracked 75 ambitious companies ([BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/tech-nation-unveils-future-fifty-2026-cohort-at-downing-st/)). In its current form it is an invite-only, late-stage growth programme run by Tech Nation and powered by Founders Forum, inducting 25 companies twice a year, in spring and autumn ([Tech Nation](https://technation.io/programmes/future-fifty/)). It looks for companies that have raised Series B funding, generate £5 million in revenue a year or are growing 50% year-on-year, are headquartered in the UK, and are digital or tech-enabled businesses ([Tech Nation](https://technation.io/programmes/future-fifty/)). Members get a C-suite supper club, partially subsidised missions to Dubai, Singapore and New York, and an annual Future Fifty Forum ([Tech Nation](https://technation.io/programmes/future-fifty/)).

The programme's alumni record is the reason it carries weight: it has supported over 30% of the UK's unicorns and 40% of its decacorns, with alumni collectively raising more than £77 billion ([Tech Nation](https://technation.io/programmes/future-fifty/)). Nearly a third of the nation's unicorns — including decacorns Revolut and Wise — are among its alumni, and since its inception it has fast-tracked more than 300 companies, including over 50 unicorns ([BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/tech-nation-unveils-2026-future-fifty-cohort/)). The 2026 cohorts show it still operating at scale: the March cohort of 25, unveiled at 11 Downing Street by Chancellor Rachel Reeves, had raised more than £1.3 billion and employed more than 2,900 staff, with 96% expanding internationally and 32% based outside London ([BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/tech-nation-unveils-future-fifty-2026-cohort-at-downing-st/)). The September cohort of 25, including Healf, Prolific and LemFi, had raised a combined £1.47 billion and employed nearly 4,000 people ([BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/tech-nation-unveils-2026-future-fifty-cohort/)).

[Behavox](/organizations/behavox/) joined Future Fifty in 2019 in the Data Analytics category, one of 24 late-stage companies selected that year alongside Monzo Bank, Revolut, Starling Bank, Currencycloud and Quantexa ([FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/new-wave-of-uk-banking-pioneers-takes-centre-stage-in-tech-nations-future-fifty-programme-for-2019/)). The 2019 cohort was notably fintech-heavy — 10 of the 24 companies — and five of the companies were headquartered in regional cities: Cambridge, Bristol, Glasgow, Nottingham and Watford ([FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/new-wave-of-uk-banking-pioneers-takes-centre-stage-in-tech-nations-future-fifty-programme-for-2019/)). Between 2013 and 2018, 127 businesses took part in Future Fifty, raising a combined $8 billion; nine alumni reached IPOs and there were 29 merger or trade-sale deals ([FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/new-wave-of-uk-banking-pioneers-takes-centre-stage-in-tech-nations-future-fifty-programme-for-2019/)).

The other programmes survived the change of ownership too: Climate, Libra, Rising Stars and the research work were all maintained at the relaunch, and Rising Stars now runs regional heats with a final at London Tech Week ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2023/09/26/relaunched-tech-nation-to-collaborate-not-compete-with-barclays-says-boss/)). [HSBC](/organizations/hsbc/) Innovation Banking is the Future Fifty programme's founding partner ([BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/tech-nation-unveils-2026-future-fifty-cohort/)).

The visa role is the part of Tech Nation that never actually stopped. Tech Nation was the vetting body for the digital-technology strand of the Global Talent visa, the route that lets recognised tech leaders and potential leaders live and work in the UK ([TechMonitor](https://www.techmonitor.ai/digital-economy/tech-nation-shuts-down-leaves-question-mark-over-administration-of-visa-scheme)). The live GOV.UK guidance still says the Home Office sends digital-technology endorsement applications to Tech Nation for review, with a decision usually within five to eight weeks ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/global-talent-digital-technology)). No job offer is needed, there is no language or minimum-salary requirement, and settlement is possible after three years for leaders or five years for potential leaders ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/global-talent-digital-technology)). The government-funded organisation ended, but the brand, the programme and the visa endorsement all carried on under new ownership.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim a single settled founding date: CSO Online dates Tech Nation to 2011, while The National Archives dates the organisation to the November 2017 merger of Tech City UK and Tech North — the profile states both and treats 2011 as the lineage and 2017 as the merger ([CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/574445/tech-nation-to-close-as-uk-government-pulls-key-funding.html); [The National Archives](https://beta.nationalarchives.gov.uk/catalogue/id/C17204907/)). It does not present Tech Nation's impact figures — 5,000+ companies, 6,000+ visa applications, £600 million of gross value added, the 95% scale rate — as audited results, because they are the organisation's own claims as reported by trade press ([CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/574445/tech-nation-to-close-as-uk-government-pulls-key-funding.html); [TechMonitor](https://www.techmonitor.ai/digital-economy/tech-nation-shuts-down-leaves-question-mark-over-administration-of-visa-scheme)). It does not assert the reported May 2025 renewal of the Global Talent endorsing-body contract or the August 2025 single-form change, because those details rest only on specialist immigration blogs that were not read in full as cited sources; the claim made here is only what live GOV.UK guidance confirms — that Tech Nation remains the endorsing body ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/global-talent-digital-technology)). It does not state a headquarters city, because no source read in full gives one. It does not treat the 2023 closure as the end of Tech Nation, because the brand was acquired within weeks and relaunched the same year ([BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/tech-nation-acquired-by-founders-forum/); [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2023/09/26/relaunched-tech-nation-to-collaborate-not-compete-with-barclays-says-boss/)). And it does not repeat the September 2023 expectation that Tech Nation would run the visa only until 2024 as if it were current, because the live guidance read in 2026 still names Tech Nation as the endorsing body ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2023/09/26/relaunched-tech-nation-to-collaborate-not-compete-with-barclays-says-boss/); [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/global-talent-digital-technology)).
