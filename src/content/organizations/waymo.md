## Overview

Waymo LLC is [Alphabet](/organizations/alphabet/)'s self-driving car company and the operator of Waymo One, the largest commercial robotaxi service in the United States ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/13/ubers-robotaxi-lobbying-effort-has-put-it-on-a-collision-course-with-waymo/)). It began in 2009 as [Google](/organizations/google/)'s self-driving car project, became the independent company Waymo under Alphabet in December 2016, and now runs driverless, paid rides in 14 U.S. cities, delivering more than 500,000 rides a week as of April 2026 ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/10/11/alphabet-is-the-robotaxi-stock-to-buy-as-waymo-chases-1-million-rides-a-week/)).

The company's core product is the Waymo Driver, a Level 4 autonomous driving system — meaning the software does all the driving with no human supervision, but only inside defined service areas ([Insurance Institute for Highway Safety](https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/waymos-driverless-cars-crash-less-often-than-people)). Waymo is led by co-CEOs Tekedra Mawakana and Dmitri Dolgov ([Tech Startups](https://techstartups.com/2026/02/03/alphabets-waymo-raises-16b-at-126b-valuation-as-robotaxi-expansion-accelerates/)), and Alphabet remains its majority investor ([Waymo](https://waymo.com/blog/2026/02/waymo-raises-usd16-billion-investment-round/)). Its rise is the clearest case yet of a research project becoming a fare-paying transport business — and the clearest test of whether driverless cars are actually safer than human drivers.

## From a Google research project to a commercial service

Waymo's history starts inside Google. The self-driving car project began in 2009, and by late 2015 it had completed the world's first fully driverless ride on public roads, with no test driver behind the wheel; the rider was Steven Mahan, who is legally blind, in Austin, Texas ([MotorTrend](https://Www.motortrend.com/news/googles-self-driving-car-project-spins-off-waymo)). By then the project had logged more than 2 million autonomous miles ([MotorTrend](https://Www.motortrend.com/news/googles-self-driving-car-project-spins-off-waymo)). On 13 December 2016 the project spun out of Google X into Waymo, an independent company owned by Alphabet; the name stands for "a new way forward in mobility," and John Krafcik, who had led the project, continued as its CEO ([MotorTrend](https://Www.motortrend.com/news/googles-self-driving-car-project-spins-off-waymo)).

Commercialisation followed two years later. Waymo One launched on 5 December 2018 in four Phoenix suburbs — Chandler, Tempe, Mesa and Gilbert — as the world's first paid autonomous taxi service, initially for several hundred members of its Early Rider test group ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/18/12/05/waymo-one-launches-as-worlds-first-commercial-autonomous-taxi-service)). Those first paid rides still carried backup drivers, and the fleet was modified Chrysler Pacifica minivans ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/18/12/05/waymo-one-launches-as-worlds-first-commercial-autonomous-taxi-service)). Fully driverless service open to the public in Phoenix came in 2020, followed by [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) in 2022, [Los Angeles](/places/los-angeles/) in 2023 and limited operation in Austin in 2024 ([Insurance Institute for Highway Safety](https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/waymos-driverless-cars-crash-less-often-than-people)). In 2022 Waymo also became the first autonomous ride-hailing service to offer paid, fully autonomous, 24/7 rides to and from an airport, at Phoenix Sky Harbor ([Waymo](https://waymo.com/about/)), and in May 2023 its Phoenix service area reached 180 square miles including Scottsdale ([Waymo](https://waymo.com/about/)). The fleet has since gone all-electric, built around the Jaguar I-PACE after the Pacifica hybrids were phased out ([Waymo](https://waymo.com/about/)).

## Scale, cities and the [Uber](/organizations/uber/) partnership

Waymo's growth since 2024 has been steep. It passed 250,000 paid rides a week in April 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/24/uber-and-waymos-commercial-robotaxi-service-is-open-for-business-in-atlanta/)), delivered 15 million rides in 2025 and crossed 20 million lifetime rides in December of that year ([Waymo](https://waymo.com/blog/2026/02/waymo-raises-usd16-billion-investment-round/); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91497064/automotive-most-innovative-companies-2026)). In late April 2026, Alphabet CEO [Sundar Pichai](/people/sundar-pichai/) said Waymo had topped 500,000 fully autonomous rides a week, "doubling in less than a year," and the company was targeting 1 million a week by the end of 2026 ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/10/11/alphabet-is-the-robotaxi-stock-to-buy-as-waymo-chases-1-million-rides-a-week/)). Commercial operations reached 14 U.S. cities on 1 September 2026 with launches in Denver, San Diego and Tampa, and a fleet of more than 4,000 vehicles ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/01/waymo-accelerates-robotaxi-expansion-with-launches-in-denver-san-diego-and-tampa/)); Las Vegas followed on 14 September ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/10/11/alphabet-is-the-robotaxi-stock-to-buy-as-waymo-chases-1-million-rides-a-week/)). London and Munich are planned as its first markets outside the U.S. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/01/waymo-accelerates-robotaxi-expansion-with-launches-in-denver-san-diego-and-tampa/)).

Uber has been both partner and rival. The two companies first clashed in court: in 2017 Waymo sued Uber alleging trade-secret theft, and the trial lasted five days before Uber agreed to settle ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/13/ubers-robotaxi-lobbying-effort-has-put-it-on-a-collision-course-with-waymo/)). They later teamed up, putting Waymo vehicles on Uber's app in Phoenix in 2023 — an arrangement that quietly ended in May 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/13/ubers-robotaxi-lobbying-effort-has-put-it-on-a-collision-course-with-waymo/)) — and then in Austin in March 2025 and Atlanta in June 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/24/uber-and-waymos-commercial-robotaxi-service-is-open-for-business-in-atlanta/)). In Atlanta the service initially covered about 65 square miles with a fleet in the dozens, and Uber handles charging, maintenance and cleaning while Waymo monitors the technology and operations; rides cost the same as an UberX ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/24/uber-and-waymos-commercial-robotaxi-service-is-open-for-business-in-atlanta/)). But in Austin and Atlanta riders cannot hail a Waymo directly — only through Uber, with no guarantee of a robotaxi match — and on 4 November 2026 co-CEO Tekedra Mawakana told Bloomberg that Waymo will end that exclusivity in January 2028 and offer rides through its own app, calling the Uber experience "a little bit of a crapshoot" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/waymo-confirms-its-shaking-up-its-uber-relationship-in-january-2028/)). The underlying contract was set to expire in May 2028, and Uber said it still plans to host Waymo vehicles "for as long as the two companies agree" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/waymo-confirms-its-shaking-up-its-uber-relationship-in-january-2028/)).

The expansion is heavily funded. Waymo closed a $5.6 billion investment round led by Alphabet in October 2024, at a reported $45 billion valuation ([Waymo](https://waymo.com/blog/2024/10/investing-to-bring-the-waymo-driver-to-more-riders/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/11/waymo-expands-its-robotaxi-service-across-silicon-valley/)), then raised $16 billion in February 2026 at a $126 billion post-money valuation, led by Dragoneer, DST Global and Sequoia, with Alphabet as majority investor ([Waymo](https://waymo.com/blog/2026/02/waymo-raises-usd16-billion-investment-round/)). Alphabet does not disclose Waymo's standalone revenue; the unit sits in its Other Bets segment, which posted a $1.8 billion operating loss in the second quarter of 2026 ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/10/11/alphabet-is-the-robotaxi-stock-to-buy-as-waymo-chases-1-million-rides-a-week/)). On vehicles, Waymo signed a multi-year partnership with Hyundai in October 2024 to put its sixth-generation Waymo Driver into the all-electric IONIQ 5, built at Hyundai's Georgia plant, with on-road testing from late 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/04/waymos-next-robotaxi-will-be-the-hyundai-ioniq-5/)); its newest vehicle, the Zeekr-made Ojai minivan, is the only autonomous vehicle serving riders in Denver and San Diego ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/01/waymo-accelerates-robotaxi-expansion-with-launches-in-denver-san-diego-and-tampa/)).

## The safety record — and the scrutiny

Safety is Waymo's central claim, and it now has independent evidence behind it. A study published by the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety on 23 July 2026, covering about 50 million driverless miles in 2021–2024, found Waymo vehicles involved in 68% fewer police-reportable crashes per mile than human drivers in the same cities, along with 81% fewer injury crashes and 85% fewer single-vehicle crashes ([Insurance Institute for Highway Safety](https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/waymos-driverless-cars-crash-less-often-than-people)). The advantage held in three of the four cities studied — 76% lower in Phoenix, 71% lower in Los Angeles and 35% lower in San Francisco — but not in Austin, where Waymo's rate was 4% higher on a small sample ([Insurance Institute for Highway Safety](https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/waymos-driverless-cars-crash-less-often-than-people)). The institute also warned that the federal crash-reporting system "isn't good enough to allow continuous monitoring of a large-scale expansion" ([Insurance Institute for Highway Safety](https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/waymos-driverless-cars-crash-less-often-than-people)). Waymo's own figure is stronger but self-reported: across 127 million fully autonomous miles, it claims a 90% reduction in serious-injury crashes ([Waymo](https://waymo.com/blog/2026/02/waymo-raises-usd16-billion-investment-round/)). The two numbers measure different things over different periods and should not be compared directly.

The record is not unblemished. On 9 November 2026 the U.S. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration opened a recall query into 2,895 Waymo vehicles over continued school-bus safety violations, reviewing whether a software update issued under a December 2025 recall adequately fixed a defect that could cause vehicles to pass school buses showing flashing red lights or extended stop arms after initially slowing or stopping ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/us-opens-probe-into-2895-waymo-vehicles-over-school-bus-safety-violations-2026-11-09/)). Most of the incidents occurred after November 2025, when Waymo said the remedy was complete ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/us-opens-probe-into-2895-waymo-vehicles-over-school-bus-safety-violations-2026-11-09/)). Separately, NHTSA opened a probe in January 2026 after one of Waymo's self-driving vehicles struck a child near an elementary school in Santa Monica, California ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/us-opens-probe-into-2895-waymo-vehicles-over-school-bus-safety-violations-2026-11-09/)).

## Waymo against Baidu's [Apollo Go](/organizations/apollo-go/)

The robotaxi race now has two clear leaders, and the rankings disagree about which is ahead. Fast Company's "most innovative companies for automotive in 2026" list ranks Waymo first, "for scaling AVs across the United States," and [Baidu](/organizations/baidu/) second, "for scaling its driverless robotaxi technology, Apollo Go, across [China](/places/china/) and beyond," framing the contest as one "especially between the robotaxi units of two global search engine juggernauts: Alphabet's Waymo and Baidu's Apollo Go" ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91497064/automotive-most-innovative-companies-2026)). By Fast Company's account Apollo Go runs more than 1,000 driverless vehicles, and Baidu said that as of February 2026 it had logged more than 300 million autonomous kilometres and provided more than 20 million rides ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91497064/automotive-most-innovative-companies-2026)).

A different yardstick gives the opposite result. AUTNMY AI's Road to Autonomy Index, released 21 June 2026, ranked Apollo Go first with a composite score of 81.7 and Waymo second at 77.6, weighing operational capability, fleet scale, commercial partnerships and mass-production readiness ([EqualOcean](https://equalocean.com/news/2026062521966-baidus-apollo-go-overtakes-waymo-as-chinese-robotaxi-operators-claim-three-top)). Apollo Go had expanded into 27 cities globally and accumulated more than 330 million kilometres of autonomous driving mileage as of May 2026, and in June 2026 it secured a Level 4 operating permit in Switzerland — the first such permit for a robotaxi operator on European soil ([EqualOcean](https://equalocean.com/news/2026062521966-baidus-apollo-go-overtakes-waymo-as-chinese-robotaxi-operators-claim-three-top)). Yet by deployed fleet size Waymo remained the world's largest robotaxi operator, with more than 3,500 active vehicles as of mid-2026 ([EqualOcean](https://equalocean.com/news/2026062521966-baidus-apollo-go-overtakes-waymo-as-chinese-robotaxi-operators-claim-three-top)). The two rankings measure different things — U.S. commercial scale against global deployment breadth — and neither settles the contest.

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not state Waymo's standalone revenue, profitability or employee headcount; Alphabet does not disclose Waymo's results separately.
- It does not claim Waymo is safer than human drivers everywhere it operates; the independent IIHS evidence covers four cities and 2021–2024, and Austin was an exception.
- It does not treat Waymo's own safety figures as independent evidence; the 90% serious-injury reduction is the company's own analysis over a different mileage base from the IIHS study.
- It does not state that the January 2028 launch of Waymo's own app in Austin and Atlanta will happen on schedule; it has been announced, not completed.
- It does not describe the status of Waymo Via, the freight programme; that was not verified from fully-read sources.

- It does not resolve the conflicting "world's largest robotaxi operator" claims; Waymo leads on deployed fleet size while Apollo Go leads on the AUTNMY composite index, and Fast Company itself gives two different city counts for Apollo Go (22 and 26).
