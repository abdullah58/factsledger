## Overview

Barnana is a [California](/places/california/) snack company that turns "imperfect" organic bananas and plantains — fruit rejected for export because of scuffs, size, shape or over-ripeness — into snacks sold across the [United States](/places/united-states/) ([B Lab](https://www.bcorporation.net/en-us/find-a-b-corp/company/barnana/); [Food Dive](https://www.fooddive.com/news/investors-go-bananas-for-barnana-with-53m-in-funding/434276/)). Founded in Santa Monica in 2012 by former professional triathlete Caue Suplicy with Matt Clifford and Nik Ingersoll, it grew from a single banana bite into a farm-to-shelf business, and it has been a Certified B Corporation since March 2016 ([Global AgInvesting](https://globalaginvesting.com/trently-advisors-leads-5-3m-round-barnana/); [B Lab](https://www.bcorporation.net/en-us/find-a-b-corp/company/barnana/)).

Barnana is a snack brand, not a banana grower or exporter. The idea came from Suplicy's childhood in [Brazil](/places/brazil/), where his father dehydrated bananas at home as a candy substitute and Suplicy later carried them as race fuel when energy bars and gels did not yet exist ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/turning-unwanted-bananas-into-energy-snacks-with-upcycling/)). After moving to the United States in 2001, he watched Brazilian coconut water and acai become premium American products — and decided bananas, a fruit Americans already knew, could follow ([USC Marshall](https://www.marshall.usc.edu/news/case-green-entrepreneurship); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/turning-unwanted-bananas-into-energy-snacks-with-upcycling/)).

## From a family recipe to a national brand

Suplicy, Clifford and Ingersoll began working on the concept in 2010, which explains why some secondary write-ups date the company to that year; the company itself launched at Natural Products Expo West in 2012, where the product was an instant hit and drew orders from scores of retailers ([FoodNavigator-USA](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2017/08/16/Barnana-rolls-out-to-8-000-Starbucks-stores/)). It took two years to line up organic growers, find a US co-manufacturer, and design the branding — by launch they had burned through almost all of their cash ([FoodNavigator-USA](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2017/08/16/Barnana-rolls-out-to-8-000-Starbucks-stores/)).

Growth was fast. Between 2013 and 2016 Barnana grew at a 139% compound annual growth rate, and SPINS ranked it the fastest-growing snack in its category ([Global AgInvesting](https://globalaginvesting.com/trently-advisors-leads-5-3m-round-barnana/)). By January 2017 its snacks were in around 5,000 stores, including Whole Foods Market, Safeway, Sprouts Farmers Market and Wegmans ([Just-Food](https://www.just-food.com/news/barnana-secures-investment-from-vc-firm-boulder/); [FoodNavigator-USA](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2017/08/16/Barnana-rolls-out-to-8-000-Starbucks-stores/)). The mainstream breakthrough came in August 2017, when a test in several Starbucks stores passed the chain's velocity thresholds and Barnana rolled out nationally to 8,000 stores, taking distribution from about 5,000 to 13,000 stores overnight, with bespoke gold-foil packaging by Ingersoll ([FoodNavigator-USA](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2017/08/16/Barnana-rolls-out-to-8-000-Starbucks-stores/)). By 2025 the products were stocked at Whole Foods, Sprouts and Costco, with growing international, private-label and e-commerce channels ([Steward](https://gosteward.com/projects/barnana/a-regenerative-supply-chain-rooted-in-the-rainforest)).

The story is also teaching material: USC Marshall lecturer Jeremy Dann built the case study *Barnana: Adventures in Upcycling* around it for his Green Entrepreneurship course, and the case won the Outstanding Case Writer Competition in the UN Sustainable Development Goals category at the Case Centre Awards in 2021 ([USC Marshall](https://www.marshall.usc.edu/news/case-green-entrepreneurship)).

## The upcycling model

Barnana's business rests on a simple arbitrage: fruit export buyers reject is cheap, and the farmers who grew it still need paying. Farmers lose as much as 20% of their bananas because the fruit is not the right size, shape or ripeness for export, a figure the company itself reports ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/turning-unwanted-bananas-into-energy-snacks-with-upcycling/)); a 2025 lending review independently puts the non-exportable share of the crop at up to 20% ([Steward](https://gosteward.com/projects/barnana/a-regenerative-supply-chain-rooted-in-the-rainforest)). Barnana buys that fruit from organic and regenerative family farms in Indigenous communities in Latin America — principally Ecuador, with Peru and [Mexico](/places/mexico/) also named as sourcing countries — and turns it into snacks ([Global AgInvesting](https://globalaginvesting.com/trently-advisors-leads-5-3m-round-barnana/); [B Lab](https://www.bcorporation.net/en-us/find-a-b-corp/company/barnana/)).

B Lab's registry lists Barnana as a Certified B Corporation since March 2016, headquartered in California and operating in Ecuador and the United States, with an overall B Impact Score of 92.6 against a 50.9 median ([B Lab](https://www.bcorporation.net/en-us/find-a-b-corp/company/barnana/)). The company is also a Delaware Public Benefit Corporation, and Suplicy describes a "triple bottom line" of people, environment and profit ([Steward](https://gosteward.com/projects/barnana/a-regenerative-supply-chain-rooted-in-the-rainforest); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/turning-unwanted-bananas-into-energy-snacks-with-upcycling/)). USC Marshall's account says the company "became a certified Benefit Corporation in 2019," but the B Lab registry — the authoritative record — says March 2016, so this profile uses 2016 ([USC Marshall](https://www.marshall.usc.edu/news/case-green-entrepreneurship); [B Lab](https://www.bcorporation.net/en-us/find-a-b-corp/company/barnana/)).

The original Banana Bite contains a single ingredient — organic bananas, gently heated to remove most of the water — and a 40-gram serving carries 110 calories, 2 grams of protein, 800 milligrams of potassium and 4 grams of fiber, with no added sugar, preservatives, colours or flavours ([FoodNavigator-USA](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2017/08/16/Barnana-rolls-out-to-8-000-Starbucks-stores/)). The range is built around occasions: a chewy bite for mornings or exercise, plantain chips for savoury snacking, brittle for crunch ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/turning-unwanted-bananas-into-energy-snacks-with-upcycling/)). Even production waste is looped back in: Barnana dries its banana peels, presses them into pellets and burns them as fuel for the dehydration ovens ([Global AgInvesting](https://globalaginvesting.com/trently-advisors-leads-5-3m-round-barnana/)).

## Buying the supply chain: the Agroapoyo acquisition

In September 2021 Barnana acquired Agroapoyo, its Latin American supply and manufacturing partner based in Quito, Ecuador, creating what the company calls a "farm-to-shelf" business ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2021/barnana-backs-indigineous-farms-through-acquisition-and-new-product)). Agroapoyo was launched in 2001 — co-founded by Maria del Carmen Narvaez with her brother — and had been Barnana's primary operations partner in the Amazon region for eight years, collecting plantains from a network that grew from several hundred family farms to over 1,500 affiliates ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2021/barnana-backs-indigineous-farms-through-acquisition-and-new-product); [Thomson Reuters Foundation](https://we-fi.org/women-led-startups-help-family-farmers-feed-latin-america/)). The deal was not entirely cash: Agroapoyo accepted shares in Barnana instead, and Narvaez now leads Barnana Ecuador ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2021/barnana-backs-indigineous-farms-through-acquisition-and-new-product); [Thomson Reuters Foundation](https://we-fi.org/women-led-startups-help-family-farmers-feed-latin-america/)).

The acquisition changed the economics for growers. When a farmer partners with Agroapoyo, their income on average quadruples, according to Suplicy, because plantains that previously had no buyer — many farmers grew the trees only to shade other crops — now have a paying market ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2021/barnana-backs-indigineous-farms-through-acquisition-and-new-product)). Barnana buys plantain from farmers at 30% above market price, and works with more than 1,700 farmers from mainly Kichwa Indigenous jungle communities, for whom adopting organic methods can boost monthly income by up to 200% ([Thomson Reuters Foundation](https://we-fi.org/women-led-startups-help-family-farmers-feed-latin-america/)). Agroapoyo also guides farmers through organic certification — a 12- to 18-month process costing about $10,000, renewed annually — and teaches regenerative methods that raise plantain yields ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2021/barnana-backs-indigineous-farms-through-acquisition-and-new-product); [Thomson Reuters Foundation](https://we-fi.org/women-led-startups-help-family-farmers-feed-latin-america/)). The deal also gave Barnana a second revenue stream: it inherited Agroapoyo's existing customers for organic plantains, becoming, as NOSH put it, the crop's leading global supplier ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2021/barnana-backs-indigineous-farms-through-acquisition-and-new-product)). Vertical integration proved its worth during the pandemic, when the close relationship with Agroapoyo meant Barnana saw less supply-chain disruption than brands buying through intermediaries ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2021/barnana-backs-indigineous-farms-through-acquisition-and-new-product)).

## Funding, CircleUp, and the 2025 loan

| Event | Date | Amount / terms | Investors or counterparty |

| Growth-equity round | January 2017 | $5.3 million | Trently Advisors (lead), Blueberry Ventures, Boulder Food Group, Finn Capital Partners |

The January 2017 round was explicitly strategic: Ingersoll said Barnana turned down money from investors who did not understand merchandising in conventional grocery, club and coffee channels, choosing backers who could help it expand beyond the natural channel — and the money funded production growth, the Banana Brittle launch, new markets and digital marketing ([FoodNavigator-USA](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2017/08/16/Barnana-rolls-out-to-8-000-Starbucks-stores/); [Food Dive](https://www.fooddive.com/news/investors-go-bananas-for-barnana-with-53m-in-funding/434276/)).

[CircleUp](/organizations/circleup/) is the best-known name on Barnana's investor list. Its $125 million Growth Partners fund, raised in 2017 to back consumer brands chosen with its Helio machine-learning platform, took part in capital raises for nearly 30 brands including Barnana ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-breakup-lending-fund-sold-vc-fund-in-transit-ai-play-fate-unknown/)). The amount and date of CircleUp's Barnana investment were not verified from a fully read source and are not stated here. In 2023 CircleUp began divesting — its credit fund went to [Brightflow AI](/organizations/brightflow-ai/) and the Growth Partners fund was put up for sale to an unidentified buyer — and whether that affected Barnana is not known ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-breakup-lending-fund-sold-vc-fund-in-transit-ai-play-fate-unknown/)).

The most recent financing on record is debt, not equity. On September 2, 2025, the lending platform Steward originated a $1,850,000 secured loan to Barnana, PBC — a 30-month term, interest-only for 12 months — to refinance an existing line of credit, with about $940,000 of the proceeds as working capital for sales and marketing ([Steward](https://gosteward.com/projects/barnana/a-regenerative-supply-chain-rooted-in-the-rainforest)). The loan landed weeks after the United States imposed a 15% tariff on goods imported from Ecuador, Barnana's primary sourcing country; Steward judged the risk moderate because Barnana owns its Ecuadorian processing facility and imported processed goods are only 30–40% of cost of goods sold — stress-testing 25% tariffs still left a 1.66x debt-service coverage ratio ([Steward](https://gosteward.com/projects/barnana/a-regenerative-supply-chain-rooted-in-the-rainforest)). Steward also names Eco Enterprises as Barnana's largest investor and Hawthorne Group as its second largest; both figures are company-reported through the lending page rather than independently verified ([Steward](https://gosteward.com/projects/barnana/a-regenerative-supply-chain-rooted-in-the-rainforest)).

## FAQ

**What does Barnana make?** Barnana makes organic snacks from upcycled fruit: chewy Banana Bites in six flavours, crunchy Banana Brittle, ridged and tortilla plantain chips, Organic Plantain Crisps, and cassava chips — all built on bananas, plantains or cassava that would otherwise go to waste ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/turning-unwanted-bananas-into-energy-snacks-with-upcycling/); [NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2021/barnana-backs-indigineous-farms-through-acquisition-and-new-product)).

**Who founded Barnana?** Brazilian-born former professional triathlete Caue Suplicy founded the company in Santa Monica, California, in 2012 with Matt Clifford and Nik Ingersoll, after the three began working on the concept in 2010 ([Global AgInvesting](https://globalaginvesting.com/trently-advisors-leads-5-3m-round-barnana/); [FoodNavigator-USA](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2017/08/16/Barnana-rolls-out-to-8-000-Starbucks-stores/)).

**What does "upcycled" mean for Barnana?** It means buying organic bananas and plantains rejected for export — scuffed, wrongly sized or slightly overripe — and turning them into snacks instead of letting them rot; farmers can lose up to 20% of their crop this way ([B Lab](https://www.bcorporation.net/en-us/find-a-b-corp/company/barnana/); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2019/turning-unwanted-bananas-into-energy-snacks-with-upcycling/)).

**Is Barnana a B Corp?** Yes. B Lab's registry lists Barnana as a Certified B Corporation since March 2016, with a B Impact Score of 92.6 against a 50.9 median; it is also a Delaware Public Benefit Corporation ([B Lab](https://www.bcorporation.net/en-us/find-a-b-corp/company/barnana/); [Steward](https://gosteward.com/projects/barnana/a-regenerative-supply-chain-rooted-in-the-rainforest)).

**Did CircleUp invest in Barnana?** Yes. Barnana was among the nearly 30 brands backed by CircleUp Growth Partners' $125 million fund, per NOSH's 2023 account of CircleUp's breakup — though the amount and date were not publicly verified, and the fund was put up for sale in 2023 ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-breakup-lending-fund-sold-vc-fund-in-transit-ai-play-fate-unknown/)).
