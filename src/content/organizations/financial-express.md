## Overview

*The Financial Express* is India's oldest English-language business daily, published by the Indian Express Group since its launch in Mumbai in 1961 ([releaseMyAd](https://learn.releasemyad.com/2023/09/)). Headquartered in Noida, Uttar Pradesh, it publishes eleven editions a day across India, covering the economy, finance, markets, industry and trade ([releaseMyAd](https://learn.releasemyad.com/2023/09/)). It is the business paper of one of India's oldest newspaper groups, and its pages have tracked every turn of the Indian economy from the licence era to the AI age — most recently the February 2026 "SaaSpocalypse," when an AI product launch wiped hundreds of billions from software and IT stocks worldwide ([The Hindu](https://vajiramandravi.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/media/2026/2/9/12/14/19/Claudes_Cowork_Plugins_Trigger_a_SaaS_Market_Shock.pdf)). This profile is about the Indian paper — not the *Financial Express* of Bangladesh, a separate Dhaka-based daily.

## A business paper from the Express stable

The *Financial Express* was launched in 1961 from Mumbai, at a time when the Indian Express Group — founded around *The Indian Express* in 1932 — was expanding beyond general news into specialist titles ([releaseMyAd](https://learn.releasemyad.com/2023/09/)). The group itself later split: after the death of owner Ramnath Goenka in 1991, his family divided the business in 1999, with the northern editions keeping *The Indian Express* name and the southern editions becoming *The New Indian Express*, while the *Financial Express* remained with the Mumbai-based group. The paper's remit has stayed constant — business, finance, markets, commodities, industry and trade — and it presents two of its own awards, the FE India's Best Bank Awards and the FE-EVI Green Business Leadership Awards ([releaseMyAd](https://learn.releasemyad.com/2023/09/)). Its website, financialexpress.com, carries the same coverage online, and the group also publishes *The Indian Express*, the Marathi daily *Loksatta* and the Hindi daily *Jansatta*.

The paper's claim to be India's oldest financial daily is its own description, repeated across its trade-press listings — and its longevity is the point. Few Indian business papers have covered as many economic regimes: the controlled economy of the 1960s and 1970s, liberalization in 1991, the IT outsourcing boom, and now the AI disruption of the very IT companies it has chronicled for decades.

## The Amodei connection: the SaaSpocalypse

The *Financial Express*'s 2026 reporting on Dario Amodei — the [Anthropic](/organizations/anthropic/) chief executive — connects it to [Baidu](/organizations/baidu/) twice over. Amodei began his industry AI career at Baidu, joining in November 2014 to work on speech recognition; it was there, watching models improve smoothly as data and compute scaled, that he grasped the scaling regularities that became the AI "scaling laws" ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)). He left Baidu in 2015 for Google Brain, joined OpenAI in 2016, and co-founded Anthropic in 2021 ([Big Technology](https://www.bigtechnology.com/p/the-making-of-dario-amodei)).

In February 2026, Amodei's company shook the markets the *Financial Express* covers. On 30 January 2026, Anthropic released eleven open-source plug-ins for Claude Cowork, its AI workplace suite, automating legal, sales, marketing and data-analysis tasks ([The Hindu](https://vajiramandravi.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/media/2026/2/9/12/14/19/Claudes_Cowork_Plugins_Trigger_a_SaaS_Market_Shock.pdf)). On 3 February, global software stocks sold off violently: nearly $300 billion in market value was erased in a single day, with Thomson Reuters down about 16 percent, LegalZoom nearly 20 percent and the London Stock Exchange Group about 13 percent, while ServiceNow and Salesforce each fell nearly 7 percent ([Invezz](https://www.tradingview.com/news/invezz:c2ede31b8094b:0-why-anthropic-s-new-claude-plugins-sparked-global-selloff-in-software-stocks/)). [Jefferies](/organizations/jefferies/)' equity desk dubbed it the "SaaSpocalypse" ([The Hindu](https://vajiramandravi.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/media/2026/2/9/12/14/19/Claudes_Cowork_Plugins_Trigger_a_SaaS_Market_Shock.pdf)). In India the Nifty IT index fell 5.87 percent — its steepest single-day fall since March 2020 — with TCS and Infosys each down more than 7 percent, wiping out nearly ₹2 lakh crore ([The Hindu](https://vajiramandravi.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/media/2026/2/9/12/14/19/Claudes_Cowork_Plugins_Trigger_a_SaaS_Market_Shock.pdf)). The fear, as *The Hindu* put it, was existential for India's headcount-based outsourcing model: "if one AI agent can do the work of teams, India's headcount-based outsourcing model faces existential repricing" ([The Hindu](https://vajiramandravi.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/media/2026/2/9/12/14/19/Claudes_Cowork_Plugins_Trigger_a_SaaS_Market_Shock.pdf)). Amodei himself had warned that AI could displace half of entry-level white-collar jobs within one to five years ([The Hindu](https://vajiramandravi.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com/media/2026/2/9/12/14/19/Claudes_Cowork_Plugins_Trigger_a_SaaS_Market_Shock.pdf)).
