## Overview

Meta's name was not coined for the 2021 rebrand — it already belonged to someone else. In 2017, the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative, the philanthropic organization run by Mark Zuckerberg and his wife Priscilla Chan, bought a Canadian company called Meta that analyzed scientific literature. By the afternoon of October 28, 2021 — the day Facebook announced its own rebrand to Meta Platforms — the old company's Meta.org site announced it would "sunset" at the end of March, while Meta.com redirected to the newly rebranded corporate site ([AP](https://techxplore.com/news/2021-10-middle-crisis-facebook-renames-meta.html)).

Meta Platforms, Inc. is the Menlo Park, California, technology company behind Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp, and Threads — the "Family of Apps" that reaches 3.60 billion daily active people as of June 2026 ([Meta 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000132680125000017/meta-20241231.htm); [Meta Q2 2026 results](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)). The company, a Delaware corporation led by [Mark Zuckerberg](/people/mark-zuckerberg/), earns substantially all of its revenue from advertising sold across those apps ([Meta 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000132680125000017/meta-20241231.htm)).

That advertising money funds two bets: artificial-intelligence infrastructure and Reality Labs, the virtual- and augmented-reality division that loses billions every quarter ([Meta Q2 2026 results](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)). The company rebranded from Facebook to Meta on October 28, 2021, at its Connect event, with the metaverse as its stated focus ([Meta announcement](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/introducing-meta:-a-social-technology-company-2021-10-28)).

## The advertising engine

Advertising is the business. Meta's own disclosure says substantially all revenue comes from ads sold across its apps, and the numbers show it: in Q2 2026, advertising revenue was $59.363 billion out of $60.801 billion total, with ad impressions up 14% and average price per ad up 12% year over year ([Meta 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000132680125000017/meta-20241231.htm); [Meta Q2 2026 results](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)). The Family of Apps segment earned $23.394 billion in operating income on $60.370 billion in revenue that quarter ([Meta Q2 2026 results](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)).

The scale was already enormous in 2024: total revenue of $164.501 billion, with the Family of Apps contributing $162.355 billion in revenue and $87.109 billion in operating income ([Meta 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000132680125000017/meta-20241231.htm)). Meta's own disclosure flags the risks of this model: dependence on advertising revenue and on third-party data signals and mobile platforms it does not control ([Meta Q2 2026 results](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)).

## Reality Labs and the metaverse bet

The hardware story starts in 2014, when Facebook announced it would buy VR maker Oculus for approximately $2 billion — $400 million in cash plus 23.1 million shares, with a $300 million earn-out — calling VR "a strong candidate to emerge as the next social and communications platform" ([Facebook Newsroom](http://newsroom.fb.com/news/2014/03/facebook-to-acquire-oculus/)). Oculus had been founded in 2012 and had taken more than 75,000 development-kit orders ([Facebook Newsroom](http://newsroom.fb.com/news/2014/03/facebook-to-acquire-oculus/)).

Reality Labs now covers Meta's VR and AR consumer hardware, software, and content — products the company names as Meta Quest, the Meta Horizon Store, Ray-Ban Meta AI glasses, and the Orion AR prototype ([Meta Q2 2026 results](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx); [Meta 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000132680125000017/meta-20241231.htm)). The division is loss-making in every period verified: in 2024 it took in $2.146 billion in revenue against a $17.729 billion operating loss, with Meta spending about 21% of its 2024 costs on the segment ([Meta 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000132680125000017/meta-20241231.htm)). In Q2 2026 the pattern held: $431 million in revenue, a $4.619 billion operating loss ([Meta Q2 2026 results](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)).

## AI at Meta

In February 2023 Meta released LLaMA, a foundation large language model in 7B, 13B, 33B, and 65B sizes — the largest trained on 1.4 trillion tokens — under a noncommercial research license with case-by-case access ([Meta AI](https://ai.meta.com/blog/large-language-model-llama-meta-ai/)). That license matters: it is not open-source in the OSI sense, whatever shorthand sometimes calls it ([Meta AI](https://ai.meta.com/blog/large-language-model-llama-meta-ai/)). Meta says it releases Llama foundation models for researchers and developers, and uses AI across content ranking, recommendations, advertising systems, generative-AI experiences, Meta AI, and product development ([Meta 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000132680125000017/meta-20241231.htm)).

The AI buildout is now the company's biggest capital commitment. On the Q2 2026 earnings call, Zuckerberg said "AI is accelerating our core business today, powering our next generation of products, and opening the door to entirely new enterprise opportunities" ([Meta Q2 2026 results](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)). Meta guided full-year 2026 capital expenditures at $130–145 billion, reflecting the scale of that infrastructure spending ([Meta Q2 2026 results](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx)).

Meta has also given away more AI infrastructure than it kept. In 2022 it announced that PyTorch — its open-source machine-learning framework, launched in 2016 and run under Meta's control for six years — would move to the Linux Foundation under a newly created PyTorch Foundation. By then more than 18,000 organizations used PyTorch in production machine learning and academic research, including Tesla's Autopilot system and the Hugging Face Transformers library; the foundation's founding members were AMD, Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud, Meta, Microsoft Azure, and NVIDIA ([IT Pro](https://www.itpro.com/development/open-source/369054/meta-passes-pytorch-ownership-to-linux-foundation-bid-transparency)).

## Building the empire: Instagram, WhatsApp, Threads

Facebook's acquisition run built the Family of Apps. On April 9, 2012, it announced an agreement to buy Instagram for approximately $1 billion in cash and shares, expecting to close that quarter; Zuckerberg committed to keeping Instagram independent — it had 13 employees and about 27 million iPhone users at the time ([NBR](https://www.nbr.co.nz/facebook-to-by-instagram-for-us1-billion-2/)). The WhatsApp deal closed on October 6, 2014: 177,760,669 Class A shares plus about $4.59 billion in cash to WhatsApp securityholders, 45,941,775 RSUs to WhatsApp employees, and co-founder Jan Koum joining Facebook's board ([Facebook 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000132680114000037/fb_8-kxclosingxofxwhatsapp.htm)). The older "$19 billion" headline from the February 2014 announcement is not repeated here as fact; the closing 8-K's terms are.

Threads came later and on a rush: Meta launched it on July 5, 2023, a day ahead of schedule, as a text-based app linked to Instagram and positioned as a rival to Twitter, with limited EU availability at launch ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/technology/2023/07/06/threads-app-instagram-twitter/)).

## Moderation, privacy, and policy reversals

The Cambridge Analytica episode ended in the largest privacy penalty of its kind: on July 24, 2019, Facebook agreed to a record $5 billion FTC settlement — the FTC voted 3–2 — with an independent privacy committee and quarterly privacy certifications; the SEC separately settled for $100 million ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-facebook-ftc/facebook-to-pay-record-5-billion-u-s-fine-over-privacy-violations-critics-call-it-a-bargain-idUSKCN1UJ1L9/?feedType=RSS&feedName=businessNews&utm_source=feedburner&utm_medium=feed&utm_campaign=Feed%3A+reuters%2FbusinessNews+%28Business+News%29)). The trigger was 2018 reporting that a third-party personality quiz had shared information belonging to 87 million users with the British political consulting firm ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-facebook-ftc/facebook-to-pay-record-5-billion-u-s-fine-over-privacy-violations-critics-call-it-a-bargain-idUSKCN1UJ1L9/?feedType=RSS&feedName=businessNews+%28Business+News%29)).

In January 2021, Facebook suspended Donald Trump indefinitely from Facebook and Instagram after his posts around the Capitol attack, extending a 24-hour block; Zuckerberg said the risks of letting him keep using the service "are simply too great," citing "use of our platform to incite violent insurrection against a democratically elected government" ([NPR](https://www.wamc.org/2021-01-07/facebook-bans-president-trump-from-posting-for-the-rest-of-his-presidency?_amp=true)).

Then came the reversal. On January 7, 2025, Meta announced the end of its U.S. third-party fact-checking program — the program ended by April 7, 2025 — replacing it with Community Notes across Facebook, Threads, and Instagram with no penalties attached, and dropping restrictions on immigration and gender topics ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/04/meta-officially-says-goodbye-to-its-us-fact-checkers-on-monday/)). Zuckerberg framed the shift as a "cultural tipping point towards once again prioritizing speech" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/04/meta-officially-says-goodbye-to-its-us-fact-checkers-on-monday/)).
