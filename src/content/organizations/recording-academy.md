## Overview

The Recording Academy — formally the National Academy of Recording Arts and Sciences — is the U.S. music-industry organization behind the [Grammy Awards](https://factsledger.com/organizations/grammy-awards/), founded in Los Angeles in 1957 ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/grammy-awards-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). Beyond the awards, its stated mission spans the GRAMMY Museum, the MusiCares charity, music advocacy, and peer-recognized honors ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241003599230/en/Recording-Academy-Surpasses-2025-Membership-Goal-Adds-3000-Women-GRAMMY-Voters-Since-2019)). Since 1993 it has been headquartered in Santa Monica, California ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/grammy-awards-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). What makes the Academy's recent history distinctive is not a trophy but a rebuild: after a leadership crisis in 2020, it tore up how its voters are chosen and who gets to be one.

## The 2020 reform package

The crisis began at the top. Deborah Dugan, hired as president/CEO, was fired after 169 days amid her public claims that the Grammy nomination process was rigged; Harvey Mason Jr., the former board chair, stepped in as interim chief in January 2020 ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2021/05/14/harvey-mason-jr-becomes-official-ceo-of-recording-academy/)). What followed was the most sweeping reform in the Academy's history. The anonymous nomination review committees — the secret panels that had shaped the shortlists — were removed ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2021/05/14/harvey-mason-jr-becomes-official-ceo-of-recording-academy/)). In June 2020, the Academy dropped the term "urban" from its category names ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/grammy-awards-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). And it set out to remake the electorate itself: a goal of adding 2,500 women voting members by 2025.

The numbers say it worked. The Academy's 2024 Membership Report records more than 3,000 women voting members added since 2019 — beating the goal early — with 66% of the current electorate having joined since the new membership model began in 2019, about 8,700 new creator voters since 2019, and a record 2,800+ new members in 2024 alone ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241003599230/en/Recording-Academy-Surpasses-2025-Membership-Goal-Adds-3000-Women-GRAMMY-Voters-Since-2019)). CEO Mason declared it "the most diverse, representative GRAMMY electorate ever" ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241003599230/en/Recording-Academy-Surpasses-2025-Membership-Goal-Adds-3000-Women-GRAMMY-Voters-Since-2019)). In May 2021, Mason was named the official president/CEO — the first Black person to lead the Academy ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2021/05/14/harvey-mason-jr-becomes-official-ceo-of-recording-academy/)). He succeeded a line that ran from Michael Greene, the first official president/CEO in 1988, through Neil Portnow, who led the Academy from 2002 ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2021/05/14/harvey-mason-jr-becomes-official-ceo-of-recording-academy/)).

## The awards machine: 1959 to 2026

The first Grammy Awards were held on 4 May 1959, with ceremonies at the Beverly Hilton in Los Angeles and the Park Sheraton in New York; 28 awards were presented, with winners including Ella Fitzgerald, Count Basie, and Perry Como ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/grammy-awards-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). The first live telecast followed in 1971, hosted by Andy Williams from the Hollywood Palladium; the Grammy Hall of Fame was established in 1973 ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/grammy-awards-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). The category map grew with the music: video in 1983, reggae in 1984, new age in 1987, rap in 1988, alternative in 1990 ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/grammy-awards-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). The Grammy Foundation was created in 1988, the Latin Recording Academy in 1997 (first Latin Grammys 13 September 2000), and the Grammy Museum opened in Los Angeles in December 2008 ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/grammy-awards-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)).

The modern record book: Beyoncé set the all-time mark with 32 Grammy wins on 5 February 2023 ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/grammy-awards-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). The 68th Grammys, held 1 February 2026 at the Crypto.com Arena, made history of its own — Album of the Year went to Bad Bunny's "DeBÍ TiRAR MáS FOToS," the first Spanish-language album to win it, with Record of the Year to "luther" (Kendrick Lamar and SZA), Song of the Year to Billie Eilish's "WILDFLOWER," and Best New Artist to Olivia Dean ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/us/grammy-awards-fast-facts?cid=external-feeds_iluminar_meta)). And the Academy's trophies keep minting firsts for its winners: in 2019, [Ariana Grande](https://factsledger.com/people/ariana-grande/) won her first Grammy, Best Pop Vocal Album for "Sweetener," at the pre-telecast ceremony — the Academy accepted the award on her behalf after she thanked it from afar, following earlier nominations in 2015 and 2017 ([Zee News](https://zeenews.india.com/music/ariana-grande-wins-her-first-ever-grammy-for-best-pop-vocal-album-2179077.html)).

## The mission beyond the trophies

The Academy describes its work as wider than the telecast: the GRAMMY Museum, the MusiCares charity for music professionals in need, advocacy work on creators' behalf, and awards it calls the only peer-recognized music honors ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241003599230/en/Recording-Academy-Surpasses-2025-Membership-Goal-Adds-3000-Women-GRAMMY-Voters-Since-2019)). Its voting membership spans 13 disciplines and more than 22 genres — the electorate the 2020 reforms set out to rebuild ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241003599230/en/Recording-Academy-Surpasses-2025-Membership-Goal-Adds-3000-Women-GRAMMY-Voters-Since-2019)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not assert the Academy's total current membership — only the growth figures its 2024 report publishes. Membership data is first-party (via Business Wire) and is presented as the Academy's own reporting. Deborah Dugan's rigging claims are reported as her claims, not as established fact. The Walk of Fame origin story sometimes attached to the Academy could not be verified from a full read and is excluded. Category history after the milestones listed (through 1990) is not covered, and nothing here predicts future winners or future rule changes.
