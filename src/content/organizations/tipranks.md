## Overview

TipRanks is a Tel Aviv-based financial technology company that grades the people giving stock advice — analysts, financial bloggers, corporate insiders and hedge fund managers — by measuring what actually happened after their recommendations, rather than by the prestige of the bank they work for ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-israeli-fintech-co-tipranks-raises-77m-1001366826)). Founded in June 2012 by Uri Gruenbaum and Gilad Gat, it built its business around the [United States](/places/united-states/) stock market and English-language financial chatter, grew to about 50 million monthly users, and in August 2024 sold control to investment firm Prytek at a $200 million valuation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/15/tipranks-an-ai-based-stock-tip-evaluator-created-after-its-founder-got-burned-by-bad-advice-sells-for-200m-to-prytek/); [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-prytek-buys-control-of-israel-fintech-co-tipranks-1001486871)).

## Founded on a bad tip

The company's origin story is unusually literal. In 2012, Gruenbaum was working at a company called ARX when he received a $20,000 bonus; he invested it in the stock market following what he thought was a bona fide good recommendation he had found online, and lost the money on what turned out to be a very bad tip ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/15/tipranks-an-ai-based-stock-tip-evaluator-created-after-its-founder-got-burned-by-bad-advice-sells-for-200m-to-prytek/)). He co-founded TipRanks with chief technology officer Gilad Gat to make better sense of that noise, starting with $700,000 in seed money ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2014/07/22/652545/10090528/en/Eliot-Spitzer-Helps-Launch-Analyst-Accountability-Site.html)).

The first product was a browser extension — a "financial firewall" that activated whenever a user read an article containing a rating call in a financial publication — and it quickly grew into a standalone website ranking over 6,500 analysts and bloggers on their success rate, average return and consistency ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2014/07/22/652545/10090528/en/Eliot-Spitzer-Helps-Launch-Analyst-Accountability-Site.html)). In 2013 the company launched its Financial Accountability Engine, using natural language processing to track and measure the performance of over 7,000 professional analysts — a system Globes reported had been adopted on Wall Street for ranking analysts and was used by most banks ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-israeli-fintech-co-tipranks-raises-77m-1001366826)).

On July 22, 2014, TipRanks launched its open beta after closing a $3 million Series A round, backed by former New York governor Eliot Spitzer — who also joined the board — along with Cornell professor Roni Michaely and angel investor John Nakamura ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2014/07/22/652545/10090528/en/Eliot-Spitzer-Helps-Launch-Analyst-Accountability-Site.html)). The beta let users see the track record of anyone publishing investment advice online, including more than 3,100 sell-side analysts and more than 3,500 prominent financial bloggers ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2014/07/22/652545/10090528/en/Eliot-Spitzer-Helps-Launch-Analyst-Accountability-Site.html)).

## From analyst rankings to a retail-investor platform

TipRanks' pitch was that ordinary investors were flying blind: professional-grade analyst data cost tens of thousands of dollars a year, while free advice online came with no accountability ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-israeli-fintech-co-tipranks-raises-77m-1001366826)). The engine aggregated analyst ratings, blogger opinions, insider trading, hedge fund activity and news sentiment into datasets and tools — including the Smart Score, a composite stock rating — and sold them both to retail subscribers and to banks, brokers and exchanges that embedded TipRanks data in their own platforms ([Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/byhoe8jq0); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/15/tipranks-an-ai-based-stock-tip-evaluator-created-after-its-founder-got-burned-by-bad-advice-sells-for-200m-to-prytek/)).

The model scaled. In April 2021 TipRanks completed a $77 million financing round led by Prytek and More Investment House, with subscription sales up 300% over the prior year, over 4 million monthly users, and 60 employees mostly in Tel Aviv ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-israeli-fintech-co-tipranks-raises-77m-1001366826)). Its enterprise solution was by then integrated by [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/), E*TRADE, TD Ameritrade, eToro and Bank Santander, among others ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-israeli-fintech-co-tipranks-raises-77m-1001366826)). In January 2023 it made its first acquisition, buying financial news publisher The Fly — founded in 1998, with 28 employees in New Jersey and clients including Bloomberg and Dow Jones — for an estimated $15 million, to add real-time breaking news to the platform ([Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/rjbt0xv9s)).

## The Prytek acquisition

On August 15, 2024, Prytek — an investor in TipRanks since 2017 and the leader of its 2021 round — bought control of the company at a $200 million valuation, acquiring a 40% stake for $80 million from the founders, employees and some investors and taking its total holding to 80%, with 20% remaining with institutional investors including More Investment House ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-prytek-buys-control-of-israel-fintech-co-tipranks-1001486871); [Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/byhoe8jq0)). TechCrunch reported a higher figure, saying Prytek now held "more than 90%" after buying out the other investors, the founders and the employees — a conflict this profile flags rather than resolves ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/15/tipranks-an-ai-based-stock-tip-evaluator-created-after-its-founder-got-burned-by-bad-advice-sells-for-200m-to-prytek/)).

By then TipRanks had about 50,000 paying subscribers, an enterprise data system integrated into the trading systems of Morgan Stanley, Nasdaq, eToro and most Israeli banks, estimated annual revenue of $20–30 million, and 120 employees ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-prytek-buys-control-of-israel-fintech-co-tipranks-1001486871)). TechCrunch put total usage higher — some 50 million monthly active individual users, with paid plans at $30 or $50 a month — and named enterprise customers including Nasdaq, Robinhood, CIBC, Morgan Stanley, TD and Rakuten ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/15/tipranks-an-ai-based-stock-tip-evaluator-created-after-its-founder-got-burned-by-bad-advice-sells-for-200m-to-prytek/)). The founders, Gruenbaum and Gat, were to continue leading the company under Prytek, which planned to push deeper into enterprise customers while keeping the focus on tools for individual investors ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-prytek-buys-control-of-israel-fintech-co-tipranks-1001486871); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/15/tipranks-an-ai-based-stock-tip-evaluator-created-after-its-founder-got-burned-by-bad-advice-sells-for-200m-to-prytek/)).

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not settle Prytek's post-deal stake: Globes and Ctech report 80% with 20% retained by institutions, while TechCrunch reports more than 90%; the discrepancy is flagged in the facts above and in openItems.
- It does not repeat TipRanks' own Smart Score performance claims, which are company-published backtested figures rather than independently verified results.
- It does not assert the 2025 acquisition of Main Street Data, which was not verified from a fully-read tier1 or tier2 source.
- It does not describe Cornell professor Roni Michaely as a co-founder; the fully-read sources establish him only as an early investor.
- TipRanks is the Tel Aviv-founded fintech behind tipranks.com; it is not affiliated with any similarly named stock-tip or analyst-ranking service.
