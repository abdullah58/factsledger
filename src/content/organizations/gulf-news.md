## Overview

**Gulf News** is an English-language daily newspaper published in Dubai, [United Arab Emirates](/places/united-arab-emirates/), launched on 30 September 1978 and owned by Al Nisr Publishing. It is the UAE's largest English-language daily, a position it held while print circulation fell and digital revenue lagged, prompting the UAE's first newspaper paywall in 2021 and the end of weekend print editions in 2023 ([Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/gcc/uae/al-nisr-publishing-to-stop-printing-gulf-news-on-weekends); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/media/gulf-news-to-charge-for-digital-content-1846621)).

This profile covers the Dubai-based *Gulf News* published at gulfnews.com — not any similarly named publication elsewhere in the Gulf. It survived a near-collapse in its early years, was bought by three Dubai businessmen in 1984, and was relaunched under Al Nisr Publishing on 10 December 1985 ([Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/gcc/uae/al-nisr-publishing-to-stop-printing-gulf-news-on-weekends)). Its own history traces the relaunch to new premises, new printing technology, and a marketing push that lifted advertising revenue and subscriptions within months ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/about-gulf-news/history-gulfnews)).

## From a struggling tabloid to market leader

*Gulf News* began as a tabloid and, by its own account, barely survived its first years with a circulation of about 3,000 before the 1984 takeover ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/about-gulf-news/history-gulfnews)). The three buyers — Obaid Humaid Al Tayer, Abdullah Hassan Al Rostamani and Juma Al Majid — formed Al Nisr Publishing and relaunched the paper in December 1985 ([Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/gcc/uae/al-nisr-publishing-to-stop-printing-gulf-news-on-weekends)). The paper then expanded across the Gulf: its own history records distribution into other Gulf Cooperation Council countries from 1987, a Manila bureau in 1990, and a New Delhi bureau in November 1995 ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/about-gulf-news/history-gulfnews)).

By 2009 it was, in the words of rival-turned-chronicler *The National*, the UAE's largest English-language daily — and an unusual one, because its circulation grew during an advertising downturn, from 118,584 in September 2008 to 123,444 by mid-2009 after a brief peak of 126,320 ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/lifestyle/motoring/is-the-newspaper-making-its-last-stand-in-the-uae-1.553069)). In 2012 it became the first newspaper in the region to adopt the Berliner format ([Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/gcc/uae/al-nisr-publishing-to-stop-printing-gulf-news-on-weekends)). Its fiercest competition has always been the other Dubai English daily founded in 1978, *Khaleej Times*, with both papers chasing the expatriate readers who form a majority of the country's population ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/media/no-toll-gates-in-our-lanes-uaes-khaleej-times-trolls-gulf-news-after-paywall-announcement-1849806)).

## Print retreat and the paywall

The digital era reversed the paper's print fortunes. In April 2021 *Gulf News* announced it would put its online content behind a paywall — the first such move by a UAE newspaper — with Standard access at 5.95 dirhams a month and Prime access at 8.96 dirhams a month or 52 dirhams a year ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/media/gulf-news-to-charge-for-digital-content-1846621)). The paper's chief executive, Abdul Hamid Ahmad, told *Arab News* that print revenue had long subsidised free online content and could no longer do so ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/media/gulf-news-to-charge-for-digital-content-1846621)). The paywall made *Gulf News* the only local Emirati newspaper requiring a paid subscription, a distinction rival *Khaleej Times* promptly mocked with its "no toll gates in our lanes" promise of free news ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/media/no-toll-gates-in-our-lanes-uaes-khaleej-times-trolls-gulf-news-after-paywall-announcement-1849806)).

*Gulf News* drew an average of 230 million monthly page views and 15 million unique visitors, yet saw no matching revenue — which implies its paywall was a monetisation fix for an audience problem it did not have ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/media/gulf-news-to-charge-for-digital-content-1846621)). Two years later, print retreated further: in a May 2023 letter to advertisers, Al Nisr Publishing said readers had shifted to online platforms and that weekend print editions would stop from 3 June 2023, leaving the paper in print Monday to Friday ([Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/gcc/uae/al-nisr-publishing-to-stop-printing-gulf-news-on-weekends)). By then *Arabian Business* reported the paper quoting a circulation of 77,781 from Saturday to Thursday and 77,381 on Fridays — far below the 123,444 *The National* had recorded in 2009 ([Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/gcc/uae/al-nisr-publishing-to-stop-printing-gulf-news-on-weekends); [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/lifestyle/motoring/is-the-newspaper-making-its-last-stand-in-the-uae-1.553069)).

## Covering South Asia for a diaspora readership

*Gulf News* matters beyond the UAE because its core audience is the expatriate workforce — above all South Asians — and its reporting follows that readership's concerns in both directions. *Arab News* notes that *Gulf News* and *Khaleej Times* compete for the expatriate segment that forms a majority of the UAE's population ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/media/no-toll-gates-in-our-lanes-uaes-khaleej-times-trolls-gulf-news-after-paywall-announcement-1849806)). The paper's own history records a New Delhi bureau opened in November 1995, giving it a reporting base in [India](/places/india/) alongside its Gulf bureaus ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/about-gulf-news/history-gulfnews)).

That two-way coverage shows up in how other outlets use its work. *South Asia Monitor* reported that Dubai-based Indian expatriates who lost their belongings in a warehouse fire pursued compensation through the Kerala High Court, crediting *Gulf News* for the underlying reporting ([South Asia Monitor](https://southasiamonitor.org/south-asia-abroad/indian-victims-dubai-warehouse-blaze-move-kerala-hc)). The *Hindustan Times* likewise cited a *Gulf News* report when covering a Kerala expatriate's return home after 51 years working in the UAE ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/trending/indian-man-who-helped-countless-people-land-uae-jobs-returns-after-51-years-greeted-by-roaring-busload-of-people-101755349445261.html)). Beyond news, the paper has run long-lived community fixtures: its own history dates the *Gulf News* Fun Drive, a desert motoring event, to March 1986 ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/about-gulf-news/history-gulfnews)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was Gulf News founded?**
*Gulf News* launched in tabloid format on 30 September 1978 in Dubai. It struggled in its early years, was bought by three Dubai businessmen in 1984, and relaunched under Al Nisr Publishing on 10 December 1985, which is the founding line of the modern paper.

**Who owns Gulf News?**
The paper is owned by Al Nisr Publishing LLC, formed by Obaid Humaid Al Tayer, Abdullah Hassan Al Rostamani and Juma Al Majid when they bought the paper in 1984. A wider Al Tayer Group link is often stated elsewhere but was not independently confirmed in this pass.

**Is Gulf News the UAE's biggest English newspaper?**
*The National* described it as the UAE's largest English-language daily in 2009, and *Arab News* in 2021 called it one of the two leading UAE English-language dailies alongside *Khaleej Times*. Both papers were founded in Dubai in 1978 and compete for expatriate readers.

**Why did Gulf News introduce a paywall?**
It announced the paywall in April 2021 because print revenue, which had long subsidised free online content, kept declining. It was the first UAE newspaper to charge for digital access, with Standard access at 5.95 dirhams a month and Prime at 8.96 dirhams a month or 52 dirhams a year.

**Does Gulf News still print on weekends?**
No. From 3 June 2023, Al Nisr Publishing stopped Saturday and Sunday print editions, citing readers' shift to online platforms. The paper remains in print from Monday to Friday, while its website carries weekend coverage and its digital subscription offers the full service.
