## Overview

*The National* is the United Arab Emirates' leading English-language daily newspaper, published in [Abu Dhabi](/places/abu-dhabi/) since 17 April 2008 ([Canadian Journalism Foundation](https://cjf-fjc.ca/new-uae-paper-launches/)). Founded by the government-owned Abu Dhabi Media as the capital's first English-language daily, it was acquired in 2016 by International Media Investments (IMI) and relaunched in 2017 under editor-in-chief Mina Al-Oraibi ([WAM](https://www.omnesmedia.com/en/media-news/international-media-investments-to-acquire)). From its Abu Dhabi headquarters, with bureaux in Beirut, Cairo, London and Washington, it covers the Middle East for an English-speaking audience in the region and beyond, publishing in print five days a week alongside its digital platforms ([The National](https://thenational-the-national-dev.web.arc-cdn.net/about-us/)). This profile is about the Abu Dhabi paper — not the newspapers of the same name in Scotland, Papua New Guinea or Wales.

## Launch: a broadsheet built from imported talent

Abu Dhabi Media announced the paper in 2007 as the UAE's new English-language national newspaper, appointing Martin Newland — editor of the UK's *Daily Telegraph* from 2003 to 2005 — as launch editor ([MEED](https://www.meed.com/abu-dhabi-to-launch-english-language-national-newspaper/)). Newland recruited heavily from British and international newspapers, and the paper launched on 17 April 2008 with a staff of about 200 reporters and editors and an 80-page first edition ([Canadian Journalism Foundation](https://cjf-fjc.ca/new-uae-paper-launches/)). Its stated mission, in Newland's words, was "to reflect society, help that society evolve and, perhaps most importantly, promote the bedrock traditions and virtues that must be preserved even in times of change" ([Canadian Journalism Foundation](https://cjf-fjc.ca/new-uae-paper-launches/)). The launch was a deliberate piece of nation-building: Abu Dhabi, which had taken ownership of Abu Dhabi Media from the federal government the previous year, wanted an English-language paper of international standard printed in the capital ([MEED](https://www.meed.com/abu-dhabi-to-launch-english-language-national-newspaper/)).

Newland left the editor's chair in June 2009, succeeded by Hassan Fattah, and the paper passed through two further editors-in-chief — Fattah until 2013 and Mohammed Al Otaiba from 2014 to 2016 — before the ownership change that reshaped it. (Those two editorship dates come from the Wikipedia lead index and were not independently verified; see open items.)

## The IMI acquisition and the 2017 relaunch

On 16 November 2016, International Media Investments (IMI) acquired ownership of *The National* from Abu Dhabi Media ([WAM](https://www.omnesmedia.com/en/media-news/international-media-investments-to-acquire)). IMI is a subsidiary of the Abu Dhabi Media Investment Corporation (ADMIC), the private investment company owned by Sheikh Mansour bin Zayed Al Nahyan — the same group behind the collaboration with Sky to launch Sky News Arabia ([WAM](https://www.omnesmedia.com/en/media-news/international-media-investments-to-acquire)). The acquisition came amid job cuts and rumoured management struggles at the paper, and the new owner relaunched it on 1 July 2017 under Mina Al-Oraibi, formerly deputy editor-in-chief of the London-based Arabic daily *Asharq Alawsat* and a Yale fellow ([Campaign Middle East](https://campaignme.com/mina-al-oraibis-new-national-new-editors-plans-abu-dhabi-daily/)).

The relaunch was physical as well as editorial: new headquarters in Abu Dhabi's twofour54 media free zone, a foreign bureau in London, a leaner print product with one main section during the week, and a rebuilt website with separate UAE and international homepages ([Campaign Middle East](https://campaignme.com/mina-al-oraibis-new-national-new-editors-plans-abu-dhabi-daily/)). Al-Oraibi retained 75–80 percent of the old staff and created departments that had not existed before, including a four-person multimedia team ([Campaign Middle East](https://campaignme.com/mina-al-oraibis-new-national-new-editors-plans-abu-dhabi-daily/)). Her stated ambition was for the paper to be "a media leader" that shapes thinking in the region, with commentary as a central pillar ([Campaign Middle East](https://campaignme.com/mina-al-oraibis-new-national-new-editors-plans-abu-dhabi-daily/)).

## The Baidu connection: [Dubai](/places/dubai/)'s robotaxi story

*The National* is the Baidu profile's cited publisher for the Dubai robotaxi launch of August 2026 — a story squarely in its home territory. On 20 August 2026, [Uber](/organizations/uber/) announced that [Baidu](/organizations/baidu/)'s fully driverless [Apollo Go](/organizations/apollo-go/) vehicles were officially available to riders on the Uber platform in Dubai, with New Horizon Luxury Transport as fleet operator ([WAM](https://omnesmedia.com/en/media-news/uber-launches-baidus-fully-driverless-apollo-go-in-dubai)). Riders requesting UberX or Uber Comfort in Umm Suqeim and [Jumeirah](/places/jumeirah/) could be matched with a fully driverless Apollo Go RT6, a purpose-built electric robotaxi carrying up to three passengers ([WAM](https://omnesmedia.com/en/media-news/uber-launches-baidus-fully-driverless-apollo-go-in-dubai)). Dubai was the inaugural step in a multi-year Uber–Baidu partnership aimed at scaling thousands of Apollo Go vehicles across Uber's global network, and the first city where Apollo Go operated both its own app and a third-party platform ([WAM](https://omnesmedia.com/en/media-news/uber-launches-baidus-fully-driverless-apollo-go-in-dubai)). For a paper whose remit is the Gulf's transformation, the launch — Chinese autonomous technology deployed on American platforms in an Emirati city — is exactly the kind of story *The National* exists to cover.
