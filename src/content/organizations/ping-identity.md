## Overview

Ping Identity is an enterprise identity and access management (IAM) software company founded in 2002, known for its PingOne platform covering single sign-on, multifactor authentication and federated identity management ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/cybersecurity/news/252523468/Thoma-Bravo-to-acquire-Ping-Identity-for-28B)). **It is not related to Ping An**, the Chinese financial-services and insurance group — Ping Identity is a US enterprise-software company. After a majority-stake acquisition by Vista Equity Partners in 2016 and a 2019 IPO, the company was taken private again in October 2022 when Thoma Bravo bought it for $28.50 a share in cash, an enterprise value of about $2.8 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/03/thoma-bravo-picks-up-ping-identity-for-2-8b-in-an-all-cash-deal/), [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/news-and-events/news/2022/10/10_18-thoma-bravo-completes-acquisition)).

## The three-year public window

Ping Identity's time as a public company lasted barely three years. Vista Equity Partners bought a majority stake in 2016 for an undisclosed amount, then took the company public in 2019 at $15 a share, raising approximately $187 million ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/cybersecurity/news/252523468/Thoma-Bravo-to-acquire-Ping-Identity-for-28B)). Vista kept a 9.7% stake after the IPO — and voted it in favor of the Thoma Bravo deal ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/03/thoma-bravo-picks-up-ping-identity-for-2-8b-in-an-all-cash-deal/)). As TechCrunch put it, the Thoma Bravo buyout was "actually the second time that Ping has bounced into the hands of a PE firm" — in substance, a private-equity asset with a brief NYSE interlude ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/03/thoma-bravo-picks-up-ping-identity-for-2-8b-in-an-all-cash-deal/)).

## The business behind the buyout

What Thoma Bravo bought was a growing but cash-strained subscription business. In the quarter that ended June 30, 2022 — the same quarter the deal was announced — Ping reported annual recurring revenue of $341 million, up 22% year over year, with SaaS ARR at $100 million, about 30% of total ending ARR ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/03/thoma-bravo-picks-up-ping-identity-for-2-8b-in-an-all-cash-deal/)). Subscriptions made up the bulk of revenue at $66.3 million, or 92% of the quarter's total, and Ping ended the quarter with 331 enterprise customers spending more than $250,000 in ARR each, up 19% from a year earlier ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/03/thoma-bravo-picks-up-ping-identity-for-2-8b-in-an-all-cash-deal/)). Yet the company missed analysts' estimates, with quarterly revenue of $72 million falling several million short — and its unlevered free cash flow was negative $8.4 million for the first half of the year, versus positive $34.5 million a year earlier ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/03/thoma-bravo-picks-up-ping-identity-for-2-8b-in-an-all-cash-deal/)). Growth, in TechCrunch's assessment, "has not been fast enough."

## A 63% premium on a bad quarter

The deal was announced on August 3, 2022 alongside Ping's second-quarter earnings, in which revenue of $72 million missed analysts' expectations and the company reported negative $8.4 million in unlevered free cash flow for the first half of the year ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/03/thoma-bravo-picks-up-ping-identity-for-2-8b-in-an-all-cash-deal/)). The $28.50 price represented a 63% premium over the August 2 closing price and a 52% premium over the 60-day volume-weighted average — a classic PE playbook move on a public software company whose shares had slid ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/03/thoma-bravo-picks-up-ping-identity-for-2-8b-in-an-all-cash-deal/), [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/cybersecurity/news/252523468/Thoma-Bravo-to-acquire-Ping-Identity-for-28B)). Ping's board unanimously approved the transaction, which was expected to close in the fourth quarter of 2022 ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/cybersecurity/news/252523468/Thoma-Bravo-to-acquire-Ping-Identity-for-28B)). TechCrunch's read: Ping was growing but "not fast enough," in "a tough spiral" of SaaS volatility, and may have been "running out of options for raising funding as an independent company" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/03/thoma-bravo-picks-up-ping-identity-for-2-8b-in-an-all-cash-deal/)). Thoma Bravo managing partner Seth Boro framed the other side of the bet — "a tectonic shift is occurring in intelligent identity solutions for the enterprise" — while CEO Andre Durand called it a "compelling transaction" for Ping's shareholders ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/03/thoma-bravo-picks-up-ping-identity-for-2-8b-in-an-all-cash-deal/)).

## Identity consolidation and the SailPoint question

The acquisition landed in the middle of a private-equity consolidation wave across identity security. Thoma Bravo had already bought SailPoint — another identity governance vendor — for $6.9 billion and Anaplan for $10.7 billion the same year, and owned Sophos and Proofpoint ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/03/thoma-bravo-picks-up-ping-identity-for-2-8b-in-an-all-cash-deal/), [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/cybersecurity/news/252523468/Thoma-Bravo-to-acquire-Ping-Identity-for-28B)). TechTarget openly speculated that Thoma Bravo could combine Ping with other portfolio security vendors ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/cybersecurity/news/252523468/Thoma-Bravo-to-acquire-Ping-Identity-for-28B)). After completion in October 2022, Ping's stock ceased trading and was delisted from the NYSE, per the deal counsel's announcement ([Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/news-and-events/news/2022/10/10_18-thoma-bravo-completes-acquisition)).
