## Overview

ZDNet is a business technology news website founded by Ziff Davis on 1 April 1991 that has changed hands four times in a quarter-century — sold to CNET for about $1.6 billion in 2000, swallowed by CBS's $1.8 billion purchase of CNET in 2008, sold to Red Ventures for $500 million in 2020, and bought back by its original owner, Ziff Davis, in 2024 for about $100 million ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2000/07/19/cnet-to-buy-zdnet-for-16bn/700764); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/09/14/red-ventures-acquires-cnet-media-group-from-viacomcbs-for-500m/); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/ziff-davis-layoffs-cnet-mashable/)). It began as a general-interest technology portal and evolved into an enterprise IT-focused publication aimed at business executives making technology decisions. Its ownership odyssey is a compact history of digital media economics: each sale fetched less than the last, until the brand came home for roughly a sixteenth of its year-2000 price. Along the way it has covered the companies that built the modern internet — including [Cloudflare](/organizations/cloudflare/), whose 2019 IPO filing ZDNet reported in detail.

## The 1990s portal boom

ZDNet was Ziff-Davis's online service and internet portal, launched in 1995 to host electronic editions of the company's magazines; it began accepting advertising in April 1995 ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/ziff-davis-inc)). The growth was fast and loss-making in equal measure: revenue rose from $10.2 million in 1996 to $32.2 million in 1997 and $56.1 million in 1998, "but it failed to turn a profit" ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/ziff-davis-inc)). Ziff-Davis also launched the ZDTV cable channel in May 1998, and its parent SoftBank — which had acquired Ziff-Davis for $2.1 billion in December 1995 — began dismantling the company and selling off its non-internet pieces ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/ziff-davis-inc)).

In 1999 Ziff-Davis spun ZDNet off as a separate company and offered it as a tracking stock; the IPO raised $190 million, with Ziff-Davis retaining an 83% interest ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/ziff-davis-inc)). By January 2000 ZDNet had more than 10 million unique visitors a month — ahead of CNET's 9.5 million — and syndicated operations in 23 countries and 15 languages ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/ziff-davis-inc)). The tracking stock was eliminated in early 2000 and revived as common stock, a preliminary move to the sale that followed ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/ziff-davis-inc)).

## Four owners in 24 years

On 19 July 2000 CNET Networks — ZDNet's largest rival — announced it would acquire ZDNet for about $1.6 billion in stock ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2000/07/19/cnet-to-buy-zdnet-for-16bn/700764)). The combined business would employ 1,600 people and was expected to turn over $500 million in 2001; together the two sites had more than 20 million readers a month, and CNET put the audience overlap at "only 25 per cent" ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2000/07/19/cnet-to-buy-zdnet-for-16bn/700764)). CNET kept operating ZDNet as a separate brand, and the two together reached 22% of all internet users ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/ziff-davis-inc)). In 2003 CNET redesigned and relaunched ZDNet as an enterprise-focused publication for business executives.

CBS acquired CNET Networks, including ZDNet, for $1.8 billion in cash in 2008, and the sites were organised under its CBS Interactive division ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/09/14/red-ventures-acquires-cnet-media-group-from-viacomcbs-for-500m/)). After CBS merged with Viacom in 2019, the combined ViacomCBS looked to offload non-core assets — and on 14 September 2020 Red Ventures announced it would buy CNET Media Group, including CNET, ZDNet, GameSpot, TV Guide, Metacritic and Chowhound, for $500 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/09/14/red-ventures-acquires-cnet-media-group-from-viacomcbs-for-500m/)). As TechCrunch noted at the time, "the significantly discounted price being paid today for a larger group of assets underscores the changing — and often declining — value of more traditional media and publishing brands" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/09/14/red-ventures-acquires-cnet-media-group-from-viacomcbs-for-500m/)).

The Red Ventures years were turbulent. In March 2023 ZDNet's editorial team was cut by about 35% in layoffs across CNET Media Group, and CNET faced criticism after AI-generated articles containing errors were discovered on the site. A union statement quoted in 2025 said CNET editorial workers had faced "three major rounds of layoffs in two years" under Red Ventures, including "the gutting of its essential news and culture teams in 2023" ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/ziff-davis-layoffs-cnet-mashable/)). In August 2024 Ziff Davis agreed to buy CNET back for about $100 million, and on 1 October 2024 it announced it had completed the acquisition in the third quarter of 2024, with terms undisclosed ([BusinessWire](https://markets.financialcontent.com/cercescourier/article/bizwire-2024-10-1-ziff-davis-completes-one-acquisition-in-q3-2024); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/ziff-davis-layoffs-cnet-mashable/)). ZDNet was back under the ownership of the company that founded it, for the first time since 2000. The homecoming did not end the cuts: in July 2025 Ziff Davis laid off 23 people across CNET, Mashable, Lifehacker and ZDNet ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/ziff-davis-layoffs-cnet-mashable/)).

## Covering Cloudflare

ZDNet has tracked Cloudflare since the company's early days. In August 2019 it reported Cloudflare's IPO filing: the San Francisco internet infrastructure startup had filed an S-1 for a New York Stock Exchange listing that could value it at more than $5 billion, had raised more than $320 million across six private rounds, served more than 20 million websites through 193 data centers, and counted co-founder and COO [Michelle Zatlyn](/people/michelle-zatlyn/) as a rare female co-founder in a senior leadership role at a tech IPO ([ZDNet](https://www.zdnet.com/article/cloudflare-ipo-a-real-tech-giant-in-the-making-with-a-female-co-founder/)). ZDNet's December 2025 year-in-review of the internet leaned heavily on Cloudflare's Radar data — global traffic up about 19% year over year, some 30% of web traffic from bots — and linked its own coverage of the major November 2025 Cloudflare outage that took down ChatGPT, X and Spotify ([ZDNet](https://platform.test.zdnet.com/home-and-office/networking/internet-review-2025-bigger-fragile-hostile-ai-influence-cloudflare/)). The through-line is the site's remit: the infrastructure layer of the internet is business technology, and Cloudflare is one of its central companies.

## FAQ

**Who owns ZDNet now?** Ziff Davis, which reacquired it in 2024 along with CNET for about $100 million. Ziff Davis also founded the brand in 1991, so the 2024 deal brought it home after 24 years under CNET, CBS and Red Ventures.

**How much was ZDNet sold for over the years?** About $1.6 billion in stock to CNET in 2000; then as part of CNET Networks to CBS for $1.8 billion in 2008; then as part of CNET Media Group to Red Ventures for $500 million in 2020; then back to Ziff Davis for about $100 million in 2024.

**What does ZDNet cover?** Business technology news — enterprise IT, cybersecurity, cloud infrastructure, AI and the companies behind them — aimed at business executives and IT professionals making technology decisions.

**What happened to ZDNet under Red Ventures?** Its editorial team was cut by about 35% in March 2023 layoffs, and its sister site CNET was criticised after AI-generated articles with errors were published. Red Ventures then sold the group to Ziff Davis in 2024.

**Has ZDNet covered Cloudflare?** Yes, for years — from its 2019 IPO filing (founders, funding, 20 million-plus sites served) to its December 2025 year-in-review built on Cloudflare's Radar traffic data and its coverage of the November 2025 outage.
