## Overview

CircleUp Growth Partners was the $125 million venture fund launched by [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) consumer-investment platform [CircleUp](/organizations/circleup/) in October 2017 to back early-stage consumer brands — companies that make things that sit on a shelf — using CircleUp's Helio machine-learning platform to pick them ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/10/31/circleup-announced-125-million-venture-fund/)). It backed nearly 30 brands, scored its first exit when Unilever bought [Liquid I.V.](/organizations/liquid-iv/) in 2020, and was sold off in CircleUp's 2023 breakup to a buyer that was never publicly named ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-breakup-lending-fund-sold-vc-fund-in-transit-ai-play-fate-unknown/)).

The fund was the equity-investing arm of CircleUp, not the company itself. CircleUp was founded in 2012 by investors [Ryan Caldbeck](/people/ryan-caldbeck/) and [Rory Eakin](/people/rory-eakin/) as an equity crowdfunding marketplace for consumer packaged goods (CPG) brands, and in 2017 it remade itself into an investment-and-financing business: the Growth Partners fund to buy equity in brands, plus a loan fund, [CircleUp Credit Advisors](/organizations/circleup-credit-advisors/), while the crowdfunding marketplace was wound down ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-breakup-lending-fund-sold-vc-fund-in-transit-ai-play-fate-unknown/)). The fund is also unrelated to any other firm using the generic name "Growth Partners." Before it, CircleUp had run a much smaller $22 million fund, announced in 2015 and run by Ben Lee ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2017/circleup-closes-125m-fund-powered-machine-learning/); [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/startups/circleup-raises-125m-invest-cpg-startups/)).

## The data-driven bet

CircleUp's pitch was that consumer investing was broken in a specific way, and data could fix it. Deals were sourced and analysed through slow, manual processes "riddled with human bias," co-founder and CEO Ryan Caldbeck wrote when the fund launched, with decisions "often based on feeling and 'gut' rather than data and facts" — and investors spent upwards of 75 percent of their time sourcing deals manually, including travelling to trade shows ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2017/circleup-closes-125m-fund-powered-machine-learning/)). Helio, CircleUp's proprietary machine-learning platform, was supposed to do the legwork instead: at launch it analysed billions of data points from over 1.2 million companies, and by 2023 it tracked more than 1.6 million brands and 16 million attributes ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2017/circleup-closes-125m-fund-powered-machine-learning/); [NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-sells-vc-funds-final-home-still-unknown/)). Helio produced a list of brands worth talking to, the investment team got to know the founders, and Helio then assisted with diligence, category analysis and deal terms ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2017/circleup-closes-125m-fund-powered-machine-learning/)).

The fund's thesis was that there was a white space at the early stage of the traditional CPG market. Early-stage consumer investing had either focused on the intersection of consumer goods and technology, or on larger checks — north of $10 million — from traditional CPG investors, according to general partner Alison Ryu, a recent hire from TSG Consumer ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2017/circleup-closes-125m-fund-powered-machine-learning/)). Growth Partners would instead write smaller checks, from $1 million to $3 million with room to flex, into [United States](/places/united-states/) companies with $1 million to $10 million in revenue, aiming to approach at least 18 brands a year ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2017/circleup-closes-125m-fund-powered-machine-learning/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/10/31/circleup-announced-125-million-venture-fund/)). It looked at food and beverage and personal care — "companies that produce something that sit on a shelf," in co-founder and COO Rory Eakin's words — and explicitly not at technology companies, even though its own selection method was technological ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/10/31/circleup-announced-125-million-venture-fund/)). Axios reported the fund was looking for companies that could eventually sell for $100 million to $500 million ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/startups/circleup-raises-125m-invest-cpg-startups/)).

Ryu joined CircleUp veteran Ben Lee as the fund's general partners, alongside Caldbeck ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2017/circleup-closes-125m-fund-powered-machine-learning/)). Its limited partners included [Temasek](/organizations/temasek/), Euclidean Capital, Annie's Homegrown co-founder and former CEO John Foraker, Plum Organics founder Gigi Lee Chang, Bumble and bumble co-founders Tevya and Deva Finger, and outsourced investment offices of pensions and endowments ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2017/circleup-closes-125m-fund-powered-machine-learning/)).

Helio's edge, as the fund described it, was spotting strong brands before their distribution caught up. Meat-snack maker [4505](/organizations/4505-meats/), one of the fund's first investments, scored in the top one percent of its category and the top 10 percent for distribution — a combination of high performance and low distribution that CircleUp called a sweet spot, because "with the right distribution and investment in capital you can solve for that and it will catapult growth" ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2017/circleup-closes-125m-fund-powered-machine-learning/)). Portfolio companies also got Helio access after the investment closed, to help with decisions such as entering a new channel, and CircleUp offered its relationships with Amazon, Starbucks, Bed Bath & Beyond and Nielsen ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2017/circleup-closes-125m-fund-powered-machine-learning/)). One concern brands raised — that information they had shared with the crowdfunding side of CircleUp could benefit the VC arm's picks — was answered with a promise that Helio data was anonymised ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2017/circleup-closes-125m-fund-powered-machine-learning/)).

## The portfolio

The fund announced its first two investments alongside the launch: female-focused supplement brand [HUM Nutrition](/organizations/hum-nutrition/), which closed a $4 million round with the fund as lead investor, and meat-snack brand 4505 Chicharrones, which was closing a round with the fund as lead ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2017/circleup-closes-125m-fund-powered-machine-learning/)). Over its life the fund took part in capital raises for nearly 30 brands ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-breakup-lending-fund-sold-vc-fund-in-transit-ai-play-fate-unknown/)).

The beauty investments show how the fund worked beyond food. Kosås, founded in 2015 by chemist and fine artist Sheena Yaitanes, had doubled its sales in 2017 without outside investment before taking the fund's money to expand distribution ([Beauty Independent](https://www.beautyindependent.com/kosas-clean-color-cosmetics-circleup-series-a-funding-round/)). Managing director Alison Ryu said the fund aimed "to partner with visionary founders who are growing their respective categories," and that clean beauty was specifically of interest because differentiated indie brands could scale fast through social media and direct-to-consumer sales ([Beauty Independent](https://www.beautyindependent.com/kosas-clean-color-cosmetics-circleup-series-a-funding-round/)). Koia's story was similar: a brand the fund had watched since 2016, whose core-line sales were up 123.5 percent ($7.6 million) year over year through February 23, 2020, according to IRI data ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2020/koia-lands-potential-last-money-with-circleup-investment)).

## The first exit and the breakup

Liquid I.V. became the fund's proof point. The hydration-powder brand was picked up by Unilever on September 1, 2020 for an undisclosed sum, after accelerating sales growth and significant direct-to-consumer distribution — the first exit for a company in the Growth Partners Fund, which had put money into the company in 2018 ([New Hope Network](https://www.newhope.com/industry-insights/unilever-s-liquid-iv-acquisition-a-proof-point-for-circleup-investment-fund)). Managing director Ben Lee said Helio had surfaced the brand through a data-driven screen flagging distribution growth and customer enthusiasm, and that CircleUp had been "the only institutional investor that the company was talking to at the time" ([New Hope Network](https://www.newhope.com/industry-insights/unilever-s-liquid-iv-acquisition-a-proof-point-for-circleup-investment-fund)).

Three years later the parent company came apart, and the fund went with it. In March 2023 CircleUp began divesting its financing vehicles: the Credit Advisors loan fund went to [Brightflow AI](/organizations/brightflow-ai/), a venture-backed financial-tracking platform that also absorbed several CircleUp team members, while the Growth Partners VC fund was in the process of being sold to an unidentified multi-strategy asset manager ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-breakup-lending-fund-sold-vc-fund-in-transit-ai-play-fate-unknown/)). Sources told NOSH the lending exit was driven by rising interest rates and a wish to lower the company's risk profile ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-breakup-lending-fund-sold-vc-fund-in-transit-ai-play-fate-unknown/)).

The rest of CircleUp followed in June 2023, when Brightflow AI acquired the company and the Helio engine. No cash was exchanged — CircleUp investors joined Brightflow AI's own cap table ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-sells-vc-funds-final-home-still-unknown/)). What happened to the venture fund remained the open question: portfolio companies had been told the book of business had been acquired by another firm, with the core investment team moving over as well, but the specific acquirer was never disclosed, and a spokesperson for the investment group said there was no update ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-sells-vc-funds-final-home-still-unknown/)). Brightflow AI said Helio would be folded into its forecasting capabilities, and its CEO argued the platform had simply been ahead of its time — "the right models, and they had the right idea… they helped hundreds of businesses and founders actually succeed" ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-sells-vc-funds-final-home-still-unknown/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What was CircleUp Growth Partners?** It was the venture fund arm of CircleUp, a San Francisco consumer-investment platform. Launched in October 2017 at $125 million, it invested directly in early-stage consumer brands, using CircleUp's Helio machine-learning platform to identify them ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/10/31/circleup-announced-125-million-venture-fund/)).

**How big was the fund and what did it invest in?** The fund was $125 million, writing $1 million to $3 million checks into US companies with $1 million to $10 million in revenue, in food and beverage and personal care — brands that make something that sits on a shelf ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2017/circleup-closes-125m-fund-powered-machine-learning/)).

**Which brands did it back?** Nearly 30 over its life, including HUM Nutrition, 4505, Kosås, Liquid I.V., Koia, Nutpods, Barnana, Rhythm Superfoods, Coconut Cult, Everything Legendary, Good Crisp and Serenity Kids ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-breakup-lending-fund-sold-vc-fund-in-transit-ai-play-fate-unknown/)).

**What happened to the fund?** In CircleUp's 2023 breakup it was sold to a multi-strategy asset manager that was never publicly identified, with the core investment team moving over; the rest of CircleUp, including Helio, was acquired by Brightflow AI ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-sells-vc-funds-final-home-still-unknown/)).
