## Overview

PayPal began in December 1998 as Confinity, a startup founded on the idea of cheap, nearly effortless digital payments between consumers and businesses — at a time when online purchases were still settled by checks and money orders sent through the mail ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/technology/history-of-paypal-15062744)). In 2000 Confinity merged with [Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk/)'s online bank X.com, and the combined company eventually took the PayPal name before going public in February 2002 — an IPO so hot the stock rose more than 50% on its first day and closed at $20 ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/company-insights/082416/top-5-companies-owned-paypal-pypl.asp)).

Eight months later eBay bought the company for $1.5 billion, and PayPal spent the next thirteen years as eBay's payments engine ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/company-insights/082416/top-5-companies-owned-paypal-pypl.asp)). Today it is independent again: 439 million account holders, $33.1 billion in 2025 revenue, and $1.79 trillion in annual payment volume ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/company-insights/082416/top-5-companies-owned-paypal-pypl.asp)).

## The activist campaign that split eBay in two

PayPal's independence was not eBay's idea — it was forced by an activist investor. In early 2014, billionaire Carl Icahn, then a major eBay shareholder, publicly demanded that eBay spin off PayPal, arguing the payments business was being held back inside the auction company ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/technology/history-of-paypal-15062744)). eBay's chief executive at the time forcefully rejected the proposal, insisting the company was better off intact — but Icahn kept up the pressure through open letters and board-nominee threats.

The turning point came in June 2014, when eBay's own directors and executives reversed course after a six-month internal study of the payments landscape concluded the split made sense ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-investments-funds-icahn/icahn-exits-ebay-stake-opts-for-paypal-after-spinoff-idUSKCN0T52SP20151117/)). The spinoff completed in mid-July 2015, and Icahn put his money where his campaign had been: regulatory filings that November showed he had swapped his entire eBay stake — 46.3 million shares — for the same number of PayPal shares, leaving him with 3.8% of the newly independent company and a board seat for his lieutenant Jonathan Christodoro ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-investments-funds-icahn/icahn-exits-ebay-stake-opts-for-paypal-after-spinoff-idUSKCN0T52SP20151117/)).

## Buying growth: Braintree, Xoom, and the $4 billion Honey deal

PayPal's post-spinoff growth has been driven as much by acquisitions as by its core checkout button. In September 2013 it paid $800 million for Braintree, the payments gateway whose crown jewel was Venmo — a bet on mobile and peer-to-peer payments that predated the spinoff ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/technology/history-of-paypal-15062744)). Weeks after independence, in July 2015, it agreed to buy international remittance firm Xoom for about $890 million to accelerate cross-border transfers ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/technology/history-of-paypal-15062744)).

The biggest swing came in November 2019, when PayPal agreed to acquire Honey Science — the shopping-rewards platform whose browser extension finds and applies coupon codes — for about $4 billion, nearly twice what it had paid for iZettle the year before ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-honeysciencecorp-m-a-paypal-hldg/paypal-to-buy-rewards-platform-honey-science-for-4-billion-idUSKBN1XU2OX/)). At the time, PayPal and Venmo together had more than 275 million active consumer accounts, and the deal was pitched as a way to make PayPal relevant earlier in the shopping journey rather than only at checkout ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-honeysciencecorp-m-a-paypal-hldg/paypal-to-buy-rewards-platform-honey-science-for-4-billion-idUSKBN1XU2OX/)). Investopedia's later tally credits PayPal with 27 acquisitions averaging $1.1 billion each — with Honey, Xoom, and Hyperwallet doing the heaviest lifting ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/company-insights/082416/top-5-companies-owned-paypal-pypl.asp)). [Peter Thiel](/people/peter-thiel/), who co-founded the company as Confinity, and Musk both exited long ago: Musk sold his shares when eBay bought the company in 2002 and has had no involvement since ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/company-insights/082416/top-5-companies-owned-paypal-pypl.asp)).
