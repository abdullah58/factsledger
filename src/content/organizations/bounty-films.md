## Overview

**Bounty Films** is an Australian home-media distributor of independent film — active in 2025 through a partnership with HALO Films to release Australian titles globally across digital platforms and make-on-demand disc ([Cinema Australia](http://cinemaaustralia.com.au/2025/01/09/bounty-to-release-first-three-halo-films-titles-worldwide/)). It entered the industry history books in 2011 as the company behind *The Human Centipede 2 (Full Sequence)*'s UK submission: when the BBFC refused the film a certificate that June, Bounty and its UK distribution partner Eureka Entertainment announced a joint appeal, and the film passed with an 18 certificate in October after 32 cuts ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/distribution/human-centipede-ii-gets-18-certificate-in-uk-after-cuts/5033015.article); [Eat My Brains](https://eatmybrains.com/shownews.php?id=1168)).

## An Australian distributor with a UK arm

Bounty Films operates from Australia as a distributor of independent cinema, with what Cinema Australia describes as expertise in home-media distribution. In January 2025 it kicked off a partnership with HALO Films to distribute a curated selection of HALO's acquired titles to home audiences worldwide — spanning transactional and ad-supported video-on-demand, selected subscription platforms, and, for select titles, make-on-demand Blu-ray and DVD sold through Bounty's own website, Amazon, MovieZyng and Walmart ([Cinema Australia](http://cinemaaustralia.com.au/2025/01/09/bounty-to-release-first-three-halo-films-titles-worldwide/)). The first wave, due in the second quarter of 2025, comprised the documentary *In The Trenches: The Making of Before Dawn*, the music documentary *Gravel Road* and the surreal drama *Violett* — Australian-made independent work aimed at audiences across Australia, New Zealand, the US, Canada, the UK and Ireland ([Cinema Australia](http://cinemaaustralia.com.au/2025/01/09/bounty-to-release-first-three-halo-films-titles-worldwide/)). "We're incredibly excited to introduce Halo Films' releases to a global audience," said Paul Anthony Nelson of Bounty Films ([Cinema Australia](http://cinemaaustralia.com.au/2025/01/09/bounty-to-release-first-three-halo-films-titles-worldwide/)). The company maintains its catalogue at bountyet.com.au ([Cinema Australia](http://cinemaaustralia.com.au/2025/01/09/bounty-to-release-first-three-halo-films-titles-worldwide/)).

## The 2011 BBFC campaign

Bounty's best-known episode came in 2011. The company was the rights-holder behind the UK submission of Tom Six's *The Human Centipede 2 (Full Sequence)*, with Eureka Entertainment acting as its UK distributor ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/distribution/human-centipede-ii-gets-18-certificate-in-uk-after-cuts/5033015.article)). When the BBFC refused the film a classification for DVD release in June 2011, the two companies issued a joint statement: "Bounty Films, and its UK distribution partner Eureka Entertainment Ltd., are disappointed by the decision of the BBFC to deny the film a classification certificate. While both companies respect the authority of the board, we strongly disagree with their decision" ([Eat My Brains](https://eatmybrains.com/shownews.php?id=1168)). The statement also took the unusual step of publicly criticising the BBFC's press release for an "unprecedented level of detail" about the film's scenes, warning it could prejudice their forthcoming appeal to the Video Appeals Committee ([Eat My Brains](https://eatmybrains.com/shownews.php?id=1168)). Bounty's Tony Romeo added that the film had been granted a certificate with no cuts in Australia, making the BBFC's decision surprising to the company ([The Raygun](http://ymlp.com/zk1ftm)). The campaign ended in October with an 18 certificate after 32 cuts totalling 2 minutes 37 seconds ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/distribution/human-centipede-ii-gets-18-certificate-in-uk-after-cuts/5033015.article)). Beyond the controversy, Bounty functioned as Eureka's "UK distributed label" that autumn, with the Korean thriller *The Yellow Sea* in UK cinemas from 21 October 2011 and Yoshihiro Nishimura's *Helldriver* on DVD and Blu-ray from 31 October ([Screen Anarchy](https://screenanarchy.com/2011/07/eureka-entertainment-announces-blu-raydvd-slate-for-fall-2011.html)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Bounty Films?**
An Australian home-media distributor of independent film. It distributes titles globally across TVOD, AVOD and selected SVOD platforms plus make-on-demand Blu-ray and DVD, and maintains its catalogue at bountyet.com.au. Its best-known episode was the 2011 UK campaign for *The Human Centipede 2*.

**What was Bounty Films' role in the Human Centipede 2 BBFC case?**
Bounty was the company behind the film's UK submission, with Eureka Entertainment as its UK distribution partner. When the BBFC refused a certificate in June 2011, the two issued a joint statement disagreeing with the decision, criticising the BBFC's unusually detailed press release, and announcing an appeal to the Video Appeals Committee.

**Who spoke for Bounty Films during the 2011 campaign?**
Tony Romeo, quoted in the trade press at the time, noted that the film had been granted a certificate with no cuts in Australia — which made the BBFC's refusal surprising to the company. In 2025 reporting on the HALO Films partnership, the company's spokesman was Paul Anthony Nelson.

**What is the connection between Bounty Films and Eureka Entertainment?**
In 2011, Eureka acted as Bounty Films' UK distributor, fronting the BBFC negotiations through its sales director Ian Sadler. Bounty simultaneously functioned as Eureka's "UK distributed label" for other titles, including *The Yellow Sea* and *Helldriver* in autumn 2011.

**Is Bounty Films still active?**
Yes. In January 2025, Cinema Australia reported its new distribution partnership with HALO Films, with the first three titles due across digital and disc platforms in the second quarter of 2025.
