## Overview

Netflix's most important year in recent memory was not 2007, when streaming began, or 1997, when the company was founded. It was 2026. In February of that year, Netflix walked away from the largest acquisition it had ever negotiated — an $82.7 billion bid for [Warner](https://factsledger.com/organizations/warner-bros/) Bros. Discovery's film, television and streaming assets — rather than overpay in a bidding war ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/warner-bros-netflix-paramount-acquisition-timeline-wbd/)). And in June, co-founder [Reed Hastings](https://factsledger.com/people/reed-hastings/) stepped down from the company's board, closing the book on nearly three decades of his formal leadership ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/netflix-names-longtime-director-jay-hoag-chairman-succeeding-reed-hastings-2026-06-05/)). The company is now led by co-chief executives Ted Sarandos and Greg Peters, with longtime director Jay Hoag — a board member since 1999 — as chairman ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/netflix-names-longtime-director-jay-hoag-chairman-succeeding-reed-hastings-2026-06-05/); [Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/netflix-chairman-to-step-down-as-streamer-posts-strong-q1-results)).

Behind those headlines sits the business the world already knows: the subscription streaming service that began as a DVD-by-mail rental operation in Scotts Valley, California, founded by Hastings and Marc Randolph in 1997 ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-when-netflix-start-brief-company-history/)). It is a public company, operating originals, licensed films and series, an ad-supported tier, video podcasts, games and live programming — with about $20 billion earmarked for films and series in 2026 alone ([Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/netflix-chairman-to-step-down-as-streamer-posts-strong-q1-results)).

## The end of the Hastings era

Netflix announced the change in April 2026, in a letter to shareholders: Hastings would not stand for re-election when his term expired at the annual meeting in June, and he planned to focus on philanthropy and other pursuits ([Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/netflix-chairman-to-step-down-as-streamer-posts-strong-q1-results)). He described his time at Netflix as "life-changing," pointing to the company's 2016 global expansion as a defining moment, and said his contribution was less about any single decision than about building a culture centered on member satisfaction and long-term success ([Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/netflix-chairman-to-step-down-as-streamer-posts-strong-q1-results)). Sarandos called Hastings a "singular source of inspiration," while Peters credited his leadership with shaping the company's culture and long-term direction ([Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/netflix-chairman-to-step-down-as-streamer-posts-strong-q1-results)).

The transition became official after the annual shareholders meeting on June 4, when Hoag assumed the role — a move Netflix disclosed in an SEC filing ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/netflix-names-longtime-director-jay-hoag-chairman-succeeding-reed-hastings-2026-06-05/)). Hoag co-founded Technology Crossover Ventures, a venture capital firm that has invested in Netflix for many years, and he had served on Netflix's board since 1999 — including more than a decade as lead independent director ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/netflix-names-longtime-director-jay-hoag-chairman-succeeding-reed-hastings-2026-06-05/)). He also sits on the boards of Zillow Group and Peloton Interactive ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/netflix-names-longtime-director-jay-hoag-chairman-succeeding-reed-hastings-2026-06-05/)).

The leadership update arrived alongside stronger-than-expected first-quarter 2026 results. Revenue rose 16% year on year, driven by membership growth, pricing adjustments and increasing advertising income; operating income climbed 18% to $4 billion, and operating margin reached 32.3% ([Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/netflix-chairman-to-step-down-as-streamer-posts-strong-q1-results)). Netflix maintained its full-year outlook of $50.7 billion to $51.7 billion in revenue with a 31.5% operating margin ([Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/netflix-chairman-to-step-down-as-streamer-posts-strong-q1-results)). Advertising has become a serious business: Netflix expects its ad unit to reach approximately $3 billion in 2026 — roughly double the previous year — with the ad-supported tier accounting for more than 60% of sign-ups in markets where it is available ([Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/netflix-chairman-to-step-down-as-streamer-posts-strong-q1-results)).

The company is also pushing into new territory. It is expanding its use of generative AI tools after acquiring InterPositive, redesigning its mobile interface with vertical video, and broadening beyond film and TV into video podcasts, gaming and live programming — its first regional live event, the World Baseball Classic, drew record viewership in Japan, while a BTS event attracted millions globally ([Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/netflix-chairman-to-step-down-as-streamer-posts-strong-q1-results)). Even so, Netflix says it accounts for only about 5% of global TV view share — which it reads as room to grow against rivals spanning Amazon, Apple and TikTok ([Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/netflix-chairman-to-step-down-as-streamer-posts-strong-q1-results)).

## The [Warner Bros.](/organizations/warner-bros/) Discovery bidding war

The contest began in the autumn of 2025, when Warner Bros. Discovery said it was exploring a sale after receiving unsolicited interest from several major players ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/warner-bros-netflix-paramount-acquisition-timeline-wbd/)). On October 21, 2025, WBD's board rejected a Paramount Skydance offer of nearly $60 billion, or $24 a share — and within weeks, Paramount Skydance, Comcast and Netflix had all submitted preliminary bids ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/paramount-skydance-and-warner-bros-discovery-merger-a-timeline-of-the-110-billion-deal/article71494273.ece)).

Netflix won the first round. On December 5, 2025, it agreed to buy WBD's film and television studios and streaming division for $72 billion, or $27.75 a share ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/paramount-skydance-and-warner-bros-discovery-merger-a-timeline-of-the-110-billion-deal/article71494273.ece)); the overall transaction was valued at $82.7 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/warner-bros-netflix-paramount-acquisition-timeline-wbd/)). Paramount Skydance went hostile four days later with a $108.4 billion bid ($30 a share), which WBD's board rejected on December 17 — and rejected again on January 7, 2026, even after Larry Ellison attached a $40.4 billion personal guarantee ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/paramount-skydance-and-warner-bros-discovery-merger-a-timeline-of-the-110-billion-deal/article71494273.ece)). On January 20, 2026, Netflix sweetened its own position by amending its agreement to an all-cash offer at $27.75 a share, winning unanimous WBD board approval without raising the $82.7 billion purchase price ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/paramount-skydance-and-warner-bros-discovery-merger-a-timeline-of-the-110-billion-deal/article71494273.ece); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/warner-bros-netflix-paramount-acquisition-timeline-wbd/)).

Paramount Skydance refused to quit. Its leaders raised the offer to $31 a share on February 24, and after the WBD board judged that bid superior, Netflix declined to match it on February 26 ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/paramount-skydance-and-warner-bros-discovery-merger-a-timeline-of-the-110-billion-deal/article71494273.ece)). Sarandos and Peters explained the decision as discipline: "The transaction we negotiated would have created shareholder value with a clear path to regulatory approval," they said, "However, we've always been disciplined, and at the price required to match Paramount Skydance's latest offer, the deal is no longer financially attractive, so we are declining to match the Paramount Skydance bid" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/warner-bros-netflix-paramount-acquisition-timeline-wbd/)). The next day, Paramount paid the $2.8 billion termination fee Netflix had been owed, and WBD entered an agreement to be acquired by Paramount Skydance at $81 billion in equity value, expected to close in the third quarter of 2026 ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/paramount-skydance-and-warner-bros-discovery-merger-a-timeline-of-the-110-billion-deal/article71494273.ece)).

The deal's aftermath has been messy. U.S. senators had grilled Sarandos on February 3, 2026 over how Netflix's proposed acquisition would affect competition ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/paramount-skydance-and-warner-bros-discovery-merger-a-timeline-of-the-110-billion-deal/article71494273.ece)). After the Paramount agreement, the Justice Department's Antitrust Division cleared the transaction on June 12 — but on July 13, a coalition of 12 state attorneys general, led by California's Rob Bonta, sued to block it, arguing it would lessen competition and harm movie theaters, cable distributors and viewers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/warner-bros-netflix-paramount-acquisition-timeline-wbd/); [The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/paramount-skydance-and-warner-bros-discovery-merger-a-timeline-of-the-110-billion-deal/article71494273.ece)). A federal judge ordered pauses on the closing, the Writers Guild of America filed its own suit, and European and British regulators granted approvals on July 22 and August 6 ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/paramount-skydance-and-warner-bros-discovery-merger-a-timeline-of-the-110-billion-deal/article71494273.ece)). On September 21, 2026, Paramount settled with California and 11 other states — agreeing to spend at least $300 million more each year on domestic production and to progressively increase annual film releases — with independent content monitoring of CNN reported as one of the terms under discussion ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/variety/paramount-skydance-and-warner-bros-discovery-merger-a-timeline-of-the-110-billion-deal/article71494273.ece)).

## From red envelopes to streaming

The company that just outbid Hollywood was born in a Santa Cruz carpool. In early 1997, Hastings — then CEO of Pure Atria — and Randolph, its vice president of corporate marketing, faced a looming merger that would leave them both out of a job; on their commutes, Randolph pitched e-commerce ideas until the pair settled on renting movies on the brand-new DVD format ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-when-netflix-start-brief-company-history/)). To test whether discs could survive the mail, they bought a Patsy Cline CD at a Santa Cruz record store and posted it to Hastings's house; it arrived intact ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-when-netflix-start-brief-company-history/)). On August 29, 1997, Netflix was registered, set up with at least $1.9 million from Hastings — who became chairman with 70% ownership — while Randolph became CEO and a minority owner ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-when-netflix-start-brief-company-history/)).

Hastings has often said the idea came from a $40 fine he paid for returning the movie Apollo 13 to Blockbuster six weeks late ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-when-netflix-start-brief-company-history/)). Randolph says that story is false — a marketing gag — and the carpool brainstorming was the true origin ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-when-netflix-start-brief-company-history/)). Either way, the pitch was the opposite of the fine: order movies online, receive them by mail, keep them as long as you like. The website launched on April 14, 1998, with a library of about 900 titles and booked 137 orders on its first day ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-when-netflix-start-brief-company-history/)). Netflix settled on a subscription model with no due dates or late fees and unlimited access at $19.95, built around a movie queue and a system that mailed the next DVD as soon as the previous one came back; the discs arrived in postage-paid return sleeves, with Netflix covering the postage ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-when-netflix-start-brief-company-history/)).

In 2000, still unprofitable, Hastings and Randolph flew to meet Blockbuster CEO John Antioco and offered to sell Netflix for $50 million; Antioco turned them down ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-when-netflix-start-brief-company-history/)). Netflix reached one million subscribers in 2001, went public in May 2002 — raising $82.1 million at a $309.7 million valuation — and Randolph left the company in 2003, selling his shares ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-when-netflix-start-brief-company-history/)). By 2006 it was profitable, earning more than $80 million with 6.3 million subscribers ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-when-netflix-start-brief-company-history/)).

The pivotal turn came in 2007, when Netflix began streaming through its Watch Now service — 1,000 titles, included in the $5.99-a-month DVD tier, first trialed in Canada ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-when-netflix-start-brief-company-history/)). The DVD business that had defined it was soon the sideshow: Blockbuster filed for bankruptcy in 2010, and in 2011 Netflix split streaming and DVD rentals into separate plans — then, after 800,000 subscribers walked away, reversed the Qwikster DVD spinoff within a month ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-when-netflix-start-brief-company-history/)). Original programming arrived in 2012 with Lilyhammer, followed by House of Cards in 2013; Netflix went live in 130 countries at once in 2016 and crossed 100 million subscribers in 2017 ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-when-netflix-start-brief-company-history/)). The modern playbook — crack down on password sharing, launch a cheaper ad-supported tier — dates to 2022, when the company lost 200,000 subscribers in the first quarter, its first subscriber decline in more than a decade ([MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-when-netflix-start-brief-company-history/)).
