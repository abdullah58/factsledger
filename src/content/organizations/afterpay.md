## Overview

Afterpay is the company that put "buy now, pay later" on the global map. Founded in Sydney in 2014 by Nick Molnar and Anthony Eisen, it let shoppers split purchases into instalments with no interest — merchant fees, not consumer interest, paid the bills. From a A$25 million IPO in May 2016, it became one of Australia's 20 most valuable companies by August 2020, hit a lifetime share high of A$160.05 in February 2021, and was bought by Block (then Square) in a deal announced at $29 billion in August 2021 — among the biggest takeovers in Australian history ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/afterpays-meteoric-rise-lending-pioneer-2022-01-20/)).

## The $29 billion exit

The arc was pandemic-fuelled. Afterpay shares "tanked" in March 2020 as COVID hit, then soared as lockdowns moved shopping online; in between, Tencent bought 5% (May 2020), Australia's financial-crime watchdog ordered an anti-money-laundering audit (June 2019), and an external review found "low risk" of breaches (November 2019) ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/afterpays-meteoric-rise-lending-pioneer-2022-01-20/)). The Block deal became unconditional in January 2022, Afterpay shares were delisted from the S&P/ASX 200 and replaced by Block's Australia-listed stock, and its financials were folded into Block's reporting from that quarter ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/afterpays-meteoric-rise-lending-pioneer-2022-01-20/)).

## Current status: a feature, not a company

Afterpay no longer exists as an independent company — it is an operating unit of Block, progressively absorbed into Cash App. The latest evidence of that absorption: Afterpay Pre-Purchase, which lets a Cash App Card user convert a debit charge into instalments at checkout, reached general availability in 2026, and Cash App commerce enablement volume grew 17% in the quarter ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/coverage/filings/2026/08/25/the-afterpay-co-founder-on-block-s-board-moved-18-000-shares-under-a-plan-set-in-march-here-s-what-to-know/)). Eisen, who ran Afterpay as co-CEO until the sale, joined Block's board in February 2025, converting his stake into Block stock ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/coverage/filings/2026/08/25/the-afterpay-co-founder-on-block-s-board-moved-18-000-shares-under-a-plan-set-in-march-here-s-what-to-know/)).

The pressure point in the combination is credit: Block's consumer lending originations grew 59% in Q2 2026, with the CFO expecting that pace to normalise as borrower cohorts season — "where consumer strain would surface before it reaches gross profit" ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/coverage/filings/2026/08/25/the-afterpay-co-founder-on-block-s-board-moved-18-000-shares-under-a-plan-set-in-march-here-s-what-to-know/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim a definitive final deal value — the all-stock price moved with Block's share price and is commonly reported around US$29 billion / A$39 billion. It does not claim the Afterpay brand will survive as a standalone product indefinitely; the evidence points to absorption into Cash App. Regulatory treatment of BNPL in Australia (credit licensing) is evolving and is not adjudicated here.
