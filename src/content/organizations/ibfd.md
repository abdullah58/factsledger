## Overview

The International Bureau of Fiscal Documentation (IBFD) is an independent non-profit foundation based in Amsterdam that researches, publishes and teaches cross-border taxation ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/about-ibfd)). Founded in 1938, it runs the Tax Research Platform, a database covering more than 220 jurisdictions ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/ibfd-landmark-partnership-international-tax)), and a tax library whose collection spans more than a century ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/library-services)). Its content also powers [Blue J](/organizations/blue-j/)'s AI tax research platform ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/news/ibfd-launches-blue-js-genai-platform)).

IBFD describes itself as the world's foremost authority on cross-border taxation and "the home of international taxation", a description echoed independently: the Prince Claus Fund calls it "the leading independent authority on international taxation" ([Prince Claus Fund](https://princeclausfund.nl/support-us/corporate-donors/ibfd)). Its stated mission is to promote and disseminate the understanding of cross-border taxation at the highest level, through research, publishing and education ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/about-ibfd)). The same expertise feeds a subscription database, a public research library, academic journals, training, and advice to governments. IBFD is a foundation, not a company, and it presents its independence — it is not a law firm, an accounting firm or a government body — as the basis for its authority ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/about-ibfd)). Its clients are governments, tax advisers and corporate clients, served by more than 250 employees from over 40 countries and a network of more than 700 authors and correspondents worldwide, across four offices on three continents ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/about-ibfd)).

## From a 1938 foundation to a global research institution

IBFD was established in 1938 in Amsterdam, and it has operated continuously from the city ever since, now from Rietlandpark 301 ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/about-ibfd)). Little about its earliest decades could be verified from sources read in full for this profile — the founding year itself is confirmed three ways, by IBFD, by the Prince Claus Fund and by Blue J, but the founders' names and the bureau's original size are not stated here because no fully-read source confirmed them ([Prince Claus Fund](https://princeclausfund.nl/support-us/corporate-donors/ibfd)). What the record does show is steady institutional growth from a documentation bureau into a research institute: by the 2020s it employed over 250 people from more than 40 countries, drew on 700-plus authors and correspondents, and ran four offices on three continents ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/about-ibfd)). The Prince Claus Fund, which has counted IBFD as a dedicated partner since 2015, notes that under its 2025 strategy IBFD is strengthening its role as the "Home of International Taxation", making tax knowledge more accessible, actionable and impactful ([Prince Claus Fund](https://princeclausfund.nl/support-us/corporate-donors/ibfd)).

## The Tax Research Platform: what it actually contains

The core of IBFD today is its Tax Research Platform, an online database that presents worldwide tax information in collections arranged by depth, from quick-reference tables to full country analyses, with an archive of historical versions ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/tax-research-platform)). The platform's scale is its distinguishing feature: no other single source assembles this much comparative tax material in one place, and university law libraries treat it as a core tool for comparing tax law across countries ([University of Melbourne Law Library](https://unimelb.libguides.com/global_comparative_tax_law)). Its contents, as IBFD states them, are:

| Collection | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| Tax tables | Over 1,500 country-based tables for quick answers |
| Country Tax Guides | Over 1,300 chapters detailing each jurisdiction's tax system |
| Primary sources | Over 13,000 documents from national governments and international organizations |
| Case law | Over 15,000 summaries of national court decisions and CJEU rulings |
| Tax treaties and models | Over 15,000 treaty and model documents |
| Journal articles and opinion pieces | Over 11,000 articles from IBFD's journals |
| Books | Over 300 reference works, including on tax accounting and BEPS |

The platform is built for comparison, not just lookup. Its Country Tax Guide tool places corresponding sections of two countries side by side, its Country Tables Comparison tool compares topics across many countries, and its Digital Taxation Monitor Map visualizes which digital taxation measures countries have implemented or are working on ([University of Melbourne Law Library](https://unimelb.libguides.com/global_comparative_tax_law)). It also carries model tax treaties — current and some superseded versions from the United States, Belgium, Germany, the Netherlands and Russia, and from the [United Nations](/organizations/united-nations/), the OECD, the Andean Community, ASEAN and ILADT — which matter because model treaties are the starting point for negotiations between countries ([Georgetown Law Library](http://guides.ll.georgetown.edu/c.php?g=363487&p=4199475)). Coverage extends to [European Union](/organizations/european-union/) law and case law as well ([University of Melbourne Law Library](https://unimelb.libguides.com/global_comparative_tax_law)). For fast-moving topics, IBFD publishes Tax Dossiers that gather the main features, implementation status and expected impact of a hot topic with links to news, primary sources, articles and tools, updated as new information arrives ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/tax-research-platform)).

## The library, the journals and the classroom

Alongside the database sits the IBFD Library and Information Centre, an internationally recognized research facility for cross-border taxation whose collection covers source materials from almost every country in the world and spans more than a century, much of it in the original languages ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/library-services)). It is a working library, not an archive: IBFD staff, students and researchers, consultants, law firms, advisory firms, companies and governments all use it for research on international taxation, tax structuring and tax management, and it is open to visitors Monday to Friday, 09:00 to 17:00 CET, with study spaces, document delivery and research guides ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/library-services)). IBFD's journals include the World Tax Journal and the International Tax Studies Journal, both now also distributed through Research4Life to institutions in low- and middle-income countries ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/news/ibfd-partners-research4life)). Education is the third leg of the mission statement — research, publishing and education — though the specifics of its current courses and qualifications were not verified from a fully-read source for this profile ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/about-ibfd)).

## Governance and leadership

IBFD is governed as a foundation. An Executive Board is responsible for achieving its objectives under the mission statement, and a Board of Trustees oversees overall management ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/about-ibfd)). The trustees are drawn from tax practice, government and business around the world, and the board is chaired by Prof. Dr. DDr. h.c. Michael Lang, head of the Institute for Austrian and International Tax Law at WU Vienna; its members include Stef van Weeghel, professor of international tax law at the University of Amsterdam, former Nigerian minister Oby Ezekwesili, Vodafone head of tax Rebecca Roes, and Juliane Kokott, Advocate General at the Court of Justice of the European Union ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/about-ibfd)). The chief executive is Jan Maarten Slagter, in post since January 2017 ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/about/ibfd-executive-board)). Slagter read law at Leiden, graduating in 1993, worked as a corporate lawyer, then spent a decade in financial journalism before leading the Dutch investors' association VEB from 2007 to 2014 ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/about/ibfd-executive-board)). The academic side is led by Prof. Dr Pasquale Pistone, Academic Chairman, who holds a Jean Monnet chair in European tax law and policy at WU Vienna and edits IBFD's World Tax Journal, International Tax Studies, Doctoral Series and Global Tax Treaty Commentaries ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/about/ibfd-executive-board)). Belema R. Obuoforibo, Director of the IBFD Knowledge Centre and an Executive Board member, chairs the Centre for Studies in African Taxation, sits on the UN Subcommittee on Wealth and Solidarity Taxes, and directs the annual Africa Tax Symposium ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/about/ibfd-executive-board)).

## Into the AI era: the Blue J partnership

The most consequential recent development for IBFD is its move into generative AI through Blue J, the Toronto tax-research company. Under a partnership announced in 2025, IBFD licensed its content — covering 220-plus jurisdictions — to Blue J, with access opening to a select group of users late in 2025 and general availability following in early 2026 ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/ibfd-landmark-partnership-international-tax)). The integration went live on 25 March 2026, putting IBFD's treaty analysis, country commentary and international tax guidance in front of tens of thousands of Blue J users, with databases updated in real time ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/news/ibfd-launches-blue-js-genai-platform)). The practical point, as Blue J puts it, is that practitioners can now ask natural-language questions and receive answers with citations to treaties, case law and regulatory guidance — evaluating permanent-establishment risk when entering a new market, assessing compliance with global minimum tax rules, or comparing transfer pricing across jurisdictions ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/ibfd-landmark-partnership-international-tax)). Blue J chief executive Benjamin Alarie called it "the first of several major content partnerships that will redefine speed, confidence, and reliability in cross-border tax research", while IBFD's Slagter said that "for 87 years, IBFD has set the standard for depth and insight in cross-border taxation" and that the partnership makes its content more actionable ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/ibfd-landmark-partnership-international-tax)). The market reaction was immediate: Vicki Heard, Group Head of Tax and Legal Services at KPMG UK and Switzerland, said her teams would be able to "instantly access reliable answers on complex matters, complete with citations from the sources to back them up and links to IBFD's Tax Research Platform" ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/ibfd-landmark-partnership-international-tax)). For a foundation founded in 1938 on paper documentation, licensing its corpus to an AI research platform founded in 2015 — a platform that says it has answered more than 6 million tax questions — is a sharp change in distribution, but not in purpose: the same content, delivered where practitioners now ask their questions ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/news/ibfd-launches-blue-js-genai-platform)).

## Tax knowledge beyond the profession

IBFD's mission reaches beyond paying subscribers. On 25 June 2025 it joined Research4Life as a contributing publisher, giving eligible institutions in low- and middle-income countries free or low-cost access to the World Tax Journal, the International Tax Studies Journal and its Open Archive Books collection ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/news/ibfd-partners-research4life)). Research4Life supports more than 11,500 institutions across over 125 countries, and IBFD's titles sit in its GOALI collection, which covers academic and peer-reviewed journals, publications and databases on law and social sciences ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/news/ibfd-partners-research4life)). IBFD's own framing is that universities and policymakers in developing economies can now use its publications to strengthen tax systems and support fair taxation and compliance ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/news/ibfd-partners-research4life)). The same outward-facing logic appears in its longest-standing partnership: since 2015 IBFD has been a dedicated partner of the Prince Claus Fund, which supports artists and cultural practitioners in challenging environments, and the two marked a decade of collaboration in 2025 ([Prince Claus Fund](https://princeclausfund.nl/support-us/corporate-donors/ibfd)). The Fund credits IBFD's tax expertise with helping cultural organizations thrive and governments invest in the arts — a reminder that IBFD presents itself as knowledge infrastructure for public reform, not just a database vendor ([Prince Claus Fund](https://princeclausfund.nl/support-us/corporate-donors/ibfd)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is IBFD?** IBFD is the International Bureau of Fiscal Documentation, an independent non-profit foundation founded in Amsterdam in 1938 that researches, publishes and teaches cross-border taxation ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/about-ibfd)). The Prince Claus Fund describes it as the leading independent authority on international taxation, serving governments, tax advisers and corporate clients ([Prince Claus Fund](https://princeclausfund.nl/support-us/corporate-donors/ibfd)).

**What does the IBFD Tax Research Platform cover?** The platform covers tax information for more than 220 jurisdictions, including over 1,300 Country Tax Guide chapters, 13,000 primary sources, 15,000 case-law summaries and 15,000 treaty and model documents ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/tax-research-platform)). University law libraries use it to compare tax law between countries, including through side-by-side country comparisons and a digital taxation monitor ([University of Melbourne Law Library](https://unimelb.libguides.com/global_comparative_tax_law)).

**Who runs IBFD?** Jan Maarten Slagter has been chief executive since January 2017, and Pasquale Pistone is Academic Chairman ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/about/ibfd-executive-board)). A Board of Trustees chaired by Michael Lang of WU Vienna oversees overall management, and an Executive Board is responsible for delivering the mission ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/about-ibfd)).

**How can I access IBFD research without a subscription?** Eligible institutions in low- and middle-income countries can access the World Tax Journal, the International Tax Studies Journal and IBFD's Open Archive Books free or at low cost through Research4Life, which supports more than 11,500 institutions ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/news/ibfd-partners-research4life)). Researchers can also visit the IBFD Library and Information Centre in Amsterdam, open Monday to Friday, 09:00 to 17:00 CET ([IBFD](https://www.ibfd.org/library-services)).
