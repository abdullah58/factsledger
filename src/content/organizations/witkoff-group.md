## Overview

The Witkoff Group is the family real estate empire behind one of Donald Trump's most important negotiators. Founded in 1997 by Steven Witkoff — a Bronx-born lawyer-turned-developer who has been Trump's friend since a 1986 deal — the firm describes itself as "one part developer, one part investor (and) one part landscape-changer," with more than 70 properties across major U.S. and overseas business districts ([NPR](https://www.wclk.com/2025-02-18/who-is-steve-witkoff-trumps-middle-east-envoy-is-a-real-estate-mogul-and-old-friend)). By 2025 the company was effectively a family operation: Witkoff's son Alex ran it as chief executive while his son Zach served as executive vice president of development ([NPR](https://www.wclk.com/2025-02-18/who-is-steve-witkoff-trumps-middle-east-envoy-is-a-real-estate-mogul-and-old-friend)). When Witkoff became Trump's special Middle East envoy in November 2024, the firm's assets — and his own entanglements with the Trump family's crypto ventures — made his divestments a matter of public scrutiny ([ABC News](https://jambroadcasting.com/steve-witkoff-earned-nearly-107m-from-trump-affiliated-crypto-venture-disclosures-show/)).

## The firm and its scale

Steven Witkoff practiced real estate law before going into development, and the Witkoff Group was his vehicle from 1997 onward ([NPR](https://www.wclk.com/2025-02-18/who-is-steve-witkoff-trumps-middle-east-envoy-is-a-real-estate-mogul-and-old-friend)). In the company's own 2013 account — published alongside its biggest hotel acquisition — the firm said that over the prior 15 years it had acquired more than 60 properties totaling about 18 million square feet in major central business districts across the United States and London, covering office buildings, hotels and more than 12,000 residential units at a total cost of roughly $7 billion ([Hotel Executive](https://www.hotelexecutive.com/newswire/47146/investment-consortium-acquires-new-yorks-helmsley-park-lane-hotel-for-660-million)). The firm keeps offices in New York, Los Angeles and Miami, and Forbes estimated Witkoff's personal wealth at $1 billion in 2024 ([NPR](https://www.wclk.com/2025-02-18/who-is-steve-witkoff-trumps-middle-east-envoy-is-a-real-estate-mogul-and-old-friend)).

## A family operation

The Witkoff Group's leadership chart reads like a family tree. NPR reported that Witkoff remains the firm's chairman, his son Alex is the chief executive officer, and his son Zach is the executive vice president of development ([NPR](https://www.wclk.com/2025-02-18/who-is-steve-witkoff-trumps-middle-east-envoy-is-a-real-estate-mogul-and-old-friend)). The family ties run through the Trump relationship too: at the 2024 Republican National Convention, Witkoff called Trump "my dear friend" and recounted that Trump showed up for him after his son Andrew died of an opioid overdose in 2011 ([NPR](https://www.wclk.com/2025-02-18/who-is-steve-witkoff-trumps-middle-east-envoy-is-a-real-estate-mogul-and-old-friend)). Zach's son, born in December 2024, was named Don James after the president ([NPR](https://www.wclk.com/2025-02-18/who-is-steve-witkoff-trumps-middle-east-envoy-is-a-real-estate-mogul-and-old-friend)). [Steve Witkoff](/people/steve-witkoff/) and [Zach Witkoff](/people/zach-witkoff/) each have their own profiles on this site.

## Trophy deals

Two purchases defined the firm's New York footprint. In 1998, the Witkoff Group partnered with Rubie Schron's Cammeby's International to acquire the landmark Woolworth Building ([CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/briefs/witkoff-cammebys-secure-279m-blackstone-loan-for-woolworth/)). The partners later sold the top 30 floors to Alchemy Properties for $68 million in 2012 for a luxury-condo conversion, while keeping the office portion — which they refinanced in 2026 with a $278.9 million Blackstone loan arranged by Iron Hound Management ([CRE Daily](https://www.credaily.com/briefs/witkoff-cammebys-secure-279m-blackstone-loan-for-woolworth/)).

The larger splash came on November 27, 2013, when a consortium led by the Witkoff Group closed on the Helmsley Park Lane Hotel at 36 Central Park South for $660 million ([Hotel Executive](https://www.hotelexecutive.com/newswire/47146/investment-consortium-acquires-new-yorks-helmsley-park-lane-hotel-for-660-million)). The majority backer was Jynwel Capital, and the other partners were New Valley LLC, Highgate Holdings and Macklowe Properties; the seller was the Leona M. and Harry B. Helmsley Charitable Trust ([Hotel Executive](https://www.hotelexecutive.com/newswire/47146/investment-consortium-acquires-new-yorks-helmsley-park-lane-hotel-for-660-million)). The 47-story, 605-key, 446,000-square-foot hotel was to be upgraded and rebranded as the Park Lane New York, with financing from Wells Fargo and Criterion Real Estate Capital ([Hotel Executive](https://www.hotelexecutive.com/newswire/47146/investment-consortium-acquires-new-yorks-helmsley-park-lane-hotel-for-660-million)).

## The envoy-era entanglement

Witkoff's appointment as special Middle East envoy in November 2024 — a week after Trump's election, a reflection of the two men's closeness — put his business holdings under a microscope ([NPR](https://www.wclk.com/2025-02-18/who-is-steve-witkoff-trumps-middle-east-envoy-is-a-real-estate-mogul-and-old-friend)). The sharpest scrutiny came from World Liberty Financial, the cryptocurrency firm he co-founded with Trump and members of their families ([ABC News](https://jambroadcasting.com/steve-witkoff-earned-nearly-107m-from-trump-affiliated-crypto-venture-disclosures-show/)). According to financial disclosures obtained by a government watchdog and reported by ABC News in September 2026, Witkoff made nearly $107 million in 2025 from a holding company affiliated with World Liberty; he had at one point been listed as a "co-founder emeritus" on the company's website, a title he shared with Trump ([ABC News](https://jambroadcasting.com/steve-witkoff-earned-nearly-107m-from-trump-affiliated-crypto-venture-disclosures-show/)). The firm is operated by Zach Witkoff and Trump's three sons, and it has secured preliminary approval from the Trump administration to operate as a bank ([ABC News](https://jambroadcasting.com/steve-witkoff-earned-nearly-107m-from-trump-affiliated-crypto-venture-disclosures-show/)).

World Liberty has repeatedly brushed aside conflict-of-interest concerns: in May 2025 it said Witkoff was in the process of divesting, with "no operational role, no financial interest in WLFI deals, and no influence on day-to-day decisions" ([ABC News](https://jambroadcasting.com/steve-witkoff-earned-nearly-107m-from-trump-affiliated-crypto-venture-disclosures-show/)). In September 2026, White House spokesperson Anna Kelly told ABC News that Witkoff has now "fully divested from World Liberty Financial, and his prior commercial activities are completely unrelated to his efforts to end global conflicts on the President's behalf" ([ABC News](https://jambroadcasting.com/steve-witkoff-earned-nearly-107m-from-trump-affiliated-crypto-venture-disclosures-show/)). Trump himself reported earning $526 million from World Liberty token sales and another $65 million from equity sales, per financial disclosures filed in the summer of 2026 ([ABC News](https://jambroadcasting.com/steve-witkoff-earned-nearly-107m-from-trump-affiliated-crypto-venture-disclosures-show/)).
