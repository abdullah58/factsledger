## Overview

AARP Financial Services Corp. is the for-profit, wholly owned subsidiary of [AARP](/organizations/aarp/) that holds the organization's real estate — the little-known property arm of the nearly 38-million-member nonprofit. ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/a/aarp.asp)) Its earliest documented role was in mutual funds: in 1984 it joined Scudder, Stevens & Clark as a general partner of the AARP/Scudder Financial Services Company, the vehicle behind a 15-fund AARP mutual-fund family. ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://pdf.secdatabase.com/1440/0000088053-98-000070.pdf))

The SEC filing identifies FSC as "a whollyowned subsidiary corporation of AARP" and dates the partnership agreement to October 9, 1984. The property at the center of FSC's later role is AARP's [Washington, D.C.](/places/district-of-columbia/) headquarters, which the organization bought for roughly $206 million, according to a 2011 congressional investigative report. ([House Committee on Ways and Means](https://longtermcareinsurancepartner.com/system/refinery/resources/W1siZiIsIjIwMTcvMTIvMjEvMjFfMTVfMDJfOTE3X0FBUlBfV2F5c19hbmRfTWVhbnNfQ29tbWl0dGVlX3JlcG9ydC5wZGYiXV0/AARP%20Ways%20and%20Means%20Committee%20report.pdf))

FSC must not be confused with AARP Financial, Inc., a separately incorporated subsidiary of [AARP Services, Inc.](/organizations/aarp-services/) that advised the AARP Funds mutual funds from 2005 to 2010 before going dormant. ([AARP Financial, Inc. disclosure brochure](https://storage.googleapis.com/advisorfinder/brochures/316492/20251210_102230.pdf))

## From mutual funds to real estate: FSC's two lives

FSC's earliest documented role has nothing to do with property. A 1998 SEC filing by the AARP Tax Free Income Trust recites that "Scudder and AARP Financial Services Corp., a whollyowned subsidiary corporation of AARP, are general partners of AARP/Scudder Financial Services Company," under a partnership agreement dated October 9, 1984. That partnership — between AARP's subsidiary and Scudder, Stevens & Clark — powered the AARP Investment Program from Scudder, a family of 15 no-load mutual funds spread across five trusts, including the AARP Income Trust and the AARP Growth Trust. The filing's signatories included Cuyler W. Findlay for Scudder and Cynthia Birchfield for AARP. Decades later, the financial press describes a different company: Investopedia's AARP overview lists "AARP Financial Services" as the for-profit affiliate that "holds AARP real estate." ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/a/aarp.asp)) The surviving public record does not show when the mutual-fund role ended and the property role began — that transition is undocumented in the sources read for this profile.

## Inside AARP's corporate family

AARP, Inc. — founded in 1958 by retired educator Dr. Ethel Percy Andrus — is a nonprofit, nonpartisan 501(c)(4) organization with nearly 38 million members and a reputation as one of the strongest lobbying groups in the [United States](/places/united-states/). ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/a/aarp.asp)) Its corporate family splits along tax lines: tax-exempt charities on one side, taxable for-profit subsidiaries on the other. A March 2011 investigative report by the House Committee on Ways and Means mapped the 2010 structure, noting that "AARP Properties LLC and various other taxable affiliated entities and properties comprised the AARP enterprise in 2010." ([House Committee on Ways and Means](https://longtermcareinsurancepartner.com/system/refinery/resources/W1siZiIsIjIwMTcvMTIvMjEvMjFfMTVfMDJfOTE3X0FBUlBfV2F5c19hbmRfTWVhbnNfQ29tbWl0dGVlX3JlcG9ydC5wZGYiXV0/AARP%20Ways%20and%20Means%20Committee%20report.pdf)) The roster below places FSC in that structure — and separates it from the similarly named AARP Financial, Inc., which the same report identifies as a taxable subsidiary of AARP Services, Inc. UnitedHealth Group, AARP's largest business partner at the time, accounted for nearly two-thirds of its royalty payments — a reminder of the commercial scale these affiliates serve. ([House Committee on Ways and Means](https://longtermcareinsurancepartner.com/system/refinery/resources/W1siZiIsIjIwMTcvMTIvMjEvMjFfMTVfMDJfOTE3X0FBUlBfV2F5c19hbmRfTWVhbnNfQ29tbWl0dGVlX3JlcG9ydC5wZGYiXV0/AARP%20Ways%20and%20Means%20Committee%20report.pdf))

## Frequently asked questions

### What is AARP Financial Services?

AARP Financial Services Corp. (FSC) is a for-profit, wholly owned subsidiary of AARP, Inc. Investopedia describes it as the affiliate that holds AARP's real estate, placing it in the taxable half of AARP's corporate family alongside entities like AARP Properties LLC. Its earliest documented activity, however, was in mutual funds, not property.

### Is AARP Financial Services the same as AARP Financial, Inc.?

No. AARP Financial, Inc. is a separate Delaware corporation and a subsidiary of AARP Services, Inc. — not of AARP, Inc. directly. It advised the AARP Funds mutual funds from 2005 to 2010, went dormant after the funds were liquidated, and re-emerged in 2023 as a referral-only investment adviser. The similar names make the distinction worth keeping straight.

### What real estate does AARP Financial Services hold?

Specifics are thin. Investopedia identifies FSC as the for-profit affiliate holding AARP's real estate, and the property at the center of AARP's portfolio is its Washington, D.C. headquarters — a complex the organization paid about $206 million for, within a seven-building, 887,141-square-foot footprint managed by JLL as of 2013. Building-level ownership records were not verified for this profile.

### When was AARP Financial Services created?

The earliest fully verified record places it in existence by October 9, 1984, when it signed as a general partner of the AARP/Scudder Financial Services Company. AARP's own financial statements reportedly date its Delaware incorporation to the prior month, but those documents could not be retrieved in full for this profile, so the precise founding date remains an open item.
