## Overview

b-next is a German regulatory-technology firm founded in Herford in 1989 that sells trade-surveillance and compliance software to banks, brokers, exchanges, regulators and energy companies. Its flagship product, the CMC:Suite platform, monitors trading for market abuse, insider dealing and best-execution failures. In January 2026, the AI surveillance firm [Behavox](/organizations/behavox/) announced a strategic partnership with b-next and took a strategic interest in the company ([MarTech Series](https://martechseries.com/predictive-ai/ai-platforms-machine-learning/behavox-strengthens-its-polaris-trade-surveillance-platform-through-strategic-partnership-with-b-next/)).

The company was founded by Wolfgang Fabisch, who remains its chief executive, and it has spent its whole life in one narrow niche: helping financial firms prove their trading complies with the rules, and helping regulators watch the markets ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2025/07/09/how-b-next-stays-ahead-of-the-curve-in-an-evolving-regtech-space/)). The firm is legally b-next AG and, by its own account, employs around 70 people, with branch offices in Germany, Great Britain and the United States ([b-next](https://www.b-next.com/general/a-growing-need-for-smart-compliance-solutions/)). Its customers, again by its own account, include banks, brokers, supervisory bodies and energy suppliers, and it says it has carried out several hundred customer projects over its history ([b-next](https://www.b-next.com/general/a-growing-need-for-smart-compliance-solutions/)).

b-next is a private, mid-sized vendor in a market dominated by much larger compliance suppliers, and that shows in its public footprint: trade-press interviews and product announcements, but no tier1 newspaper profile and no Wikipedia article. What the record does show is unusual longevity — a specialist founded in 1989 that was still independently operating, and attracting a strategic investor, 37 years later ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2025/07/09/how-b-next-stays-ahead-of-the-curve-in-an-evolving-regtech-space/); [MarTech Series](https://martechseries.com/predictive-ai/ai-platforms-machine-learning/behavox-strengthens-its-polaris-trade-surveillance-platform-through-strategic-partnership-with-b-next/)).

## Origins and the CMC:Suite platform

The company's origin story starts not in compliance but in market data. In the early 1980s, Fabisch was working at [Reuters](/organizations/reuters/) when a client who managed a bank pointed out how fast regulatory change was accumulating across financial markets ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2025/07/09/how-b-next-stays-ahead-of-the-curve-in-an-evolving-regtech-space/)). "Right away, I recognized that regulatory oversight would expand for decades," Fabisch told FinTech Global in 2025, and that insight led him to build a mid-sized specialist company focused on compliance — a mission that, in his words, evolved into "creating the first dedicated compliance platform for equity markets" ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2025/07/09/how-b-next-stays-ahead-of-the-curve-in-an-evolving-regtech-space/)). The company was founded in 1989 and is headquartered in Herford, Germany ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/page/7/?finovate_video_cat=a-d)).

The product that carries that mission is CMC:Suite, short for Capital Markets Compliance: an integrated, modular platform sold to sell-side and buy-side firms, regulators, exchanges and commodity-market participants as a means of managing risk and detecting attempts at market manipulation ([b-next](https://www.b-next.com/capital-markets-compliance/)). The product line was known as the MACOC suite in 2009 ([A-Team Insight](https://a-teaminsight.com/blog/b-next-extends-data-reach-with-interactive-data-agreement-extends-ambitions-to-uk/?brand=ati)). Finovate's company profile names its modules as CMC:eMarket Abuse, CMC:eCompliance and CMC:TradeS ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/page/7/?finovate_video_cat=a-d)). The platform ships with more than 100 out-of-the-box scenarios, and the areas they cover show how broad "compliance" is for a trading firm:

| Surveillance area | What it covers |
| --- | --- |
| Market abuse | Detecting manipulation and abusive trading patterns |
| Insider trading | Flagging trades that may use non-public information |
| Conflicts of interest | Spotting conflicts in trading and client handling |
| FX benchmarking | Monitoring foreign-exchange benchmark behaviour |
| Derivatives / OTC monitoring | Watching derivatives and over-the-counter trades |
| Best execution | Reporting and monitoring whether clients got the best available terms |
| Trading activity | General monitoring of trading across venues and asset classes |

The scenario list is the company's own description of its platform ([b-next](https://www.b-next.com/capital-markets-compliance/)). Because the suite is modular, customers use only the scenarios that apply to their business model, and the platform is delivered as a cloud subscription rather than an outright purchase, running on Refinitiv's Elektron infrastructure ([b-next](https://www.b-next.com/capital-markets-compliance/)).

The technology has moved with the market. In 2012 b-next was already offering its applications from the cloud via the [Thomson Reuters](/organizations/thomson-reuters/) Elektron platform, pitching that as a way for smaller firms to avoid buying their own hardware ([Securities Finance Times](https://www.securitiesfinancetimes.com/interviews/interview.php?interview_id=49&navigationaction=interviews&page=7&newssection=interviews)). By 2025 the company was applying machine-learning models to large datasets covering trades, order books and communications, saying the models can identify emerging patterns of market abuse that static rules would miss — and insisting the models stay explainable, so compliance teams understand why an alert was triggered ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2025/07/09/how-b-next-stays-ahead-of-the-curve-in-an-evolving-regtech-space/)). Fabisch's stated roadmap from the same interview is advanced AI models and expanded coverage of digital assets ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2025/07/09/how-b-next-stays-ahead-of-the-curve-in-an-evolving-regtech-space/)).

## Clients, regulators and the German home market

b-next's home market has always been Germany, and its most notable client is a regulator rather than a bank. The German regulator BaFin is a b-next client, and the company completed a project connecting BaFin to the Committee of European Securities Regulators for reporting purposes ([A-Team Insight](https://a-teaminsight.com/blog/b-next-extends-data-reach-with-interactive-data-agreement-extends-ambitions-to-uk/?brand=ati)). By 2009 the vendor said it had won "a large percentage of the banking community" in Germany as clients, and that buy-side interest had surged in the preceding six months on the back of market-abuse regulation; the 2008 Société Générale fraud, the same account says, had sparked a wave of investment in compliance-tracking software ([A-Team Insight](https://a-teaminsight.com/blog/b-next-extends-data-reach-with-interactive-data-agreement-extends-ambitions-to-uk/?brand=ati)). A 2012 interview put the client base at BaFin, exchanges and banks, with some clients processing hundreds of thousands of transactions a day and using modular "cockpit" applications to get a graphical view across business silos ([Securities Finance Times](https://www.securitiesfinancetimes.com/interviews/interview.php?interview_id=49&navigationaction=interviews&page=7&newssection=interviews)).

The same 2009 account shows the company trying to grow beyond Germany. It signed an agreement with Interactive Data under which the data vendor would supply global pricing and reference data — static, business-entity, corporate-actions and class-actions data, plus ratings and classifications — to feed its compliance dashboard, after six to eight months of discussions driven by customer requests for consolidated data for best-execution purposes ([A-Team Insight](https://a-teaminsight.com/blog/b-next-extends-data-reach-with-interactive-data-agreement-extends-ambitions-to-uk/?brand=ati)). The deal was explicitly part of an ambition to crack the UK market, where the company saw a different sales problem: German prospects interrogated specific functionality, while UK prospects wanted to see what the solution delivered for the firm overall ([A-Team Insight](https://a-teaminsight.com/blog/b-next-extends-data-reach-with-interactive-data-agreement-extends-ambitions-to-uk/?brand=ati)). Its main competition in continental Europe at the time was the Italian vendor List Group and its GRC Evolution platform ([A-Team Insight](https://a-teaminsight.com/blog/b-next-extends-data-reach-with-interactive-data-agreement-extends-ambitions-to-uk/?brand=ati)).

The UK push was still under way in 2012, when Fabisch said the vendor was branching out into the UK first, with multilateral trading facilities in the pipeline, and noted that surveillance was moving towards real time — from monthly or weekly checks to daily, and for algorithmic and high-frequency trading, down to seconds ([Securities Finance Times](https://www.securitiesfinancetimes.com/interviews/interview.php?interview_id=49&navigationaction=interviews&page=7&newssection=interviews)). The company had also been among the first to offer a short-selling surveillance module after Germany's first short-selling ban on certain bank securities, roughly 16 to 18 months before that interview ([Securities Finance Times](https://www.securitiesfinancetimes.com/interviews/interview.php?interview_id=49&navigationaction=interviews&page=7&newssection=interviews)).

## The Behavox partnership and strategic investment

b-next's most consequential recent event is its tie-up with Behavox, the [London](/places/london/)-headquartered AI surveillance firm whose profile on this site covers its $100 million [SoftBank](/organizations/softbank-group/) round in 2020 and its $175 million HPS Investment Partners round in 2026 ([RegTech Analyst](https://regtechanalyst.com/behavox-raises-175m-from-hps-to-fuel-global-growth/)). In January 2026 Behavox announced a strategic partnership with b-next, describing it as a specialist Capital Markets Trading Surveillance and Compliance solution provider ([MarTech Series](https://martechseries.com/predictive-ai/ai-platforms-machine-learning/behavox-strengthens-its-polaris-trade-surveillance-platform-through-strategic-partnership-with-b-next/)). The partnership gives Behavox deeper subject-matter expertise for its Unified Controls Platform, including its Quantum communications-surveillance product, Intelligent Archive, Trade Surveillance and Policy Management, while b-next gains Behavox's AI, research-and-development support and global customer-success support — and will license Behavox technology and its proprietary large language model, with b-next customers able to use Intelligent Archive immediately to unify regulatory retention, search and retrieval of communications and transaction data ([MarTech Series](https://martechseries.com/predictive-ai/ai-platforms-machine-learning/behavox-strengthens-its-polaris-trade-surveillance-platform-through-strategic-partnership-with-b-next/)).

The same announcement states that "Behavox has taken a strategic interest in b-next, reinforcing a shared long-term vision and commitment to innovation" ([MarTech Series](https://martechseries.com/predictive-ai/ai-platforms-machine-learning/behavox-strengthens-its-polaris-trade-surveillance-platform-through-strategic-partnership-with-b-next/)). Behavox chief operating officer Kiryl Trembovolski said the partnership lets Behavox share its "battle tested AI capabilities" with b-next and continuously improve its [Polaris](/creative-works/polaris/) trade-surveillance offering with b-next's subject-matter expertise, "that has been trusted by markets and regulators for over 25 years" ([MarTech Series](https://martechseries.com/predictive-ai/ai-platforms-machine-learning/behavox-strengthens-its-polaris-trade-surveillance-platform-through-strategic-partnership-with-b-next/)).

One part of the story is genuinely unclear: when the investment was made. Behavox's June 2026 funding announcement, as reported by Financial IT, says the company secured a $70 million venture-debt facility from [Hercules Capital](/organizations/hercules-capital/) in autumn 2024 "to support strategic expansion, including its acquisition of [Mosaic Smart Data](/organizations/mosaic-smart-data/) and its strategic investment in b-next" ([Financial IT](https://financialit.net/news/fundraising-news/behavox-raises-175-million-hps-investment-partners-part-blackrock-accelerate)). A-Team Insight and RegTech Analyst report the same framing ([A-Team Insight](https://a-teaminsight.com/blog/behavox-secures-175-million-investment-to-expand-unified-controls-platform/); [RegTech Analyst](https://regtechanalyst.com/behavox-raises-175m-from-hps-to-fuel-global-growth/)). That language ties the b-next investment to a facility secured in autumn 2024, while the partnership and the interest itself were announced in January 2026 — so the investment may have been made in 2024 and announced later, or made alongside the January 2026 announcement, and the sources read do not settle which. This profile states both datings and flags the gap rather than picking one.

What is settled is the strategic logic as Behavox presents it: the Mosaic Smart Data acquisition and the b-next investment together accelerated its move into trade surveillance, adding fixed-income, currencies and commodities front-office analytics and capital-markets trade-surveillance expertise ([Financial IT](https://financialit.net/news/fundraising-news/behavox-raises-175-million-hps-investment-partners-part-blackrock-accelerate)). Behavox says it has been profitable since 2023, and its June 2026 round — $175 million of preferred equity from HPS Investment Partners, part of [BlackRock](/organizations/blackrock/) — was its first equity financing since SoftBank's 2020 investment ([Financial IT](https://financialit.net/news/fundraising-news/behavox-raises-175-million-hps-investment-partners-part-blackrock-accelerate)). Its Polaris trade-surveillance platform, introduced in 2025, supports market-abuse detection across all 10 major asset classes ([Financial IT](https://financialit.net/news/fundraising-news/behavox-raises-175-million-hps-investment-partners-part-blackrock-accelerate)) — the product line the b-next partnership is meant to strengthen.
