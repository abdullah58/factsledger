## Overview

Viz.ai is an artificial-intelligence healthcare company whose software detects suspected strokes in medical scans and alerts specialists within minutes ([MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2021/03/stroke-detection-startup-viz-ai-raises-71m/)). Founded in 2016 by Dr. Chris Mansi, a former NHS neurosurgeon, and David Golan, a machine-learning postdoc at Stanford, the San Francisco-based company built its name on a single regulatory first: in February 2018 its stroke-triage software became the first AI algorithm to receive FDA De Novo clearance for detecting large vessel occlusion strokes in CT angiography, creating an entirely new regulatory classification for computer-aided triage software ([MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2021/03/stroke-detection-startup-viz-ai-raises-71m/); [BioCentury](https://www.biocentury.com/article/293229/ai-software-to-detect-strokes-gets-fda-clearance)). Four years later, a $100 million Series D led by Tiger Global and Insight Partners valued the company at $1.2 billion, with total venture investment above $250 million ([MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/stroke-care-coordination-company-vizai-lands-100m/)).

## The regulatory first: a De Novo for AI triage

Before Viz.ai, no AI triage device had been through FDA's De Novo premarket review pathway. BioCentury reported that the FDA's February 2018 approval of the Viz LVO Stroke Platform — granted via De Novo — opened up a new regulatory classification under the 510(k) pathway for future computer-aided triage software devices with the same intended use ([BioCentury](https://www.biocentury.com/article/293229/ai-software-to-detect-strokes-gets-fda-clearance)). The FDA's own decision summary, DEN170073, records the device under the proprietary name ContaCT: a Class II device under special controls (21 CFR 892.2080, product code QAS) that analyzes CT angiogram images of the brain in the acute setting and notifies a neurovascular specialist when it identifies a suspected large vessel occlusion ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN170073.pdf)). The indication is carefully bounded — the device is a "notification-only, parallel workflow tool," and the suspected findings are not for diagnostic use beyond notification ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN170073.pdf)).

The submission data, as reported by BioCentury, came from a 300-patient retrospective reader and performance study comparing the software's image analysis against standard of care: the software identified large vessel occlusions with 90% accuracy, the median scan-to-notification time was under six minutes, and it notified specialists faster in more than 95% of cases, saving an average of 52 minutes ([BioCentury](https://www.biocentury.com/article/293229/ai-software-to-detect-strokes-gets-fda-clearance)). The FDA's decision summary independently records a standalone-performance area under the curve (AUC) of 0.91 for the algorithm ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN170073.pdf)).

The regulatory story did not end in 2018. In February 2022 the company landed an FDA 510(k) clearance for its algorithm designed to detect cerebral aneurysms, and its LVO software earned European CE Mark approval in 2021 ([MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/stroke-care-coordination-company-vizai-lands-100m/); [Endovascular Today](https://evtoday.com/news/vizais-lvo-software-receives-ce-mark-approval-for-stroke-care)). Perhaps more consequentially for hospital economics, its AI software received approval for a New Technology Add-on Payment from the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services — the first such reimbursement for AI software ([Endovascular Today](https://evtoday.com/news/vizai-funding-will-support-expansion-of-ai-driven-viz-platform-for-disease-detection-and-triage)).

## The clinical evidence: does it change treatment times?

The profile of record for Viz.ai's effectiveness is not its funding history but a peer-reviewed retrospective cohort study published in Internal and Emergency Medicine in 2025 ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11739-025-04183-z)). Researchers at a comprehensive stroke center analyzed 222 patients who underwent endovascular thrombectomy between July 2018 and July 2022 — 84 before the hospital implemented Viz LVO in July 2020 and 138 after — with thrombectomy within 90 minutes as the primary outcome ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11739-025-04183-z)). The results were substantial: median door-to-groin time fell from 109 minutes to 75 minutes (p < 0.001), and the fraction of patients receiving thrombectomy within 90 minutes rose from 26.5% to 68.1% (p < 0.001) ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11739-025-04183-z)). An interrupted time-series analysis confirmed the reduction was tied to the implementation itself (p = 0.001), not a background trend ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11739-025-04183-z)).

The authors were careful about what the study does not show: there were no significant differences in reperfusion scores, length of stay, or mortality and hospice rates, the study was not powered for those outcomes, and 90-day functional outcomes were unknown — a limitation of its retrospective design ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11739-025-04183-z)). It was also a single-center study, though its findings align with other groups' reported door-to-groin reductions of 12 to 22.5 minutes after AI implementation ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11739-025-04183-z)).

A large real-world multi-center evaluation reported in Endovascular Today points the same way at scale: across 2,544 consecutive patients at 139 hospitals using scanners from multiple manufacturers, the Viz LVO workflow software showed a median time-to-notification of 5 minutes and 45 seconds, with 96% sensitivity and 94% specificity in identifying large vessel occlusions ([Endovascular Today](https://evtoday.com/news/vizais-lvo-software-receives-ce-mark-approval-for-stroke-care)).

## From stroke-only to a multi-disease platform

Viz.ai's commercial arc is one of expansion from a single stroke product into adjacent time-critical conditions. In March 2021 it raised $71 million in Series C funding led by Scale Venture Partners and Insight Partners — bringing total investment above $150 million — explicitly to carry its platform into cardiology, pulmonary and trauma care ([MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2021/03/stroke-detection-startup-viz-ai-raises-71m/)). By April 2022 it had launched AI detection products for aortic disease, pulmonary embolism and cerebral aneurysms, with a 510(k) submission pending for subdural hematoma, and had begun building an AI-driven life-sciences platform to help medical-device and pharmaceutical companies run clinical trials ([MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/stroke-care-coordination-company-vizai-lands-100m/); [Endovascular Today](https://evtoday.com/news/vizai-funding-will-support-expansion-of-ai-driven-viz-platform-for-disease-detection-and-triage)). In 2019, Medtronic struck a deal to distribute the company's detection and triage software, widening its hospital distribution through the medtech incumbent ([MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2021/03/stroke-detection-startup-viz-ai-raises-71m/)).

The clearest sign of its move up the hospital technology stack came in November 2024, when Viz.ai entered an agreement with [Microsoft](/organizations/microsoft/) to launch 13 of its AI workflow models on Microsoft's Precision Imaging Network, part of Microsoft Cloud for Healthcare ([AuntMinnie.com](http://cdn.auntminnie.com/imaging-informatics/artificial-intelligence/article/15709262/vizai-vizai-to-launch-ai-models-on-microsofts-cloud-network)). The models — covering stroke, hemorrhage, pulmonary embolism and aortic dissection — are designed to help radiologists create reports more efficiently, streamline communication with treating physicians, and support care-team collaboration on follow-up treatment ([AuntMinnie.com](http://cdn.auntminnie.com/imaging-informatics/artificial-intelligence/article/15709262/vizai-vizai-to-launch-ai-models-on-microsofts-cloud-network)).
