## Overview

The Centro Santa Ángela de la Cruz is [Spain's](/places/spain/) first and only specialised residential and day centre for young people and adults with deafblindness, in [Salteras](/places/salteras/), near [Seville](/places/seville/), run by [APASCIDE (Asociación Española de Familias de Personas con Sordoceguera)](/organizations/asociacion-espanola-de-familias-de-personas-con-sordoceguera-apascide/) ([Europa Press via SID](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/); [Andalucía Económica](https://andaluciaeconomica.com/nuevos-equipos-informaticos-facilitan-el-aprendizaje-de-personas-con-sordoceguera/)). Queen Sofía inaugurated it on 26 October 2010, and it holds 45 places — 26 residential and 19 day-centre — for people who cannot see or hear and need one-to-one communication support to live, learn and take part in the world around them ([Europa Press via SID](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/); [Andalucía Económica](https://andaluciaeconomica.com/nuevos-equipos-informaticos-facilitan-el-aprendizaje-de-personas-con-sordoceguera/)).

Deafblindness is a disability in its own right, not simply blindness plus deafness: the combination of severe visual and hearing impairment cuts a person off from communication and from perceiving the world around them, and the effect is isolation unless specialist support is provided ([Andalucía Económica](https://andaluciaeconomica.com/nuevos-equipos-informaticos-facilitan-el-aprendizaje-de-personas-con-sordoceguera/); [Fundación Caser](https://www.fundacioncaser.org/discapacidad/tipos-de-discapacidad/discapacidad-sensorial/discapacidad-auditiva/sordoceguera)). Some people with deafblindness are totally deaf and blind; others keep some residual sight or hearing, and the condition can be congenital or acquired — for example through Usher syndrome, which progressively takes sight from people born deaf ([Fundación Caser](https://www.fundacioncaser.org/discapacidad/tipos-de-discapacidad/discapacidad-sensorial/discapacidad-auditiva/sordoceguera)). In Spain, schooling for children with deafblindness is provided through ONCE, the national organisation for blind people, but at 18 those young people aged out of the specialist units — and the centre exists to close that gap ([elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/); [Fundación Caser](https://www.fundacioncaser.org/discapacidad/tipos-de-discapacidad/discapacidad-sensorial/discapacidad-auditiva/sordoceguera)).

## A centre built by families

The centre was a family project before it was a building. APASCIDE, the Spanish association of families of people with deafblindness, began as a campaigning body and ended up providing services because its members' children, on turning 18, left the specialist units where they had learned and had nowhere to continue ([elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)). The association "risked the little it had" to build a residential centre with a day unit, and the project became reality in October 2010 ([elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)).

The building stands on the Finca El Almuédano, land ceded by the Archdiocese of Seville, in the municipality of Salteras, about 20 kilometres from Seville ([Europa Press via SID](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/); [elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)). Construction cost €2.2 million, paid for by Plan E, the Andalusian regional administration and the Obra Social de Caja Madrid ([Europa Press via SID](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/)). Residents had already been using the facilities since September 2010, before the formal inauguration by Queen Sofía on 26 October 2010 ([Europa Press via SID](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/)).

The scale of the need is uncertain even in official counting. APASCIDE estimates a minimum of 7,000 people with deafblindness in Spain, about 10% of them totally deafblind, while a study by the Ministry of Social Rights and 2030 Agenda put the figure at about 8,000, rising to 34,000 counting people over 65 who acquired deafblindness through age-related deterioration ([Andalucía Económica](https://andaluciaeconomica.com/nuevos-equipos-informaticos-facilitan-el-aprendizaje-de-personas-con-sordoceguera/); [elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)). Against any of those numbers, a single centre with 45 places is small — and in 2022 about as many people again were on its waiting list ([elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)).

## What the centre is: facilities and capacity

The centre combines two services on one adapted site of about 5,400 square metres: a residence for severely affected people and a day unit ([Europa Press via SID](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/); [Fundación Ebro Foods](https://www.fundacionebrofoods.es/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/apascide-programa-formativo-y-de-rehabilitacion-educativa.pdf)). The total capacity has held at 45 places since opening, but the split between the two services changed as demand shifted:

| Service | Places at opening (2010) | Places in later sources (2016–2023) |
| ------- | ------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| Residential | 17 | 26 |
| Day unit | 28 | 19 |
| Total | 45 | 45 |

Sources: [Europa Press via SID](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/); [Andalucía Económica](https://andaluciaeconomica.com/nuevos-equipos-informaticos-facilitan-el-aprendizaje-de-personas-con-sordoceguera/); [Fundación Ebro Foods](https://www.fundacionebrofoods.es/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/apascide-programa-formativo-y-de-rehabilitacion-educativa.pdf).

The residential places are contracted with several autonomous communities, not only Andalusia: in 2016 the split was 13 with the Junta de Andalucía, 3 with the Community of Madrid and 2 with Castilla-La Mancha, with 8 awaiting contracting, and by 2023 the centre had places contracted with three autonomous communities ([Fundación Ebro Foods](https://www.fundacionebrofoods.es/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/apascide-programa-formativo-y-de-rehabilitacion-educativa.pdf); [Andalucía Económica](https://andaluciaeconomica.com/nuevos-equipos-informaticos-facilitan-el-aprendizaje-de-personas-con-sordoceguera/)). By late 2022 the centre housed 40 people in total, with 26 residents aged 21 to 62 from different parts of Spain ([elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)).

The building is designed so that people who cannot see or hear can move through it on their own: bedrooms differentiated by colour and texture, handrails along corridors and rooms, and aromatic plants marking the outdoor areas ([Europa Press via SID](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/); [elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)). The facilities include gardens and a vegetable garden tended by residents, a sensory stimulation room with musical instruments, living and television rooms, a covered pool and spa, a gym, a speech-therapy room, an infirmary and workshops for literacy, IT, ceramics and crafts ([Europa Press via SID](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/); [Fundación Ebro Foods](https://www.fundacionebrofoods.es/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/apascide-programa-formativo-y-de-rehabilitacion-educativa.pdf)). At least two leisure outings a week are organised, and bus line 176 stops at the door ([Europa Press via SID](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/)).

Staffing is the centre's defining feature — and its defining cost. At opening there were 40 workers, almost one carer or mediator per user, all of them familiar with sign language, including special educators, psychologists, social workers and guide-interpreters, plus at least a dozen APASCIDE volunteers ([Europa Press via SID](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/)). The reason is simple: care for people with deafblindness has to be one-to-one ([elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)).

## Daily life: communication, work and choice

Communication at the centre is built on touch. Through supported sign language — signs made on the palms of the hands — deafblind people can converse with communication mediators, using hands as their eyes and ears; in other cases gestures, sounds and facial expressions do the talking ([elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)). The wider repertoire includes braille, the manual alphabet traced on the palm, tactile sign language, communication boards and cards, and assistive technology such as braille lines and screen magnifiers ([Fundación Caser](https://www.fundacioncaser.org/discapacidad/tipos-de-discapacidad/discapacidad-sensorial/discapacidad-auditiva/sordoceguera); [Fundación Ebro Foods](https://www.fundacionebrofoods.es/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/apascide-programa-formativo-y-de-rehabilitacion-educativa.pdf)).

Days combine everyday tasks — eating, personal care — with workshops in literacy, gardening, ceramics and physical activity, and the residents themselves decide what they do: activities are often a means to prevent isolation and connect residents with the world rather than ends in themselves ([elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)). Weekly equine therapy takes place at La Herradura, and some residents train for sport: Cristian trains two or three hours a day and has taken part in the Carrera Nocturna del Guadalquivir, Seville's night race, with fellow residents ([elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)).

The centre's philosophy is summed up by a poster in the residence's living room quoting Antoine de Saint-Exupéry's *The Little Prince*: "One sees clearly only with the heart. What is essential is invisible to the eye" ([elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)). Juanjo, the oldest resident at 62, was born deaf and progressively lost his sight through Usher syndrome; before coming to the centre in 2011 he lived in an old people's home in his native Cáceres, where nobody knew sign language and he could not communicate with anyone ([elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)). Jenny, who became deafblind after meningitis hours after birth, used to injure herself out of frustration; since attending the day unit she has learned to ask for what she needs ([elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)). Places at the centre are for life, which is what gives families peace of mind ([elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)).

## Funding: the one-to-one problem

One-to-one care is expensive, and the centre's finances have always been tight. By 2022 the annual cost of running the centre had reached about €1,900,000, and public funding did not cover it ([elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)):

| Item | Amount (2022) |
| ---- | ------------- |
| Total annual cost | €1,900,000 |
| Day unit cost | €450,000 (Junta de Andalucía contributes €226,000) |
| Residence cost | €1,450,000 (Junta de Andalucía contributes €721,000) |
| User contributions | 75% of income (residence), 25% (day service) |
| Annual deficit | About €430,000, covered by donations |

Source: [elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/).

The gap is filled by donations, above all through APASCIDE's "club de amigos" and the contributions of the association's 220 families, plus corporate help from neighbours such as the Fundación Cobre Las Cruces, which in 2023 renewed the centre's computer workshop — unmodernised since the 2010 opening for lack of funds — donating six latest-generation desktop computers with large screens, one of them touch, plus a braille line and a tablet ([elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/); [Andalucía Económica](https://andaluciaeconomica.com/nuevos-equipos-informaticos-facilitan-el-aprendizaje-de-personas-con-sordoceguera/)). As APASCIDE puts it, public funding is not enough, and contributions like this one are vital ([Andalucía Económica](https://andaluciaeconomica.com/nuevos-equipos-informaticos-facilitan-el-aprendizaje-de-personas-con-sordoceguera/)).

## Recognition

In 2013 the centre received the XXI Premio San Juan de Dios, awarded annually by the Colegio Oficial de Enfermería of the province of Seville ([ArteSacro](https://www.artesacro.org/Noticia/Ver/86322/xxi-premio-san-juan-dios-colegio-oficial-enfermeria-ha-recaido-centro)). The jury cited the effort APASCIDE had made to bring a socio-health centre of this kind into being, its service vocation, and the trajectory observed since the centre opened in 2010 ([ArteSacro](https://www.artesacro.org/Noticia/Ver/86322/xxi-premio-san-juan-dios-colegio-oficial-enfermeria-ha-recaido-centro)). The award was presented on 21 June 2013 at the Hotel NH Convenciones in Seville ([ArteSacro](https://www.artesacro.org/Noticia/Ver/86322/xxi-premio-san-juan-dios-colegio-oficial-enfermeria-ha-recaido-centro)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the Centro Santa Ángela de la Cruz?**
It is Spain's first and only specialised residential and day centre for young people and adults with deafblindness, in Salteras, Seville, run by APASCIDE and opened on 26 October 2010, with 45 places split between a residence and a day unit ([Europa Press via SID](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/); [Andalucía Económica](https://andaluciaeconomica.com/nuevos-equipos-informaticos-facilitan-el-aprendizaje-de-personas-con-sordoceguera/)).

**Who can live at the centre?**
Young people and adults with deafblindness — people who combine severe visual and hearing impairment — from anywhere in Spain, with places contracted by several autonomous communities ([Europa Press via SID](https://sid-inico.usal.es/noticias/salteras-sevilla-acoge-el-primer-centro-para-sordo-ciegos-de-espana/); [elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)).

**How do residents communicate?**
Mainly through supported sign language made on the palms of the hands, plus gestures, sounds and facial expressions, and — depending on the person — braille, the manual alphabet on the palm, communication boards and cards, and assistive technology such as braille lines ([elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/); [Fundación Caser](https://www.fundacioncaser.org/discapacidad/tipos-de-discapacidad/discapacidad-sensorial/discapacidad-auditiva/sordoceguera)).

**How is the centre funded?**
It costs about €1,900,000 a year to run (2022 figure); the contracting administrations and user contributions cover most of it, but a deficit of about €430,000 a year is met through donations, including APASCIDE's "club de amigos" of 220 families ([elDiario.es Andalucía](https://solidaridadintergeneracional.es/wp/salir-de-la-oscuridad-sentir-con-las-manos-y-ver-con-el-corazon-en-el-unico-centro-para-sordociegos-de-espana/)).

**Has the centre won any awards?**
Yes: the XXI Premio San Juan de Dios in 2013, awarded by the Colegio Oficial de Enfermería of Seville for the centre's work since its opening in 2010 and its unique role in Spain ([ArteSacro](https://www.artesacro.org/Noticia/Ver/86322/xxi-premio-san-juan-dios-colegio-oficial-enfermeria-ha-recaido-centro)).
