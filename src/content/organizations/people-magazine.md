## Overview

**People** — styled **PEOPLE** in all caps by the brand itself — is the American celebrity and human-interest weekly founded by Time Inc. on March 4, 1974, with Mia Farrow on the inaugural cover, and owned since late 2021 by Dotdash Meredith, the company formed when IAC's Dotdash bought Meredith Corporation's magazine group for $2.7 billion ([New-York Historical Society](https://nyhistory.org/blogs/journalistic-popcorn-fifty-years-of-people-magazine); [AP](https://www.shootonline.com/news-tags/barry-diller)). In the *Michael* (2026) biopic saga, it is the outlet a production source chose for reassurance: telling PEOPLE in January 2025 that the film was "not in total chaos" and had reshoots slated for March 2025 — a dating that conflicts with Variety's later report of a 22-day June 2025 reshoot ([PEOPLE](https://people.com/why-isnt-michael-jackson-1993-lawsuit-in-michael-biopic-11957205)).

Five decades on, the brand reports 32 million weekly print readers and 77 million monthly website visitors — a print audience that still rivals the scale of major digital-native brands, implying the celebrity-weekly format survived the internet transition better than almost any other print category ([New-York Historical Society](https://nyhistory.org/blogs/journalistic-popcorn-fifty-years-of-people-magazine)).

## Born in a hair salon: the 1974 launch

PEOPLE came together fast. In the spring of 1973, Time Inc. CEO Andrew Heiskell pitched the head of the company's Magazine Development Group, Otto Fuerbringer, with a hallway question — "why don't we start a magazine called People?" — and by April 1973 Fuerbringer had drafted the prospectus: not slick, but on heavy-enough paper for good reproduction, since pictures would be a large part of the edit, with each week's topicality dictating content about people "in almost any posture or situation but passive" ([New-York Historical Society](https://nyhistory.org/blogs/journalistic-popcorn-fifty-years-of-people-magazine)). Two printer's dummies — full dress rehearsals — were produced: one with Elizabeth Taylor and Richard Burton, amid their high-profile divorce, on the cover; a second with Billie Jean King and her then-husband Larry King ([New-York Historical Society](https://nyhistory.org/blogs/journalistic-popcorn-fifty-years-of-people-magazine)). Time Inc.'s own all-male editorial brain trust found the dummy "sleazy" and "cheap"; one called it "journalistic popcorn" ([New-York Historical Society](https://nyhistory.org/blogs/journalistic-popcorn-fifty-years-of-people-magazine)).

The dissent that mattered came from outside the boardroom. Heiskell sent the dummy to Clare Boothe Luce, widow of Time Inc. co-founder Henry R. Luce, who ran her own market research at her hair salon — and reported back in an August 11, 1973 letter that it would be "the best read magazine in any hairdressing salon in America," adding that women's questions about people required women editors reviewing the bios ([New-York Historical Society](https://nyhistory.org/blogs/journalistic-popcorn-fifty-years-of-people-magazine)). Founding editor Richard Stolley — the former *LIFE* assistant managing editor who had acquired the Zapruder film of the JFK assassination — set the formula in his first editor's letter: the headliners, the stars, the important doers, the comers, "and on plenty of ordinary men and women caught up in extraordinary situations"; the emphasis would be on people, not issues ([HISTORY](http://history.com/this-day-in-history/march-4/people-magazine-launches)). The March 4, 1974 debut — Farrow as Daisy Buchanan on the cover, 35 cents, 72 pages, a 1.4-million-copy press run — sold almost a million copies, and an outside researcher's fall-1974 verdict ("never in my 25 years of research have I seen a product with greater consumer acceptance") made it official: Time Inc. had a hit ([New-York Historical Society](https://nyhistory.org/blogs/journalistic-popcorn-fifty-years-of-people-magazine); [HISTORY](http://history.com/this-day-in-history/march-4/people-magazine-launches)).

## From Time Inc. to Dotdash Meredith

The magazine that invented mainstream celebrity journalism kept getting bought. Meredith Corporation acquired Time Inc.'s magazines — including *People* — for $1.8 billion, and four years later, in October 2021, IAC's digital arm Dotdash agreed to buy Meredith's National Media Group for $2.7 billion ($42.18 per share in cash), combining brands like People, Better Homes & Gardens, and Allrecipes with Investopedia, Serious Eats, and Brides under CEO Neil Vogel as Dotdash Meredith ([AP](https://www.shootonline.com/news-tags/barry-diller); [Reuters](https://d2233.cms.socastsrm.com/2021/10/06/iacs-dotdash-to-buy-meredith-corps-national-media-group/)). The combined company was described as reaching more than 175 million people online, with print positioned to support the digital effort — the reverse of the old model ([AP](https://www.shootonline.com/news-tags/barry-diller)). The franchises carried the brand through the transition: the 100 Most Beautiful, Sexiest Man Alive, the "Half Their Size" issue, and "Heroes Among Us" ([HISTORY](http://history.com/this-day-in-history/march-4/people-magazine-launches)). HISTORY credits the launch with dragging entertainment and celebrity reporting into the mainstream media — spawning copycats from *US Weekly* to television's *Entertainment Tonight* — and notes the magazine's standing rule, unlike the tabloids, of refusing to publish unsubstantiated rumors ([HISTORY](http://history.com/this-day-in-history/march-4/people-magazine-launches)).

## PEOPLE and the *Michael* reshoot-timing conflict

PEOPLE's role in the [Michael](/creative-works/michael-2026-film/) production saga is a case study in sourced access. When Puck reported in January 2025 that a settlement clause barring depiction of Jordan Chandler had been overlooked — a "nightmare scenario," since Chandler was reportedly the backbone of John Logan's script — a production source went to PEOPLE to push back: the biopic was "not in total chaos," the source said, the headlines about the production halting were untrue, and the film was "moving forward" with reshoots slated for March 2025 ([PEOPLE](https://people.com/why-isnt-michael-jackson-1993-lawsuit-in-michael-biopic-11957205)). PEOPLE's own later explainer carries that March dating alongside Variety's subsequent report — a 22-day June 2025 reshoot adding $10–15 million, the estate covering the cost and taking equity — without reconciling the two, and notes the year-long delay from the original April 18, 2025 date through October 3, 2025 to April 24, 2026 ([PEOPLE](https://people.com/why-isnt-michael-jackson-1993-lawsuit-in-michael-biopic-11957205)). Separately, PEOPLE carried Bloomberg's claim that director [Antoine Fuqua](/people/antoine-fuqua/) and producer Graham King received an extra $15 million and $10 million respectively over their original deals once the reshoots forced a new budget — a claim King's spokesperson tied to "a new budget" and postponed projects ([PEOPLE](https://people.com/michael-jackson-biopic-director-producer-made-millions-extra-to-remove-child-sex-abuse-allegations-report-11958840)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who owns People magazine?**
Dotdash Meredith, formed when IAC's digital media arm Dotdash bought Meredith Corporation's National Media Group — publisher of People, Better Homes & Gardens, and Allrecipes — in a $2.7 billion all-cash deal announced in October 2021. The combined company is led by CEO Neil Vogel and was described as reaching more than 175 million people online.

**How did People magazine start?**
Time Inc. CEO Andrew Heiskell pitched the idea in spring 1973; founding editor Richard Stolley (the LIFE editor who acquired the Zapruder film) launched it on March 4, 1974, with Mia Farrow on the cover. A 1973 Elizabeth Taylor/Richard Burton test issue "flew off the shelves"; the debut sold almost a million copies of a 1.4-million press run.

**What are People's signature franchises?**
The annual "100 Most Beautiful People," the "Sexiest Man Alive" (for which PEOPLE is the self-described annual arbiter), the "Half Their Size" weight-loss issue, and the "Heroes Among Us" feature — alongside pop-culture reviews, product picks, and human-interest stories.

**What did PEOPLE report about the *Michael* biopic?**
In January 2025, a production source told PEOPLE the film was "not in total chaos" and had reshoots slated for March 2025. PEOPLE later carried Bloomberg's claim that director Antoine Fuqua and producer Graham King got an extra $15M and $10M respectively after the reshoots, and its explainer juxtaposes the March dating with Variety's 22-day June 2025 reshoot without resolving the conflict.
