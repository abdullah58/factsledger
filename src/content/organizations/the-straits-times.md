## Overview

*The Straits Times* is Singapore's English-language newspaper of record, founded on 15 July 1845 and now owned by the not-for-profit SPH Media Trust ([National Library Board](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=382e8b41-dc5a-4091-af00-56ae985f0c5c); [Reuters Institute](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2022/singapore)). It is the most widely read newspaper in Singapore and its most popular traditional news brand, used weekly by 37% of Singaporeans, with its website ranking third in frequency of use ([Reuters Institute](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2022/singapore)). Its 180-year history runs from an eight-page colonial weekly printed on a hand-operated press to a digital newsroom owned by a not-for-profit trust — and it remains the paper through which Singapore reads about the world, including the global technology disputes that reach its courts, such as the November 2025 Tokyo ruling against [Cloudflare](/organizations/cloudflare/).

## From colonial weekly to national paper

The paper began as *The Straits Times, and Singapore Journal of Commerce*, a weekly first published on 15 July 1845 from No. 7 Commercial Square (present-day Raffles Place), with the English journalist Robert Carr Woods as its first editor ([National Library Board](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=382e8b41-dc5a-4091-af00-56ae985f0c5c)). The first issue ran to eight folio pages of advertisements, an editorial, local news and foreign news extracted from overseas newspapers, and the paper had fewer than 100 subscribers paying $16 a year. How it was conceived remains in dispute: the account of amateur historian Charles Burton Buckley credits the Armenian merchant Marterus Thaddeus Apcar with ordering the press, and his fellow Armenian Catchick Moses with buying the equipment and launching the paper with Woods — while historian Constance Mary Turnbull argued it was unlikely an Armenian merchant would have wanted to found an English-language newspaper to rival the established *Singapore Free Press* ([National Library Board](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=382e8b41-dc5a-4091-af00-56ae985f0c5c)).

The paper became a daily in 1858, survived a fire that destroyed its offices and press on 16 February 1869 — still publishing the next day's issue with another printer's help — and took back the name *The Straits Times* in January 1883 ([National Library Board](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=382e8b41-dc5a-4091-af00-56ae985f0c5c)). It became a joint-stock company in May 1900, stopped publication during the Japanese Occupation of 1942–1945, and resumed with its first postwar issue on 7 September 1945 ([National Library Board](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=382e8b41-dc5a-4091-af00-56ae985f0c5c)). In 1959 the headquarters moved to Kuala Lumpur, a decision driven by the Federation of Malaya's independence and the paper's rising circulation there; Singapore's separation from Malaysia in 1965 made that untenable, and a 1973 restructure split the group into the Malaysian New Straits Times and the Singaporean Straits Times Press ([National Library Board](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=382e8b41-dc5a-4091-af00-56ae985f0c5c)).

In 1984 the Straits Times Press merged with Times Publishing Berhad and Singapore News and Publications Ltd to form Singapore Press Holdings (SPH), and in 2002 SPH consolidated its English, Malay and Chinese newsrooms at a new headquarters in Toa Payoh North ([National Library Board](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=382e8b41-dc5a-4091-af00-56ae985f0c5c)). Along the way the paper collected the 2014 Newspaper of the Year title from *Marketing Magazine* and launched the Straits Times School Pocket Money Fund in 2000, a community project for children from low-income families ([National Library Board](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=382e8b41-dc5a-4091-af00-56ae985f0c5c)).

## The 2021 not-for-profit turn

The most consequential recent change came after SPH recorded its first-ever financial loss in 2020 ([Reuters Institute](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2022/singapore)). In May 2021 SPH proposed transferring its entire media business into a company limited by guarantee; shareholders approved the plan in September, and on 1 December 2021 the transfer to SPH Media Trust was completed, with 2,500 employees and all media assets — including the News Centre and Print Centre properties and the related intellectual property — moving to the trust under a not-for-profit structure ([SPH Media](https://www.sph.com.sg/our-history/); [Reuters Institute](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2022/singapore)). SPH describes the trust as operating "under a not-for-profit structure" and its own legacy as traceable "back to 1845 with the first run of The Straits Times, an English daily" ([SPH Media](https://www.sph.com.sg/our-history/)).

The Reuters Institute notes the restructuring was seen as paving the way for other revenue streams, such as private donations and government support in the face of shrinking advertising revenues ([Reuters Institute](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2022/singapore)). SPH Media has since expanded beyond Singapore, acquiring the technology media company Tech in Asia on 3 January 2024 as part of a push to become a regional player in business and technology news ([SPH Media](https://www.sph.com.sg/our-history/)).

## Covering Cloudflare: the Tokyo manga ruling

*The Straits Times* group covered one of 2025's most consequential internet-law stories: on 19 November 2025 the [Tokyo District Court](/organizations/tokyo-district-court/) ordered Cloudflare to pay 500 million yen (S$4.2 million) to four major Japanese publishers — [Kodansha](/organizations/kodansha/), [Shueisha](/organizations/shueisha/), [Shogakukan](/organizations/shogakukan/) and [Kadokawa](/organizations/kadokawa/) — after the firm was accused of hosting servers for manga piracy sites ([Red Hot Singapore](https://www.redhot.sg/japan-court-orders-cloudflare-to-pay-4-2m-over-manga-piracy-606438.html)). The publishers, who filed suit in [Tokyo](/places/tokyo/) in 2022, said Cloudflare provided a server for "two massive manga piracy sites that distribute over 4,000 manga titles without permission and rack up 300 million views a month" ([Red Hot Singapore](https://www.redhot.sg/japan-court-orders-cloudflare-to-pay-4-2m-over-manga-piracy-606438.html)). The report noted the publishers had previously asked Cloudflare to stop serving a piracy site and reached a settlement in 2019, but said the company kept providing servers for piracy sites — and that Cloudflare planned to appeal ([Red Hot Singapore](https://www.redhot.sg/japan-court-orders-cloudflare-to-pay-4-2m-over-manga-piracy-606438.html)). The dispatch was an [Agence France-Presse](/organizations/agence-france-presse/) wire carried by the paper — this site's Cloudflare profile records the article's publisher as "The Straits Times (Agence France-Presse)" — a reminder that even a national paper of record leans on the global wire agencies for fast-moving international court stories.

## FAQ

**When was The Straits Times founded?** The first issue was published on 15 July 1845 as *The Straits Times, and Singapore Journal of Commerce*, an eight-page weekly edited by Robert Carr Woods. The founding story itself is disputed between two historical accounts, but the date is not.

**Who owns The Straits Times now?** SPH Media Trust, a not-for-profit company limited by guarantee, has operated the paper since 1 December 2021, when Singapore Press Holdings spun off its entire media business. Before that it was owned by the listed company SPH.

**Is The Straits Times Singapore's newspaper of record?** Yes — the Reuters Institute's Digital News Report 2022 calls it "Singapore's newspaper of record" and the country's most popular traditional news brand, used weekly by 37% of Singaporeans.

**What is The Straits Times' circulation?** The latest verified figure is a daily average circulation of 481,700 as of August 2015, with combined print and digital readership of 1.34 million in 2014. No current audited figure was verified for this profile.

**How did The Straits Times cover the Cloudflare manga-piracy case?** Its group carried an AFP dispatch reporting that the Tokyo District Court ordered Cloudflare to pay 500 million yen (S$4.2 million) to four Japanese publishers on 19 November 2025, over servers for manga piracy sites.
