## Overview

Man GLG was the discretionary investment arm of Man Group, the London-listed alternative asset manager. Founded in 1995 as a unit of [Lehman Brothers](/organizations/lehman-brothers/) in [London](/places/london/), it listed on the New York Stock Exchange in 2007 and was bought by Man Group for $1.6 billion in 2010, before the GLG brand was retired in early 2024 and its teams were folded into a combined discretionary division ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/man-group-marks-20th-anniversary-man-glg); [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btg8nzejt406mpen94w0/portfolio/man-glg-merger-underscores-importance-of-scale-in-alternatives); [Citywire](https://citywire.com/wealth-manager/news/man-group-drops-glg-brand-and-division-head-exits/a2435414)). At its peak as a named division it managed just over $30 billion; its last reported figure was $28.6 billion in September 2023 ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/man-group-marks-20th-anniversary-man-glg); [Citywire](https://citywire.com/wealth-manager/news/man-group-drops-glg-brand-and-division-head-exits/a2435414)).

What made Man GLG distinct inside Man Group was that its managers picked stocks by judgement rather than by computer. The GLG brand was built entirely on human-led portfolio managers, in contrast to Man's quantitative engines, Man AHL and Man Numeric, which run systematic strategies ([Citywire](https://citywire.com/wealth-manager/news/man-group-drops-glg-brand-and-division-head-exits/a2435414)). That contrast was also the stated logic of the 2010 deal: the low correlation between Man's quantitative returns and GLG's discretionary returns was expected to steady the combined firm's performance fees ([Man Group plc](https://www.lse.co.uk/rns/EMG/acquisition-mxzxo9n1nqmuhik.html)). The firm's registered vehicle, GLG Partners LP, remains active at Riverbank House, 2 Swan Lane, London, registered on 3 March 2000 ([Companies House](http://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/LP006776)).

## From a Lehman Brothers unit to a New York listing

GLG was founded in 1995 as a division of Lehman Brothers by Noam Gottesman and Pierre Lagrange, and it became an independent business at the turn of the millennium, spinning out in 2000 ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/man-group-marks-20th-anniversary-man-glg); [Citywire](https://citywire.com/wealth-manager/news/man-group-drops-glg-brand-and-division-head-exits/a2435414)). Institutional Investor describes Gottesman and Lagrange as two of the firm's founders, and Gottesman was chairman and co-chief executive alongside Emmanuel Roman by the time of the firm's public listing ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btg8nzejt406mpen94w0/portfolio/man-glg-merger-underscores-importance-of-scale-in-alternatives); [Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/glg-partners-obtain-us-listing-through-reverse-acquisition)).

A public listing followed, engineered through a reverse merger. In 2007 GLG agreed to be acquired by Freedom Acquisition Holdings, a Delaware "blank check" company led by Nicolas Berggruen, in a deal valuing GLG at around $3.4 billion; the combined company took the GLG Partners name and shifted its listing to the [New York Stock Exchange](/organizations/new-york-stock-exchange/) ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/glg-partners-obtain-us-listing-through-reverse-acquisition); [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btg8nzejt406mpen94w0/portfolio/man-glg-merger-underscores-importance-of-scale-in-alternatives)). At the time GLG was Europe's third-largest hedge fund manager, running more than 40 funds with over $20 billion in assets ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/glg-partners-obtain-us-listing-through-reverse-acquisition)). GLG's owners received $1 billion in cash and 230 million Freedom shares, leaving Freedom's shareholders with about 28% of the combined company and GLG's holders with about 72% ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/glg-partners-obtain-us-listing-through-reverse-acquisition)). Two new investors bought in: Istithmar, an investment vehicle of the government of Dubai, and the German private bank Sal. Oppenheim each agreed to take 3% stakes ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/glg-partners-obtain-us-listing-through-reverse-acquisition)).

The financial crisis followed quickly. By the end of 2008 GLG's assets had fallen 40% to $15 billion on poor performance and heavy client redemptions, triggered in part by the departure of star portfolio manager Greg Coffey, and its stock had sunk to about $2 ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btg8nzejt406mpen94w0/portfolio/man-glg-merger-underscores-importance-of-scale-in-alternatives)). In the same period GLG bought Société Générale's UK asset management arm, with $8 billion in assets, expanding its reach into the UK retail market ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btg8nzejt406mpen94w0/portfolio/man-glg-merger-underscores-importance-of-scale-in-alternatives)).

## The Man Group acquisition and the Man GLG years

The firms had already explored combining once before. In late 2006 Man's then-chief executive Stanley Fink met GLG co-CEO Emmanuel Roman to discuss how the two firms might work together, but the talks broke off when Fink stepped down early in 2007 ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btg8nzejt406mpen94w0/portfolio/man-glg-merger-underscores-importance-of-scale-in-alternatives)). When the firms sat down again in February 2010 they agreed to combine, and in May Man agreed to acquire GLG in a $1.6 billion takeover that Institutional Investor called the largest ever between hedge fund firms ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btg8nzejt406mpen94w0/portfolio/man-glg-merger-underscores-importance-of-scale-in-alternatives)).

The terms, announced on 17 May 2010, valued GLG's fully diluted share capital at about $1.6 billion and created a combined manager with about $63 billion of funds under management ([Man Group plc](https://www.lse.co.uk/rns/EMG/acquisition-mxzxo9n1nqmuhik.html)). Public shareholders were offered $4.50 in cash per share, a premium of about 55% to the 14 May 2010 closing price, while the principals — Gottesman, Lagrange and Roman — took 1.0856 new Man shares for each GLG share and agreed to a three-year lock-up on those shares ([Man Group plc](https://www.lse.co.uk/rns/EMG/acquisition-mxzxo9n1nqmuhik.html)). Man identified about $50 million of annual cost savings and expected the deal to close by the end of September 2010, subject to shareholder and regulatory approvals including the Financial Services Authority ([Man Group plc](https://www.lse.co.uk/rns/EMG/acquisition-mxzxo9n1nqmuhik.html)). The acquisition completed later in 2010, GLG became a wholly owned subsidiary, and Man chief executive Peter Clarke promised a fast start as a fully integrated business ([Share Compare](https://www.sharecompare.co.uk/news/man-pledges-fast-integration-of-glg-7sl1yfl3yh7ebys.html)). The acquired business became known as Man GLG ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/man-group-marks-20th-anniversary-man-glg)).

At the acquisition GLG brought more than $10 billion in long-short equity hedge funds, credit funds, macro funds and emerging-markets funds, plus about $12 billion in long-only funds, while Man brought its $21 billion managed-futures business and nearly $15 billion in multi-manager products ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btg8nzejt406mpen94w0/portfolio/man-glg-merger-underscores-importance-of-scale-in-alternatives)). Man said GLG had generated adjusted net income of about $81 million in 2009, and claimed that since its first fund launched in 1997 GLG had earned a 14.1% annualised return on its alternative strategies and 7.3% on long-only strategies, beating its blended benchmark by 2.7% a year ([Man Group plc](https://www.lse.co.uk/rns/EMG/acquisition-mxzxo9n1nqmuhik.html)).

The combined firm kept growing the discretionary side. Mark Jones and Teun Johnston were appointed co-chief executives of Man GLG in 2012, and Roman — who had joined GLG as chief operating officer in 2005 — became Man Group's chief operating officer after the acquisition and its chief executive in 2013 ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/man-group-marks-20th-anniversary-man-glg)). In 2015, its twentieth anniversary year, Man GLG bought leveraged-loan specialist Silvermine in the US and equity manager NewSmith in the UK, adding $5 billion in assets and deepening its expertise in equities, credit and multi-asset strategies; the firm then managed $31 billion with more than 140 investment professionals ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/man-group-marks-20th-anniversary-man-glg)). It was a founding member of the Hedge Fund Standards Board, a signatory of the Principles for Responsible Investment and fully compliant with the Global Investment Performance Standards ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/man-group-marks-20th-anniversary-man-glg)).

The $32 billion figure comes from September 2021, when Man GLG launched the RI Global Sustainable Growth Alternative, a long/short equity fund classified as Article 8 under the EU's Sustainable Finance Disclosures Regulation ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/man-glg-unveils-new-global-sustainability-focused-longshort-hedge-fund); [Trustnet](https://www.trustnet.com/News/13274848/man-glg-launches-global-sustainable-long-short-fund-with-rory-powe/fund/sectors)). Lead portfolio manager Rory Powe, who then managed over $3 billion in European equities, ran it with co-managers Virginia Nordback and Ikitsa Anastasov and a team of four analysts, pairing a high-conviction long portfolio of 25–45 stocks with a short book designed to hedge market and factor risk ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/man-glg-unveils-new-global-sustainability-focused-longshort-hedge-fund)). Powe also led the £1.5 billion Man GLG Continental European Growth fund, which he had run since 2014 ([Trustnet](https://www.trustnet.com/News/13274848/man-glg-launches-global-sustainable-long-short-fund-with-rory-powe/fund/sectors)).

## Retirement of the GLG brand

The GLG name did not survive Man Group's 2024 restructuring. In early 2024 Man Group dropped the $28.6 billion GLG brand, alongside its Global Private Markets and Varagon brands — Varagon being a US private-credit firm bought for $183 million in July 2023 — and created a combined discretionary investing division merging the teams together ([Citywire](https://citywire.com/wealth-manager/news/man-group-drops-glg-brand-and-division-head-exits/a2435414)). The move was the first major change by Robyn Grew, who had taken over as Man Group chief executive in September 2023, at a group then managing $161 billion in total ([Citywire](https://citywire.com/wealth-manager/news/man-group-drops-glg-brand-and-division-head-exits/a2435414)).

Teun Johnston, who had joined the business in 2012 from private equity firm Oakley Capital and become chief executive of GLG in 2016, left as part of the change ([Citywire](https://citywire.com/wealth-manager/news/man-group-drops-glg-brand-and-division-head-exits/a2435414)). The new discretionary division, headed by Eric Burl, was built around public markets, US direct lending and community housing; an internal note from Grew and Burl said the reorganisation would "facilitate freer cross-pollination of ideas – particularly in credit" ([Citywire](https://citywire.com/wealth-manager/news/man-group-drops-glg-brand-and-division-head-exits/a2435414)). The Numeric and AHL brands were retained but kept as separate entities from the discretionary business ([Citywire](https://citywire.com/wealth-manager/news/man-group-drops-glg-brand-and-division-head-exits/a2435414)). Man Group did not formally comment on the restructure ([Citywire](https://citywire.com/wealth-manager/news/man-group-drops-glg-brand-and-division-head-exits/a2435414)). The retirement ended the GLG name as a client-facing brand after nearly three decades, although its legal vehicle, GLG Partners LP, remains registered and active in London ([Companies House](http://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/LP006776)).

## The Behavox connection

Man GLG's most consequential alumnus for this site's entity graph is Erkin Adylov, founder and chief executive of [Behavox](/organizations/behavox/), the AI compliance-surveillance firm. Before founding Behavox in 2014, Adylov worked as an equities analyst at [Goldman Sachs](/organizations/goldman-sachs/) and then ran a $2 billion hedge fund as a partner at GLG ([Fintech Global](https://fintech.global/amltechforum/speaker/erkin-adylov/)). His career is the link between the two profiles: the surveillance software Behavox sells to banks, hedge funds and asset managers was built by someone who had managed money inside the institutions it now monitors, first at Goldman Sachs and then at GLG ([Fintech Global](https://fintech.global/amltechforum/speaker/erkin-adylov/)).
