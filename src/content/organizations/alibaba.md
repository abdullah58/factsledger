## Overview

Alibaba Group is a Chinese technology company founded in Hangzhou in 1999 by Jack Ma and 17 co-founders, which grew from a wholesale marketplace for small exporters into the owner of [China](/places/china/)'s largest consumer shopping platforms, Taobao and Tmall, and its largest cloud provider, Alibaba Cloud ([British Council](https://www.britishcouncil.org/voices-magazine/how-jack-ma-built-one-worlds-biggest-companies)). Its stated mission is to "make it easy to do business anywhere" ([British Council](https://www.britishcouncil.org/voices-magazine/how-jack-ma-built-one-worlds-biggest-companies)). In its latest act, Alibaba has become a central player in China's foundation-model race: its open-weight Qwen models power [Apple](/organizations/apple/) Intelligence in China, after Apple evaluated [Baidu](/organizations/baidu/), [Tencent](/organizations/tencent/), ByteDance, Alibaba and DeepSeek and chose Alibaba ([Longbridge](https://longbridge.com/en/news/304602549)).

## From a Hangzhou apartment to China's biggest marketplaces

Alibaba began in 1999 when Jack Ma, a former English teacher, founded the company with his wife and 16 others in an apartment in Hangzhou, the capital of Zhejiang province ([British Council](https://www.britishcouncil.org/voices-magazine/how-jack-ma-built-one-worlds-biggest-companies)). Its first business was Alibaba.com, a business-to-business platform that helped small and medium-sized Chinese enterprises reach export buyers they had previously struggled to access ([British Council](https://www.britishcouncil.org/voices-magazine/how-jack-ma-built-one-worlds-biggest-companies)). Early backing followed quickly: a SoftBank-led group invested $20 million in January 2000, and Yahoo paid $1 billion for a 40% stake in August 2005, becoming Alibaba's largest shareholder ([CNBC](https://cnbc.africa/2019/billionaire-jack-ma-steps-down-as-alibabas-chairman-here-are-key-moments-in-the-companys-history)).

The move into consumer shopping came in May 2003, when Alibaba launched Taobao, a marketplace where small merchants and individuals could set up storefronts for free ([CNBC](https://cnbc.africa/2019/billionaire-jack-ma-steps-down-as-alibabas-chairman-here-are-key-moments-in-the-companys-history)). Taobao was built to win the domestic market as eBay expanded into China, and by the mid-2010s more than nine million merchants generated much of their revenue on the platform ([British Council](https://www.britishcouncil.org/voices-magazine/how-jack-ma-built-one-worlds-biggest-companies)). Tmall followed in April 2008, launched as Taobao Mall as a dedicated home for brands and larger retailers, which pay a set-up fee and a commission on sales ([CNBC](https://cnbc.africa/2019/billionaire-jack-ma-steps-down-as-alibabas-chairman-here-are-key-moments-in-the-companys-history)). Payments arrived in December 2004 with Alipay, which grew into one of China's two big payments platforms alongside Tencent's WeChat Pay ([CNBC](https://cnbc.africa/2019/billionaire-jack-ma-steps-down-as-alibabas-chairman-here-are-key-moments-in-the-companys-history)).

Alibaba's infrastructure bets came next. It launched its cloud business in September 2009 — then chief executive Daniel Zhang later called cloud the firm's future "main business" — and pioneered the Singles Day shopping festival that November ([CNBC](https://cnbc.africa/2019/billionaire-jack-ma-steps-down-as-alibabas-chairman-here-are-key-moments-in-the-companys-history)). Alibaba Cloud has held on to that lead: it retained pole position in mainland China's cloud market in the first quarter of 2024 with a 37% share, ahead of Huawei Cloud at 19% and Tencent Cloud at 16%, according to Canalys ([Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/cloud/mainland-china-s-cloud-service-spend-grew-by-20-in-q1-2024-canalys)).

## Public markets and the regulatory reckoning

Alibaba first went public in [Hong Kong](/places/hong-kong/) in November 2007, when Alibaba.com raised HK$13.1 billion, and was taken private again in 2012 for $2.45 billion ([CNBC](https://cnbc.africa/2019/billionaire-jack-ma-steps-down-as-alibabas-chairman-here-are-key-moments-in-the-companys-history)). Its defining market moment came in September 2014, when it listed on the New York Stock Exchange and raised $25 billion in what was then the largest initial public offering in history ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2014/10/hong-kong-v-new-york-the-competition-for-chinese-t)). The following month it created Ant Financial to house Alipay and its other financial services, and in February 2018 it bought a 33% stake in Ant ([CNBC](https://cnbc.africa/2019/billionaire-jack-ma-steps-down-as-alibabas-chairman-here-are-key-moments-in-the-companys-history)).

The relationship with regulators then turned. In May 2011 Alibaba had already sold control of Alipay to a group controlled by Jack Ma, a move Yahoo said happened without its knowledge and which raised questions about the company's governance ([CNBC](https://cnbc.africa/2019/billionaire-jack-ma-steps-down-as-alibabas-chairman-here-are-key-moments-in-the-companys-history)). The sharper break came on 3 November 2020, when regulators suspended Ant Group's planned dual listing in Shanghai and Hong Kong just two days before it was due to happen, citing "major issues" that "may fail to meet information disclosure requirements" ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/generationlisten/2020-11-03/regulators-squash-giant-ant-ipo)). The offering had been expected to raise an estimated $37 billion and value Ant at more than $300 billion, and Alibaba's own shares fell more than 9% on the news ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/generationlisten/2020-11-03/regulators-squash-giant-ant-ipo)). Ant had been spun off from Alibaba and was controlled by Ma; its Alipay app was China's largest mobile payment platform, handling an estimated $16 trillion in online payments in the prior year ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/generationlisten/2020-11-03/regulators-squash-giant-ant-ipo)).

Five months later, on 10 April 2021, China's State Administration for Market Regulation fined Alibaba RMB 18.2 billion (about $2.8 billion) for abusing its dominant position in online retail, centred on "forced exclusivity" — barring merchants from selling on rival platforms — equal to 4% of its 2019 revenue in China ([TechNode](http://technode.com/2021/04/10/china-regulator-fines-alibaba-2-8-billion-for-antitrust-violations/)). It was the largest antitrust penalty issued in China at the time, and Alibaba said it accepted the penalty ([TechNode](http://technode.com/2021/04/10/china-regulator-fines-alibaba-2-8-billion-for-antitrust-violations/)).

Alibaba's response to the squeeze was structural. On 28 March 2023 it announced its "1+6+N" restructuring — the biggest in its 24-year history — splitting into six business groups (Cloud Intelligence, Taobao Tmall Commerce, Local Services, Cainiao Smart Logistics, Global Digital Commerce, and Digital Media and Entertainment), each with its own chief executive and able to seek outside funding or separate listings, while Taobao Tmall would remain wholly owned ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/will-alibabas-historic-split-into-6-new-business-units-supercharge-its-stock)). Jack Ma himself had already stepped back, leaving the chairmanship on 10 September 2019, when Daniel Zhang succeeded him ([CNBC](https://cnbc.africa/2019/billionaire-jack-ma-steps-down-as-alibabas-chairman-here-are-key-moments-in-the-companys-history)).

## Qwen and the race for China's foundation models

Alibaba entered the large-language-model race in April 2023 with Tongyi Qianwen, months after ChatGPT had jolted China's technology industry, and began releasing model weights publicly soon afterwards ([Longbridge](https://longbridge.com/en/news/304602549)). The Qwen family became the vehicle: Qwen 2.5-Max was presented by Alibaba as outperforming DeepSeek-V3, and in February 2025 the company pledged at least RMB 380 billion — about $52–53 billion — over three years for cloud and AI infrastructure, more than it had spent on the two areas over the previous decade ([Tech Startups](https://techstartups.com/2025/02/24/alibaba-to-invest-52-billion-in-ai-after-qwen-2-5-models-breakthrough-success/)). Chief executive Eddie Wu described AI as a "once-in-a-generation" opportunity, with artificial general intelligence as the company's primary long-term objective ([Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/ai-machine-learning/alibaba-on-a-roll-as-it-preps-53b-ai-plunge)).

Qwen3, released in late April 2025, pushed the open-weights strategy further: eight models ranging from 0.6 billion to 235 billion parameters, offered under the Apache 2.0 licence, with a hybrid design that switches between slow "thinking" mode for complex tasks and fast answers for simple ones ([Tech Edition](https://www.techedt.com/alibaba-reveals-qwen3-a-powerful-new-series-of-ai-models)). The models were trained on nearly 36 trillion tokens across 119 languages, and Alibaba said some of them surpassed leading rival models on key benchmarks — a company claim, not independent verification ([Tech Edition](https://www.techedt.com/alibaba-reveals-qwen3-a-powerful-new-series-of-ai-models)). Reuters framed the launch as part of an intensifying Chinese AI race following DeepSeek's rise ([Longbridge](https://longbridge.com/en/news/304602549)).

The commercial payoff arrived through Apple. After evaluating Chinese AI providers including Baidu, Tencent, ByteDance, Alibaba and DeepSeek, Apple chose Alibaba as its partner for Apple Intelligence in China ([Longbridge](https://longbridge.com/en/news/304602549)). China requires generative-AI services to be registered with regulators before they reach the public, and Reuters reported on 9 November 2026 that Apple Intelligence had been registered for tablets and laptops in China, following approval for iPhones in July ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/apple-intelligence-ai-service-registers-with-chinese-regulator-use-tablets-2026-11-09/)). For Alibaba, the deal turns Qwen from a developer favourite — with more than 2 billion downloads on Hugging Face in the first seven months of 2026, according to Longbridge — into the AI layer inside one of the world's largest hardware ecosystems in China ([Longbridge](https://longbridge.com/en/news/304602549)). It also recasts the old rivalry: Baidu was among the providers Apple evaluated and passed over in favour of Alibaba's Qwen.
