## Overview

Tourism 4.0 is a Slovenian tourism-innovation initiative launched in 2018 by Arctur, the Nova Gorica
technology company led by CEO Tomi Ilijaš, with the aim of transferring technologies such as
high-performance data analytics, the Internet of Things and blockchain from Industry 4.0 into tourism
([BlueInvest](https://blueinvest-community.converve.io/./pipeline_front.html?action=detail&params%5Bshow%5D=comp&params%5B_filtered%5D=1&params%5Bq%5D=&params%5Bpage%5D=3&params%5Bevent_id%5D=1&params%5Bother_id%5D=907)).
SPIRIT Slovenia — the country's public agency for entrepreneurship and internationalization — calls it
the largest R&D partnership in the Slovenian tourism sector, and the European Commission's Transition
Pathways platform describes the underlying project as Slovenia's largest publicly funded tourism R&D
initiative ([SPIRIT Slovenia](https://tourism4-0.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Razvoj-Turizma-4.0-izjava-za-javnostang_20211020.pdf);
[European Commission](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu)).

## Origins and the 2018–2021 R&D project

The core funded project ran from September 2018 to August 2021 at technology readiness levels 3 to 6,
with Arctur and three Slovenian universities — Ljubljana, Maribor and Primorska — as partners, working
toward a smart-tourism data platform ([European Commission](https://transition-pathways.europa.eu)). A
2026 retrospective by the University of Nova Gorica corroborates that starting point, quoting Arctur's
innovation lead on launching "the first Tourism 4.0 project under the TRL 3–6 programs" eight years
earlier — that is, in 2018 ([University of Nova Gorica](https://ung.si/en/news/news/7106/university-of-nova-gorica-participates-in-the-flux-40-project-the-next-generation-of-data-supported-sustainable-tourism/)).
Around the initiative, Arctur built an international partnership reported at 180+ members from academia,
business and other institutions in a third-party EU-project profile, and at 200+ members worldwide in the
European Commission's BlueInvest pipeline ([Digital meets Culture](https://www.digitalmeetsculture.net/article/meet-weave-team-arctur/?upm_export=pdf);
[BlueInvest](https://blueinvest-community.converve.io/./pipeline_front.html?action=detail&params%5Bshow%5D=comp&params%5B_filtered%5D=1&params%5Bq%5D=&params%5Bpage%5D=3&params%5Bevent_id%5D=1&params%5Bother_id%5D=907)).

On June 17, 2021, the initiative held its national "Development of Tourism 4.0" conference online,
attended by five Slovenian state secretaries, all the country's large universities, SPIRIT, the
Association of Slovenian Municipalities and UNESCO. The conference produced ten key points for tourism
development — including permanent mechanisms for systematic data gathering at public institutions — and
Nova Gorica's mayor said the city would draw on Tourism 4.0 knowledge to manage visitor growth for its
2025 European Capital of Culture year ([SPIRIT Slovenia](https://tourism4-0.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Razvoj-Turizma-4.0-izjava-za-javnostang_20211020.pdf)).

## The Tourism Impact Model

The initiative's flagship tool is the Tourism Impact Model (TIM): an online, data-driven instrument built
on slightly more than 300 indicators spanning environment, economy, society, culture and collaboration,
designed to give an objective picture of both the positive and negative effects of tourism at a
destination — effectively a digital twin of the destination for strategy-makers ([BlueInvest](https://blueinvest-community.converve.io/./pipeline_front.html?action=detail&params%5Bshow%5D=comp&params%5B_filtered%5D=1&params%5Bq%5D=&params%5Bpage%5D=3&params%5Bevent_id%5D=1&params%5Bother_id%5D=907);
[SPIRIT Slovenia](https://www.sloveniabusiness.eu/success-stories/ambassadors/arctur-an-online-tool-tourism-impact-model/)).
According to the Slovenian Tourist Board, assessments with TIM were being carried out at six Black Sea
destinations, 24 in the Danube region and five Slovenian municipalities as of late 2020 — though
SPIRIT's business portal gives a slightly different count of 24 European destinations plus six in
Slovenia, a discrepancy this profile does not resolve ([Slovenian Tourist Board](https://www.slovenia.info/en/press-centre/news-of-the-tourism-press-agency/14589-a-slovenian-company-awarded-for-their-tourism-impact-model-tool);
[SPIRIT Slovenia](https://www.sloveniabusiness.eu/success-stories/ambassadors/arctur-an-online-tool-tourism-impact-model/)).

The initiative's technology pillars are described as the T4.0 Core APIs — built on high-performance
computing infrastructure with AI, blockchain and high-performance data analytics — connecting the
pillars DOTI (a personal digital passport for tourist data ownership), CIT (a blockchain-based voucher
rewarding positive behaviour and redirecting tourist flows), FLOWS (tourist-flow analysis) and TIM
itself; one of its larger programmes is Heritage+, which uses 3D scanning, augmented reality and virtual
reality to build enriched cultural-heritage experiences ([Digital meets Culture](https://www.digitalmeetsculture.net/article/meet-weave-team-arctur/?upm_export=pdf)).

## The Black Sea project

The "Tourism 4.0 for the Black Sea" (T4BS) project, co-funded by the EU's European Maritime and
Fisheries Fund under the Blue Growth programme and coordinated by Arctur, tested Tourism 4.0 tools —
the impact model, big-data analysis and digitalisation — with public and private stakeholders around
the Black Sea ([European Commission (CINEA)](http://cinea.ec.europa.eu/featured-projects/t4bs_en)).
Partners included HCL Consultants (Greece), the Tourism Institute (Georgia), Ovidius University of
Constanța and Sano Touring (Romania), and the NGO Agricola (Ukraine); the project ran about ten
stakeholder-engagement events, including three rounds of local workshops in Batumi, Constanța and
Odessa engaging roughly 100 experts, and closed with a final event in Ljubljana on November 18, 2021
([ICBSS](https://icbss.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/T4BS-Tourism-4.0-potentials-for-the-Black-Sea_ICBSS-Paper_final-.pdf);
[European Commission (CINEA)](http://cinea.ec.europa.eu/featured-projects/t4bs_en)).

In Georgia, TIM was piloted in Batumi and the Adjara region from November 2019 to November 2021, with
the Department of Tourism and Resorts of Adjara and the Georgian Maritime Transport Agency as official
partners ([Caucasus University](https://cu.edu.ge/en/schoolss/cts/cts-news/cts-is-tsarmomadgenlebis-monatsileoba-forumshi-turizmi-digitalizatsia-da-mdgradi-ganvitareba-shavi-zghvis-kveqnebshi)).
An independent assessment by the International Centre for Black Sea Studies found the project raised
real awareness — in Georgia, an unprecedented 200+ indicators were collected and shared with public,
private and academic stakeholders — but also exposed hard limits: private data owners treated data as
trade secrets, much of the needed data was infrequently collected or inaccessible, and in several
places only partial assessments were possible ([ICBSS](https://icbss.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/T4BS-Tourism-4.0-potentials-for-the-Black-Sea_ICBSS-Paper_final-.pdf)).

## Recognition

At the Tourism Innovation Summit held November 25–27, 2020 in Seville, TIM won the award for best
innovation in artificial intelligence and data analytics — an event the Slovenian Tourist Board
describes as a deliberate statement that such gatherings could still succeed under COVID-19 conditions
([Slovenian Tourist Board](https://www.slovenia.info/en/press-centre/news-of-the-tourism-press-agency/14589-a-slovenian-company-awarded-for-their-tourism-impact-model-tool)).
The same year, the Chamber of Commerce and Industry of Slovenia awarded TIM its golden plaquette as the
best innovation of the North Primorska region ([SPIRIT Slovenia](https://www.sloveniabusiness.eu/success-stories/ambassadors/arctur-an-online-tool-tourism-impact-model/)).
In 2021, SPIRIT Slovenia selected Arctur as one of 41 ambassadors in its "I feel Slovenia. Green.
Creative. Smart." campaign promoting the Slovenian economy abroad, citing TIM as the breakthrough
solution ([SPIRIT Slovenia](https://tourism4-0.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Razvoj-Turizma-4.0-izjava-za-javnostang_20211020.pdf)).

## People and continuation

The initiative's public face has been Tomi Ilijaš, Arctur's CEO, alongside Dr. Urška Starc Peceny,
identified at the 2025 Slovenian Global Forum as Arctur's Head of Innovation and Head of Tourism 4.0
and in 2026 as the company's Director of Innovation and Head of its Tourism Department
([The International](https://theinternational.si/slovenian-global-forum-2025/);
[University of Nova Gorica](https://ung.si/en/news/news/7106/university-of-nova-gorica-participates-in-the-flux-40-project-the-next-generation-of-data-supported-sustainable-tourism/)).

In August 2026, Arctur launched Flux 4.0 — Managing Flows for Sustainable Tourism, a three-year R&D
programme bringing together twelve partners from industry and research, including the University of
Ljubljana (three faculties), the University of Maribor (two faculties), the University of Nova Gorica
and the University of Primorska's tourism faculty. Its stated goal is a comprehensive data ecosystem —
the Flux 4.0 platform and a prototype digital twin of tourism — combining real-time data for
forecasting, simulation and decision support at local, regional and national levels
([University of Nova Gorica](https://ung.si/en/news/news/7106/university-of-nova-gorica-participates-in-the-flux-40-project-the-next-generation-of-data-supported-sustainable-tourism/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not resolve the small discrepancy between sources on how many destinations TIM was
tested at, nor does it merge the partnership-size figures reported at different times (180+ and 200+
members). It does not treat the European Commission Transition Pathways citation as a directly
viewable project page — the URL is the platform's general page — though the "largest" characterization
is now independently echoed by SPIRIT Slovenia. It does not present the technology-pillar descriptions
as independently verified engineering assessments; they come from initiative-aligned material. It makes
no claim about whether Tourism 4.0 continues to operate as a distinct active initiative after the 2021
project phase — Flux 4.0 is documented here as a separate follow-on programme led by the same company,
not as the same entity under a new name.
