## Overview

The All-In podcast began as four venture capitalists kibitzing on air during COVID, when they could no longer gather for their weekly poker game — and by September 2026 it had become, in Axios's framing, the template for the Trump administration's entire AI doctrine ([Axios](https://canisgallicus.com/2026/09/23/axios-the-all-in-ization-of-trump/)). The show's hosts — Chamath Palihapitiya, Jason Calacanis, [David Sacks](https://factsledger.com/people/david-sacks/) and David Friedberg, who call themselves "The Besties" — built a weekly roundtable into a franchise that now includes an annual $7,500-a-ticket summit, a tequila line, and a direct personnel pipeline into the White House: Sacks served as Trump's AI czar in the administration's first year and now co-chairs the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology, Friedberg sits on the same council, and Palihapitiya was floated for the new AI czar job Trump announced in September 2026 ([Axios](https://canisgallicus.com/2026/09/23/axios-the-all-in-ization-of-trump/)). Along the way the podcast stopped being merely a place to discuss politics and became a place where policy is announced: Vice President JD Vance used a September 15, 2026 episode to unveil the administration's administrative strategy behind the $100,000 H-1B fee, then clipped it for his own official feed twelve days later ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/world/us-news/jd-vance-supports-100000-h1b-visa-fee-trump-policy-tech-layoffs-10897482/)).

## The show

All-In is a weekly business-and-technology podcast hosted by four investors and entrepreneurs: Chamath Palihapitiya, the founder and CEO of Social Capital; Jason Calacanis, the entrepreneur and angel investor who serves as the show's moderator; David Sacks, a Craft Ventures general partner and former PayPal executive; and David Friedberg, founder and CEO of The Production Board ([Axios](https://canisgallicus.com/2026/09/23/axios-the-all-in-ization-of-trump/)). The show launched in March 2020 — the feed dates to March 15 of that year per Listen Notes, though some listings give March 19, a minor discrepancy noted below ([Listen Notes](https://www.listennotes.com/podcasts/all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-0eWaLuirNTJ/)). Its own self-description is the closest thing to a mission statement: "Industry veterans, degenerate gamblers & besties Chamath Palihapitiya, Jason Calacanis, David Sacks & David Friedberg cover all things economic, tech, political, social & poker" ([Listen Notes](https://www.listennotes.com/podcasts/all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-0eWaLuirNTJ/)).

The numbers show how large the audience has become. Listen Notes ranks the show in the top 0.01% of podcasts globally with a Listen Score of 73, counting 431 episodes averaging 77 minutes each, with roughly 63.8% of its measured listeners in the United States ([Listen Notes](https://www.listennotes.com/podcasts/all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-0eWaLuirNTJ/)). The show's own site illustrates the cadence and the topic mix: by late August 2026 it was at episode #287 — the archive shows episodes on August 14, 15 and 21 covering AI regulation, NVIDIA and Salesforce earnings, government spending, media's influence, the Ukraine-Russia conflict, and pharmaceutical pricing ([All-In Podcast](https://allin.com/)). Episodes routinely stretch well past an hour, and the video edition has made the hosts recognizable faces rather than disembodied voices ([Listen Notes](https://www.listennotes.com/podcasts/all-in-with-chamath-jason-sacks-friedberg-0eWaLuirNTJ/)).

## From poker table to the White House

The show's origin is a card game. Axios's September 23, 2026 analysis — the sharpest single account of what the podcast has become — describes it as having "grown out of four hyperconnected tech entrepreneurs kibitzing on air during COVID, when they could no longer gather for their weekly poker game," and notes that it has since expanded into "lucrative events and luxe tequila" ([Axios](https://canisgallicus.com/2026/09/23/axios-the-all-in-ization-of-trump/)). The thesis of that piece is blunt: "President Trump's accelerationist AI doctrine increasingly bears the stamp of the 'All-In' podcasters, whose tech-investor network now feeds ideas and talent directly into the White House" ([Axios](https://canisgallicus.com/2026/09/23/axios-the-all-in-ization-of-trump/)).

The bridge between the two worlds is David Sacks. Axios credits him with building Trump's original AI agenda around a simple premise: "Move fast, sweep away regulatory barriers and treat China — not catastrophic risk — as the overriding threat" ([Axios](https://canisgallicus.com/2026/09/23/axios-the-all-in-ization-of-trump/)). Sacks spent the administration's first year as Trump's AI czar and, after leaving the White House, stayed in the president's inner circle as co-chair of the Trump-created President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology (PCAST), remaining what Axios calls "a powerful outside adviser with direct access to the president" ([Axios](https://canisgallicus.com/2026/09/23/axios-the-all-in-ization-of-trump/)). The council seat did not stay lonely: fellow host David Friedberg also serves on PCAST, alongside investor Marc Andreessen and Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg among others, while Palihapitiya — described by Axios as "a prominent Trump booster" — was among the names floated for the new AI czar job Trump announced the weekend before the piece ran ([Axios](https://canisgallicus.com/2026/09/23/axios-the-all-in-ization-of-trump/)).

Calacanis is the outlier in that lineup. Axios notes that the moderator is "the political outlier — frequently criticizing Trump on Iran and inflation, while otherwise aligning with his co-hosts on the revolutionary nature of AI" ([Axios](https://canisgallicus.com/2026/09/23/axios-the-all-in-ization-of-trump/)). That tension — a show that routinely defends Trump while its own moderator publicly breaks with him on major issues — is part of what makes the franchise work: disagreement is the format, not a bug.

The fusion of the show and the administration has produced moments that read like stagecraft from another era. When former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon's AI-safety warnings exploded across Washington, the hosts devoted much of their next episode to portraying the backlash as an orchestrated "doomer" campaign; three days later, NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang paused his live All-In Summit interview to take a call from Trump, who "drew a raucous reception as he dismissed AI catastrophe fears as a 'hoax'" ([Axios](https://canisgallicus.com/2026/09/23/axios-the-all-in-ization-of-trump/)). Axios also reports that Sacks has blessed "a $100 million-plus midterm operation built to advance Trump's AI agenda and punish candidates who favor tougher regulation" — and that inside AI labs, the tight coupling of the show to one president is feeding a fear that warnings about catastrophic risk will be "coded as anti-Trump politics, turning AI safety into yet another partisan litmus test" ([Axios](https://canisgallicus.com/2026/09/23/axios-the-all-in-ization-of-trump/)).

## The summit: a $7,500-a-ticket media business

Parallel to the podcast runs a live-events franchise that is a business in its own right. The All-In Summit began in 2022 — the 2027 edition, set for September 12–14 in Los Angeles at the Shrine Auditorium and Expo Hall, is billed as the sixth annual ([All-In Summit](https://theallinsummit.com)). Admission is deliberately exclusive: attendance requires a $500 application fee, reviewed through a "competitive application process" to produce a "curated," "hand-selected" audience, and the total attendee pass costs $7,500, covering programming, dining, excursions and local transportation ([All-In Summit](https://theallinsummit.com)). The program leans into the show's poker-table origins — "our biggest poker tournament ever" is a marquee feature — alongside epic parties at LA venues, high-end dinners and structured networking ([All-In Summit](https://theallinsummit.com)). The organizers do not pre-announce speakers: "More of the world's top founders, investors, and leaders will take this stage. We're not announcing them. Get your ticket and find out in the room" ([All-In Summit](https://theallinsummit.com)).

## Announcing policy on air

Long before the Axios thesis, the show had already crossed from commenting on power to hosting it. In June 2024, after months of the hosts saying Donald Trump would appear, Trump became the official guest of that week's show — a booking Bloomberg Law said "confirms the show's status as an emerging voice in politics with Silicon Valley flavor," noting the hosts "mostly discuss technology trends, but also give their takes on regulation, policy and elections with some chatter about poker in between" ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/private-equity/vcs-sacks-palihapitiya-land-trump-as-all-in-podcast-guest)).

The deeper institutional step came on July 23, 2025, when the podcast co-hosted the "Winning the AI Race" summit in Washington, D.C., with the bipartisan Hill and Valley Forum ([Associated Press](https://www.republicworld.com/world-news/from-tech-podcasts-to-policy-trump-s-new-ai-plan-leans-heavily-on-silicon-valley-industry-ideas)). The half-day program — moderated by the hosts alongside the forum's co-founders — served as Trump's first major AI address of his second term, following his Day One executive order on AI and previewing the upcoming AI Action Plan; the keynote coincided with Trump's signing of AI executive orders ([Associated Press](https://www.republicworld.com/world-news/from-tech-podcasts-to-policy-trump-s-new-ai-plan-leans-heavily-on-silicon-valley-industry-ideas)). The expected contents read like a wish list from the show's own audience: accelerating AI chip exports abroad, easier permitting for the energy-hungry data centers the industry needs, and a "woke AI" counteroffensive against perceived liberal bias in chatbots — a cause Sacks had been pushing for over a year ([Associated Press](https://www.republicworld.com/world-news/from-tech-podcasts-to-policy-trump-s-new-ai-plan-leans-heavily-on-silicon-valley-industry-ideas)).

By 2026 the pattern was routine. Vice President JD Vance sat for the September 15 episode and used it to unveil the administration's rationale for the $100,000 H-1B fee — that the administration had examined the "fraud" in the system, that "Congress is not going to pass a law changing this. There just isn't the political will to do it," and that the fee would force companies to reserve H-1B visas for what Vance called "an actual genius" rather than replacing a $60,000-a-year American accountant with a $45,000 foreign one ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/world/us-news/jd-vance-supports-100000-h1b-visa-fee-trump-policy-tech-layoffs-10897482/)). Vance's sharpest line — "The H-1B should not exist to replace American workers with low-wage foreigners. It should exist to enrich the American economy" — was then clipped and posted to his own @VP feed on September 27 ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/world/us-news/jd-vance-supports-100000-h1b-visa-fee-trump-policy-tech-layoffs-10897482/)). The episode doubled as the administration's explainer for a September 18 order directing agencies to weigh employers' layoff histories when reviewing H-1B cases ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/world/us-news/jd-vance-supports-100000-h1b-visa-fee-trump-policy-tech-layoffs-10897482/)).
