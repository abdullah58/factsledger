## Overview

The Mastercard Foundation is an independent Canadian charity created in 2006 from a gift of shares when [Mastercard](/organizations/mastercard/) became a public company, and it is now one of the world's largest foundations, with more than $53 billion in assets. Based in [Toronto](/places/toronto/), [Ontario](/places/ontario/), it funds education and financial inclusion so young people in Africa and Indigenous youth in [Canada](/places/canada/) can access dignified and fulfilling work — and it is a separate organisation from Mastercard, governed by its own board ([Mastercard Foundation](https://mastercardfdn.org/en/news/mastercard-foundation-board-of-directors-announces-seasoned-technology-leader-sewit-ahderom-as-next-president-and-ceo/); [OECD](https://read.oecd-ilibrary.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/06/development-co-operation-profiles_02ffa45c/mastercard-foundation_71a064d2/7a272340-en.pdf)).

It works through about 400 partner organisations in 33 countries, with offices in Toronto, Kigali, Accra, Nairobi, Kampala, Lagos, Dakar and Addis Ababa ([Mastercard Foundation](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20240502ae03296/)). Its two strategies are Young Africa Works — 30 million young Africans into dignified and fulfilling work by 2030 — and EleV — 100,000 Indigenous young people in Canada through post-secondary education into work by the same year ([Mastercard Foundation](https://mastercardfdn.org/en/news/mastercard-foundation-board-of-directors-announces-seasoned-technology-leader-sewit-ahderom-as-next-president-and-ceo/)). To date it says its programs have equipped more than 66 million people with skills and tools and enabled nearly 18 million young people to access work ([Mastercard Foundation](https://mastercardfdn.org/en/news/mastercard-foundation-board-of-directors-announces-seasoned-technology-leader-sewit-ahderom-as-next-president-and-ceo/)).

## Created by an IPO gift — and set free

When Mastercard became a public company in 2006, it gifted the new foundation company shares plus US$40 million, to advance financial inclusion and education for people living in poverty ([Mastercard Foundation](https://mastercardfdn.org/en/about-us/foundation-history/)). For its first 17 years the Foundation's Mastercard stockholdings were its sole source of funding ([Mastercard Investor Relations](https://investor.mastercard.com/investor-news/investor-news-details/2023/Mastercard-Foundation-Proposes-Diversified-Investment-Strategy-to-Deliver-on-Long-Term-Philanthropic-Goals/default.aspx)). The original Deed of Gift barred the Foundation from selling those shares, except to meet the Canadian disbursement quota and running costs, until 1 May 2027 ([Mastercard Investor Relations](https://investor.mastercard.com/investor-news/investor-news-details/2023/Mastercard-Foundation-Proposes-Diversified-Investment-Strategy-to-Deliver-on-Long-Term-Philanthropic-Goals/default.aspx)).

Independence was built in: the Foundation is an independent organisation whose policies, operations and program decisions are set by its own board and leadership, not by Mastercard ([OECD](https://read.oecd-ilibrary.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/06/development-co-operation-profiles_02ffa45c/mastercard-foundation_71a064d2/7a272340-en.pdf)). In 2023 it applied to vary the Deed of Gift so diversification could begin on 1 January 2024, planning a seven-year orderly sell-down of 13–15% of its initial shares a year, keeping roughly 10% of its portfolio in Mastercard stock ([Mastercard Investor Relations](https://investor.mastercard.com/investor-news/investor-news-details/2023/Mastercard-Foundation-Proposes-Diversified-Investment-Strategy-to-Deliver-on-Long-Term-Philanthropic-Goals/default.aspx)). It created a separate asset manager, the Mastercard Foundation Asset Management Corporation, introduced in May 2024 under chief investment officer John Barker, to run the diversified portfolio ([Mastercard Foundation](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20240502ae03296/)).

## Young Africa Works and EleV

In 2009 the board decided to focus its resources on Africa, home to the youngest and fastest-growing population in the world ([Philanthropic Foundations Canada](https://pfc.ca/blog/together-25-ensemble-qa-reeta-roy-mastercard-foundation/)). Young Africa Works aims to enable 30 million young Africans — 70% of them young women — to access dignified and fulfilling work by 2030, focusing also on refugees and displaced people and on people with disabilities ([Mastercard Foundation](https://mastercardfdn.org/en/about-us/foundation-history/); [Philanthropic Foundations Canada](https://pfc.ca/blog/together-25-ensemble-qa-reeta-roy-mastercard-foundation/)). The Foundation has set itself a benchmark of 75% African-led partners, meaning institutions based in Africa, registered in Africa and led by Africans ([Philanthropic Foundations Canada](https://pfc.ca/blog/together-25-ensemble-qa-reeta-roy-mastercard-foundation/)). [Colleges and Institutes Canada](/organizations/colleges-and-institutes-canada/) counts Young Africa Works among the foundation partnerships in its own network.

In Canada the counterpart is EleV, co-created in September 2017 with Indigenous communities in response to the Truth and Reconciliation Commission of Canada's Calls to Action ([Mastercard Foundation](https://mastercardfdn.org/en/about-us/foundation-history/)). EleV aims to enable 100,000 Indigenous young people to access post-secondary education and transition to meaningful livelihoods by 2030, backed by US$500 million through more than 45 partners ([Philanthropic Foundations Canada](https://pfc.ca/blog/together-25-ensemble-qa-reeta-roy-mastercard-foundation/)). The Foundation has also approved inclusion strategies aimed at 1.5 million youth with disabilities and 2.5 million refugees and displaced youth ([Mastercard Foundation](https://mastercardfdn.org/en/about-us/foundation-history/)).

## Scholars, vaccines and the money

The Foundation's best-known program is the Scholars Program, launched on 26 September 2012 at a [United Nations](/organizations/united-nations/) special session: a US$500 million initiative giving 15,000 talented, economically disadvantaged students, particularly from Africa, full scholarships and comprehensive support ([The MasterCard Foundation](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/the-mastercard-foundation-launches-500-million-scholars-program-to-empower-young-people-to-lead-change-across-africa-510831291.html)). Early partners included [Arizona State University](/universities/arizona-state-university/), [Duke University](/universities/duke-university/) and [Stanford University](/universities/stanford-university/), and UC Berkeley received US$30 million for 113 students over eight years ([UC Berkeley News](http://news.berkeley.edu/2012/09/26/mastercard-foundation-brings-sub-saharan-african-students-to-uc-berkeleyer/)). By its tenth anniversary the program had deployed US$1.7 billion to benefit nearly 40,000 young people, over 72% of them young women, and it aims to double its reach to 100,000 by 2030 ([African Media Agency](https://africanmediaagency.com/mastercard-foundation-scholars-program-celebrates-a-decade-of-developing-young-leaders/)).

The pandemic produced its largest single partnership: on 8 June 2021 it committed US$1.3 billion over three years with the Africa CDC to buy COVID-19 vaccines for at least 50 million people, support delivery to millions more, lay the groundwork for vaccine manufacturing in Africa and strengthen the Africa CDC ([Devex](https://www.devex.com/news/mastercard-foundation-to-give-1-3b-for-covid-19-response-in-africa-100107)). The OECD puts its 2023 development finance at US$1.3 billion, up 11.4% in real terms on 2022 and entirely grants, with US$1.2 billion — 92.5% — going to Africa, led by Kenya, Ghana and Ethiopia ([OECD](https://read.oecd-ilibrary.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/06/development-co-operation-profiles_02ffa45c/mastercard-foundation_71a064d2/7a272340-en.pdf)).

## FAQ

### Is the Mastercard Foundation part of Mastercard?

No. It is an independent Canadian charity with its own board and leadership, created in 2006 from a gift of shares when Mastercard became a public company. Its policies, operations and program decisions are determined by the Foundation itself, and it works with about 400 partner organisations rather than through the company.

### How big is the Mastercard Foundation?

It is one of the largest foundations in the world. Its assets were about US$37 billion in 2023 and more than US$53 billion by the time of its 2025 leadership announcement. It provided US$1.3 billion in development finance in 2023, according to the OECD, delivered entirely as grants.

### What is Young Africa Works?

Young Africa Works is the Foundation's Africa strategy, aiming to enable 30 million young Africans — 70% of them young women — to access dignified and fulfilling work by 2030. It focuses on young women, refugees and displaced people, and people with disabilities, and targets 75% African-led partner institutions.

### What is the Mastercard Foundation Scholars Program?

Launched in 2012 as a US$500 million initiative, it gives talented, economically disadvantaged students — particularly from Africa — full scholarships plus academic, social and careers support for secondary and university education. By 2022 it had deployed US$1.7 billion to benefit nearly 40,000 young people and aims to reach 100,000 by 2030.

### Who leads the Mastercard Foundation now?

Reeta Roy led the Foundation as President and CEO for 18 years, through 2025. Sewit Ahderom, a former co-founder of Gro Intelligence and former vice-president at Helios Investment Partners, was selected as the next President and CEO and takes over on 1 January 2026.
