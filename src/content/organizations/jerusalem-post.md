## Overview

The Jerusalem Post is Israel's oldest and largest English-language daily, published in Jerusalem — and by its own account the most-read English news website in Israel and the Jewish world ([The Jerusalem Post — About Us](http://www.jpost.com/landedpages/aboutus.aspx)). It was founded on 1 December 1932 as The Palestine Post by Gershon Agron — born Gershon Agronsky in Russia in 1894, a refugee from pogroms who emigrated to the United States, served in the Jewish Legion in World War I, and worked in the Zionist Commission's Press Bureau — together with Ted Lurie ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel/the-story-behind-the-headlines/article-87318)). The first press run was 1,200 copies; it soon rose to 4,000 ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel/the-story-behind-the-headlines/article-87318)). A Labor Zionist paper from birth, it was renamed The Jerusalem Post in 1950, though Palestine Post Ltd. remains its incorporated name ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel/the-story-behind-the-headlines/article-87318)).

What distinguishes the paper's history is how many times it has nearly been destroyed — by bombs, by owners, and by its own staff walking out — and kept publishing anyway.

## A car bomb couldn't stop the presses

On 1 February 1948, during the war surrounding Israel's founding, Arab terrorists detonated a car bomb that destroyed the paper's offices — killing a typesetter and wounding 20 people. The Post published the next morning anyway ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel/the-story-behind-the-headlines/article-87318)). The paper's own history records its defiant line from that era: "The truth is louder than TNT" ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel/the-story-behind-the-headlines/article-87318)). Three and a half months later, on 16 May 1948, its front page carried the headline "State of Israel Is Born" ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel/the-story-behind-the-headlines/article-87318)). Agron left the paper in 1955 to become mayor of Jerusalem, serving until his death in 1959 ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel/the-story-behind-the-headlines/article-87318)).

## The Hollinger years and the walkout

The paper's modern political identity was forged in a hostile takeover. In 1989, after its Histadrut-linked owner Koor put it up for auction, the Post was bought by Hollinger — the company of Conrad Black and David Radler — and shifted sharply to the right ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel/the-story-behind-the-headlines/article-87318)). In November 1989, Prime Minister Yitzhak Shamir publicly attacked the paper; the editor, Frenkel, resigned ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel/the-story-behind-the-headlines/article-87318)). Then, in January 1990, 35 staff members walked out in protest at the paper's new direction — one of the most dramatic newsroom revolts in Israeli journalism ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel/the-story-behind-the-headlines/article-87318)). New editors were installed — first Gross, then David Bar-Illan — and the paper continued under Hollinger's rightward line ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel/the-story-behind-the-headlines/article-87318)).

The Hollinger era ended in scandal. In 2003, Black and Radler resigned amid the fraud scandal at Hollinger that led to their convictions in the United States ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel/the-story-behind-the-headlines/article-87318)). In November 2004, the paper was sold for $13.2 million to Eli Azur's Mirkaei Tikshoret Group; a rival ownership claim by Canada's CanWest was litigated, and Azur emerged with full ownership — which he holds to this day ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel/the-story-behind-the-headlines/article-87318); [Haaretz/AP](https://www.haaretz.com/2004-11-17/ty-article/jerusalem-post-sold-to-ta-media-company-and-canwest/0000017f-ed8c-d0f7-a9ff-efcd84d20000)).

## Azur, JPost.com, and the modern paper

Under Azur, the Post describes itself as centrist, with columnists spanning the political spectrum ([The Jerusalem Post — About Us](http://www.jpost.com/landedpages/aboutus.aspx)). The print edition appears Sunday through Friday, supplemented by JPost Magazine, the In Jerusalem supplement, the biweekly Jerusalem Report (described as editorially independent), and a weekly International Edition ([The Jerusalem Post — About Us](http://www.jpost.com/landedpages/aboutus.aspx)). JPost.com, launched in 1996, is the engine of its international reach, and the company runs conferences in Jerusalem and New York, with editions in London, Dubai, and Marrakech ([The Jerusalem Post — About Us](http://www.jpost.com/landedpages/aboutus.aspx)). The current CEO is Inbar Ashkenazi and the editor-in-chief is Zvika Klein ([The Jerusalem Post — About Us](http://www.jpost.com/landedpages/aboutus.aspx)).

The newsroom that survived the car bomb and the walkout still breaks the region's biggest stories in English — including its own October 2024 reporting on the killing of [Hamas](https://factsledger.com/organizations/hamas/) leader [Yahya Sinwar](/people/yahya-sinwar/) ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/israel-hamas-war/article-813628)).
