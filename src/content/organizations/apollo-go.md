## Overview

Apollo Go — known in [China](/places/china/) as Luobo Kuaipao (萝卜快跑) — is [Baidu](/organizations/baidu/)'s autonomous ride-hailing service, running on the Baidu Apollo autonomous-driving platform ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/07/baidu-to-operate-fully-driverless-commercial-robotaxi-in-wuhan-and-chongqing/)). In August 2022 it became the first service in China to win permits to charge fares for fully driverless rides, with no human driver in the car ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/07/baidu-to-operate-fully-driverless-commercial-robotaxi-in-wuhan-and-chongqing/)). By the first quarter of 2026 it was delivering 3.2 million fully driverless rides a quarter — up more than 120% year over year — with cumulative rides past 22 million, and by August 2026 its footprint reached 28 cities across China, the Middle East and Europe ([Baidu Q1 2026 results](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/baidu-announces-first-quarter-2026-results-302774476.html); [EqualOcean](https://equalocean.com/news/2026072922063-baidus-apollo-go-begins-supervised-road-testing-london-freenow-lyft)). Apollo Go is a Baidu service, not an independent company — but it is covered as a standalone subject in its own right, with its own history, scale and international footprint.

## First permits and the Wuhan scale-up

The service's defining moment came on 8 August 2022, when Baidu announced permits in Wuhan and Chongqing to offer fully driverless commercial robotaxi rides, which it called the first such permits in China ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/07/baidu-to-operate-fully-driverless-commercial-robotaxi-in-wuhan-and-chongqing/)). The launch was deliberately small — five fifth-generation robotaxis in each city, running daytime hours over 13 square kilometres in Wuhan's Economic and Technological Development zone and 30 square kilometres in Chongqing's Yongchuan District — but it was the first time an autonomous-vehicle company could charge for a ride with nobody in the car in China ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/07/baidu-to-operate-fully-driverless-commercial-robotaxi-in-wuhan-and-chongqing/)). It followed a Beijing permit a few months earlier that still required a human operator in the front passenger seat, and from 20 July 2022 Baidu could charge fares there too ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/07/baidu-to-operate-fully-driverless-commercial-robotaxi-in-wuhan-and-chongqing/)).

Wuhan became the scale test. Apollo Go had been charging for rides since November 2021, and by the fourth quarter of 2023 it provided 839,000 rides, up 49% year over year, with 45% of Wuhan rides fully driverless; the Wuhan service area covered 3,000 square kilometres and half the city's population ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2024/05/17/baidus-robotaxi-division-to-wheel-into-profit-next-year/341988)). The economics depended on cheaper vehicles: the sixth-generation Apollo RT6, revealed in July 2022, cost about $37,000 per unit, and by 2024 Baidu put the figure at about ¥200,000 (roughly $28,000), about half the previous model ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/07/baidu-to-operate-fully-driverless-commercial-robotaxi-in-wuhan-and-chongqing/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2024/05/17/baidus-robotaxi-division-to-wheel-into-profit-next-year/341988)). In May 2024 Baidu said it expected the robotaxi division to be profitable in 2025 — an expectation whose outcome has not been verified from a fully-read source ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2024/05/17/baidus-robotaxi-division-to-wheel-into-profit-next-year/341988)).

Scale arrived in 2026. In the first quarter Apollo Go delivered 3.2 million fully driverless rides, with weekly rides peaking above 350,000 in March, and cumulative rides provided to the public passed 22 million in April ([Baidu Q1 2026 results](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/baidu-announces-first-quarter-2026-results-302774476.html)). By May its footprint reached 27 cities, and its fleets had accumulated over 330 million autonomous kilometres, including over 220 million kilometres driven with no one in the driver's seat ([Baidu Q1 2026 results](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/baidu-announces-first-quarter-2026-results-302774476.html)).

## Going global in 2026

Apollo Go's international expansion ran through local partners. In [Abu Dhabi](/places/abu-dhabi/) it teamed with K2's AutoGo: the partnership was announced in March 2025, the partners won one of the emirate's inaugural fully driverless commercial permits in mid-November 2025, and on 17 January 2026 they launched fully autonomous commercial ride-hailing on Yas Island, bookable through the AutoGo app, with phased expansion planned to Reem, Al Maryah and Saadiyat Islands and a goal of hundreds of vehicles by 2026 ([Time Out Abu Dhabi](https://www.timeoutabudhabi.com/news/baidu-apollo-go-k2-autogo-new-robotaxi-service)). In [Dubai](/places/dubai/), the Roads and Transport Authority launched commercial driverless taxi operations in Umm Suqeim and [Jumeirah](/places/jumeirah/) on 30 March 2026, and in August 2026 Apollo Go robotaxis became bookable through the [Uber](/organizations/uber/) app there — the first market for the Baidu-Uber partnership, which the companies had announced in February 2026 ([Devo Dubai](https://devodubai.com/article/2026-10-26/dubai-robotaxis-where-you-can-ride-one-now-which-apps-and-what-it-costs); [EqualOcean](https://equalocean.com/news/2026072922063-baidus-apollo-go-begins-supervised-road-testing-london-freenow-lyft); [Automarketplace](https://automarketplace.substack.com/p/dispatch-from-dubai-riding-baidus)).

Europe followed in July 2026. On 23 July Apollo Go received what Baidu described as [Hong Kong](/places/hong-kong/)'s first permit for a fully driverless trial without an onboard safety operator, with testing beginning on Airport Island on 27 July — its first fully driverless trial in a right-hand-drive market ([EqualOcean](https://equalocean.com/news/2026072922063-baidus-apollo-go-begins-supervised-road-testing-london-freenow-lyft)). The next day it began supervised road testing in London with Freenow by Lyft, running sixth-generation RT6 vehicles with safety operators on board in the borough of Brent, under a Baidu–Lyft partnership announced in August 2025; public rides are planned for 2027, subject to regulatory approvals ([EqualOcean](https://equalocean.com/news/2026072922063-baidus-apollo-go-begins-supervised-road-testing-london-freenow-lyft)).

The year also brought a public setback. On the night of 31 March 2026, several Apollo Go vehicles stopped on roads in Wuhan; police said initial findings pointed to a system malfunction, passengers got out safely, and no injuries were reported, with the cause still under investigation ([dailyinference.news](https://dailyinference.news/articles/2026-11-07-the-wheel-turns-on-its-own-the-passenger-relaxes/)). No final finding has been verified, and the incident is recorded here as reported rather than as an established cause.
