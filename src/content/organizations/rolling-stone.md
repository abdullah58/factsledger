## Overview

*Rolling Stone* is an American monthly magazine covering music, politics and popular culture, founded in San Francisco, California, in the [United States](/places/united-states/) in 1967 by Jann Wenner and the jazz critic Ralph J. Gleason. Its first issue, cover-dated 9 November 1967, carried John Lennon on the cover ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rolling-Stone)). After five decades under Wenner, [Penske Media Corporation](/organizations/penske-media-corporation/) bought a controlling stake in 2017 and full ownership in 2019, and the magazine is now based in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) ([MusicRow](https://musicrow.com/?p=303154)).

This profile is about the magazine, not the British rock band The Rolling Stones. What made the magazine matter was never just its music coverage: it combined reporting on rock and pop with political journalism, most famously the work of Hunter S. Thompson, and its cover became a mark of critical success that established and up-and-coming musicians alike coveted ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rolling-Stone)). Its "all-time greatest" lists, above all the 500 Greatest Albums, became reference points argued over far beyond its own pages, and its typical issues mix music and movie reviews, celebrity stories and photographs, new artists, fashion advice and politics ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rolling-Stone)).

## Founding and the San Francisco years

Wenner, a former University of California at Berkeley student, and Gleason, a jazz critic for the *San Francisco Chronicle*, founded *Rolling Stone* in San Francisco in 1967, intending it as a barometer of the artistic tastes and political sensibilities of students ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rolling-Stone)). It mixed proper English with street language, and its first issue led with the Monterey International Pop Festival and put John Lennon, in costume for *How I Won the War*, on the cover ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rolling-Stone)).

The magazine's reputation was built in the 1970s, when Hunter S. Thompson's political reporting — including *Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas*, first published in its pages — made it a home for gonzo journalism, and writers Cameron Crowe, Lester Bangs and Greil Marcus began their careers there ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rolling-Stone)). Its cover became a milestone: the Beatles, Bob Dylan, Madonna, Jack Nicholson, Susan Sontag and Bill Clinton all appeared on it, and in May 2006 it printed its 1,000th issue ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rolling-Stone)).

## From Wenner to Penske: the ownership changes

The sale was slow and reluctant. Wenner had turned down a buyout around 2008, when his accountants valued Wenner Media at about $1 billion, and a 2006 buyback of a *US Weekly* stake from Disney had saddled it with $300 million of debt ([NPR](https://www.publicradioeast.org/us/2017-12-21/rolling-stones-parent-company-sells-controlling-stake)). In September 2016 he sold 49% of the magazine to Singapore's BandLab Technologies for a reported $40 million ([NPR](https://www.publicradioeast.org/us/2017-12-21/rolling-stones-parent-company-sells-controlling-stake)). A year later, with the remaining 51% for sale, Penske Media Corporation, owner of [Variety](/organizations/variety/), *Deadline*, *WWD* and *Hollywood Life*, bought the controlling stake ([NPR](https://www.publicradioeast.org/us/2017-12-21/rolling-stones-parent-company-sells-controlling-stake)).

Terms were never officially disclosed: reports valued Wenner Media at $100 million, while *Vanity Fair* put Penske Media's initial 2017 investment at about $50 million ([NPR](https://www.publicradioeast.org/us/2017-12-21/rolling-stones-parent-company-sells-controlling-stake); [MusicRow](https://musicrow.com/?p=303154)). Jann Wenner stayed as editorial director and his son Gus remained president and chief operating officer ([NPR](https://www.publicradioeast.org/us/2017-12-21/rolling-stones-parent-company-sells-controlling-stake)). On 31 January 2019 Penske Media bought BandLab's remaining 49%, taking full ownership, including event and licensing rights and Rolling Stone International, and planning 15–20% more editorial staff ([MusicRow](https://musicrow.com/?p=303154)).

## Lists, charts and cultural influence

*Rolling Stone*'s lists became its most argued-over product. The "500 Greatest Albums of All Time", first issued in November 2003, and the "100 Greatest Singers of All Time" in November 2008, turned its taste into a public scoreboard ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rolling-Stone)). In 2020 the albums list was remade from scratch: over 300 artists, producers, critics and industry figures voted, 154 albums were new, and Marvin Gaye's *What's Going On* topped the list ahead of the Beach Boys' *Pet Sounds* and Joni Mitchell's *Blue*, with [Aretha Franklin](/people/aretha-franklin/)'s *I Never Loved a Man the Way I Love You* 13th ([BrooklynVegan](https://www.brooklynvegan.com/rolling-stone-remade-their-500-greatest-albums-of-all-time-list-from-scratch/)).

It also tried to become a chart publisher. In May 2019 it announced Rolling Stone Charts, powered by analytics firm Alpha Data, built from sales plus on-demand streaming, with five charts, led by the Top 100 Songs and Top 200 Albums ([Music Connection](https://www.musicconnection.com/rolling-stone-to-launch-music-charts/)). The charts put it in direct competition with *Billboard*'s rankings — a rivalry folded into partnership a year later ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)).

## The UVA retraction

*Rolling Stone*'s worst editorial failure came in November 2014, when it published "A Rape on Campus" by Sabrina Rubin Erdely, about an alleged gang rape of a University of Virginia student, known only as "Jackie". When scrutiny found the account unverified, publisher Jann Wenner commissioned the Columbia University Graduate School of Journalism to review the reporting. Its 5 April 2015 report called the story "a story of journalistic failure that was avoidable", faulting reporting, editing, editorial supervision and fact-checking, and the magazine retracted it and apologised ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2015/icymi-no-one-was-fired-at-rolling-stone/)).

The fallout was legal as well as reputational. The author apologised for not going far enough to verify the account, and no staff member was fired ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2015/icymi-no-one-was-fired-at-rolling-stone/)). UVA associate dean Nicole P. Eramo sued and settled; in June 2017 *Rolling Stone* settled with the Phi Kappa Psi fraternity for $1.65 million — the fraternity had originally sought $25 million — which the fraternity said it would partly donate to sexual-assault awareness programmes, closing a three-year legal battle ([Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/p/rolling-stone-the-uva-fraternity-named-in-a-rape-on-campus-have-finally-settled-64275)).

## Rolling Stone today

Today *Rolling Stone* sits inside a larger media group. On 23 September 2020 Penske Media Corporation and [MRC](/organizations/mrc/) announced PMRC, a joint venture operating *Billboard*, *[The Hollywood Reporter](/organizations/the-hollywood-reporter/)* and *[Vibe](/organizations/vibe/)* alongside *Variety*, *Rolling Stone* and *[Music Business Worldwide](/organizations/music-business-worldwide/)*, led day to day by Penske Media, with both companies continuing to operate independently outside the venture ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)). That made *Billboard* a fellow title under the same umbrella — not a Penske Media purchase, but a partnership that ended the old rivalry between *Variety* and *The Hollywood Reporter*, rivals for some 90 years ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)).

Editorially, the magazine turned a page in 2025, when Sean Woods and Shirley Halperin were named co-editors-in-chief, with Halperin also head of music — the first woman to hold the magazine's top editorial job since its founding in 1967 ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/07/31/shirley-halperin-sean-woods-named-co-editors-in-chief-at-rolling-stone-maer-roshan-sole-eic-at-the-hollywood-reporter/)). They work with chief executive Julian Holguin, and Woods, a *Rolling Stone* editor for more than two decades, had already earned it its first Emmy nomination in 2023 for "The DJ and the War Crimes" ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/07/31/shirley-halperin-sean-woods-named-co-editors-in-chief-at-rolling-stone-maer-roshan-sole-eic-at-the-hollywood-reporter/)).

## Frequently asked questions

### Who founded Rolling Stone?

Jann Wenner and the jazz critic Ralph J. Gleason founded *Rolling Stone* in San Francisco in 1967. Wenner was a former University of California at Berkeley student and Gleason wrote for the *San Francisco Chronicle*; the first issue, cover-dated 9 November 1967, featured John Lennon ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rolling-Stone)).

### Who owns Rolling Stone now?

Penske Media Corporation has owned *Rolling Stone* outright since 31 January 2019, when it bought BandLab Technologies' remaining 49% stake. Penske Media had bought a controlling 51% stake from Wenner Media in December 2017, after BandLab bought its 49% in 2016 ([MusicRow](https://musicrow.com/?p=303154)).

### What is Rolling Stone's relationship to Billboard?

Both titles operate under PMRC, a joint venture announced on 23 September 2020 by Penske Media Corporation and MRC. It operates *Billboard*, *The Hollywood Reporter* and *Vibe* alongside *Variety*, *Rolling Stone* and *Music Business Worldwide*, with day-to-day operations led by Penske Media ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)).

### What happened with Rolling Stone's UVA story?

In November 2014 the magazine published "A Rape on Campus", an unverified account of an alleged gang rape at the University of Virginia. A Columbia Journalism School review called it an avoidable journalistic failure, it was retracted on 5 April 2015, and the magazine settled defamation claims, including $1.65 million to the Phi Kappa Psi fraternity ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2015/icymi-no-one-was-fired-at-rolling-stone/); [Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/p/rolling-stone-the-uva-fraternity-named-in-a-rape-on-campus-have-finally-settled-64275)).

### Is Rolling Stone the same as the band The Rolling Stones?

No. This profile covers *Rolling Stone* the magazine, founded in 1967. The Rolling Stones are a separate British rock band; the magazine's name nods to the same roots — the old saying, Muddy Waters' song and Bob Dylan's "Like a Rolling Stone" — but the two are different entities ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rolling-Stone)).
