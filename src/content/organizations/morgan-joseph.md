## Overview

Morgan Joseph & Co. was a middle-market investment bank built by alumni of Drexel Burnham Lambert's wreckage — incorporated in Delaware on June 29, 2001, renamed in 2002, merged with Tri-Artisan Capital Partners in 2010, and now defunct ([Delaware Court of Chancery](https://law.justia.com/cases/delaware/court-of-chancery/2012/ca-6424-cs.html); [Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/people/1500144/morgan-joseph)). Its principals were Fred Joseph — the former CEO of Drexel Burnham Lambert — and John F. Sorte as president and CEO, and its strategy was national full-service investment banking for companies with $40–500 million in enterprise value: M&A advice, high-yield origination and distribution, research, and trading ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/people/1500144/morgan-joseph)). The firm's one direct link to this site's corpus: it rendered the fairness opinion on Liberté Investors' 2003 acquisition of USAuto Holdings, the merger that created [First Acceptance Corporation](/organizations/acceptance-insurance/) ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1017907/000104746903040833/a2124990zex-99_1.htm)).

## From Drexel's wreckage: the 2001 formation

The lineage starts with John A. Morgan's firm — Morgan Joseph was "originally founded 20 years ago by John A Morgan," in the firm's own telling, as the successor to Morgan Lewis Githens & Ahn ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/people/1500144/morgan-joseph)). The 2001 rebirth belonged to Fred Joseph: the son of a Boston cab driver, a 1963 Harvard Business School graduate, and the CEO of Drexel Burnham Lambert when it collapsed in 1989–90 ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/people/1500144/morgan-joseph)). After a failed bid to buy the U.S. operations of ING Barings' corporate finance division, Joseph "teamed up with a number of others" in May 2001 to acquire Morgan Lewis Githens & Ahn, which became Morgan Joseph & Co. in 2002 ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/people/1500144/morgan-joseph)). The Delaware Court of Chancery later confirmed the mechanics: a certificate of incorporation dated June 29, 2001, with Series A and Series B preferred stock sold to the initial investors ([Delaware Court of Chancery](https://law.justia.com/cases/delaware/court-of-chancery/2012/ca-6424-cs.html)).

The Drexel diaspora followed Joseph. John F. Sorte — Joseph's corporate-finance colleague since 1972, a Drexel veteran who had run the reorganized New Street Capital after the collapse — became president and CEO in June 2001; Denis Galgano (Drexel's corporate-finance chief administrative officer) and Mary Lou Malanoski (Drexel M&A managing director) joined alongside him ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/people/1500144/morgan-joseph)). Of the firm's 55 investment bankers, 27 were senior professionals — an unusually top-heavy roster the partners pitched as an advantage for founders who wanted "a grown-up investment banker" rather than a 32-year-old from a bulge-bracket firm ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/people/1500144/morgan-joseph)). In December 2002 the firm recruited a 13-person high-yield and convertible-bond sales, research, and trading team to complete the franchise ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/people/1500144/morgan-joseph)).

## The Tri-Artisan merger and the end of the firm

On December 28, 2010, Morgan Joseph merged with Tri-Artisan Capital Partners, LLC; both firms survived the merger as wholly owned subsidiaries of a newly formed entity ([Delaware Court of Chancery](https://law.justia.com/cases/delaware/court-of-chancery/2012/ca-6424-cs.html)). In February 2011, Tri-Artisan Partners LLC changed its name to Morgan Joseph TriArtisan Capital LLC — a Delaware LLC providing merchant-banking, M&A advisory, private-financing, and capital-raising services from 600 Fifth Avenue in New York ([SEC](https://www.SEC.GOV/Archives/edgar/vprr/1201/12014127.pdf)). That November it formed CSMJ Capital Markets, a U.S. equity-capital-markets joint venture with Collins Stewart's American securities division, with Sorte as executive chairman ([Private Equity Wire](https://www.privateequitywire.co.uk/collins-stewart-and-morgan-joseph-triartisan-form-us-equity-capital-markets-jv/)). The merger's preferred-stock terms were litigated in *Shiftan v. Morgan Joseph Holdings*, decided by the Delaware Court of Chancery on January 13, 2012 ([Delaware Court of Chancery](https://law.justia.com/cases/delaware/court-of-chancery/2012/ca-6424-cs.html)). The firm then faded: no closure announcement was found, and deal directories now list it as no longer operational.

## Frequently asked questions

**What was Morgan Joseph & Co.?**
A New York middle-market investment bank (2001–2010s) founded by Drexel Burnham Lambert alumni Fred Joseph and John F. Sorte, advising companies with $40–500 million in enterprise value on M&A and capital-raising.

**Who founded Morgan Joseph?**
The predecessor firm traces to John A. Morgan (~1983); the 2001 investment bank was formed by Fred Joseph and others acquiring Morgan Lewis Githens & Ahn. No source supports a founder named "John Sutter" — see "What this profile does not claim."

**What happened to Morgan Joseph?**
It merged with Tri-Artisan Capital Partners on December 28, 2010, becoming Morgan Joseph TriArtisan, formed a 2011 joint venture with Collins Stewart, and subsequently wound down.

**What was Morgan Joseph's connection to First Acceptance Corporation?**
It served as Liberté Investors' financial advisor and rendered a fairness opinion to its board on the December 2003 acquisition of USAuto Holdings — the merger that created First Acceptance Corporation.
