## Overview

QBE Insurance Group is Australia's second-largest international insurer, headquartered in Sydney with a local presence in 27 countries across personal, business, corporate, and institutional markets, spanning underwriting and reinsurance ([Motley Fool Australia](https://www.fool.com.au/tickers/asx-qbe/page/40/)). Its name is an acronym with a 140-year pedigree: the company was first established as the North Queensland Insurance Company in Townsville in 1886 by two Scottish shipping partners, who later set up the Bankers' and Traders' Insurance Company in the 1920s; the two merged with the Equitable Probate and General Insurance Company in 1973 to become QBE — Queensland Insurance, Bankers' and Traders', Equitable — in what was regarded at the time as one of the biggest corporate mergers in Australian history, with QBE listing on the ASX the same year ([Motley Fool Australia](https://www.fool.com.au/tickers/asx-qbe/page/40/)). By its 125th anniversary in 2011 it had grown into one of the 25 largest global insurance and reinsurance companies ([insuranceNEWS.com.au](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/corporate/qbe-set-to-mark-125-years)).

## The coal exit

QBE's most distinctive strategic move of the last decade was climatic. In 2019 it announced it would stop offering any new direct insurance for construction projects tied to thermal coal mines, coal-fired power stations, or thermal coal transport infrastructure from July 1 of that year, and phase out all direct insurance services for thermal coal customers by 2030 ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/uk/news/environmental/qbe-delivers-climate-change-action-163677.aspx)). Thermal coal customers made up less than 0.5% of its premium revenue, and it committed to targeting zero direct investment in the thermal coal industry — while continuing to insure metallurgical coal and oil and gas ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/uk/news/environmental/qbe-delivers-climate-change-action-163677.aspx)). The move made QBE the 11th major global insurer to restrict coal underwriting, and the first headquartered outside Europe ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/uk/news/environmental/qbe-delivers-climate-change-action-163677.aspx)).

## The turnaround

The coal exit coincided with a long financial rehabilitation that culminated in record results. For FY2025, reported in February 2026, QBE posted a full-year net profit of US$2.2 billion — up 21.2% — on gross written premiums of US$24 billion, up 7%, sending the shares up 7.1% on the day ([AAP](https://aapnews.aap.com.au/news/general-insurer-lifts-profit-as-disaster-payouts-drop)). Catastrophe claims came in at just US$751 million, or 4.1% of net insurance revenue, well under allowance despite Cyclone Alfred and east-coast storms ([AAP](https://aapnews.aap.com.au/news/general-insurer-lifts-profit-as-disaster-payouts-drop)). The combined operating ratio improved to 91.9% from 93.1%, adjusted return on equity reached 19.8%, and the full-year dividend rose 25% to A$1.09 per share ([AAP](https://aapnews.aap.com.au/news/general-insurer-lifts-profit-as-disaster-payouts-drop)). CEO Andrew Horton credited portfolio pruning — including winding down the North America middle-market segment from 2024 to cut property-catastrophe exposure — for the result ([AAP](https://aapnews.aap.com.au/news/general-insurer-lifts-profit-as-disaster-payouts-drop)).
