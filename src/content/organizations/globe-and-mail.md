## Overview

The Globe and Mail is [Canada](/places/canada/)'s national newspaper, a [Toronto](/places/toronto/)-based daily that traces its roots to The Globe, founded in 1844, and took its current form when The Globe merged with The Mail and Empire in 1936. Owned outright by the Thomson family's Woodbridge holding company since 2015, it is Canada's largest national newspaper, reaching a combined print and digital audience of 6 million readers a week.

The paper calls itself "Canada's National Newspaper" — a claim it has carried on its masthead since just after the turn of the twentieth century ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/globe-and-mail)). It is also, unusually among large newspapers, a profitable reader-revenue business: its chief executive said in 2025 that the paper is profitable, with 63% of its revenue coming from readers and print advertising down to less than 15% ([WAN-IFRA](https://wan-ifra.org/2025/06/globe-and-mails-acceleration-strategy-paying-off-in-challenged-canadian-market/)). Its reputation rests heavily on public-service investigations — it has won more Michener Awards, Canada's top prize for public-service journalism, than any other news organisation ([Canadian Journalism Foundation](https://cjf-fjc.ca/skinny-micheners/)).

## Two papers become one: 1844–1936

The Globe was launched in 1844 by Scottish immigrant George Brown, with the support of a group of Reform Liberals, and quickly became required reading for Toronto's educated and business community through a mixture of news, features, forceful editorials and technological innovation ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/globe-and-mail)). Brown, who later became a Father of Confederation, put a quote on the paper that has survived every change of ownership since: "The subject who is truly loyal to the Chief Magistrate will neither advise nor submit to arbitrary measures", attributed to the unidentified eighteenth-century writer Junius, which still appears on the editorial page ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/globe-and-mail)). By 1900 The Globe had a combined circulation of 69,545 across its editions and had added "Canada's National Newspaper" to its masthead ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/globe-and-mail)).

Its rival was built to match it. After several Toronto papers failed to give the Conservative Party an organ to match The Globe's power, The Mail was launched in 1872; when it developed a streak of political independence, Conservative officials launched a new paper, The Empire, and the two merged in 1895 as The Mail and Empire ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/globe-and-mail)). On 23 November 1936, mining executive William Henry Wright and financial investor George McCullagh bought both papers and merged them, consolidating Toronto's morning newspaper market ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/globe-and-mail)). McCullagh became the new paper's first publisher, and although The Mail and Empire had the larger circulation, it was The Globe's heritage that immediately dominated the merged paper's style and contributors ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/globe-and-mail)).

## Ownership: from Webster to Woodbridge

The paper has changed hands five times since the merger, and each change is a chapter of Canadian media history:

| Era | Owner | What changed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1936–1955 | Wright / McCullagh | Merger; McCullagh's rightward drift and the failed Leadership League |
| 1955–1980 | R. Howard Webster / FP Publications | Flagship of the FP chain from 1965; editorial independence kept |
| 1980–2001 | Thomson Corporation | Bought after a bidding war; Ken Thomson's last-minute bid beat Conrad Black's group |
| 2001–2010 | Bell Globemedia / BCE | Folded into BCE's broadcast empire with CTV |
| 2010–present | The Woodbridge Company | 85% from 2010; 100% from 2015 |

Webster bought the paper in 1955 after the deaths of Wright and McCullagh, and it became the flagship of his FP Publications chain in 1965 while maintaining its editorial independence ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/globe-and-mail)). In 1980 a brief bidding war involving Webster and a group headed by Conrad Black ended with a last-minute bid by Ken Thomson, and the paper was sold to the Thomson Corporation ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/globe-and-mail)). In 2001 a Competition Bureau decision cleared BCE to take a majority ownership position, making the paper part of Bell Globemedia alongside the CTV network ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/globe-and-mail)).

The Thomson family came back in 2010. Woodbridge, the family's investment vehicle and the holder of a 55% stake in [Thomson Reuters](/organizations/thomson-reuters/), announced on 10 September 2010 that it would acquire direct ownership of 85% of the paper, selling its 40% interest in CTVglobemedia to BCE, which kept 15% ([The Woodbridge Company](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/woodbridge-acquires-direct-ownership-of-the-globe-and-mail-545462382.html)). On 14 August 2015 BCE sold its remaining 15% to Woodbridge, leaving the Thomson family the paper's sole owner ([BCE](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/bce-divests-equity-stake-in-the-globe-and-mail-521902361.html)). David Thomson chairs the paper ([News Media Canada](https://nmc-mic.ca/2023/06/01/andrew-saunders-will-become-the-globe-and-mails-new-president-and-ceo/)). At the 2025 World News Media Congress, chief executive Andrew Saunders said the family has "supported independent journalism, free from management interference for three generations" ([WAN-IFRA](https://wan-ifra.org/2025/06/globe-and-mails-acceleration-strategy-paying-off-in-challenged-canadian-market/)).

## Report on Business and the paper's identity

The paper's business coverage is its signature. Report on Business launched in 1962, initially twice a week, growing out of editor-in-chief Oakley Dalgleish's belief that Canada had room for a business publication built on daily-journalism principles, and a monthly Report on Business magazine is published as an insert ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/globe-and-mail)). The paper's website, globeandmail.com, launched in 2000, and the print edition went full colour in October 2010 ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/globe-and-mail)). It maintains foreign bureaus in London, Rome, Johannesburg, Hong Kong and Washington, though print distribution has been phased out in the Maritimes — Newfoundland in 2013, and New Brunswick, Nova Scotia and Prince Edward Island in 2017 ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/globe-and-mail)).

The Canadian Encyclopedia describes the paper as "a journal of record as well as of the business, political and cultural communities" and a "writer's newspaper" on the strength of its columnists and contributors ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/globe-and-mail)). Its editors-in-chief have included Oakley Dalgleish, William Thorsell, Edward Greenspon, John Stackhouse and the current editor, David Walmsley ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/globe-and-mail)). Since the end of 2016 the paper has been headquartered in the Globe and Mail Centre, a 17-storey building at 351 King Street East in downtown Toronto, where it occupies the top five floors on a 15-year lease ([Canadian Journalism Foundation](https://cjf-fjc.ca/globe-and-mail-announces-move-new-headquarters-starting-fall-2016/)).

## The reader-revenue turnaround

The Globe was early to the paywall. Globe Unlimited, its metered digital subscription, launched on 22 October 2012: casual visitors could read 10 items a month free, after which a subscription cost $19.99 a month, free for five- or six-day print subscribers and $4.99 for weekend-only subscribers ([The Globe and Mail](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/r-e-p-e-a-t---the-globe-and-mail-to-launch-online-subscription-service-on-october-22-510938701.html)). By October 2013 it had 100,000 digital subscribers, 70,000 of whom also took the print edition ([Nieman Journalism Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/encyclo/globe-and-mail/?from=front)). In 2015 it launched Sophi, an AI-backed platform for automating and predicting how its content is consumed ([WAN-IFRA](https://wan-ifra.org/2025/06/globe-and-mails-acceleration-strategy-paying-off-in-challenged-canadian-market/)).

The model compounded. A company spokesperson told Press Gazette in November 2023 that the paper had around 246,000 digital-only subscriptions, excluding Apple News+ ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/paywalls/100k-club-exclusive-ranking-of-worlds-top-paywalled-news-publishers/)). At the World News Media Congress in June 2025, president and chief executive Andrew Saunders — who took the job on 1 September 2023 after joining the paper in 2002 ([News Media Canada](https://nmc-mic.ca/2023/06/01/andrew-saunders-will-become-the-globe-and-mails-new-president-and-ceo/)) — said "I am happy to say we are profitable", with reader revenue at 63% of revenue, print advertising under 15%, and digital subscriptions typically growing 10–15% a year ([WAN-IFRA](https://wan-ifra.org/2025/06/globe-and-mails-acceleration-strategy-paying-off-in-challenged-canadian-market/)). The paper charges nearly $400 a year for a digital subscription and has diversified into events, conferences, premium travel and education, while its Global Alliance Network — in which it represents CNN, the Guardian and Hearst in Canada — reaches close to 20 million Canadians a month ([WAN-IFRA](https://wan-ifra.org/2025/06/globe-and-mails-acceleration-strategy-paying-off-in-challenged-canadian-market/)).

## Public-service investigations

The paper's most decorated work is its investigations. It had already won nine Michener Awards by 2010 — more than any other Canadian news organisation — for subjects including political conflicts of interest (1972), child protection (1977), the tainted-blood scandal (1993), the sponsorship scandal (2004) and breast cancer (2005) ([Canadian Journalism Foundation](https://cjf-fjc.ca/skinny-micheners/)). Its recent run of Micheners shows the same pattern: long investigations that changed law and policy.

| Year | Investigation | What it changed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2017 | Unfounded (Robyn Doolittle) | Police dismissed 1 in 5 sexual-assault complaints; 37,000 cases reviewed, 400+ reopened; $100M federal pledge |
| 2019 | False Promises (Kathy Tomlinson) | Exploitation of temporary workers and foreign students; new open work visas and a consultant-regulation law |
| 2021 | Residential Schools (joint with CBC Saskatoon) | First analysis of the Catholic Church's net assets in Canada; bishops' apology, $30M fundraising, papal apology |
| 2022 | Hockey Canada's Secret Funds | Registration fees secretly used to settle assault claims; CEO exit, board resignation, police inquiry restarted |

"Unfounded", Doolittle's 2017 series, drew on more than 250 freedom-of-information requests and showed police were dismissing one in five sexual-assault complaints; a follow-up survey found one in three legitimate reports had been wrongfully dismissed, and Statistics Canada resumed collecting unfounded rates ([Michener Awards Foundation](https://www.michenerawards.ca/organization/the-globe-and-mail-4/)). The 2022 Hockey Canada series revealed the organisation had secretly used parents' registration fees collected over decades to settle sexual-assault claims, keeping the allegations out of public view; the fallout included the chief executive's exit, the entire board's resignation and a restarted police investigation ([Michener Awards Foundation](http://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/michener-award-recognizes-powerful-investigations-into-residential-school-funds-and-hockey-canada-mismanagement-in-joint-ceremony-honoring-public-service-journalism-864134022.html)). The paper has also won more National Newspaper Awards than any other Canadian newspaper — 138 in total by April 2012, including eight in 2011 alone ([The Globe and Mail](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/the-globe-and-mail-wins-eight-national-newspaper-awards-510120801.html)).

## The Globe and Blue J

The paper's business reporting reaches beyond its own pages. Its coverage of Toronto legal-tech company [Blue J](/organizations/blue-j/) reported the company's August 2025 funding round — US$122 million (CAD$167 million), led by Sapphire Ventures and Oak HC/FT, valuing the company above US$300 million — citing filings with Canadian securities regulators ([University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/u-t-startup-blue-j-legal-raises-167-million-globe-and-mail)). The Globe's reporting is the source the University of Toronto relayed for the round's size, valuation and leads, and for Blue J's history as a 2015 University of Toronto spinout that relaunched on OpenAI language-model technology in 2023 ([University of Toronto](https://www.utoronto.ca/news/u-t-startup-blue-j-legal-raises-167-million-globe-and-mail)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was The Globe and Mail founded?** The current paper dates to 23 November 1936, when The Globe merged with The Mail and Empire ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/globe-and-mail)). Its roots go back to The Globe, founded 5 March 1844 by George Brown — which is why the paper itself says it was "founded in 1844" ([The Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/globe-and-mail)).

**Who owns The Globe and Mail?** The Woodbridge Company, the Thomson family's private holding company, has owned 100% of the paper since buying BCE's remaining 15% stake in August 2015 ([BCE](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/bce-divests-equity-stake-in-the-globe-and-mail-521902361.html)). Woodbridge also holds a controlling stake in Thomson Reuters ([The Woodbridge Company](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/woodbridge-acquires-direct-ownership-of-the-globe-and-mail-545462382.html)).

**How does The Globe and Mail make money now?** Mostly from readers: 63% of revenue is reader revenue, print advertising is under 15%, and the paper says it is profitable ([WAN-IFRA](https://wan-ifra.org/2025/06/globe-and-mails-acceleration-strategy-paying-off-in-challenged-canadian-market/)). Its metered paywall, Globe Unlimited, launched in 2012, and it had about 246,000 digital-only subscribers in November 2023 ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/paywalls/100k-club-exclusive-ranking-of-worlds-top-paywalled-news-publishers/)).

**What is the Michener Award, and why does the Globe win it so often?** The Michener Award, founded in 1970 by Governor General Roland Michener, is Canada's top prize for public-service journalism ([News Media Canada](https://nmc-mic.ca/2020/12/10/the-globe-and-mail-wins-the-2019-michener-award/)). The Globe has won it more than any other organisation, including four times between 2017 and 2022 for investigations into sexual-assault policing, migrant-worker exploitation, residential schools and Hockey Canada ([Michener Awards Foundation](http://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/michener-award-recognizes-powerful-investigations-into-residential-school-funds-and-hockey-canada-mismanagement-in-joint-ceremony-honoring-public-service-journalism-864134022.html)).
