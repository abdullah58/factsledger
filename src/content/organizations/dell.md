## Overview

Dell Technologies is the Round Rock, [Texas](/places/texas/), technology company founded by Michael Dell in 1984 as PC's Limited, which grew from a dorm-room PC business into the world's largest PC maker by 2001 and, after the $67 billion EMC acquisition in 2016, into a $95.6 billion infrastructure giant whose AI servers now drive its growth ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/dell-inc); [Dell Technologies](https://www.dell.com/en-us/dt/corporate/newsroom/announcements/detailpage.press-releases~usa~2025~02~dell-technologies-delivers-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-fiscal-2025-financial-results.htm)).

The company began with a 19-year-old freshman at the University of Texas at Austin, who started with $1,000 of his own money, buying parts and customizing and reselling IBM PCs before building machines from components and selling them by phone and magazine advertisement at prices far below IBM's ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/dell-inc)). It went public in 1988, reached the Fortune 500 by 1992, and built its name on a direct-sales model — custom-configured machines, low prices, quick delivery — that later became a case study in supply-chain management ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/dell-inc)). Today Dell Technologies is organized into two businesses, the Infrastructure Solutions Group (servers, networking and storage) and the Client Solutions Group (PCs and peripherals), operates in more than 170 countries, and employs about 108,000 people ([Dell Technologies Form 10-K](https://investors.delltechnologies.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001571996-25-000034/0001571996-25-000034.pdf)). It is not the same company it was in 1984: the business that once sold PCs by mail now books billions of dollars of AI server orders in a single quarter ([Dell Technologies](https://www.dell.com/en-us/dt/corporate/newsroom/announcements/detailpage.press-releases~usa~2025~11~dell-technologies-delivers-third-quarter-fiscal-2026-financial-results.htm)).

## From a dorm room to the world's biggest PC maker

Michael Dell's opening was a gap in the market: the IBM PC came only in standard configurations, IBM could not meet demand, and the operating system and the chips were controlled by other companies — [Microsoft](/organizations/microsoft/) and [Intel](/organizations/intel/) — which left room for compatible machines built from parts ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/dell-inc)). He put together a team to build new models rather than upgrading IBM machines, advertised in computer magazines, took phone orders over a toll-free number, and sold machines priced far cheaper than IBM's offerings ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/dell-inc)). Sales boomed: the company was renamed Dell Computer Corp. and became publicly held in 1988, with shares selling at $8.50, and it began selling its first laptop the following year ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/dell-inc)). By 1992 sales had pushed it onto the Fortune 500 list, and Dell, only 27 years old, was the youngest CEO on the list ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/dell-inc)). In 1996 it launched Dell.com, where sales quickly reached $1 million a day, and began selling computer servers to businesses the same year ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/dell-inc)). By 2001 it was the world's largest maker of PCs ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/dell-inc)).

The rise also seeded a region: headquartered in Round Rock, Texas, Dell helped facilitate the rise of high-technology companies centred on [Austin](/places/austin-texas/), which became home to more than a thousand high-tech companies, many of them offshoots of Dell founded by former employees or drawn there by its growth ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/dell-inc)). But 2004 brought an unusual legal footnote that standard company profiles omit. In April 2004, Forgent Networks — the company now known as [Asure Software](/organizations/asure-software/) — sued Dell and 30 other companies through its [Compression Labs](/organizations/compression-labs/) subsidiary, alleging they infringed U.S. Patent No. 4,698,672, a "coding system for reducing redundancy" that Forgent said was integral to JPEG image compression ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/171812/forgent.html)). The defendants named alongside Dell included [Apple](/organizations/apple/), [Adobe](/organizations/adobe/), [IBM](/organizations/ibm/), [HP](/organizations/hp/), [Kodak](/organizations/kodak/) and [Xerox](/organizations/xerox/) ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/171812/forgent.html)). Forgent had been asserting the patent since 2002 and by April 2004 had struck licensing deals worth about $90 million, including a one-time fee of about $16 million from [Sony](/organizations/sony/) ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/171812/forgent.html)).

## Crisis, restatement and reinvention

Michael Dell stepped down as chief executive in July 2004, remaining chairman, and Kevin Rollins took over ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/dell-inc); [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/complaints/2010/comp21599.pdf)). The company then faltered: complaints over poor customer service and shipping delays hurt its reputation and sales, and in 2006 a faulty Sony-made battery caused some Dell laptops to catch fire, forcing a recall of 4.2 million machines ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/dell-inc)). Behind the operational troubles sat a deeper problem. In August 2006 Dell's audit committee began an independent investigation into accounting practices, and by November the informal SEC probe had become a formal investigation ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/complaints/2010/comp21599.pdf); [CFO.com](https://www.cfo.com/news/dell-finds-accounting-errors-misconduct/675061/)). In December 2006 Donald J. Carty was named vice chairman and chief financial officer, replacing James M. Schneider, and in late January 2007 Michael Dell returned as chief executive, succeeding Rollins ([CFO.com](https://www.cfo.com/news/dell-finds-accounting-errors-misconduct/675061/)).

In March 2007 the company announced it had found accounting errors, evidence of misconduct and deficiencies in its financial-control environment, and it delayed filing its annual report for the year ended 2 February 2007 ([CFO.com](https://www.cfo.com/news/dell-finds-accounting-errors-misconduct/675061/)). The SEC's 2010 complaint laid out the mechanism: from 2002 to 2006 Dell failed to disclose large payments from Intel, effectively payment not to use processors made by AMD, Intel's arch-rival, while publicly crediting "cost reduction initiatives" and "declining component costs" for its improving profitability ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/complaints/2010/comp21599.pdf)). The payments grew from about 10% of Dell's operating income in fiscal 2003 to 38% in fiscal 2006, peaking at 76% in the first quarter of fiscal 2007 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/complaints/2010/comp21599.pdf)). When Dell announced in May 2006 that it would begin using AMD chips, Intel cut the payments, and operating income fell 36% in the following quarter — a drop the payments cut accounted for about 75% of ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/complaints/2010/comp21599.pdf)). Separately, senior accounting personnel maintained "cookie jar" reserves to cover operating shortfalls from fiscal 2002 to fiscal 2005 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/complaints/2010/comp21599.pdf)). In August 2007 the audit committee concluded that the financial statements for fiscal 2003 through fiscal 2006 and the first quarter of fiscal 2007 should no longer be relied upon, and the company restated them ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/complaints/2010/comp21599.pdf)). In 2010 Dell paid a $100 million settlement over SEC charges that it had falsely stated earnings for several years ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/dell-inc)).

The company kept expanding even as it repaired itself: in 2009 it bought Perot Systems, Ross Perot's computer systems company, for nearly $4 billion ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/dell-inc)). In 2013 Michael Dell decided he wanted more personal control of the company, and, teaming with the private equity firm Silver Lake and with Microsoft, bought it back for $24.4 billion and took it private ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/dell-inc)). Two years later, citing continued weak demand in the PC market, Dell announced its huge move into data storage by purchasing EMC Corporation for $67 billion, the largest technology merger in history ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/dell-inc)). The deal closed on 7 September 2016, creating Dell Technologies, a $74 billion business serving 98% of the Fortune 500 with 140,000 employees ([Bloomberg](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/deals/dell-emc-complete-largest-tech-deal-look-to-invest)). EMC shareholders received $24.05 per share in cash plus a tracking stock linked to VMware, EMC's majority-owned software business ([Bloomberg](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/deals/dell-emc-complete-largest-tech-deal-look-to-invest)).

## Dell Technologies today: AI servers and record revenue

The EMC combination was unwound in part on 1 November 2021, when Dell completed the spin-off of its 81% equity ownership of VMware through a special dividend of VMware shares to Dell stockholders ([Dell Technologies](https://www.dell.com/en-nz/dt/corporate/newsroom/announcements/detailpage.press-releases~usa~2021~11~20211101-dell-technologies-announces-completion-of-vmware-spin-off.htm)). VMware paid an $11.5 billion special cash dividend, of which Dell received $9.3 billion and used the funds to pay down debt ([Dell Technologies](https://www.dell.com/en-nz/dt/corporate/newsroom/announcements/detailpage.press-releases~usa~2021~11~20211101-dell-technologies-announces-completion-of-vmware-spin-off.htm)). Michael Dell remained chair and chief executive of Dell Technologies as well as chair of the VMware board, and the two companies kept a commercial agreement covering co-development and sales and marketing alignment ([Dell Technologies](https://www.dell.com/en-nz/dt/corporate/newsroom/announcements/detailpage.press-releases~usa~2021~11~20211101-dell-technologies-announces-completion-of-vmware-spin-off.htm)).

Demand for computers surged during the COVID-19 pandemic, and the company reported record revenue of $101.2 billion for fiscal 2022, ranking 31st on the Fortune 500 that year ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/dell-inc)). Fiscal 2025, the year ended 31 January 2025, brought revenue of $95.6 billion, up 8% year over year, with operating income of $6.2 billion and diluted earnings per share of $6.38 ([Dell Technologies](https://www.dell.com/en-us/dt/corporate/newsroom/announcements/detailpage.press-releases~usa~2025~02~dell-technologies-delivers-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-fiscal-2025-financial-results.htm)). The growth came from infrastructure, not PCs: the Infrastructure Solutions Group grew 29% to $43.6 billion while the Client Solutions Group slipped 1% to $48.4 billion ([Dell Technologies](https://www.dell.com/en-us/dt/corporate/newsroom/announcements/detailpage.press-releases~usa~2025~02~dell-technologies-delivers-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-fiscal-2025-financial-results.htm)). The driver was AI: Dell's chief operating officer Jeff Clarke said the deals booked with xAI and others put the AI server backlog at roughly $9 billion as of February 2025 ([Dell Technologies](https://www.dell.com/en-us/dt/corporate/newsroom/announcements/detailpage.press-releases~usa~2025~02~dell-technologies-delivers-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-fiscal-2025-financial-results.htm)).

The AI momentum accelerated through 2025: in the third quarter of fiscal 2026, reported on 25 November 2025, Dell booked record AI server orders of $12.3 billion in the quarter and $30 billion year to date, ending the quarter with an AI backlog of $18.4 billion and raising full-year AI shipment guidance to roughly $25 billion, up more than 150% year over year ([Dell Technologies](https://www.dell.com/en-us/dt/corporate/newsroom/announcements/detailpage.press-releases~usa~2025~11~dell-technologies-delivers-third-quarter-fiscal-2026-financial-results.htm)). Total revenue for the quarter was a record $27.0 billion, up 11%, and Dell guided full-year fiscal 2026 revenue to $111.7 billion, up 17% ([Dell Technologies](https://www.dell.com/en-us/dt/corporate/newsroom/announcements/detailpage.press-releases~usa~2025~11~dell-technologies-delivers-third-quarter-fiscal-2026-financial-results.htm)). The company that began by undercutting IBM on price now sells its growth story in AI infrastructure — a pivot its own numbers support: servers and networking revenue rose 37% in that quarter to $10.1 billion, while storage was flat and the consumer PC business fell 7% ([Dell Technologies](https://www.dell.com/en-us/dt/corporate/newsroom/announcements/detailpage.press-releases~usa~2025~11~dell-technologies-delivers-third-quarter-fiscal-2026-financial-results.htm)).

The combination created a $74 billion business serving 98% of the Fortune 500, though Dell borrowed $48.6 billion to fund it ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/03/10/dell-spent-67b-buying-emc-more-than-3-years-later-was-it-worth-the-debt/)).

**How big is Dell's AI server business now?**
In the third quarter of fiscal 2026 Dell booked $12.3 billion of AI server orders and ended the quarter with an $18.4 billion AI backlog, guiding full-year AI shipments to roughly $25 billion, up more than 150% year over year ([Dell Technologies](https://www.dell.com/en-us/dt/corporate/newsroom/announcements/detailpage.press-releases~usa~2025~11~dell-technologies-delivers-third-quarter-fiscal-2026-financial-results.htm)). AI servers are now the company's growth engine, with servers and networking revenue up 37% in that quarter.
