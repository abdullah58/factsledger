## Overview

Euronews is a pan-European television news network launched in 1993, broadcasting world news from a European perspective in 13 languages around the clock, with more than 400 journalists of 25 nationalities ([World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/organizations/euronews)). Founded in Lyon, France, by the European Broadcasting Union, it is now majority-owned by Portugal's Alpac Capital, and its headquarters moved from Lyon to Brussels in 2024 ([The Desk](https://thedesk.net/2023/03/euronews-layoffs-closing-lyon-france-headquarters/); [IFJ/EFJ](https://www.ifj.org/media-centre/news/detail/article/france-journalists-union-calls-for-the-opening-of-a-parliamentary-inquiry-into-euronews-takeover)).

The channel was established in 1992 by the European Broadcasting Union (EBU) — a consortium of public broadcasters from various European countries — following the success of American broadcaster CNN in covering the start of the Persian Gulf War, one of the first times a conflict was carried live on television ([The Desk](https://thedesk.net/2023/03/euronews-layoffs-closing-lyon-france-headquarters/)). The EBU launched Euronews to replicate CNN's reporting, but from a European perspective ([The Desk](https://thedesk.net/2023/03/euronews-layoffs-closing-lyon-france-headquarters/)). The World Economic Forum describes it as one of the most-watched news channels in Europe and a full multimedia platform, broadcasting in Arabic, English, French, German, Greek, Hungarian, Italian, Persian, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish, Turkish and Ukrainian ([World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/organizations/euronews)).

## From public broadcasters to private equity

Euronews spent its first two decades as a creature of Europe's public broadcasters, then changed hands three times in seven years. In 2017 it approved a minority stake acquisition by Comcast's NBCUniversal, through which the American media company would pay $30 million for a minority stake in the channel ([The Desk](https://thedesk.net/2023/03/euronews-layoffs-closing-lyon-france-headquarters/)). By then Euronews had grown to include localised variants in Albanian, Arabic, Bulgarian, English, French, German, Greek, Polish, Russian, Spanish, Ukrainian and other languages ([The Desk](https://thedesk.net/2023/03/euronews-layoffs-closing-lyon-france-headquarters/)). NBCUniversal sold its 88 percent share in April 2020, about a month after the start of the coronavirus pandemic ([The Desk](https://thedesk.net/2023/03/euronews-layoffs-closing-lyon-france-headquarters/)). In late 2021, Portugal-based investment group Alpac Capital announced it had acquired an 88 percent stake in Euronews, giving it controlling interest, and public broadcasters in France, Italy, Switzerland and Belgium subsequently withdrew their investments ([The Desk](https://thedesk.net/2023/03/euronews-layoffs-closing-lyon-france-headquarters/)). The European Commission records that the acquisition by Alpac was finalised in June 2022, following approval by Euronews' Supervisory Board and a green light from the French Ministry of Economy and Finance ([European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/questions/reponses_qe/2022/002444/P9_RE(2022)002444_EN.pdf)).

Since the Alpac takeover, Euronews has reduced its original content output, relying more on news packages produced by its member state broadcast partners and international newswires, and has shifted production towards short-form news packages and documentaries aimed at streaming audiences ([The Desk](https://thedesk.net/2023/03/euronews-layoffs-closing-lyon-france-headquarters/)). The stat that captures the scale of the change is the workforce: the March 2023 restructuring cut 198 jobs from a total of 478 — more than 40 percent of the channel's staff — and put the Lyon headquarters complex up for sale ([Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2023/03/06/euronews-dismantled/)). One reporter told *Le Monde* that "the dream of a European channel is definitively shattered", because the institution's spirit had been to bring together journalists of all nationalities in one place ([Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2023/03/06/euronews-dismantled/)).

## The takeover and the questions around it

The sale to Alpac Capital drew scrutiny from the start because of the buyer's political connections. The European Parliament's record notes that Alpac is "said to have close ties to Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán and strong corporate links to the business ecosystem around him" ([European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/questions/reponses_qe/2022/002444/P9_RE(2022)002444_EN.pdf)). A joint investigation published on 11 April 2024 by the French newspaper *Le Monde*, the Portuguese newspaper *Expresso* and the Hungarian outlet Direkt36 revealed that at least a third of the takeover funds came from sources linked to Orbán and government-related actors ([IFJ/EFJ](https://www.ifj.org/media-centre/news/detail/article/france-journalists-union-calls-for-the-opening-of-a-parliamentary-inquiry-into-euronews-takeover)).

The leaked documents behind that investigation put numbers on the deal: a €150 million purchase price plus €20 million to finance Euronews and cover its restructuring, €170 million in total, with €45 million provided by Széchenyi Funds (SZTA), a Hungarian state capital fund formerly controlled by the Ministry of Finance, and a €12.5 million long-term loan from New Land Media, a company owned by businessman Gyula Balásy ([Direkt36](https://xpatloop.com/channels/2024/04/secret-documents-reveal-orbans-people-were-behind-purchase-of-one-of-europes-biggest-tv-channels.html)). The same documents stated political aims for the acquisition, including "mitigating left-wing ideology and bias/one-sidedness" in cooperation with universities ([Direkt36](https://xpatloop.com/channels/2024/04/secret-documents-reveal-orbans-people-were-behind-purchase-of-one-of-europes-biggest-tv-channels.html)). The implication the investigators drew is that a channel built to give Europe its own voice was bought, in part, with Hungarian state money for political reasons — though sources familiar with the channel's workings told the investigators there was no indication the new owners were interfering in editorial decisions, and then-CEO Guillaume Dubois told *Le Monde* the newsroom was under no pressure ([Direkt36](https://xpatloop.com/channels/2024/04/secret-documents-reveal-orbans-people-were-behind-purchase-of-one-of-europes-biggest-tv-channels.html)).

The financial dependence underneath the story is the [European Union](/organizations/european-union/) itself: Euronews receives EU funding under a multi-year agreement, €18.8 million in 2022 and €12.2 million in 2023, and the leaked risk assessment noted that 44 percent of its revenues come from EU contracts ([Direkt36](https://xpatloop.com/channels/2024/04/secret-documents-reveal-orbans-people-were-behind-purchase-of-one-of-europes-biggest-tv-channels.html)). The Commission is not a shareholder and says it has no role in Euronews' financial decisions, but its relationship with the channel is governed by a Framework Partnership Agreement implemented through annual grant agreements, and Euronews must publish an annual transparency report and report its audience figures ([European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/questions/reponses_qe/2022/002444/P9_RE(2022)002444_EN.pdf)). Following the transaction, 175 of 370 employees left in a redundancy plan, and the channel moved its headquarters from Lyon to Brussels in 2024 ([IFJ/EFJ](https://www.ifj.org/media-centre/news/detail/article/france-journalists-union-calls-for-the-opening-of-a-parliamentary-inquiry-into-euronews-takeover)).

## What Euronews broadcasts today

Euronews still presents itself as the European alternative to the American rolling-news model it was founded to answer, and its multilingual reach remains its defining feature: 13 languages, 24 hours a day, seven days a week, from a newsroom of more than 400 journalists of 25 nationalities ([World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/organizations/euronews)). Beyond the core channel it has licensed its name to franchised local channels in south-eastern Europe: since November 2018 it has signed five branded affiliate agreements, with Euronews Albania starting broadcasting in November 2019 and Euronews Georgia in September 2020, followed by Euronews Serbia and Euronews Romania ([Broadband TV News](https://www.broadbandtvnews.com/2021/04/29/euronews-set-for-bulgaria/)). The channel's reporting ranges across European and world news, and its video journalism still reaches small, unusual stories: in March 2023 it aired a video segment on the [Banned Books Museum](/organizations/banned-books-museum/) in [Tallinn](/places/tallinn/), [Estonia](/places/estonia/), a one-room museum of censored books ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/video/2023/03/14/banned-books-from-all-over-the-world-displayed-in-tallinn-museum)). Its reach has also made it a target: Russian TV providers blocked Euronews two days before the invasion of Ukraine, and the channel's website reached its highest number of unique visitors ever when the invasion started ([European Week of Regions and Cities](https://eu-regions-week.europa.eu/european-channel-attracts-outside-its-continent)).

The relocation announced in March 2023 reshaped where that journalism is made: the Lyon headquarters complex was sold, 198 positions were eliminated there, and new bureaus opened in Berlin, Rome, Lisbon, Madrid and London while the principal headquarters moved to Brussels ([Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2023/03/06/euronews-dismantled/)). The French journalists' union SNJ has called for a parliamentary inquiry into the origins of the funds used to buy the channel, a call backed by the International and European Federations of Journalists ([IFJ/EFJ](https://www.ifj.org/media-centre/news/detail/article/france-journalists-union-calls-for-the-opening-of-a-parliamentary-inquiry-into-euronews-takeover)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim a single settled price for the 2022 takeover: the €170 million figure covers a €150 million purchase price plus €20 million for financing and restructuring, and both numbers are stated. It does not claim the takeover was finalised in a single month, because the Commission records June 2022 and the IFJ records July 2022. It does not claim a single language count, because counts of 12, 13 and 18 all circulate depending on whether franchised channels are included; it uses the World Economic Forum's 13 for the core channel. It does not name the British buyer of the early 49 percent stake, because The Desk and other accounts disagree over whether it was ITV or ITN, and it does not state a percentage for NBCUniversal's 2017 stake for the same reason. It does not claim the new owners have interfered editorially: the investigators themselves reported no sign of interference, and that absence of evidence is stated rather than filled in. It does not claim a current audience figure — the 145 million monthly viewers is the company's own claim from the 2021 sale announcement, not an independently audited number.
