## Overview

The New York Observer was, for most of its print life, exactly what its pink newsprint suggested: a cheeky, insider chronicle of Manhattan's business and social elite ([The Harvard Crimson](http://www.thecrimson.com/article/2006/8/4/kushner-buys-ny-observer-jared-c/)). It was first published in September 1987 by Arthur L. Carter, a former Wall Street executive, and made its name covering the city's power brokers in a format no one could mistake on a newsstand ([The Harvard Crimson](http://www.thecrimson.com/article/2006/8/4/kushner-buys-ny-observer-jared-c/)).

In July 2006, that paper was bought by a 25-year-old: [Jared Kushner](/people/jared-kushner/), who paid $10 million for a controlling stake in a weekly losing about $2 million a year ([The Harvard Crimson](http://www.thecrimson.com/article/2006/8/4/kushner-buys-ny-observer-jared-c/)). Kushner, then attending NYU's law and business schools, promised not to interfere in editorial content and said he would focus on advertising and circulation ([The Harvard Crimson](http://www.thecrimson.com/article/2006/8/4/kushner-buys-ny-observer-jared-c/)). He would serve as the paper's publisher for roughly the next decade ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner)).

## The rightward drift

Under Kushner the paper's politics moved. It endorsed Mitt Romney over Barack Obama in 2012 — an early sign of the shift ([GantNews](https://gantnews.com/2016/04/13/new-york-observer-loses-top-reporter-over-trump-coverage/amp/)). Then, in April 2016, the Observer endorsed Donald Trump in the Republican presidential primary, becoming one of only four papers to do so, alongside the New York Post, the Santa Barbara News-Press, and the National Enquirer ([St. Louis Jewish Light](https://stljewishlight.org/news/world-news/news-outlet-owned-by-trump-son-in-law-wont-endorse-candidate/)). The editorial addressed the conflict of interest head-on: "Donald Trump is the father-in-law of the Observer's publisher. That is not a reason to endorse him," it read ([GantNews](https://gantnews.com/2016/04/13/new-york-observer-loses-top-reporter-over-trump-coverage/amp/)).

The endorsement broke the newsroom. Ross Barkan, the paper's national political reporter, announced he was leaving the week of the endorsement over concerns with its Trump coverage ([GantNews](https://gantnews.com/2016/04/13/new-york-observer-loses-top-reporter-over-trump-coverage/amp/)). The proximate cause was a revelation that Kushner had helped write the speech Trump delivered to the American Israel Public Affairs Committee and that editor-in-chief Ken Kurson — a longtime Kushner friend — had reviewed it before it went public; the paper then said it was "re-visiting" its Trump coverage policy and that no editorial staffer would give input to the campaign ([GantNews](https://gantnews.com/2016/04/13/new-york-observer-loses-top-reporter-over-trump-coverage/amp/)). A Jewish staff writer published an open letter calling out Kushner over perceived anti-Semitic messaging in his father-in-law's campaign ([St. Louis Jewish Light](https://stljewishlight.org/news/world-news/news-outlet-owned-by-trump-son-in-law-wont-endorse-candidate/)). The paper did not repeat the endorsement in the general election, Kurson said ([St. Louis Jewish Light](https://stljewishlight.org/news/world-news/news-outlet-owned-by-trump-son-in-law-wont-endorse-candidate/)).

## Print dies, a fire sale fails

In November 2016, days after Trump's election, the Observer stopped printing physical copies and became a digital-only publication — dropping "New York" from the masthead to become simply the "Observer" ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/jared-kushner-donald-trump-white-house-observer-senior-adviser/); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/biography/jared-kushner)).

Behind the scenes, Kushner was trying to get out. Days after the election, he and Observer Media chair Joseph Meyer quietly shopped the paper to two die-hard Hillary Clinton supporters: mega-donor Haim Saban and Media Matters founder David Brock ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/jared-kushner-tried-to-sell-his-newspaper-to-hillary-clinton-biggest-supporters-haim-saban/)). Saban confirmed to TheWrap that Kushner offered him the paper — after initially denying it — and editor-in-chief Kurson confirmed he and Meyer had talked to Saban by phone in late 2016 and that he met Brock in January 2017 ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/jared-kushner-tried-to-sell-his-newspaper-to-hillary-clinton-biggest-supporters-haim-saban/)). The talks collapsed over money: a source said Kushner demanded $20 million for the paper — double what he had paid exactly a decade earlier ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/jared-kushner-tried-to-sell-his-newspaper-to-hillary-clinton-biggest-supporters-haim-saban/)).

## The 2017 divestiture

With the sale talks dead, Kushner divested as he entered the White House. In January 2017, as he resigned all his professional roles to become a senior adviser to the president, his lawyers told CNNMoney the Observer would be sold to a family trust — one whose beneficiaries excluded Kushner, his wife Ivanka Trump, and their children ([GantNews](https://gantnews.com/2017/01/09/jared-kushner-to-transfer-observer-interest-to-family-trust/)). Joseph Meyer, the chairman and CEO, took over as publisher, writing in a staff memo: "I will be taking over as Observer publisher and Jared will no longer have an ownership stake in the Observer. He has resigned from our editorial board and will play no role in the publication's affairs going forward" ([GantNews](https://gantnews.com/2017/01/09/jared-kushner-to-transfer-observer-interest-to-family-trust/)).

Today the paper continues as a digital publication under Observer Media, which also publishes the Commercial Observer; first-party company coverage identifies James R. Freiman as CEO of Observer Media, which owns the Commercial Observer ([Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2023/09/commercial-observer-power-gala-2023/)). Kushner has no ownership stake and no role in the company ([GantNews](https://gantnews.com/2017/01/09/jared-kushner-to-transfer-observer-interest-to-family-trust/)).
