## Overview

FiscalNote Holdings, Inc. is an AI-driven enterprise software company that sells policy and regulatory intelligence — tools for tracking legislation, analyzing regulation, and managing government-affairs workflows — from its headquarters at 1201 Pennsylvania Avenue NW in Washington, DC ([U.S. SEC, Form 8-K](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001823466/a2a28fa8-5725-4c25-b179-aa1621b8c869.pdf)). Founded in 2013 by Tim Hwang, the company built its early product around aggregating public legislative data from all 50 states and analyzing bills against past voting history to predict their likelihood of passage ([TechCrunch](http://techcrunch.com/2013/07/17/fiscalnote-advisors/)). By the time it went public, it counted more than 3,000 customers — corporations, and, at one point, the U.S. Supreme Court among them — with roughly 650 employees across eight countries ([Artificial Lawyer](https://www.artificiallawyer.com/2021/11/09/fiscalnote-to-make-next-legal-tech-ipo-values-at-1-3bn/)).

The company is a registered filer with the [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](/organizations/us-securities-and-exchange-commission/), and its financial history below is drawn from those regulatory filings rather than from company marketing.

## The CQ Roll Call bet and the public listing

FiscalNote's defining acquisition came in August 2018, when it bought CQ Roll Call — the Washington political-news operation — from The Economist Group for $180 million in a combination of cash and equity ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/dc/2018/08/21/fiscalnote-officially-acquires-cq-roll-call-from-economist-group/)). The deal made The Economist Group FiscalNote's largest single shareholder with an 18% stake, put the group's CEO on FiscalNote's board, and brought in a strategic equity investment from S&P Global ([Financial News](https://financial-news.co.uk/fiscalnote-completes-acquisition-of-cq-roll-call-201808/)). The acquisition fused the newsroom's journalism with FiscalNote's data products and made the combined company one of the largest technology employers headquartered in Washington, DC ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/dc/2018/08/21/fiscalnote-officially-acquires-cq-roll-call-from-economist-group/)).

FiscalNote went public in 2022 through a business combination with the special purpose acquisition company Duddell Street Acquisition Corp. Shareholders approved the deal with 92.5% of votes cast, and FiscalNote's Class A common stock and warrants began trading on the New York Stock Exchange on August 1, 2022, under the ticker symbols NOTE and NOTE WS ([SPACInsider](https://www.spacinsider.com/news/market-intel/duddell-street-shareholders-approve-fiscalnote-deal)). Less than two years later, Hwang disclosed in an SEC filing that he was exploring putting together a consortium to take the company private again; the board set up a special committee, but no transaction materialized ([Benzinga](https://markets.financialcontent.com/pennwell.laserfocusworld/article/newsdirect-2023-12-26-m-and-a-trends-in-tech-fiscalnote-nyse-note-ceo-tim-hwang-explores-taking-company-private-company-sets-up-special-committee)).

## From profitable-on-paper to shrinking

In November 2024 the company announced a leadership succession: Hwang, after nearly 12 years as chief executive, would move to Executive Chairman, and President and COO Josh Resnik — previously the company's general counsel — would become President and CEO effective January 1, 2025 ([U.S. SEC, Form 8-K](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001823466/a2a28fa8-5725-4c25-b179-aa1621b8c869.pdf)). FiscalNote's 2024 annual report showed revenue of $120.3 million, down from $132.6 million in 2023, and net income of $9.5 million — but that profit was entirely an accounting artifact: a $72.0 million gain on the sale of businesses offset an operating loss of $31.9 million ([Form 10-K](https://www.annualreports.com/HostedData/AnnualReports/PDF/NYSE_NOTE_2024.pdf)).

The selloff continued through 2025. The company divested Aicel Technologies, Oxford Analytica, Dragonfly Intelligence, and TimeBase (after Board.org in 2024), shrinking full-year revenue to $95.4 million — a 21% decline — and producing a net loss of $65.2 million. Annual recurring revenue ended 2025 at $84.1 million, down 21% year over year ([Form 8-K, March 2026](https://api-gw-prd.stocktwits.com/earnings-api/v1/documents/463d1bf2-ad35-4008-a42f-229e56ffcd71/8-K.pdf)).

## Delisting and the OTC chapter

On March 25, 2026, the NYSE notified FiscalNote that its 30-trading-day average share price had fallen below $1.00 and that delisting proceedings would commence; trading in the stock and warrants was suspended immediately ([Form 8-K](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/NOTE/8-k-fiscal-note-holdings-inc-reports-material-event-05ec91ac4ed4.html)). FiscalNote chose not to appeal, the NYSE filed Form 25, and the delisting became effective April 13, 2026 — the shares are now quoted on the OTCID Basic Market, still under the ticker NOTE ([DEF 14A proxy](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/NOTE/def-14a-fiscal-note-holdings-inc-definitive-proxy-578d6a8becf0.html)). In the same March 2026 announcement, the company unveiled a workforce reduction of approximately 25%, guided 2026 revenue at just $80–83 million, and said its board continued to review strategic options ([Form 8-K, March 2026](https://api-gw-prd.stocktwits.com/earnings-api/v1/documents/463d1bf2-ad35-4008-a42f-229e56ffcd71/8-K.pdf)).
