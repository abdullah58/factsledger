## Overview

Most companies that get big get bought. Eli Lilly and Company, founded in Indianapolis in 1876 and now 150 years old, never was — and the reason is verifiable, not sentimental: the Lilly Endowment, organized by the founding family in 1937, holds about 10% of the company's shares, making it the largest shareholder of a drugmaker whose stock has made the foundation itself the largest private charity in the United States, with $79.9 billion in assets at the end of 2024 ([Crain Currency](https://www.craincurrency.com/philanthropy/weight-loss-drug-craze-boosts-80-billion-lilly-endowment-largest-us)). That friendly stake has been a standing takeover defense through a century of pharmaceutical consolidation ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/11881-endowment-s-plan-to-diversify-erodes-one-of-lilly-s-takeover-safeguards)). The company's first blockbuster was equally structural: in 1923 it became the first company to mass-produce insulin, after the University of Toronto granted it a one-year monopoly — expiring June 1923 — to produce "Isletin-Lilly," one of the earliest academic-industry partnerships explicitly created to bring a new drug to market ([in.gov](https://www.in.gov/history/files/Kurtz,-Reagan.pdf)). The latest chapter is tirzepatide: marketed as Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes and Zepbound for obesity, it became the world's best-selling medicine in October 2025, surpassing Merck's Keytruda ([STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2025/10/30/eli-lilly-zepbound-mounjaro-bestselling-obesity-drug/)).

## What the organization is known for

Lilly is known as America's most consistently inventive drugmaker — breakthroughs arriving roughly one a generation. Colonel Eli Lilly, a trained chemist and Civil War veteran, founded the company on May 10, 1876, on Pearl Street in Indianapolis, "to create effective, high-quality medicines — revolutionary in an era of untested remedies," according to the Indiana state historical marker dedicated in the company's 150th year ([events.in.gov](https://events.in.gov/event/isl-ihb-eli-lilly-and-company-state-historical-marker-dedicated-in-indianapolis)). He started with three other people — his 14-year-old son and two employees — and the sign above the door read simply "Eli Lilly, Chemist" ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis](https://indyencyclopedia.org/eli-lilly-and-company/)). (On the name: the company is named for its founder, Colonel Eli Lilly — not the other way around.)

The firm industrialized medicine earlier than its rivals: it was one of the first pharmaceutical companies to establish a dedicated research program, in 1886, under its first pharmaceutical chemist, Ernest Eberhardt ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis](https://indyencyclopedia.org/eli-lilly-and-company/)). The founder's son and grandsons ran the firm until 1953, when the first non-family president was appointed ([events.in.gov](https://events.in.gov/event/isl-ihb-eli-lilly-and-company-state-historical-marker-dedicated-in-indianapolis)). Lilly hit $1 million in sales by 1905, survived the Great Depression without a single layoff, and opened its first overseas subsidiary in England in 1934 ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis](https://indyencyclopedia.org/eli-lilly-and-company/)).

## Products and services

Lilly's product history reads as a timeline of 20th-century medicine. The first breakthrough came in 1923 with the mass production of the world's first commercial insulin, Iletin ([events.in.gov](https://events.in.gov/event/isl-ihb-eli-lilly-and-company-state-historical-marker-dedicated-in-indianapolis)). After Toronto researchers isolated insulin but could not scale it, Lilly's biochemical expert George Clowes partnered with Frederick Banting and Charles Best at the University of Toronto in 1921; two-plus years and more than $100,000 of research later, Lilly had the world's first commercial supply, and within a year of the contract was producing enough insulin to satisfy demand across all of North America ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis](https://indyencyclopedia.org/eli-lilly-and-company/); [in.gov](https://www.in.gov/history/files/Kurtz,-Reagan.pdf)).

The state historical marker lists the milestones that followed: penicillin, the polio vaccine, Oncovin, Keflex, Humulin, Prozac and Mounjaro ([events.in.gov](https://events.in.gov/event/isl-ihb-eli-lilly-and-company-state-historical-marker-dedicated-in-indianapolis)). Lilly was among the first companies to mass-produce penicillin in the 1940s, and was the first company to produce and distribute the Salk polio vaccine in the mid-1950s ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis](https://indyencyclopedia.org/eli-lilly-and-company/)). In 1982 it introduced Humulin, the first human insulin made with recombinant DNA technology, and in 1986 Prozac (fluoxetine), developed by company researchers Ray Fuller, David Wong and Bryan Molloy ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis](https://indyencyclopedia.org/eli-lilly-and-company/)).

The current franchise is tirzepatide, one molecule sold under two indication-specific brand names ([NovaPharmaNews](https://novapharmanews.com/us/articles/mounjaro-fda-approval-obesity-tirzepatide-eli-lilly)). **Mounjaro** was approved on May 13, 2022, for type 2 diabetes — the first-in-class dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist ([Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20220613/fda-approves-tirzepatide-first-dual-incretin-agonist-for-type-2-diabetes)). **Zepbound** was approved on November 8, 2023, for chronic weight management; the SURMOUNT-1 trial showed 20.9% mean weight loss at the 15 mg dose at 72 weeks versus 3.1% for placebo ([NovaPharmaNews](https://novapharmanews.com/us/articles/mounjaro-fda-approval-obesity-tirzepatide-eli-lilly)). In June 2024 Zepbound became the first pharmacologic treatment for moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity ([NovaPharmaNews](https://novapharmanews.com/us/articles/mounjaro-fda-approval-obesity-tirzepatide-eli-lilly)). Note the safety profile documented at approval: thyroid C-cell tumors in rats; contraindicated with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2; not indicated for type 1 diabetes ([Healio](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20220613/fda-approves-tirzepatide-first-dual-incretin-agonist-for-type-2-diabetes)).

## Ownership, founders and leadership

The company was founded by Colonel Eli Lilly and remained under family leadership — his son, then his grandsons — until 1953 ([events.in.gov](https://events.in.gov/event/isl-ihb-eli-lilly-and-company-state-historical-marker-dedicated-in-indianapolis)). The founding family's influence persists through the Lilly Endowment, organized in 1937, which holds about 10% of the company's shares as its largest investor; at the end of 2024 its $79.9 billion in assets made it the largest private foundation in the United States, surpassing the Gates Foundation's $77.2 billion ([Crain Currency](https://www.craincurrency.com/philanthropy/weight-loss-drug-craze-boosts-80-billion-lilly-endowment-largest-us)). The endowment is a separate entity from the company, founded by the family with stock donations that still account for more than 90% of its assets; it disburses billions annually, focused on religion, education and community development, largely in Indiana ([Crain Currency](https://www.craincurrency.com/philanthropy/weight-loss-drug-craze-boosts-80-billion-lilly-endowment-largest-us)).

The company is publicly traded on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker LLY. Its chair and chief executive is David A. Ricks ([events.in.gov](https://events.in.gov/event/isl-ihb-eli-lilly-and-company-state-historical-marker-dedicated-in-indianapolis)).

## Funding and acquisitions

Lilly grew on product revenue rather than venture funding. In 2023 it became the world's most valuable drugmaker, with a $412.2 billion market capitalization, surpassing Johnson & Johnson and Pfizer ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis](https://indyencyclopedia.org/eli-lilly-and-company/)). On October 30, 2025, its tirzepatide franchise became the world's best-selling medicine, with $10.1 billion in third-quarter sales and $24.8 billion year-to-date — passing Merck's Keytruda, which posted $8.1 billion and $23.3 billion in the same periods ([STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2025/10/30/eli-lilly-zepbound-mounjaro-bestselling-obesity-drug/)).

## Regulation, licenses and compliance record

In 1982 Lilly released the arthritis drug Oraflex in the US after a British medical journal had linked it to deaths in Europe; the company withdrew the drug, a federal court found it not guilty of willfully withholding information or endangering patients, and it was fined $25,000 ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis](https://indyencyclopedia.org/eli-lilly-and-company/)). In the late 1980s the FDA charged Lilly with dishonest reporting and manufacturing irregularities; the company was again acquitted of the underlying charges but fined $75,000 ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis](https://indyencyclopedia.org/eli-lilly-and-company/)).

In the late 2010s the company faced sustained criticism over insulin pricing — the average price of a vial was between $200 and $300 — and responded by opening the Lilly Diabetes Solution Center in 2018 and introducing a lower-cost generic Humalog in 2019 ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis](https://indyencyclopedia.org/eli-lilly-and-company/)).

## Competitors and partners

Lilly's defining competitor in the current metabolic market is Novo Nordisk — earlier to market with semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy), but now widely seen as surpassed by Lilly's dual-mechanism tirzepatide on weight-loss outcomes ([STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2025/10/30/eli-lilly-zepbound-mounjaro-bestselling-obesity-drug/)). Historically it partnered with the University of Toronto on insulin ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis](https://indyencyclopedia.org/eli-lilly-and-company/)) and with Boehringer Ingelheim on diabetes drugs including Tradjenta (2011), Jardiance and Trulicity (2014) ([Encyclopedia of Indianapolis](https://indyencyclopedia.org/eli-lilly-and-company/)).
