## Overview

Payability is a New York fintech lender that finances a very specific kind of small business: the online seller. Founded in 2015 and led by co-founder and CEO Keith Smith, the company builds lending and working-capital products exclusively for eCommerce sellers on marketplaces such as Amazon, Walmart and Shopify — businesses that traditional banks, in PYMNTS's framing, left "empty-handed because banks, big and small, smelled risk and uncertainty" ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/connectedeconomy/2022/online-sellers-needing-capital-turn-to-fintech-lenders-who-know-the-terrain/)).

The core problem Payability solves is the marketplace payout lag. Marketplaces release seller funds on delayed cycles — Amazon pays out every two weeks, and with holdbacks and limiters it can take a month or more for transaction funds to actually reach a seller's bank account — while sellers need daily cash flow to buy inventory and keep growing ([Glenbrook](https://glenbrook.com/payments_on_fire/episode-141-the-data-driven-money-supply-chain-keith-smith-ceo-payability-payments-on-fire-risk-series/)). Payability inserts itself into that gap: it pays sellers first and then waits out the standard marketplace payment terms itself ([Glenbrook](https://glenbrook.com/payments_on_fire/episode-141-the-data-driven-money-supply-chain-keith-smith-ceo-payability-payments-on-fire-risk-series/)).

## Two products, one underwriting insight

The company offers two primary financing products. Instant Access provides accelerated daily payouts against sales the seller has already made — next-day money instead of waiting 60 days or more after orders on delayed-term marketplaces like Amazon or Walmart ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/payability-expands-fintech-services-to-more-marketplaces/526931/)). Instant Advance is a shorter-duration capital advance against future receivables, meant to be recycled: sellers draw it to buy inventory or fund advertising, sell through, and draw again ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/connectedeconomy/2022/online-sellers-needing-capital-turn-to-fintech-lenders-who-know-the-terrain/); [Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/payability-expands-fintech-services-to-more-marketplaces/526931/)).

What distinguishes the model is underwriting. Rather than scoring the individual's personal credit — which for online-only businesses often reveals little — Payability builds its risk assessment around the seller's history with the platforms they sell through: sales volumes, product categories and platform performance data, analyzed with machine-learning models trained on marketplace data signals ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/connectedeconomy/2022/online-sellers-needing-capital-turn-to-fintech-lenders-who-know-the-terrain/); [Glenbrook](https://glenbrook.com/payments_on_fire/episode-141-the-data-driven-money-supply-chain-keith-smith-ceo-payability-payments-on-fire-risk-series/)). PYMNTS framed it as a lifeline for "small sellers without the business bona fides for a bank loan" — some of whom, it noted, were being helped to grow big enough to become acquisition targets ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/connectedeconomy/2022/online-sellers-needing-capital-turn-to-fintech-lenders-who-know-the-terrain/)).

## Pandemic boom and scale

The COVID-19 eCommerce surge was Payability's accelerant. Bank Innovation reported in 2020 that its financing portfolio had jumped 50% since the start of the pandemic, that it had financed more than $300 million since then, and that its customer base had nearly doubled — taking cumulative financing past $2.5 billion since the 2015 founding ([Bank Innovation](https://finainews.com/allposts/retail/payability-eyes-product-expansion-amid-pandemic-boom/)). By early 2021, Glenbrook's podcast host described the firm as having "loaned over $2.5B since 2015 to Amazon and Shopify sellers" ([Glenbrook](https://glenbrook.com/payments_on_fire/episode-141-the-data-driven-money-supply-chain-keith-smith-ceo-payability-payments-on-fire-risk-series/)).

The company expanded its footprint beyond Amazon early: in 2018 it extended its financing and payout services to Newegg, Tophatter, Walmart and Jet.com, alongside the launch of Instant Advance — a move Retail Dive framed as following sellers who were increasingly spreading across multiple marketplaces, with nearly half of Amazon sellers surveyed still drawing 81%–100% of their revenue from Amazon even as Walmart and Jet.com gained ground ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/payability-expands-fintech-services-to-more-marketplaces/526931/)). Its customers sell on [Amazon](/organizations/amazon/) and other major marketplaces, and its advances reach up to $250,000 for growth-stage sellers ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/connectedeconomy/2022/online-sellers-needing-capital-turn-to-fintech-lenders-who-know-the-terrain/)).

Glenbrook's framing puts the model in a longer arc: since the 2008 banking crisis left traditional lenders more risk-averse toward small business, fintechs have stepped into the "money supply chain" as new distributors of funds, enabled by machine-learning underwriting and risk models — with banks and institutional investors still supplying the money, now routed through the fintech channel ([Glenbrook](https://glenbrook.com/payments_on_fire/episode-141-the-data-driven-money-supply-chain-keith-smith-ceo-payability-payments-on-fire-risk-series/)).
