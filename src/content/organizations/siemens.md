## Overview

Siemens AG is a German technology company headquartered in Berlin and Munich that traces its origins to a ten-person Berlin workshop founded on October 1, 1847, and now employs about 318,000 people with annual revenue of €78.9 billion ([Siemens AG](https://press.siemens.com/jp/en/pressrelease/pr-20221012); [Siemens AG](http://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:4f2a7444-211a-4f8c-b01c-16f4e4cbe3b9/251113-press-release-en.pdf)). It focuses on industry, infrastructure, mobility and healthcare, and under President and CEO [Roland Busch](/people/roland-busch/) it has been reshaping itself from a classic industrial conglomerate into an industrial-software company — most visibly by acquiring [Altair Engineering](/organizations/altair-engineering/) in 2025 and folding it into the Siemens Xcelerator platform.

The company began as Telegraphen-Bauanstalt von Siemens & Halske, founded by Werner von Siemens and precision mechanic Johann Georg Halske ([Siemens AG](https://press.siemens.com/jp/en/pressrelease/pr-20221012)). Operations started on October 12, 1847, in a courtyard workshop on Schoeneberger Strasse in Berlin, making pointer telegraphs — Werner's invention, which used electricity to transmit messages faster and more reliably than before ([Siemens AG](https://press.siemens.com/jp/en/pressrelease/pr-20221012)). The pattern of that first century — a laboratory invention turned into an industrial business — repeated with the dynamo machine of 1866, the first device able to convert mechanical energy into electric power economically, which paved the way for the age of electricity ([Siemens AG](https://press.siemens.com/jp/en/pressrelease/pr-20221012)). Siemens was also early on social policy for its time, introducing an employee pension fund in 1872, roughly twenty years before comparable German state benefits ([Siemens AG](https://press.siemens.com/jp/en/pressrelease/pr-20221012)).

## From conglomerate to ecosystem

For most of its history Siemens grew by accumulating businesses, from power generation to medical imaging to trains. The reversal of that logic — the defining corporate story of its recent decades — came under the Vision 2020+ strategy: give each business entrepreneurial independence under the shared Siemens brand ([Siemens AG](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/siemens-writes-new-chapter-powerful-ecosystem-instead-conglomerate)). Siemens Healthineers, the medical-technology business, was listed separately in March 2018, with Siemens keeping a majority stake ([Siemens AG](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/siemens-writes-new-chapter-powerful-ecosystem-instead-conglomerate)). Then, on September 28, 2020, Siemens Energy was listed in Frankfurt after Siemens spun off 55 percent of it to its own shareholders and transferred a further 9.9 percent to its pension trust, leaving the three companies — Siemens AG, Healthineers and Energy — to work together "within an ecosystem of common interests" rather than inside one conglomerate ([Siemens AG](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/siemens-writes-new-chapter-powerful-ecosystem-instead-conglomerate)). The slimmed-down Siemens AG, with some 240,000 employees at that point, concentrated on digital transformation of industry, smart infrastructure and sustainable transportation ([Siemens AG](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/siemens-writes-new-chapter-powerful-ecosystem-instead-conglomerate)).

The next phase was about software, not shedding. On June 29, 2022, Siemens launched Siemens Xcelerator, an open digital business platform for industry, buildings, grids and mobility, combining a curated portfolio of IoT-enabled hardware, software and services with a partner ecosystem and a marketplace ([Siemens AG](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/node/6496)). The platform became the frame for the company's biggest software bets. The stat that captures the pivot: Siemens says it has invested over $100 billion in the United States over the past twenty years, and its two largest recent software deals both landed in 2025 ([Siemens AG](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/siemens-acquires-altair-create-most-complete-ai-powered-portfolio-industrial-software)).

## The Altair and Dotmatics acquisitions

On October 30, 2024, Siemens agreed to acquire Altair Engineering, the [Troy, Michigan](/places/troy-michigan/) simulation-software company founded by [James Scapa](/people/james-scapa/), at $113 per share in cash ([Siemens AG](https://press.siemens.com/jp/en/pressrelease/pr-20241030)). The price is quoted two ways, and both are correct: Altair announced an equity value of about $10.6 billion, while Siemens announced an enterprise value of about $10 billion — equity value counts only the shares, enterprise value adjusts for cash and debt ([Altair Engineering Inc. via GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2024/10/30/2972140/0/en/Altair-Signs-Definitive-Agreement-with-Siemens-to-be-Acquired-for-10-6-Billion.html); [Siemens AG](https://press.siemens.com/jp/en/pressrelease/pr-20241030)). The deal, fully cash-financed, was expected to add about €600 million — 8 percent — to Siemens' digital business revenue and to become earnings-accretive about two years after closing ([Siemens AG](https://press.siemens.com/jp/en/pressrelease/pr-20241030)). Siemens completed it on March 26, 2025, adding Altair's mechanical and electromagnetic simulation, high-performance computing, data science and AI capabilities to Xcelerator under its ONE Tech Company growth program ([Siemens AG](https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/siemens-acquires-altair-create-most-complete-ai-powered-portfolio-industrial-software); [Manufacturing Dive](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/siemens-acquires-altair-engineering-10-billion-boost-ai-capabilities/743798/)).

Four months later, on July 1, 2025, Siemens completed the $5.1 billion acquisition of Dotmatics, a Boston-based provider of life-sciences R&D software, folding it into Digital Industries Software and extending its product-lifecycle-management portfolio into life sciences ([Siemens AG](https://news.siemens.com/pl-pl/dotmatics-closing/)). Siemens said the deal expands its addressable market for industrial software by $11 billion and that Dotmatics was expected to generate more than $300 million in fiscal 2025 revenue at an adjusted EBITDA margin above 40 percent ([Siemens AG](https://news.siemens.com/pl-pl/dotmatics-closing/)).

The results followed. In fiscal 2025, which ended September 30, 2025, Siemens generated revenue of €78.9 billion, up 5 percent on a comparable basis, and net income of €10.4 billion, up 16 percent — a record for the third consecutive year — with free cash flow of €10.8 billion, also a record ([Siemens AG](http://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:4f2a7444-211a-4f8c-b01c-16f4e4cbe3b9/251113-press-release-en.pdf)). The company's stated ambition under the ONE Tech Company program is to double its digital business revenue by 2030 ([Siemens AG](http://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:4f2a7444-211a-4f8c-b01c-16f4e4cbe3b9/251113-press-release-en.pdf)).
