## Overview

BetaNews is a technology news website launched on 1 June 1998 by entrepreneur Nate Mook, covering [Microsoft](/organizations/microsoft/) Windows, software, security, AI and the cloud, and known for its FileForum software-download sister site ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/welcome-new-betanews-launches/); [Skoll](http://skoll.org/contributor/nate-mook/)). Its reporting has served as a cited record of tech-industry events, notably its 2 November 2006 account of Forgent's JPEG patent settlement, which reported more than $110 million collected from over 60 companies ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/forgent-settles-jpeg-patent-litigation/)). It is not the Serbian Beta News Agency, an unrelated Belgrade wire service of a similar name.

The site began as a community project. From day one it sought to be community-driven, relying heavily on reader submissions and interaction, and by its sixth birthday in 2004 it described its readership as numbering in the millions ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/welcome-new-betanews-launches/)). Mook, who signed the site's 2004 relaunch letter as Editor-in-Chief, went on to a filmmaking career and later became CEO of World Central Kitchen ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/welcome-new-betanews-launches/); [McCain Institute](https://www.mccaininstitute.org/about/global-advisory-council/nate-mook/)). More than 28 years after launch, the site still publishes daily, describing itself as staffed by named journalists who install, test and interrogate the products they cover, under an editorial and corrections policy, with no AI-generated filler and no sponsored spin ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/)).

## Founding and the FileForum years

BetaNews launched on 1 June 1998, founded or co-founded by Nate Mook before the age of 27, alongside his other early tech ventures ([Skoll](http://skoll.org/contributor/nate-mook/); [McCain Institute](https://www.mccaininstitute.org/about/global-advisory-council/nate-mook/)). Its name reflected its original beat: the beta releases of Windows and other software, tracked for a community of testers and early adopters ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/welcome-new-betanews-launches/)). The download side of that mission lived on FileForum, the site's sister service, whose editor-in-chief in 2004 was Eddie Elmore ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/welcome-new-betanews-launches/)).

The 2004 relaunch rebuilt the site from the ground up to mark its sixth birthday, adding a software submission process that gave authors direct control over their titles through an Author Center, a Download Cart for saving programs, and overhauled software search and browsing ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/welcome-new-betanews-launches/)). The staff named in the relaunch letter were Eric Steil as lead programmer, David Worthington as senior editor and Karl Kwasny as lead designer, alongside Mook and Elmore ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/welcome-new-betanews-launches/)). FileForum remained a working distribution channel long after the relaunch: in March 2011 it obtained the final release of Firefox 4 from Mozilla's FTP servers a day before its official release, and the Windows version passed 61,000 downloads within a few hours ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/get-firefox-4-final-a-day-ahead-of-its-official-release/)).

## Coverage: Windows, betas and the Forgent story

BetaNews' durable beat has been the Windows ecosystem and the software release cycle: beta builds, final releases, security flaws and the companies behind them ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/)). Its most consequential single story in the FactsLedger corpus is its coverage of the JPEG patent litigation, a campaign that ran for years before its 2006 conclusion ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/forgent-settles-jpeg-patent-litigation/)).

On 2 November 2006, BetaNews' Ed Oswald reported that patent holding firm Forgent had settled its lawsuit over its JPEG patent for significantly less than the $1 billion it had hoped to make, taking about $8 million from about 30 companies ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/forgent-settles-jpeg-patent-litigation/)). The settlement closed a campaign in which more than 60 companies had already paid some $110 million in royalties, while a group of about 45 PC makers and other companies had refused and been sued, and 15 companies — including Yahoo — had settled before the final deal ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/forgent-settles-jpeg-patent-litigation/)). Much of Forgent's lost leverage came from the [Public Patent Foundation](/organizations/public-patent-foundation/), which succeeded in May 2006 in having the patent rejected in an initial review, after which a court in June limited the portions of the patent Forgent could assert ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/forgent-settles-jpeg-patent-litigation/)). Forgent's chairman and CEO Richard Snyder still called the outcome satisfactory, saying that over the life of the '672 patent the company had "collected in excess of $110M", and said its focus would shift to the '746 patent covering digital video recorder technology and to growing its NetSimplicity software business ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/forgent-settles-jpeg-patent-litigation/)).

## Criticism and the DMCA episode

BetaNews' editorial voice has drawn public criticism as well as readers. In June 2005, Mook reported that [Google](/organizations/google/) had begun bundling its Google Toolbar and Desktop Search software with the WinZip archive utility, opening with the claim that Google was using "a marketing tactic used primarily by spyware and adware companies" ([Ed Bott](https://edbott.com/2005/06/15/todays-clinic-in-bad-journalism/)). Tech journalist Ed Bott called the story an example of bad journalism, arguing the lede created an impression the details did not support — users were given an opt-out on the first setup screen — and quoted Techdirt's Mike Masnick to the same effect ([Ed Bott](https://edbott.com/2005/06/15/todays-clinic-in-bad-journalism/)). Bott also noted an irony: BetaNews itself ran Google AdSense ads and Vibrant Media's IntelliTXT technology, which placed ads directly inside editorial content ([Ed Bott](https://edbott.com/2005/06/15/todays-clinic-in-bad-journalism/)).

Eight years later the site was on the receiving end of a platform dispute. In October 2013, BetaNews reported that Microsoft was invoking the Digital Millennium Copyright Act to force websites such as BetaNews — FileForum in particular — to remove links to old versions of its software, rather than simply asking for updated links ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/microsoft-uses-dmca-to-force-betanews-and-others-to-remove-old-software-listings/)). Google contacted BetaNews after one such complaint from Microsoft; the complaint, published on the Chilling Effects website, concerned a FileForum listing for Microsoft Project that simply linked to a Microsoft page which no longer existed ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/microsoft-uses-dmca-to-force-betanews-and-others-to-remove-old-software-listings/)). BetaNews argued that forcing sites to link to the most recent version of an application, or to nothing at all, "is not what the DMCA was designed for" ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/microsoft-uses-dmca-to-force-betanews-and-others-to-remove-old-software-listings/)).

## BetaNews today

The site still presents itself as a working newsroom rather than an archive: its homepage says it has covered "the technology that matters since 1998 — from Windows and AI to security and the cloud", with every story written and edited by named journalists who test the products themselves ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/)). Its self-reported scale in 2026 is 28 years of reporting, more than 40 expert contributors and editors, more than 68,000 articles published, and 43,000 monthly readers worldwide ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/)). That readership figure is far smaller than the "millions of readers" the site claimed in 2004, a gap no independent audience measurement in this pass explains; both numbers are stated here only as the site's own claims at their dates ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/welcome-new-betanews-launches/); [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/)). What the record does establish is longevity: a publication founded in the first browser-war era that is still publishing daily in the AI era, still on the Windows-and-software beat it started with ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is BetaNews?**
BetaNews is a technology news website launched on 1 June 1998 by entrepreneur Nate Mook, covering Windows, software, security, AI and the cloud, with a FileForum sister site for software downloads ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/welcome-new-betanews-launches/); [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/)).

**Who founded BetaNews?**
Nate Mook founded or co-founded BetaNews before the age of 27, as confirmed by two independent institutional biographies, from Skoll and the McCain Institute ([Skoll](http://skoll.org/contributor/nate-mook/); [McCain Institute](https://www.mccaininstitute.org/about/global-advisory-council/nate-mook/)). Mook later became a filmmaker and CEO of World Central Kitchen ([McCain Institute](https://www.mccaininstitute.org/about/global-advisory-council/nate-mook/)).

**Is BetaNews the same as the Beta News Agency?**
No. BetaNews (betanews.com) is a technology news website founded in 1998. The Beta News Agency (beta.rs) is an unrelated Serbian news agency based in Belgrade, established in 1992. The similar names are a coincidence, and the two organisations have no connection.

**What is FileForum?**
FileForum is BetaNews' sister site for software downloads, run by the same team since the site's early years ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/welcome-new-betanews-launches/)). In March 2011 it distributed the final release of Firefox 4 a day before its official release, with the Windows version passing 61,000 downloads within hours ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/get-firefox-4-final-a-day-ahead-of-its-official-release/)).

**Why does FactsLedger cite BetaNews?**
Because BetaNews reported the end of Forgent Networks' JPEG patent campaign: its 2 November 2006 article recorded the final $8 million settlement, the $110 million-plus already collected from over 60 companies, and the Public Patent Foundation's invalidation of the patent ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/forgent-settles-jpeg-patent-litigation/)). Forgent later became Asure Software, whose FactsLedger profile cites that article for the settlement figures ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/forgent-settles-jpeg-patent-litigation/)).
