## Overview

The business began, literally, with two Susitas. In 1973, Shlomo Shmeltzer hired out two Israeli-made Susita cars to the Israel Defense Forces — and that was the seed of what would become Israel's largest car-leasing group ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/2004-12-06/ty-article/major-car-group-restructures-itself/0000017f-db77-df62-a9ff-dff73ab20000)). From that two-car start, Shmeltzer built a conglomerate that by 2014 employed more than 5,000 people, ran annual revenues above NIS 5.5 billion, and was valued at roughly NIS 2.5 billion — a group that stayed closely held in family hands even as it swallowed shipyards, telecom equipment makers, and insurers ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/business/2014-07-09/ty-article/.premium/shlomo-shmeltzer-dies-at-67/0000017f-e5dc-da9b-a1ff-ffdedd230000)).

The deal coverage that dominates search results skips all of that. In September 2023, Jared Kushner's Affinity Partners agreed to invest about $150 million for 15% of a new Shlomo subsidiary consolidating the automotive and credit businesses at a NIS 3.8 billion valuation ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article.aspx?did=1001457191)). The transaction was framed in the Israeli press as a milestone: the first Israeli investment by the Saudi-backed U.S. private equity firm — and, by one account, the first outside equity the family-owned group had ever taken ([Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/ry2vu0s0n); [Private Equity Insights](https://pe-insights.com/jared-kushners-saudi-backed-affinity-to-invest-150m-in-israeli-auto-company/)). Then the Gaza war intervened, the valuation sagged, and the deal finally closed in February 2024 at $110 million — a $40 million discount and a roughly 29% fall in value between signing and closing ([Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/hys3sau5a)).

## Two Susita cars and the IDF

Shlomo Shmeltzer was born in Haifa in 1947 and began his working life in the 1970s at his father-in-law's car repair business in the city ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/business/2014-07-09/ty-article/.premium/shlomo-shmeltzer-dies-at-67/0000017f-e5dc-da9b-a1ff-ffdedd230000)). In 1973 he hired out two Susita cars to the IDF — the first rental fleet of what would become the Shlomo Group ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/2004-12-06/ty-article/major-car-group-restructures-itself/0000017f-db77-df62-a9ff-dff73ab20000)). (Ctech dates the group's formal founding to 1974 — the one-year discrepancy in the record of a business born in a Haifa repair shop ([Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/ry2vu0s0n)).)

Shmeltzer managed his empire with his hands, not just his signature. Haaretz's 2004 portrait described a man who checked car batteries in the Shlomo garage on Saturday mornings and examined tires on a rental lot during a company visit; he told the paper he sometimes called the rental hotline from abroad "and tell[s] them there's a defect in the car I rented. And then I hear how they respond to my 'complaint'" ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/2004-12-06/ty-article/major-car-group-restructures-itself/0000017f-db77-df62-a9ff-dff73ab20000)). His growth strategy was buying failing businesses and fixing them: he bought the Maestro breakdown service in 2000 out of receivership and AFCON Industries in 2002 from a court-appointed receiver, and described himself as an "economic doctor" — "I take problematic businesses and I heal them" ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/2004-12-06/ty-article/major-car-group-restructures-itself/0000017f-db77-df62-a9ff-dff73ab20000)).

The acquisitions went well beyond cars. Over the years Shlomo picked up the Skoda import agency in 1991 and bought Israel Shipyards from the state in 1995 ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/2004-12-06/ty-article/major-car-group-restructures-itself/0000017f-db77-df62-a9ff-dff73ab20000)). His son Oren bought the Tadiran Telecom factory equipment in a 2000 receivership, made the company profitable, and Shlomo then took control of it outright; in 2003 the family bought Afcon Holdings from its receiver ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/business/2014-07-09/ty-article/.premium/shlomo-shmeltzer-dies-at-67/0000017f-e5dc-da9b-a1ff-ffdedd230000)). By the time of his death the group had evolved into a conglomerate with more than 5,000 employees in contracting, energy, real estate, infrastructure, shipping and hotels, with annual revenues exceeding NIS 5.5 billion and a valuation of about NIS 2.5 billion ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/business/2014-07-09/ty-article/.premium/shlomo-shmeltzer-dies-at-67/0000017f-e5dc-da9b-a1ff-ffdedd230000)).

In 2004 the automotive core was restructured: six car companies were consolidated into two — New Kopel SIXT, for operational leasing and car sales (publicly traded), and Shlomo SIXT, for car rentals, garages and towing ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/2004-12-06/ty-article/major-car-group-restructures-itself/0000017f-db77-df62-a9ff-dff73ab20000)). At the time the group ran 37,000 vehicles with turnover of NIS 1.8 billion; Shlomo held 51% of the combined company, and the plan was to sell up to 30% of New Kopel on the stock exchange and the rest of Shlomo SIXT to a strategic investor ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/2004-12-06/ty-article/major-car-group-restructures-itself/0000017f-db77-df62-a9ff-dff73ab20000)).

Shmeltzer died suddenly in July 2014 at 67, prompting tributes from Israel's business establishment — Strauss Group's Ofra Strauss called him "a real friend and the man who taught me how to manage a board meeting" ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/business/2014-07-09/ty-article/.premium/shlomo-shmeltzer-dies-at-67/0000017f-e5dc-da9b-a1ff-ffdedd230000)). Management passed to his son Asi, who was still chairman when the Affinity deal was signed ([Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/ry2vu0s0n)). Notably, while the group's bonds trade publicly, its stock never has — the family kept full ownership ([Haaretz](https://www.haaretz.com/israel-news/business/2014-07-09/ty-article/.premium/shlomo-shmeltzer-dies-at-67/0000017f-e5dc-da9b-a1ff-ffdedd230000)).

## The Affinity deal: signed at 3.8 billion, closed at 2.7

On September 6, 2023, the group announced that [Affinity Partners](/organizations/affinity-partners/) would invest NIS 570 million — roughly $150 million — for 15% of a newly formed subsidiary consolidating the automotive operations (leasing, rental, sales) and the credit business, at a valuation of NIS 3.8 billion ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article.aspx?did=1001457191); [Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/ry2vu0s0n)). It was reported as Affinity's first Israeli investment — a landmark for a firm founded in 2021 and overwhelmingly financed by Saudi capital ([Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/ry2vu0s0n)).

The terms were unusually protective. Affinity's stake was capped at 15%, with an 85/15 split in the new entity; the fund would appoint a director and an observer to the board, and a value-adjustment mechanism allowed Affinity's holding to rise as high as 18% in certain scenarios ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article.aspx?did=1001457191)). Saudi Arabia approved the investment — a necessary step given the fund's Saudi backing ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article.aspx?did=1001457191)). From the fifth year after closing, Affinity could demand that Shlomo buy it out or that the subsidiary pursue an IPO ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article.aspx?did=1001457191)). Chairman Asi Shmeltzer said the agreement reflected the group's "immense growth potential," while [Jared Kushner](/people/jared-kushner/) said he was "bullish on the long-term growth prospects of Israel and the broader new Middle East" ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article.aspx?did=1001457191); [Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/ry2vu0s0n)).

Then the market moved against both sides. The Gaza war, which began weeks after the signing, drove a selloff in Israeli equities; Shlomo's share price plunged, and the auto-leasing subsidiary's market capitalization fell 29% from NIS 3.8 billion to NIS 2.7 billion ([Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/hys3sau5a)). The deal closed on February 1, 2024 at $110 million for the 15% stake — a $40 million discount to the original price ([Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/hys3sau5a)). In a striking footnote, Shlomo itself lent Affinity $46.75 million to finance the purchase — the seller helping the buyer pay ([Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/hys3sau5a)). The agreement also gave the parties a 12-month window to apply for a license to hold more than 10% of Credit Path, the group's credit company ([Ctech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/hys3sau5a)).

The political significance of the investment grew with time. By 2026 the Shlomo stake — listed at about $110 million alongside roughly $300 million into [Phoenix Financial](/organizations/phoenix-financial/) — was being described as part of an effort to build an economic link between Saudi Arabia and Israel, with Gulf capital flowing through a Trump-family firm into the Israeli private sector ([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/worldbiz/middle-east/kushners-affinity-partners-raises-5b-amid-diplomatic-role-middle-east-1543866)). That framing arrived in the same reporting that said Kushner was raising another $5 billion from Gulf investors while serving as a U.S. diplomatic envoy — a dual role that drew ethics scrutiny ([TBS News](https://www.tbsnews.net/worldbiz/middle-east/kushners-affinity-partners-raises-5b-amid-diplomatic-role-middle-east-1543866)).
