## Overview

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau is the federal agency created after the 2007–2008 financial crisis to police consumer finance — the watchdog that sues the credit bureaus, writes the rules for lenders, and runs the complaint system Americans use when a bank or bureau will not fix a problem ([National Association of Consumer Advocates](https://www.consumeradvocates.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NACA_Voughthrg_HFSC072026.pdf)). Congress created it in the Dodd-Frank Act of 2010, it opened its doors on 21 July 2011, and over fourteen years it returned $21 billion to more than 200 million harmed consumers ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/anticipating-potential-cfpb-enforcement-priorities-as-agency-marks-10-year-anniversary.html); [National Association of Consumer Advocates](https://www.consumeradvocates.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NACA_Voughthrg_HFSC072026.pdf)). Then, in February 2025, the Trump administration fired its director, installed Russell Vought as acting director, and set out to dismantle it — and three federal judges have since ruled its refusal to ask the Federal Reserve for funding unlawful ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/-/media/files/perspectives-events/publications/2026/01/regulatory-rollback-and-lingering-limbo-the-cfpb-in-2025.pdf); [Vermont Attorney General](https://ago.vermont.gov/blog/2026/09/25/attorney-general-clark-celebrates-final-win-consumer-financial-protection-bureau-funding-lawsuit)).

The Bureau's own enforcement record is the reason its fate matters to the other profiles in this batch: it sued [Experian](/organizations/experian/) in January 2025 over sham dispute investigations and ordered [Equifax](/organizations/equifax/) to pay a $15 million penalty the same month, while its earlier order against [TransUnion](/organizations/transunion/) dates to 2017 ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/experian-information-solutions-inc/); [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/equifax-inc-and-equifax-information-services-llc/)). On [Bilt](/organizations/bilt/), by contrast, it took no formal public action at all — only an unofficial promise to repay, according to the National Association of Consumer Advocates — while [Protect Borrowers](/organizations/protect-borrowers/) wrote to it in March 2026 urging supervision of Bilt and its issuer-processor [Cardless](/organizations/cardless/) ([National Association of Consumer Advocates](https://www.consumeradvocates.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NACA_Voughthrg_HFSC072026.pdf)).

## Built to be independent

The Bureau was designed to be hard to kill. Dodd-Frank gave it an unusual funding mechanism: instead of annual appropriations, the director asks the Federal Reserve for the amount "reasonably necessary" to do the job, subject to an inflation-adjusted cap — $734 million in 2022 — and the Fed exercises no oversight over how the money is used ([Sullivan & Cromwell](https://www.sullcrom.com/SullivanCromwell/_Assets/PDFs/Memos/Supreme-Court-Holds-CFPB-Funding-Constitutional.pdf)). That design was tested in the Supreme Court, which ruled 7–2 in May 2024 that the funding structure is constitutional, reversing a Fifth Circuit decision that had invalidated the Bureau's Payday Lending Rule ([Sullivan & Cromwell](https://www.sullcrom.com/SullivanCromwell/_Assets/PDFs/Memos/Supreme-Court-Holds-CFPB-Funding-Constitutional.pdf)). Richard Cordray, the first director from 2012 to 2017, built the Bureau into an enforcer that had recovered about $14.4 billion in relief and imposed $1.7 billion in civil penalties by its tenth anniversary ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/anticipating-potential-cfpb-enforcement-priorities-as-agency-marks-10-year-anniversary.html)). Its complaint system became the public's main lever against the financial industry — and, by 2025, the single largest window into the credit bureaus' behaviour.

## The 2025 shutdown fight

In February 2025 President Trump fired Director Rohit Chopra, Russell Vought took over as acting director, and the Bureau was ordered to stop nearly all work — supervision, enforcement and rulemaking — while an attempt to eliminate about 90% of its employees was blocked by the courts ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/-/media/files/perspectives-events/publications/2026/01/regulatory-rollback-and-lingering-limbo-the-cfpb-in-2025.pdf)). Vought then declined the Bureau's quarterly Federal Reserve funding, writing to Chair Jerome Powell on 8 February 2025, on the theory that "combined earnings of the Federal Reserve System" means the Fed's profits — and the Fed has run losses since 2022 ([National Association of Consumer Advocates](https://www.consumeradvocates.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NACA_Voughthrg_HFSC072026.pdf); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/-/media/files/perspectives-events/publications/2026/01/regulatory-rollback-and-lingering-limbo-the-cfpb-in-2025.pdf)). The rollback spread: 67 guidance documents withdrawn in May 2025, the repeat-offender registry rescinded, the proposed data-broker rule withdrawn, and examiners told to read a "humility pledge" before examinations ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/-/media/files/perspectives-events/publications/2026/01/regulatory-rollback-and-lingering-limbo-the-cfpb-in-2025.pdf)). The Bureau has brought a single enforcement action since February 2025, against 28 in 2024 ([National Association of Consumer Advocates](https://www.consumeradvocates.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NACA_Voughthrg_HFSC072026.pdf)) — a near-total halt that means the regulator that once returned billions to harmed consumers is, for practical purposes, no longer bringing cases.

## The courts push back

The funding theory has now lost in three federal courts. Judge Amy Berman Jackson ruled in December 2025 that the Bureau must keep requesting funds; the CFPB never appealed her order ([Ballard Spahr](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/cfpb-surprisingly-appeals-judge-davila-7347815/)). Judge Edward Davila went further in March 2026, granting summary judgment to Rise Economy, the National Community Reinvestment Coalition and the Woodstock Institute, holding that "combined earnings" means the Federal Reserve Banks' combined revenues and that the refusal was arbitrary, capricious and contrary to law, then ordering the Bureau to keep requesting the funds "reasonably necessary" to carry out its duties, with no time limit ([Ballard Spahr](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/cfpb-surprisingly-appeals-judge-davila-7347815/)). Judge Ann Aiken added a constitutional holding in September 2026: the refusal violated the Separation of Powers Clause ([Vermont Attorney General](https://ago.vermont.gov/blog/2026/09/25/attorney-general-clark-celebrates-final-win-consumer-financial-protection-bureau-funding-lawsuit)). The Bureau complied under protest — Vought requested $145 million in January 2026 and $75.8 million more for the quarter ending in June — and appealed to the Ninth Circuit in May 2026 ([AskWhy.News](https://askwhy.news/us/money-influence/cfpb-defunding-ruled-unlawful/); [Ballard Spahr](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/cfpb-surprisingly-appeals-judge-davila-7347815/)). Meanwhile the public kept complaining: 6.6 million complaints reached the Bureau in 2025, more than double 2024, with 88% about credit reporting ([Orrick](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-04-10/cfpb-reports-complaint-volume-doubled-in-2025-citing-surge-in-credit-reporting-disputes/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What does the CFPB do?** It protects consumers in financial markets: it supervises banks and nonbanks, brings enforcement actions, writes consumer-finance rules, educates the public, and runs the complaint system that forwards grievances to companies ([Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/anticipating-potential-cfpb-enforcement-priorities-as-agency-marks-10-year-anniversary.html)). It was created by the Dodd-Frank Act of 2010 after the 2007–2008 crisis.

**How is the CFPB funded?** Through transfers from the Federal Reserve, outside the annual congressional appropriations process, with the director requesting the amount "reasonably necessary" up to an inflation-adjusted cap — $734 million in 2022 ([Sullivan & Cromwell](https://www.sullcrom.com/SullivanCromwell/_Assets/PDFs/Memos/Supreme-Court-Holds-CFPB-Funding-Constitutional.pdf)). The Supreme Court upheld that structure 7–2 in 2024.

**Is the CFPB still operating?** It is operating under court orders, but sharply reduced: it lost over a third of its staff since the start of 2025, closed 40% of open investigations, and brought a single enforcement action since February 2025, against 28 in 2024 ([National Association of Consumer Advocates](https://www.consumeradvocates.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NACA_Voughthrg_HFSC072026.pdf)). Three federal judges have ordered it to keep requesting funding.

**Who runs the CFPB now?** Mark Paoletta has been acting director since 1 August 2026, a change driven by the Federal Vacancies Reform Act clock running out on Russell Vought's service; Vought remains director of the Office of Management and Budget ([AskWhy.News](https://askwhy.news/us/money-influence/cfpb-defunding-ruled-unlawful/)). On 17 September 2026 the Senate Banking Committee advanced Brian Johnson's nomination as permanent director by 13–11, with no floor vote yet scheduled.

**What did the CFPB do about Bilt?** According to the National Association of Consumer Advocates, its "action" involved no formal public action — only an unofficial promise to repay ([National Association of Consumer Advocates](https://www.consumeradvocates.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NACA_Voughthrg_HFSC072026.pdf)). Protect Borrowers wrote to the Bureau in March 2026 urging risk-based supervision of Bilt and Cardless, and public enforcement where appropriate.
