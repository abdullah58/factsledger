## Overview

Valley National Bancorp (NASDAQ: VLY) is a regional bank holding company headquartered in Morristown, New Jersey, whose principal subsidiary, Valley National Bank — doing business as Valley Bank — holds about $67.5 billion in assets and operates 228 branches across eight states ([SEC Form 10-Q](https://files.quartr.com/reports/e77a2ee711fad68e15b22b928adddeba-2026-11-05-21-12-08.pdf?ref=TWFya2V0QmVhdCBNZWRpYSBMTEM=)). Founded in 1927 and approaching its centenary in 2027, it agreed in September 2026 to buy the small-business fintech [BlueVine](/organizations/bluevine/) for $340 million ([SEC Form 8-K](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0000714310/77c30452-353f-45b6-8174-57ee5217c376.pdf)).

Five weeks earlier, Valley had also agreed to buy Providence Financial Corporation, a Chicago-area bank, for about $247 million ([SEC Form 10-Q](https://files.quartr.com/reports/e77a2ee711fad68e15b22b928adddeba-2026-11-05-21-12-08.pdf?ref=TWFya2V0QmVhdCBNZWRpYSBMTEM=)). Neither deal has closed: as of the bank's third-quarter 2026 filing, both are still pending, with completion expected in early 2027 ([SEC Form 10-Q](https://files.quartr.com/reports/e77a2ee711fad68e15b22b928adddeba-2026-11-05-21-12-08.pdf?ref=TWFya2V0QmVhdCBNZWRpYSBMTEM=)).

## A century of acquisition-led growth

Valley traces its roots to 1927, when it was founded in Passaic, New Jersey, as the Passaic Park Trust Company ([BankGrader](https://www.bankgrader.com/valley-bank-review)); its bank has been chartered as a national banking association since that year ([Valley Pillar 3 disclosure](https://s204.q4cdn.com/203646286/files/doc_downloads/regulatory_disclosures/2026/08/2Q26-Basel-III-Regulatory-Capital-Disclosures-Report.pdf)). The Valley National Bank name dates to 1976, adopted after the acquisition of the Bank of Wayne, in reference to the lower Passaic River valley shared by Wayne, Clifton and Passaic ([BankGrader](https://www.bankgrader.com/valley-bank-review)). Valley National Bancorp itself was organized as the holding company in 1983 and is registered as a bank holding company and financial holding company ([Valley Pillar 3 disclosure](https://s204.q4cdn.com/203646286/files/doc_downloads/regulatory_disclosures/2026/08/2Q26-Basel-III-Regulatory-Capital-Disclosures-Report.pdf)). Growth since then has come largely through acquisitions of smaller banks across New Jersey, New York and Florida, a pattern that continued into the 2020s.

The defining deal of the modern era was the acquisition of Bank Leumi USA, completed on April 1, 2022, for about $1.2 billion — roughly 85 million Valley shares plus $113 million in cash ([SEC Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/714310/000119312522092782/d330167d8k.htm)). Bank Leumi USA brought about $8.3 billion in assets, $5.8 billion in loans and $7.1 billion in low-cost deposits, and its parent, Bank Leumi le-Israel, became a major Valley shareholder with the right to designate directors to Valley's board ([SEC Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/714310/000119312522092782/d330167d8k.htm)). The merger also pulled Valley deeper into technology banking: it expanded the bank's work with technology companies, venture-capital funds and Israel-linked clients, and Valley has since acted as a limited partner in venture funds, including Israeli ones ([Calcalistech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjh5ocv5me)).

Leadership has been stable under that strategy. Ira Robbins, who joined Valley in 1996, became chief executive on January 1, 2018, succeeding Gerald H. Lipkin, who had spent 42 years at the bank including 29 as chairman, president and chief executive ([ROI-NJ](https://www.roi-nj.com/2017/11/03/finance/valley-national-bancorp-ceo-to-retire-successor-named/)). In October 2023 Valley moved its headquarters from its longtime base in Wayne to a new roughly 120,000-square-foot building on Speedwell Avenue in Morristown, housing about 600 employees ([NJBIZ](https://njbiz.com/valley-bank-brand-campaign-100th-anniversary-growth/)). The move is recent enough that many directories and data aggregators still list Wayne as the headquarters — they are out of date. Valley describes itself as the largest bank headquartered in New Jersey ([NJBIZ](https://njbiz.com/valley-bank-brand-campaign-100th-anniversary-growth/)), and in January 2026 it launched its first-ever brand campaign, "That's How," ahead of its 100th anniversary in 2027 ([NJBIZ](https://njbiz.com/valley-bank-brand-campaign-100th-anniversary-growth/)).

The bank serves clients across New Jersey, New York, Florida, Alabama, California, Illinois, Pennsylvania and Arizona, offering consumer, commercial and wealth-management services alongside insurance, asset-management, brokerage and title subsidiaries ([Valley Pillar 3 disclosure](https://s204.q4cdn.com/203646286/files/doc_downloads/regulatory_disclosures/2026/08/2Q26-Basel-III-Regulatory-Capital-Disclosures-Report.pdf)). Third-quarter 2026 net income was $206.3 million, or $0.36 per diluted share, up from $163.4 million a year earlier — a figure chief executive Ira Robbins described as record quarterly net income ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/globe-newswire/9850817/valley-national-bancorp-announces-third-quarter-2026-results)). Executives told NJBIZ that 2025 was a strong year — deposits up about $4 billion overall, commercial deposits up more than $2.5 billion and commercial loans up about $1.6 billion — and that the bank employs more than 3,800 people ([NJBIZ](https://njbiz.com/valley-bank-brand-campaign-100th-anniversary-growth/)).

## Two deals at once: Providence and BlueVine

Valley announced two acquisitions in the second half of 2026, both aimed at the same problem: a funding base that relied too heavily on higher-cost wholesale money. On August 25, 2026, it agreed to acquire Providence Financial Corporation, the parent of Providence Bank & Trust of South Holland, Illinois, for about $247 million in cash and stock — 4.3854 Valley shares plus $21.47 in cash per Providence share ([SEC Form 10-Q](https://files.quartr.com/reports/e77a2ee711fad68e15b22b928adddeba-2026-11-05-21-12-08.pdf?ref=TWFya2V0QmVhdCBNZWRpYSBMTEM=)). Providence had about $1.6 billion in assets, $1.3 billion in deposits, $1.1 billion in loans and $800 million in wealth assets under management across a 14-branch network, giving Valley a physical foothold in the Chicago area to supplement its existing commercial presence there ([SEC Form 10-Q](https://files.quartr.com/reports/e77a2ee711fad68e15b22b928adddeba-2026-11-05-21-12-08.pdf?ref=TWFya2V0QmVhdCBNZWRpYSBMTEM=)).

Five weeks later, on September 28, 2026, Valley announced the larger and more strategically important deal: the $340 million acquisition of BlueVine, a [Jersey City](/places/jersey-city/)-based digital banking platform for small businesses ([SEC Form 8-K](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0000714310/77c30452-353f-45b6-8174-57ee5217c376.pdf)). The merger agreement had been signed the day before, and the consideration is about $255 million in cash plus about 6.3 million Valley shares ([SEC Form 8-K](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0000714310/77c30452-353f-45b6-8174-57ee5217c376.pdf)). BlueVine brings roughly 175,000 active small-business customers and about $2.1 billion in digitally sourced deposits that had grown at roughly 35% a year since 2023, with about 99% of those deposits coming from customers who do not borrow ([SEC Form 8-K](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0000714310/77c30452-353f-45b6-8174-57ee5217c376.pdf)). The deposits are cheap — a 1.44% cost against Valley's 2.28% average — which is the core of the deal's economics ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/valley-national-bank-acquire-fintech-bluevine-340m-small-business-banking/831503/)). Valley expects the acquisition to add about 8% to projected 2028 earnings per share, at the cost of about 5% tangible book value dilution at closing and a roughly three-year earn-back ([SEC Form 8-K](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0000714310/77c30452-353f-45b6-8174-57ee5217c376.pdf)).

The BlueVine deal is also a technology purchase. About 180 of BlueVine's research-and-development professionals and engineers will join Valley, and the bank says owning the platform will reduce its reliance on third-party software providers ([SEC Form 8-K](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0000714310/77c30452-353f-45b6-8174-57ee5217c376.pdf)). BlueVine's co-founder and chief executive, [Eyal Lifshitz](/people/eyal-lifshitz/), will join Valley as head of Small Business Banking when the transaction closes ([SEC Form 8-K](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0000714310/77c30452-353f-45b6-8174-57ee5217c376.pdf)). Robbins has said the deal would grow Valley's small-business customer base about twentyfold and give it a national digital distribution channel, with about 40% of BlueVine's customers already inside Valley's branch footprint ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/valley-national-bank-acquire-fintech-bluevine-340m-small-business-banking/831503/)). On the same call, he said Valley does not anticipate pursuing additional acquisitions "for the foreseeable future" after Providence and BlueVine ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/valley-national-bank-acquire-fintech-bluevine-340m-small-business-banking/831503/)).

The mechanics after closing matter as much as the price. BlueVine's deposits currently sit at partner bank Coastal Community Bank; they are expected to transfer to Valley in the first half of 2027, and Valley expects the digitally sourced deposit relationships to exceed $2.3 billion once the current banking-partner relationship ends and the transition to Valley begins in April 2027 ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/valley-national-bank-acquire-fintech-bluevine-340m-small-business-banking/831503/); [SEC Form 10-Q](https://files.quartr.com/reports/e77a2ee711fad68e15b22b928adddeba-2026-11-05-21-12-08.pdf?ref=TWFya2V0QmVhdCBNZWRpYSBMTEM=)). Until then the two companies operate separately, and the transaction remains subject to standard regulatory approvals and customary closing conditions ([SEC Form 8-K](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0000714310/77c30452-353f-45b6-8174-57ee5217c376.pdf)). Robbins has described both pending deals as "a natural extension of our strategy to further optimize our funding base" ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/globe-newswire/9850817/valley-national-bancorp-announces-third-quarter-2026-results)).

## The funding problem behind the deals

Both 2026 deals are answers to the same balance-sheet problem. Valley grew loans faster than cheap deposits for years, leaving it reliant on higher-cost wholesale funding; in the third quarter of 2026 alone, total loans rose $722.7 million to $53.2 billion, driven by $363.0 million of commercial and industrial lending and $343.5 million of commercial real estate lending, while deposits rose $1.1 billion to $55.2 billion ([SEC Form 10-Q](https://files.quartr.com/reports/e77a2ee711fad68e15b22b928adddeba-2026-11-05-21-12-08.pdf?ref=TWFya2V0QmVhdCBNZWRpYSBMTEM=)). Robbins has said the bank remains on track to reach — and likely exceed — a 15% return on average tangible common equity by the end of 2027, a goal that depends on cheaper funding ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/globe-newswire/9850817/valley-national-bancorp-announces-third-quarter-2026-results)). Providence, praised by Valley for its low-cost core funding base and top-tier profitability, is expected to be about 2% accretive to earnings and less than 1% dilutive to tangible book value, with an earn-back of under three years ([Valley press release](https://www.valley.com/about/newsroom/Valley-National-Bancorp-acquisition-of-Providence-Financial-Corporation)). Its president and chief executive, Steven G. Van Drunen, will join Valley as market president for the Chicago area after closing, and Valley has committed $3 million over three years to Chicago-area civic and nonprofit organizations ([Valley press release](https://www.valley.com/about/newsroom/Valley-National-Bancorp-acquisition-of-Providence-Financial-Corporation)).

The retail push behind the deals predates them: Valley hired Patrick Smith as president of consumer banking in September 2025, and the Providence acquisition was described as a continuation of the retail and small-business investments that began with that hire ([Valley press release](https://www.valley.com/about/newsroom/Valley-National-Bancorp-acquisition-of-Providence-Financial-Corporation)). Executives have also set a pointed ambition for the small-business push — Valley's commercial banking president said there is "no reason that Valley should not be the number one SBA [Small Business Administration] lender in New Jersey; and we're not" ([NJBIZ](https://njbiz.com/valley-bank-brand-campaign-100th-anniversary-growth/)). On a pro-forma basis, the combined company after Providence would hold about $67.9 billion in assets, $55.5 billion in deposits and $53.5 billion in loans ([Valley press release](https://www.valley.com/about/newsroom/Valley-National-Bancorp-acquisition-of-Providence-Financial-Corporation)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim that either the BlueVine or the Providence acquisition has closed — both were announced in 2026 and remain pending, with completion expected in early 2027. The ~8% earnings accretion and ~5% book-value dilution figures for BlueVine are management projections, not results, and the deposit figures for BlueVine are company-reported, not independently audited. The bank's pre-1983 name history is corroborated only by tier3 sources and is flagged as such. The profile also does not resolve the address question in Valley's own filings: SEC cover pages list One Penn Plaza, New York, as the principal executive office address, while the bank describes Morristown, New Jersey, as its headquarters. Finally, it does not state which specific regulatory clearances the BlueVine deal requires, since that was not verified from a fully-read tier1 or tier2 source.
