## Overview

Founders Fund is a San Francisco-based venture capital firm founded in 2005 by [Peter Thiel](/people/peter-thiel/), Ken Howery and Luke Nosek, three former [PayPal](/organizations/paypal/) colleagues ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/brian-singerman-of-founders-fund-a-power-player/46785/1)). Based in the [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) Presidio, it opened with a $50 million first fund and became known for concentrated, contrarian bets on hard technology — an early investor in [Facebook](/organizations/facebook/), [SpaceX](/organizations/spacex/) and [Palantir](/organizations/palantir/) — and, two decades on, for multi-billion-dollar growth funds ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/brian-singerman-of-founders-fund-a-power-player/46785/1); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/09/founders-fund-nears-6-billion-close-for-latest-growth-fund-sources-say/)).

The firm's identity is inseparable from Thiel, its co-founder and public face, and from the PayPal network around him: the partners backed friends' companies, including [Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk/)'s SpaceX and Max Levchin's [Affirm](/organizations/affirm/) ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/brian-singerman-of-founders-fund-a-power-player/46785/1)). Its record rests on a small number of very large bets rather than a broad spread: for every dollar in its first fund, investors took home seven, and at the time of the [Forbes](/organizations/forbes/) profile the firm was on track to generate more than $9.5 billion in returns ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/brian-singerman-of-founders-fund-a-power-player/46785/1)).

## Founding and the PayPal roots

Thiel, Howery and Nosek met while building PayPal, which [eBay](/organizations/ebay/) acquired in 2002, and they were unimpressed by the venture capitalists they had dealt with — one had helped start a competitor, another had tried to replace the founders with older managers ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/brian-singerman-of-founders-fund-a-power-player/46785/1)). The trio was already investing in friends' startups from a small personal fund, and they spent a year asking, "How do we make a venture capital firm that we actually like?" ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/brian-singerman-of-founders-fund-a-power-player/46785/1)). The answer, named by Nosek, was a firm that would live and die by a startup's founders: it would not veto a financing round, push a company to sell, demand a board seat, or force founders out when a startup faltered ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/brian-singerman-of-founders-fund-a-power-player/46785/1)). They raised the $50 million first fund in 2005, establishing their office in the Presidio, near the Golden Gate Bridge and some 35 miles from the Sand Hill Road corridor in Menlo Park ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/brian-singerman-of-founders-fund-a-power-player/46785/1)).

## Investment philosophy: contrarian and founder-first

The firm's philosophy is Thiel's contrarianism applied to investing: back the company almost nobody else will, and then keep backing it. Thiel's interview question, set out in *Zero to One* (2014, with Blake Masters, based on his 2012 Stanford class), is "What important truth do very few people agree with you on?" ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsum0uej6rfkowfcywao/portfolio/peter-thiel-and-silicon-valleys-obsession-with-superficial-thinking)). His own answer was that most people think the future will be defined by globalization, but technology matters more — and the best business ideas, he argued, rely on a secret about physical or human nature that the rest of the world is unaware of ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsum0uej6rfkowfcywao/portfolio/peter-thiel-and-silicon-valleys-obsession-with-superficial-thinking)). Startups, in this view, should build creative monopolies and escape competition altogether, asking "What valuable company is nobody building?" ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsum0uej6rfkowfcywao/portfolio/peter-thiel-and-silicon-valleys-obsession-with-superficial-thinking)). The 2011 manifesto put the same idea in a single line: "We wanted flying cars, instead we got 140 characters" — a rebuke to peers building social apps while ambitious technology went unfunded ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/brian-singerman-of-founders-fund-a-power-player/46785/1)).

## The portfolio: Facebook, SpaceX, Palantir and beyond

The early record was built on three bets. Thiel, dipping into the firm's initial $50 million fund, became the first investor in [Mark Zuckerberg](/people/mark-zuckerberg/)'s Facebook, and the firm eventually turned $8 million into $376 million; Sean Parker, Facebook's former president, joined Founders Fund as a general partner shortly afterwards ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/brian-singerman-of-founders-fund-a-power-player/46785/1)). In 2008 the firm put $20 million into SpaceX when every rocket had blown up — one potential investor walked away, and another accidentally copied the partners on an email saying they had lost their minds ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/brian-singerman-of-founders-fund-a-power-player/46785/1)). It was also the first institutional investor in Palantir, the data-analytics company Thiel had helped start ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/09/founders-fund-nears-6-billion-close-for-latest-growth-fund-sources-say/)). Later wins spread across sectors: the AI startup DeepMind, acquired by Google for more than $500 million in 2014; The Climate Corporation, acquired by Monsanto for $1 billion in 2013; and [Stripe](/organizations/stripe/), where a $100 million investment had roughly quadrupled in value by the time of the Forbes profile ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/brian-singerman-of-founders-fund-a-power-player/46785/1)).

Growth III, closed in April 2025, was a step up from the $3.4 billion growth fund of early 2022; 270 limited partners piled in, and a substantial amount of the capital came from the firm's own general partners ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/15/founders-funds-new-4-6b-fund-indicates-an-era-of-big-vc-spending/); [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/thiel-founders-fund-late-stage-growth-fund/)). The turn to growth capital followed a humbler moment: in 2023 Thiel cut the eighth early-stage fund from $1.8 billion to $900 million, judging that venture capital was due for a "slimdown", and reallocated the rest into a separate early-stage fund ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/09/founders-fund-nears-6-billion-close-for-latest-growth-fund-sources-say/)). By March 2026 the firm was nearing the close of Growth IV at $6 billion, with about $1.5 billion from its own partners and demand exceeding capacity, and it has raised no new early-stage fund since early 2022 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/09/founders-fund-nears-6-billion-close-for-latest-growth-fund-sources-say/)).

## Blend: the fintech bet

Founders Fund's link to [Blend](/organizations/blend/) runs through both Thiel personally and the firm itself. Blend raised $2.5 million in seed funding in 2012 from Andreessen Horowitz and angel investor Peter Thiel, according to Business Insider as reported by Global Venturing ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/08/13/blend-cements-75m-series-f/)). The firm then led Blend's $40 million Series C in 2016, backed by existing investors Lightspeed and [Formation 8](/organizations/formation-8/) ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/08/13/blend-cements-75m-series-f/)). Three years later Founders Fund was still on the cap table: in June 2019 [Temasek](/organizations/temasek/) and General Atlantic led Blend's $130 million Series E, with Founders Fund participating as an existing backer alongside [8VC](/organizations/8vc/), [Greylock Partners](/organizations/greylock/) and [Lightspeed Venture Partners](/organizations/lightspeed-venture-partners/), bringing Blend's total funding since its 2012 inception to $310 million ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/mortgage-tech-startup-blend-raises-130m-reportedly-nears-unicorn-status/)). At that point Blend's software platform "routinely processes nearly $2 billion in loans every day" in partnership with more than 150 lender customers, including Wells Fargo and U.S. Bank ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/mortgage-tech-startup-blend-raises-130m-reportedly-nears-unicorn-status/)).

## Defense, AI and the Anthropic turn

By the mid-2020s the firm's centre of gravity had moved toward defense technology and artificial intelligence. TechCrunch described it as holding perhaps the most formidable defense-tech portfolio in Silicon Valley, including [Anduril](/organizations/anduril/), SpaceX, Flock Safety and Biofire ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/15/founders-funds-new-4-6b-fund-indicates-an-era-of-big-vc-spending/)). The Anduril relationship is the deepest: partner Trae Stephens co-founded the company, the firm backed it from its first seed round, co-led its $1.5 billion Series F with Sands Capital at a $14 billion valuation, and in June 2025 led its $2.5 billion Series G with a $1 billion investment — the largest cheque the firm has ever written — at a $30.5 billion valuation ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/thiel-founders-fund-late-stage-growth-fund/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/05/anduril-raises-2-5b-at-30-5b-valuation-led-by-founders-fund/)). In February 2026 the firm made its first direct investment in [Anthropic](/organizations/anthropic/), co-leading a $30 billion investment at a $380 billion post-money valuation with D. E. Shaw Ventures, Dragoneer, ICONIQ and MGX ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/09/founders-fund-nears-6-billion-close-for-latest-growth-fund-sources-say/)). Because it is also an investor in [OpenAI](/organizations/openai/), that deal made it one of the few firms with significant stakes in both leading AI labs.

## Controversy: Silicon Valley Bank

The firm's most public controversy came in March 2023, when it advised its portfolio companies to withdraw their money from Silicon Valley Bank. Founders Fund asked its companies to move their money out of SVB, according to a person familiar with the matter, and [Coatue Management](/organizations/coatue/), Union Square Ventures and Founder Collective gave similar advice ([Bloomberg](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/markets/silicon-valley-bank-races-to-prevent-bank-run-as-funds-advise-pulling-out-cash/article66605054.ece)). The turmoil followed SVB's announcement that it was issuing $2.25 billion of shares to bolster its capital after a significant loss on its investment portfolio; its stock had fallen 60% the day before and plunged 63% in premarket trading ([Bloomberg](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/markets/silicon-valley-bank-races-to-prevent-bank-run-as-funds-advise-pulling-out-cash/article66605054.ece)). Founders Fund declined to comment at the time. The sources read here do not establish whether the advice caused the run that followed or merely responded to one already under way.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Founders Fund?** Founders Fund is a San Francisco-based venture capital firm founded in 2005 by Peter Thiel, Ken Howery and Luke Nosek, former PayPal colleagues ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/brian-singerman-of-founders-fund-a-power-player/46785/1)). It invests in hard technology, defense, artificial intelligence, fintech and biotech, and is known for concentrated, founder-first bets rather than a broad spread of small cheques.

**What companies did Founders Fund back early?** It was an early investor in Facebook, where it turned $8 million into $376 million, and it invested $20 million in SpaceX in 2008 and was the first institutional investor in Palantir ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/brian-singerman-of-founders-fund-a-power-player/46785/1); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/09/founders-fund-nears-6-billion-close-for-latest-growth-fund-sources-say/)). Its later portfolio includes Stripe, Airbnb, Spotify, DeepMind, Anduril and Anthropic, with Blend among its fintech bets.

**How big are Founders Fund's funds?** Its first fund was $50 million in 2005, and Growth III closed at $4.6 billion in April 2025 with 270 limited partners ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/15/founders-funds-new-4-6b-fund-indicates-an-era-of-big-vc-spending/)). In March 2026 it was nearing the close of Growth IV at $6 billion, with about $1.5 billion from its own partners ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/09/founders-fund-nears-6-billion-close-for-latest-growth-fund-sources-say/)).

**What is Founders Fund's investment philosophy?** It follows Thiel's contrarian question, "What important truth do very few people agree with you on?", and seeks companies built on a secret the rest of the world does not yet believe ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsum0uej6rfkowfcywao/portfolio/peter-thiel-and-silicon-valleys-obsession-with-superficial-thinking)). Its manifesto line, "We wanted flying cars, instead we got 140 characters", sums up its preference for ambitious technology over incremental apps.

**What was Founders Fund's role in the Silicon Valley Bank collapse?** In March 2023 it advised its portfolio companies to move their money out of SVB after the bank announced a $2.25 billion share issue following investment losses ([Bloomberg](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/markets/silicon-valley-bank-races-to-prevent-bank-run-as-funds-advise-pulling-out-cash/article66605054.ece)). Other firms gave similar advice, and the sources read here do not establish whether the advice caused the run or responded to one already under way.
