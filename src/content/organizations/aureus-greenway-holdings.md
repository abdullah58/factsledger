## Overview

Aureus Greenway Holdings Inc. is a golf-course company that trades like a defence contractor. Since May 15, 2026, its shares have carried the Nasdaq ticker "PUSA" — the intended ticker of the combined company — even though the reverse merger that would make it a defence company has not closed. In its own press releases, the company still describes itself the same way: an owner and operator of golf course properties in Florida ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/14/3294887/0/en/Aureus-Greenway-Holdings-to-Change-Ticker-Symbol-to-PUSA-Ahead-of-Expected-Combination-With-Powerus.html)). The ticker changed before the business did.

That gap between the ticker and the company underneath it is the defining fact about Aureus Greenway. In March 2026, Reuters reported that the company — described in the report as a golf club company backed by the sons of U.S. President Donald Trump — would merge with [Powerus](/organizations/powerus/), a drone technology company, in a deal designed to take Powerus public ([Reuters](https://wabx.net/2026/03/09/trump-brothers-backed-aureus-to-merge-with-drone-maker-powerus/)). From that moment, the market began pricing a golf operator as a proxy for a Pentagon-linked drone roll-up. Two months later the company made the proxy official, adopting the PUSA ticker in anticipation of a combination that was then expected to close in summer 2026. The close slipped. As of mid-September 2026, the merger was expected in the fourth quarter of 2026, subject to customary closing conditions — and the company warned plainly that there is no assurance the transaction will be completed or as to its timing ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/18/3364693/0/en/powerus-receives-pakistan-ministry-of-defence-order-and-signs-strategic-memorandum-of-understanding.html)).

So the strangest document in this story is the September 18, 2026 press release, issued jointly by Aureus Greenway and Powerus. It announced a limited procurement order from the Ministry of Defence of Pakistan for unmanned aerial systems and associated support, plus a strategic memorandum of understanding on expanded cooperation in unmanned and autonomous defence technologies ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/18/3364693/0/en/powerus-receives-pakistan-ministry-of-defence-order-and-signs-strategic-memorandum-of-understanding.html)). A golf-course company co-announced a foreign military drone order before it owned the drone company. The release quotes Matthew Saker, identified as Interim Chief Executive Officer of AGH, saying the order and the memorandum "reflect the kind of international defense traction that we believe supports the strategic rationale for the pending combination."

## The golf business underneath the ticker

Before any of this, Aureus Greenway was what its boilerplate still says it is. The company owns and operates golf course properties in Florida, including Kissimmee Bay Country Club and Remington Golf Club in the greater Orlando region ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/14/3294887/0/en/Aureus-Greenway-Holdings-to-Change-Ticker-Symbol-to-PUSA-Ahead-of-Expected-Combination-With-Powerus.html)). That is the entire disclosed business: golf courses, in one Florida market, run under an interim CEO whose background the company has not made public.

Nothing in the company's releases explains how a small Florida golf operator became the Nasdaq-listed shell of choice for a Trump-family-linked defence roll-up. The "Interim" in Saker's title is worth noticing too — the company is being steered through the largest transaction in its history by a caretaker chief executive, with the permanent leadership of the combined company expected to come from the other side: Reuters reported that Powerus founder Andrew Fox was expected to serve as chief executive officer and chairman of the combined company, per a regulatory filing ([Reuters](https://wabx.net/2026/03/09/trump-brothers-backed-aureus-to-merge-with-drone-maker-powerus/)).

## The March 2026 deal

The reverse merger was announced in March 2026 as a way to take Powerus public without a traditional IPO. Powerus — formally Autonomous Power Corporation, doing business as Powerus — was formed in 2025 by Andrew Fox. Reuters described its products as heavy-lift drones able to carry industrial payloads of up to 675 kilograms, plus services that convert existing manned boats into remotely operated or fully autonomous vessels. The value of the deal was not disclosed ([Reuters](https://wabx.net/2026/03/09/trump-brothers-backed-aureus-to-merge-with-drone-maker-powerus/)).

The financing around the announcement reveals the orbit the deal lives in. Aureus engaged Dominari Securities to help raise about $9 million from investors including the drone-components company [Unusual Machines](/organizations/unusual-machines/) and the Agostinelli Group. Dominari counts both Trump brothers among its shareholders, with roughly 6% stakes each; Unusual Machines had tapped Donald Trump Jr. as an adviser in November 2024. Separately, the Korea Climate & Governance Improvement Fund agreed to purchase $50 million of Powerus stock. At the time, the merger was expected to close in summer 2026 ([Reuters](https://wabx.net/2026/03/09/trump-brothers-backed-aureus-to-merge-with-drone-maker-powerus/)).

Reuters placed the deal in a wider pattern: it followed a $1.5 billion tie-up between the Israeli drone maker [XTEND](/organizations/xtend/) and Florida-based JFB Construction, and it arrived as drones became a major Pentagon procurement priority — widely used in Ukraine, where dense air defences near the front lines limit conventional aircraft, and increasingly backed by Silicon Valley money flowing into military AI startups like [Anduril Industries](/organizations/anduril) and Shield AI ([Reuters](https://wabx.net/2026/03/09/trump-brothers-backed-aureus-to-merge-with-drone-maker-powerus/)). Aureus Greenway's golf courses were being repurposed as the listed chassis for one small piece of that wave.

## The ticker changed before the company did

On May 14, 2026, the company announced it would change its Nasdaq ticker symbol from AGH to PUSA, effective the next day. The stated reason was the company's focus on "the Powerus platform and brand": upon completion of the merger, the combined company would operate as Powerus Corporation and continue trading on Nasdaq under PUSA. Shareholders holding AGH shares needed to take no action; they would hold shares of Powerus Corporation upon closing ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/05/14/3294887/0/en/Aureus-Greenway-Holdings-to-Change-Ticker-Symbol-to-PUSA-Ahead-of-Expected-Combination-With-Powerus.html); [Taiwan News](https://taiwannews.com.tw/en/news/6362623)).

The release was careful on one point: the ticker change did not affect the terms or timing of the pending merger, which remained subject to customary closing conditions including the effectiveness of the S-4 registration statement and required regulatory approvals. That caution proved warranted. Summer 2026 passed without a close. The Form S-4 was declared effective on August 12, 2026 — a genuine milestone toward completion — but by mid-September the expected close had moved to the fourth quarter of 2026, with the standard warning that the transaction might never be consummated ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/18/3364693/0/en/powerus-receives-pakistan-ministry-of-defence-order-and-signs-strategic-memorandum-of-understanding.html)).

## The Pakistan order

The September 18 joint announcement is the most recent verified development, and the strangest. Two days earlier, on September 16, 2026, a Powerus delegation led by co-founder Brett Velicovich was received by Field Marshal Syed [Asim Munir](/people/asim-munir), Pakistan's Chief of Army Staff and Chief of Defence Forces, at General Headquarters in Rawalpindi. Pakistan's military media wing, Inter-Services Public Relations, said the meeting covered mutual interests, evolving defence-technology trends, and cooperation in defence procurement, production, and capacity building ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/18/3364693/0/en/powerus-receives-pakistan-ministry-of-defence-order-and-signs-strategic-memorandum-of-understanding.html)).

Out of that meeting came two things: a limited procurement order from Pakistan's Ministry of Defence for unmanned aerial systems and associated support, and a separate strategic memorandum of understanding establishing a framework for potential expanded cooperation in unmanned and autonomous defence technologies. The release is explicit about the limits: no other terms of the order were disclosed; the memorandum is not a definitive agreement, creates no purchase obligation, and obligates neither party to proceed; anything contemplated under it would be subject to definitive agreements, U.S. laws and regulations, and government approvals. An order is not a relationship, and a memorandum is not a contract.

The release also discloses the merger's structure: Powerus will merge with and into a newly formed subsidiary of AGH, with Powerus continuing as the surviving entity and AGH adopting the name "Powerus Corporation." And it carries an unusual piece of honesty — each company says it has provided the information about its own business, that neither has independently verified the other's information, and that each disclaims any representation or warranty about the other party's claims. Aureus Greenway, in other words, is asking the market to trust Powerus's story about itself, while stating on the record that it has not checked that story.
