## Overview

Focus on the Family is an American evangelical nonprofit founded in 1977 by child psychologist [James Dobson](/people/james-dobson/) and now based in Colorado Springs, [Colorado](/places/colorado/), where it runs a daily radio broadcast, publishes books and magazines, produces the children's drama *Adventures in Odyssey*, and advocates conservative positions on marriage, parenting, abortion and religious liberty ([MinistryWatch](https://ministrywatch.com/focus-on-the-family-board-launches-national-search-for-successor/)). It serves more than 2 million families a year and reported more than $138 million in revenue in its 2025 annual report ([MinistryWatch](https://ministrywatch.com/focus-on-the-family-board-launches-national-search-for-successor/)). Jim Daly has been its president since 2005, and in 2026 its board launched a national search for his successor ahead of the ministry's 50th anniversary in 2027 ([MinistryWatch](https://ministrywatch.com/focus-on-the-family-board-launches-national-search-for-successor/)).

Dobson founded the organization to promote conservative views on parenting, defending spanking as a means of discipline, and it became hugely influential first among evangelical Christians and then among a broader public through internationally syndicated radio programs ([Religion News Service](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/james-dobson-focus-on-the-family-founder-and-family-values-advocate-dies-at-89/)). The ministry's own account of its reach, carried by Religion News Service, puts its broadcast on more than 4,000 North American radio stations, translated into 27 languages in more than 160 countries, while Christianity Today estimates its multimedia enterprise reached about 220 million people in more than 150 countries ([Religion News Service](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/james-dobson-focus-on-the-family-founder-and-family-values-advocate-dies-at-89/); [Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/08/died-james-dobson/)). Those figures describe different eras and are not a single current audience claim: today's daily show reaches more than 5 million listeners weekly, according to The Gazette as reported by MinistryWatch ([MinistryWatch](https://ministrywatch.com/focus-on-the-family-board-launches-national-search-for-successor/)).

## The Dobson era: from a two-room office to Colorado Springs

Dobson left academia in 1976 and, the next year, opened a small two-room office in Arcadia, California, that he called Focus on the Family ([Religion News Service](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/james-dobson-focus-on-the-family-founder-and-family-values-advocate-dies-at-89/)). The radio program began in 1977 as a 15-minute weekly show and became a daily half-hour broadcast in 1981, reaching 800 Christian radio stations every day by 1982 ([Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/08/died-james-dobson/)). The organization moved its headquarters to Colorado Springs in 1991 ([NPR](https://www.publicradioeast.org/2025-08-22/focus-on-the-family-founder-james-dobson-has-died-at-age-89)), where it built an international organization with, at peak, more than 1,300 employees according to Religion News Service (the Associated Press puts the peak above 1,000), a welcome center costing $8.5 million, and about 200,000 visitors a year ([Religion News Service](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/james-dobson-focus-on-the-family-founder-and-family-values-advocate-dies-at-89/); [Associated Press](https://www.wvlt.tv/2025/08/21/james-dobson-founder-focus-family-has-died/)).

Perhaps the most durable product of the Dobson era was not a policy campaign but a children's show. *Adventures in Odyssey*, a radio drama narrating the adventures of youngsters who gather at a small-town soda shop and ice cream emporium and learn life lessons from the inventor John Avery Whittaker, became one of the most-listened-to programs on Christian radio and a cultural touchpoint for evangelicals of a certain age ([Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/08/died-james-dobson/)). Dobson also used the ministry's platform for political organization beyond its own walls: in 1994 he co-founded the Alliance Defense Fund, now [Alliance Defending Freedom](/organizations/alliance-defending-freedom/), with Bill Bright, D. James Kennedy and other conservative Christian leaders ([Religion News Service](https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/james-dobson-focus-on-the-family-founder-and-family-values-advocate-dies-at-89/)).

## Under Daly: counseling, content, and a succession search

Dobson turned leadership of Focus on the Family over to Jim Daly in 2005, and Daly, who had worked at Focus since 1989, has led it for more than two decades ([Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/08/died-james-dobson/); [MinistryWatch](https://ministrywatch.com/focus-on-the-family-board-launches-national-search-for-successor/)). Dobson left the organization in 2010 and launched the Dr. James Dobson Family Institute ([Associated Press](https://www.wvlt.tv/2025/08/21/james-dobson-founder-focus-family-has-died/)). Under Daly the ministry produces radio broadcasts and print and online content and offers counseling services and conferences on marriage, parenting, foster care and adoption, special needs, and spiritual development ([MinistryWatch](https://ministrywatch.com/focus-on-the-family-board-launches-national-search-for-successor/)). Its 2025 annual report, as carried by MinistryWatch, shows more than $138 million in revenue against $142 million in expenses, with $81 million in assets and roughly 880 people working at its Colorado Springs headquarters ([MinistryWatch](https://ministrywatch.com/focus-on-the-family-board-launches-national-search-for-successor/)).

In July 2026 the board launched a national search for Daly's successor, working with executive search firm FaithSearch Partners on a 12- to 18-month timeline that would coincide with the ministry's 50th anniversary in 2027 ([MinistryWatch](https://ministrywatch.com/focus-on-the-family-board-launches-national-search-for-successor/)). Daly, then approaching his 65th birthday, framed the transition as a deliberate handover while the ministry is healthy rather than a response to crisis, and he planned to keep hosting the daily radio show until a replacement is ready ([MinistryWatch](https://ministrywatch.com/focus-on-the-family-board-launches-national-search-for-successor/)). The board, not Daly, will vote on the final candidates, and the profile names no successor because the search was still open at the time of reporting.

## Advocacy, opposition, and the 2025 hate-group designation

Focus on the Family's public-policy work has always been the most contested part of its identity. In Colorado in the early 1990s, Dobson backed Amendment 2, which tried to prevent state or local governments from passing laws protecting people from discrimination based on sexual orientation; voters passed it, but the U.S. Supreme Court eventually struck it down ([NPR](https://www.publicradioeast.org/2025-08-22/focus-on-the-family-founder-james-dobson-has-died-at-age-89)). The organization has said that homosexuality is a "particularly evil lie of Satan," a position NPR quoted in its obituary coverage of Dobson ([NPR](https://www.publicradioeast.org/2025-08-22/focus-on-the-family-founder-james-dobson-has-died-at-age-89)). The Associated Press notes that Focus's platform was used to weigh in on legislation and to advocate against laws banning conversion therapy ([Associated Press](https://www.wvlt.tv/2025/08/21/james-dobson-founder-focus-family-has-died/)).

In May 2025 the [Southern Poverty Law Center](/organizations/southern-poverty-law-center/) added Focus on the Family to its list of "anti-LGBTQ+ hate groups," posting an entry in its "Extremist Files" section that says the organization "has long relied on its biblical worldview strategy to push back against LGBTQ+ progress and reproductive rights" and criticizes its online publication The Daily Citizen ([The Christian Post](https://www.christianpost.com/news/focus-on-the-family-added-to-splcs-anti-lgbt-hate-group-list.html)). Focus spokesman Glenn T. Stanton, director of Global Family Formation Studies, welcomed the listing, telling the Christian Post, "To be honest, our reaction was 'What took them so long?'" and calling the SPLC's list "a silly fundraising tool" ([The Christian Post](https://www.christianpost.com/news/focus-on-the-family-added-to-splcs-anti-lgbt-hate-group-list.html)). The designation is the SPLC's characterization, reported here as such, and it joins earlier SPLC designations of Alliance Defending Freedom and the Family Research Council noted in the same report ([The Christian Post](https://www.christianpost.com/news/focus-on-the-family-added-to-splcs-anti-lgbt-hate-group-list.html)). Dobson died three months later, on August 21, 2025, aged 89 ([Associated Press](https://www.wvlt.tv/2025/08/21/james-dobson-founder-focus-family-has-died/)).
