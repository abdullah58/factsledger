## Overview

HP Inc. is the American technology company that makes personal computers and printers, headquartered in Palo Alto, California and traded on the New York Stock Exchange as HPQ ([HP Inc.](https://investor.hp.com/news-events/news/news-details/2025/HP-Inc--Reports-Fiscal-2025-Full-Year-and-Fourth-Quarter-Results/default.aspx)). It is the legal successor to Hewlett-Packard Company: on 1 November 2015 the old company was renamed HP Inc. and its enterprise business was spun off as Hewlett Packard Enterprise ([Hewlett-Packard Company](https://investor.hp.com/news-events/news/news-details/2015/HP-Board-of-Directors-Approves-Separation/default.aspx)). Its roots go back to a Palo Alto garage in 1939, when Bill Hewlett and Dave Packard formalised their partnership ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)).

Today HP Inc. is one of the world's largest PC makers, second only to Lenovo in quarterly shipments ([Canalys](https://www.canalys.com/newsroom/worldwide-pc-shipments-up-7-in-q2-2025)). In fiscal 2025 it reported net revenue of $55.3 billion, up 3.2% on the prior year, and operates in more than 170 countries ([HP Inc.](https://investor.hp.com/news-events/news/news-details/2025/HP-Inc--Reports-Fiscal-2025-Full-Year-and-Fourth-Quarter-Results/default.aspx)). Its two businesses are Personal Systems (desktops, notebooks and workstations) and Printing (printers, supplies and services), and its chief executive is Enrique Lores ([HP Inc.](https://investor.hp.com/news-events/news/news-details/2025/HP-Inc--Reports-Fiscal-2025-Full-Year-and-Fourth-Quarter-Results/default.aspx)).

## From a Palo Alto garage to a global giant

Hewlett-Packard Company was founded on 1 January 1939 by William R. Hewlett and David Packard, two recent electrical-engineering graduates of [Stanford University](/universities/stanford-university/) ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)). Their Stanford professor and mentor Frederick Terman had encouraged them to start a business rather than leave California, and in 1938 they began part-time work in the garage behind the house at 367 Addison Avenue, Palo Alto, with $538 in working capital — cash plus a used Sears-Roebuck drill press ([HP Inc.](https://www.hp.com/hpinfo/abouthp/histnfacts/timeline/hist_30s.html)). Their first product was the Model 200A resistance-capacitance audio oscillator, an instrument for testing sound equipment, named "200A" to make the young company look established ([HP Inc.](https://www.hp.com/hpinfo/abouthp/histnfacts/timeline/hist_30s.html)). The company formalised the partnership on 1 January 1939 and settled its name with a coin toss ([HP Inc.](https://www.hp.com/hpinfo/abouthp/histnfacts/timeline/hist_30s.html)). Its first customer was Walt Disney Productions, which bought eight Model 200B oscillators to use in making the animated film *Fantasia* (1940) ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)). First-year revenue was $5,369, with two employees ([HP Inc.](https://www.hp.com/hpinfo/abouthp/histnfacts/timeline/hist_30s.html)).

War work followed: during the Second World War the company developed military products, Packard received a draft exemption and Hewlett served in the Army Signal Corps, while the company worked with the Naval Research Laboratory on counter-radar technology and artillery shell fuses ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)). After the war Packard ran the business and Hewlett led research and development, and by 1947 revenue was back to wartime levels ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)). The company went public in 1957 and grew by acquisition, buying F.L. Moseley in 1958 and Sanborn Company in 1961 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)). Its 1972 HP-35 was the first pocket scientific calculator, and its first computer, the HP 2116A, dated from 1966 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)). In 1976 an engineering intern, Stephen G. Wozniak, built a prototype personal computer and offered it to HP; HP declined and gave him the rights, and he later joined Steven P. Jobs to create [Apple](/organizations/apple/) ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)). HP's own early PCs failed — the HP-85 in 1980 and the touch-screen HP-150 — but its first successful PC-market product was a printer: the HP LaserJet of 1984 became the company's single most successful product ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)).

The company kept expanding: it bought Apollo Computer in 1989 to become the number-one workstation maker, joined the Dow Jones Industrial Average in 1997, and in 1999 spun off its measurement, electronic components and medical businesses as Agilent Technologies ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)). It collaborated with [Intel](/organizations/intel/) on the 64-bit Itanium microprocessor, introduced in 2001 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)). In 2002 it acquired Compaq Computer Corporation at the urging of chief executive Carly Fiorina, the first woman to lead a company listed in the Dow Jones — a move bitterly opposed by some directors and major stockholders, including Walter Hewlett, son of the co-founder ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)). Fiorina was forced out in 2005, but by 2007 Hewlett-Packard had become the first technology company to exceed $100 billion in annual sales revenue ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)). The founders' management style — "management by objective" and "management by walking around", with first-name informality — became known as the HP Way, and the company donated as much as 4.4% of its pre-tax profits to charity ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)).

## The 2015 split: how Hewlett-Packard became HP Inc.

The 2010s brought costly missteps. HP acquired Palm in 2010, then discontinued its smartphones and the TouchPad tablet in August 2011 and considered spinning off the PC business; that same year it bought the British software company Autonomy Corporation for $11.1 billion ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)). In November 2012 it wrote down Autonomy's value by $8.8 billion, accusing its management of inflating the company's value through accounting improprieties ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)). Meg Whitman, who had been chief executive of [eBay](/organizations/ebay/), replaced Leo Apotheker as CEO in September 2011 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)).

On 1 October 2015 the board approved the separation of Hewlett-Packard into two independent companies, to be completed on 1 November 2015 ([Hewlett-Packard Company](https://investor.hp.com/news-events/news/news-details/2015/HP-Board-of-Directors-Approves-Separation/default.aspx)). The split was structured as a rename, not a sale: Hewlett-Packard Company changed its name to HP Inc. and distributed 100% of the shares of the newly created Hewlett Packard Enterprise to its stockholders, one HPE share for each HP share held on 21 October 2015 ([Hewlett-Packard Company](https://investor.hp.com/news-events/news/news-details/2015/HP-Board-of-Directors-Approves-Separation/default.aspx)). HPE began regular trading on the New York Stock Exchange on 2 November 2015 under the ticker "HPE", while HP Inc. kept trading as "HPQ" ([Hewlett-Packard Company](https://investor.hp.com/news-events/news/news-details/2015/HP-Board-of-Directors-Approves-Separation/default.aspx)). HP Inc. kept the printing and personal systems businesses, while Hewlett Packard Enterprise took the Enterprise Group, Enterprise Services, Software and Financial Services businesses ([Hewlett-Packard Company](https://investor.hp.com/news-events/news/news-details/2015/HP-Board-of-Directors-Approves-Separation/default.aspx)). That legal detail matters: today's HP Inc. is the direct continuation of the original Hewlett-Packard Company, not a new company, which is why its history — including its lawsuits — belongs to HP Inc. ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Hewlett-Packard-Company)).

## The Forgent JPEG patent case: HP as a defendant

One episode the standard histories skip is the 2004 patent case that made Hewlett-Packard a defendant alongside its biggest rivals. The patent at issue, U.S. Patent 4,698,672, covered compression technology used in the JPEG image format and had been filed by [Compression Labs](/organizations/compression-labs/) in October 1986 ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2004/04/23/forgent_jpeg_suit/)). Forgent Networks acquired Compression Labs in 1997, and in June 2002 announced it held the sole and exclusive right to license the patent in all fields except satellite broadcasting ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2004/04/23/forgent_jpeg_suit/)). [Sony](/organizations/sony/) paid $15 million for a licence, and Forgent claimed to have collected $90 million in licensing royalties since then ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2004/04/23/forgent_jpeg_suit/)).

In April 2004 Forgent sued 30 companies that had rejected its royalty terms, including PC makers [IBM](/organizations/ibm/), [Dell](/organizations/dell/), Apple, HP, [Toshiba](/organizations/toshiba/) and [Gateway](/organizations/gateway/), camera companies [Fuji Photo Film](/organizations/fujifilm/), [Agfa](/organizations/agfa/) and Eastman [Kodak](/organizations/kodak/), and software companies including [Adobe](/organizations/adobe/), [Macromedia](/organizations/macromedia/) and JASC ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2004/04/23/forgent_jpeg_suit/)). The patent was due to expire in the United States about two years later, twenty years from its filing date, which put Forgent under pressure to extract value quickly ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2004/04/23/forgent_jpeg_suit/)). Forgent Networks — the company now known as [Asure Software](/organizations/asure-software/) — went on to collect more than $110 million from over 60 companies before settling its final cases ([BetaNews](http://betanews.com/article/forgent-settles-jpeg-patent-litigation/)). Because the defendant in 2004 was Hewlett-Packard Company, and that company was renamed HP Inc. in 2015, the case is part of HP Inc.'s own legal history ([Hewlett-Packard Company](https://investor.hp.com/news-events/news/news-details/2015/HP-Board-of-Directors-Approves-Separation/default.aspx)).

## HP Inc. today: PCs, printers and hybrid work

HP Inc. survived a hostile takeover attempt shortly after the split's first years. [Xerox](/organizations/xerox/) launched a hostile bid in late 2019 and raised its offer from $22 to $24 per share in March 2020, but on 31 March 2020 it withdrew its tender offer and dropped its attempt to replace HP's board, blaming the COVID-19 pandemic and market turmoil ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/03/31/xerox-drops-34b-hp-take-over-bid-amid-covid-19-uncertainty/)). The bid had never looked proportionate: Xerox's market capitalisation was about $4 billion against HP's about $25 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/03/31/xerox-drops-34b-hp-take-over-bid-amid-covid-19-uncertainty/)).

Under chief executive Enrique Lores, HP has pushed into hybrid-work products. On 28 March 2022 it announced a $3.3 billion agreement to acquire Poly, a provider of video conferencing, headset and voice products, paying $40 per share — a 50% premium — to build a leading portfolio of hybrid-work solutions ([CRN](https://www.crn.com/slide-shows/components-peripherals/hp-s-3-3b-acquisition-of-poly-six-things-you-need-to-know?cid=nl_crn&)). HP said it expected $500 million of revenue synergies from the deal by fiscal 2025, and Poly had projected $1.7 billion in revenue for fiscal 2022 ([CRN](https://www.crn.com/slide-shows/components-peripherals/hp-s-3-3b-acquisition-of-poly-six-things-you-need-to-know?cid=nl_crn&)). A month earlier HP had bought HyperX, the gaming-peripherals unit of Kingston Technology ([CRN](https://www.crn.com/slide-shows/components-peripherals/hp-s-3-3b-acquisition-of-poly-six-things-you-need-to-know?cid=nl_crn&)).

In the PC market HP remains second worldwide. In the second quarter of 2025 it shipped 14.1 million desktops and notebooks, up 3.2% year over year, behind Lenovo's 17.0 million and ahead of Dell's 9.8 million, in a market of 67.6 million units ([Canalys](https://www.canalys.com/newsroom/worldwide-pc-shipments-up-7-in-q2-2025)).

Fiscal 2025, ended 31 October 2025, showed a company growing revenue but earning less: net revenue of $55.3 billion, up 3.2%, with GAAP net earnings of $2.5 billion and diluted earnings per share of $2.65, down 5.7% ([HP Inc.](https://investor.hp.com/news-events/news/news-details/2025/HP-Inc--Reports-Fiscal-2025-Full-Year-and-Fourth-Quarter-Results/default.aspx)). The Personal Systems business drove the growth while Printing shrank: in the fourth quarter Personal Systems revenue rose 8% to $10.4 billion while Printing revenue fell 4% to $4.3 billion ([HP Inc.](https://investor.hp.com/news-events/news/news-details/2025/HP-Inc--Reports-Fiscal-2025-Full-Year-and-Fourth-Quarter-Results/default.aspx)). HP generated $2.9 billion of free cash flow and returned $1.9 billion to shareholders through dividends and buybacks, raising its quarterly dividend to $0.30 per share ([HP Inc.](https://investor.hp.com/news-events/news/news-details/2025/HP-Inc--Reports-Fiscal-2025-Full-Year-and-Fourth-Quarter-Results/default.aspx)).

In November 2025 HP also announced a company-wide initiative to cut costs through artificial-intelligence adoption, targeting about $1 billion of gross run-rate savings by the end of fiscal 2028 at a cost of about $650 million in restructuring charges, and a reduction of about 4,000–6,000 employees from its global headcount ([HP Inc.](https://investor.hp.com/news-events/news/news-details/2025/HP-Inc--Reports-Fiscal-2025-Full-Year-and-Fourth-Quarter-Results/default.aspx)). The plan's implication is clear: after a decade as an independent company, HP Inc. is betting that AI-powered devices and a leaner workforce, rather than printing supplies alone, will carry its next growth.

**How big is HP Inc. in the PC market?**
HP was the world's second-largest PC maker in the second quarter of 2025, shipping 14.1 million units, up 3.2% year over year, behind Lenovo's 17.0 million and ahead of Dell's 9.8 million ([Canalys](https://www.canalys.com/newsroom/worldwide-pc-shipments-up-7-in-q2-2025)). Its Personal Systems segment is its largest business, far bigger than Printing ([HP Inc.](https://investor.hp.com/news-events/news/news-details/2025/HP-Inc--Reports-Fiscal-2025-Full-Year-and-Fourth-Quarter-Results/default.aspx)).
