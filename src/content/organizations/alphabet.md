## Overview

The Justice Department spent six years trying to break Google apart. Twice it won the liability case — a federal judge found the search business an illegal monopoly in 2024, and a second court reached the same conclusion about the ad-tech business in April 2025. Twice it lost the remedy it wanted most. In September 2025, Judge Amit Mehta refused to order Google to divest Chrome or Android, imposing behavioral remedies instead: no more exclusive default-placement deals, and mandatory sharing of some search data with "Qualified Competitors" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/02/google-spared-from-ad-business-breakup-but-judge-orders-changes-to-how-it-operates/)). One year later the pattern repeated: Judge Leonie Brinkema ruled that [Google](https://factsledger.com/organizations/google/) could keep its advertising business, ordering only unspecified changes to business practices — her written ruling stayed sealed for 14 days while the parties redacted it ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/02/google-spared-from-ad-business-breakup-but-judge-orders-changes-to-how-it-operates/)).

In between those two American victories came the European hammer. On September 4, 2025, the European Commission fined Alphabet €2.95 billion for abusing its dominance in ad tech — its fourth abuse-of-dominance decision against Google — finding that self-preferencing practices dating to at least 2014 had raised costs for advertisers and cut revenues for publishers. Brussels gave Google 60 days to propose compliance measures and warned that divestiture might be "the only effective way" to end the conflicts of interest; Alphabet called the fine "unjustified" and appealed ([MarketMinute](https://markets.financialcontent.com/kelownadailycourier/article/marketminute-2025-9-18-alphabet-navigates-regulatory-gauntlet-antitrust-ruling-spares-breakup-eu-slaps-hefty-ad-tech-fine)).

## The $4 trillion machine

While the lawyers argued, the business kept growing. Alphabet's market capitalization stood at roughly $4 trillion in the summer of 2026 ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/technology/history-of-alphabet-company-timeline-facts)). Full-year 2025 revenue was $402.84 billion, up 15.1% and the first time the company crossed $400 billion, with net income of $132.2 billion ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/technology/history-of-alphabet-company-timeline-facts)). On June 29, 2026, the company joined the Dow Jones Industrial Average, replacing Verizon — a blue-chip coronation for a company that began in a rented garage ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/technology/history-of-alphabet-company-timeline-facts)). The model underneath never changed: advertising has historically supplied 75 to 80% of revenue, an even higher share of operating profit ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/technology/history-of-alphabet-company-timeline-facts)).

## From BackRub to the holding company

The origin story is the industry's best-known: Larry Page and Sergey Brin started the BackRub research project at Stanford in the mid-1990s, ranking pages by their link structure — the PageRank algorithm — and renamed it Google, a play on "googol," the number 1 followed by 100 zeros ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/technology/history-of-alphabet-company-timeline-facts)). Sun Microsystems co-founder Andy Bechtolsheim wrote a $100,000 check to "Google Inc." in August 1998 before the company legally existed, forcing a scramble to incorporate on September 7 ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/technology/history-of-alphabet-company-timeline-facts)). The milestones stacked up fast: a billion pages indexed by 2000, AdWords in 2000, Gmail in 2004, an $85-a-share IPO in August 2004, Android for about $50 million in 2005, YouTube in 2006 ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/technology/history-of-alphabet-company-timeline-facts)).

Alphabet itself was Page's answer to the sprawl. On August 10, 2015, he announced that Google would become a subsidiary of a new holding company, effective October 2 — a structure that let the core internet business stay focused while the speculative "Other Bets" (Waymo, Calico, Verily, GV, the moonshots of Google X) ran separately ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/technology/history-of-alphabet-company-timeline-facts)). In December 2019 Page and Brin stepped back from executive posts entirely; Sundar Pichai, who had run Google since 2015, took both CEO jobs ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/technology/history-of-alphabet-company-timeline-facts)). And the machine keeps reorganizing: in August 2026, DeepMind co-founder [Demis Hassabis](https://factsledger.com/people/demis-hassabis/) handed day-to-day leadership of Google DeepMind to Koray Kavukcuoglu and took the titles Chair of Google DeepMind and Chief Scientist of Alphabet, to focus on AGI ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/08/05/demis-hassabis-deepmind/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not treat the September 2025 search remedies as final: they were reported as under appeal, with an outcome that could reach the U.S. Supreme Court. It does not state the specifics of Brinkema's September 2026 ad-tech remedies, which were sealed at the time of the reporting read for this profile. It does not assert whether the European Commission's divestiture warning became an order — the €2.95 billion fine and the 60-day compliance window were reported as the live facts. It gives scale figures (market cap, revenue, headcount) only at stated points in time. And it presents the 2015 restructure through contemporary and retrospective reporting, not the original blog post, which was not read in full.
