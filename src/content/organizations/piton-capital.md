## Overview

Piton Capital is a [London](/places/london/)-based venture capital firm that invests in companies with strong network effects, predominantly at seed and Series A, with tickets targeting about €3 million. Its partners include Greg Lockwood, Edouard Mercier, Andrin Bachmann and Mira Mihaylova ([Vestbee](https://www.vestbee.com/insights/articles/vc-of-the-month-piton-capital)). Piton is the most consistent institutional backer in the [Booksy](/organizations/booksy/) story: it participated in the $800,000 round in 2015, led the $13.2 million Series B in 2018, co-led the $28.5 million round in 2019, and participated in the $70 million Series C in 2021 ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2021/01/29/booksy-slots-in-70m/)).

## The network-effects thesis

Piton describes itself as geography- and industry-agnostic, with one filter that decides everything: a company must have the potential for strong network effects ([Vestbee](https://www.vestbee.com/insights/articles/vc-of-the-month-piton-capital)). Partner Mira Mihaylova sets out five tests — potential for strong network effects, an outstanding team, proven product-market fit, a tested go-to-market, and a big market (or a small market as a wedge) — and three disqualifiers: no network-effects potential, pre-launch, or little traction ([Vestbee](https://www.vestbee.com/insights/articles/vc-of-the-month-piton-capital)).

The lessons Mihaylova draws from the firm's failures are unusually specific: too much money can destroy companies; premature international or adjacent expansion is a recurring mistake; irrational competitors in a money-abundant market can wreck a good business; and teams that fail to attract talent fail ([Vestbee](https://www.vestbee.com/insights/articles/vc-of-the-month-piton-capital)). The current focus is on markets resilient to AI — proprietary datasets, marketplaces with differentiated supply, social and collectibles — and on models that benefit from AI ([Vestbee](https://www.vestbee.com/insights/articles/vc-of-the-month-piton-capital)).

## Early bets: FanDuel and Treatwell

Piton's best-known early bet was FanDuel. The firm led a $4 million round in 2011 and retained a board seat through FanDuel's 2017 capital restructuring, when KKR and Shamrock tightened their grip and the FTC threatened to block the DraftKings merger ([Insider.co.uk](https://www.insider.co.uk/news/edinburgh-tech-vc-pioneer-pentech-10969575?int_source=amp_continue_reading&int_medium=amp&int_campaign=continue_reading_button)). The FanDuel bet is the template for the thesis: a two-sided marketplace where each new player makes the product better for every other player.

The same logic runs through Piton's booking-platform investments. Piton backed the Dutch [Treatwell](/organizations/treatwell/) alongside Project A Ventures, and partner Andrin Bachmann was quoted when Wahanda acquired the Dutch company for €34 million in June 2015 ([SpaBusiness](https://www.spabusiness.com/index.cfm?pagetype=news&codeID=316283)). Treatwell, DocPlanner and Booksy are one thread in the portfolio: marketplaces that match supply and demand in a local service, where the network itself is the moat.

## Booksy: four rounds, one backer

No other investor appears in as many Booksy rounds as Piton. The firm participated in the $800,000 round in 2015 alongside [Müller Medien](/organizations/muller-medien/) and [Inovo Venture Partners](/organizations/inovo-venture-partners/), led the $13.2 million Series B in 2018, co-led the $28.5 million round in September 2019 with [Enern](/organizations/enern/), [Industry Ventures](/organizations/industry-ventures/) and XG Ventures, and participated in the $70 million Series C in 2021 led by [Cat Rock Capital](/organizations/cat-rock-capital/) ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2021/01/29/booksy-slots-in-70m/)). The 2018 Series B also included [OpenOcean](/organizations/openocean/), Kulczyk Investments and [Zach Coelius](/people/zach-coelius/) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/07/06/booksy-the-worldwide-booking-system-raises-13-2-million/)).

Piton's other headline deal of 2019 was DocPlanner's €80 million Series E, led by One Peak Partners and Goldman Sachs Private Capital, "with existing investors Piton Capital and ENERN Investments also participating", bringing DocPlanner's total raised to about €130 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/05/14/docplanner-series-e/?fbclid=IwAR0BCvLdGeOH3NZOMQgPmfsOGuJWraUCT-a5tC5dv72mfe0b-YTalfAcQB8&utm_campaign=Last+week+in+the+Spanish+startup+ecosystem&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue+newsletter)). DocPlanner, a healthcare booking platform processing 1.5 million bookings a month, is the same marketplace shape as Booksy in a different vertical ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/05/14/docplanner-series-e/?fbclid=IwAR0BCvLdGeOH3NZOMQgPmfsOGuJWraUCT-a5tC5dv72mfe0b-YTalfAcQB8&utm_campaign=Last+week+in+the+Spanish+startup+ecosystem&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue+newsletter)).

Beyond the booking platforms, the portfolio Piton names includes Auto1, ManoMano, DocPlanner and Watchfinder ([Vestbee](https://www.vestbee.com/insights/articles/vc-of-the-month-piton-capital)). The common thread is the one Mihaylova states: each is a marketplace or platform where the network itself is the product, and where scale makes the leader harder to displace. That is also why the firm's failures are instructive rather than incidental — the lessons it draws are about what breaks a network-effects business (too much money, premature expansion, a team that cannot hire) rather than about any single deal ([Vestbee](https://www.vestbee.com/insights/articles/vc-of-the-month-piton-capital)).
