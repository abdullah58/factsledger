The Ministry of Culture and Tourism of the Republic of Türkiye is the Turkish government ministry responsible for cultural policy and the country's tourism sector. Created in 2003 from the merger of separate culture and tourism portfolios, it runs museums, heritage sites, and film funding from Ankara — and behind the tourist brochures it operates a state Oscar-campaign machine, financing Turkey's Academy Award entries and their public-relations pushes abroad ([culturalpolicies.net](https://www.culturalpolicies.net/wp-content/uploads/pdf_short/Türkiye/Turliye2023_shortprofile.pdf); [Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)).

## How the ministry is organized

The ministry is the key public actor in Turkish culture, established as a new ministry in 2003 with central, provincial, and overseas administrations; its provincial arms sit in all 81 of the country's provinces ([culturalpolicies.net](https://www.culturalpolicies.net/wp-content/uploads/pdf_short/Türkiye/Turliye2023_shortprofile.pdf)). Its duties are defined in Presidential Decree No. 1 of 2018, issued when the presidential government system reorganized the executive: bodies such as the Radio and Television Supreme Council (RTÜK), the Turkish Cooperation and Coordination Agency (TİKA), the Yunus Emre Institute, and the National Library were attached to it, while the State Theatres and State Opera and Ballet lost much of their post-1940s autonomy ([culturalpolicies.net](https://www.culturalpolicies.net/wp-content/uploads/pdf_short/Türkiye/Turliye2023_shortprofile.pdf)). General directorates cover cinema, libraries and publications, cultural heritage and museums, fine arts, manuscripts, promotion, and investments; the Türkiye Tourism Promotion and Development Agency (TGA) is a related organisation with its own boards ([culturalpolicies.net](https://www.culturalpolicies.net/wp-content/uploads/pdf_short/Türkiye/Turliye2023_shortprofile.pdf)). Strikingly, the ministry has never worked from an explicitly stated cultural policy document — the only national cultural policy report was issued in 2013 through the Council of Europe's review process ([culturalpolicies.net](https://www.culturalpolicies.net/wp-content/uploads/pdf_short/Türkiye/Turliye2023_shortprofile.pdf)).

## The Oscar machine: cinema as statecraft

The least-understood of the ministry's powers runs through its General Directorate of Cinema, which funds Turkish films annually under the Law on Evaluation, Classification and Support of Cinema Films ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkish-films-soaring-with-support-143623)). The scale is stark: Turkish films released domestically rose from 9 in 2002 to 180 in 2018, filmgoers for Turkish films jumped from 2 million to 44 million, and a 63 percent local-film preference put Turkey top in Europe ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkish-films-soaring-with-support-143623); [İKSV](https://film.iksv.org/i/content/1550_1_Koprude_Bulusmalar_Sektor_Raporu_2017.pdf)). Between 2010 and 2017 the ministry granted roughly ₺235 million to Turkish film productions ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkish-films-soaring-with-support-143623)). Funding is only half the story: the ministry also picks Turkey's Academy Award entries and lobbies for them — producer Baran Seyhan framed the Oscars as "like the Olympic Games," where state backing is expected ([Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/where-the-fire-burns-eyes-support-for-oscar--32630)). The [Ayla: The Daughter of War](/creative-works/ayla-the-daughter-of-war/) campaign is the signature case: ₺1.5 million in 2017 production support, then Turkish Airlines as main sponsor and the ministry as a main supporter of the Oscar public-relations campaign that carried the film to South Korea and Los Angeles ([İKSV](https://film.iksv.org/i/content/1550_1_Koprude_Bulusmalar_Sektor_Raporu_2017.pdf); [Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)).

## Heritage economics — and its critics

The ministry's day job is running the heritage economy: museums, ruins, hotel inspections, and 46 Culture and Promotion offices in 41 countries selling Türkiye as a destination ([OECD](https://ec.europa.eu/docsroom/documents/33358/attachments/1/translations/en/renditions/native)). Its most visible invention is Night Museum, a 2024 pilot that opened Ephesus, Hierapolis, Aspendos, and Patara after dark against summer heatwaves; Ephesus alone drew 157,000 night visitors in July 2024, out of 1.4 million total that year ([The Art Newspaper](https://WWW.THEARTNEWSPAPER.COM/2024/09/16/turkey-keeps-ruins-open-after-dark-to-combat-heatwaves)). That single-month figure explains why a culture ministry behaves like an economic agency: tourism contributes more than 10 percent of Türkiye's GDP and an estimated two million jobs ([The Art Newspaper](https://WWW.THEARTNEWSPAPER.COM/2024/09/16/turkey-keeps-ruins-open-after-dark-to-combat-heatwaves)). Critics push back: conservation specialist Özgün Özçakır asked whether the state is "protecting history or commodifying it," and former tourism minister Bahattin Yücel warned of disorderly, poorly supervised night museology ([The Art Newspaper](https://WWW.THEARTNEWSPAPER.COM/2024/09/16/turkey-keeps-ruins-open-after-dark-to-combat-heatwaves)). The darker side of its regulatory role surfaced after the January 2025 Grand Kartal Hotel fire in Kartalkaya, which killed 78 people; a TMMOB engineering-union report assigned inspection responsibility to the ministry, which closed uncertified facilities across all 81 provinces while opposition figures demanded Ersoy's resignation ([Turkish Minute](https://turkishminute.com/2025/01/30/top-engineer-union-puts-blame-on-tourism-ministry-in-preliminary-report-on-deadly-hotel-fire/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the ministry's official English name?**
The ministry's own English-language self-description gives it as the Ministry of Culture and Tourism of the Republic of Türkiye (T.C. Kültür ve Turizm Bakanlığı). Its headquarters are at İsmet İnönü Bulvarı No:32, 06100 Emek, Ankara, reachable on +90 (312) 470 8000 ([ktb.gov.tr](https://www.ktb.gov.tr/)).

**When was the ministry created?**
It was established as a new ministry in 2003, merging the culture and tourism portfolios; its current duties are defined in Presidential Decree No. 1 of 2018, which folded bodies including RTÜK, TİKA, the Yunus Emre Institute, and the National Library into its structure ([culturalpolicies.net](https://www.culturalpolicies.net/wp-content/uploads/pdf_short/Türkiye/Turliye2023_shortprofile.pdf)).

**What did the ministry have to do with the film Ayla?**
In 2017 the ministry's General Directorate of Cinema granted Ayla: The Daughter of War ₺1.5 million in production support, and Hürriyet Daily News reported the ministry as a main supporter of the film's Oscar public-relations campaign — with Turkish Airlines as main sponsor — carrying it to South Korea and Los Angeles ([İKSV](https://film.iksv.org/i/content/1550_1_Koprude_Bulusmalar_Sektor_Raporu_2017.pdf); [Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkeys-oscar-nominee-ayla-brings-korean-war-memories-117633)).

**What is the Night Museum project?**
A 2024 pilot that keeps sites including Ephesus, Hierapolis, Aspendos, and Patara open after dark with low-heat LED lighting, pitched as relief from summer heatwaves. More than 157,000 visitors toured Ephesus at night in July 2024, and minister Ersoy described it as pulling tourists away from all-inclusive hotel poolsides ([The Art Newspaper](https://WWW.THEARTNEWSPAPER.COM/2024/09/16/turkey-keeps-ruins-open-after-dark-to-combat-heatwaves)).

**Who leads the ministry, and has it faced criticism?**
Mehmet Nuri Ersoy has been minister since 10 July 2018, and received the WTTC's Global Champion Award on 9 October 2026 for government-industry cooperation in tourism ([travelprnews](https://travelprnews.com/wttc-honours-turkiye-tourism-minister-mehmet-nuri-ersoy-at-valletta-global-summit/travel-press-release/2026/10/09/)). Critics include conservationists questioning the commercialization of heritage sites and the TMMOB engineers' union, which assigned the ministry inspection responsibility after the 2025 Kartalkaya hotel fire ([The Art Newspaper](https://WWW.THEARTNEWSPAPER.COM/2024/09/16/turkey-keeps-ruins-open-after-dark-to-combat-heatwaves); [Turkish Minute](https://turkishminute.com/2025/01/30/top-engineer-union-puts-blame-on-tourism-ministry-in-preliminary-report-on-deadly-hotel-fire/)).
