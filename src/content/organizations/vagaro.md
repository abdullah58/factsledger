## Overview

Vagaro is a cloud business-management platform and consumer marketplace for salons, spas, medical spas, barbershops and fitness studios, based in Pleasanton, California and founded in April 2009 by Fred Helou. It sells an all-in-one system — online booking, calendaring, point-of-sale and payment processing, payroll, inventory, client management and marketing — and it reached a $1 billion valuation in November 2021 on a follow-on investment from FTV Capital ([FTV Capital](https://ftvcapital.com/insights/vagaro-reaches-1-billion-valuation-why-were-investing-again/)). What sets Vagaro apart from pure booking rivals such as [Booksy](/organizations/booksy/), [Treatwell](/organizations/treatwell/) and [StyleSeat](/organizations/styleseat/) is payments: Helou built payment processing end-to-end in-house over about seven years, and payments now account for roughly half of the company's revenue ([FTV Capital](https://ftvcapital.com/insights/vagaro-founder-and-ceo-fred-helou-on-how-embedding-payments-into-vertical-software-led-to-outsized-growth/)).

## A haircut that could not be booked

The company began with a missed haircut. In 1999, on a trip to Korea, Helou wanted to book a haircut for his return home but could not call — over $2 a minute — or book online ([FTV Capital](https://ftvcapital.com/insights/vagaro-founder-and-ceo-fred-helou-on-how-embedding-payments-into-vertical-software-led-to-outsized-growth/)). A decade later, after being laid off from a director of software development job in December 2008, he founded Vagaro in April 2009 ([FTV Capital](https://ftvcapital.com/insights/vagaro-founder-and-ceo-fred-helou-on-how-embedding-payments-into-vertical-software-led-to-outsized-growth/)).

Vagaro's first product claim was a first: the first iPhone app letting consumers search, read reviews, book and pay by credit card in one app, which Helou says "took off like wildfire" ([FTV Capital](https://ftvcapital.com/insights/vagaro-founder-and-ceo-fred-helou-on-how-embedding-payments-into-vertical-software-led-to-outsized-growth/)). The harder work came after: the company built payments end-to-end in-house over about seven years — its own software, back-end, banking connections and card readers — rather than reselling a third-party processor ([FTV Capital](https://ftvcapital.com/insights/vagaro-founder-and-ceo-fred-helou-on-how-embedding-payments-into-vertical-software-led-to-outsized-growth/)). That decision is the thesis of the company: in vertical software, owning payments turns a booking tool into a revenue engine, and payments now make up about half of Vagaro's revenue ([FTV Capital](https://ftvcapital.com/insights/vagaro-founder-and-ceo-fred-helou-on-how-embedding-payments-into-vertical-software-led-to-outsized-growth/)).

## What the platform does

Vagaro's product is an all-in-one cloud platform for the businesses it serves: online booking, calendaring, point-of-sale and payment processing, payroll, inventory, client management and marketing across email, text and social, plus a consumer marketplace where customers discover and book services ([FTV Capital](https://ftvcapital.com/insights/vagaro-founder-and-ceo-fred-helou-on-how-embedding-payments-into-vertical-software-led-to-outsized-growth/)). It serves salons, spas, medical spas, barbershops and fitness studios, and by 2023 it counted more than 75,000 businesses in four countries, with more than 200 developers among its 400 employees ([FTV Capital](https://ftvcapital.com/insights/vagaro-founder-and-ceo-fred-helou-on-how-embedding-payments-into-vertical-software-led-to-outsized-growth/)). The four countries are the United States, Canada, Australia and the UK, the last three reached in the expansion that followed the 2021 funding round ([FTV Capital](https://ftvcapital.com/insights/vagaro-reaches-1-billion-valuation-why-were-investing-again/)).

## From first institutional capital to unicorn

Vagaro ran for nearly a decade without institutional capital. Its first institutional round was a $63 million growth-equity investment led by FTV Capital, announced on 5 December 2018, with FTV partners Robert Anderson and Richard Garman joining the board ([FTV Capital](https://ftvcapital.com/insights/vagaro-reaches-1-billion-valuation-why-were-investing-again/)). At the first meeting between the two firms, Vagaro had 28,000 customers and was growing 50% ([FTV Capital](https://ftvcapital.com/insights/vagaro-reaches-1-billion-valuation-why-were-investing-again/)).

Three years later, on 2 November 2021, FTV announced a follow-on investment at a $1 billion valuation, with the terms undisclosed ([FTV Capital](https://ftvcapital.com/insights/vagaro-reaches-1-billion-valuation-why-were-investing-again/)). The company used the round to expand into Canada, Australia and the UK; its clients' sales had grown more than 105% in the prior year, selling about 15% more than before Covid, and more than 313,000 classes had been livestreamed during the pandemic pivot ([Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/vagaro-reaches-unicorn-status/)).

## The Schedulicity acquisition

On 13 January 2025 Vagaro acquired Schedulicity, a scheduling software company based in Bozeman, Montana and founded by Jerry Nettuno, effective immediately ([Salon Today](https://www.salontoday.com/news/vagaro-acquires-schedulicity-to-expand-service-to-industry)). Vagaro said it would continue supporting Schedulicity's businesses with onboarding to Vagaro's tools and 24/7 support ([Salon Today](https://www.salontoday.com/news/vagaro-acquires-schedulicity-to-expand-service-to-industry)). The deal fits the pattern of Vagaro's history: growth by absorbing the scheduling layer and then monetising it through its own payments stack, rather than by buying marketplaces for their demand.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Vagaro?**
A cloud platform for salons, spas, medical spas, barbershops and fitness studios, combining booking, payments, payroll, inventory and marketing with a consumer marketplace ([FTV Capital](https://ftvcapital.com/insights/vagaro-founder-and-ceo-fred-helou-on-how-embedding-payments-into-vertical-software-led-to-outsized-growth/)).

**How is Vagaro different from Booksy?**
Booksy charges salons a monthly subscription for booking software; Vagaro also sells booking software, but it built its own payments processing in-house and payments now account for about half its revenue ([FTV Capital](https://ftvcapital.com/insights/vagaro-founder-and-ceo-fred-helou-on-how-embedding-payments-into-vertical-software-led-to-outsized-growth/)).

**When did Vagaro become a unicorn?**
In November 2021, when a follow-on investment from FTV Capital valued it at $1 billion; the amount was not disclosed ([FTV Capital](https://ftvcapital.com/insights/vagaro-reaches-1-billion-valuation-why-were-investing-again/)).

**What did Vagaro acquire in 2025?**
Schedulicity, a Montana scheduling software company, on 13 January 2025 ([Salon Today](https://www.salontoday.com/news/vagaro-acquires-schedulicity-to-expand-service-to-industry)).

**Where is Vagaro based?**
Pleasanton, California, currently; it was founded in Dublin, California, and 2018 materials still place it there. The date of the move was not verified.
