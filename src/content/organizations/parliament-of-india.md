## Overview

**The Parliament of India is the bicameral legislature of India, made up of the President and two Houses — the directly elected Lok Sabha and the indirectly elected Rajya Sabha. It makes laws, controls public finance through the Lok Sabha, and holds the executive to account; since September 2023 it has met in a new triangular building in New Delhi.**

Article 79 of the Constitution establishes Parliament for the Union as the President plus the Council of States and the House of the People — and the President, though a component of Parliament, is not a member of either House ([AAP Tax Law](https://www.aaptaxlaw.com/constitution-of-india/article-79-80-81-constitution-constitution-of-parliament-composition-of-the-council-of-states-composition-of-the-house-of-the-people-article-79-80-81-of-constitution-of-india-1949.html)). This profile is about the institution as a whole — not the [Lok Sabha](/organizations/lok-sabha/) alone, not the Rajya Sabha alone, and not any other country's parliament: the United Kingdom's Parliament, the United States Congress, and the other national legislatures are different bodies. Parliament sits in [India](/places/india/)'s capital, New Delhi, and its two Houses together carry the whole of the Union's legislative work.

## What Parliament is made of

Parliament has three components. The President is the head of state and a constituent part of Parliament under Article 79, whose assent turns bills into law ([AAP Tax Law](https://www.aaptaxlaw.com/constitution-of-india/article-79-80-81-constitution-constitution-of-parliament-composition-of-the-council-of-states-composition-of-the-house-of-the-people-article-79-80-81-of-constitution-of-india-1949.html)). The Lok Sabha, the House of the People, is directly elected: Article 81 provides for not more than 530 members from state constituencies and 20 from union territories, and the Election Commission records 543 Lok Sabha seats ([AAP Tax Law](https://www.aaptaxlaw.com/constitution-of-india/article-79-80-81-constitution-constitution-of-parliament-composition-of-the-council-of-states-composition-of-the-house-of-the-people-article-79-80-81-of-constitution-of-india-1949.html)). The Rajya Sabha, the Council of States, is indirectly elected by state legislators, with a constitutional maximum of 250 members and a present strength of 245 ([AAP Tax Law](https://www.aaptaxlaw.com/constitution-of-india/article-79-80-81-constitution-constitution-of-parliament-composition-of-the-council-of-states-composition-of-the-house-of-the-people-article-79-80-81-of-constitution-of-india-1949.html)). The older provision for nominating Anglo-Indian members to the Lok Sabha has ceased to have effect ([AAP Tax Law](https://www.aaptaxlaw.com/constitution-of-india/article-79-80-81-constitution-constitution-of-parliament-composition-of-the-council-of-states-composition-of-the-house-of-the-people-article-79-80-81-of-constitution-of-india-1949.html)).

The division of labour is asymmetric. Only the Lok Sabha can decide money bills and remove the government through a no-confidence motion, while only the Rajya Sabha can authorise Parliament to legislate on State List subjects or create new All-India Services ([Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/loksabha-rajyasabha-discussions/perspective-rajya-sabha-the-upper-house)). Parliament's roots reach back to the Montagu-Chelmsford Report of 1918, which introduced a bicameral legislature with an upper Council of State ([Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/loksabha-rajyasabha-discussions/perspective-rajya-sabha-the-upper-house)).

| Component | How chosen | Strength |
| --- | --- | --- |
| President of India | Elected (not a member of either House) | 1 |
| Lok Sabha (House of the People) | Direct election by voters | 543 seats |
| Rajya Sabha (Council of States) | Indirect election by state legislators + 12 nominated | 245 present (max 250) |

## Sessions: three a year, but a shrinking calendar

India has no fixed parliamentary calendar. By convention Parliament meets for three sessions a year: the Budget Session, the longest, from late January to late April or early May, with a recess so committees can examine budget proposals; the three-week Monsoon Session in July–August; and the three-week Winter Session in November–December ([PRS Legislative Research](https://prsindia.org/articles-by-prs-team/explained-a-look-at-how-and-when-parliament-is-convened)). The power to convene a session rests with the government, through the Cabinet Committee on Parliamentary Affairs, formalised by the President, in whose name MPs are summoned ([PRS Legislative Research](https://prsindia.org/articles-by-prs-team/explained-a-look-at-how-and-when-parliament-is-convened)). Article 85 requires only that no more than six months pass between sessions — a rule B. R. Ambedkar drafted in the Constituent Assembly to stop an executive from avoiding legislative scrutiny, after rejecting a proposal from K. T. Shah that Parliament sit year-round ([PRS Legislative Research](https://prsindia.org/articles-by-prs-team/explained-a-look-at-how-and-when-parliament-is-convened)).

The convention is flexible to a fault. Governments have delayed sessions for state elections, cut them short for campaigning, split sessions to issue ordinances, and stretched one session for months to block a no-confidence motion ([PRS Legislative Research](https://prsindia.org/articles-by-prs-team/explained-a-look-at-how-and-when-parliament-is-convened)). And the calendar is shrinking: the Lok Sabha averaged a little over 120 sitting days a year in Parliament's first two decades, against about 70 in the last decade, and in 2020 Parliament met for just 33 days — the fewest since 2008's 46 ([PRS Legislative Research](https://prsindia.org/articles-by-prs-team/explained-a-look-at-how-and-when-parliament-is-convened)) — which means the body that is supposed to scrutinise the executive now sits barely half as often as it once did.

| Session | Typical timing | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget Session | Late January to late April / early May | President's address, Union Budget, committee scrutiny of spending |
| Monsoon Session | July–August (about three weeks) | Legislation and national issues between budget and winter |
| Winter Session | November–December (about three weeks) | Clearing pending bills before the year ends |

(The table follows PRS Legislative Research's account of the session convention.)

## The new building and the old one

The new Parliament building is the centrepiece of a $2.4 billion project to remake the colonial-era buildings at the capital's centre, and it was inaugurated by Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](/people/narendra-modi/) in May 2023 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/india-gets-new-parliament-building-modi-remakes-capitals-centre-2023-05-26/)). The triangular complex stands across from the heritage building completed in 1927 by British architects Edwin Lutyens and Herbert Baker; the government says the old building, "highly stressed" on capacity, infrastructure, technology and safety, will become a museum ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/india-gets-new-parliament-building-modi-remakes-capitals-centre-2023-05-26/)). The new building has 1,272 seats across its two chambers — nearly 500 more than the old one — and at least three times as much space, with halls themed on the peacock, the lotus and the banyan tree, and art from across the country capturing 5,000 years of Indian civilisation ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/india-gets-new-parliament-building-modi-remakes-capitals-centre-2023-05-26/)).

The inauguration was contested. Opposition parties boycotted it, arguing the President should open the new building rather than the Prime Minister, and the Supreme Court dismissed a public-interest petition seeking exactly that ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/india-gets-new-parliament-building-modi-remakes-capitals-centre-2023-05-26/)). Critics called the decision abrupt and lacking in transparency ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/india-gets-new-parliament-building-modi-remakes-capitals-centre-2023-05-26/)). Members entered the new building that September, leaving the old one, which was renamed Samvidhan Sadan ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/politics-and-nation/womens-quota-bill-first-piece-of-legislation-to-be-passed-by-new-parliament/articleshow/103850021.cms)).

The new building's first business was historic. The women's reservation bill — the Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, guaranteeing 33 per cent reservation for women in the Lok Sabha and the state legislative assemblies — was the first bill taken up there; the Lok Sabha passed it 454 votes to 2, the Rajya Sabha then passed it, and both Houses adjourned sine die ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/politics-and-nation/womens-quota-bill-first-piece-of-legislation-to-be-passed-by-new-parliament/articleshow/103850021.cms)). A similar bill had passed the Rajya Sabha in 2010 under the Congress-led UPA government but lapsed in the Lok Sabha ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/politics-and-nation/womens-quota-bill-first-piece-of-legislation-to-be-passed-by-new-parliament/articleshow/103850021.cms)).

## Deadlock and joint sittings

When the two Houses disagree on a bill — one rejects it, they deadlock on amendments, or six months pass without passage — Article 108 lets the President summon a joint sitting, presided over by the Lok Sabha Speaker, where a simple majority decides ([Times of India](http://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/home/lok-sabha-elections-2014/news/New-govt-will-call-joint-sittings-of-Houses-to-push-through-key-bills/articleshow/35315855.cms)). There is no joint sitting for money bills, where the Lok Sabha prevails, nor for Constitution Amendment Bills, which each House must pass separately ([The Legal Quotient](https://thelegalquotient.com/constitutional-law/rajya-sabha-the-council-of-states-in-india/5758/)). The device has been used only three times: for the Dowry Prohibition Bill in 1961, the Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill in 1978, and the Prevention of Terrorism Bill in 2002 ([Times of India](http://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/home/lok-sabha-elections-2014/news/New-govt-will-call-joint-sittings-of-Houses-to-push-through-key-bills/articleshow/35315855.cms)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the Parliament of India?**
Parliament is the Union's legislature under Article 79: the President plus two Houses, the Rajya Sabha (Council of States) and the Lok Sabha (House of the People). It makes laws, approves taxation and spending, and holds the executive accountable through debates, questions and committees.

**How many sessions does Parliament hold each year?**
By convention, three: the Budget Session from late January to late April or early May, with a recess for committees to examine budget proposals; the three-week Monsoon Session in July–August; and the three-week Winter Session in November–December. The Constitution requires only that the gap between sessions not exceed six months.

**When did Parliament move into the new building?**
The new triangular Parliament building was inaugurated by Prime Minister Narendra Modi in May 2023, and members entered it that September, leaving the old 1927 building, which was renamed Samvidhan Sadan. The new complex holds 1,272 seats across its two chambers, nearly 500 more than the old building.

**What was the first bill passed in the new Parliament building?**
The women's reservation bill, the Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, guaranteeing 33 per cent reservation for women in the Lok Sabha and state assemblies. The Lok Sabha passed it 454 votes to 2, the Rajya Sabha then passed it, and both Houses adjourned sine die.

**How often must Parliament meet?**
The Constitution requires that no more than six months pass between sessions, so Parliament must meet at least twice a year. In practice it meets three times a year by convention, though governments have shuffled, shortened, stretched or cancelled sessions for political and legislative reasons.
