## Overview

Sprints Capital — branded simply as Sprints — is a London-based growth investor that backs a small, concentrated portfolio of European technology companies and holds them as they scale ([Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/revolut-and-vinted-backer-sprints-closes-e725m-fund-after-fin-exit/)). Founded in 2015, with its first investment made in December 2016, the firm has built its reputation on a handful of large bets: it led Vinted's €50 million round in 2018, joined Revolut's $250 million Series C the same year, and invested in Intercom — now Fin — in February 2021, a holding Salesforce bought for about $3.6 billion in 2026 ([Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/revolut-and-vinted-backer-sprints-closes-e725m-fund-after-fin-exit/)). In October 2026 it closed its fifth fund at a €725 million hard cap, roughly 40% larger than its predecessor, with more than half the capital already deployed ([Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/revolut-and-vinted-backer-sprints-closes-e725m-fund-after-fin-exit/)).

The firm's own summary of its method has not changed since it started: back growth-stage companies with proven business models, strong and durable market positions, and excellent teams, and screen them on customer satisfaction ([Verdane](https://verdane.com/game-set-matchi-verdane-and-sprints-capital-invest-in-racket-sports-platform-to-back-international-growth/), [Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/revolut-and-vinted-backer-sprints-closes-e725m-fund-after-fin-exit/)). What distinguishes Sprints is how few bets it makes — eight to ten companies per fund — and how large each bet is. A concentrated portfolio means every investment has to work; it also means the firm can spend real time with each company, which is the point of the model.

## Concentration as a strategy

Most venture firms spread risk across dozens of companies per fund, accepting that most will fail and a few will pay for the rest. Sprints does the opposite. Its fifth fund will back a concentrated portfolio of eight to ten companies, and its earlier funds ran the same way ([Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/revolut-and-vinted-backer-sprints-closes-e725m-fund-after-fin-exit/)). The discipline is a quality checklist: companies with strong customer satisfaction, durable business models, and — in co-founder and managing partner Henrik Persson's words, as reported by Tech Funding News — only about 1% of Europe's roughly 35,000 venture-backed startups ever become unicorns, so the firm screens for the durability that separates the 1% from the rest ([Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/revolut-and-vinted-backer-sprints-closes-e725m-fund-after-fin-exit/)).

The firm's own numbers, reported in its December 2025 partnership letter, describe what that screen is meant to produce: across its funds, ownership-weighted portfolio growth of 35% and an EBITDA margin of 25%, giving a portfolio-level "Rule of 40" — growth plus margin — of 60% ([Sprints](https://www.sprints.com/articles/sprints-partnership-letter-the-long-arc-of-growth)). Those are the firm's own figures, and the letter is explicit that the ambition is a 3x return on each fund. It also reports that Sprints II ranks in the top 5% of global growth funds of its vintage, with Sprints III and IV "following a similar trajectory" ([Sprints](https://www.sprints.com/articles/sprints-partnership-letter-the-long-arc-of-growth)). The letter is a first-party document, so these are claims rather than verified results — but they are the claims the firm's own investors are asked to judge it by, and the October 2026 Fund V close, oversubscribed at its hard cap, suggests those investors were willing to re-up.

The team behind the strategy is small and senior. Sprints employed 27 people at the end of 2025, including five former company chief technology officers, and had recently moved to larger offices in Stockholm and London and opened a new office in Milan ([Sprints](https://www.sprints.com/articles/sprints-partnership-letter-the-long-arc-of-growth)). The team behind Sprints had invested more than €2 billion in over 50 companies in the last 20 years, as of 2021 — a figure that counts the partners' earlier investing careers as well as the firm's own funds ([Verdane](https://verdane.com/game-set-matchi-verdane-and-sprints-capital-invest-in-racket-sports-platform-to-back-international-growth/)).

## The portfolio: Vinted, Revolut, Fin

Sprints' two defining bets were both made in 2018. It first backed Vinted and Revolut that year, leading Vinted's €50 million round in August and joining Revolut's $250 million Series C ([Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/revolut-and-vinted-backer-sprints-closes-e725m-fund-after-fin-exit/)). Bloomberg describes Sprints as Vinted's biggest shareholder, and the firm sold part of both stakes in secondary deals in 2025 ([Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/revolut-and-vinted-backer-sprints-closes-e725m-fund-after-fin-exit/)). Both companies were marketplaces or fintechs with the durable, customer-loved characteristics the firm's checklist demands — Vinted in secondhand fashion, Revolut in consumer banking — and both became the kind of category-defining European companies that a concentrated fund needs.

The cleanest exit, in the firm's own telling, is Fin. Sprints invested in Intercom, now renamed Fin, in February 2021 ([Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/revolut-and-vinted-backer-sprints-closes-e725m-fund-after-fin-exit/)). Salesforce signed a deal on June 15, 2026 to buy the AI customer-service company for about $3.6 billion and completed the acquisition on September 10, 2026 ([Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/revolut-and-vinted-backer-sprints-closes-e725m-fund-after-fin-exit/)).

That preference is explicit: Persson has said Sprints has not backed any foundational AI company because most have yet to build a business model ([Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/revolut-and-vinted-backer-sprints-closes-e725m-fund-after-fin-exit/)). The firm is not sceptical about AI — it lists Planhat, an agentic customer platform, among its companies, and Fin is itself an AI product — but its AI exposure sits in applications rather than in model developers. The stance is consistent with the checklist: a company must already have customers who love it and a business model that works before Sprints will pay a growth-stage price for it.

The newer portfolio shows the same pattern in different sectors. In the year to December 2025 the firm invested €560 million — almost one-third of all capital deployed since its first investment in December 2016 — across four new companies: Willhaben, Checkout.com, Flightradar24, in which Sprints bought a 35% stake in September 2025 at a reported valuation of about $500 million, and Base (formerly BaseLinker), the Polish e-commerce operating system ([Sprints](https://www.sprints.com/articles/sprints-partnership-letter-the-long-arc-of-growth), [Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/revolut-and-vinted-backer-sprints-closes-e725m-fund-after-fin-exit/)). The older portfolio, as described in 2021, also included Hemnet, Vivino, Teamtailor and Chrono24 ([Verdane](https://verdane.com/game-set-matchi-verdane-and-sprints-capital-invest-in-racket-sports-platform-to-back-international-growth/)).

## Fund V and the Booksy connection

Sprints took about a year to raise its fifth fund, closing at the €725 million hard cap in October 2026 — roughly 40% more than the roughly €525 million Fund IV — with endowments, foundations, pension funds and family offices supplying the capital, and more than half of it already invested ([Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/revolut-and-vinted-backer-sprints-closes-e725m-fund-after-fin-exit/)). A €725 million growth fund closing at its hard cap in a weak fundraising market is itself evidence that the firm's limited partners believe the concentrated model works. The firm's own commitment is material too: liquidity from Sprints II was to let the general-partner commitment in Sprints V exceed 10% of total fund size ([Sprints](https://www.sprints.com/articles/sprints-partnership-letter-the-long-arc-of-growth)).

Sprints' connection to this site's loop is [Booksy](/organizations/booksy/). It participated in Booksy's $70 million Series C in January 2021, led by [Cat Rock Capital](/organizations/cat-rock-capital/), alongside [OpenOcean](/organizations/openocean/), [Piton Capital](/organizations/piton-capital/), [VNV Global](/organizations/vnv-global/), [Enern](/organizations/enern/) and angels including Kai Hansen and Zach Coelius; the round took Booksy's total raised to $119 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)). Booksy fits the Sprints checklist on its face: a marketplace with strong customer satisfaction (salons and their clients), a durable subscription model, and a roll-up strategy in a fragmented market. It is also the kind of company Sprints backs at growth stage rather than seed — established, revenue-generating, and expanding internationally.
