## Overview

Flexport is a [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/)-based freight forwarder that moves cargo for businesses around the world and wraps the whole process in software: booking, customs brokerage, cargo insurance, tracking and billing on a single cloud platform ([Reuters](https://gcaptain.com/flexport-valuation-doubles-to-8-billion-in-latest-funding-round/)). Founded in 2013 by Ryan Petersen, it grew from a [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/) startup into a company valued at $8 billion after a $935 million Series E in February 2022, moving nearly $19 billion of merchandise across 112 countries in 2021 ([Reuters](https://gcaptain.com/flexport-valuation-doubles-to-8-billion-in-latest-funding-round/)). It is still private, and its last published valuation dates from that 2022 round.

The arc since then has been a reversal: a pandemic-era boom, a bet on e-commerce fulfilment through Shopify, the abrupt exit of star hire Dave Clark after a year as CEO, and two rounds of ~20% staff cuts in 2023, with a third reported in January 2024. Flexport then did something unusual with a failed rival's technology — bought it cheap, rebuilt it, and sold it on at a gain — and in October 2026 it settled a trade-secrets lawsuit against a startup founded by two of its own former employees, a case that turned on whether confidential shipping documents had been used to train AI software ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/partnerships/2025/dat-acquire-convoy-platform-expand-freight-matching-network-capabilities/); [The Loadstar](https://theloadstar.com/freightmate-admits-using-flexport-docs-as-ai-trade-secrets-case-settles/)).

## From scooters to software: the founding

Petersen's route into freight started with the freight itself. He and his brother David imported motorbikes and electric scooters from China, and Petersen lived in China for two years from 2005 running the supply chain for that business ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/26/ryan-petersen-flexport/)). The frustration of that work led to ImportGenius, co-founded in 2006 with David and Michael Kanko: shipping manifests are public records, so the trio scanned them into a searchable database that importers used to research factories and competitors ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/26/ryan-petersen-flexport/)). Petersen's own explanation of the business he then built is the clearest available: a freight forwarder is a relay race, handing cargo from a trucking company to a warehouse to a port to an ocean carrier and back again, with unstructured data changing hands at every step — and Flexport's job is to structure that data into one platform ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/ryan-petersen-on-building-flexport-a-modern-freight-forwarder/)).

Getting licensed was the hard part: satisfying 43 different US trade regulators before the platform could operate ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/26/ryan-petersen-flexport/)). Flexport went through Y Combinator's Winter 2014 batch, and by 2016 it served 700 clients across 64 countries ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/26/ryan-petersen-flexport/)). By 2018 it was established enough to be named among the 1,000 customers in [Brex](/organizations/brex/)'s private launch, alongside [Algolia](/organizations/algolia/) and [Affirm](/organizations/affirm/) ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/brex-launches-credit-card-for-startups-raises-57-million-from-y-combinator-paypal-founders-others)). Early capital came from Founders Fund, and by 2020 the company counted 1,800 employees across 14 offices and 6 warehouses serving 10,000 clients ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/26/ryan-petersen-flexport/)).

## Peak valuation, then a brutal reset

Flexport's funding climb was steep, and it is best read as a table — the numbers below are the published round figures, not estimates:

| Round | Date | Amount | Valuation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DST-led round | 2017 | $110 million | $910 million post-money |
| SF Express-led round | 2018 | $100 million | Not disclosed |
| Series D (SoftBank-led) | February 2019 | $1 billion | $3.2 billion |
| Series E (a16z / MSD-led) | February 2022 | $935 million | $8 billion |

The 2019 SoftBank round came as Flexport vertically integrated — adding freight financing and chartering its own plane — and the 2022 round, co-led by Andreessen Horowitz and MSD Partners with Shopify participating, took total funding past $2.2 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/26/ryan-petersen-flexport/); [Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/flexport-receives-935m-funding/)). The pandemic also showed a different side of the company: its impact arm, Flexport.org, delivered around 62 million pieces of personal protective equipment, funding delivery of more than 10 million of them ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/26/ryan-petersen-flexport/)).

The reset began with the freight downturn. In January 2023 Flexport cut about 20% of its staff, around 700 people, saying it was overstaffed as shipping volumes fell ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/flexport-ceo-dave-clark-steps-down)). In May it bought Shopify's logistics business — including Deliverr, which Shopify had bought for $2.1 billion a year earlier — in an all-stock deal that gave Shopify about 13% of Flexport and made Flexport Shopify's official logistics partner ([Associated Press](https://techxplore.com/news/2023-05-shopify-narrowing-ambition-deliverr-staff.html)). Then, in September 2023, ex-Amazon executive Dave Clark — hired as co-CEO a year earlier and sole CEO since March — abruptly left, with founder Ryan Petersen returning to the top job; Clark said the departure was mutual, while FreightWaves' sources said the board voted to fire him, and the [Wall Street Journal](/organizations/wall-street-journal/) reported Clark was considering a run for governor of Texas ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/flexport-ceo-dave-clark-steps-down)). Petersen rescinded more than 75 signed job offers, the chief financial officer left, and in October Flexport cut roughly another 20% of staff — about 600 people — aiming to return to profitability by the end of 2024 with more than $1 billion in net cash ([Air Cargo News](https://www.aircargonews.net/freight-forwarder/2023/10/flexport-confirms-it-will-cut-its-global-workforce-by-20/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/26/flexport-may-lay-off-workers-yet-again/)). In January 2024 Shopify invested a further $260 million, the same month The Information reported a third ~20% cut was planned — a report Flexport declined to comment on ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/26/flexport-may-lay-off-workers-yet-again/)). Petersen, meanwhile, had become a partner at Founders Fund, the venture firm led by [Peter Thiel](/people/peter-thiel/) ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/flexport-ceo-dave-clark-steps-down)).

## The Convoy arc — and the AI lawsuit that followed

Flexport's most unusual deal of the downturn was Convoy. The Seattle truck-brokerage unicorn shut down in October 2023, and in November Flexport confirmed it would buy Convoy's technology — not the business or its liabilities — for what Petersen called a "modest" sum, keeping a small group of product and engineering staff ([The Loadstar](https://theloadstar.com/flexport-confirms-a-modest-sum-paid-for-convoy-tech/)). Rather than restarting Convoy's brokerage, Flexport relaunched the technology in April 2024 as the Convoy Platform, a neutral digital marketplace for freight brokers, and in July 2025 sold it to DAT Freight & Analytics; Petersen said that in 18 months Flexport had "improved the core technology platform, reengaged the market and significantly increased its value" ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/partnerships/2025/dat-acquire-convoy-platform-expand-freight-matching-network-capabilities/)). Buy, rebuild, sell — the whole arc took under two years, and it is the clearest evidence in Flexport's record of Petersen's stated strategy of keeping cash to ride out cycles and win "on some time horizon" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/26/ryan-petersen-flexport/)).

The Freightmate case tested a newer question: who owns the operational data used to build AI logistics software? In March 2025 Flexport sued Freightmate and its co-founders Bryan Lacaillade and Yingwei (Jason) Zhao, two former employees who had joined Flexport from Amazon in 2021, alleging they secretly formed the rival startup while still employed and that Zhao exfiltrated "tens of thousands" of sensitive documents, including source code ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/flexport-sues-freightmate-alleges-that-founders-stole-trade-secrets-to-launch-seattle-area-startup/)). The case settled in October 2026, days before trial, when a California federal court approved a stipulated judgment: Freightmate and both founders acknowledged acquiring and using some of about 2,000 confidential Flexport shipping documents, and Zhao acknowledged acquiring information including trade secrets, while the court made no findings of fact or determination of liability ([The Loadstar](https://theloadstar.com/freightmate-admits-using-flexport-docs-as-ai-trade-secrets-case-settles/)). The agreed facts say Zhao downloaded the documents in May 2024 while still employed and that small samples were run through ChatGPT to test extraction — but they do not establish that the documents trained Freightmate's AI models or were built into its products ([The Loadstar](https://theloadstar.com/freightmate-admits-using-flexport-docs-as-ai-trade-secrets-case-settles/)). An independent forensic examiner must report by 30 January 2027 on whether any Flexport data remains in Freightmate's software, while Freightmate may keep competing and selling its products ([The Loadstar](https://theloadstar.com/freightmate-admits-using-flexport-docs-as-ai-trade-secrets-case-settles/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What does Flexport do?**
Flexport is a technology-led freight forwarder and customs broker: it books ocean, air, truck and rail freight for businesses, handles customs and cargo insurance, and gives customers a cloud platform to track shipments and manage billing in one place ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/flexport-receives-935m-funding/)).

**Who founded Flexport?**
Ryan Petersen founded Flexport in 2013 in San Francisco, after importing scooters from China and co-founding ImportGenius, a search engine for public shipping manifests, in 2006; the company went through Y Combinator's Winter 2014 batch ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/26/ryan-petersen-flexport/)).

**What is Flexport worth?**
Its last published valuation was $8 billion, set by the $935 million Series E in February 2022; total funding then stood at over $2.2 billion. Flexport is private, and no later valuation has been verified from a fully-read source ([Reuters](https://gcaptain.com/flexport-valuation-doubles-to-8-billion-in-latest-funding-round/)).

**What happened to Convoy, the trucking startup?**
Convoy shut down in October 2023; Flexport bought its technology (not the business or liabilities) for a "modest" sum, relaunched it as the neutral Convoy Platform in April 2024, and sold it to DAT Freight & Analytics in July 2025 ([The Loadstar](https://theloadstar.com/flexport-confirms-a-modest-sum-paid-for-convoy-tech/); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/partnerships/2025/dat-acquire-convoy-platform-expand-freight-matching-network-capabilities/)).

**Is Flexport a public company?**
No. Flexport is privately held and has not announced an IPO in any source read for this profile; its funding has come from venture investors including SoftBank, Andreessen Horowitz, MSD Partners, Founders Fund and Shopify ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/flexport-receives-935m-funding/)).
