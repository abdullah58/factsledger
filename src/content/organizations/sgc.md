## Overview

SGC, short for Sportscard Guaranty Corporation, is a Boca Raton, Florida, company that authenticates and grades trading cards, sealing each assessed card in a tamper-evident holder with a label stating its condition grade. Founded in July 1998, it built its reputation on vintage cards, was bought in February 2024 by Collectors Holdings, the parent of rival grader [PSA](/organizations/professional-sports-authenticator/), and still grades under its own brand.

This profile is about that trading-card grading company only. It is not any other business, school, government body or organisation that happens to share the SGC initials, and it is not Beckett's former SCG operation. SGC grades sports and other trading cards: it authenticates a card, judges its condition, and seals it in a holder so that buyers and sellers can trade on a shared, third-party assessment rather than on a seller's own description ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)).

## From a 100-point scale to the industry standard

SGC opened its doors in July 1998, in the heart of what its owner Dave Forman called the grading revolution, when the home-run race and the rise of eBay were pushing third-party grading into the mainstream ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)). One of its first graders, Derek Grady, recalled that SGC was "really only the legitimate competition to PSA" when it opened and was "swamped immediately", growing from about three graders to 20 within months ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)). Forman himself had come to grading as a collector and dealer who sold mostly vintage PSA-graded cards in the 1990s, and he acquired SGC around 2003 ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)).

For its first 20 years SGC used a distinctive 1-100 grading scale rather than the 1-10 ladder its rivals used ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)). In 2018 it switched to the 1-10 range, with Forman saying the reason was simplification and making the scale easier for collectors to understand, and that the change was "almost universally well received" ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)). The holders changed at the same time, from a distinctive green to a black-and-white look that collectors nicknamed the "tuxedo" ([Cardlines](https://cardlines.com/sgc-grading-review/); [Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/collectibles/sgc-s-peter-steinberg-stepping-down-effective-immediately)). Half-point steps remained part of the system: a 2021 review submission came back graded 1.5, for example ([Cardlines](https://cardlines.com/sgc-grading-review/)).

SGC's reputation was made on vintage cards, especially cards printed before World War II, and it long marketed itself on fast turnaround and customer service ([Sports Collectors Daily](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/report-psa-parent-company-set-to-acquire-sgc/?fbclid=IwAR20PaCzu2MwB1V8l1PRALeKcxnnI3hGL3O2_71FX8dl3bQJhO4vQQqe-XQ_aem_AVyuOiVs9kQ_DdRVjkjbFWp6GsBfoovL_1IKO0Fv1qCABjenICultingPc7KnfHG4Rk)). But the vintage-only label was already out of date by 2020: Forman said the business by then graded more modern cards than vintage ones ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)). The pandemic boom accelerated that shift — SGC grew from 12 employees going into 2021 to more than 90, marketing itself as the grader with no backlog while PSA and Beckett paused submissions ([Cardlines](https://cardlines.com/sgc-grading-review/)). Its standing in the wider market was recognised beyond the hobby press, too: cards sold on [Alt](/organizations/alt/) had to be graded first by PSA, BGS or SGC before entering Alt's vault ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/11/collectible-card-trading-platform-alt-raises-75m-as-it-eyes-other-asset-categories-and-preps-its-mobile-launch/)).

## The Collectors acquisition and the boutique turn

On 29 February 2024, SGC announced that Collectors Holdings, the parent company of PSA, had acquired it ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/news/collectors-psa-acquire-card-grading-rival-sgc-)). SGC was to continue operating independently within the Collectors portfolio, led by its current management team, with president Peter Steinberg continuing to run day-to-day operations ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/news/collectors-psa-acquire-card-grading-rival-sgc-)). Steinberg's pledge was explicit: "SGC will remain an independent grading brand and no changes are contemplated", with customer service, turnaround time and grading standards unchanged ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/news/collectors-psa-acquire-card-grading-rival-sgc-)). Collectors chief executive Nat Turner framed SGC as a strong reputation for expertise, quality and value that would only add to Collectors' range of collecting services ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/news/collectors-psa-acquire-card-grading-rival-sgc-)). At the time SGC was still promoting a $15 service with a 5-10 business-day turnaround ([Sports Collectors Daily](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/report-psa-parent-company-set-to-acquire-sgc/?fbclid=IwAR20PaCzu2MwB1V8l1PRALeKcxnnI3hGL3O2_71FX8dl3bQJhO4vQQqe-XQ_aem_AVyuOiVs9kQ_DdRVjkjbFWp6GsBfoovL_1IKO0Fv1qCABjenICultingPc7KnfHG4Rk)).

That independence pledge lasted about seventeen months in its original form. On 24 July 2025 Steinberg resigned as president effective immediately, giving no specific reason beyond feeling it was "the right time", after seven years in the role ([Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/collectibles/sgc-s-peter-steinberg-stepping-down-effective-immediately); [Sports Collectors Daily](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/peter-steinberg-exits-as-sgc-president-as-collectors-resources-shift-to-psa/)). PSA president Ryan Hoge's role was expanded to oversee authentication and grading across Collectors, including PSA, SGC and coin grader PCGS, while SGC kept its own grading team and standards and still operated independently from PSA ([Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/collectibles/sgc-s-peter-steinberg-stepping-down-effective-immediately)).

The substance underneath that assurance had already changed. PSA had opened an office next door to SGC's Boca Raton headquarters, and the two companies had begun sharing resources, including grading staff, while SGC's typical 5-10 business-day turnarounds wavered ([Sports Collectors Daily](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/peter-steinberg-exits-as-sgc-president-as-collectors-resources-shift-to-psa/)). Hoge said Collectors wanted to "right size" SGC back toward its pre-COVID levels of 2017 to 2019 and position it as a boutique brand serving modern and vintage customers, with a decent chunk of SGC staff shifting to PSA because PSA was "drowning in all of this demand" and needed capacity for a daily intake of what is often 80,000 cards per day ([Sports Collectors Daily](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/peter-steinberg-exits-as-sgc-president-as-collectors-resources-shift-to-psa/)). He added that vintage grading would remain "a pillar" for SGC, that Collectors was "committed to the SGC brand", and that turnarounds would return to 10-15 days "pretty soon" ([Sports Collectors Daily](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/peter-steinberg-exits-as-sgc-president-as-collectors-resources-shift-to-psa/)). In other words, the information most grading guides still miss is that the SGC of 2025 is not the independent, fast-turnaround challenger of 2021: it is a deliberately smaller, vintage-anchored brand inside the same group as its largest rival, kept alive by customer submissions rather than by expansion.

## What this profile does not claim

- The price Collectors Holdings paid for SGC was not stated in any source read in full, so no figure is given.
- SGC's current legal or registered entity name was not verified from a fully-read source; this profile uses its trading name and the long form Sportscard Guaranty Corporation.
- Market-share figures are GemRate figures carried by trade outlets, not audited totals, and the 78% all-grading figure and the 76% TCG-only figure for PSA cover different scopes and must not be quoted interchangeably.
- Steinberg gave no specific reason for his July 2025 resignation beyond feeling it was "the right time"; no motive is inferred here.
- No current pricing or turnaround time is stated as a present-day fact, because grading prices and turnaround estimates change frequently and the most recent verified figures in the sources read are from 2024 and 2025.
- SGC-graded cards' resale values relative to PSA-graded equivalents are not stated as a general rule: a 2021 review found PSA premiums varying widely by era, from roughly 15-20% on 1970s cards to about double on some 1990s and modern cards, and that is a dated sample, not a market law ([Cardlines](https://cardlines.com/sgc-grading-review/)).
