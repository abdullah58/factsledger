## Overview

[Salesforce](/organizations/salesforce/) is the corporate venture capital arm of Salesforce, founded in 2009 by Marc Benioff and John Somorjai to back enterprise software companies. Funded solely by Salesforce, it has invested over $7 billion in more than 700 companies, guided 35 of them to initial public offerings and more than 190 to acquisitions, and committed $1 billion to artificial intelligence startups ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/about/); [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/ventures-deploys-ai-fund/?bc=OTH)).

The firm is based in [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/), California, with offices in [London](/places/london/), [New York](/places/new-york-city/) and Tokyo, and invests across the [United States](/places/united-states/), Europe, Israel, Japan, Korea, India and Australia ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/about/)). It describes its model as patient capital under an evergreen funding structure: it invests from seed to growth, with cheques from under $5 million to more than $50 million, thinking in years and decades rather than quarters ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/perspectives/the-long-game-how-salesforce-ventures-is-reimagining-vc/); [Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/about/)). Its stated themes are generative AI, data and infrastructure, security, and vertical and horizontal applications, plus new frontiers such as energy and space — and it generally does not invest in consumer software or hardware ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/about/)).

## Born of the financial crisis

Salesforce Ventures began in the depths of the 2009 financial crisis, when many of Salesforce's AppExchange partners — the independent software vendors building on its platform — were struggling to raise capital ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/perspectives/the-long-game-how-salesforce-ventures-is-reimagining-vc/)). Marc Benioff and John Somorjai, who led corporate development, saw strong businesses that could not get funded and decided to supply the capital themselves, starting with a team of two ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/perspectives/the-long-game-how-salesforce-ventures-is-reimagining-vc/)). The idea was a fund that offered capital and a foundation of support, not just money: portfolio companies would get customer introductions, early product feedback from enterprise executives and credibility from having Salesforce on their side ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/perspectives/the-long-game-how-salesforce-ventures-is-reimagining-vc/)). As enterprises moved their systems to the cloud, the ecosystem — and the fund — grew with it, from small cheques in AppExchange startups to leading large growth rounds ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/perspectives/the-long-game-how-salesforce-ventures-is-reimagining-vc/)).

## From ecosystem bets to late-stage investing

The early portfolio was built on companies extending Salesforce itself: DocuSign, Twilio and nCino were among the close ecosystem partners of the first years, and the firm still names DocuSign and nCino among its notable investments ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/about/)). The mandate then widened from ecosystem adjacency to category leadership, with later bets on data infrastructure and security such as Snowflake, Databricks and Wiz ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/about/)). At its fifteenth anniversary in November 2024 the firm said it had invested more than $6 billion in over 630 enterprise software businesses across 32 countries; by 31 July 2026 that had grown to more than $7 billion in over 700 companies ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/perspectives/the-long-game-how-salesforce-ventures-is-reimagining-vc/); [Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/about/)). The team grew from two people to 39 over the same fifteen years, and the firm's own account stresses that its mandate is to generate top-tier venture returns, not only strategic value for Salesforce ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/perspectives/the-long-game-how-salesforce-ventures-is-reimagining-vc/); [Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/about/)).

## The Snowflake moment

The clearest demonstration of the late-stage turn came at [Snowflake](/organizations/snowflake/)'s September 2020 listing, the largest software initial public offering ever at the time ([CEO Today](https://www.ceotodaymagazine.com/2020/09/warren-buffett-backed-snowflake-nets-largest-software-ipo-ever/)). Snowflake priced its shares at $120, sold 28 million of them and raised $3.38 billion at a valuation above $33 billion, with [Goldman Sachs](/organizations/goldman-sachs/) leading the offering ([CEO Today](https://www.ceotodaymagazine.com/2020/09/warren-buffett-backed-snowflake-nets-largest-software-ipo-ever/)). Berkshire Hathaway and Salesforce Ventures each pledged to buy $250 million of Snowflake's Class A stock in a private placement at the IPO price — a bet placed alongside Warren Buffett, who had famously avoided both technology stocks and IPOs ([CEO Today](https://www.ceotodaymagazine.com/2020/09/warren-buffett-backed-snowflake-nets-largest-software-ipo-ever/)). The placement shows the firm's dual character: a financial investor chasing returns at public-market scale, with a parent's platform giving it a reason to be at the table — the same combination behind its later AI bets ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/about/); [CEO Today](https://www.ceotodaymagazine.com/2020/09/warren-buffett-backed-snowflake-nets-largest-software-ipo-ever/)).

## The invest-then-acquire loop

Salesforce Ventures also functions as a scouting arm for its parent: of the more than 700 companies it has backed, Salesforce has acquired fewer than 5% ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/about/)). Two of those acquisitions show the pattern working end to end. Salesforce Ventures first invested in Airkit in 2020; Salesforce acquired it in 2023, and its founder Adam Evans is now executive vice president and general manager of Salesforce AI ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/perspectives/the-long-game-how-salesforce-ventures-is-reimagining-vc/)). The firm's own retrospective says the foundation for Agentforce, Salesforce's agentic AI product, was built at Airkit ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/perspectives/the-long-game-how-salesforce-ventures-is-reimagining-vc/)). The second case is Own Company, a data-protection provider and AppExchange partner since 2012 that Salesforce agreed to buy for about $1.9 billion in cash in September 2024 — net of the roughly 10% stake it already owned — completing the deal on 18 November 2024 ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2024/09/05/salesforce-signs-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-own-company/?bc=OTH)). The loop is selective by design: stay close to hundreds of companies, and buy the few that prove strategically central ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/about/)).

## The $1 billion AI fund

The AI fund is the largest single bet in the firm's history ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/salesforce-ventures-doubles-down-ai-investment/)). In March 2023 Salesforce Ventures launched a $250 million Generative AI Fund, initially backing [Anthropic](/organizations/anthropic/), Cohere, Hearth.AI and You.com, focused on responsible generative AI ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/12/salesforce-pledges-to-invest-500m-in-generative-ai-startups/)). Three months later it doubled the fund to $500 million, adding Humane and Tribble, and in September 2024 a second $500 million fund took total AI commitments to $1 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/12/salesforce-pledges-to-invest-500m-in-generative-ai-startups/); [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/salesforce-ventures-doubles-down-ai-investment/)). By October 2025 more than $850 million of that $1 billion was deployed across 35 AI-first companies whose combined valuations exceeded $270 billion, and the firm had increased its investment in over a quarter of its existing AI portfolio ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/ventures-deploys-ai-fund/?bc=OTH)). The portfolio spans model builders such as Anthropic and Cohere, application companies including [Runway](/organizations/runway/) and [ElevenLabs](/organizations/elevenlabs/), and newer bets on robotics and vertical AI such as Covecta, Dyna, OneVest and PointFive ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/ventures-deploys-ai-fund/?bc=OTH)).

## Blend: the fintech case study

The May 2019 investment in [Blend](/organizations/blend/) shows the same playbook applied to financial services ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/48996-salesforce-invests-in-blend-as-startup-shaves-weeks-off-mortgage-process/)). Salesforce Ventures made an undisclosed strategic investment in Blend, a digital lending software company founded in 2012, announced on 8 May 2019 and reported more widely on 10 May ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/48996-salesforce-invests-in-blend-as-startup-shaves-weeks-off-mortgage-process/); [National Mortgage Professional](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/70977/blend-receives-strategic-investment-salesforce-ventures)). Spencer Chavez, a principal at Salesforce Ventures, framed it as expanding Salesforce's mortgage-industry presence, arguing that the loan origination system and the digital lending platform were, alongside the customer relationship system, the pieces needed to move the mortgage workflow to the cloud ([National Mortgage Professional](https://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/70977/blend-receives-strategic-investment-salesforce-ventures)). Blend then had more than 130 banking customers, including [Wells Fargo](/organizations/wells-fargo/) and US Bank, together accounting for more than a quarter of the US mortgage market ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/48996-salesforce-invests-in-blend-as-startup-shaves-weeks-off-mortgage-process/)).

## Values and the Impact Fund

Salesforce Ventures presents its values work as a source of returns, not a side project: its retrospective argues that trust, equality and courage produce better decisions, and that backing overlooked founders is where outlier value accrues ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/perspectives/the-long-game-how-salesforce-ventures-is-reimagining-vc/)). The concrete vehicle is the Impact Fund, launched at $50 million in 2017 and doubled to $100 million in October 2020 to back cloud companies working on education and reskilling, climate action, diversity, equity and inclusion, and technology for nonprofits and foundations ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/10/06/salesforce-ventures-launches-100m-impact-fund-to-invest-in-cloud-startups-with-social-mission/)). The firm says the Impact Fund's portfolio companies facilitated $72.8 million in grants and donations in 2023 ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/perspectives/the-long-game-how-salesforce-ventures-is-reimagining-vc/)). It is also a founding member of Pledge 1%, and says more than 150 portfolio companies have donated 1% of their time, product, profits or equity, contributing over $2 billion in new philanthropy ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/perspectives/the-long-game-how-salesforce-ventures-is-reimagining-vc/)). John Somorjai serves as president and Paul Drews as managing partner ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/ventures-deploys-ai-fund/?bc=OTH)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Salesforce Ventures?** Salesforce Ventures is the corporate venture capital arm of Salesforce, founded in 2009 by Marc Benioff and John Somorjai ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/perspectives/the-long-game-how-salesforce-ventures-is-reimagining-vc/)). Funded solely by Salesforce, it invests from seed to growth in enterprise software, with over $7 billion deployed in more than 700 companies ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/about/)).

**How big is Salesforce Ventures' AI fund?** The AI commitment totals $1 billion: a $250 million Generative AI Fund launched in March 2023, doubled to $500 million three months later, plus a second $500 million fund in September 2024 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/12/salesforce-pledges-to-invest-500m-in-generative-ai-startups/); [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/salesforce-ventures-doubles-down-ai-investment/)). By October 2025 over $850 million was deployed across 35 AI-first companies ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/ventures-deploys-ai-fund/?bc=OTH)).

**When did Salesforce Ventures invest in Blend?** In May 2019, when it made an undisclosed strategic investment in the digital lending company, announced on 8 May 2019 ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/48996-salesforce-invests-in-blend-as-startup-shaves-weeks-off-mortgage-process/)). Global Venturing estimates the amount was likely about $14 million, inferred from Blend's cumulative funding totals, but the figure was never disclosed ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/blend-labs-lands-on-nyse-in-360m-ipo/)).

**Does Salesforce buy the companies its venture arm backs?** Rarely: Salesforce has acquired under 5% of the more than 700 companies in the portfolio ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/about/)). The notable exceptions are Airkit, first backed in 2020 and acquired in 2023, and Own Company, acquired for about $1.9 billion in 2024 ([Salesforce Ventures](https://salesforceventures.com/perspectives/the-long-game-how-salesforce-ventures-is-reimagining-vc/); [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2024/09/05/salesforce-signs-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-own-company/?bc=OTH)).
