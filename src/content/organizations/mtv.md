## Overview

MTV's end as a music channel was announced in December 2025, when Paramount Skydance began winding down MTV Music, MTV Hits, and the 80s and 90s channels in the UK, France, Germany, Poland, Australia, and Brazil — but the people who built the brand's music era say the real ending came two decades earlier. Former VJ Simone Angel called the early-2000s decision to split MTV Europe into regional subsidiaries and swap music for reality programming "the beginning of the end": "I was heartbroken when it started to split up into different regions. To me that was like the beginning of the end" ([AFP](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1180477/features/music/end-of-an-era-mtv-pulling-plug-on-global-music-channels)).

The channel began as a bet on a format most of the music industry barely took seriously. On August 1, 1981, just after midnight, executive John Lack said, "Ladies and gentlemen, rock and roll," and MTV aired its first video — the Buggles' "Video Killed the Radio Star," a knowingly ironic choice for a network built on the premise that video had not yet killed anything ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/MTV); [HISTORY](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/august-1/mtv-launches)). At launch it reached only households in parts of New Jersey; the VJ format was modeled on Top 40 radio, and record labels supplied promotional clips free of charge ([HISTORY](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/august-1/mtv-launches)). Video supply was thin at first, so the playlist ran shallow and repetitive — but when [Michael Jackson](/people/michael-jackson/)'s "Billie Jean" and "Beat It" proved MTV airtime could mint a superstar, the labels piled in and the format became a genuine commercial force ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/MTV)).

## Viacom buys it and changes the formula

In 1985, entertainment conglomerate Viacom Inc. purchased MTV Networks, the channel's parent, from Warner Communications — and the new ownership quickly remade the programming logic ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/MTV)). Videos were no longer played in free-form VJ shifts; they were repackaged into genre blocks such as *120 Minutes*, *Headbangers Ball*, and *Yo! MTV Raps* ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/MTV)). Then the videos themselves started losing airtime to game shows, reality programs, and animation — by the mid-1990s, non-music programming filled most of the daily schedule ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/MTV)).

The channel still produced culture-defining television. The MTV Video Music Awards began in 1984 and became a spectacle in their own right, and *Total Request Live*, debuting in 1998, turned the daily countdown into a ratings machine before its 2008 cancellation (it returned in 2017) ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/MTV); [HISTORY](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/august-1/mtv-launches)). On the reality side, *The Real World* (1992) set the template, followed by *Jackass* (2000), *The Osbournes* (2002), and *Jersey Shore* (2009) — shows that made MTV a pop-culture engine long after it had stopped being a music channel ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/MTV)). Animation had its own moment with *Beavis and Butt-Head* ([HISTORY](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/august-1/mtv-launches)). Meanwhile the adult-contemporary sibling VH1 had run since 1985, and the 1996 launch of MTV2 was explicitly meant to "recapture the spirit" of the original 1980s channel — but by 2005, MTV2 was carrying reality programming too ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/MTV)).

## The European chapter and the Glasgow EMAs

MTV Europe carried the music-channel brand abroad for decades, and its 2014 Europe Music Awards captured the channel at its celebrity zenith. On November 9, 2014, the 20th EMAs (the awards were founded in 1994) played the SSE Hydro in Glasgow, with [Nicki Minaj](/people/nicki-minaj/) hosting — and herself winning Best Hip-Hop for the second year running ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2014/11/10/nicki-minaj-ariana-grande-winners-at-mtv-emas/)). [Ariana Grande](/people/ariana-grande/) won Best Female and Best Song for "Problem" featuring Iggy Azalea; One Direction took three awards, including Best Pop and Best Live Act; the whole show was decided by fan vote ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2014/11/10/nicki-minaj-ariana-grande-winners-at-mtv-emas/)). Pollstar's account of Minaj as a "confident if conventional host" reads, in hindsight, like a snapshot of MTV's last genuinely global music era — before the regional split Angel and Hyman lamented finished the job.

At the height of the network's popularity, MTV's cultural footprint was, in Fairclough's word, "seismic": it brought both famous and up-and-coming artists into the homes of music fans around the world ([AFP](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1180477/features/music/end-of-an-era-mtv-pulling-plug-on-global-music-channels)). Moments like the premiere of Michael Jackson's "Thriller" video and Madonna's "Like a Virgin" performance at the first VMAs in 1984 shaped the cultural conversation, and Hyman recalled a time when MTV's power over fashion, film, and music in Britain and Europe was total ([AFP](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1180477/features/music/end-of-an-era-mtv-pulling-plug-on-global-music-channels)).

## The wind-down

ViacomCBS was renamed Paramount Global in 2022, and the 2025 completion of the Paramount Skydance merger gave the company a reason to audit its portfolio ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/MTV)). AFP reported on December 1, 2025 that Paramount Skydance was pulling the plug on the remaining international music channels by year-end — including MTV Music, MTV Hits, and the 80s and 90s channels — in the UK, France, Germany, Poland, Australia, and Brazil ([AFP](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1180477/features/music/end-of-an-era-mtv-pulling-plug-on-global-music-channels)). The numbers told the story: in 2001 MTV Music reached more than 10 million homes in Britain; by July 2025 it reached about 1.3 million UK households, per ratings firm Barb ([AFP](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1180477/features/music/end-of-an-era-mtv-pulling-plug-on-global-music-channels)). MTV HD UK was to continue with an entertainment focus, and some US music channels would stay — a small consolation for a brand that once defined an era ([AFP](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1180477/features/music/end-of-an-era-mtv-pulling-plug-on-global-music-channels/)). Hyman and Angel have been urging Paramount to make the archive tapes public, convinced "people still want their MTV," and Angel described the current state of affairs as MTV having been "on life support for such a long time" — adding, "now that they're actually threatening to pull the plug, we have all suddenly realised... this means too much to us" ([AFP](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1180477/features/music/end-of-an-era-mtv-pulling-plug-on-global-music-channels/)).

Fairclough's diagnosis is that the conditions that made MTV "revolutionary" simply "don't exist anymore": YouTube and TikTok have "completely refigured how we engage with music and images," and audiences now expect the immediacy and interactivity that rolling music videos on television cannot provide ([AFP](https://www.omanobserver.om/article/1180477/features/music/end-of-an-era-mtv-pulling-plug-on-global-music-channels/)).
