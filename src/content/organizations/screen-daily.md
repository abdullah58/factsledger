## Overview

**Screen Daily** (screendaily.com) is the online arm of *Screen International*, the British trade journal covering the international film business — described by the *Film Reference* encyclopedia as the key surviving UK film-trade publication, begun in its current form in 1975 ([Film Reference](http://www.filmreference.com/encyclopedia/Independent-Film-Road-Movies/Journals-and-Magazines-NEWS-WEEKLIES-NEWSPAPERS-ANDTRADE-JOURNALS.html)). It is published by Media Business Insight, which was sold in February 2015 by Top Right Group to a Mobeus Equity Partners-backed management buyout ([InPublishing](https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/broadcast-and-screen-international-sold-by-top-right-group-5840)). The outlet independently reported both the BBFC's October 2011 refusal of [The Bunny Game](/creative-works/the-bunny-game/) — including Trinity's Cannes acquisition of the film — and the *Human Centipede 2* 18-certificate outcome the same month ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/distribution/bbfc-bans-adam-rehmeiers-the-bunny-game-/5033304.article); [Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/distribution/human-centipede-ii-gets-18-certificate-in-uk-after-cuts/5033015.article)).

## From Screen International to Screen Daily

Screen International's lineage runs through a chain of UK trade papers — *Cinema TV Today* (1971–1975), *Today's Cinema*, *The Daily Cinema* and earlier titles — before the publication took its current form in 1975 ([Film Reference](http://www.filmreference.com/encyclopedia/Independent-Film-Road-Movies/Journals-and-Magazines-NEWS-WEEKLIES-NEWSPAPERS-ANDTRADE-JOURNALS.html)). It serves the global film business across finance, production, distribution and sales, and its digital edition, Screen Daily, carries real-time industry news. The publisher, Media Business Insight, also published the UK television title *Broadcast* and brands including Shots, ALF and BRAD; at the time of the 2015 sale it reported £11 million in turnover and 70 staff across the two magazine titles and the other brands ([InPublishing](https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/broadcast-and-screen-international-sold-by-top-right-group-5840)). The management buyout was led by existing directors Conor Dignam — a former *Broadcast* editor — and Alison Pitchford, with Mobeus providing debt and equity ([InPublishing](https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/broadcast-and-screen-international-sold-by-top-right-group-5840)). The same month, Screen hired former *Total Film* editor Matt Mueller as its new editor, succeeding Wendy Mitchell ([InPublishing](https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/broadcast-and-screen-international-sold-by-top-right-group-5840)). By its own advertising figures, the brand reaches 76,000 registered online users and an average print readership of 34,000 per issue ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/advertise-with-screen?blocktitle=LATEST-FILM-NEWS&contentID=40562)).

## The festival dailies and the BBFC beat

Screen's industry role is most visible at the major festivals, where it publishes daily editions. At Cannes it presents "its pre-eminent dailies" with festival and market news, reviews and screening information, alongside the monthly edition and daily updates on ScreenDaily.com — with its editorial and sales teams based at the Majestic hotel and events including the Marché du Film opening-night party ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/cannes/screen-lines-up-dailies-and-events-schedule-for-cannes/5013225.article)). That trade-press function is exactly what put Screen on the record in the 2011 censorship fights. On 13 October 2011 it reported that the BBFC had denied Adam Rehmeier's *The Bunny Game* a DVD release — noting the film had been picked up by Trinity for its DVD genre label Trinity X in Cannes that year — and quoted Trinity co-director Mark Sandell saying the company had expected cuts but not an outright ban, and was "considering its options" ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/distribution/bbfc-bans-adam-rehmeiers-the-bunny-game-/5033304.article)). Days earlier it had reported the contrast case: Eureka Entertainment's *Human Centipede 2* passing with an 18 certificate after cuts, with sales director Ian Sadler's statement on the four-month negotiation ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/distribution/human-centipede-ii-gets-18-certificate-in-uk-after-cuts/5033015.article)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Screen Daily?**
Screen Daily (screendaily.com) is the website and digital news arm of *Screen International*, the British trade journal covering the international film business. The publication in its current form dates to 1975 and is published by Media Business Insight.

**Who owns Screen International?**
Media Business Insight (MBI), a British B2B media company. In February 2015, Top Right Group sold MBI — publisher of *Screen International* and the TV title *Broadcast* — to a management buyout backed by the private equity firm Mobeus Equity Partners, in a deal believed to be worth more than £10 million.

**What did Screen Daily report about The Bunny Game?**
On 13 October 2011, Screen Daily reported that the BBFC had refused Adam Rehmeier's horror film a UK DVD release. Its report noted the film had been acquired by UK company Trinity for its Trinity X DVD genre label at Cannes that year, and quoted Trinity co-director Mark Sandell saying the company had expected possible cuts but not an outright ban.

**What is Screen's role at film festivals?**
Screen publishes daily editions at the Cannes Film Festival and Market — carrying deal news, reviews and screening information — alongside events and parties. Its Cannes operation runs from the Majestic hotel with dailies distributed at hotels and on the Croisette.

**How big is Screen's audience?**
By the publication's own advertising figures: 76,000 registered online users and an average print readership of 34,000 per issue.
