## Overview

SoftBank Corporation — SoftBank Corp., listed in Tokyo as 9434 — is the Japanese telecommunications company behind the SoftBank, Y!mobile and LINEMO mobile brands, and the group that runs Yahoo! JAPAN, the LINE messaging app and the PayPay cashless payment service ([SoftBank Corp.](https://www.softbank.jp/en/corp/ir/faq/)). It is a consolidated subsidiary of [SoftBank Group](/organizations/softbank-group/) Corp. and the group's primary operating company, but it is not the Group itself: the Group is a separate listed investment holding company, while SoftBank Corp. runs the stable domestic telecom business ([SoftBank Corp.](https://www.softbank.jp/en/corp/ir/faq/)). Reuters described it at its 2018 listing as Japan's third-largest mobile phone company, after NTT Docomo and KDDI ([Reuters](https://www.gadgets360.com/telecom/news/softbank-steps-closer-to-transformation-with-21-billion-mobile-ipo-1945982/amp)).

The company was established on 9 December 1986 as Railway Telecommunication Co., Ltd., became a SoftBank Group subsidiary in 2006, and took its current trade name in 2015 after merging its mobile, broadband and fixed-line arms ([SoftBank Corp.](https://www.softbank.jp/en/corp/ir/faq/)). Its headquarters is in Minato, Tokyo, in the Takeshiba building ([SoftBank News](https://www.softbank.jp/en/sbnews/entry/20210427_01)), and its president and CEO since 1 April 2021 is Junichi Miyakawa ([SoftBank News](https://www.softbank.jp/en/sbnews/entry/20210427_01)). In the first nine months of fiscal 2025 it reported about 41.5 million total mobile contracts and more than 32 million smartphone subscribers ([TelecomLead](https://telecomlead.com/5g/softbank-fy2025-results-highlight-revenue-growth-subscriber-strength-and-major-ai-investment-strategy-124472)).

## From Railway Telecommunication to SoftBank Mobile

SoftBank Corp.'s corporate history starts in 1986, not 1981 — the 1981 founding date belongs to the parent group. The company was established on 9 December 1986 as Railway Telecommunication Co., Ltd. ([SoftBank Corp.](https://www.softbank.jp/en/corp/ir/faq/)). Two decades later it entered the mobile business by acquiring Vodafone K.K., the Japan business of Vodafone Group, in 2006 ([SoftBank News](https://www.softbank.jp/en/sbnews/entry/20261014_01)). Vodafone K.K. announced on 18 May 2006 that it would change its name to SOFTBANK MOBILE Corp. on 1 October 2006, migrating the brand from Vodafone to SOFTBANK and rebranding 1,856 shops nationwide ([Vodafone K.K.](https://cdn.softbank.jp/en/corp/set/data/group/sbm/news/press/vodafone/pdf/20060518e-5.pdf)). At the end of April 2006 the carrier had over 15 million customers ([Vodafone K.K.](https://cdn.softbank.jp/en/corp/set/data/group/sbm/news/press/vodafone/pdf/20060518e-5.pdf)).

The new mobile operator moved fast to make the phone an internet device. In October 2006 it launched "Yahoo! Keitai", putting the Yahoo! JAPAN portal one button-press away on handsets, and in 2007 it introduced the "White Plan", offering free domestic calls between SoftBank phones for a basic monthly fee of 980 yen ([SoftBank News](https://www.softbank.jp/en/sbnews/entry/20261014_01)). The decisive moment came in 2008: SoftBank and [Apple](/organizations/apple/) announced on 9 June 2008 that the iPhone 3G would be available in Japan on 11 July ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2008/06/09Softbank-and-Apple-to-Bring-iPhone-3G-to-Japan-on-July-11/)), and SoftBank launched it exclusively in Japan that July ([SoftBank News](https://www.softbank.jp/en/sbnews/entry/20261014_01)). Apple said at the time that SoftBank Mobile had been number one in net additions for fiscal 2007 and had 18,952,800 subscribers at the end of May 2008 ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2008/06/09Softbank-and-Apple-to-Bring-iPhone-3G-to-Japan-on-July-11/)).

Network investment followed the subscriber growth. SoftBank launched its "SoftBank 4G" service in 2012 and began using the 900 MHz "Platinum Band" of spectrum the same year ([SoftBank News](https://www.softbank.jp/en/sbnews/entry/20261014_01)). The Y!mobile brand launched in 2014, and in April 2015 SoftBank BB Corp., SoftBank Telecom Corp. and Ymobile Corporation merged with SoftBank Mobile Corp., uniting mobile and fixed-line operations in one company ([SoftBank News](https://www.softbank.jp/en/sbnews/entry/20261014_01)). On 1 July 2015 the parent renamed itself SoftBank Group Corp. to mark its holding-company role, and SoftBank Mobile Corp. took the name SoftBank Corp. ([SoftBank Group Corp.](https://group.softbank/en/news/press/20150511_4)).

## The 2018 listing — and an outage days before it

The listing of SoftBank Corp. was designed to separate two different businesses for investors. Reuters reported on 12 November 2018 that the Group's domestic telecoms unit had received approval to list on the Tokyo Stock Exchange, with 1.6 billion shares offered at a tentative price of ¥1,500 each, raising ¥2.4 trillion (about $21.04 billion), and the parent retaining around two-thirds of the company ([Reuters](https://www.gadgets360.com/telecom/news/softbank-steps-closer-to-transformation-with-21-billion-mobile-ipo-1945982/amp)). The listing would mark the parent's transformation "from a mobile phone network provider … into one of the world's biggest technology investors" under Masayoshi Son ([Reuters](https://www.gadgets360.com/telecom/news/softbank-steps-closer-to-transformation-with-21-billion-mobile-ipo-1945982/amp)). The company's own FAQ states the same rationale from the other side: the Group operates as a strategic holding company pursuing global investment including the Vision Fund, while SoftBank Corp. focuses on stable domestic telecom operations, so listing lets each be valued on its own merits ([SoftBank Corp.](https://www.softbank.jp/en/corp/ir/faq/)).

The run-up to the listing was disrupted by one of the worst outages in the company's history. On Thursday 6 December 2018, SoftBank's LTE services under the SoftBank and Y!mobile brands, its "Ouchi-no-Denwa" fixed-line service and part of "SoftBank Air" were unavailable for over four hours across Japan ([TelecomTV](https://www.telecomtv.com/content/mobile/ericsson-apologises-for-software-fault-that-brought-down-cellular-networks-33522/)). The cause was software errors in Ericsson-made packet switches at the company's Tokyo and Osaka centres, and Ericsson said an expired certificate in two versions of its SGSN–MME software was the main issue identified in its initial root-cause analysis ([TelecomTV](https://www.telecomtv.com/content/mobile/ericsson-apologises-for-software-fault-that-brought-down-cellular-networks-33522/)). The same fault hit operators in a reported 11 countries, including O2 in the UK, and Ericsson chief executive Börje Ekholm apologised to customers and their customers ([TelecomTV](https://www.telecomtv.com/content/mobile/ericsson-apologises-for-software-fault-that-brought-down-cellular-networks-33522/)). The shares nevertheless listed on 19 December 2018, on the First Section of the Tokyo Stock Exchange, under code 9434 ([SoftBank Corp.](https://www.softbank.jp/en/corp/ir/faq/)).

## Business today: carrier, platforms and AI

SoftBank Corp. now describes itself as pursuing a "Beyond Carrier" strategy — using telecom as a foundation to expand into new fields ([SoftBank News](https://www.softbank.jp/en/sbnews/entry/20261014_01)). Its business is reported in five segments: Consumer (mobile and broadband), Enterprise (mobile, fixed-line, cloud, security, AI, IoT and digital marketing for companies), Distribution, Media & EC (Yahoo! JAPAN Shopping, ZOZOTOWN, Yahoo! JAPAN Auction and LINE advertising), and Financial (PayPay payments and smartphone securities services) ([SoftBank Corp.](https://www.softbank.jp/en/corp/ir/faq/)). In fiscal 2024, the year ended 31 March 2025, revenue was ¥6,544.3 billion, up 8% year on year, operating income was ¥989.0 billion, up 13%, and net income was ¥526.1 billion, up 8% ([SoftBank Corp.](https://www.softbank.jp/en/corp/ir/documents/presentations/fy2024/q4_earnings_summary/)). Segment operating income was led by Consumer at ¥530.4 billion, followed by Media & EC at ¥258.8 billion and Enterprise at ¥170.3 billion ([SoftBank Corp.](https://www.softbank.jp/en/corp/ir/documents/presentations/fy2024/q4_earnings_summary/)).

| --- | --- | --- |
| Consumer | ¥530.4 billion | Mobile and broadband for consumers |
| Media & EC | ¥258.8 billion | Yahoo! JAPAN, LINE media and e-commerce |
| Enterprise | ¥170.3 billion | Cloud, security, AI and IoT for companies |

The scale behind those figures is large: smartphone subscribers grew by a net 1.04 million in fiscal 2024 to a cumulative 31.77 million ([SoftBank Corp.](https://www.softbank.jp/en/corp/ir/documents/presentations/fy2024/q4_earnings_summary/)), and by the first nine months of fiscal 2025 total mobile contracts had reached about 41.5 million with smartphone churn at a low 1.41% and mobile ARPU of approximately ¥3,720 ([TelecomLead](https://telecomlead.com/5g/softbank-fy2025-results-highlight-revenue-growth-subscriber-strength-and-major-ai-investment-strategy-124472)). The multi-brand SoftBank, Y!mobile and LINEMO lineup — LINEMO launched in 2021 as an online-exclusive brand — covers different price segments ([TelecomLead](https://telecomlead.com/5g/softbank-fy2025-results-highlight-revenue-growth-subscriber-strength-and-major-ai-investment-strategy-124472); [SoftBank News](https://www.softbank.jp/en/sbnews/entry/20261014_01)).

Two platform businesses now matter as much as the network. PayPay, a joint venture launched in 2018 by SoftBank and Yahoo Japan Corporation (now LY Corporation), recorded gross merchandise value of ¥15.4 trillion in fiscal 2024, up 23% year on year, with consolidated EBITDA of ¥45.6 billion and operating income turning positive at over ¥30.0 billion; PayPay has started preparations for an IPO ([SoftBank Corp.](https://www.softbank.jp/en/corp/ir/documents/presentations/fy2024/q4_earnings_summary/)). Yahoo Japan became a consolidated subsidiary in June 2019 ([SoftBank News](https://www.softbank.jp/en/sbnews/entry/20261014_01)), and as of 31 March 2026 SoftBank Corp. has 271 subsidiaries, including the listed LY Corporation and PayPay Corporation ([SoftBank Corp.](https://www.softbank.jp/en/corp/ir/faq/)).

The newest direction is AI infrastructure. The company is developing "Sarashina mini", a Japanese-language large language model with 70 billion parameters, through its subsidiary SB Intuitions, and in March 2025 it acquired the land and buildings of Sharp Corporation's Sakai Plant for approximately ¥100.0 billion to build a large-scale AI data center ([SoftBank Corp.](https://www.softbank.jp/en/corp/ir/documents/presentations/fy2024/q4_earnings_summary/)). It is also in discussions with OpenAI on developing "Cristal intelligence", and plans "Sovereign Cloud" and "Sovereign AI" services from fiscal 2026 ([SoftBank Corp.](https://www.softbank.jp/en/corp/ir/documents/presentations/fy2024/q4_earnings_summary/)). In 2026 it launched "SoftBank Starlink Direct", connecting satellites directly to smartphones ([SoftBank News](https://www.softbank.jp/en/sbnews/entry/20261014_01)). The stat that captures the shift: in the first nine months of fiscal 2025 revenue was ¥5.2 trillion, up 8% year on year, with operating income of ¥884.1 billion — growth now driven as much by enterprise AI, fintech and media as by connectivity ([TelecomLead](https://telecomlead.com/5g/softbank-fy2025-results-highlight-revenue-growth-subscriber-strength-and-major-ai-investment-strategy-124472)).

SoftBank Corp. is also a customer of the compliance-software firm [Behavox](/organizations/behavox/). Behavox's June 2026 announcement of a $175 million investment from HPS Investment Partners, part of BlackRock, states that the relationship with SoftBank "extend[s] beyond capital to include SoftBank Investment Advisers in the UK and SoftBank Corporation in Japan as Behavox customers" ([Financial IT](https://financialit.net/news/fundraising-news/behavox-raises-175-million-hps-investment-partners-part-blackrock-accelerate)). The capital side of that relationship belongs to the parent: SoftBank's Vision Fund 2 invested $100 million in Behavox in 2020 ([Financial IT](https://financialit.net/news/fundraising-news/behavox-raises-175-million-hps-investment-partners-part-blackrock-accelerate)).

([SoftBank Corp.](https://www.softbank.jp/en/corp/ir/faq/)).
