## Overview

12Go is a multi-modal ground and sea transport booking platform incorporated in Singapore in 2012
and headquartered in Bangkok, Thailand, founded by Alexey Abolmasov
([Rooster PR](https://rooster.co.uk/newsroom/2021/07/16/world-first-ground-and-sea-transportation-group-launches-backed-with-46-million-funding/)).
It began as a resource for booking and comparing bus journeys in Thailand and grew into a network
that, by 2024, listed more than 4,000 transport operators covering nearly 50,000 routes across 26
Asian countries
([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/airasia-partners-12go-ground-transportation)).
In 2021 it joined the Bookaway Group, and in 2024 it partnered with [AirAsia](/organizations/airasia/) to add
ground-transport booking inside the AirAsia super app
([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/airasia-partners-12go-ground-transportation)).

## Origins: the "Agoda of ground transport"

Company accounts trace 12Go to 2012, when 12Go Asia Pte. Ltd. was incorporated in Singapore
([Rooster PR](https://rooster.co.uk/newsroom/2021/07/16/world-first-ground-and-sea-transportation-group-launches-backed-with-46-million-funding/)).
Trade-press dating varies: TechNode described the company in July 2014 as "founded in late 2013,"
while TTG Asia wrote that September that the platform had "launched earlier this year" — a pattern
consistent with a 2012 incorporation followed by a 2013–2014 commercial launch
([TechNode](http://technode.com/2014/07/28/singaporian-startup-12go/);
[TTG Asia](https://www.ttgasia.com/2014/09/05/12go-asia-navigates-complex-transport-landscape-with-multi-modal-e-ticketing/59248/)).

Founder and chief executive Alexey Abolmasov, described by TTG Asia as Singapore-based with the
main operation office in Bangkok, positioned the young company against a familiar reference point.
"We are pretty similar to Agoda, except that we operate in the transportation world," said Guido
Neil, the company's business development director
([TTG Asia](https://www.ttgasia.com/2014/09/05/12go-asia-navigates-complex-transport-landscape-with-multi-modal-e-ticketing/59248/)).
By mid-2014 the platform had signed up more than 100 operators across Thailand, Malaysia,
Singapore, Cambodia, Vietnam, Laos and Brunei, selling through its website and iOS and Android
apps, with payment by credit card, PayPal and even 7-Eleven counter service in Thailand
([TTG Asia](https://www.ttgasia.com/2014/09/05/12go-asia-navigates-complex-transport-landscape-with-multi-modal-e-ticketing/59248/)).
TechNode reported the company had raised US$300,000 from angel investors, employed 20 people in
Singapore, drew about 30,000 unique visitors a month — a fifth of its bookers then Chinese — and
already covered most of Thailand plus parts of Malaysia and Singapore
([TechNode](http://technode.com/2014/07/28/singaporian-startup-12go/)).

The early customer base was distinctive: "the vast majority of users are foreign backpackers from
Europe, America and Australia," Neil told TTG Asia
([TTG Asia](https://www.ttgasia.com/2014/09/05/12go-asia-navigates-complex-transport-landscape-with-multi-modal-e-ticketing/59248/)).
From the start 12Go worked as both a consumer and a business platform, giving partner operators
booking and travel data and integrating its system into the operators' own websites
([TechNode](http://technode.com/2014/07/28/singaporian-startup-12go/)).

## How the platform works

12Go is an intermediary, not a transport operator: travelers booking through it are buying seats
from underlying local bus, train or ferry companies, and local operators handle post-booking
servicing
([The Traveler](https://www.thetraveler.org/why-travelers-book-asia-transport-with-12go/);
[PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/airasia-partners-12go-ground-transportation)).
Its distinctive product is the combined ticket — for example, a single Bangkok-to-Koh Tao booking
aligning a night train, a station-to-pier transfer and a morning ferry — which removes the risk of
a missed connection for travelers who do not speak local languages
([The Traveler](https://www.thetraveler.org/why-travelers-book-asia-transport-with-12go/)).

That convenience carries a markup: 12Go typically adds a service fee of a few US dollars per
ticket — on the Bangkok–Chiang Mai night train, roughly $2–$5 above the station price for a
second-class air-conditioned berth — in exchange for advance online booking in English with
international card payment
([The Traveler](https://www.thetraveler.org/why-travelers-book-asia-transport-with-12go/)).
The platform separates its own service fee from bank or card charges at checkout
([The Traveler](https://www.thetraveler.org/why-travelers-book-asia-transport-with-12go/)).
Where rail systems still run on paper, 12Go uses partner offices in Bangkok and Chiang Mai for
ticket pickup, or hotel delivery for a small fee
([The Traveler](https://www.thetraveler.org/why-travelers-book-asia-transport-with-12go/)).

The middleman role has limits that show up in traveler reports: when a local train or ferry is
cancelled, the change is not always reflected promptly in 12Go's inventory, and some port staff
have been unfamiliar with digital vouchers
([The Traveler](https://www.thetraveler.org/why-travelers-book-asia-transport-with-12go/)).
The company itself frames the opportunity in stark terms: only about 10% of bus, ferry and train
tickets are booked digitally today, with most ground and sea transport still bought in person
([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/airasia-partners-12go-ground-transportation)).

## Bookaway Group ownership and the AirAsia deal

In 2021, Israel-based Bookaway — founded in 2017 in Tel Aviv by Noam Toister, David Yitzhaki and
Omer Chehmer — raised a $35 million Series B round and formed the Bookaway Group, with GetByBus of
Croatia and 12Go as its first two brands
([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/Bookaway-catches-35M-round-creates-group-of-ground-transport-platforms)).
Bookaway had previously raised $11 million in seed and Series A funding in 2018 from backers
including Aleph, Corner Ventures and Entrée Capital
([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/Bookaway-catches-35M-round-creates-group-of-ground-transport-platforms)).
The new group launched with access to more than 100,000 transport routes across 14,600 cities and
80 countries
([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/Bookaway-catches-35M-round-creates-group-of-ground-transport-platforms)).

In March 2022 the group acquired Argentina-based Plataforma 10, then raised a $35 million Series C
round led by Red Dot Capital Partners, taking total funding to $80 million; chief executive Noam
Toister said the group would keep acquiring with the aim of becoming the only player able to offer
complete ground-transport inventory worldwide
([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/Bookaway-35M-Series-C)).
By then the group's brands spanned Europe through GetByBus and Latin America through Plataforma
10, with local operators in 112 countries
([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/airasia-partners-12go-ground-transportation)).

In 2024, 12Go — described in the announcement as founded in 2012 and led by chief executive Alexey
Abolmasov — struck its largest third-party distribution deal: a partnership adding a "transport"
category inside AirAsia's super app, exposing 12Go's trains, buses, ferries and private transfers
to the app's roughly 10.6 million monthly users
([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/airasia-partners-12go-ground-transportation)).
AirAsia presented the move as making it the first super app to offer a complete journey experience
at scale; Abolmasov called it 12Go's largest deal opening its inventory to a third party and said
the company intended to make its booking API available across the tech and travel ecosystems
([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/airasia-partners-12go-ground-transportation)).

company's incorporation record and the majority of trade-press accounts, while noting that
TechNode wrote "founded in late 2013" and TTG Asia described a 2014 launch. It does not claim
independently audited scale figures; operator, route, country and review counts are
company-reported numbers carried by trade press and date to 2021–2024 statements. The 2024 date on
the AirAsia partnership is carried over from the previously validated profile; the announcement
article read for this rewrite does not print an explicit publication date. Pricing examples and
traveler-experience reports come from a 2026 travel blog and describe snapshots in time, not
current fares or a systematic survey of users. Finally, no FactsLedger profiles exist yet for
