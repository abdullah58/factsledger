## Overview

Ford Motor Company is an American automaker headquartered in Dearborn, [Michigan](/places/michigan/), founded on June 16, 1903 by Henry Ford, and the oldest of the Big Three American automakers ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/ford-motor-company/)). Ford put the world on wheels: its moving assembly line cut Model T assembly from over twelve hours to ninety minutes and dropped the car's price from over $800 to $345, turning the automobile from a rich man's toy into middle-class transport ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/ford-motor-company/)). Today it sells under the Ford and Lincoln brands, wholesaled about 4.395 million vehicles worldwide in 2025, and remains under the effective voting control of Henry Ford's descendants ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/ford-motor-company/)).

## The assembly line and the $5 day

Henry Ford was 39 when he launched the company with $28,000 of investor backing, after two earlier car companies — the Detroit Automobile Company and the Henry Ford Company — each failed within two years ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/ford-motor-company/)). The new company was incorporated with 12 investors and 1,000 shares, sold its first Model A on July 23, 1903, and had turned a $37,000 profit by October 1 of that year ([Ford Motor Company](https://ophelia.sdsu.edu:8443/ford/12-21-2014/company/history.html)).

The Model T, introduced on October 1, 1908, was the car that made Ford's name: 15 million were sold before production ceased in May 1927, making it one of the best-selling vehicles of all time ([Ford Motor Company](https://ophelia.sdsu.edu:8443/ford/12-21-2014/company/history.html)). The production method mattered more than any single model. In 1913 Ford introduced the integrated moving assembly line, cutting Model T chassis assembly from 12.5 hours to 1.5 hours ([Ford Motor Company](https://ophelia.sdsu.edu:8443/ford/12-21-2014/company/history.html)). A year later came the famous $5 day: double the prevailing factory wage, with the workday cut from nine hours to eight, so that the people building the cars could afford to buy them ([Ford Motor Company](https://ophelia.sdsu.edu:8443/ford/12-21-2014/company/history.html)). The method became known worldwide as Fordism, and by the 1930s there was one car for every five people in the [United States](/places/united-states/), with Americans accounting for over 80 percent of the world's car owners ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/ford-motor-company/)).

Scale followed. Construction began in 1917 on the River Rouge Complex, which became the largest integrated factory in the world within a decade, employing over 100,000 workers in the 1930s ([Ford Motor Company](https://ophelia.sdsu.edu:8443/ford/12-21-2014/company/history.html)). During World War II the company suspended civilian production to build planes, trucks and aircraft engines, and afterward it introduced the F-Series trucks in 1948 — a line that has been the best-selling vehicle in the US since 1982 ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/ford-motor-company/); [Ford Motor Company](https://ophelia.sdsu.edu:8443/ford/12-21-2014/company/history.html)).

## Icons, acquisitions, and the ones that got away

Ford's model history reads as a catalog of American car culture. It acquired Lincoln in 1922 and created the mid-range Mercury brand in 1938, discontinued in 2011 ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/ford-motor-company/)). The Thunderbird (1954–2005) carved out the personal-luxury niche, the Mustang, first sold in 1964, created the pony-car market, and the Edsel (1957–60) failed so visibly that its name entered the language as a synonym for failure ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/ford-motor-company/)). The Explorer, launched in 1990, helped expand the modern SUV market, and the Lincoln Navigator followed in 1998 ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/ford-motor-company/)). On the track, three Ford GT40 Mk. IIs swept the podium at the 24 Hours of Le Mans in 1966, the first American manufacturer to win the race, starting four consecutive victories ([Ford Motor Company](https://ophelia.sdsu.edu:8443/ford/12-21-2014/company/history.html)).

The company's attempts to buy its way into luxury mostly failed. It bought Volvo in 1999 and sold it in 2010 to the Chinese manufacturer [Geely](/organizations/geely/), and acquired Jaguar in 1989 and Land Rover in 2000 before selling both to India's Tata Motors in 2008 ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/ford-motor-company/)). The 1956 IPO — the largest in history at the time — came about because the Ford Foundation, endowed with nearly 90 percent of Ford's nonvoting stock at Henry Ford's death in 1947, decided to divest and diversify ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/ford-motor-company/)). Yet the family never lost control: although Henry Ford's descendants own less than 2 percent of the outstanding shares, a special class of stock has kept effective voting control — and leadership roles — in family hands throughout the company's existence ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/ford-motor-company/)).

## The bailout Ford didn't take

The defining modern chapter is the financial crisis. Alan Mulally became president and CEO in 2006 and launched the One Ford plan, uniting the global enterprise behind a single team and culture ([Ford Motor Company](https://ophelia.sdsu.edu:8443/ford/12-21-2014/company/history.html)). When the crisis hit, Ford weathered 2008 as the only American automaker to avoid receiving a government bailout loan, while [General Motors](/organizations/general-motors/) and Chrysler took federal help and went through bankruptcy ([Ford Motor Company](https://ophelia.sdsu.edu:8443/ford/12-21-2014/company/history.html)). Ford kept investing in product through the downturn, and the aluminum-bodied 2015 F-150 — cutting up to 750 pounds and generating more than 100 patents — was one result ([Ford Motor Company](https://ophelia.sdsu.edu:8443/ford/12-21-2014/company/history.html)).

In 2022 Ford restructured into three divisions — Ford Blue for combustion and hybrid vehicles, Ford Model e for electric vehicles, and Ford Pro for commercial vehicles and services ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/ford-motor-company/)). The split made the cost of the EV transition visible, and in 2024 Ford adjusted its electrification strategy, planning hybrid technology for some future three-row SUVs instead of building them as all-electric ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/ford-motor-company/)). In the cabin, Ford was an early voice-tech adopter: SYNC, offered in 2008 models, was a hands-free voice-activated system built in partnership with Microsoft ([Ford Motor Company](https://ophelia.sdsu.edu:8443/ford/12-21-2014/company/history.html)). Today [Cerence](/organizations/cerence/) — spun off from [Nuance Communications](/organizations/nuance-communications/) on October 1, 2019 — counts Ford among the nearly all major automakers its voice technology supports, in nearly 300 million vehicles as of 2020 ([CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2020/10/08/nio-cerence-collaboration-on-voice-tech/)).

## 2025: record revenue, an $8.2 billion loss

Ford's 2025 results tell two stories at once. Full-year revenue reached a record $187.3 billion, the fifth consecutive year of growth, but the company reported a net loss of $8.2 billion — against a $5.9 billion profit in 2024 — reflecting special items tied to EV program cancellations and impairments, with adjusted EBIT of $6.8 billion ([StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/F/8-k-ford-motor-co-reports-material-event-fa52ead3a25f.html)). Wholesale units fell 2 percent to 4,395,000 ([StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/F/8-k-ford-motor-co-reports-material-event-fa52ead3a25f.html)).

The US sales picture was stronger than the global one. Ford's total US sales climbed 6.0 percent in 2025 to 2,204,124 vehicles, with market share reaching 13.2 percent, its best annual sales since 2019 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/37996/000003799626000002/exhibit99q42025salesrele.htm)). The F-Series sold 828,832 trucks, up 8.3 percent — its 49th consecutive year as America's best-selling truck and its 44th as America's best-selling vehicle — while hybrids set a record at 228,072 vehicles, up 21.7 percent, and pure EVs totaled 84,113 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/37996/000003799626000002/exhibit99q42025salesrele.htm)). The company is led by president and CEO Jim Farley, with William Clay Ford Jr. as executive chairman, and employs about 170,000 people worldwide ([StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/F/8-k-ford-motor-co-reports-material-event-fa52ead3a25f.html); [EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/ford-motor-company/)).

## FAQ

**When was Ford founded?**
Ford Motor Company was incorporated on June 16, 1903, by Henry Ford and 11 associates in [Detroit](/places/detroit/), Michigan; the first Model A sold on July 23, 1903 ([Ford Motor Company](https://ophelia.sdsu.edu:8443/ford/12-21-2014/company/history.html); [U.S. Government Publishing Office](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-108hres100eh/html/BILLS-108hres100eh.htm)).

**Did Ford take a government bailout in 2008?**
No. Ford was the only American automaker to avoid a government bailout loan during the 2008 financial crisis, while General Motors and Chrysler received federal help ([Ford Motor Company](https://ophelia.sdsu.edu:8443/ford/12-21-2014/company/history.html)).

**What is Ford's best-selling vehicle?**
The F-Series pickup. It sold 828,832 trucks in the US in 2025, its 49th straight year as America's best-selling truck and 44th as America's best-selling vehicle overall ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/37996/000003799626000002/exhibit99q42025salesrele.htm)).

**How many vehicles did Ford sell in 2025?**
Ford wholesaled about 4.395 million vehicles worldwide and sold 2,204,124 in the US, up 6.0 percent for its best US year since 2019 ([StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/F/8-k-ford-motor-co-reports-material-event-fa52ead3a25f.html); [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/37996/000003799626000002/exhibit99q42025salesrele.htm)).

**Who runs Ford today?**
Jim Farley is president and CEO, and Henry Ford's great-grandson William Clay Ford Jr. is executive chairman; the Ford family retains effective voting control through a special share class despite owning under 2 percent of the shares ([StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/F/8-k-ford-motor-co-reports-material-event-fa52ead3a25f.html); [EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/ford-motor-company/)).
