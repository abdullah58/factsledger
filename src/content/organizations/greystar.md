## Overview

Greystar Real Estate Partners is the largest apartment company in the [United States](/places/united-states/): in 2024 it became the first firm ever to top the National Multifamily Housing Council's lists for apartment owner, manager and developer at the same time, owning 108,566 homes, managing 798,272 apartments and starting 9,151 new ones ([National Multifamily Housing Council](https://www.nmhc.org/news/press-release/2024/2024-nmhc-50-underscores-challenges-over-past-year-but-projects-cautious-optimism-for-2024/); [Multifamily Dive](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/NMHC-Top-Greystar-Morgan-MAA-Asset-Living-RPM-Living/712568/)). Founded by Bob Faith in Houston in 1993 and now headquartered in Charleston, South Carolina, it is a private company that manages, invests in, develops and builds rental housing ([Greystar](https://www.greystar.com/contact-us/our-people/bob-faith); [Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/power-series/power-100-2024/player/bob-faith/)).

That scale is the story of this profile, because it cuts both ways. Greystar managed 798,272 apartments in the United States as of 1 January 2024 — more than 500,000 more than the second-largest manager, Asset Living, at 291,322 — which made it the natural test case when regulators turned on algorithmic rent-setting and hidden fees ([Multifamily Dive](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/NMHC-Top-Greystar-Morgan-MAA-Asset-Living-RPM-Living/712568/); [Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/power-series/power-100-2024/player/bob-faith/)). In 2025 alone it settled three separate government and private cases for a combined $81 million, while admitting wrongdoing in none of them ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/multifamily/greystar-reaches-deal-with-doj-will-cooperate-in-realpage-antitrust-case-130508); [Multifamily Dive](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/realpage-class-action-lawsuit-settlement/802392/); [Oregon Department of Justice](https://www.doj.state.or.us/media-home/news-media-releases/ag-rayfield-announces-settlement-w-largest-u-s-landlord-over-price-fixing-scheme/); [Colorado Attorney General](https://coag.gov/press-releases/weiser-ftc-announce-24m-settlement-with-greystar/)). The concentration matters beyond Greystar itself: the NMHC 50 owners collectively hold nearly 10% of all United States apartment stock, and its 50 managers oversee about a fifth of the nation's apartments, so the largest firm's practices set the pattern for the industry ([National Multifamily Housing Council](https://www.nmhc.org/news/press-release/2024/2024-nmhc-50-underscores-challenges-over-past-year-but-projects-cautious-optimism-for-2024/)). Growth was still continuing when the rankings were published: Greystar was set to pick up a further 38,000 units by assuming Wood Partners' management operations, and Faith said the company would "remain focused on smart, sustainable growth" ([Multifamily Dive](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/NMHC-Top-Greystar-Morgan-MAA-Asset-Living-RPM-Living/712568/)). About three-fourths of its business remains in US multifamily, with the rest spread across student housing, active-adult and build-to-rent housing in Europe, East Asia and Australia ([Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/power-series/power-100-2024/player/bob-faith/)).

## Built by acquisition: from 9,000 units to the triple crown

Bob Faith founded Greystar in 1993 after a career at the Trammell Crow Company, where he became a partner in Charlotte, and after co-founding Starwood Capital Partners in 1991; he later served as South Carolina Secretary of Commerce from 2002 to 2006 ([Greystar](https://www.greystar.com/contact-us/our-people/bob-faith)). The company grew from managing 9,000 units through the acquisition of its predecessor Greystone, and then by buying other managers outright: JPI Management Services in 2008, Riverstone Residential Group in 2014, Monogram Residential Trust in 2017 and student-housing firm EdR in 2018 ([Greystar](https://www.greystar.com/contact-us/our-people/bob-faith); [National Apartment Association](https://naahq.org/news/greystar-acquires-alliance-residential-property-management-business)). In June 2020 it acquired Alliance Residential Company's property management business — 500 multifamily properties and nearly 130,000 primarily Class A units across 21 states — taking it to more than 660,000 units, over 2,400 communities and nearly 19,000 team members ([National Apartment Association](https://naahq.org/news/greystar-acquires-alliance-residential-property-management-business)). In May 2023 it launched Ltd. by Greystar for the "missing middle" between luxury and subsidised housing, with 708 units built by the time of its April interview ([Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/power-series/power-100-2024/player/bob-faith/)).

## The RealPage cases: three settlements in one year

In October 2022 ProPublica reported on RealPage's YieldStar software, which recommended a price for every available unit; former RealPage executives told reporter Heather Vogell that typically about 90% of its suggestions were adopted, and company marketing boasted of helping landlords beat the market by 3% to 7% ([NPR](https://www.publicradioeast.org/2022-10-17/this-computer-software-might-have-told-your-landlord-they-could-raise-your-rent)). In August 2024 the Justice Department sued RealPage, joined by eight state attorneys general, alleging landlords shared non-public, competitively sensitive information to train and run the pricing software, in violation of the Sherman Act; Greystar was later added as a defendant, alongside firms including [Blackstone](/organizations/blackstone/)'s LivCor, Camden Property Trust and Cushman & Wakefield ([JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/post/contentViewerEmbed.aspx?fid=e743796d-9dd8-4de8-baa5-65e6f7fcfa11); [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/multifamily/greystar-reaches-deal-with-doj-will-cooperate-in-realpage-antitrust-case-130508)). In August 2025 Greystar reached a non-monetary consent decree with the Justice Department: no fine and no admission of wrongdoing, but it agreed to stop using pricing software that relies on competitors' data, stop sharing rental data with rivals, skip RealPage-hosted meetings of competing landlords, and accept a court-appointed monitor if it uses an uncertified third-party pricing tool ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/multifamily/greystar-reaches-deal-with-doj-will-cooperate-in-realpage-antitrust-case-130508); [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/post/contentViewerEmbed.aspx?fid=e743796d-9dd8-4de8-baa5-65e6f7fcfa11)).

## Hidden fees, a crane verdict, and what scale means

In October 2025 Greystar agreed to pay $50 million — the largest share — of 26 preliminary class-action settlements worth more than $141.8 million; renters had alleged managers shared information with RealPage and coordinated rents, and the companies denied wrongdoing and agreed to stop feeding RealPage non-public data ([Multifamily Dive](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/realpage-class-action-lawsuit-settlement/802392/)). In November 2025 nine states settled with Greystar for $7 million on allegations; it was described as the largest landlord in the country, managing nearly 950,000 rental units nationwide ([Oregon Department of Justice](https://www.doj.state.or.us/media-home/news-media-releases/ag-rayfield-announces-settlement-w-largest-u-s-landlord-over-price-fixing-scheme/)). Then in December 2025 the Federal Trade Commission and Colorado settled for $24 million — $23 million for consumer refunds, $1 million to Colorado — over allegations Greystar had since 2019 advertised low rents while mandatory fees for pest control, valet trash, package concierge and utility administration surfaced only late, often after a non-refundable application fee ([Colorado Attorney General](https://coag.gov/press-releases/weiser-ftc-announce-24m-settlement-with-greystar/)). A second batch in May 2026, worth $218 million, brought the private total near $360 million; Greystar's $50 million was in the first ([Multifamily Dive](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/realpage-settlement-algorithmic-pricing/820745/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who founded Greystar?** Bob Faith founded Greystar Real Estate Partners in Houston, Texas, in 1993, and remains its Founder, Chairman and CEO ([Greystar](https://www.greystar.com/contact-us/our-people/bob-faith)). Before that he worked at the Trammell Crow Company from 1986, became a partner in Charlotte, co-founded Starwood Capital Partners in 1991, and later served as South Carolina Secretary of Commerce from 2002 to 2006 ([Greystar](https://www.greystar.com/contact-us/our-people/bob-faith)).

**How big is Greystar?** As of 1 January 2024 it owned 108,566 apartments, managed 798,272 and started 9,151 in development — No. 1 in the United States on all three NMHC lists ([National Multifamily Housing Council](https://www.nmhc.org/news/press-release/2024/2024-nmhc-50-underscores-challenges-over-past-year-but-projects-cautious-optimism-for-2024/)). Globally, the company reports managing over one million units and beds and operating about $350 billion of real estate in more than 265 markets ([Greystar](https://www.greystar.com/contact-us/our-people/bob-faith)).

**What did Greystar admit in the RealPage settlements?** Nothing. Its Justice Department consent decree was non-monetary and contained no admission of wrongdoing, and the class-action and nine-state settlements likewise involved no admission ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/multifamily/greystar-reaches-deal-with-doj-will-cooperate-in-realpage-antitrust-case-130508); [Multifamily Dive](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/realpage-class-action-lawsuit-settlement/802392/)). Greystar said it believed its use of RealPage software complied with the law ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/multifamily/greystar-reaches-deal-with-doj-will-cooperate-in-realpage-antitrust-case-130508)).

**What were the hidden fees?** The FTC and Colorado alleged Greystar advertised a low base rent, then charged mandatory recurring fees — for pest control, valet trash, package concierge, utility administration and certain amenities — that tenants often saw only at the lease stage ([Colorado Attorney General](https://coag.gov/press-releases/weiser-ftc-announce-24m-settlement-with-greystar/)). The December 2025 settlement requires upfront disclosure of the total monthly price ([Colorado Attorney General](https://coag.gov/press-releases/weiser-ftc-announce-24m-settlement-with-greystar/)).

**What happened in the Dallas crane case?** In June 2019 a tower crane at Greystar's Gabriella Tower development collapsed into the neighbouring Elan City Lights complex, killing 29-year-old Kiersten Smith and injuring five others ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/construction-development/jury-awards-860m-to-family-of-woman-who-died-in-dallas-crane-collapse-118704)). In April 2023 a Dallas County jury awarded her family more than $860 million, finding Greystar bore some responsibility and not finding the crane owner liable ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/construction-development/jury-awards-860m-to-family-of-woman-who-died-in-dallas-crane-collapse-118704)).

This profile does not claim Greystar admitted wrongdoing in any RealPage settlement: the Justice Department decree was non-monetary with no admission, and the class-action and state settlements likewise contain no admission ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/multifamily/greystar-reaches-deal-with-doj-will-cooperate-in-realpage-antitrust-case-130508); [Multifamily Dive](https://www.multifamilydive.com/news/realpage-class-action-lawsuit-settlement/802392/)). It does not claim the settlements were finally approved by a court: each was described as proposed or subject to approval at filing, and final approval was not verified from a fully-read source. It does not state how much of the $860 million crane verdict was actually paid, or whether it was reduced on appeal, because that was not verified ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/construction-development/jury-awards-860m-to-family-of-woman-who-died-in-dallas-crane-collapse-118704)). It does not settle whether Greystar's top-manager streak was 13 or 14 years — sources disagree — and it does not present the company's global figures (over one million units/beds, about $350 billion operated) as independently verified, since they come from Greystar itself ([Greystar](https://www.greystar.com/contact-us/our-people/bob-faith)). It also does not state Greystar's assets under management or headcount, because the figures in reference entries were not re-verified from a fully-read source here.
