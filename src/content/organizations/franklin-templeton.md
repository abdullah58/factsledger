## Overview

Franklin Templeton — legally Franklin Resources, Inc., listed on the New York Stock Exchange as BEN — is a global investment management organisation founded in New York in 1947 by Rupert H. Johnson Sr. and headquartered in San Mateo, California since 1973, with US$1.58 trillion in assets under management as of February 2025 and clients in more than 150 countries ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/franklin-resources-fund-family-driven-innovation-2014-04-23); [Franklin Templeton](https://www.franklintempleton.lu/press-releases/news-room/2025/franklin-templeton-extends-putnam-investment-capabilities-to-international-clients)). It grew by acquisition — Templeton in 1992, Legg Mason in 2020, Putnam in 2024 — and in 2018 it joined [Axoni](/organizations/axoni/)'s Series B as an investor ([Finadium](https://finadium.com/hsbc-invests-in-enterprise-blockchain-fintech-axoni/)).

The firm's name honours Benjamin Franklin, whom its founder admired for frugality and prudence in savings and investments, and that conservative instinct shaped its early decades: it began as a bond house, survived the 1987 crash better than most because of its fixed-income tilt, and only then diversified into equities ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/franklin-resources-fund-family-driven-innovation-2014-04-23)). Its most recent chapter is darker — a multi-year trade-allocation scandal at its Western Asset subsidiary that ended in a US$100 million penalty and a guilty plea in 2026 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-103-sec-seeks-final-judgment-against-former-western-asset-co-cio-ken-leech-cherry-picking-case)).

## A bond house that bought its way global

Franklin's growth was extraordinary: from 1982 to 1986 its assets under management nearly or more than doubled every year, and by 2014 it managed US$508 billion, sixth among fund firms ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/franklin-resources-fund-family-driven-innovation-2014-04-23)). It innovated early — one of the first Ginnie Mae bond funds in 1970 and one of the first national tax-exempt bond funds in 1977 — and its Franklin U.S. Government Securities fund was the world's largest fund from March 1985 to June 1989 ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/franklin-resources-fund-family-driven-innovation-2014-04-23)). Going public in 1971 and moving headquarters to San Mateo in 1973, through the Winfield & Co. merger, put it on the West Coast as southern California grew, and its California muni-bond franchise followed ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/franklin-resources-fund-family-driven-innovation-2014-04-23)). The October 1992 Templeton acquisition bolted a global equity franchise onto a bond house, combining US$69 billion of Franklin assets with US$20 billion at Templeton and giving Franklin the home of the Templeton Growth Fund, opened in 1954 and the longest-running US fund focused on global investing ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/franklin-resources-fund-family-driven-innovation-2014-04-23)).

## Legg Mason, Putnam — and a family firm

The acquisitions kept coming, each one widening the platform. The 1996 purchase of Heine Securities added the Mutual Series funds and a deep-value approach from noted fund manager Michael Price, and between the Templeton and Heine deals Franklin's stock gained 118% against 68% for the S&P 500 ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/franklin-resources-fund-family-driven-innovation-2014-04-23)). In August 2020 Franklin Templeton completed its largest transaction ever, buying Legg Mason for US$4.5 billion in cash and assuming about US$2 billion of its debt, creating a group with a combined US$1.4 trillion in assets under management ([The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/franklin-templeton-completes-4-5-billion-legg-mason-takeover/)). Jenny Johnson, who had become chief executive in November 2019 after her brother Gregory Johnson moved to executive chairman, said the strategic rationale had only strengthened since the February announcement ([The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/franklin-templeton-completes-4-5-billion-legg-mason-takeover/)). Then, on 1 January 2024, Franklin Templeton completed its acquisition of Putnam Investments, a Boston-based active equity manager with roots back to 1937, and began extending Putnam's strategies to international clients ([Franklin Templeton](https://www.franklintempleton.lu/press-releases/news-room/2025/franklin-templeton-extends-putnam-investment-capabilities-to-international-clients)).

## The Western Asset scandal

In November 2024 the SEC charged Stephen Kenneth Leech, ex-co-chief investment officer of Western Asset Management, alleging that from January 2021 through October 2023 he delayed allocating trades until after futures markets set daily settlement prices, steering hundreds of millions of dollars in first-day gains to favoured portfolios and similar losses to disfavoured ones ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-103-sec-seeks-final-judgment-against-former-western-asset-co-cio-ken-leech-cherry-picking-case)).

In June 2026 Western Asset paid a US$100 million civil penalty, a Fair Fund was set up for harmed investors, and Leech pleaded guilty in Manhattan federal court to obstruction of justice over false SEC testimony ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-103-sec-seeks-final-judgment-against-former-western-asset-co-cio-ken-leech-cherry-picking-case)). The SEC called the conduct "an egregious breach of fiduciary obligations to their clients", and the October 2026 final judgment added a US$3 million penalty against Leech, an officer-and-director bar and a permanent antifraud injunction, bringing total repayments to US$103 million — the measurable cost of a supervision failure at a giant fixed-income manager ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-103-sec-seeks-final-judgment-against-former-western-asset-co-cio-ken-leech-cherry-picking-case)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was Franklin Templeton founded?**
Franklin Templeton was founded in New York City in 1947 by Rupert H. Johnson Sr. as Franklin Distributors, Inc., named after Benjamin Franklin for his frugality and prudence in savings and investments ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/franklin-resources-fund-family-driven-innovation-2014-04-23)). It went public in 1971, and its headquarters moved to San Mateo, California, in 1973 ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/franklin-resources-fund-family-driven-innovation-2014-04-23)).

**How did Franklin Templeton get its name?**
Franklin acquired Templeton, Galbraith & Hansberger in October 1992, combining US$69 billion of Franklin assets with US$20 billion at Templeton and adding Sir John Templeton's global-investing expertise ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/franklin-resources-fund-family-driven-innovation-2014-04-23)). The deal made it the world's largest publicly traded fund company at the time and added the Templeton Growth Fund, opened in 1954 ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/franklin-resources-fund-family-driven-innovation-2014-04-23)).

**How big is Franklin Templeton?**
It had US$1.58 trillion in assets under management as of 28 February 2025, serves clients in over 150 countries, and employs more than 1,500 investment professionals ([Franklin Templeton](https://www.franklintempleton.lu/press-releases/news-room/2025/franklin-templeton-extends-putnam-investment-capabilities-to-international-clients)). Its specialist managers cover fixed income, equity, alternatives and multi-asset strategies, and its Putnam acquisition closed on 1 January 2024 ([Franklin Templeton](https://www.franklintempleton.lu/press-releases/news-room/2025/franklin-templeton-extends-putnam-investment-capabilities-to-international-clients)).

**Did Franklin Templeton invest in Axoni?**
Yes. Franklin Templeton Investments was on the investor list for Axoni's 2018 Series B, which brought the round to US$36 million and total funding to US$59 million ([Finadium](https://finadium.com/hsbc-invests-in-enterprise-blockchain-fintech-axoni/)). It joined [HSBC](/organizations/hsbc/), [Goldman Sachs](/organizations/goldman-sachs/), [Andreessen Horowitz](/organizations/andreessen-horowitz/), Citi, [Coatue](/organizations/coatue/), J.P. Morgan, Wells Fargo and [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/) on the investor list ([Finadium](https://finadium.com/hsbc-invests-in-enterprise-blockchain-fintech-axoni/)).

**What happened at Western Asset?**
The SEC alleged ex-co-CIO Stephen Kenneth Leech cherry-picked trade allocations from 2021 to 2023; Western Asset paid a US$100 million penalty in June 2026, Leech pleaded guilty to obstruction, and an October 2026 judgment brings total repayments to harmed investors to US$103 million ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-103-sec-seeks-final-judgment-against-former-western-asset-co-cio-ken-leech-cherry-picking-case)).
