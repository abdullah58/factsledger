## Overview

Riverview Health Westfield Hospital is a 16-bed community hospital at 17600 Shamrock Blvd in Westfield, Indiana, operated by Riverview Health, a hospital system owned by Hamilton County ([Riverview Health](https://www.riverview.org/location/riverview-health-westfield-hospital?utm_source=local-listing&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=website-link); [Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/62694-riverview-embraces-health-care-free-for-all)). This is Westfield, Indiana — not New Jersey — and the hospital should not be confused with any similarly named facility in another state. It opened in 2018 as Westfield's first hospital and has since grown from a phased opening into a full-service community facility, even as its parent system weathered significant financial strain and a change of chief executive.

## From county hospital to Westfield

Riverview Health traces its roots to May 10, 1909, when Hamilton County's first hospital — the Harrell Hospital and Sanatorium, built by Dr. Samuel Harrell and his brother Dr. Madison Harrell — opened in Noblesville, Indiana ([Riverview Health](https://www.riverview.org/history-0)). The county purchased it in 1914 and renamed it Hamilton County Hospital; a new building opened on May 12, 1951, was renamed Riverview Hospital in 1952 for its White River location, and became Riverview Health in April 2014 ([Riverview Health](https://www.riverview.org/history-0)).

The Westfield project began modestly. In 2015 Riverview Health announced a $34 million outpatient medical facility at the northeast corner of U.S. 31 and State Road 32 ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/north-of-96th-lindsey-erdody/62297-riverview-health-expands-scope-of-services-for-westfield-facility)). During construction the scope was expanded into an actual hospital — inpatient beds, three surgery suites and an emergency room were added to the 108,000-square-foot, six-story building — making it the first hospital in the city of Westfield ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/north-of-96th-lindsey-erdody/62297-riverview-health-expands-scope-of-services-for-westfield-facility); [Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/62694-riverview-embraces-health-care-free-for-all)). Then-CEO Seth Warren framed the move as serving unmet demand further north in fast-growing Hamilton County, where Westfield's population had risen 22 percent between 2010 and 2016 ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/62694-riverview-embraces-health-care-free-for-all)).

## Opening and early years

The hospital celebrated its opening over two days: a VIP ribbon-cutting on August 30, 2018, attended by the project architect American Structurepoint's leadership, and a three-hour public open house on August 31 ([American Structurepoint](https://tothepointblog.com/2018/09/riverview-health-westfield-hospital-celebrates-grand-opening/)). Only the first two floors opened initially — with three operating rooms and four inpatient beds — while three more floors were scheduled to open by year's end and the sixth floor was held for future growth ([American Structurepoint](https://tothepointblog.com/2018/09/riverview-health-westfield-hospital-celebrates-grand-opening/)). The hospital's formal opening followed on October 23, 2018 ([Current Publishing](https://youarecurrent.com/2018/10/22/riverview-health-westfield-hospital-to-open-oct-23/)).

Its signature feature from the start was the area's first combined emergency and urgent care center: a single access point where patients are guided to the appropriate level of care — emergency around the clock, urgent care from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. daily ([Riverview Health](https://www.riverview.org/location/riverview-health-westfield-hospital?utm_source=local-listing&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=website-link)). By 2023 the hospital marked its fifth anniversary serving Westfield ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/community-health-planning-new-335m-hospital-in-westfield)).

## Financial strain and a change of leadership

Growth came at a cost. Riverview Health posted operating losses totaling more than $75 million over three years as pandemic-era expenses outran revenue, and the Westfield hospital added tens of millions in debt to the system's books ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/riverview-health-ceo-anticipates-growth-despite-recent-red-ink)). Annual debt service stood at about $6 million through 2028, scheduled to rise to about $14 million a year through 2032 ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/riverview-health-ceo-anticipates-growth-despite-recent-red-ink)).

Seth Warren, president and CEO since 2016, resigned in December 2022 and later took the helm of Meridian Health Services, a Muncie-based mental health and addiction group practice ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/riverview-health-ceo-anticipates-growth-despite-recent-red-ink)). Dave Hyatt — previously chief operating officer after nearly 15 years with Indiana University Health, including presidencies of rural hospitals — became CEO in March 2023 with a growth-oriented turnaround plan rather than cuts ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/riverview-health-ceo-anticipates-growth-despite-recent-red-ink)).

## Expansion, partnership and a CEO exit

The growth strategy continued into 2024-2026. In June 2024 Hyatt told the Indianapolis Business Journal the system would open a primary-care office across from Meijer at Springmill and State Road 32 in summer 2025, alongside continued growth at the Westfield hospital ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/community-health-planning-new-335m-hospital-in-westfield)). A separate May 2025 announcement detailed a 13,630-square-foot primary-care facility at Indiana 32 and Spring Mill Road, developed by Innovcare/Innovative Healthcare Real Estate with Wurster Construction as general contractor, Arc Design, Tesco for civil engineering and GBC Bank financing, expected to open in late 2025 or early 2026 ([Current Publishing](https://youarecurrent.com/2025/05/05/riverview-health-plans-westfield-expansion/)).

In 2026 the hospital added the Westfield Endoscopy Center on its sixth floor — an 11,500-square-foot digestive-care center led by gastroenterologists Dr. Timothy Imler, Dr. Matthew Bohm and Dr. Sashidhar Sagi, built to cut regional scheduling waits that had stretched to three to six months down to seven to 14 days ([Indianapolis Monthly](https://www.indianapolismonthly.com/faces-of-indianapolis/the-face-of-endoscopic-care-westfield-endoscopy-center-riverview-health/)).

Two system-level developments reshaped the picture in late 2025 and 2026. On November 11, 2025, Riverview Health and Fort Wayne-based Parkview Health finalized a managed services agreement to expand service access and improve operational efficiency; Riverview kept its independence, identity and local presence, and Becker's described the system as comprising the 156-bed Noblesville hospital, the 16-bed Westfield hospital, three combined emergency/urgent care facilities and 10 primary care locations ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/hospital-transactions-and-valuation/2-indiana-systems-finalize-partnership/)). Then, in reporting dated to late April/early May 2026, Becker's noted that Hyatt had exited as Riverview Health's president and CEO to become regional president of the South region at Community Health Network ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/hospital-executive-moves/10-recent-hospital-health-system-ceo-moves-21/)) — the same Community Health Network that is building a $335 million, 100-bed hospital campus in Westfield, about four miles north of Riverview's hospital, with completion targeted for 2026-2027 ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/community-health-planning-new-335m-hospital-in-westfield)).

## Services today

The hospital's current listed services include the Bone & Breast Center, emergency and urgent care, an employer clinic, imaging, an inpatient unit, laboratory, an orthopedics and sports medicine practice, outpatient and inpatient pharmacy, outpatient rehabilitation therapy, and surgery ([Riverview Health](https://www.riverview.org/location/riverview-health-westfield-hospital?utm_source=local-listing&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=website-link)). A 2023 tour described floors dedicated to surgery, therapy, pain management and breast and bone care, with a robust orthopedic program including joint replacements, while more complex surgeries are routed to the 156-bed Noblesville flagship ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/riverview-health-ceo-anticipates-growth-despite-recent-red-ink)). The hospital remains listed as open with 24-hour emergency services ([Riverview Health](https://www.riverview.org/location/riverview-health-westfield-hospital?utm_source=local-listing&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=website-link)).
