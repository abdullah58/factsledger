## Overview

Lightspeed Venture Partners is a Menlo Park-based, multi-stage venture capital firm founded in the early 2000s by four Stanford classmates ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/10/lightspeeds-cofounders-on-their-17-year-long-overnight-success-story/?ncid=rss)), known for writing the first institutional check into Snapchat ([Reuters](https://www.businessoffashion.com/news/news-analysis/success-with-snap-helps-vc-firm-lightspeed-raise-1-8-billion-for-new-funds/)) and, two decades later, for leading [Anthropic](/organizations/anthropic/)'s $3.5 billion Series E in 2025 ([Reuters](https://www.lapost.com/content/from-amazon-to-google-the-deep-pocketed-investors-in-anthropic)). It invests from seed to growth across enterprise, consumer, fintech, and health, and raised more than $7 billion across four funds in 2022, taking its committed capital to $18 billion ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/lightspeed-venture-partners-new-fund/)).

This profile covers the venture firm Lightspeed Venture Partners — not Lightspeed Commerce, the Canadian point-of-sale company, or other businesses using the Lightspeed name. Most coverage of the firm is fragmented fund announcements and, recently, Anthropic cap-table reporting; this profile assembles the arc from its enterprise-first founding to its late-stage AI-era scale.

## Enterprise roots and the Snap bet

Lightspeed began when four founders — Ravi Mhatre, Barry Eggers, Peter Nieh, and Chris Schaepe — who had all gone to school at Stanford around the same time, set out to stake out enterprise Series A investing in what Eggers called the early-2000s "venture desert," after the dot-com bubble had cleared out most Series A firms ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/10/lightspeeds-cofounders-on-their-17-year-long-overnight-success-story/?ncid=rss)). The bet LPs made, Mhatre said, was on the four people and whether they had enough "connective fabric" to work together over a long period — and almost twenty years later, all four were still working together ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/10/lightspeeds-cofounders-on-their-17-year-long-overnight-success-story/?ncid=rss)). An early enterprise deal, Riverbed Technologies, gave the young firm a platform, and the consumer push that followed was spearheaded by Jeremy Liew ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/10/lightspeeds-cofounders-on-their-17-year-long-overnight-success-story/?ncid=rss)).

The defining early bet was Snap. Lightspeed was the first outside investor in Snap's messaging app Snapchat, and [Reuters](/organizations/reuters/) reported in 2018 that the firm hoped to build on that streak with a new $1.8 billion fundraise ([Reuters](https://www.businessoffashion.com/news/news-analysis/success-with-snap-helps-vc-firm-lightspeed-raise-1-8-billion-for-new-funds/)). About $1.05 billion of that raise was earmarked for a separate growth-stage fund — more than double the size of its previous growth fund — reflecting an industry shift as startups stayed private longer and firms had to invest repeatedly to hold their stakes ([Reuters](https://www.businessoffashion.com/news/news-analysis/success-with-snap-helps-vc-firm-lightspeed-raise-1-8-billion-for-new-funds/)). The 2017–2018 run of exits gave the firm its reputation: AppDynamics, where Lightspeed wrote the first check, was sold to Cisco for $3.7 billion on the eve of its IPO; MuleSoft and Snap both went public in March 2017; and Lightspeed-backed companies had held 17 IPOs in five years, with $2.7 billion returned to investors since the start of 2017 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/10/lightspeeds-cofounders-on-their-17-year-long-overnight-success-story/?ncid=rss); [Reuters](https://www.businessoffashion.com/news/news-analysis/success-with-snap-helps-vc-firm-lightspeed-raise-1-8-billion-for-new-funds/)). "If you point to one moment in time for the firm it was probably the Snap IPO," said Liew, who led the Snap investment, "but really, it's a decade of hard work and it all came to fruition at the same time" ([Reuters](https://www.businessoffashion.com/news/news-analysis/success-with-snap-helps-vc-firm-lightspeed-raise-1-8-billion-for-new-funds/)).

## Scale: mega-funds, fintech, and the AI era

In July 2022, Lightspeed raised three US funds totaling $6.6 billion plus a $500 million India early-stage fund, bringing its total committed capital to $18 billion ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/lightspeed-venture-partners-new-fund/)). By then the firm had invested in more than 500 companies across enterprise, consumer, fintech, and health, with around a quarter gone public or acquired and 33 companies through IPOs; notable exits included Snap, Affirm, MuleSoft, and GrubHub ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/lightspeed-venture-partners-new-fund/)). Alongside the funds it formed Lightspeed Faction, a team dedicated to crypto and blockchain investments ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/lightspeed-venture-partners-new-fund/)). The raise landed in a funding slowdown, in sharp contrast to the record venture dollars of 2021 — yet, as Crunchbase News noted, investment firms had not slowed their pace of closing new funds even as startup funding cooled ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/lightspeed-venture-partners-new-fund/)).

The fintech thread runs through [Alloy](/organizations/alloy/): in September 2021 Lightspeed's Justin Overdorff led Alloy's $100 million Series C at a $1.35 billion valuation, with [Canapi Ventures](/organizations/canapi-ventures/), [Bessemer Venture Partners](/organizations/bessemer-venture-partners/), Avid Ventures, and Felicis Ventures participating ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/alloy-earns-1-35-billion-valuation-after-securing-series-c-investment/)). The firm's latest chapter is AI scale: Reuters reported that Lightspeed led Anthropic's $3.5 billion Series E round in March 2025 and later co-led its $13 billion Series F at a $183 billion valuation in September 2025 ([Reuters](https://www.lapost.com/content/from-amazon-to-google-the-deep-pocketed-investors-in-anthropic)).
