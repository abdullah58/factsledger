## Overview

WeWork is an American coworking company founded in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) in 2010 by Adam Neumann and Miguel McKelvey. It leases office space on long terms, furnishes it, and sublets desks and offices on short, flexible contracts. Backed by [SoftBank](/organizations/softbank-group/), it reached a $47 billion valuation in 2019, collapsed after its failed IPO, filed for Chapter 11 in November 2023, and emerged in June 2024 under Yardi Systems and chief executive John Santora ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91139538/wework-exits-bankruptcy-new-ceo-tolley-steps-down)).

At its height in 2019 it managed more leased office space than any other company in New York City and [London](/places/london/), with about 500,000 paying customers in more than 500 locations ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)). SoftBank's cumulative reported loss reached $14.3 billion ([Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2023/11/softbank-wework-losses/)); the restructured company was profitable again by 2025 on $2.3 billion in revenue ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91633118/how-weworks-latest-ceo-changed-the-company-and-how-it-changed-him)).

## Founding and the coworking model

WeWork grew out of GreenDesk, a shared-office business Neumann and McKelvey started in the summer of 2008 with a Brooklyn landlord, beginning operations that May with subdivided furnished offices on short-term leases ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)). It soon had about 350 tenants, but the landlord was uninterested in further growth, so the pair sold their shares in 2010 for about $500,000 each ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)). That same year they founded WeWork on a similar model, except that they signed long-term leases themselves and sublet desks for short periods ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)). The first location opened at 154 Grand Street in SoHo, Manhattan, and the company had 450 members that year, according to a 2019 regulatory filing ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/455728088/wework-through-the-years-from-bold-beginnings-to-bankruptcy)).

Its well-lit spaces with coffee, beer and arcade games, and the networking Neumann called a "physical social network", appealed to start-ups ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)). By the end of 2011 it had four New York locations, a wait-list and two new investors; it entered San Francisco in 2011 and ended 2014 with 15,000 members in eight cities, having expanded to London and Israel ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork); [CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/455728088/wework-through-the-years-from-bold-beginnings-to-bankruptcy)).

The model carried a structural risk: unlike a software company, WeWork's costs did not plateau as it scaled, and it signed leases of more than a decade while subletting on terms of months, so fixed costs stayed if demand dried up — yet Neumann pitched it as a technology company, with a member app and the WeWork Labs incubator as its closest thing to a tech product ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)).

## SoftBank and the $47 billion peak

A 2012 round of $17 million from investors including Benchmark — an early backer of eBay, [Uber](/organizations/uber/) and Twitter — implied a valuation of about $100 million; McKelvey persuaded a reluctant Neumann to take it by ceding him a large share of control and future profits ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)). A $150 million round headed by [JPMorgan Chase](/organizations/jpmorgan-chase/) in early 2014 pushed the valuation to $1.5 billion — unicorn status — and successive rounds took it to $5 billion and then $10 billion ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)). By September 2018 WeWork said it was Manhattan's largest private office occupier, at 5.3 million square feet, and rented whole floors to corporate giants such as [Amazon](/organizations/amazon/), [Microsoft](/organizations/microsoft/), [Salesforce](/organizations/salesforce/) and [Facebook](/organizations/facebook/) ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/2075463430/wework-company-credited-with-popularizing-coworking-space-files-for-bankruptcy); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)).

In 2017 SoftBank invested $4.4 billion at a $20 billion valuation — then the second-largest private investment ever in a US start-up — funding Asia-Pacific expansion and buying out early investors including Neumann ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)). WeWork had entered China in 2016 at a $16 billion valuation and opened its first India centre in 2017 with Embassy Group ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)). In January 2019 SoftBank was said to have invested another $2 billion at a $47 billion valuation ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/455728088/wework-through-the-years-from-bold-beginnings-to-bankruptcy)). The collapse became the reference point for SoftBank's later deals: its Vision Fund 2's $100 million investment in [Behavox](/organizations/behavox/) in February 2020 was reported against the WeWork and Uber setbacks.

In 2018 Neumann pitched Son on a massive real-estate fund; Son first agreed to inject roughly $20 billion at a $47 billion valuation, then backed out ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)). Neumann claimed WeWork was profitable and heading for a trillion-dollar valuation, but losses were widening; its 2018 "community-adjusted EBITDA" metric excluded design, marketing and administration costs, which critics said misled on profitability ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)).

The August 2019 prospectus showed losses of $1.9 billion in 2018 and $900 million in the first half of 2019, and exposed governance problems: Neumann's 20-times voting power, a structure saving taxes for executives while public shareholders were taxed twice, his ownership of buildings WeWork leased, and his entity's $5.9 million sale of the "We" trademark to the company ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)). The IPO was put on hold and the prospectus withdrawn, as banks cut its estimated worth to $15 billion ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)). Under pressure from shareholders, Neumann stepped down as CEO in September 2019; in November the company laid off 2,400 employees, 20% of its headcount ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork); [CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/455728088/wework-through-the-years-from-bold-beginnings-to-bankruptcy)).

SoftBank kept the company afloat in October 2019 with a bailout valuing it at less than $8 billion, becoming its largest shareholder; Neumann gave up his board role and controlling stake for a $1.7 billion exit package ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)). Son publicly apologised — "My investment judgment was poor in many ways and I am reflecting deeply on that" — as SoftBank wrote down the Vision Fund's WeWork investment by as much as $4.7 billion, posting its first quarterly loss in 14 years ([interactive investor](https://www.ii.co.uk/analysis-commentary/softbank-apologises-wework-errors-ii509648)). Bloomberg estimated Neumann had pulled out more than $2.1 billion during his tenure, a figure he later disputed ([Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2023/11/softbank-wework-losses/)). After leaving, Neumann founded Flow, a residential real estate company reportedly valued at about $2.5 billion in 2025, backed by [Andreessen Horowitz](/organizations/andreessen-horowitz/) ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)).

## Bankruptcy and rebirth

Under SoftBank, WeWork cut costs and restructured debt, then went public at a $9 billion valuation via a SPAC merger in 2021 — but kept losing billions ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)). The BowX Acquisition Corp. merger was announced in March 2021; the listing followed that October ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/455728088/wework-through-the-years-from-bold-beginnings-to-bankruptcy)). Sandeep Mathrani, chief executive since February 2020, stepped down in May 2023; board member David Tolley became interim chief executive. McKelvey had announced his departure in June 2020 ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/455728088/wework-through-the-years-from-bold-beginnings-to-bankruptcy)).

Lease liabilities were more than two-thirds of operating expenses and 74% of revenue in the second quarter of 2023, while occupancy in the [United States](/places/united-states/) and Canada fell to 67%, trailing other markets by more than ten points; one analyst put the occupancy needed for profitability at about 80% ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/2075463430/wework-company-credited-with-popularizing-coworking-space-files-for-bankruptcy)). In August 2023 WeWork warned that "substantial doubt exists" about its survival, and in September it said it would renegotiate nearly all its leases — the "primary challenge and obstacle" to profitability ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/2075463430/wework-company-credited-with-popularizing-coworking-space-files-for-bankruptcy)). That month it completed a 1-for-40 reverse split after its shares fell below $1, facing delisting from the [New York Stock Exchange](/organizations/new-york-stock-exchange/) ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/455728088/wework-through-the-years-from-bold-beginnings-to-bankruptcy)). It had already exited or amended 590 leases since late 2019, cutting $12.7 billion in future lease payments ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/2075463430/wework-company-credited-with-popularizing-coworking-space-files-for-bankruptcy)).

WeWork filed for Chapter 11 on 6 November 2023 in the United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of New Jersey, listing $18.66 billion in debts and over $15.06 billion in assets as of 30 June 2023 ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/2075463430/wework-company-credited-with-popularizing-coworking-space-files-for-bankruptcy)). The filing covered only its United States operation, with Canada to file its own equivalent; other locations and franchises were excluded ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/2075463430/wework-company-credited-with-popularizing-coworking-space-files-for-bankruptcy)). Holders of about 92% of its secured notes agreed to a restructuring cutting funded debt by about $3 billion ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/2075463430/wework-company-credited-with-popularizing-coworking-space-files-for-bankruptcy)). It had missed about $95 million in interest payments the previous month, and [Fitch Ratings](/organizations/fitch-ratings/) had downgraded it to "restricted default" the Friday before ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/2075463430/wework-company-credited-with-popularizing-coworking-space-files-for-bankruptcy)). A Barclays study found about $7.5 billion of potential commercial mortgage-backed securities exposure to WeWork, $2.9 billion of it in New York ([CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/2075463430/wework-company-credited-with-popularizing-coworking-space-files-for-bankruptcy)).

For SoftBank, the filing closed the book on its most high-profile bet: about $16 billion invested since 2017, with total accumulated losses of $14.3 billion reported in November 2023 earnings, including a $6.2 billion Vision Fund loss that quarter ([Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2023/11/softbank-wework-losses/)). Its chief financial officer, Yoshimitsu Goto, told investors: "As a company, we need to accept this reality and also need to learn the lesson from this for our future investment activity" ([Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2023/11/softbank-wework-losses/)). SoftBank, which owned about 71% of WeWork at the bankruptcy, wrote down most of its investment and was set to keep only a minority stake, on account of its loans ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91139538/wework-exits-bankruptcy-new-ceo-tolley-steps-down)).

A US bankruptcy judge approved the plan in late May 2024, and WeWork emerged from Chapter 11 on 11 June 2024, having eliminated $4 billion in debt and handed its equity to lenders and Yardi Systems ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91139538/wework-exits-bankruptcy-new-ceo-tolley-steps-down)). Yardi Systems, a real-estate software company headed by Anant Yardi, became majority owner with a 60% stake; WeWork slimmed to about 330 locations ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)). Under Tolley, WeWork renegotiated over 190 leases, exited over 170 unprofitable locations, cut annual rent and tenancy expenses by over $800 million and secured $400 million in new equity ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91139538/wework-exits-bankruptcy-new-ceo-tolley-steps-down)). Post-bankruptcy equity was estimated at about $750 million, against the $47 billion valuation of 2019 ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91139538/wework-exits-bankruptcy-new-ceo-tolley-steps-down)). Neumann offered to buy the company back — more than $500 million per Britannica, $650 million in April 2024 per Reuters — and was rebuffed ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91139538/wework-exits-bankruptcy-new-ceo-tolley-steps-down)).

John Santora, a 47-year Cushman & Wakefield veteran and its most recent Tri-State chairman, became chief executive effective 12 June 2024 as Tolley stepped down ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91139538/wework-exits-bankruptcy-new-ceo-tolley-steps-down); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91633118/how-weworks-latest-ceo-changed-the-company-and-how-it-changed-him)). He set out to make WeWork more disciplined and financially sustainable, giving regional presidents responsibility for profitability and shifting design to a quieter, corporate style ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91633118/how-weworks-latest-ceo-changed-the-company-and-how-it-changed-him)). The restructuring had cut future rent obligations by about $12 billion ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91633118/how-weworks-latest-ceo-changed-the-company-and-how-it-changed-him)). WeWork returned to profitability in 2025 on $2.3 billion in revenue, and about 300 artificial-intelligence companies now occupy its spaces worldwide ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91633118/how-weworks-latest-ceo-changed-the-company-and-how-it-changed-him)). WeWork India, majority owned by Embassy Group and unaffected by the bankruptcy, went public in October 2025, raising $338 million at nearly $1 billion ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim a single settled figure for SoftBank's October 2019 bailout — Britannica reports $10 billion, interactive investor $9.5 billion — or for its total investment, about $16 billion in 2023 against $18.5 billion in a 2020 report; both variants are flagged as open items ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork); [interactive investor](https://www.ii.co.uk/analysis-commentary/softbank-apologises-wework-errors-ii509648); [Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2023/11/softbank-wework-losses/)). It does not claim the exact day the first SoHo location opened: sources read in full date it to 2010, while other accounts give April 2011. It does not give the sexual-harassment lawsuit's details, mentioned only in passing by Britannica, and it does not present the $2.1 billion Bloomberg estimate of Neumann's takings as settled fact, since he disputed it ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/WeWork); [Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2023/11/softbank-wework-losses/)). It does not assert a current location count beyond Britannica's "about 330 locations worldwide" at emergence, nor claim the $2.3 billion revenue figure is audited — it comes from Fast Company's reporting of the company's own statement ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91633118/how-weworks-latest-ceo-changed-the-company-and-how-it-changed-him)).
