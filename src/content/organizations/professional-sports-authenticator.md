## Overview

Professional Sports Authenticator (PSA) is the trading-card authentication and grading company founded in 1991 by David G. Hall, whose sealed holder and numbered grade turned condition — once a dealer's word — into a shared, tradable standard ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/collectors-universe-inc)). PSA authenticates cards, autographs and memorabilia, seals graded cards in tamper-evident holders, and is owned by Collectors, which by late 2025 also owned former rival [SGC](/organizations/sgc/) and announced the purchase of [Beckett](/organizations/beckett-grading-services/) ([ESPN](https://www.espn.ph/espn/story/_/id/47319181/psa-parent-company-buys-card-grading-rival-beckett)).

This profile covers PSA the card grader — not its sister coin grader, Professional Coin Grading Service (PCGS), which Hall had founded five years earlier ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/collectors-universe-inc)). It follows PSA from a loss-making startup dealers initially resisted, through its pandemic-era backlog and take-private sale, to its present position at the centre of a consolidated grading industry whose independence is now being tested in court.

## From a coin grader's side project to the hobby's standard

Hall founded PSA in 1991 because sports-card buyers needed a way to be sure the vintage cards they were buying were real and had not been re-glossed or re-cut ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/collectors-universe-inc)). Dealers feared grading would diminish the price of their wares, and for its first three years PSA lost an average of $10,000 a month, staying afloat only through infusions from PCGS ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/collectors-universe-inc)). Demand changed that: by 1996 PSA was grading almost 10,000 cards a month, and when internet card trading exploded in 1999 it held 90 percent of the card-grading market and rose to roughly 80,000 cards a month ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/collectors-universe-inc)).

The early years were slow for a second reason: collectors and dealers simply had not accepted third-party grading yet ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)). PSA was launched in 1991 as a business dedicated to grading trading cards, but in the mid-1990s its own staff considered shutting it down for lack of interest ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)). Between 1991 and 1998 it graded roughly 1 million cards in total; from that point on it graded at least 1 million cards every year ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)). The 1998 home-run race, the dot-com boom and the rise of eBay then combined into what PSA's later president Joe Orlando called a "perfect storm" for grading ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)).

In February 1999 Hall combined PSA and PCGS with three other companies to form Collectors Universe, the company that would take PSA public and, two decades later, private again ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/collectors-universe-inc)). That same year PSA launched PSA/DNA, at first letting collectors mark memorabilia with a unique strand of synthetic DNA in invisible ink, before branching into autograph verification ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/collectors-universe-inc)). In 2000 the NFL and NFL Players Inc. selected PSA/DNA to authenticate all player-related merchandise for the new NFL Auction on eBay ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/collectors-universe-inc)). Orlando became PSA's president in 2002 and made it a "one-stop shop," so a collector could get cards, autographs, bats, original photos, tickets and unopened packs certified under one brand ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)).

## What a PSA grade is — and why the slab became the market

Grading exists, in the account PSA's own leaders gave, because the pre-grading market had no structure: every seller described their own card, and trimming, altering and faking cards were prevalent, most prolifically in the late 1990s ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)). A third-party grade gives buyers, sellers and auction houses a common basis for what they are trading, and its value rests on the grader having no financial interest in the item itself ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)). That promise — an independent opinion, sealed in a holder — is what lets a card trade sight unseen.

PSA's published scale runs from Poor 1 to Gem Mint 10, with half-point grades available only for cards between Good 2 and Mint 9 ([PSA](https://www.psacard.com/resources/cardgradingstandards)). A Gem Mint 10 is defined as "a virtually perfect card" with four perfectly sharp corners, sharp focus and full original gloss, free of staining of any kind, though a slight printing imperfection may be allowed if it does not impair the card's overall appeal ([PSA](https://www.psacard.com/resources/cardgradingstandards)). Its image must be centered within approximately 55/45 to 60/40 percent on the front and 75/25 percent on the reverse ([PSA](https://www.psacard.com/resources/cardgradingstandards)). A Mint 9, by contrast, is a superb card allowed only one minor flaw, such as a very slight wax stain on the reverse, a minor printing imperfection or slightly off-white borders ([PSA](https://www.psacard.com/resources/cardgradingstandards)). PSA adopted the half-point scale in 2008, following Beckett, which had used half points from the start ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/collecting-101/history-of-grading-cards-psa-bgs-sgc-dominating)).

Two lesser-known engines drove PSA's growth beyond star cards. First, PSA/DNA's autograph authentication "really expanded" as third-party autograph authentication became accepted across the industry, and PSA added ticket grading, ball grading, game-used bat authentication and grading, and original-photo authentication ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/news/orlando-part-2-autograph-grading-ready-to-explode)). Second, the PSA Set Registry, which lets collectors rank complete sets against one another, produced what Orlando described as a huge explosion in grading commons: instead of receiving only the top 15 to 20 cards in a set, PSA began receiving thousands of cards a month it rarely saw before, in some cases all 800 cards of a set ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/news/orlando-part-2-autograph-grading-ready-to-explode)). The registry turned set completion into a competition, and competition into submissions.

## From independent opinion to vertically integrated platform

The pandemic broke PSA's capacity. On March 30, 2021, weeks after Nat Turner's investor group completed an $853 million purchase of Collectors Universe that took it private at $92 a share, PSA suspended incoming submissions for its Value, Economy, Regular and Express service levels ([Sports Collectors Daily](https://www.sportscollectorsdaily.com/collectors-universe-deal-complete-new-investors-include-collectors-athletes/); [Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/news/psa-card-grading-suspension-shutdown-impact-collectors-dealers-sports-cards-collectibles)). Turner, who became Executive Chairman with D1 Capital Partners and Cohen Private Ventures behind the deal, told Sports Collectors Digest on April 21 that PSA was backlogged by 10 million cards and working on cards received up to seven months earlier ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/news/psa-card-grading-suspension-shutdown-impact-collectors-dealers-sports-cards-collectibles)). PSA said it would not accept new submissions until at least July 1, 2021 ([Sports Collectors Digest](https://sportscollectorsdigest.com/news/psa-card-grading-suspension-shutdown-impact-collectors-dealers-sports-cards-collectibles)). The episode showed both PSA's dominance — a company secure enough to cut off its own intake for months — and its fragility: it could not hire graders fast enough to meet demand it had itself created.

Under Turner, Collectors then bought the competition rather than just outgrowing it. It acquired SGC, announced February 29, 2024, when GemRate figures cited by ICv2 put PSA at 76 percent of overall trading-card grading share and SGC adding about 2 percent ([ICv2](https://icv2.com/articles/news/view/56365/collectors-acquires-sgc)). In April 2024 eBay and Collectors signed deals under which Goldin moved from Collectors to eBay while the eBay vault moved to PSA, alongside a commercial agreement ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/ebay-collectors-enter-into-commercial-agreement-sign-deals-for-acquisition-of-goldin)). PSA would fold its existing vault services together with eBay's vault program into a newly branded offering, and introduce a service letting customers list and sell cards on eBay at the moment a card is graded ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/ebay-collectors-enter-into-commercial-agreement-sign-deals-for-acquisition-of-goldin)). The same release listed Collectors' divisions as including PSA, PCGS, WATA, SGC, the pricing tool Card Ladder and the Long Beach Expo ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/ebay-collectors-enter-into-commercial-agreement-sign-deals-for-acquisition-of-goldin)). In December 2025 Collectors purchased Beckett, PSA's longtime rival, on undisclosed terms, saying Beckett would remain an independent brand with no current change in pricing ([ESPN](https://www.espn.ph/espn/story/_/id/47319181/psa-parent-company-buys-card-grading-rival-beckett)). Per GemRate figures cited by ESPN, PSA then accounted for over 75 percent of graded items in a week, with CGC just over 18 percent and Beckett nearly 3 percent ([ESPN](https://www.espn.ph/espn/story/_/id/47319181/psa-parent-company-buys-card-grading-rival-beckett)).

That arc — from a grader whose selling point was having no stake in the sale, to a group that grades the card, stores it in its vault, tracks its price and can list it for sale — is the structural question now facing PSA. On July 28, 2026, a proposed class action filed in Maryland against PSA and Collectors Holdings alleged that PSA falsely markets its grading as objective, consistent and independent while relying on subjective "eye appeal" evaluations and business practices that benefit from repeated submissions ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/psa-hit-with-class-action-lawsuit-accusing-trading-card-grading-giant-of-widespread-fraud-3393244/)). The complaint further alleged that Collectors' ownership of vault, pricing and marketplace businesses creates financial conflicts of interest not adequately disclosed to customers, and that PSA intentionally limits Gem Mint 10 grades on certain cards to preserve scarcity ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/psa-hit-with-class-action-lawsuit-accusing-trading-card-grading-giant-of-widespread-fraud-3393244/)). Its most striking claim concerned PSA's first certification, alleging the company knowingly graded an altered T206 Honus Wagner card and never revoked the certification ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/psa-hit-with-class-action-lawsuit-accusing-trading-card-grading-giant-of-widespread-fraud-3393244/)). None of these allegations had been proven in court when reported, and PSA had not yet filed a response to the complaint ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/psa-hit-with-class-action-lawsuit-accusing-trading-card-grading-giant-of-widespread-fraud-3393244/)). The case therefore tests, in legal form, the same question collectors have asked since 1991: whether the opinion in the slab is truly independent of the business around it.
