## Overview

The Caisse nationale d'assurance sur la vie was a French state life-insurance fund, created in 1949 inside the [Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations](/organizations/caisse-des-depots-et-consignations/) by merging its death-insurance fund with its retirement fund, and dissolved ten years later when it absorbed the national accident fund and took the new name Caisse Nationale de Prévoyance, the direct ancestor of [CNP Assurances](/organizations/cnp-assurances/).

It existed for only a decade, from 1949 to 1959, and it never had a life outside the French state: it was a fund of the Caisse des Dépôts, run under a supervisory commission, and its whole history is a sequence of state-directed mergers — first the fusion that created it, then the fusion that ended it ([Sénat](https://www.senat.fr/rap/r98-0452/r98-04528.html); [Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000299325/)). Its importance is genealogical rather than commercial: it is the middle link between the nineteenth-century state insurance funds and the modern insurer CNP Assurances ([CNP Assurances](https://www.cnp.fr/le-groupe-cnp-assurances/newsroom/publications/2020/le-groupe-cnp/actionnaires-individuels/assemblee-generale/document-d-enregistrement-universel-2019)).

## Two nineteenth-century funds, one of them failing

The life fund was born from two much older institutions. The first, the Caisse nationale d'assurances en cas de décès, was created by the law of 11 July 1868 to offer a public refuge for death risks that private insurers rejected, and to act as official competition holding private insurers in check ([Sénat](https://www.senat.fr/rap/r98-0452/r98-04528.html)). The second, the Caisse des retraites pour la vieillesse, dated from the law of 18 June 1850 and became the Caisse nationale des retraites under the laws of 1884 and 1886 ([Sénat](https://www.senat.fr/rap/r98-0452/r98-04528.html)). The death fund was a commercial failure: chronically loss-making, it held only 0.55% of the market in 1936, facing 133 private companies covering workplace accidents ([Sénat](https://www.senat.fr/rap/r98-0452/r98-04528.html)). That tiny share is the key to the 1949 merger — the state was not combining two strong businesses but rescuing a marginal fund by joining it to the retirement fund, and the combined life fund inherited both the death business and the retirement business in a single state vehicle.

## A ten-year fund

In 1949 the death fund, known by its initials CNAD, merged with the retirement fund, the CRV, to form the Caisse nationale d'assurance sur la vie, known as the CNAV ([Sénat](https://www.senat.fr/rap/r98-0452/r98-04528.html)). The merger decree of that year, decree no. 49-669 of 16 May 1949, is cited among the governing texts in the preamble of the later 1959 merger decree ([Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000299325/)). The new fund sat inside the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations, under the commission supérieure that had supervised the life fund's operations ([Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000299325/)). Nothing in the sources read gives the fund's premium income, policyholder numbers or balance sheet for its ten years of existence, so this profile records its institutional shape — a state life fund, governed by civil servants and overseen by a mixed commission — rather than any commercial results it may have achieved.

## The 1959 merger and the CNP afterlife

The life fund's end came by decree no. 59-863 of 18 July 1959, whose first article states that the Caisse nationale d'assurance en cas d'accidents "is incorporated into the Caisse nationale d'assurance sur la vie, which takes the name of Caisse nationale de prévoyance" ([Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000299325/)). The decree was signed by Prime Minister Michel Debré, Labour Minister Paul Bacon and Finance Minister Antoine Pinay, and took effect on 19 July 1959 ([Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000299325/)). It kept the life fund's commission supérieure in place for the new Caisse Nationale de Prévoyance, with seats for employer and employee union representatives chosen by the Conseil économique, social et environnemental, the director-general of the Caisse des Dépôts, and the president of the Paris Chamber of Commerce ([Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000299325/)). The new fund later became an EPIC in 1987 and the société anonyme CNP Assurances in 1992, seated in [Paris](/places/paris/) ([Assemblée Nationale](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/15/questions/QANR5L15QE199)).

## FAQ

### What was the Caisse nationale d'assurance sur la vie?

It was a French state life-insurance fund, created in 1949 inside the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations by merging the state's death-insurance fund (created 1868) with its retirement fund (created 1850). It existed for only ten years before becoming the Caisse Nationale de Prévoyance in 1959.

### Why was it created in 1949?

The death fund it was formed from was chronically loss-making, holding only 0.55% of the market in 1936 against 133 private insurers, according to a Sénat report. Merging it with the older retirement fund put the state's small life-insurance business into a single, larger public vehicle instead of leaving it to compete alone.

### What happened to it in 1959?

Decree no. 59-863 of 18 July 1959 incorporated the national accident fund into the life fund, and the combined fund took the new name Caisse Nationale de Prévoyance. The decree kept the life fund's supervisory commission and carried its governing legislation over to the new body.

### Is it the same as CNP Assurances?

Not exactly. It is the direct ancestor: the life fund became the Caisse Nationale de Prévoyance in 1959, which was given EPIC status in 1987 and converted into the société anonyme CNP Assurances by law no. 92-665 of 16 July 1992, still inside the Caisse des Dépôts group.

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not resolve the Sénat report's parenthetical "(1954)" date for the life fund becoming the CNP; the merger decree, the Assemblée Nationale record and CNP's own history all give 1959, which this profile uses.
- It does not describe the contents of decree no. 49-669 of 16 May 1949, whose text was not read — only its citation as a legal basis in the 1959 decree.
- It does not give any financial figures for the fund; no premium, policyholder or balance-sheet numbers were found in the sources read.
- It does not state the exact day in 1949 on which the merger took effect, which was not verified.
- "Caisse nationale d'assurance sur la vie" here means the 1949–1959 state fund — not its successor, the Caisse Nationale de Prévoyance, and not the accident fund it absorbed in 1959.
