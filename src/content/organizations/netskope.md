## Overview

Netskope is a cloud-security company that spent 13 years as a private startup before going public — an unusually long private run in a sector where startups are more often acquired than listed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/08/netskope-follows-rubrik-as-a-rare-cybersecurity-ipo-both-backed-lightspeed/)). Founded in October 2012 in Los Altos, California, by four enterprise-security veterans, it sells "secure access service edge" (SASE) software: cloud-delivered security — secure web gateways, firewall-as-a-service, and data protection — for enterprises whose employees work from anywhere ([Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/680608/cloud-computing-start-up-netskope-keeps-watch-on-enterprise-cloud-usage.html); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/08/netskope-follows-rubrik-as-a-rare-cybersecurity-ipo-both-backed-lightspeed/)). On 18 September 2025 it debuted on Nasdaq under the ticker NTSK, jumping 21% on its first day to an $8.79 billion valuation ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/cybersecurity-firm-netskope-valued-879-billion-shares-rise-nasdaq-debut-2025-09-18/)).

## The "anti-startup": four veterans, October 2012

When Netskope came out of stealth in October 2013, it already had $21 million in venture funding from Social+Capital Partnership and Lightspeed Venture Partners — and, unusually for a startup, a staff of some 55 engineers, many of them 15-to-20-year industry veterans, assembled around a founding team drawn entirely from the networking and security establishment ([Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/680608/cloud-computing-start-up-netskope-keeps-watch-on-enterprise-cloud-usage.html)). Chief executive Sanjay Beri had been vice president and general manager of Juniper Networks' access security and Pulse business unit; chief architect Ravi Ithal came from Palo Alto Networks; chief scientist Krishna Narayanaswami had worked in engineering at NetScreen and Juniper and had founded Top Layer Networks; and Lebin Cheng, head of the applications engineering teams, came from VMware's virtual desktop products group ([Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/680608/cloud-computing-start-up-netskope-keeps-watch-on-enterprise-cloud-usage.html)).

The product they launched addressed a problem created by the consumerization of IT: employees were adopting cloud services — Salesforce, file storage, social apps — without consulting their IT departments, and Netskope's service let enterprises see which cloud applications were in use, block data transfers, or receive alerts when policies were violated ([Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/680608/cloud-computing-start-up-netskope-keeps-watch-on-enterprise-cloud-usage.html)). At launch it covered about 3,000 cloud-based applications across SaaS, PaaS, and IaaS, priced at $180 per user per year, running on four data centers in San Jose, Ashburn (Virginia), Amsterdam, and Singapore — with early pilots at Vegas.com, Netflix, Universal Music Group, and Genomic Health ([Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/680608/cloud-computing-start-up-netskope-keeps-watch-on-enterprise-cloud-usage.html)). In industry shorthand, it was building what Gartner would later call SASE — secure access service edge — before the category label existed.

## The long private run: from $2.8B to $7.5B to a down-IPO valuation

Netskope's 2021 funding round became a case study in the pandemic-era cybersecurity boom. As ransomware attacks took down Colonial Pipeline, software maker Kaseya, and meat processor JBS, venture capitalists flooded the sector: more than $12.2 billion poured into cybersecurity startups that year, according to PitchBook, and Netskope's Beri found himself fielding unsolicited bids from seven investors ([The Straits Times / NYT](https://www.straitstimes.com/business/invest/money-pours-in-for-anti-hacking-firms)). "We weren't looking for capital," he said, but the company took $300 million at a $7.5 billion valuation, up from $2.8 billion the year before ([The Straits Times / NYT](https://www.straitstimes.com/business/invest/money-pours-in-for-anti-hacking-firms)). Beri's read on the moment was blunt: "when your next-door neighbours go, 'Hey, what's up with this ransomware stuff', then you know that it's reached public consciousness" — and investors follow public consciousness ([The Straits Times / NYT](https://www.straitstimes.com/business/invest/money-pours-in-for-anti-hacking-firms)).

The ZIRP-era peak did not hold. By 2023 Netskope was raising a $401 million convertible note, and when it finally filed to go public in August 2025, the company was aiming for a valuation of up to $6.5 billion — below its last private valuation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/08/netskope-follows-rubrik-as-a-rare-cybersecurity-ipo-both-backed-lightspeed/)). Lightspeed, which had first backed Netskope in 2013 by leading its $21 million Series B, still owned 19.3% of the company pre-IPO — the same firm that had been Rubrik's earliest and largest investor, making the two IPOs a paired payoff for the Silicon Valley firm ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/08/netskope-follows-rubrik-as-a-rare-cybersecurity-ipo-both-backed-lightspeed/)). ICONIQ Growth held 19.2% and Accel nearly 9% ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/08/netskope-follows-rubrik-as-a-rare-cybersecurity-ipo-both-backed-lightspeed/)).

## The September 2025 IPO

The offering was priced on 17 September 2025: 47.8 million shares at $19 apiece — the top end of a range that had already been bumped up from $15–$17 to $17–$19 — raising $908.2 million at a $7.26 billion valuation, with Morgan Stanley and J.P. Morgan as lead underwriters ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/cybersecurity-firm-netskope-raises-9082-million-us-ipo-2025-09-17/?outputType=chromeless); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/08/netskope-follows-rubrik-as-a-rare-cybersecurity-ipo-both-backed-lightspeed/)). When trading began on Nasdaq under "NTSK" the next day, the shares opened at $23 and closed up 21%, valuing the company at $8.79 billion — a debut that added to a string of successful tech IPOs that included Figma and Circle ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/cybersecurity-firm-netskope-valued-879-billion-shares-rise-nasdaq-debut-2025-09-18/)).

The numbers showed a company still spending to grow: net loss had narrowed to $170 million on $328 million of revenue in the six months ended 31 July, compared with a $207 million loss on $251 million of revenue a year earlier ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/cybersecurity-firm-netskope-raises-9082-million-us-ipo-2025-09-17/?outputType=chromeless)). Beri told Reuters the IPO had been oversubscribed 20 times and that going public was partly about visibility — "they want more people to know about Netskope" — and pitched the company's AI positioning directly: "AI is kind of right in our wheelhouse … securing it, enabling companies to say yes to leveraging it, by putting guardrails around it" ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/cybersecurity-firm-netskope-valued-879-billion-shares-rise-nasdaq-debut-2025-09-18/)). Reuters named its main rivals as Palo Alto Networks and [Zscaler](/organizations/zscaler/) — the latter another cloud-security IPO story of the previous cycle, whose own 2018 debut had been built on the same architectural bet that the corporate firewall was obsolete ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/cybersecurity-firm-netskope-valued-879-billion-shares-rise-nasdaq-debut-2025-09-18/)).

## What this profile does not claim

The co-founder surname is spelled "Narayanaswami" here as in Network World, the source read in full; some tertiary pages use "Narayanaswamy" — the profile does not assert which spelling is canonical. Network World dates the founding to October 2012, while some other references say May 2012; the discrepancy is flagged rather than resolved. The early-tester names (Netflix, Universal Music Group, Genomic Health, Vegas.com) describe pilot testing in October 2013, not current customer relationships. Customer counts, current ARR, and post-IPO operating figures beyond the S-1 financials were not verified from independently read sources and are deliberately omitted. This profile does not repeat the company's own marketing claims about market leadership or analyst rankings, which appeared only in first-party or vendor materials.
