## Overview

Nuance Communications was an American speech-recognition and artificial-intelligence software company headquartered in [Burlington, Massachusetts](/places/burlington-massachusetts/), best known for its Dragon dictation software, for supplying the voice-recognition technology behind [Apple](/organizations/apple/)'s Siri, and for Dragon Medical, the clinical documentation software used across American healthcare ([Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2021/04/12/microsoft-accelerates-industry-cloud-strategy-for-healthcare-with-the-acquisition-of-nuance/)). Founded from SRI International's speech research lab and rebuilt through a 2005 merger with ScanSoft, it narrowed itself to healthcare and enterprise AI in 2019 — selling its imaging division and spinning off its automotive business as [Cerence](/organizations/cerence/) — before [Microsoft](/organizations/microsoft/) acquired it in 2022 for $19.7 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/04/12/microsoft-is-acquiring-nuance-communications-for-19-7b/?guccounter=1); [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1768267/000156459021057931/R9.htm)).

## From a Stanford lab to the voice of Siri

Nuance began as a spin-off of SRI International's Speech Technology and Research (STAR) Laboratory, which had built the DECIPHER speech-recognition system on Hidden Markov Model techniques; the lab's work "eventually resulted in a spin-off company, Corona Corporation (renamed, Nuance Communications)" ([SRI International](https://www.sri.com/75-years-of-innovation/75-years-of-innovation-speech-recognition/)). The technology reached the public early: in 1996, Charles Schwab & Co. used Nuance's speech recognition to let customers hear stock quotes over the telephone, and in 1997 Nuance built the first large-scale commercial dialog system for United Parcel Service to handle package-status inquiries ([SRI International](https://www.sri.com/75-years-of-innovation/75-years-of-innovation-speech-recognition/)).

The company that survives today is the product of a merger. The corporate lineage traces to Visioneer, a scanner company founded in 1992 that acquired ScanSoft and took its name; ScanSoft then bought the assets of Lernout & Hauspie in December 2001 — acquiring the Dragon speech-recognition line, whose maker Dragon Systems had been bought by Lernout & Hauspie earlier that year — and acquired SpeechWorks in 2003 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/04/12/microsoft-is-acquiring-nuance-communications-for-19-7b/?guccounter=1)). In September 2005, ScanSoft acquired and merged with the original Nuance Communications, and the combined company adopted the Nuance name ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/04/12/microsoft-is-acquiring-nuance-communications-for-19-7b/?guccounter=1)). Nuance had gone public in 2000 on the [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/) under the ticker NUAN ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/04/12/microsoft-is-acquiring-nuance-communications-for-19-7b/?guccounter=1)).

Nuance's most famous, least visible product was inside the iPhone. For years it was widely suspected that Siri ran on Nuance technology, but neither company confirmed it until May 30, 2013, when CEO Paul Ricci said at the D11 conference, "We're a fundamental provider for Apple" ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2013/05/30/nuance-ceo-confirms-siri-partnership-with-apple/)). Ricci was careful about the scope: Nuance supplied voice recognition only, not Siri's artificial-intelligence layers, which came from other providers ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2013/05/30/nuance-ceo-confirms-siri-partnership-with-apple/)).

## Narrowing the focus: two exits in 2019

By 2018, under CEO Mark Benjamin, Nuance was deliberately shrinking toward conversational AI. On February 1, 2019, it completed the sale of its Document Imaging division — the scanner and PDF business inherited from the ScanSoft side — to Kofax, Inc. for $400 million ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1002517/000095014219000145/0000950142-19-000145.txt)). Eight months later, on October 1, 2019, it completed the tax-free spin-off of its Automotive business as Cerence Inc., distributing one Cerence share for every eight Nuance shares held as of September 17, 2019; Cerence began regular-way trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker CRNC the next day ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1768267/000156459021057931/R9.htm)). What remained was a healthcare and enterprise AI company, and its healthcare cloud revenue had grown 37% year over year in fiscal 2020 ([Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2021/04/12/microsoft-accelerates-industry-cloud-strategy-for-healthcare-with-the-acquisition-of-nuance/)).

## The Microsoft acquisition — and the price-figure puzzle

On April 12, 2021, Microsoft announced it would acquire Nuance for $56.00 per share, a 23% premium to the previous Friday's close, in an all-cash transaction valued at $19.7 billion, inclusive of Nuance's net debt ([Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2021/04/12/microsoft-accelerates-industry-cloud-strategy-for-healthcare-with-the-acquisition-of-nuance/)). The stated prize was healthcare: Nuance's Dragon Ambient eXperience, Dragon Medical One and PowerScribe One were already built on Microsoft Azure, and Microsoft said the deal would double its total addressable market in the healthcare provider space to nearly $500 billion ([Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2021/04/12/microsoft-accelerates-industry-cloud-strategy-for-healthcare-with-the-acquisition-of-nuance/)). At the announcement, Nuance had about 6,000 employees across 27 countries and a revenue run rate above $1.4 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/04/12/microsoft-is-acquiring-nuance-communications-for-19-7b/?guccounter=1)). It was Microsoft's second-largest acquisition, behind only the $26.2 billion LinkedIn purchase of 2016 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/04/12/microsoft-is-acquiring-nuance-communications-for-19-7b/?guccounter=1)).

The deal was not a formality. It needed clearance from the U.S. Justice Department, the European Union and the UK Competition and Markets Authority, which approved it in the week of the closing after finding no evidence the combination would harm competition in the healthcare transcription market ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/03/04/after-clearing-all-regulatory-hurdles-microsoft-closes-20b-nuance-deal/)). Microsoft announced completion on March 4, 2022, with Mark Benjamin remaining CEO of Nuance and reporting to Scott Guthrie, Microsoft's executive vice president for Cloud + AI ([Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2022/03/04/microsoft-completes-acquisition-of-nuance-ushering-in-new-era-of-outcomes-based-ai/)).

One number deserves care: the $19.7 billion is the announced enterprise value including net debt. Microsoft's own Form 10-K records the completed deal "for a total purchase price of $18.8 billion, consisting primarily of cash", with $16.3 billion allocated to goodwill and $4.4 billion to intangible assets, and Nuance reported inside Microsoft's Intelligent Cloud segment ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000095017023035122/R17.htm)). Some press coverage quoted about $16 billion — the equity value excluding debt. The three figures describe the same transaction from different accounting angles, not three different prices.

## After the deal: Dragon Copilot

Inside Microsoft, Nuance's healthcare products were folded into a single offering. On March 3, 2025, Microsoft unveiled Dragon Copilot, combining Dragon Medical One's dictation with DAX Copilot's ambient listening and generative AI into what it called the first unified voice AI assistant for clinical workflow ([Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2025/03/03/microsoft-dragon-copilot-provides-the-healthcare-industrys-first-unified-voice-ai-assistant-that-enables-clinicians-to-streamline-clinical-documentation-surface-information-and-automate-task/)). By then, DAX's ambient technology had assisted more than 3 million patient conversations across 600 healthcare organizations in a single month, and Dragon Medical One had helped clinicians document billions of patient records ([Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2025/03/03/microsoft-dragon-copilot-provides-the-healthcare-industrys-first-unified-voice-ai-assistant-that-enables-clinicians-to-streamline-clinical-documentation-surface-information-and-automate-task/)). Dragon Copilot became generally available in the U.S. and Canada in May 2025, followed by the UK, Germany, France and the Netherlands ([Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2025/03/03/microsoft-dragon-copilot-provides-the-healthcare-industrys-first-unified-voice-ai-assistant-that-enables-clinicians-to-streamline-clinical-documentation-surface-information-and-automate-task/)). The Nuance name survives mainly as a product brand; the company itself is a Microsoft subsidiary, and its former automotive arm trades independently as Cerence.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who owns Nuance Communications?** Microsoft has owned Nuance since March 4, 2022, when it completed an all-cash acquisition announced at $19.7 billion including net debt; Nuance operates as a Microsoft subsidiary, with its former CEO Mark Benjamin reporting into Microsoft's Cloud + AI group ([Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2022/03/04/microsoft-completes-acquisition-of-nuance-ushering-in-new-era-of-outcomes-based-ai/)).

**What is Nuance best known for?** Its Dragon speech-recognition software, the voice-recognition technology behind Apple's Siri (confirmed in 2013), and Dragon Medical, the clinical documentation software used by a large share of American physicians, radiologists and hospitals ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2013/05/30/nuance-ceo-confirms-siri-partnership-with-apple/); [Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2021/04/12/microsoft-accelerates-industry-cloud-strategy-for-healthcare-with-the-acquisition-of-nuance/)).

**What happened to Nuance's automotive business?** It was spun off as a separate public company, Cerence Inc., on October 1, 2019, with Cerence shares distributed to Nuance shareholders and trading on the Nasdaq as CRNC from October 2, 2019 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1768267/000156459021057931/R9.htm)).

**How much did Microsoft pay for Nuance?** The announced price was $19.7 billion including net debt, at $56.00 per share; Microsoft's Form 10-K records the accounting purchase price as $18.8 billion, and some press quoted about $16 billion for the equity value alone ([Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2021/04/12/microsoft-accelerates-industry-cloud-strategy-for-healthcare-with-the-acquisition-of-nuance/); [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000095017023035122/R17.htm)).

**Is Nuance still a separate company?** No. It is a Microsoft subsidiary, and its healthcare products were merged in 2025 into Dragon Copilot, a single clinical AI assistant combining dictation and ambient listening ([Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2025/03/03/microsoft-dragon-copilot-provides-the-healthcare-industrys-first-unified-voice-ai-assistant-that-enables-clinicians-to-streamline-clinical-documentation-surface-information-and-automate-task/)).

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not assert a single founding date: the surviving company's lineage traces to Visioneer (1992), while the original Nuance was the 1994 SRI spin-off, and both dates are stated.
- It does not treat any one acquisition price as the only figure: $19.7 billion (announced, including net debt), $18.8 billion (10-K purchase price) and ~$16 billion (equity value in some press) are different measures of the same deal.
- It does not assert that Nuance exposed more than 45,000 patient records between 2014 and 2017; that claim circulates from a low-tier source and was not verified from a tier1 or tier2 source.
- It does not state post-acquisition headcount or layoff figures for Nuance; reported May 2023 layoffs were not verified from a fully-read source.
- The healthcare scale figures (55% of physicians, 75% of radiologists, 77% of U.S. hospitals) are company-stated figures from the acquisition announcement, not independently audited numbers.
- Nuance Communications is not the same company as Cerence Inc.; Cerence is the former automotive division, spun off in 2019 and independently traded.
