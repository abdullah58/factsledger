In January 2026, CNBC did something most reference pages still haven't caught up with: it stopped being a [Comcast](/organizations/comcast/) company. The business-news network spun out into Versant, a new publicly traded company that began trading on Nasdaq under the ticker VSNT after a 1-for-25 share distribution completed on January 2, 2026, with December 16, 2025 as the record date. ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20260104224814/versant-begins-trading-on-nasdaq-today-as-independent-company-following-completion-of-comcast-spin-off)) An internal memo reported by Talking Biz News said CNBC would keep its name under the new company while getting a new logo described as "a look back at its history," under incoming Versant CEO Mark Lazarus. ([Talking Biz News](https://talkingbiznews.com/media-news/cnbc-to-retain-name-create-new-logo/)) Every top search result still calls it an NBCUniversal/Comcast unit — that description is now out of date.

The separation had been in the works since November 2024, when Comcast announced it would spin off the bulk of its NBCUniversal cable networks — MSNBC, CNBC, USA, Oxygen, [E!](/organizations/e-network/), Syfy and Golf Channel — into the new company, a business of roughly $7 billion in annual revenue, in a tax-free deal expected to take about a year, with Lazarus as CEO and Anand Kini as chief operating and financial officer. ([SRN News](https://srnnews.com/comcast-to-spin-off-some-cable-tv-networks-as-streaming-dominates/)) Under the completed Versant, CNBC sits alongside USA, Golf Channel, Oxygen, E!, SYFY, Fandango, Rotten Tomatoes, GolfNow, GolfPass and SportsEngine. ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20260104224814/versant-begins-trading-on-nasdaq-today-as-independent-company-following-completion-of-comcast-spin-off)) The MSNBC that went into the deal had already been renamed [MS NOW](/organizations/msnbc/) in 2025.

## How CNBC got here

CNBC launched on April 17, 1989, as a joint venture between NBC and Cablevision, its initials short for "Consumer News and Business Channel." ([Talking Biz News](https://talkingbiznews.com/media-news/cnbc-to-retain-name-create-new-logo/)) Two years later NBC acquired the rival Financial News Network for $154 million — beating a competing Westinghouse–Dow Jones bid — and folded FNN's staff into CNBC, including future fixtures Bill Griffeth, Ron Insana, Joe Kernen and Sue Herera. ([Talking Biz News](https://talkingbiznews.com/they-talk-biz-news/cnbc-celebrates-25th-anniversary/)) David Faber, Mark Haines and Maria Bartiromo (in 1995) joined later. ([Talking Biz News](https://talkingbiznews.com/they-talk-biz-news/cnbc-celebrates-25th-anniversary/))

The early years were not the dominance the brand suggests today. In a first-person anniversary account, longtime correspondent Bob Pisani recalled that the network "had no ratings" for its first five years and only took off in 1995–96, when surging market trading volume finally gave business television an audience. ([Talking Biz News](https://talkingbiznews.com/they-talk-biz-news/cnbc-celebrates-25th-anniversary/))

## The ratings duel

CNBC's standing is measured against one rival: [Fox Business Network](/organizations/fox-business-network/). The scorecard as of April 2026 was a genuine split verdict. Fox Business won the business day (Monday–Friday, 9:30 a.m.–5 p.m. ET) with 254,000 total viewers against CNBC's 229,000 — its 12th straight monthly business-day win. ([Barrett Media](https://barrettmedia.com/2026/04/30/fox-business-network-leads-cnbc-in-total-business-ratings-during-april/)) But CNBC swept the adults 25–54 demographic in every daypart, drawing 58,000 demo viewers to Fox Business's 12,000 in business day. ([Barrett Media](https://barrettmedia.com/2026/04/30/fox-business-network-leads-cnbc-in-total-business-ratings-during-april/))

At the program level, CNBC's top draw that month was *Fast Money Halftime Report* with 277,000 viewers, while Fox Business's *Varney & Co.* (313,000) and *Kudlow* (292,000) led individual business programs. ([Barrett Media](https://barrettmedia.com/2026/04/30/fox-business-network-leads-cnbc-in-total-business-ratings-during-april/)) In other words: Fox Business wins the headcount, CNBC wins the advertiser demo — and neither network's press operation phrases it that way.

## CNBC+ and the streaming push

At the start of 2025, CNBC announced a paid streaming service, CNBC+, in an email to subscribers, with a launch planned for the first quarter of 2025, per iDownloadBlog's report. ([iDownloadBlog](https://www.idownloadblog.com/2025/01/02/cnbc-plus-streaming-service-launch-subscription-plans-prices/)) The entry tier was priced at $15 a month and included a global livestream (U.S., U.K. and Asia), on-demand programming, market data, watchlists and charting tools; above it sat CNBC Pro at $35 a month or $300 a year, the Investing Club at $50 a month or $400 a year, and an All Access tier at $600 a year that included Jim Cramer's investing club. ([iDownloadBlog](https://www.idownloadblog.com/2025/01/02/cnbc-plus-streaming-service-launch-subscription-plans-prices/)) Notably, the service launched with no free trial. ([iDownloadBlog](https://www.idownloadblog.com/2025/01/02/cnbc-plus-streaming-service-launch-subscription-plans-prices/))

## A general-news experiment, tried and dropped

In 2022 CNBC briefly tried to be more than a markets channel. [Shomari Stone](/people/shomari-stone/) joined that April as a general-assignment reporter for *The News with Shepard Smith*, working out of the network's Washington, D.C. bureau starting April 4 — a hire the newscast's senior executive producer Dan Colarusso called evidence the show was "building a top-notch team of journalists." ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/tvnewser/shomari-stone-joins-cnbc-as-reporter-for-shepard-smiths-newscast/)) Smith, who had left [Fox News](/organizations/fox-news/) in 2019, anchored the 7 p.m. ET weeknight newscast. ([WTMJ](https://www.tmj4.com/news/national/cnbc-axes-shepard-smiths-nightly-newscast-after-two-years))

It lasted two years. In November 2022 CNBC canceled the newscast after it averaged just 222,000 viewers that year, with president KC Sullivan — who had replaced Mark Hoffman that September — saying the network would refocus on business news. ([WTMJ](https://www.tmj4.com/news/national/cnbc-axes-shepard-smiths-nightly-newscast-after-two-years)) The experiment's end marked the network's return to its core: business, all day.
