## Overview

Natixis is the wholesale banking arm of [Groupe BPCE](/organizations/groupe-bpce/), [France](/places/france/)'s second-largest banking group: a [Paris](/places/paris/)-based financial services firm specialising in corporate & investment banking and asset & wealth management, wholly owned by BPCE since a 2021 buyout delisted it from [Euronext Paris](/organizations/euronext-paris/).

Created in 2006 from the merger of Natexis Banques Populaires and Ixis, it nearly collapsed in the 2008 financial crisis, posting a €2.8 billion loss, before BPCE rescued it and, thirteen years later, took it private ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/markets/natixis-faces-formal-investigation-into-sub-prime-losses-14002748)). Today it employs nearly 14,700 people in nearly 40 countries, and its asset manager Natixis Investment Managers oversees €1,317 billion — a scale that makes the 2008 near-death experience, not just the current business, essential to understanding the firm ([Natixis URD 2024](https://natixis.groupebpce.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Natixis_URD_2024_VA_.pdf); [Groupe BPCE](https://echanges.dila.gouv.fr/OPENDATA/AMF/MKW/2025/02/FCMKW116925_20250205.pdf)).

## Created in 2006, nearly destroyed in 2008

Natixis was formed in 2006 when Groupe Banque Populaire and Groupe Caisse d'Epargne merged their wholesale banking arms, Natexis Banques Populaires and Ixis, into a single listed investment bank, and it was affiliated with the new Groupe BPCE from 2009 ([Federal Reserve](http://federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/resolution-plans/bpce-3g-20220701.pdf)). Two years later the financial crisis nearly destroyed it: Natixis posted a €2.8 billion loss in 2008, driven by exposure to Lehman Brothers, the Bernard Madoff fraud and other sub-prime assets, and its shares fell more than 90% from over €20 to under €1 ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/markets/natixis-faces-formal-investigation-into-sub-prime-losses-14002748)). Its parent injected cash and guaranteed $50 billion of toxic assets, while the French state in turn backed BPCE ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/markets/natixis-faces-formal-investigation-into-sub-prime-losses-14002748)). In December 2008 Natixis put its maximum net indirect Madoff exposure at about €450 million, saying it held no proprietary investments in Madoff funds ([Natixis](https://echanges.dila.gouv.fr/OPENDATA/AMF/CNS/2008/12/FCCNS015907_20081215.pdf)). The episode did not end there: in February 2017 Natixis was placed under formal investigation in France over statements it made in the second half of 2007, in a magistrate-led process opened in 2010 ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/markets/natixis-faces-formal-investigation-into-sub-prime-losses-14002748)).

## Taken private: the 2021 buyout and a simpler Natixis

On 9 February 2021 BPCE announced it would buy the roughly 29.3% of Natixis it did not already own, offering €4 per share and planning a mandatory squeeze-out for any shares left behind ([Groupe BPCE newsroom](https://newsroom-en.groupebpce.fr/news/natixis-press-release-following-bpces-announcement-of-its-intention-to-file-a-tender-offer-022b-53927.html)). The simplified tender offer ran in June and July 2021; BPCE then held 92.03% of the capital and voting rights, squeezed out the remaining shareholders on 21 July 2021 at the same €4 price, and Natixis shares were delisted from Euronext Paris that day ([BPCE](https://www.ppr.groupebpce.com/app/uploads/2023/11/Communique-norme-retrait-obligatoire-RO_EN_-13_07_2021.pdf)). By February 2022 BPCE owned 99.78% of Natixis, ending its life as a listed company and making it a wholly owned subsidiary in all but name ([Federal Reserve](http://federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/resolution-plans/bpce-3g-20220701.pdf)). The buyout was the centrepiece of a wider simplification: under the BPCE 2024 strategic plan, Natixis's insurance and specialised financial services moved to BPCE, leaving two global business lines — corporate & investment banking and asset & wealth management — grouped from 22 March 2022 into a single Global Financial Services division ([Federal Reserve](http://federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/resolution-plans/bpce-3g-20220701.pdf); [Natixis URD 2024](https://natixis.groupebpce.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Natixis_URD_2024_VA_.pdf)).

## What Natixis does now

Natixis Corporate & Investment Banking serves companies, banks, institutions and the customers of the Banque Populaire and Caisse d'Epargne networks, with Global Markets, investment banking, mergers & acquisitions and distribution & portfolio management; in the Americas it operates through a New York branch licensed by the New York State Department of Financial Services and a broker-dealer, Natixis Securities Americas ([Federal Reserve](http://federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/resolution-plans/bpce-3g-20220701.pdf)). Natixis Investment Managers runs a multi-affiliate model of more than 20 active managers — including Harris Associates, Loomis, Sayles & Company, Mirova and [Ostrum Asset Management](/organizations/ostrum-asset-management/) — and held €1,317 billion in assets under management at the end of 2024, an all-time high after record €40 billion net inflows ([Groupe BPCE](https://echanges.dila.gouv.fr/OPENDATA/AMF/MKW/2025/02/FCMKW116925_20250205.pdf)). In May 2022 it completed the purchase of [La Banque Postale](/organizations/la-banque-postale/)'s 45% stake in Ostrum and 40% in [AEW Europe](/organizations/aew-europe/), taking both to 100% ([Groupe BPCE newsroom](https://newsroom-en.groupebpce.fr/download-pdf/627e80c308de164c260fe401)). A planned combination of Natixis IM with Generali Investments, announced in January 2025, was abandoned in December 2025 ([IPE](https://www.ipe.com/news/generali-scraps-plans-to-create-joint-venture-with-natixis-investment-managers/10134257.article)).

Natixis Investment Managers' assets under management reached €1,317 billion at the end of 2024, a record after €40 billion of net inflows in the year ([Groupe BPCE](https://echanges.dila.gouv.fr/OPENDATA/AMF/MKW/2025/02/FCMKW116925_20250205.pdf)); that scale is what made the attempted combination with Generali's asset manager — a deal that would have created a $2.2 trillion manager — the group's biggest strategic bet of 2025 ([IPE](https://www.ipe.com/news/generali-scraps-plans-to-create-joint-venture-with-natixis-investment-managers/10134257.article)).

The same 2021 reshaping that took Natixis private also rewired its ownership map: while BPCE sold its 16.1% stake in [CNP Assurances](/organizations/cnp-assurances/) to La Banque Postale at €21.90 a share — a 36.2% premium valuing the insurer at about €15 billion — Natixis bought La Banque Postale's stakes in Ostrum and AEW Europe, and by 3 June 2022 La Banque Postale held 97.79% of CNP's capital and 98.56% of its voting rights ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2021/10/28/639703.htm); [Groupe BPCE newsroom](https://newsroom-en.groupebpce.fr/download-pdf/627e80c308de164c260fe401)).

## FAQ

### Is Natixis still listed on the stock market?

No. BPCE bought out the minority shareholders at €4 a share in 2021, squeezed out the remaining shares on 21 July 2021, and Natixis shares were delisted from Euronext Paris the same day. By February 2022 BPCE owned 99.78% of Natixis, so it is now a wholly owned subsidiary in all but name.

### Who owns Natixis?

Groupe BPCE, France's second-largest banking group, owns Natixis almost entirely — 99.78% as of February 2022, with the remainder held by employees and in treasury shares. Natixis is BPCE's wholesale banking arm, while the Banque Populaire and Caisse d'Epargne networks handle retail banking.

### What does Natixis do?

Natixis has two business lines: Corporate & Investment Banking, serving companies, banks and institutions with markets, financing and M&A advice; and Asset & Wealth Management, run through Natixis Investment Managers and its 20-plus affiliated firms. Together they form Groupe BPCE's Global Financial Services division.

### What happened to Natixis in 2008?

Natixis lost €2.8 billion in 2008 on exposure to Lehman Brothers, the Bernard Madoff fraud and sub-prime assets, and its shares fell more than 90%. Its parent BPCE injected cash and guaranteed $50 billion of toxic assets, backed by French state support. In 2017 Natixis was placed under formal investigation over its 2007 statements.

### What happens if Natixis gets into trouble?

Natixis is covered by Groupe BPCE's legal solidarity mechanism under the French Monetary and Financial Code: BPCE must do everything necessary to restore an affiliated institution in difficulty and may mobilise the cash and capital of all affiliates. A mutual guarantee fund held €394 million at the end of 2024, backed by €900 million in network guarantee funds.
