# New York State Assembly

Two hundred and forty-nine years after its creation, the New York State Assembly still runs on a simple mechanical premise: 150 members, two-year terms, and a Speaker elected by those members who directs the chamber's business and appoints members to its standing committees ([New York State Assembly](https://assembly.ny.gov/); [New York State Division of the Budget](https://ht.budget.ny.gov/pubs/archive/fy1516archive/eBudget1516/agencyPresentations/pdf/Legislature.pdf)). It is one half of New York's two-house Legislature — the 63-member Senate is the other — and all 213 legislators face the voters every two years ([New York State Division of the Budget](https://ht.budget.ny.gov/pubs/archive/fy1516archive/eBudget1516/agencyPresentations/pdf/Legislature.pdf)).

The chamber calls itself the "People's House," and its own homepage says that since its creation in 1777, it has "helped shape history, often leading the way with progressive action and reform" ([New York State Assembly](https://assembly.ny.gov/)). On February 3, 2015, it made history again by electing New York's first African American Assembly Speaker ([New York State Assembly](https://assembly.ny.gov/)).

## The machinery

The Assembly's operating rules are best laid out in an unlikely place: a state budget document. The Division of the Budget's agency presentation on the Legislature describes the chamber's powers as set by the State Constitution — proposing laws, overriding a gubernatorial veto with two-thirds of the Senate and Assembly voting to do so, reapportioning legislative and congressional districts every ten years after the national census, and voting on ratification of proposed federal constitutional amendments ([New York State Division of the Budget](https://ht.budget.ny.gov/pubs/archive/fy1516archive/eBudget1516/agencyPresentations/pdf/Legislature.pdf)). The Legislature convenes annually on the first Wednesday after the first Monday in January and remains in session until it concludes its business ([New York State Division of the Budget](https://ht.budget.ny.gov/pubs/archive/fy1516archive/eBudget1516/agencyPresentations/pdf/Legislature.pdf)).

Inside the chamber, power runs through the Speaker. The members elect a Speaker who directs and guides the business of the Assembly, appoints members to the 38 standing committees and to Assembly leadership positions, and serves as the presiding officer; the minority party, meanwhile, chooses a Minority Leader from its own membership ([New York State Division of the Budget](https://ht.budget.ny.gov/pubs/archive/fy1516archive/eBudget1516/agencyPresentations/pdf/Legislature.pdf)). The 38 standing committees are where the Assembly's legislative business is actually conducted — fiscal bills, for example, run through the Ways and Means Committee, whose chair, with the Senate Finance chair, functions as part of an audit committee selecting an independent accountant to audit the state's annual financial statements ([New York State Division of the Budget](https://ht.budget.ny.gov/pubs/archive/fy1516archive/eBudget1516/agencyPresentations/pdf/Legislature.pdf)).

## The Heastie era

Carl E. Heastie has led the chamber since February 3, 2015, as its 100th Speaker — the first African American to lead the chamber's 150 members ([New York State Assembly member directory](https://nyassembly.gov/mem/Carl-E-Heastie/about/)). He represents the 83rd Assembly District in the northeast Bronx, where he was first elected to the Assembly in 2000, and before joining the Legislature he served as a budget analyst in the New York City Comptroller's office, with an MBA in finance from Baruch College and a bachelor's in applied mathematics and statistics from Stony Brook ([New York State Assembly member directory](https://nyassembly.gov/mem/Carl-E-Heastie/about/)).

His signature fights are catalogued on his official biography, and they read like a map of New York's last decade. Before becoming Speaker he was the principal negotiator on a minimum-wage increase and the prime sponsor of the Wage Theft Prevention Act; from 2015 he led the fight for a $15 minimum wage that culminated in 2016 with the enactment of a graduated plan to raise the wage statewide ([New York State Assembly member directory](https://nyassembly.gov/mem/Carl-E-Heastie/about/)). In the pandemic he pushed legislation placing a moratorium on evictions and foreclosures and helped allocate $3 billion in combined federal and state dollars for rent and homeowner relief during the 2021-22 budget negotiations ([New York State Assembly member directory](https://nyassembly.gov/mem/Carl-E-Heastie/about/)).

The list keeps going: a comprehensive 2019 package establishing a nine-day early voting period and streamlining voter registration; extended rent laws with the strongest tenant protections in state history, also in 2019; a 2018-19 budget provision requiring domestic abusers to surrender all guns, plus a 2019 gun-safety package including the Red Flag bill; the 2021 HALT Solitary Confinement Act ending long-term solitary confinement for vulnerable people; the 2021 Marijuana Regulation and Taxation Act, making New York the 15th state to legalize adult-use marijuana; the 2019 Climate Leadership and Community Protection Act; and a spring 2020 legislative package on police accountability after the deaths from police brutality that spring ([New York State Assembly member directory](https://nyassembly.gov/mem/Carl-E-Heastie/about/)).

## A district's story: the 36th and Mamdani

If the Assembly's machinery explains what the chamber is, one district explains how it can launch a career. The 36th District of Queens — covering Astoria and parts of Long Island City — became one of the most watched Assembly seats in America when Zohran Mamdani won the 2020 Democratic primaries and took office as its Assembly member in 2021 ([CNBC TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/world/who-is-zohran-mamdani-indian-origin-assemblyman-contesting-for-nyc-mayoral-polls-mira-nair-son-19625112.htm/amp)). He was re-elected without opposition in both 2022 and 2024 ([CNBC TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/world/who-is-zohran-mamdani-indian-origin-assemblyman-contesting-for-nyc-mayoral-polls-mira-nair-son-19625112.htm/amp)).

[Zohran Mamdani](/people/zohran-mamdani/)'s rise — from DSA-backed primary challenger to a figure whose mayoral bid dominated the 2025 political conversation — played out entirely inside the 150-member chamber he joined in 2021 ([CNBC TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/world/who-is-zohran-mamdani-indian-origin-assemblyman-contesting-for-nyc-mayoral-polls-mira-nair-son-19625112.htm/amp)). His full biography is documented in his own profile; what matters here is the pattern the Assembly has repeated for two and a half centuries — a district seat, a two-year term, and a new face stepping onto a statewide stage.
