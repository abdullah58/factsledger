## Overview

This profile covers **Alt (alt.xyz)** — the alternative-asset platform founded by Leore Avidar, who previously created the API company Lob — and not other businesses using the name "Alt," nor crypto "alt" entities ([Boardroom](https://boardroom.tv/alt-trading-card-investment-platform-launch/)). Alt launched in March 2021 as a marketplace for physical sports trading cards, offering authentication, a Zestimate-style valuation tool called Alt Value, and a temperature- and light-controlled vault for storing cards ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/03/11/sports-trading-card-platform-alt-launches-with-31-million-in-funding-and-plenty-of-market-hype/)). It then built a second act on top of that custody infrastructure: **ALT Advance**, a lending product letting collectors borrow cash against vaulted cards without selling them, funded by a $200 million debt facility ([ABL Advisor](https://www.abladvisor.com/news/34590/atalaya-capital-management-leads-200mm-in-new-debt-funding-for-alt?id=34590)).

## The marketplace and the vault

Alt entered a trading-card market that had exploded during the pandemic, positioning itself as a GOAT-like marketplace that authenticated transactions and gave buyers confidence the cards they were bidding thousands of dollars on were genuine ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/03/11/sports-trading-card-platform-alt-launches-with-31-million-in-funding-and-plenty-of-market-hype/)). Founder and CEO Leore Avidar, a longtime collector, set out to make acquiring alternative assets as easy as stock-trading apps had made buying equities — including by undercutting rivals' fee structures at 1.5% of the total sale price including processing fees ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/03/11/sports-trading-card-platform-alt-launches-with-31-million-in-funding-and-plenty-of-market-hype/)).

Alt will authenticate each asset through an in-house team, then store purchased cards in a user's vault; users can also show off their portfolios through a shareable gallery with high-resolution photos of each collectible ([Boardroom](https://boardroom.tv/alt-trading-card-investment-platform-launch/)). Avidar framed the mission as increasing the transparency and liquidity of alternative assets, arguing that sports cards represented "a great store of value and return uncorrelated with broader public markets" ([Boardroom](https://boardroom.tv/alt-trading-card-investment-platform-launch/)). The launch was backed by more than $31 million: a seed round led by First Round and a Series A led by Reddit co-founder Alexis Ohanian's new firm Seven Seven Six, with John and Patrick Collison, Kevin Durant, SV Angel, BoxGroup and Chapter One among the investors ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/03/11/sports-trading-card-platform-alt-launches-with-31-million-in-funding-and-plenty-of-market-hype/)). Cards had to be graded by established groups (PSA, BGS or SGC) before sale, then were sent to Alt's "Vault" — a light-controlled, temperature-controlled, fire-protected facility Avidar called "the Fort Knox of cards" — where ownership could transfer instantly from buyer to buyer and 99% of cards stayed after transactions, bought as investments rather than display pieces ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/11/collectible-card-trading-platform-alt-raises-75m-as-it-eyes-other-asset-categories-and-preps-its-mobile-launch/)). Alt also published market data — trending categories, biggest value movers, a card's sale history and an "Alt value" estimate from its transaction data ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/11/collectible-card-trading-platform-alt-raises-75m-as-it-eyes-other-asset-categories-and-preps-its-mobile-launch/)).

Eight months later the company raised a $75 million Series B led by Spearhead at a valuation Alt put at over $325 million, backed by Seven Seven Six, Vibe Capital, Breyer Capital, Shrug Capital, Apollo Projects and athletes including Tom Brady, Giannis Antetokounmpo, Alex Morgan, Candace Parker and Marlon Humphrey ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/11/collectible-card-trading-platform-alt-raises-75m-as-it-eyes-other-asset-categories-and-preps-its-mobile-launch/)). By then it had expanded beyond sports cards to first-edition Charizards and Yu-Gi-Oh! cards, held over $70 million of cards in the vault, employed about 60 people and had hired former eBay collectibles chief Nicole Colombo as its first president ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/11/collectible-card-trading-platform-alt-raises-75m-as-it-eyes-other-asset-categories-and-preps-its-mobile-launch/)).

## From custody to credit: ALT Advance

The vault was never just storage — it was the collateral base for a lending business. In July 2022, after quietly beta-testing card-backed loans, Alt announced ALT Advance, which lets investors borrow cash against vaulted assets without selling them, extending the utility of traditional-asset lending to graded collectibles such as trading cards, watches and sneakers ([ABL Advisor](https://www.abladvisor.com/news/34590/atalaya-capital-management-leads-200mm-in-new-debt-funding-for-alt?id=34590)). Alt's head of lending, Mark Ricciardi, said the company planned to extend the product to venture-backed equity interests in pursuit of "unlocking the value of high-quality alternative assets" ([ABL Advisor](https://www.abladvisor.com/news/34590/atalaya-capital-management-leads-200mm-in-new-debt-funding-for-alt?id=34590)).

To fund the lending, Alt raised $200 million in new debt financing led by Atalaya Capital Management ([ABL Advisor](https://www.abladvisor.com/news/34590/atalaya-capital-management-leads-200mm-in-new-debt-funding-for-alt?id=34590)). Investor and adviser Alexis Ohanian called the Atalaya partnership a milestone for the alternative-assets industry, while Atalaya principal Brian Moore said the firm's financial-assets practice was well aligned with Alt's approach to extending financial services' reach ([ABL Advisor](https://www.abladvisor.com/news/34590/atalaya-capital-management-leads-200mm-in-new-debt-funding-for-alt?id=34590)).
