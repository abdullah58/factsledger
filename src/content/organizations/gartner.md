# Gartner

Gartner's most famous instrument began life as a sketch on an easel that its founder never intended to publish. In the 1980s, when the Gartner Group was still small enough for most of its analysts to fit in one room at 50 Topgallant Road in Stamford, Connecticut, Gideon Gartner ran research meetings built around "Stalking Horses" thought experiments. During one meeting, as an analyst presented on a new technology sector, Gideon stepped to the easel and drew a quadrant with two axes bisecting it. "He never meant Quadrants to be published and they never were on his watch." The term "Magic Quadrant" was not used in public until 1994. Nothing about Gartner is more widely copied — and nothing began more casually.

## Overview

Gartner is a research and advisory firm in information technology, founded as the Gartner Group in 1979 in Stamford, Connecticut. It sells research subscriptions, advisory services, and conferences to technology vendors, enterprises, investors, and government organizations, and its Magic Quadrant graphic is one of the most reproduced artifacts in the technology industry.

## The founder

Gideon Gartner was born in 1935 in Mandatory Palestine and grew up in Brooklyn, New York, where he attended Midwood High School. He earned a BS in Mechanical Engineering from MIT and an MBA from MIT's Sloan School of Management. He entered the computer industry at IBM, becoming a marketing manager in the corporation's Data Processing Division and serving as a systems engineer and manager at IBM and IBM World Trade Corporation. He also worked at Philco and the System Development Corporation.

His broad knowledge and industry contacts made him, in the 1970s, the leading analyst focused on information technology on Wall Street, working for Oppenheimer. Having spent years analyzing IT firms, divisions, products and services, he saw an entrepreneurial opportunity and founded the Gartner Group in Stamford, Connecticut, in 1979.

Gideon's product innovations were aimed at busy decision-makers: the company became known for the Magic Quadrant and for one-page, front-and-back analytical reports written for executives in industry and government. Equally important was the business model. Unlike consulting — limited in scope to a shorter-term contract — the advisory-services field he advanced was built on longer-term, open-ended relationships with client firms, spanning report subscriptions, events, and ongoing strategy advice, all aimed at helping clients make better managerial and investment decisions.

## Ownership twists

In 1992 the company was acquired by the London-based Saatchi & Saatchi for about $90 million. Later in the decade, Gartner executives bought the firm back. With the Gartner name no longer his to use for a new firm, Gideon took the first two letters of his first and last name and launched a new market-research and IT-advisory firm, Giga Information Group, Inc., in 1995. As with his prior firm, it thrived.

## The Magic Quadrant

The modern Magic Quadrant is a two-dimensional matrix that positions technology providers on Completeness of Vision (the horizontal axis) against Ability to Execute (the vertical axis). Four quadrant types result: Leaders (top right), Challengers (top left), Visionaries (bottom right), and Niche Players (bottom left). Gartner's research process involves vendors responding to an in-depth technical RFI document and more than 20 customers of each vendor completing lengthy online surveys, with analysts then exercising judgment to place each vendor on the chart.

The instrument has drawn sustained criticism. CIO Wiki summarizes the main objections: its simplicity, subjectivity and blind spots; criticism that its criteria serve investors and large vendors more than buyers; and questions about the money received from the vendors it rates, raising conflict-of-interest concerns. In 2009, software vendor ZL Technologies filed a federal lawsuit challenging the legitimacy of the Magic Quadrant rating system; Gartner moved to dismiss on First Amendment grounds, arguing the reports are "pure opinion" — opinions not based on fact — and the court threw out the case for lack of a specific complaint, a decision upheld on appeal.

## The quadrant today

Sometime in the 1990s the quadrant evolved from internal brainstorming tool into an official Research Note, and there are now hundreds of Magic Quadrants covering sectors from firewalls to cloud infrastructure. In December 2025, [Sheer Logistics](/organizations/sheer-logistics/) announced that it had received an honorable mention in the inaugural 2025 Gartner Magic Quadrant for Fourth-Party Logistics, having previously been named a representative vendor in the 2024 Gartner Market Guide for 4PL. Gartner does not endorse any vendor depicted in its research publications, and its research publications consist of the opinions of its research organization — a disclaimer carried in the announcement itself.
