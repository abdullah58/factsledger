## Overview

Toyota Motor Corporation is a Japanese multinational automaker headquartered in Toyota City, Aichi, [Japan](/places/japan/), founded in 1937 by Kiichiro Toyoda as a spin-off of his family's loom-making business ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)). It is the world's largest automaker by sales, a title it has held every year since 2020, selling a record 11.3 million group vehicles in 2025 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/toyota-retains-top-auto-crown-2025-with-record-sales-2026-01-29/)). Its reputation rests on two things: the Toyota Production System, the lean-manufacturing philosophy copied across industries, and the Prius, the first mass-produced hybrid car ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)).

## From looms to cars

Toyota's origins are in textiles, not cars. Sakichi Toyoda built the Toyoda Automatic Loom Works on nearly a hundred patents, and the 1929 sale of his automatic-loom patent to a British textile manufacturer for one million yen financed his son Kiichiro's move into automobiles ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)). Kiichiro launched the Automobile Division in 1933, and by 1937 it was an independent company, Toyota Motor Company, Ltd., the name chosen because "Toyota" was thought easier to pronounce and luckier — eight strokes in katakana ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)).

The early years were slow: Toyota produced only 1,500 automobiles in its first seven years, finding more success early on in truck manufacturing, and passenger-car production only resumed in 1947 with the compact Toyopet ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)). The American breakthrough came later and gradually — Toyota shipped its first cars to the [United States](/places/united-states/) in 1957, selling just 288 that year, and the Corolla became its first notable US success from 1968 ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)). By 1990 Toyota held a 10 percent share of the US market, and less than two decades later it was manufacturing over two million vehicles a year in the United States ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)).

## The Toyota Production System and the hybrid bet

Toyota is as renowned for how it builds cars as for the cars themselves. Under Eiji Toyoda and machinist Taiichi Ohno, the company developed kanban and kaizen — pull-based parts flow and continuous improvement — into the Toyota Production System, built on just-in-time manufacturing pioneered by Kiichiro Toyoda ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)). American firms that once taught Toyota's executives their methods ended up adopting Toyota's in return by the late twentieth century ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)).

The same long-horizon thinking shaped its bet on hybrids. In the early 1990s Toyota invested over $1 billion in hybrid technology, and the Prius became the first mass-produced hybrid automobile, first sold in Japan in 1997 and worldwide from 2000 ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)). That bet still defines the company's sales mix: gasoline-electric hybrids made up a record 40.8 percent of parent-company sales in 2024 and 42 percent in 2025, while battery-electric vehicles were just 1.4 percent and then 1.9 percent ([Thomson Reuters](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025:newsml_L2N3OQ03J:0-toyota-sells-10-8-million-vehicles-in-2024-to-remain-world-s-top-selling-automaker/); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/toyota-retains-top-auto-crown-2025-with-record-sales-2026-01-29/)). The hybrid-first stance draws the criticism that Toyota lags rivals in pure electric vehicles, even as its 2021 "beyond zero" vision commits it to carbon neutrality across products, services and operations ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)).

Alongside the mainstream range, Toyota built a luxury arm: the Lexus sales network began operating in the United States in September 1989, with the LS 400 flagship developed over six years from 1984 ([Toyota Motor Corporation](https://www.toyota-global.com/company/history_of_toyota/75years/text/leaping_forward_as_a_global_corporation/chapter2/section3/item5.html)). Toyota also expanded through partnerships, taking a 5 percent stake in Mazda in 2017 and announcing a joint $1.6 billion assembly plant in Alabama with Mazda in 2018 ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)).

## Scale today: the sales crown

Toyota first became the world's largest automaker in 2008 and passed 200 million vehicles of cumulative production in 2012 ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)). The scale kept compounding: by September 2023 cumulative global production reached 300 million cars, 88 years and two months after the first G1 truck was built in August 1935, with 180.52 million built in Japan and 119.6 million abroad — and the Corolla alone accounting for 53.399 million of the total ([Toyota Motor Corporation](https://global.toyota/en/newsroom/corporate/39996185.html)).

Leadership changed with the record year: Kenta Kon, previously chief financial officer, became president and CEO on April 1, 2026, succeeding Koji Sato, who moved to vice chairman and chief industry officer, with Akio Toyoda remaining chairman ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)). The company employs over 390,000 people and was ranked seventeenth on Forbes' 2026 list of the world's biggest public companies ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)).

## Setbacks and scrutiny

Toyota's reputation for reliability has been dented twice in the last two decades. A series of safety recalls between 2009 and 2014 tarnished its record, and in 2014 the company agreed to pay a $1.2 billion fine to settle a US Justice Department criminal investigation for failing to report known safety defects in over 12 million vehicles worldwide ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)). A decade later, in 2024, Japan's transport ministry issued Toyota a correction order after irregularities in vehicle-certification testing, part of the governance fallout that also hit its Daihatsu subsidiary and helped push 2024 group sales down 3.7 percent ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/); [Thomson Reuters](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025:newsml_L2N3OQ03J:0-toyota-sells-10-8-million-vehicles-in-2024-to-remain-world-s-top-selling-automaker/)). Earlier, the 2011 earthquake and tsunami in Japan had temporarily dropped Toyota's production ranking to third in the world ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)).

## The GM connection and the voice-AI cabin

Toyota's history is entangled with its Detroit rivals. In 1984 it joined [General Motors](/organizations/general-motors/) in NUMMI, resurrecting a closed GM plant in Fremont, California to build vehicles under both brands — a partnership that lasted 25 years and firmly established Toyota in the US market ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)). Decades later the three automakers in this batch share a supplier: [Cerence](/organizations/cerence/), the conversational-AI company spun off from [Nuance Communications](/organizations/nuance-communications/) on October 1, 2019, whose technology powered nearly 300 million vehicles in more than 70 languages as of 2020 and supported nearly all major automakers, including Toyota, [Ford](/organizations/ford/) and GM ([CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2020/10/08/nio-cerence-collaboration-on-voice-tech/)). That shared supplier is a quiet marker of how the industry's competition has shifted from engines to the software inside the cabin.

## FAQ

**Who founded Toyota?**
Kiichiro Toyoda founded Toyota Motor Company in 1937, building on his father Sakichi Toyoda's loom-making business; the 1929 sale of Sakichi's automatic-loom patent financed the move into cars ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)).

**Is Toyota the largest automaker in the world?**
Yes. Toyota sold a record 11.3 million group vehicles in 2025, its sixth consecutive year as the world's top-selling automaker, ahead of Volkswagen at just under 9 million ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/toyota-retains-top-auto-crown-2025-with-record-sales-2026-01-29/)).

**What is the Toyota Production System?**
A lean-manufacturing system built on just-in-time production and kaizen (continuous improvement), developed under Eiji Toyoda and Taiichi Ohno from the 1950s, and one of the most studied and copied management philosophies in modern business ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)).

**What was the first hybrid car?**
The Toyota Prius, launched in Japan in 1997 and sold worldwide from 2000, was the first mass-produced hybrid automobile; hybrids still make up over 40 percent of Toyota's sales ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)).

**Who leads Toyota now?**
Kenta Kon became president and CEO on April 1, 2026, succeeding Koji Sato, who moved to vice chairman and chief industry officer; Akio Toyoda is chairman ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)).
