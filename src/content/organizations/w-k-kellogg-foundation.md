## Overview

The W.K. Kellogg Foundation is an independent American private foundation based in Battle Creek, [Michigan](/places/michigan/), founded in 1930 by cereal pioneer Will Keith Kellogg to improve the health, happiness and well-being of children. Originally named the W.K. Kellogg Child Welfare Foundation, it now funds early childhood education, health equity, food systems, economic security and racial equity, concentrating most of its grants in Michigan, Mississippi, New Mexico and New Orleans ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/W-K-Kellogg-Foundation); [Inside Philanthropy](https://www.insidephilanthropy.com/find-a-grant/grants-k/wk-kellogg-foundation)).

The foundation is distinct from the Kellogg Company, the cereal business its founder also built, and its endowment is held separately by the W.K. Kellogg Foundation Trust, which Kellogg established and funded alongside the foundation in the 1930s ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/W-K-Kellogg-Foundation); [W.K. Kellogg Foundation](https://www.wkkf.org/news-and-media/article/2026/04/by-the-numbers-3/)). In the fiscal year ending Aug. 31, 2025, the foundation distributed over $400 million for charitable purposes, and combined foundation-and-trust assets stood at more than $10 billion, with over $13 billion distributed across its 95-year history ([W.K. Kellogg Foundation](https://www.wkkf.org/news-and-media/article/2026/04/by-the-numbers-3/)).

## From rural clinics to a national funder

Kellogg's first major initiative was the Michigan Community Health Project, which served seven southwestern Michigan counties — Allegan, Barry, Branch, Calhoun, Eaton, Hillsdale and Van Buren — offering rural children hearing tests, eye examinations, immunizations and school lunches ([W.K. Kellogg Foundation](https://wkkf.issuelab.org/resources/35354/35354.pdf); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/W-K-Kellogg-Foundation)). The project ran until the 1940s, and the Battle Creek area remains a priority place today. In 1943 the foundation answered requests from Latin American countries worried about child nutrition, funding studies of Indigenous foods and diets that led to nutrition programs across [Mexico](/places/mexico/), Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, Panama and Ecuador, including the Institute of Nutrition of Central America and Panama ([W.K. Kellogg Foundation](https://wkkf.issuelab.org/resources/35354/35354.pdf)). By 2005 the foundation had awarded more than $4 billion in grants, including $1.6 billion to people and organizations in Michigan, and its endowment had grown from Kellogg's lifetime gift of $66 million in company stock and other investments to nearly $7 billion ([Congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-151/issue-95/extensions-of-remarks-section/article/E1490-3)).

## How the foundation gives today

The foundation organizes its grantmaking around five areas: early care and education; health and health equity; food systems; jobs and family economic security; and the promise of an equitable future, with racial equity, racial healing, community engagement and leadership development running across all of them ([Inside Philanthropy](https://www.insidephilanthropy.com/find-a-grant/grants-k/wk-kellogg-foundation)). It concentrates about two-thirds of its grantmaking in priority places — Michigan, Mississippi, [New Mexico](/places/new-mexico/) and New Orleans in the United States, and the Chiapas highlands and Yucatán Peninsula in Mexico and central and southwest Haiti — while also funding national organizations ([Grantmakers in Health](https://www.gih.org/grantmaker-profile/w-k-kellogg-foundation/)). Its work starts young, focusing on children from birth through age eight, when investment makes the biggest difference to long-term development ([Grantmakers in Health](https://www.gih.org/grantmaker-profile/w-k-kellogg-foundation/)). It accepts unsolicited letters of inquiry year-round and uses no fixed grant cycles, though full proposals are by invitation only ([Inside Philanthropy](https://www.insidephilanthropy.com/find-a-grant/grants-k/wk-kellogg-foundation)). Its international reach is old as well as wide: in 1976 an operating grant helped launch [Colleges and Institutes Canada](/organizations/colleges-and-institutes-canada/)'s Canadian Studies Office, an international program and a publication house ([Colleges and Institutes Canada](https://www.collegesinstitutes.ca/cicans-50th-anniversary/evolution-of-cican/)).

## Racial equity, racial healing and Racial Equity 2030

Racial equity and racial healing are central to the foundation's current work. It was among the first foundations to frame its funding around health equity and the social determinants of health, and it treats racism — and the false belief in a hierarchy of human value — as a determinant of health itself ([Grantmakers in Health](https://www.gih.org/grantmaker-profile/w-k-kellogg-foundation/)). Its Truth, Racial Healing & Transformation enterprise, led by senior advisor Gail Christopher, began community-by-community implementation in 2017, and the foundation also hosts an annual National Day of Racial Healing ([Grantmakers in Health](https://www.gih.org/grantmaker-profile/w-k-kellogg-foundation/); [Inside Philanthropy](https://www.insidephilanthropy.com/find-a-grant/grants-k/wk-kellogg-foundation)). In 2020, for its 90th anniversary, it launched Racial Equity 2030, a $90 million global challenge that drew 1,453 submissions from 72 countries; in October 2022 it named five awardees to share $80 million over eight years ([Lever for Change](https://leverforchange.org/article/press-release/wk-kellogg-foundation-announces-five-awardees-to-receive-80-million-to-advance-global-racial-equity/)).

## FAQ

### What is the W.K. Kellogg Foundation?

It is an independent American private foundation based in Battle Creek, Michigan, founded in 1930 by cereal pioneer Will Keith Kellogg to promote the health, happiness and well-being of children. It funds early childhood education, health equity, food systems, economic security and racial equity, concentrating about two-thirds of its grants in priority places in the U.S., Mexico and Haiti.

### Is the W.K. Kellogg Foundation part of the Kellogg Company?

No. The foundation is a separate organization from the cereal company, though both were created by Will Keith Kellogg and are based in Battle Creek. The foundation's endowment is held separately by the W.K. Kellogg Foundation Trust, which Kellogg established and funded alongside the foundation in the 1930s.

### Who leads the W.K. Kellogg Foundation?

La June Montgomery Tabron, who was named president and CEO in October 2013, becoming the first woman and the first African American to lead the foundation. Born in [Detroit](/places/detroit/), she joined the foundation in 1987 as a financial controller and rose through finance and operations roles before taking the top job.

### What is Racial Equity 2030?

It is the foundation's $90 million global challenge, launched in 2020 for its 90th anniversary, seeking bold solutions to racial inequity. It drew 1,453 submissions from 72 countries, and in October 2022 five awardees were named to share $80 million over eight years, working on anti-racist education, youth mental health, Indigenous land rights, environmental law and ending youth incarceration.

### Where does the foundation concentrate its grants?

About two-thirds of its grantmaking goes to priority places: Michigan, Mississippi, New Mexico and New Orleans in the United States, and the Chiapas highlands and Yucatán Peninsula in Mexico and central and southwest Haiti. It also funds national organizations, and accepts unsolicited letters of inquiry year-round.
