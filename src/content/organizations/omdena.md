## Overview

Omdena is a global collaborative platform that organizes volunteer AI engineers and data scientists into teams to build AI solutions for social problems — for nonprofits, development organizations and impact startups rather than paying enterprise clients. It was started in 2019 by Rudradeb Mitra, an AI researcher who, as he told the New Books Network, had concluded after years in AI research and startups that making money was not his primary goal, and who saw hiring bias and unequal access to AI opportunity as problems worth designing around ([New Books Network](https://newbooksnetwork.com/building-a-global-ai-community-a-conversation-with-rudradeb-mitra)). By around 2023, Mitra reported that the platform had run more than 320 projects with 120 startups and nonprofit organizations, involving over 10,000 people from 105 countries, with local chapters in more than 60 countries ([New Books Network](https://newbooksnetwork.com/building-a-global-ai-community-a-conversation-with-rudradeb-mitra)).

## How the model works

The core unit is the challenge: an organization posts a social problem it wants solved with AI, and Omdena assembles a selected global group of engineers and enthusiasts to collaborate through its platform for about two months ([AI Time Journal](https://aitimejournal.com/ai-for-good-interview-with-rudradeb-mitra-founder-of-omdena/)). Mitra positions the model explicitly against competitive platforms such as Kaggle: "all other platforms are competitive in nature while in Omdena people collaborate. I do not understand why to compete if we can collaborate" ([AI Time Journal](https://aitimejournal.com/ai-for-good-interview-with-rudradeb-mitra-founder-of-omdena/)). Newcomers first get access to educational resources and practice projects; after completing those they can join real challenges, and strong performers are paired with programs and helped toward paid work with startups — what the New Books Network describes as a mix between a business and a school ([New Books Network](https://newbooksnetwork.com/building-a-global-ai-community-a-conversation-with-rudradeb-mitra)).

Mitra's own founding account, given in a late-2019 interview, traces the idea to a year of world travel in 2018: online AI education had made knowledge accessible even in remote places, but access to *opportunity* was still concentrated in major cities — and while "AI4Good" was much discussed, little was actually being built ([AI Time Journal](https://aitimejournal.com/ai-for-good-interview-with-rudradeb-mitra-founder-of-omdena/)).

## Projects and partners

The early challenges read like an AI-for-development portfolio. By November 2019 Omdena had run 12 challenges, including work with the World Food Programme in Nepal to identify crop types from satellite images for food-stock planning, and with UNHCR in Somalia on the relationship between climate anomalies and conflicts — toward a model that could potentially predict future conflicts ([AI Time Journal](https://aitimejournal.com/ai-for-good-interview-with-rudradeb-mitra-founder-of-omdena/)). Other early challenges addressed sexual harassment in India, gang violence in Chicago, PTSD diagnosis and earthquake disaster management for Istanbul ([AI Time Journal](https://aitimejournal.com/ai-for-good-interview-with-rudradeb-mitra-founder-of-omdena/)). One participant's 2019 account of the Chicago challenge describes joining a sentiment-analysis team (classifying tweets as threatening or not) in a 30-person team from 15 countries working remotely for eight weeks ([Medium](https://medium.com/@frederickauthur/my-journey-solving-problems-working-with-omdena-2a9b0704c5f9)).

Institutional partners named in independent coverage include UNHCR, the World Resources Institute, the World Energy Council, the World Food Programme, UNDP and Save the Children — named by Mitra in the New Books Network interview, which also lists the [UN](/organizations/united-nations/) itself among organizations that have worked with the platform ([New Books Network](https://newbooksnetwork.com/building-a-global-ai-community-a-conversation-with-rudradeb-mitra); [Analytics Insight](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/amp/story/latest-news/this-new-incubator-will-bring-ai-to-50-impact-startups-in-2021)). In December 2020 Omdena announced an incubator program to support 50 impact startups building AI solutions in 2021, backed by tool partnerships with companies including Activeloop, Miro and Labelbox; a pre-launch version had already served startups such as XPRIZE contestant Zzapp Malaria ([Analytics Insight](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/amp/story/latest-news/this-new-incubator-will-bring-ai-to-50-impact-startups-in-2021)). By 2024 the platform was still running local-chapter challenges — one participant documented the Nakuru (Kenya) chapter's project modeling climate change's impact on maize yields, from data collection through a deployed Streamlit app for local stakeholders ([Medium](https://medium.com/@lintosunny111/from-idea-to-impact-completing-my-first-omdena-challenge-e70c50e72b3b)).

## What this profile does not claim

- All scale figures — project counts, engineer counts, country counts — are founder self-reports in interviews, and they shift across dates (1,700 engineers from 86 countries in December 2020; 10,000+ people from 105 countries around 2023). They are not independently audited; this profile reports them as reported, with dates.
- The profile does not state Omdena's funding, revenue or legal-entity details; no citable source for any of these was found.
- A Forbes contributor article on Omdena ("why Omdena is here to lead") was referenced by Analytics Insight but could not be fetched and is not cited here.
- The UN-family partnership list rests on the founder's statements in independent interviews, not on the partner organizations' own records.
- Building a sustainable business was described by Mitra himself as Omdena's main challenge; this profile does not claim it has been solved ([AI Time Journal](https://aitimejournal.com/ai-for-good-interview-with-rudradeb-mitra-founder-of-omdena/)).
