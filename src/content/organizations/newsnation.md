# NewsNation

## The gap: a launch built on local newsrooms, not studio opinion

Most coverage of NewsNation treats it as another entry in the cable-news branding wars: the WGN America rebrand, the "News for All Americans" slogan, the arrival of Chris Cuomo. What those accounts tend to skip is the part of the story Nexstar itself put in writing on launch night, and which still shapes the network's operating model. When NewsNation debuted on September 1, 2020, it was not a full channel but a three-hour primetime newscast, running 8 to 11 p.m. ET seven days a week on WGN America [^1]. Behind it sat roughly 150 dedicated reporters and producers, but the deeper bench was the owner's: Nexstar said the newscast would draw on the resources of its 5,400 local journalists across 110 local newsrooms, and the company backed the launch with a $100 million commitment to promote NewsNation in its own local markets [^1]. The reach at launch was about 75 million U.S. television households, and the founding anchor team included Joe Donlon, Marni Hughes, Rob Nelson, and Albert Ramon on weekdays, with Rudabeh Shahbazi, Nichole Berlie, Aaron Nolan, and Gerard Jebaily on weekends [^1].

That model — a national desk grafted onto the country's largest local-station portfolio — is the undercovered core of the NewsNation story. The press releases kept restating it for years: by December 2025, the figure Nexstar cited had grown to 6,000 journalists in 110 newsrooms, and the network was fully distributed on every pay television platform in the United States [^2].

## From newscast block to 24/7 network

NewsNation began life as a programming block and became a full network in stages. The rebrand of WGN America as NewsNation arrived in March 2021 [^3]. The most consequential operational step followed: on June 1, 2024, the network expanded to a 24/7 schedule [^4]. The numbers attached to that move were striking — Nexstar said primetime viewership was up nearly 210 percent since the March 2021 rebrand, the network reached about 67 million households, and the expansion came with talent additions including Anna Kooiman, Laura Ingle, and Alicia Nieves [^4].

The lineup kept shifting. On January 15, 2025, Nexstar announced that *On Balance with Leland Vittert* would move to 9 p.m. ET and *Elizabeth Vargas Reports* to 7 p.m. ET, effective February 10, 2025 [^5]. Then on December 17, 2025, the company announced another expansion of live news programming to debut in late January 2026: a new weeknight 11 p.m. ET hour hosted by the network's legal contributor Jesse Weber, more weekend live-news hours, *Morning in America* weekend editions with Hena Doba starting at 6 a.m. ET, and *Batya!* — hosted by Batya Ungar-Sargon — on Saturdays at 7 p.m. ET with a Sunday encore [^2].

## Talent, identity, and the neutrality argument

The Columbia Journalism Review's 2026 feature described NewsNation as a haven for television-news exiles: Fox News alumni including Connell McShane, Laura Ingle, and Chris Stirewalt, along with Geraldo Rivera, former ABC *World News Tonight* anchor Elizabeth Vargas, and Michael Corn, the former head of ABC's *Good Morning America* [^3]. The network's most prominent face is Chris Cuomo, whom CNN fired in 2021 after it surfaced that he had helped his brother, then-New York governor Andrew Cuomo, respond to sexual-harassment allegations; on NewsNation, Cuomo's show was averaging around 260,000 viewers, against about 1.4 million at CNN [^3]. Total-day viewership had grown 65 percent since the network's first year, though CJR noted it remains a marginal presence next to its rivals [^3].

The neutrality pitch has been contested from early on. Within months of launch, NewsNation's stated impartiality was questioned by its own staffers [^3]. The pressure intensified around Nexstar's pending acquisition of Tegna — a $3.54 billion deal that rests on Federal Communications Commission approval, since the combined company would reach roughly 80 percent of U.S. households against a 39 percent ownership cap [^3]. FCC chair Brendan Carr publicly backed the deal, President Donald Trump pushed for it, and Newsmax CEO Chris Ruddy argued against it on Capitol Hill; a *Status* report quoted staffers saying the network had moved "hard right to appeal to Trump and Brendan Carr" while the deal was pending [^3]. NewsNation counters with audience data: an MRI research survey it shared showed 40 percent of its audience self-identifying as Republican, 33 percent as Democrats, and 27 percent as independent or otherwise affiliated [^3].

## The Leavitt farewell interview

One concrete recent example of the network as a newsmaking platform came on Thursday, August 27, 2026 — the final day of [Karoline Leavitt](/people/karoline-leavitt/) as White House press secretary. Leavitt sat down with NewsNation's *Morning in America* on her last day [^6]. She had been 27 when appointed, making her the youngest press secretary in U.S. history, and she had served 585 days — the second-longest tenure of any of Trump's press secretaries [^6]. She turned 29 that same week and was stepping down roughly four months after welcoming her second child, born in May 2026 [^7]. She told viewers the role had been "the adventure of a lifetime" [^7]. Leavitt was heading to [MAGA Inc.](/organizations/maga-inc/), and no successor had been named, though Scott Jennings was rumored as a candidate [^6].
