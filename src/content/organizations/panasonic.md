## Overview

Panasonic Holdings Corporation is a major Japanese manufacturer of electric appliances, consumer electronics, rechargeable automotive batteries and residential heating and cooling systems, headquartered in Kadoma, near Osaka ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Panasonic)). It was founded in Osaka in March 1918 by Konosuke Matsushita as a maker of lamp sockets, grew under the Matsushita name into one of [Japan](/places/japan/)'s largest industrial groups, took the Panasonic name in 2008, and became a holding company in April 2022 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Panasonic); [Panasonic Holdings Corporation](https://holdings.panasonic/global/corporate/phd/history.html)). In fiscal 2025 it reported net sales of 8.46 trillion yen ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/panasonic-fy25-profit-declines-sales-down-slightly-05-reduce-10000-employees)).

The company's story is one of repeated reinvention: from wiring instruments to radios and televisions, from the VHS format to Hollywood studios, and from consumer electronics to electric-vehicle batteries and supply-chain software. Its best-known modern pivot is the battery partnership with Tesla, agreed in 2014, which made Panasonic a central supplier to the electric-vehicle industry ([Tesla Motors, Inc.](https://ir.tesla.com/press-release/panasonic-and-tesla-sign-agreement-gigafactory)). Less widely noted is that three Panasonic units were among the companies sued in 2004 over the JPEG patent at the centre of the Forgent Networks litigation ([eWeek](http://www.eweek.com/enterprise-apps/jpeg-hits-new-patent-infringement-snag)).

## From lamp sockets to a global electronics group

Konosuke Matsushita founded Matsushita Electric Housewares Manufacturing Works in March 1918 at Ohiraki-cho, Fukushima-ku, Osaka, to manufacture wiring instruments ([Panasonic Holdings Corporation](https://holdings.panasonic/global/corporate/phd/history.html)). He had set out to manufacture and market the electric lamp sockets and plugs he designed himself, and the company was incorporated in 1935 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Panasonic)). The "National" brand was established in April 1927, and in May 1933 the head office and factory moved to Kadoma, where the company is still based ([Panasonic Holdings Corporation](https://holdings.panasonic/global/corporate/phd/history.html)). During the 1930s it added irons, radios, phonographs and light bulbs, and in the 1950s it began producing transistor radios, television sets, tape recorders, stereo equipment and large household appliances ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Panasonic)).

Growth came through alliances as well as products. In December 1952 Matsushita Electronics Corporation was established through a technology alliance with Philips of the Netherlands ([Panasonic Holdings Corporation](https://holdings.panasonic/global/corporate/phd/history.html)). In the 1960s and 1970s the company added microwave ovens, air conditioners and videotape recorders, and in 1976 its JVC subsidiary introduced the VHS video format, which eventually beat out Sony's Betamax to become the videotape standard ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Panasonic)). Beyond consumer goods it built minicomputers, telephone equipment, electric motors, chemical and solar batteries, cathode-ray tubes, measuring instruments, copy machines and traffic-control devices ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Panasonic)).

The company also made one of the era's boldest diversifications: in December 1990 it acquired MCA Inc., a leading U.S. entertainment company, sold 80% of its MCA shares to Seagram in June 1995, and sold the remaining shares of the Universal Studios affiliate to Vivendi Universal in February 2006 ([Panasonic Holdings Corporation](https://holdings.panasonic/global/corporate/phd/history.html)). In April 2003 it unified its corporate brands as "Panasonic" worldwide, and in October 2008 it changed its legal name from Matsushita Electric Industrial Co., Ltd. to Panasonic Corporation ([Panasonic Holdings Corporation](https://holdings.panasonic/global/corporate/phd/history.html)). Around the same time it merged its JVC division with rival Kenwood to create JVCKenwood Corp., and the following year it acquired rival Sanyo, phasing out the Sanyo name a few years later ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Panasonic)).

## Batteries, supply chains and the post-consumer pivot

Panasonic's most consequential recent move was into batteries for electric vehicles. On 31 July 2014 Panasonic Corporation and Tesla Motors signed an agreement to build the Gigafactory, a large-scale battery manufacturing plant in the United States ([Tesla Motors, Inc.](https://ir.tesla.com/press-release/panasonic-and-tesla-sign-agreement-gigafactory)). Under the agreement Panasonic would manufacture and supply cylindrical lithium-ion cells and invest in the associated equipment, while Tesla would provide the land, buildings and utilities and assemble the cells into battery modules and packs ([Tesla Motors, Inc.](https://ir.tesla.com/press-release/panasonic-and-tesla-sign-agreement-gigafactory)). Panasonic was to occupy about half of the planned manufacturing space, and the plant was planned to produce 35 GWh of cells and 50 GWh of packs per year by 2020 ([Tesla Motors, Inc.](https://ir.tesla.com/press-release/panasonic-and-tesla-sign-agreement-gigafactory)). A 2016 agreement then had Panasonic supplying batteries for Tesla's Model 3, Model S and Model X electric vehicles ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Panasonic)).

The pivot away from low-margin consumer electronics continued with software. In September 2021 Panasonic made Blue Yonder Holding, Inc. — having taken a 20% stake in July 2020 — a wholly-owned subsidiary ([Panasonic Holdings Corporation](https://holdings.panasonic/global/corporate/phd/history.html)). Britannica describes Blue Yonder Group, formerly JDA Software, as a supply-chain management software giant, and the acquisition as one of Panasonic's notable 21st-century initiatives ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Panasonic)). In January 2022 the company announced a new environmental vision, "Panasonic GREEN IMPACT", and in April 2022 it became a holding company, transferring each business to nine companies including the operating companies and changing its name to Panasonic Holdings Corporation ([Panasonic Holdings Corporation](https://holdings.panasonic/global/corporate/phd/history.html)). Yuki Kusumi had become Representative Director and President in June 2021 ([Panasonic Holdings Corporation](https://holdings.panasonic/global/corporate/phd/history.html)).

The scale remains large but the direction is shifting. In 2023 more than half of the company's $60 billion in sales came from foreign markets in Europe and North America, with the other half split between the domestic market in Japan and the rest of Asia ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Panasonic)). In May 2025 the company announced a management reform to resolve structural issues: a thorough review of operational efficiency at each group company, mainly in sales and indirect departments, termination of loss-making businesses with no prospect of improving profit, and the integration and closing of sites ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/panasonic-fy25-profit-declines-sales-down-slightly-05-reduce-10000-employees)). The reform targets 10,000 employees — 5,000 in Japan and 5,000 overseas — to be implemented mainly in fiscal 2026, with the aim of improving profit by at least 150 billion yen compared with fiscal 2025 and achieving adjusted operating profit of at least 600 billion yen in fiscal 2027 ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/panasonic-fy25-profit-declines-sales-down-slightly-05-reduce-10000-employees)).

## The JPEG patent suits and the Asure Software connection

In April 2004 Forgent Networks filed two patent infringement lawsuits in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Texas against 31 companies, seeking injunctions and damages over digital image compression technology it said was fundamental to the JPEG format ([eWeek](http://www.eweek.com/enterprise-apps/jpeg-hits-new-patent-infringement-snag)). Forgent held the patent through its subsidiary [Compression Labs](/organizations/compression-labs/), which it had acquired when its predecessor VTEL Corp. bought Compression Labs in the mid-1990s ([eWeek](http://www.eweek.com/enterprise-apps/jpeg-hits-new-patent-infringement-snag)). Three of the companies named were Panasonic units: Matsushita Electric Corp. of America, Panasonic Communications Corp. of America, and Panasonic Mobile Communications Development Corp. of USA ([eWeek](http://www.eweek.com/enterprise-apps/jpeg-hits-new-patent-infringement-snag)).

The patent had been issued in 1987, and Forgent began seeking licences about two years before the suits; in mid-2002 it reached a $16 million licensing deal with [Sony](/organizations/sony/), and in all it had reached deals with 30 companies totalling $90 million in licensing revenue ([eWeek](http://www.eweek.com/enterprise-apps/jpeg-hits-new-patent-infringement-snag)). The episode is the direct link between Panasonic and [Asure Software](/organizations/asure-software/): Forgent Networks is the former name of Asure Software, the Austin company founded in 1985 as Forgent Networks that later became a cloud payroll and HR software provider ([Asure Software](https://factsledger.com/organizations/asure-software/)). The JPEG-related patent was due to expire in October 2006, though Forgent said it could continue to seek claims of past infringement ([eWeek](http://www.eweek.com/enterprise-apps/jpeg-hits-new-patent-infringement-snag)).
