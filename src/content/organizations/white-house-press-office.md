## Overview

The White House Press Office, headed by the White House press secretary, is the primary spokesperson for the White House ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/White_House_Press_Secretary)). Its daily briefings are one of the most visible rituals of the American presidency: the press secretary conveys the president's messages, manages press briefings, and serves as a liaison between the executive branch and the news media ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/White_House_Press_Secretary)).

## A century of the role

The position was formally established in 1929 during the administration of President Herbert Hoover, with George Akerson appointed as the first official holder of the title ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/White_House_Press_Secretary)). Before that, communication with the press was handled informally by secretaries or aides. According to the National Archives, the role was created in response to the growing influence of mass media and the increasing demand for regular, structured communication between the presidency and the public ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/White_House_Press_Secretary)).

Over the decades the office grew alongside changes in media. The introduction of radio, television, and later digital platforms expanded the visibility and scope of the press secretary's duties, and notable developments included the establishment of the James S. Brady Press Briefing Room in the West Wing and the increasing frequency of televised briefings ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/White_House_Press_Secretary)). The role has been held by figures from varied backgrounds — journalism, law, and political strategy — and during Trump's first term alone it cycled through four holders listed by Ballotpedia: Sean Spicer (2017), [Sarah Huckabee Sanders](/people/sarah-huckabee-sanders/) (2017–2019), Stephanie Grisham (2019–2020), and [Kayleigh McEnany](/people/kayleigh-mcenany/) (2020–2021) ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/White_House_Press_Secretary)). Under President Biden, the role passed from Jen Psaki to [Karine Jean-Pierre](/people/karine-jean-pierre/) (2022–2025) ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/White_House_Press_Secretary)). The same table records some of the office's longest and most dramatic tenures: James Hagerty served all eight Eisenhower years (1953–1961), and James Brady held the title under Reagan from 1981 to 1989 though he never briefed the press again after being paralyzed in the 1981 assassination attempt on the president ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/White_House_Press_Secretary)).

## Karoline Leavitt: the youngest ever, 2025–2026

[Karoline Leavitt](/people/karoline-leavitt/) took the office at the start of Trump's second term in January 2025, becoming the youngest White House press secretary in history at age 27 ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/commentary/2026/karoline-leavitt-says-goodbye-as-white-house-press-secretary/)). She held the job for more than 19 months, longer than any of Trump's first-term press secretaries.

Her final day came on Thursday, August 27, 2026 — her last day on the government payroll — which she announced in a final-day interview with NewsNation's "Morning in America" anchor Markie Martin ([People](https://people.com/karoline-leavitt-last-day-press-secretary-update-on-repalcement-search-12069328)). The reason she gave was her family: a two-year-old son, Niko, and a daughter, Viviana, born in May 2026, with husband Nicholas Riccio. "I am a mom and I am a wife, and I am going to go home and take care of my babies," she said, describing the job as "the honor of a lifetime" ([People](https://people.com/karoline-leavitt-last-day-press-secretary-update-on-repalcement-search-12069328)). Trump praised her service publicly: "Nobody did it better than her — she did a GREAT job!" ([People](https://people.com/karoline-leavitt-last-day-press-secretary-update-on-repalcement-search-12069328)).

Leavitt is not leaving Trump's orbit. Trump announced she would continue as a top outside adviser working for his super PAC, MAGA Inc., keeping her at the center of the 2026 midterm and 2028 operations ([People](https://people.com/karoline-leavitt-last-day-press-secretary-update-on-repalcement-search-12069328)). Before departing, she joked on NewsNation that Trump — whom she called the "true communications director and press secretary" — would be just fine holding the briefing room himself ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/commentary/2026/karoline-leavitt-says-goodbye-as-white-house-press-secretary/)).

## A successor search with no frontrunner

As of September 30, 2026, no permanent successor has been named, and the office is in an unusual interregnum. CBS reported that, with Leavitt gone, various White House officials would rotate through briefing duties, and Poynter notes there is no public frontrunner for the permanent role ([People](https://people.com/karoline-leavitt-last-day-press-secretary-update-on-repalcement-search-12069328); [Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/commentary/2026/karoline-leavitt-says-goodbye-as-white-house-press-secretary/)). Names floated in coverage include CNN political commentator Scott Jennings, White House principal deputy press secretary Harrison Fields, communications director Steven Cheung, State Department chief of staff Michael Anton, former deputy communications director Margo Martin, State Department spokeswoman Tammy Bruce, and former CNN contributor Kayleigh McEnany, among others ([People](https://people.com/karoline-leavitt-last-day-press-secretary-update-on-repalcement-search-12069328)). Trump himself told reporters at the time that he had a replacement "in mind," but weeks later the office remained under rotating coverage ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/commentary/2026/karoline-leavitt-says-goodbye-as-white-house-press-secretary/)).
