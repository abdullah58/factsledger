## Overview

UnitedHealth Group is the largest health insurance provider in the [United States](/places/united-states/), a for-profit parent company that sells insurance through its UnitedHealthcare arm and health services through Optum — a two-engine model spanning insurance, care delivery, pharmacy benefits, and data analytics that made it No. 3 on the 2025 Fortune 500 with $400.28 billion in 2024 revenue ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/rankings-and-ratings/fortune-500s-top-25-healthcare-companies/)). It serves more than 29 million Americans and more than 50 million people worldwide ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/UnitedHealth-Group)).

This profile is about the **parent company**, not to be confused with its insurance subsidiary. [UnitedHealthcare](/organizations/unitedhealthcare/) — the insurer, headquartered in Minnetonka, Minnesota — is one of two operating platforms; the Group itself is headquartered in Eden Prairie, Minnesota. The other platform is Optum, the health-services business. The company was founded in 1974 by Richard Taylor Burke as Charter Med Incorporated, became United Healthcare Corporation in 1977, was chosen as [AARP](/organizations/aarp/)'s insurance provider in 1997, and reorganized as UnitedHealth Group in 1998 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/UnitedHealth-Group)). Since May 2025 its chief executive has been Stephen J. Hemsley, who returned to the role after Andrew Witty resigned ([NPR](https://www.mtpr.org/2025-05-13/unitedhealth-group-abruptly-replaces-ceo-andrew-witty-deepening-a-terrible-year)).

## The two-engine model: UnitedHealthcare and Optum

UnitedHealth Group's defining feature is that it is not just an insurer. UnitedHealthcare provides the insurance plans — employer and individual coverage, Medicare Advantage and Part D, Medicaid, and international plans — and accounts for over half of the company's revenue ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/UnitedHealth-Group)). Optum, created in 2011 from the company's health-services businesses, does everything else: Optum Health owns or manages clinics and delivers care, Optum Insight sells analytics and digital services, and Optum Rx runs the company's pharmacy benefit management business ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/UnitedHealth-Group)).

The economics show why the model matters. In 2024, UnitedHealthcare reported $298.2 billion in revenue (up 6%) while Optum reported $253 billion (up 12%), on consolidated revenue of $400.3 billion; the insurance arm added 2.4 million commercial members and Optum Health served 4.7 million patients in value-based care arrangements ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/unitedhealth-group-posts-144b-profit-4003b-revenue-2024)). The company keeps expanding the services engine: it acquired the payment processor Change Healthcare for $13 billion despite a [Justice Department](/organizations/us-department-of-justice/) challenge, after a February 2024 cyberattack on that same company ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/UnitedHealth-Group); [NPR](https://www.mtpr.org/2025-05-13/unitedhealth-group-abruptly-replaces-ceo-andrew-witty-deepening-a-terrible-year)).

## The $9 billion AARP advance — from the payer's side

In 2024, UnitedHealth restructured its decades-old royalty agreement with AARP and handed the nonprofit a single advance payment of **$9.062 billion** — in exchange for an exclusive license to use AARP's trade name and intellectual property in marketing a health insurance program, with AARP recognizing the royalties over the 12-year life of the deal, roughly $755 million a year ([Juniper Research Group](http://www.juniperresearchgroup.com/post/7233/the-9000000000-question-how-can-anyone-trust-aarp), quoting AARP's financial statements). The $9 billion is about 31 times what AARP collects each year in member dues and more than four times its total 2024 operating revenues ([DC Journal](https://dcjournal.com/aarps-9-billion-reasons-to-side-with-unitedhealth-and-make-obamacare-subsidies-permanent/)). The implication for UnitedHealth: the nation's largest insurer prepaid more than a decade of brand-licensing fees to a single partner.

The advance sits on top of an already enormous flow: AARP is estimated to have received $10.8 billion in royalties from UnitedHealth since 2007, excluding the $9.062 billion, with total royalty revenue of nearly $17.6 billion over 25 years, per an analysis by the nonprofit [American Commitment](/organizations/american-commitment/) of AARP's Form 990 filings ([Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/op-eds/4648693/aarp-membership-dues-unitedhealth-group/)). In late January 2026, CEO Stephen Hemsley testified before Congress that UnitedHealth would "voluntarily eliminate and rebate our profits this year" on its Obamacare exchange plans — and was asked by Rep. Diane Harshbarger (R-TN) why the company pays AARP roughly $9 billion and what it gets in return, a question his later written follow-up did not answer ([Juniper Research Group](http://www.juniperresearchgroup.com/post/7233/the-9000000000-question-how-can-anyone-trust-aarp)). Critics frame the arrangement as a conflict of interest: AARP's critics argue the percentage-of-premium royalty structure gives the senior lobby a financial stake in higher premiums — an argument presented here as the critics' claim, not as proven fact ([DC Journal](https://dcjournal.com/aarps-9-billion-reasons-to-side-with-unitedhealth-and-make-obamacare-subsidies-permanent/)).

## Investigations and the vertical-integration backlash

The same two-engine model that drives the company's scale is now the target of federal scrutiny. The Justice Department has been conducting **criminal and civil investigations** into aspects of UnitedHealth's Medicare business — a fact the company confirmed in July 2025 — and those probes have not been resolved and no charges have been announced ([Medical Daily](https://www.medicaldaily.com/unitedhealth-calpers-investor-lawsuit-medicare-advantage-ruling-479394)). Separately, September 2026 audits by the HHS Office of Inspector General found that medical records did not support diagnosis codes in 172 of 230 sampled Texas cases (an estimated $24.4 million in overpayments) and estimated $46.9 million in overpayments at a Wisconsin plan — findings the company disputed ([Medical Daily](https://www.medicaldaily.com/unitedhealth-calpers-investor-lawsuit-medicare-advantage-ruling-479394)). The stakes are large: the Medicare Payment Advisory Commission estimates Medicare will spend about $76 billion more in 2026 for Medicare Advantage enrollees than for traditional Medicare, with $22 billion tied to coding differences ([Medical Daily](https://www.medicaldaily.com/unitedhealth-calpers-investor-lawsuit-medicare-advantage-ruling-479394)).

Congress has joined in. On September 17, 2025, lawmakers introduced the Patients Over Profits Act, which "prevents large insurance companies and their subsidiaries, such as UnitedHealth Group and its subsidiary Optum, from buying up clinics" — citing UnitedHealth-owned Optum's purchase of dozens of clinics in Oregon, New York, and Washington ([U.S. House of Representatives](https://hoyle.house.gov/media/press-releases/hoyle-merkley-warren-ryan-jayapal-introduce-new-bill-to-crack-down-on-health-insurers-buying-up-clinics-across-the-us)). The bill's backers cite a report that UnitedHealth employs or contracts with over 90,000 doctors — roughly 10 percent of the U.S. physician workforce — and owns more than 750 clinical subsidiaries nationwide ([U.S. House of Representatives](https://hoyle.house.gov/media/press-releases/hoyle-merkley-warren-ryan-jayapal-introduce-new-bill-to-crack-down-on-health-insurers-buying-up-clinics-across-the-us)). In a parallel civil track, a federal judge ruled on September 30, 2026 that part of a CalPERS securities lawsuit over 2024 transactions could proceed, while dismissing most claims including upcoding allegations — none of them proven, all denied by the company ([Medical Daily](https://www.medicaldaily.com/unitedhealth-calpers-investor-lawsuit-medicare-advantage-ruling-479394)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is UnitedHealth Group the same as UnitedHealthcare?**
No. UnitedHealth Group is the parent company, headquartered in Eden Prairie, Minnesota. UnitedHealthcare is its insurance subsidiary — the nation's largest health insurer, headquartered separately in Minnetonka, Minnesota. The Group's second platform is Optum, the health-services business covering clinics, pharmacy benefits, and data analytics.

**What is Optum?**
Optum is UnitedHealth Group's non-insurance health-services arm, created in 2011. It has three segments: Optum Health (owns or manages clinics and delivers care, including 4.7 million patients in value-based arrangements), Optum Insight (analytics and digital services), and Optum Rx (pharmacy benefit management). Optum reported $253 billion in 2024 revenue.

**Why did UnitedHealth pay AARP $9 billion?**
In 2024 the two sides restructured a royalty agreement dating to 1997: UnitedHealth paid AARP a $9.062 billion lump-sum advance for a 12-year exclusive license to market insurance under the AARP name. Critics call it a conflict of interest; in January 2026, Congress questioned CEO Stephen Hemsley about what UnitedHealth gets in return.

**Is UnitedHealth under DOJ investigation?**
Yes — the company confirmed in July 2025 that the U.S. Department of Justice is conducting criminal and civil investigations into aspects of its Medicare business. As of October 2026 the probes were unresolved and no charges had been announced. Separate HHS audits found unsupported diagnosis codes; the company disputes those findings.

**Who is the CEO of UnitedHealth Group?**
Stephen J. Hemsley, who returned as CEO on May 13, 2025 after Andrew Witty resigned for personal reasons. Hemsley had previously led the company from 2006 to 2017 and remained chairman. He was still CEO as of October 2026.
