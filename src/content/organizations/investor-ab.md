## Overview

Investor AB is the Stockholm-based investment company that serves as the Wallenberg family's primary investment vehicle — "an investment vehicle of Sweden's prominent Wallenberg family," as Dow Jones Newswires describes it ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202607161554/swedens-investor-ab-registers-total-shareholder-return-of-15)). Listed on Nasdaq Stockholm, it operates as an industrial holding company across three segments: Listed Core Investments, the EQT private-equity partnership, and Patricia Industries, which holds wholly-owned subsidiaries ([Stockopedia](https://www.stockopedia.com/share-prices/investor-ab-STO:INVE%20B/)). Its listed portfolio reads like a roster of Swedish industry — ABB, AstraZeneca, Atlas Copco, Electrolux, Ericsson, Husqvarna, [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/), Saab, SEB, Sobi and Wärtsilä — while Patricia Industries owns companies including Mölnlycke, Permobil, BraunAbility and Laborie outright ([Stockopedia](https://www.stockopedia.com/share-prices/investor-ab-STO:INVE%20B/)).

## Born from a banking law

Investor AB exists because of legislation. During World War I, new Swedish banking laws severely restricted banks' ability to hold long-term shares in industrial companies — so the Wallenbergs moved Stockholms Enskilda Bank's vast industrial shareholdings into a new holding company set up in 1916 ([Company-Histories](https://Company-Histories.com/Investor-AB-Company-History.html)). From the start it held majority positions in some of Sweden's oldest industrial names, such as Scania and Atlas Copco ([Company-Histories](https://Company-Histories.com/Investor-AB-Company-History.html)). The company describes its model as active ownership rather than passive holding: appointing CEOs, taking board seats, and steering mergers and acquisitions across its portfolio ([Company-Histories](https://Company-Histories.com/Investor-AB-Company-History.html)).

The vehicle became the family's flagship by accident of banking history. When SEB merged with a rival in 1972 and the Wallenbergs lost majority control of the bank, Investor — under Marcus Wallenberg — became the center of the family's business, and began aggressively restructuring its industrial holdings ([Company-Histories](https://Company-Histories.com/Investor-AB-Company-History.html)). In 1992 it absorbed the Providentia investment vehicle to become the family's primary investment arm, and in 1994 formed the EQT private-equity partnership with SEB ([Company-Histories](https://Company-Histories.com/Investor-AB-Company-History.html)).

## The modern portfolio in motion

Recent quarters show the active-ownership model still operating. In the second quarter of 2026 Investor posted a 15% total shareholder return, with net asset value per share rising to 354 kronor (397 on an adjusted basis) and listed holdings returning 14%, led by ABB ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202607161554/swedens-investor-ab-registers-total-shareholder-return-of-15)). During the quarter it invested 1.7 billion kronor in its pro-rata share of Electrolux's rights issue, bought nearly 57,000 shares in Nasdaq for 46 million kronor, and agreed to divest 2 million SEB shares for expected proceeds of around 375 million kronor ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202607161554/swedens-investor-ab-registers-total-shareholder-return-of-15)).
