## Overview

Club for the Future is [Blue Origin](/organizations/blue-origin/)'s nonprofit foundation, founded in 2019 with the stated mission of inspiring future generations to pursue careers in STEM and helping invent the future of life in space ([Space Explored](https://spaceexplored.com/2021/07/14/bezos-donates-1-1000th-of-net-worth/)). It is best known for two things that happened for very different reasons: its signature Postcards to Space program, which flies students' postcards past the Kármán line and mails them back stamped "Flown to Space," and the $19 million in grants it paid out in 2021 after [Justin Sun](/people/justin-sun/)'s $28 million auction bid landed in its account ([Blue Origin](https://www.blueorigin.com/news/club-for-the-future-selects-19-charities); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/crypto-justin-sun-28m-blue-origin-space/)).

## A rocket-mail club for kids

The Club's founding is a story the auction coverage usually skips. On May 9, 2019, Jeff Bezos unveiled the Club at the same press event where he revealed Blue Origin's Blue Moon lunar lander design. The pitch was disarmingly simple: to get young people thinking about their future in space, Bezos would launch and return 10,000 stamped postcards carrying students' visions for humanity beyond Earth ([collectSPACE](http://www.collectspace.com/ubb/Forum14/HTML/001730.html)). "One of the things we have to do is inspire the future generations," Bezos said at the event, announcing "that Blue Origin is founding the Club for the Future, whose mission is to inspire young people to build the future of life in space" ([collectSPACE](http://www.collectspace.com/ubb/Forum14/HTML/001730.html)).

The postcards poured in. By August 2019, Bezos reported the Club had received thousands of postcards from 22 countries — "I've seen the messages and they are so hopeful and inspiring. We are in good hands" — and on December 11, 2019, the NS-12 New Shepard launch carried 8,000 of them to space and back ([collectSPACE](http://www.collectspace.com/ubb/Forum14/HTML/001730.html)). After the flight, each card got the Club's official "Flown to Space" stamp and was mailed back to the student who wrote it ([collectSPACE](http://www.collectspace.com/ubb/Forum14/HTML/001730.html)). Blue Origin CEO Bob Smith told GeekWire at the time that the company hoped to fly postcards on as many missions as possible: "If we get the number of cards that we're targeting, we'll be stuffing them in every nook and cranny" ([collectSPACE](http://www.collectspace.com/ubb/Forum14/HTML/001730.html)).

The pandemic years were, improbably, the program's best. With schools shifting online in spring 2020, the Club leaned into at-home activities, asking students to answer "What would you build in space that could help Earth?" on a postcard mailed to a PO box in Kent, Washington. "With Blue Origin's access to space on the New Shepard rocket, Club for the Future is able to offer students around the world the unique opportunity to send their hopes and dreams to space — for the cost of a stamp," said Club director Josef Reinke ([collectSPACE](http://www.collectspace.com/ubb/Forum14/HTML/001730.html)). In April 2021, a single New Shepard flight carried more than 25,000 postcards from students and classrooms in more than a dozen countries, pushing the program past 100 participating countries across all seven continents, with postcards returned bearing both the "Flown to Space" and NASA Artemis stamps ([collectSPACE](http://www.collectspace.com/ubb/Forum14/HTML/001730.html)). The Club also began partnering with established STEM organizations — the Challenger Center's Destination Moon virtual experience, Microsoft Hacking STEM, NASA-funded Project Ianos, and the STEM2 Hub's Dream Big Florida initiative ([collectSPACE](http://www.collectspace.com/ubb/Forum14/HTML/001730.html)).

## The $28 million that changed everything

As recently as November 2020, the Club had no plans to issue grants to other organizations — it was a postcard program with curriculum, not a funder ([Space Explored](https://spaceexplored.com/2021/07/14/bezos-donates-1-1000th-of-net-worth/)). Then came the auction. In June 2021, Blue Origin auctioned the first paid seat on New Shepard; the winning bid was $28 million, and the proceeds went to Club for the Future ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/crypto-justin-sun-28m-blue-origin-space/)).

On July 14, 2021, the Club announced that 19 space-focused nonprofits would each receive a $1 million grant — $19 million total — funded by the auction. "Our recent auction for the first seat on New Shepard resulted in a donation of $28 million to our non-profit foundation, Club for the Future," said Blue Origin CEO Bob Smith. "This donation is enabling Club for the Future to rapidly expand its reach by partnering with 19 organizations to develop and inspire the next generation of space professionals. Our generation will build the road to space and these efforts will ensure the next generation is ready to go even further" ([Blue Origin](https://www.blueorigin.com/news/club-for-the-future-selects-19-charities)).

The recipients read like a directory of space education and advocacy: Space Camp, the Challenger Center, the AIAA Foundation, the Astronaut Scholarship Foundation, the Planetary Society, the Mars Society, the National Space Society, Space for Humanity, the International Astronautical Federation, AstraFemina, the Brooke Owens Fellowship, Higher Orbits, SciArt Exchange, Space Center Houston, the Space For Art Foundation, the Space Generation Advisory Council, Students for the Exploration and Development of Space, Teachers in Space, and the Space Frontier Foundation ([Blue Origin](https://www.blueorigin.com/news/club-for-the-future-selects-19-charities)). Blue Origin said the remaining auction funds would continue the Club's space-focused curriculum and Postcards to Space program ([Blue Origin](https://www.blueorigin.com/news/club-for-the-future-selects-19-charities)).

## The postcards kept flying

The auction winner remained anonymous for months, deferred the trip, and was eventually identified as crypto entrepreneur Justin Sun, who flew on NS-34 from West Texas on August 3, 2025 — four years after placing the bid ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/crypto-justin-sun-28m-blue-origin-space/)). The flight was the 14th crewed New Shepard mission, and it carried the Club's program along with it: more than 1,500 digital postcards and 16,000 physical postcards for Postcards in Space rode alongside the six-person crew ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/crypto-justin-sun-28m-blue-origin-space/)). Sun brought his own homage to the program — a "To the Sun" initiative collecting messages, more than 1,000 of which he carried into space on a thumb drive ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/crypto-justin-sun-28m-blue-origin-space/)).
