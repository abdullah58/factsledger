## Overview

Hercules Capital, Inc. (NYSE: HTGC) is an American business development company that lends to venture-backed technology and life-sciences companies, providing senior secured venture debt — loans to high-growth firms that lack the cash flow or collateral a traditional bank would require. Founded in December 2003 by Manuel Henriquez and listed on the [New York Stock Exchange](/organizations/new-york-stock-exchange/), it has committed $29 billion to more than 700 companies since inception ([SEC Form 8-K](https://investor.htgc.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001280784-26-000055/0001280784-26-000055.pdf)).

The company is headquartered in San Mateo, California, and is incorporated in Maryland ([SEC Form 8-K](https://investor.htgc.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001280784-26-000055/0001280784-26-000055.pdf)). It is internally managed — unlike most business development companies, it has no external manager charging a fee — and its common stock trades on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker HTGC, alongside a 6.25% Notes issue due 2033 that trades as HCXY ([Hercules Capital](https://investor.htgc.com/company-information/faq)). As a regulated investment company it must distribute at least 90% of its investment-company taxable income to shareholders ([Hercules Capital](https://investor.htgc.com/company-information/faq)).

Scale, as reported for the third quarter of 2026, is the story of the last two years: record originations and a growing private-credit arm. The table below gives the position at September 30, 2026 ([SEC Form 8-K](https://investor.htgc.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001280784-26-000055/0001280784-26-000055.pdf)).

## The business model: venture debt, first-lien and floating-rate

Hercules' pitch is that venture-backed companies need non-dilutive capital between equity rounds, which a specialist lender can underwrite better than a bank. Venture debt is financing for venture capital and private equity-backed companies to fund working capital or operating expenses; it is available to companies without positive cash flow or significant collateral, with warrants — rights to buy equity — compensating the lender for the higher risk of default ([Hercules Capital](https://investor.htgc.com/company-information/faq)).

In practice the book is conservative in form: at September 30, 2026, 88.8% of the debt portfolio was first-lien senior secured and 97.9% was floating-rate with interest-rate floors ([SEC Form 8-K](https://investor.htgc.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001280784-26-000055/0001280784-26-000055.pdf)). The loans carry warrants and small equity stakes: equity positions in 73 portfolio companies worth $146.8 million and warrant positions in 114 companies worth $46.3 million at the same date ([SEC Form 8-K](https://investor.htgc.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001280784-26-000055/0001280784-26-000055.pdf)). The debt book spans technology and life sciences.

Funding comes from public equity, unsecured notes, [Small Business Administration](/organizations/small-business-administration/) debentures and bank facilities — $440 million from MUFG Bank and $475 million from SMBC ([SEC Form 8-K](https://investor.htgc.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001280784-26-000055/0001280784-26-000055.pdf)). In October 2026 the company issued a further $400 million of 6.700% notes due in 2029 ([SEC Form 8-K](https://investor.htgc.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001280784-26-000055/0001280784-26-000055.pdf)). Hercules Adviser LLC, a wholly owned registered investment adviser, manages private credit funds for institutional investors ([SEC Form 8-K](https://investor.htgc.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001280784-26-000055/0001280784-26-000055.pdf)).

The economics are high-yield by design: the GAAP effective yield on the debt portfolio was 12.3% in the third quarter of 2026, with a core yield (excluding prepayment accelerations and one-time items) of 11.9% ([SEC Form 8-K](https://investor.htgc.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001280784-26-000055/0001280784-26-000055.pdf)). That income funds a $0.47 quarterly distribution — a $0.40 base plus a $0.07 supplemental — covered 118% by net investment income on a base-distribution basis ([SEC Form 8-K](https://investor.htgc.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001280784-26-000055/0001280784-26-000055.pdf)).

## Founding, listing and the founder's fall

Hercules was founded in December 2003 by Manuel Henriquez as Hercules Technology Growth Capital, made its first originations in October 2004, and completed its initial public offering on June 9, 2005 at $13.00 per share ([Hercules Capital](https://investor.htgc.com/company-information/faq)). The listing moved from the [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/) Global Select Market to the New York Stock Exchange effective April 30, 2012, keeping the ticker HTGC ([SEC Form 8-K](https://investor.htgc.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001193125-12-166375/d335881d8k.htm)). The company was renamed Hercules Capital in 2016 ([p05.org](https://www.p05.org/fund-of-the-week-hercules-capital/)).

By its 20th anniversary in April 2024 the firm had passed $20 billion in cumulative commitments, with more than 650 portfolio companies and 250-plus IPO or M&A exits ([Hercules Capital](https://investor.htgc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/532/hercules-capital-celebrates-20th-anniversary-with-20-0)). It held investment-grade ratings from four agencies and had paid $1.87 billion in dividends over 75 consecutive quarters since the IPO ([Hercules Capital](https://investor.htgc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/532/hercules-capital-celebrates-20th-anniversary-with-20-0)).

The founder did not survive to that anniversary in office. Henriquez resigned as chairman and chief executive officer in March 2019, when federal indictments in the Operation Varsity Blues college-admissions scandal were announced ([Palo Alto Online](https://www.paloaltoonline.com/news/2020/07/29/former-venture-capital-ceo-sentenced-in-college-admissions-scam/)). He pleaded guilty on October 21, 2019 to conspiracy to commit mail and wire fraud and conspiracy to commit money laundering ([Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/investigations-and-enforcement-blog/federal-judge-sentences-financier-to-six-months-in-varsity-blues-scandal.html)). Prosecutors alleged he and his wife paid William "Rick" Singer nearly $50,000 to cheat on their children's entrance exams five times, and $400,000 to get their oldest daughter into Georgetown as a fake tennis recruit ([Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/investigations-and-enforcement-blog/federal-judge-sentences-financier-to-six-months-in-varsity-blues-scandal.html)). On July 29, 2020 a federal judge sentenced him to six months in prison, two years of supervised release, 200 hours of community service and a $200,000 fine ([Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/investigations-and-enforcement-blog/federal-judge-sentences-financier-to-six-months-in-varsity-blues-scandal.html)).

Scott Bluestein, then chief investment officer, took over as chief executive officer in 2019 and still holds both roles ([Hunterbrook Media](https://hntrbrk.com/breaking-news/hercules-capital)). In 2026 Seth Meyer, chief financial officer since 2019, was promoted to president effective May 18, and Andrew Olson — a former Hercules finance executive who had been chief financial officer of TriplePoint Capital and TriplePoint Venture Growth — returned as chief financial officer and head of corporate development ([ABF Journal](https://www.abfjournal.com/hercules-capital-enters-next-phase-of-growth-with-expanded-leadership-team/)).

## The 2026 borrower book: Behavox, Beren and exits

The clearest recent example is [Behavox](/organizations/behavox/), the AI compliance-surveillance company founded by [Erkin Adylov](/people/erkin-adylov/). Behavox secured a $70 million credit facility from Hercules in autumn 2024 to support strategic expansion, including its acquisition of [Mosaic Smart Data](/organizations/mosaic-smart-data/) and a strategic investment in [b-next](/organizations/b-next/), and fully repaid and retired the facility in June 2026 as part of a $175 million preferred-equity investment from [HPS Investment Partners](/organizations/hps-investment-partners/), part of [BlackRock](/organizations/blackrock/) ([Financial IT](https://financialit.net/news/fundraising-news/behavox-raises-175-million-hps-investment-partners-part-blackrock-accelerate); [RegTech Analyst](https://regtechanalyst.com/behavox-raises-175m-from-hps-to-fuel-global-growth/)). Hercules managing director Ruslan Sergeyev called the partnership "highly successful" ([Financial IT](https://financialit.net/news/fundraising-news/behavox-raises-175-million-hps-investment-partners-part-blackrock-accelerate)).

On June 10, 2026, Beren Therapeutics announced $300 million of combined financing, including up to $165 million from Hercules — up to $110 million in senior secured term loans ($30 million drawn) plus a $55 million royalty financing, funded only if the FDA approves adrabetadex, Beren's investigational therapy for infantile-onset Niemann-Pick disease type C ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260610248466/en/Beren-Therapeutics-Secures-%24300M-Financing-to-Support-the-Potential-Commercial-Launch-of-Adrabetadex-and-Long-Term-Care-Initiatives-for-Infantile-Onset-Niemann-Pick-Disease-Type-C)). The royalty carries 7.5% of US net sales and 5% outside the US, capped at 1.75 times through 2031; the drug was unapproved at announcement, with an FDA target action date of November 17, 2026 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260610248466/en/Beren-Therapeutics-Secures-%24300M-Financing-to-Support-the-Potential-Commercial-Launch-of-Adrabetadex-and-Long-Term-Care-Initiatives-for-Infantile-Onset-Niemann-Pick-Disease-Type-C)).

## Scrutiny: the Hunterbrook report, PIK income and the class action

On February 27, 2026, Hunterbrook Media published "The Myth of Hercules Capital", an investigation arguing that the firm's premium valuation rested on flattered numbers ([Hunterbrook Media](https://hntrbrk.com/breaking-news/hercules-capital)). Hunterbrook Capital, an affiliated fund, disclosed that it was short HTGC shares and long a basket of comparable securities at publication ([Hunterbrook Media](https://hntrbrk.com/breaking-news/hercules-capital)).

The report's central claim was concentration. About 35% of the loan portfolio — roughly $1.5 billion — was software debt, meaning about 70 cents of every dollar of net asset value was backed by software, yet the firm marked its software book at about 100 cents on the dollar even as software loans elsewhere fell into distressed territory — and argued true exposure was higher still, since Houzz and AppDirect were categorised outside software ([Hunterbrook Media](https://hntrbrk.com/breaking-news/hercules-capital)).

A second claim concerned payment-in-kind, or PIK, income — interest borrowers pay by adding to the loan balance rather than in cash. Hunterbrook reported PIK income of $55.9 million in 2025, up from about $24.7 million in 2023, while cash collected on PIK loans fell 73% to $4.9 million and the outstanding PIK receivable nearly tripled to $109.1 million ([Hunterbrook Media](https://hntrbrk.com/breaking-news/hercules-capital)). It also argued the dividend was thin: 2025 net investment income of $1.91 per share against $1.88 in distributions, and cash coverage of the base distribution of only 105.7% ([Hunterbrook Media](https://hntrbrk.com/breaking-news/hercules-capital)).

Hercules responded that its valuations were as of December 31, 2025 and did not reflect 2026 volatility; that its PIK exposure was about the median of similarly sized BDCs; that its valuations process was rigorous and cross-functional, with PwC audit, Sarbanes-Oxley compliance and board oversight; and that its loss metric was required by GAAP ([Hunterbrook Media](https://hntrbrk.com/breaking-news/hercules-capital)). Two claims are directly contested. On turnover, Hunterbrook counted about 36% of funded-company events as new relationships over 16 quarters, against the company's 63% of new fundings since 2022 — figures measured in different units ([Hunterbrook Media](https://hntrbrk.com/breaking-news/hercules-capital)). On the loss metric, Hunterbrook argued the 3-basis-point headline nets warrant and equity gains from the DocuSign, Palantir and Pinterest exits against loan losses, a method it said no other major BDC uses ([Hunterbrook Media](https://hntrbrk.com/breaking-news/hercules-capital)).

The report had legal consequences. On March 20, 2026 a securities class action, Taylor v. Hercules Capital, Inc., was filed in the Northern District of California against the company, Bluestein and Meyer, covering purchasers between May 1, 2025 and February 27, 2026 and alleging overstated diligence in deal sourcing and valuation, misclassified investments and overstated valuations ([Taylor v. Hercules Capital, Inc.](https://kehoelawfirm.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Hercules-Capital-Class-Action-Copy.pdf)). The complaint treats the Hunterbrook report as the corrective disclosure: the shares fell $1.22, or 7.9%, to close at $14.21 on February 27, 2026 ([Taylor v. Hercules Capital, Inc.](https://kehoelawfirm.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Hercules-Capital-Class-Action-Copy.pdf)). The allegations are unproven.

Since then, PIK income fell year over year in 2026 ($35.8 million for the first nine months, against $41.6 million), credit stayed stable with two loans on non-accrual, and originations hit a record $3.44 billion of new commitments, up 20% year over year ([SEC Form 8-K](https://investor.htgc.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001280784-26-000055/0001280784-26-000055.pdf)).
