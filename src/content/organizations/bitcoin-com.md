## Overview

Bitcoin.com is a Saint Kitts and Nevis–based cryptocurrency services company founded by early bitcoin investor Roger Ver, the figure known as "Bitcoin Jesus," who took control of the bitcoin.com domain in April 2014 ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2015/05/25/roger-ver-and-okcoin-at-war-over-bitcoincom-domain-name)). It is best known for its self-custodial Bitcoin.com Wallet and its crypto newsroom, and it later expanded into mining, a centralized exchange, and the VERSE ecosystem token ([Bitcoin.com](https://www.bitcoin.com/about-us/)). The operating company behind the site is Saint Bitts LLC, which is listed as the seller of the wallet app on the Apple App Store ([Apple](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bitcoin-com-wallet-buy-sell/id1252903728?ls=1)).

The company has lived at the center of crypto's loudest fights. Ver first leased the domain to the wallet service Blockchain.info and then to the exchange OKCoin, which was to manage the site for five years and pay him at least $10,000 a month ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2015/05/25/roger-ver-and-okcoin-at-war-over-bitcoincom-domain-name)). When OKCoin stopped paying, Ver accused the exchange of forging a doctored version of their contract — a dispute that ended up in Hong Kong court filings and remains the clearest window into who actually owns one of crypto's most valuable domains ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2015/05/25/roger-ver-and-okcoin-at-war-over-bitcoincom-domain-name)).

## Business lines: wallet, news, exchange, VERSE

The Bitcoin.com Wallet is the company's flagship — a self-custodial mobile wallet for buying, selling, swapping and holding crypto "across every major chain," with the user holding the private keys ([Bitcoin.com](https://www.bitcoin.com/about-us/)). Independent app data shows the wallet listed on Google Play since June 2017, with more than 15 million installs and a 4.7-star rating ([AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/bitcoin-com-crypto-wallet/com.bitcoin.mwallet)). Bitcoin.com News operates as one of the largest crypto newsrooms by the company's account, which claims roughly a million monthly readers and says the desk is editorially independent from the company's commercial products ([Bitcoin.com](https://www.bitcoin.com/about-us/)).

The company tried centralized exchange services too: the Bitcoin.com Exchange launched in early September 2019 ([Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/bitcoin-com-exchange-lists-more-tokens-for-trading/)). Its biggest fundraising came through the VERSE ecosystem token — an ERC-20 utility token with a fixed 210 billion supply — whose private Sale A raised $33.6 million in 2022 from investors including Blockchain.com, KuCoin, Digital Strategies, Roger Ver, Jihan Wu and David Wachsman ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/verse-token/)). A further $50 million pre-sale completed that December, and VERSE began trading on the company's Verse DEX on December 8, 2022 at $0.0015 per token ([Bitcoin.com](https://api.news.ops.bitcoin.com/bitcoin%e2%80%a4com-completes-verse-50m-token-sale-launches-token-on-verse-dex/); [ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2084232)). By October 2026 the product lineup had narrowed to the Wallet, News, OrangeRock (onchain perpetuals trading built on Hyperliquid) and Checkout (merchant crypto payments), with team bases in Tokyo, Singapore, Dubai and Warsaw and an ISO 27001 certification ([Bitcoin.com](https://www.bitcoin.com/about-us/)).

## The domain's custody wars

The bitcoin.com domain has been contested property almost from the moment Ver acquired it. Brave New Coin reported in May 2015 that Ver's ownership was only "unveiled" when his dispute with OKCoin went public — until then, the domain's owner had never been known ([Brave New Coin](https://bravenewcoin.com/insights/roger-ver-revealed-as-bitcoin-com-owner-in-bitter-dispute-with-okcoin)). The five-year management deal signed in December 2014 called for OKCoin to redesign the site and share revenue with a $10,000 monthly floor, but the exchange later declared the contract invalid — arguing it never named OKCoin's actual legal entity and pointing to a FinCEN action against Ripple Labs that had referenced Ver as reason to cut the business relationship ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2015/05/25/roger-ver-and-okcoin-at-war-over-bitcoincom-domain-name)).

Ver's response was cryptographic: he said OKCoin had produced a doctored "v8" of the contract by scanning his signature from the "v7" he had PGP-signed, and an independent security consultant's analysis supported the forgery claim ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2015/05/25/roger-ver-and-okcoin-at-war-over-bitcoincom-domain-name)). Ver's team put the deal's value at $550,000 over its term and offered to settle for $200,000; Ver later sued OKCoin's Hong Kong entity over the terminated contract ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2015/05/25/roger-ver-and-okcoin-at-war-over-bitcoincom-domain-name); [CCN](https://www.ccn.com/chinese-investor-sues-okcoin-over-bitcoin-cash-from-2017-hard-fork/)). The domain drama flared again in April 2021, when GoDaddy briefly listed bitcoin.com as "available" for $100 million; Ver demanded its removal on social media, called the listing fake news spread by Bitcoin maximalists to hurt Bitcoin Cash, and GoDaddy took it down without explanation ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/godaddy-briefly-put-bitcoin-com-up-for-sale-by-mistake/)).

## The newsroom and the "American Sell-Out" report

Bitcoin.com's news desk sits unusually close to crypto's political fault lines — in September 2025 it was among the outlets to cover the Accountable.US report "American Sell-Out," which accused the Trump-family-linked [World Liberty Financial](/organizations/world-liberty-financial/) of selling hundreds of thousands of $WLFI tokens to sanctioned-linked entities ([Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/tag/world-liberty-financial/page/2/)). The report's claims, as summarized by [cryptonews.net](https://cryptonews.net/news/legal/31655716/), included a 600,000-$WLFI purchase by the trader [Shryder](/people/shryder/).eth on Inauguration Day, roughly 3,500 $WLFI sold to a trader using Iran's [Nobitex](/organizations/nobitex/) exchange, more than 10,000 $WLFI sold to a user of the Russian ruble-backed [A7A5](/organizations/a7a5/) sanctions-evasion token, and token sales to 62 users who had previously used the mixer Tornado Cash ([cryptonews.net](https://cryptonews.net/news/legal/31655716/)).

The stat that gives the story its shape is the follow-through the report itself acknowledged: after the token sales ended, World Liberty blocked 272 wallets, with five of them blacklisted for high-risk exposure ([cryptonews.net](https://cryptonews.net/news/legal/31655716/)) — the same 272-wallet figure that had driven the early-September controversy around [Justin Sun](/people/justin-sun/)'s frozen tokens. But the report's blockchain analysis did not go unchallenged: security researcher [Nick Bax](/people/nick-bax/) disputed its Lazarus Group link, writing that a user's roughly $95,000 in $WLFI had been frozen because of a false positive ([Standing Record](https://standingrecord.com/entries/2025/09/18/federal-self-dealing-6ac10a32/)). Bitcoin.com News kept following the thread, later covering the November 2025 letter from Senators Elizabeth Warren and Jack Reed pressing the DOJ and Treasury to investigate the platform ([Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/tag/world-liberty-financial/page/2/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Bitcoin.com the same thing as Bitcoin?** No. Bitcoin is an open protocol that nobody owns; Bitcoin.com is a private company that builds apps for using it — a distinction the company itself makes on its about page ([Bitcoin.com](https://www.bitcoin.com/about-us/)). It is also distinct from Bitcoin Cash, the network Roger Ver championed, though the company's early branding blurred that line and drew criticism.

**Who owns Bitcoin.com?** The domain was acquired by early bitcoin investor Roger Ver in April 2014, and his ownership became public during the 2015 OKCoin dispute ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2015/05/25/roger-ver-and-okcoin-at-war-over-bitcoincom-domain-name)). The operating company is listed as Saint Bitts LLC, headquartered in Saint Kitts and Nevis according to secondary sources, though the precise legal relationship between Ver and the entity is not in the public record.

**What happened in Roger Ver's tax case?** The DOJ indicted Ver in April 2024, alleging he evaded exit tax on about 131,000 bitcoins when renouncing U.S. citizenship in 2014 and hid $240 million in 2017 sales; he was arrested in Spain ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2024/04/30/roger-ver-indicted-for-tax-fraud)). In October 2026 Reuters reported a deferred-prosecution agreement under which Ver pays up to $49.9 million and the charges are dropped ([PANews](https://panews.io/articles/01a14f65-6d9b-7354-b469-0fc999471cb6)).

**What is the VERSE token?** VERSE is Bitcoin.com's ERC-20 rewards and utility token, with a fixed supply of 210 billion, meant to reward buying, selling, storing and learning within the ecosystem ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/verse-token/)). Its 2022 sales raised $33.6 million privately and $50 million in a pre-sale, and it began trading on the Verse DEX on December 8, 2022 ([ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2084232)).

**Did Bitcoin.com report on World Liberty Financial's token sales?** Yes — its news desk covered the September 2025 Accountable.US "American Sell-Out" report alleging $WLFI sales to sanctioned-linked entities, and later the Warren/Reed probe letter ([Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/tag/world-liberty-financial/page/2/)). As with all such coverage, the underlying allegations remain contested: a security researcher publicly challenged the report's North Korea-link analysis ([Standing Record](https://standingrecord.com/entries/2025/09/18/federal-self-dealing-6ac10a32/)).
