## Overview

RenoFi is a Philadelphia-based fintech whose most important fact is a negative: it is not a lender ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/renofi-raises-6-4-million-to-help-lenders-offer-home-renovation-loans/)). Instead, it operates an AI-enabled renovation-financing platform that lets partner credit unions offer home equity products based on a property's after-renovation value (ARV) rather than its current value — including what HousingWire describes as the first U.S. home equity line of credit built on ARV, the "Renovation HELOC" ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/renofi-22m-series-b-funding/)). Founded in 2018 by Justin Goldman, Robert Shedd and Lee Miller, the company has raised $65 million in total capital, most recently a $22 million Series B led by Fifth Wall in 2025 ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/renofi-22m-series-b-funding/)).

## The equity-light problem and the ARV answer

Goldman founded RenoFi after a personal frustration: in 2016, his young family moved to a Philadelphia suburb and discovered the house needed more work than they had budgeted for — but they had not built up enough equity to borrow against ([Business of Home](https://businessofhome.com/articles/renofi-wants-to-make-it-easier-for-homeowners-to-finance-renovations)). The standard options — cash-out refinancing, traditional home equity loans, construction loans — were either unavailable to equity-light homeowners or cumbersome and poorly suited to smaller projects ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/renofi-raises-6-4-million-to-help-lenders-offer-home-renovation-loans/)).

RenoFi's answer was to adapt the after-renovation-value concept from construction lending to the simplicity of home equity products: a homeowner borrows against the home's anticipated post-renovation value as determined by a third-party appraiser ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/renofi-raises-6-4-million-to-help-lenders-offer-home-renovation-loans/)). Goldman told HousingWire the model could boost a homeowner's buying power by as much as three times, with loans fundable in under 30 days ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/renofi-raises-6-4-million-to-help-lenders-offer-home-renovation-loans/)). By 2022 the company said its "renovation underwrite" technology increased customers' borrowing power by 11 times on average, allowing homeowners to borrow up to 90% of the home's future value, with partner lenders offering loans up to $500,000 ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/04/05/renofi-lands-14m-series-a-to-connect-home-renovators-to-lenders/)).

The platform also performs the diligence most lenders lack the staff for: feasibility analysis of the renovation, vetting that contractors are licensed and insured, reviewing the homeowner–contractor contract, and monitoring renovations in progress ([Business of Home](https://businessofhome.com/articles/renofi-wants-to-make-it-easier-for-homeowners-to-finance-renovations); [Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/04/05/renofi-lands-14m-series-a-to-connect-home-renovators-to-lenders/)). RenoFi charges lenders a fee each time a homeowner is placed into a RenoFi Loan — its primary revenue — plus a demand-generation fee when it matches a homeowner who arrived through its own site ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/renofi-raises-6-4-million-to-help-lenders-offer-home-renovation-loans/)).

## The credit-union network

Rather than building a national lending operation, RenoFi called more than 500 banks and credit unions early on and found that most lacked ARV-lending capabilities — then built the rails for the ones willing to learn ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/renofi-raises-6-4-million-to-help-lenders-offer-home-renovation-loans/)). Ardent Credit Union was the first financial institution to partner, piloting the program for over a year before launching the Ardent Renovation Loan in June 2020: a two-year draw period converting to a fixed-rate term loan, with Ardent underwriting the credit and RenoFi acting as "renovation concierge" ([CUInsight](http://cuinsight.com/press-release/ardent-credit-union-partners-with-philadelphia-based-fintech-renofi-on-first-of-its-kind-home-renovation-loan/)). By early 2021 the company reported relationships with regional credit unions in 49 states ([Business of Home](https://businessofhome.com/articles/renofi-wants-to-make-it-easier-for-homeowners-to-finance-renovations)), and by the 2025 Series B its backers included credit-union partners Ardent, Chartway, First Community and USALLIANCE Financial — lenders putting their own capital behind the platform ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/renofi-22m-series-b-funding/)). The company operates as a credit union service organization (CUSO), which it says gives its loan officers access to credit-union products their competitors at big mortgage banks lack ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/renofi-22m-series-b-funding/)).

## The funding arc

RenoFi's venture backing has been concentrated among a small set of repeat investors: a $750,000 unannounced seed from First Round Capital before 2020; a $6.4 million Series A in June 2020 led by Canaan Partners with Comcast Ventures and First Round, bringing total raised to $7.15 million and adding Canaan's Brendan Dickinson to the board; and a $14 million Series A in April 2022 led by Canaan with Nyca Partners and CMFG Ventures ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/renofi-raises-6-4-million-to-help-lenders-offer-home-renovation-loans/); [Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/04/05/renofi-lands-14m-series-a-to-connect-home-renovators-to-lenders/)). The March 2025 Series B — $22 million led by Fifth Wall with Progressive Insurance participating — brought total capital raised to $65 million, with Goldman saying the money would triple the retail renovation-financing team and accelerate the AI-enabled renovation-underwriting engine ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/renofi-22m-series-b-funding/)). The company reported at that point that it had facilitated more than 8,000 renovation loans totaling over $1.5 billion funded, was licensed in 48 states, and attracted more than 10,000 new homeowners per month ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/renofi-22m-series-b-funding/)).

## Independent assessment

Bankrate's 2026 review of RenoFi scores it 4.6 overall, while being candid about the model's tradeoffs: the company is a licensed mortgage broker (NMLS 1802847), not the direct lender — borrowers are matched with credit-union partners, rates are only disclosed after applying, and fees include 1–3% closing costs plus an appraisal covering both current and after-renovation values ([Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/home-equity/reviews/renofi/)). Its strengths per Bankrate: ARV-based borrowing can let homeowners borrow more than with a traditional HELOC, some partner lenders go as low as 620 on credit scores, and the product is available in most states and D.C. (excluding Hawaii, New York and Massachusetts) ([Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/home-equity/reviews/renofi/)). Reputation-wise, Bankrate notes a 4.8-out-of-5 "excellent" TrustPilot score but only an A- Better Business Bureau rating (not BBB-accredited) and a middling 3.7 customer score on Bankrate itself ([Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/home-equity/reviews/renofi/)).
