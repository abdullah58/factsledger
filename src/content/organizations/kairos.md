## Overview

Kairos is the venture studio founded by [Ankur Jain](/people/ankur-jain/) that builds companies around the biggest everyday costs — housing, healthcare, childcare and student debt — and it is the incubator behind [Bilt Rewards](/organizations/bilt/), the rent-loyalty company valued at $3.1 billion in January 2024, when its programme covered nearly 4 million apartment units with annualized member spend nearing $20 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/24/bilt-rewards-brings-in-200m-led-by-general-catalyst-doubles-valuation-to-3-1b/)). It began in 2008 as the Kairos Society, a student network Jain started while at the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania, and became a venture studio and fund that backs and builds its own companies rather than only mentoring other people's ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/zoe-henry/kairos-fund-25-million-average-americans.html); [The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/a-newboy-network-for-entrepreneurs/article8871541.ece/amp/)).

This profile covers that studio — not any other company called Kairos, and not the student society as a standalone organisation. The distinction matters because the name has meant three things over time: a fellowship network, a $25 million fund announced in 2017, and the venture studio that incubated Rhino and Bilt ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/zoe-henry/kairos-fund-25-million-average-americans.html); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/01/rhino-looks-to-replace-renters-security-deposits-with-a-small-monthly-fee/)). Jain's thesis was that technology should cut the cost of everyday life rather than serve an elite sliver — the argument behind the 2017 fund ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/zoe-henry/kairos-fund-25-million-average-americans.html)). It is based in the [United States](/places/united-states/); whether its headquarters is Philadelphia, as Inc. described it in 2017, or New York City, as current reference entries say, could not be verified from a fully-read source.

## From student society to venture fund

Jain started the Kairos Society in 2008 while studying at Wharton, aiming to give young entrepreneurs with good ideas but poor resources direct access to venture capitalists, mentors, influencers and business leaders ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/a-newboy-network-for-entrepreneurs/article8871541.ece/amp/)). It worked as a network: by 2016 it had chapters in 54 countries and about 3,500 members globally, with 140 in India, and members were invited to a multi-day summit co-hosted by the Rockefeller family ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/a-newboy-network-for-entrepreneurs/article8871541.ece/amp/); [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/zoe-henry/kairos-fund-25-million-average-americans.html)). The network's practical value showed when Branch, a Silicon Valley startup that had raised over $50 million, used it to open Indian operations: one email to the society produced a local team of ten in India ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/a-newboy-network-for-entrepreneurs/article8871541.ece/amp/)). But Jain's argument by 2017 was that networks were not enough: Silicon Valley built $400 juicers for an elite sliver while middle-class costs rose and the median American income sat at $59,039 ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/zoe-henry/kairos-fund-25-million-average-americans.html)). So in November 2017 he put up a $25 million fund, planning $250,000-to-$1 million investments in about 30 businesses, with operational support ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/zoe-henry/kairos-fund-25-million-average-americans.html)).

## The studio's companies: Rhino and the housing bet

The fund's first named recipients showed the thesis in miniature: Cera, a London startup connecting families with on-demand home-care specialists at £20 an hour, and Little Spoon, a San Francisco baby-food subscription launched by Tinder co-founder Sean Rad and Chobani executive Kyle O'Brien ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/zoe-henry/kairos-fund-25-million-average-americans.html)). The housing bet came first, though, in Rhino — the insurtech incubated by Kairos and co-founded by Jain, which replaces a renter's cash security deposit with an insurance policy costing as little as $3 a month ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/01/rhino-looks-to-replace-renters-security-deposits-with-a-small-monthly-fee/)). The citation-magnet figure is Rhino's 2019 result: it had saved renters upwards of $60 million that year, with users in more than 300,000 rental units across the country — money that would otherwise sit dead in escrow accounts, since landlords typically hold a month's rent against damage ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/01/rhino-looks-to-replace-renters-security-deposits-with-a-small-monthly-fee/)). Rhino closed a $21 million Series A in October 2019 led by Kairos and Lakestar, and by then the studio's portfolio also included June Homes, Little Spoon and Cera ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/01/rhino-looks-to-replace-renters-security-deposits-with-a-small-monthly-fee/)).

## Bilt Rewards: the incubator's biggest outcome

Bilt is the company that turned Kairos from an interesting experiment into a consequential incubator. Launched in June 2021 out of Kairos, it lets renters earn points on rent payments, with a co-branded [Mastercard](/organizations/mastercard/) issued by [Wells Fargo](/organizations/wells-fargo/) and no transaction fees ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/24/bilt-rewards-brings-in-200m-led-by-general-catalyst-doubles-valuation-to-3-1b/)). Kairos stayed in the cap table: it participated in Bilt's $150 million October 2022 round, led by [Left Lane Capital](/organizations/left-lane-capital/), alongside [Prosus](/organizations/prosus/), Greystar, Invitation Homes and others, at a $1.5 billion valuation ([Bloomberg](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/real-estate/bilt-startup-turning-rent-into-points-valued-at-1-5-billion); [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/technology/rent-loyalty-program-bilt-rewards-valuation-skyrockets-to-15b-116053)). At that point Bilt processed $3 billion in annualized rent payments and had more than 500,000 active members ([Bloomberg](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/real-estate/bilt-startup-turning-rent-into-points-valued-at-1-5-billion)). Bilt also launched Bilt Homes, showing renters homes they could own for a mortgage payment equal to their rent, and reports on-time rent payments to Equifax, Experian and TransUnion ([Bloomberg](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/real-estate/bilt-startup-turning-rent-into-points-valued-at-1-5-billion)). The January 2024 round doubled the valuation to $3.1 billion on $200 million led by General Catalyst, taking total funding since launch to $413 million; Bilt said it would use the capital to expand its Rewards Alliance, bolster Neighborhood Rewards and enter mortgage rewards ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/24/bilt-rewards-brings-in-200m-led-by-general-catalyst-doubles-valuation-to-3-1b/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Kairos?** Kairos is a venture studio founded by Ankur Jain that builds companies tackling everyday costs such as housing, healthcare and childcare ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/01/rhino-looks-to-replace-renters-security-deposits-with-a-small-monthly-fee/)). It began in 2008 as the Kairos Society, a student entrepreneur network Jain started at the University of Pennsylvania's Wharton School ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/a-newboy-network-for-entrepreneurs/article8871541.ece/amp/)).

**Is Kairos the same as the Kairos Society?** The society was Kairos's first form: a fellowship network for young entrepreneurs that grew to chapters in 54 countries and about 3,500 members by 2016 ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/a-newboy-network-for-entrepreneurs/article8871541.ece/amp/)). The venture studio grew out of it, adding a fund in 2017 and companies it builds itself, such as Rhino and Bilt Rewards ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/zoe-henry/kairos-fund-25-million-average-americans.html)).

**What companies did Kairos incubate?** The verified ones are Rhino, the security-deposit insurtech co-founded by Jain, and Bilt Rewards ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/01/rhino-looks-to-replace-renters-security-deposits-with-a-small-monthly-fee/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/24/bilt-rewards-brings-in-200m-led-by-general-catalyst-doubles-valuation-to-3-1b/)). Rhino replaces a cash deposit with an insurance policy costing as little as $3 a month, and closed a $21 million Series A in October 2019 led by Kairos and Lakestar ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/01/rhino-looks-to-replace-renters-security-deposits-with-a-small-monthly-fee/)). The portfolio has also included Cera, Little Spoon and June Homes ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/10/01/rhino-looks-to-replace-renters-security-deposits-with-a-small-monthly-fee/)).

**Did Kairos invest in Bilt Rewards?** Yes, twice over in effect: Bilt was incubated inside Kairos and launched out of it in June 2021, and Kairos then participated as an investor in Bilt's $150 million October 2022 round at a $1.5 billion valuation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/24/bilt-rewards-brings-in-200m-led-by-general-catalyst-doubles-valuation-to-3-1b/); [Bloomberg](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/real-estate/bilt-startup-turning-rent-into-points-valued-at-1-5-billion)). By January 2024 Bilt was valued at $3.1 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/24/bilt-rewards-brings-in-200m-led-by-general-catalyst-doubles-valuation-to-3-1b/)).

**Who founded Kairos?** Ankur Jain, while a student at the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania, in 2008 ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/zoe-henry/kairos-fund-25-million-average-americans.html); [The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/a-newboy-network-for-entrepreneurs/article8871541.ece/amp/)). Jain later served as vice-president of product at Tinder, and by 2017 was running the Kairos fund before founding Bilt Rewards, where he is chief executive ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/a-newboy-network-for-entrepreneurs/article8871541.ece/amp/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/24/bilt-rewards-brings-in-200m-led-by-general-catalyst-doubles-valuation-to-3-1b/)).
