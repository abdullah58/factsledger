## Overview

La Poste is [France](/places/france/)'s national postal operator, created on 1 January 1991 from the split of the state PTT and headquartered in [Paris](/places/paris/) ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/la-poste-history/)). It is a public limited company whose capital is wholly public: since March 2020 the [Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations](/organizations/caisse-des-depots-et-consignations/) has held 66% of its shares and the French State 34% ([La Poste Groupe](https://lapostegroupe.net.extra.laposte.fr/en/our-public-service-missions)).

Today it is far more than a mail carrier — a €34.6 billion group spanning parcels, banking through [La Banque Postale](/organizations/la-banque-postale/), insurance through [CNP Assurances](/organizations/cnp-assurances/), and digital services, while still carrying four statutory public service missions ([La Poste Groupe](https://www.lapostegroupe.com/en/news/la-poste-groupe-2024-results)).

## From royal couriers to a public company

France's postal service is conventionally traced to 1477, when King Louis XI created coaching inns to deliver his own letters ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/la-poste-history/)). Mail became a state-owned agency in 1793, and the service grew through the 19th century: the Caisse Nationale d'Epargne was created in 1881 so savers could use any post office as a savings bank, and postal current accounts arrived under the law of 7 January 1918 ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/la-poste-history/)). A 1971 reform law separated telecommunications from the post, and in 1990 the two were converted into independently operating public-sector companies, adopting the names La Poste and France Télécom; La Poste was officially created on 1 January 1991 as a Public Industrial and Commercial Establishment ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/la-poste-history/)). In 1997 the government forced La Poste to detach its financial services from mail, and those activities became the distinct subsidiary La Banque Postale on 1 January 2006 ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/la-poste-history/)). La Poste became a public limited company in 2010 ([La Poste Groupe](https://lapostegroupe.net.extra.laposte.fr/en/our-public-service-missions)).

## Four missions the state still entrusts to it

La Poste's identity is defined by four public service missions entrusted to it by the French government under the law of 2 July 1990: the universal postal service; its contribution to regional planning and development; banking accessibility; and the transport and distribution of the press ([La Poste Groupe](https://lapostegroupe.net.extra.laposte.fr/en/our-public-service-missions)). It serves the French people six days a week, collecting and delivering mail and parcels throughout France, and the fulfilment of these missions is enshrined in a company contract with the French State that is renewed every five years ([La Poste Groupe](https://lapostegroupe.net.extra.laposte.fr/en/our-public-service-missions)). The missions are also a financial burden: the Cour des comptes found in 2016 that their cost was not fully offset by public funding, and that public funding equated to 61% of the group's operating profit in 2015 while declining since 2010 ([Cour des comptes](https://ccomptes.fr/en/documents/33948)). The group itself said in 2024 that insufficient compensation for these services weighs on its results and investment capacity ([La Poste Groupe](https://www.lapostegroupe.com/en/news/la-poste-groupe-2024-results)).

## A postal group that now earns most of its money elsewhere

Mail is no longer La Poste's business. In 2024 mail accounted for 15.8% of group revenue, against 52% in 2010, while parcels accounted for 53%, up from 23% in 2010 ([La Poste Groupe](https://www.lapostegroupe.com/en/news/la-poste-groupe-2024-results)) — a reversal that makes the former mail monopoly a parcels-and-banking group that still delivers letters. The group delivered 2.6 billion parcels in 2024 and is present in more than 60 countries on five continents ([La Poste Groupe](https://le-groupe-laposte.cdn.prismic.io/le-groupe-laposte/aKbWU6Tt2nPbaj8n_LAPOSTE_RI_seul_2024_UK-PDF-UA.pdf)). Its Geopost parcels arm moved 2.1 billion parcels in 2024 on turnover of €15.8 billion, with EBIT rising to €614 million from €330 million ([CEP Research](https://www.cep-research.com/2025/03/04/geopost-reports-steady-growth-in-2024/)). Banking and insurance contributed through La Banque Postale, whose 2024 net banking income was €7,554 million, while Docaposte's digital services brought in €880 million and new local services €889 million ([La Poste Groupe](https://www.lapostegroupe.com/en/news/la-poste-groupe-2024-results)). Overall revenue was €34.6 billion with net profit of €1.4 billion, and the group employed 226,800 people in 2024 ([La Poste Groupe](https://le-groupe-laposte.cdn.prismic.io/le-groupe-laposte/aKbWU6Tt2nPbaj8n_LAPOSTE_RI_seul_2024_UK-PDF-UA.pdf)).

## FAQ

### When was La Poste created?

The modern company was created on 1 January 1991, when the French PTT was split into La Poste for postal services and France Télécom for telecommunications. Its postal roots are conventionally traced to 1477, when King Louis XI created coaching inns to deliver his letters.

### What are La Poste's four public service missions?

Under the law of 2 July 1990 they are the universal postal service, contribution to regional planning and development, banking accessibility, and transport and distribution of the press. Their fulfilment is set out in a company contract with the French State that is renewed every five years.

### Who owns La Poste?

Since March 2020 the Caisse des Dépôts et Consignations has held 66% of La Poste's capital and the French State 34%. The company is a public limited company whose capital is entirely public, and the 2020 change was part of the creation of a large public financial pole.

### Is mail still La Poste's main business?

No. In 2024 mail was only 15.8% of group revenue, down from 52% in 2010, while parcels were 53% and financial services 22%. The group delivered 2.6 billion parcels in 2024 and is present in more than 60 countries on five continents.
