## Overview

Vimeo is an American video-hosting and software company founded in 2004 in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) that built its name on high-quality video for filmmakers, reinvented itself as a business video-software provider, went public in 2021 — and was taken private four years later when Milan-based [Bending Spoons](/organizations/bending-spoons/) bought it for $1.38 billion ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2025/09/10/bending-spoons-buys-video-platform-vimeo-for-1-38-billion/)). Its arc runs from creator haven to enterprise tool to a portfolio asset in a serial acquirer's roll-up, and the sale price tells the story: about $8.5 billion at its public debut, $1.38 billion at its exit.

## The creator haven that HD built

Vimeo began in 2004 as a side project of web developers Jake Lodwick and Zach Klein at CollegeHumor's parent company, Connected Ventures, with a name coined as both a blend of "video" and "me" and an anagram of "movie" ([Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/brief-history-vimeo); [CineD](https://www.cined.com/how-vimeo-betrayed-the-filmmakers-who-built-it/)). IAC bought a 51% controlling stake in Connected Ventures for $21 million in August 2006, chiefly interested in CollegeHumor's audience — but after Google bought YouTube for $1.65 billion three months later, IAC pushed Vimeo as its strategic video asset ([CineD](https://www.cined.com/how-vimeo-betrayed-the-filmmakers-who-built-it/)). Both founders were gone by 2008, so the platform filmmakers romanticised was already a corporate subsidiary before most of its beloved features existed ([CineD](https://www.cined.com/how-vimeo-betrayed-the-filmmakers-who-built-it/)).

What set Vimeo apart was quality. In October 2007 it became the first video-sharing site to support consumer high-definition video, at a time when its great rival was still largely standard-definition ([Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/brief-history-vimeo); [CineD](https://www.cined.com/how-vimeo-betrayed-the-filmmakers-who-built-it/)). The Staff Picks programme, launched in 2008, turned that technical edge into cultural capital: a human-curated channel that, according to CineD, gave early career boosts to filmmakers including the Daniels, Ari Aster and the Safdie brothers ([CineD](https://www.cined.com/how-vimeo-betrayed-the-filmmakers-who-built-it/)). For a generation of independent filmmakers, a Staff Pick was validation from a community that valued craft over clicks.

## The pivot to enterprise — and the public-market rise and fall

Vimeo could not win the consumer video war on scale, so under chief executive Anjali Sud it changed the war. In 2017 it pivoted to a software-as-a-service model, selling video creation, editing, distribution and review tools to businesses rather than competing for mass audiences ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/12/22/iac-vimeo-spin-off/); [CineD](https://www.cined.com/how-vimeo-betrayed-the-filmmakers-who-built-it/)). It bought its way into the new model — VHX in 2016, Livestream in 2017 and Magisto in 2019 — and by November 2020 it reported 1.5 million paying subscribers and 3,500 enterprise clients, with positive EBITDA and revenue growing 44% year over year ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/12/22/iac-vimeo-spin-off/); [CineD](https://www.cined.com/how-vimeo-betrayed-the-filmmakers-who-built-it/)).

IAC announced in December 2020 that it would spin Vimeo off as an independent public company, its eleventh such spin-off, and the separation completed in May 2021 with Vimeo trading on Nasdaq as VMEO ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/12/22/iac-vimeo-spin-off/); [The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/iac-completes-vimeo-spin-off/article34648453.ece/amp/)). At debut it carried more than 200 million users across over 190 countries, about 1.6 million paying subscribers and a market capitalisation of roughly $8.5 billion, though the shares fell 13% on the first day to close at $45.39 ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/iac-completes-vimeo-spin-off/article34648453.ece/amp/); [CineD](https://www.cined.com/how-vimeo-betrayed-the-filmmakers-who-built-it/)). The timing was brutal: tech valuations collapsed, the stock lost roughly 90% of its value over the following years, and layoff rounds followed in 2022, 2023 and September 2025 ([CineD](https://www.cined.com/how-vimeo-betrayed-the-filmmakers-who-built-it/)). By 2024 the business had stabilised at $417 million in revenue, but the public-market story was over ([CineD](https://www.cined.com/how-vimeo-betrayed-the-filmmakers-who-built-it/)).

## The Bending Spoons takeover — and what came after

On September 10, 2025, Vimeo announced a definitive agreement to be acquired by Bending Spoons in an all-cash deal worth about $1.38 billion, or $7.85 per share — a 91% premium over its 60-day average price — with the board unanimous and the company to go private ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2025/09/10/bending-spoons-buys-video-platform-vimeo-for-1-38-billion/)). Chief executive Philip Moyer, named to the role in 2024, had cut 10% of staff the week before the announcement ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2025/09/10/bending-spoons-buys-video-platform-vimeo-for-1-38-billion/)). Bending Spoons chief executive Luca Ferrari said the firm acquires companies expecting to own and operate them indefinitely, and promised ambitious investment in the United States and priority markets across creator and enterprise offerings ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2025/09/10/bending-spoons-buys-video-platform-vimeo-for-1-38-billion/)). The deal completed in November 2025 ([Resident Advisor](https://fr.ra.co/news/84404)).

What followed is the part most acquisition coverage missed. Shortly before the close, Vimeo had unveiled an ambitious roadmap at its REFRAME conference — a rebuilt Vimeo Review with [Adobe](/organizations/adobe/) Premiere Pro integration, natural-language library search and support for VR180 and Apple Immersive Video ([CineD](https://www.cined.com/vimeo-and-filmic-pro-owner-bending-spoons-surges-in-1-68-billion-nasdaq-ipo-debut/)). Then, in January 2026, Bending Spoons confirmed a second round of global layoffs at Vimeo, declining to say how many roles were affected; former employees described the cuts as sweeping, including the video engineering team ([Resident Advisor](https://fr.ra.co/news/84404); [CineD](https://www.cined.com/vimeo-and-filmic-pro-owner-bending-spoons-surges-in-1-68-billion-nasdaq-ipo-debut/)). Six months later, on July 1, 2026, Bending Spoons itself listed on Nasdaq, pricing at $29 a share, raising about $1.68 billion and closing its first day at $40.50 — turning the owner of Vimeo into a public company even as Vimeo itself stayed private ([CineD](https://www.cined.com/vimeo-and-filmic-pro-owner-bending-spoons-surges-in-1-68-billion-nasdaq-ipo-debut/)).
