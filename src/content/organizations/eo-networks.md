## Overview

EO Networks S.A. is a Polish software development and IT services company based in Warsaw and listed on the Warsaw Stock Exchange's NewConnect market under the ticker EON since 25 April 2012. It is best known outside Poland as the first company founded by [Stefan Batory](/people/stefan-batory/), who later founded the beauty-booking platform [Booksy](/organizations/booksy/) in 2014 ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2021/01/29/booksy-slots-in-70m/)). The company builds custom software, portals, transactional systems and CRM and ERP software, and also sells hardware and runs maintenance and help-desk services ([Stockopedia](https://www.stockopedia.com/share-prices/eo-networks-sa-WAR:EON/)).

## From eight founders to a listed company

Batory says he launched his first software development company in January 2000 with seven other colleagues, all previously working on what he describes as "the equivalent of Yahoo in Poland" ([Sramana Mitra](https://www.sramanamitra.com/2020/03/09/bootstrapping-with-services-from-poland-to-a-us-saas-company-stefan-batory-ceo-of-booksy-part-1/)). The dot-com bust hit the young company hard: three business-side colleagues left, and Batory, who had been chief technology officer, became chief executive ([Sramana Mitra](https://www.sramanamitra.com/2020/03/09/bootstrapping-with-services-from-poland-to-a-us-saas-company-stefan-batory-ceo-of-booksy-part-1/)). He has described a period of about two years in which the company paid no salaries ([Meb Faber Show](https://mebfaber.com/2020/07/27/episode-240-stefan-batory-booksy-this-is-probably-the-last-major-vertical-thats-not-digitalizedand-this-is-a-massive-space/)).

The company survived and then grew fast. Batory says it began winning major clients around 2000–2001 and then tripled its revenue year by year, and that Deloitte ranked it among the 50 fastest-growing companies in Central and Eastern Europe for seven consecutive years and in the top 500 fastest-growing companies in EMEA for three consecutive years ([Sramana Mitra](https://www.sramanamitra.com/2020/03/09/bootstrapping-with-services-from-poland-to-a-us-saas-company-stefan-batory-ceo-of-booksy-part-1/)). Those rankings are Batory's own account; no Deloitte list was read in full for this profile, so the claim is attributed to him rather than stated as independently verified.

Batory also ran a second software company in parallel, a spinoff serving classified-media publishers such as PennySaver, while EO Networks remained the core services business ([Meb Faber Show](https://mebfaber.com/2020/07/27/episode-240-stefan-batory-booksy-this-is-probably-the-last-major-vertical-thats-not-digitalizedand-this-is-a-massive-space/)).

## The listing, iTaxi, and the road to Booksy

The one fact about EO Networks that is verified by a tier1 source is its stock-market debut. The Warsaw Stock Exchange's Fact Book 2013 lists "EONET" among the new companies on NewConnect in 2012, with a first quotation on 25 April 2012, an issue price of PLN 11.31 and a year-end closing price of PLN 11.07 ([Warsaw Stock Exchange](https://www.yumpu.com/en/document/view/48451488/the-warsaw-stock-exchange-gpw/211)). NewConnect is the exchange's alternative market for smaller companies, so the listing was a debut on that market rather than a main-market IPO, even though Batory describes it as having "IPOed" the company ([Meb Faber Show](https://mebfaber.com/2020/07/27/episode-240-stefan-batory-booksy-this-is-probably-the-last-major-vertical-thats-not-digitalizedand-this-is-a-massive-space/)). Batory says he left the company about a year after the listing ([Meb Faber Show](https://mebfaber.com/2020/07/27/episode-240-stefan-batory-booksy-this-is-probably-the-last-major-vertical-thats-not-digitalizedand-this-is-a-massive-space/)).

The same year as the listing, Batory started the taxi-hailing app [iTaxi](/organizations/itaxi/) as a project inside his software business and later bought it out as a standalone company ([Meb Faber Show](https://mebfaber.com/2020/07/27/episode-240-stefan-batory-booksy-this-is-probably-the-last-major-vertical-thats-not-digitalizedand-this-is-a-massive-space/)). Two years later he founded Booksy, in 2014, with Konrad Howard ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2021/01/29/booksy-slots-in-70m/)). The arc — services company, listing, taxi app, then a booking marketplace — is the thread that puts EO Networks in the Booksy entity graph: it is where Batory learned to build and sell software to businesses before applying the same playbook to beauty salons.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is EO Networks still listed?**
As of the January 2026 market-data crawl, the company was listed on NewConnect under the ticker EON, with 157 employees and a Warsaw address ([Stockopedia](https://www.stockopedia.com/share-prices/eo-networks-sa-WAR:EON/)). No later listing status was verified from a fully-read source.

**When was EO Networks founded?**
That is genuinely unclear. Founder Stefan Batory says January 2000; market data gives an incorporation date of 1 July 2009 and a 2001 founding by a different named founder. This profile states Batory's account as his account and flags the conflict rather than picking one date.

**What does EO Networks do?**
It develops and maintains custom software, portals, transactional systems and CRM and ERP software, and sells hardware with maintenance and help-desk services ([Stockopedia](https://www.stockopedia.com/share-prices/eo-networks-sa-WAR:EON/)).

**What is its connection to Booksy?**
EO Networks was Stefan Batory's first company. He built it, listed it in 2012, left about a year later, started iTaxi, and then founded Booksy in 2014 ([Meb Faber Show](https://mebfaber.com/2020/07/27/episode-240-stefan-batory-booksy-this-is-probably-the-last-major-vertical-thats-not-digitalizedand-this-is-a-massive-space/), [Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2021/01/29/booksy-slots-in-70m/)).
