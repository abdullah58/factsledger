## Overview

tech.eu is a pan-European technology news, data and events publication launched in the fall of 2013 by Robin Wauters, a former TechCrunch writer and former European editor of The Next Web ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/04/23/tech-eu/)). Its founding idea was simple and, in 2013, unfashionable: Europe's technology industry deserved in-depth, data-based coverage of its own, rather than being treated as a footnote to Silicon Valley news. Over eleven years it grew from a small blog into a media, market-intelligence and events company, was acquired by Turkey's Webrazzi in October 2021, and lost its founder in May 2024 — three moments that, taken together, describe the whole arc of European tech media in the 2010s ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/07/turkish-tech-publisher-webrazzi-acquires-tech-eu-consolidating-tech-news-across-europe/?tpcc=tcplustwitter#!), [Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/robin-wauters-announces-his-departure/)).

The publication's identity is best captured in its own founding mission, stated in April 2014: "data-based analysis, insights, research and 'news with context' relating to the wider European technology industry" — explicitly not chasing fast news and "cheap traffic" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/04/23/tech-eu/)). That stance shaped everything that followed: a funding database and market-intelligence reports alongside the news, a summit rather than a newsroom arms race, and a deliberately small funding round designed to keep investors away from editorial decisions.

## Launch and the small seed round

Wauters' own account of the founding is disarmingly plain. He had just moved back from Barcelona to Brussels and had recently been let go from The Next Web — "no harm, no foul," he wrote — partly because his desire to exclusively cover Europe's "in-many-ways-still-fledgling" startup ecosystems was considered too niche for a growing international media organisation ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2021/10/07/webrazzi-acquisition)). So, "with a group of friends," he set out to cover European startups and innovation "in a way that hadn't been done before, delivering curated news combined with analysis and insights on a pan-European level, in English and targeting a global audience" ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2021/10/07/webrazzi-acquisition)).

The co-founders were Roxanne Varza — then at Microsoft and formerly editor of TechCrunch France — and Jon Bradford of Techstars London ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/04/23/tech-eu/)). In April 2014 the company closed a seed round of approximately €150,000 from around 11 investors, including Dave McClure's 500 Startups, Founder Institute founder Adeo Ressi, and a number of prominent European angels ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/04/23/tech-eu/)). The round was still closing when the news broke, and not all investors had been cleared for disclosure; the partial list TechCrunch published also included Tim O'Shea, Doug Scott, Toon Vanagt, Cedric Kutlu, T-REGS, Simon McDermott, Oriol Juncosa and Daniel Waterhouse ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/04/23/tech-eu/)).

What matters more than the amount is why it was small. Wauters told TechCrunch the round was raised "so we could afford to dedicate some resources to building the best European technology publication we think is possible," and he was explicit about the firewall: the round was kept small deliberately to keep "the wall between our investors and anything to do with editorial impenetrably thick" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/04/23/tech-eu/)). For a publication whose product was credibility — data and analysis that investors, corporates and policymakers would trust — that wall was the business model, not a slogan.

## The Webrazzi acquisition

Eight years later, tech.eu was acquired. On October 7, 2021, Webrazzi, the main tech startup media title in Turkey, acquired tech.eu; the terms were not disclosed, and the pooled entity would offer combined editorial, data and research platforms providing news and market intelligence ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/07/turkish-tech-publisher-webrazzi-acquires-tech-eu-consolidating-tech-news-across-europe/?tpcc=tcplustwitter#!)). Webrazzi had been launched in 2006 by Arda Kutsal and reached 1.25 million readers a month — 15 million a year — and the acquisition was a way to expand its European and English-language reach ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/07/turkish-tech-publisher-webrazzi-acquires-tech-eu-consolidating-tech-news-across-europe/?tpcc=tcplustwitter#!)).

The deal had a long prehistory. Kutsal had first approached Wauters about investing in 2014 — "unfortunately right after we closed our seed round," Wauters wrote — and the two stayed in touch for seven years before catching up late in 2020 to discuss how their companies were doing "in times of massive growth of the tech sector globally" ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2021/10/07/webrazzi-acquisition)). "We talked for hours, followed up with another call, and another … and one thing led to another," Wauters wrote; the negotiation itself took eight to nine months and closed during the pandemic ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2021/10/07/webrazzi-acquisition)).

Wauters gave two reasons for selling, and both were about capability rather than money. The first was Webrazzi's technology: its own content-management system, events platform and native advertising stack, built by a 25-person team, against tech.eu's small team. The second was scale: the combined organisation would span Europe, the UK, Russia and Turkey, and could cover the European scene "consistently, and qualitatively" in a way a small independent could not ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2021/10/07/webrazzi-acquisition)). He was also candid with readers that change was coming — "it would be a lie to pretend that nothing will change" — and promised "positive change in the form of various new initiatives," with reader feedback taken on board ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2021/10/07/webrazzi-acquisition)). By the time of the sale, tech.eu had, in Wauters' words, built "a solid reputation for covering the European tech ecosystems in a consistent, qualitative way, and — importantly — to build a sustainable, even profitable business on top of that" ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2021/10/07/webrazzi-acquisition)).

The competitive field TechCrunch named at the time puts tech.eu in context: EU-Startups, Silicon Canals, and the Financial Times-backed Sifted ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/07/turkish-tech-publisher-webrazzi-acquires-tech-eu-consolidating-tech-news-across-europe/?tpcc=tcplustwitter#!)). European tech media in 2021 was a small, crowded field, and the Webrazzi deal was a consolidation play — two regional leaders pooling editorial, data and research rather than competing for the same readers and sponsors.

## The business after 2021

By the time Wauters left, tech.eu had become something broader than the blog he founded. Silicon Canals described it in May 2024 as "the European tech media, market intelligence, and events company," and its business by then spanned editorial, data-driven market-intelligence reports — broken down by investment stage, geography and sector — events led by the Tech.eu Summit, and research and consultancy services ([Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/robin-wauters-announces-his-departure/)).

Wauters' departure, announced on LinkedIn on May 31, 2024, closed the founding era. "Today is my last day at Tech.eu, the European tech media, market intelligence, and events company I co-founded almost 11 years ago," he wrote. "It feels like the right time just after the third Tech.eu Summit, and almost three years after the company was acquired by Webrazzi founder Arda Kutsal and his team" ([Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/robin-wauters-announces-his-departure/)). He said he would take time off to consider his next moves, and later joined the EIC Scaling Club to build a stronger European deep-tech community ([Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/robin-wauters-announces-his-departure/)). Silicon Canals noted that since its founding in October 2013, Wauters had grown tech.eu "to become one of the leading sources of European tech news, data analysis, and market intelligence, assisting investors, corporates, policymakers, and other professionals in navigating the region's vast potential" ([Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/robin-wauters-announces-his-departure/)).

## The Booksy connection

tech.eu's connection to this site's loop is as a source, not as an investor. It is the tier2 European tech press cited in the [Booksy](/organizations/booksy/) profile for the 2017 Series A and 2018 Series B coverage, alongside TechCrunch and Global Venturing, and its 2021 Series C article is also part of the Booksy record. That role is itself a measure of what tech.eu became: a publication whose coverage of a Polish beauty-booking startup's funding rounds was treated as a citable record by the companies and investors involved. For a site founded to fill a gap in European tech coverage, being the source of record for a European company's funding history is the mission fulfilled.
