## Overview

The oldest executive department of the United States government is shrinking itself. Created in 1789, the Department of State — the country's foreign ministry, headed by the secretary of state, the official often called America's "chief diplomat" — began 2025 with a new secretary and a wrecking plan ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/US-Department-of-State); [NPR](https://www.weku.org/newsbreak/2025-07-11/hundreds-laid-off-in-state-department-overhaul)). In July, the department fired more than 1,300 career diplomats and civil servants as part of what officials called the largest overhaul of the agency in decades, cutting its Washington-based staff by about 15% ([AP](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/us-state-department-is-laying-off-more-than-1-300-staffers); [NPR](https://www.weku.org/newsbreak/2025-07-11/hundreds-laid-off-in-state-department-overhaul)). The department that once ran the mint, issued patents and conducted the census is now being deliberately unwound and rebuilt by its own leadership.

Secretary of State [Marco Rubio](https://factsledger.com/people/marco-rubio/) is leading the overhaul, eliminating 132 offices he has described as part of a "bloated bureaucracy" ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/newsbreak/2025-07-11/hundreds-laid-off-in-state-department-overhaul)). His staff rewrote key personnel rules to allow the department to fire foreign service and civil service officials in roles now being phased out ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/newsbreak/2025-07-11/hundreds-laid-off-in-state-department-overhaul)). Rubio has defended the move as essential to speeding up internal processes: "There were 40 boxes on this piece of paper," he told senators in May — "that means 40 people had to check off 'yes' before it even got to me. That's ridiculous. And if any one of those boxes didn't get checked, the memo didn't move. That can't continue" ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/newsbreak/2025-07-11/hundreds-laid-off-in-state-department-overhaul)).

## The layoffs and what they hit

The numbers came in an internal notice obtained by the Associated Press. The department sent layoff notices to 1,107 civil servants and 246 foreign service officers with domestic assignments in the United States ([AP](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/us-state-department-is-laying-off-more-than-1-300-staffers)). Affected foreign service officers were placed on administrative leave for 120 days before formally losing their jobs; most civil servants got a 60-day separation period ([AP](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/us-state-department-is-laying-off-more-than-1-300-staffers)). "In connection with the departmental reorganization … the department is streamlining domestic operations to focus on diplomatic priorities," the notice said, adding that cuts were "carefully tailored to affect non-core functions, duplicative or redundant offices, and offices where considerable efficiencies may be found" ([AP](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/us-state-department-is-laying-off-more-than-1-300-staffers)).

The critics answered in apocalyptic terms. The American Academy of Diplomacy, which represents former ambassadors, accused Rubio of gutting the department's institutional knowledge and called the move "an act of vandalism" ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/newsbreak/2025-07-11/hundreds-laid-off-in-state-department-overhaul)). "This isn't just about trimming fat," said Thomas Shannon, a former undersecretary of state in the first Trump administration: "We're removing a significant chunk of our civil service and foreign service employees and restructuring in ways that reflect a diminished global agenda" ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/newsbreak/2025-07-11/hundreds-laid-off-in-state-department-overhaul)). Shannon warned the shake-up could leave the U.S. lagging behind rivals like China, and pointed to the closure of USAID and the loss of experts with critical language and cultural skills as blows to American influence ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/newsbreak/2025-07-11/hundreds-laid-off-in-state-department-overhaul)). Senate Foreign Relations Democrats, led by Ranking Member Jeanne Shaheen, said that if the administration was serious about "America First," it should invest in the diplomatic corps rather than erode the institutions that protect U.S. interests ([NPR](https://www.weku.org/newsbreak/2025-07-11/hundreds-laid-off-in-state-department-overhaul)).

## 236 years of growth, then the reversal

The irony of the 2025 cuts is the department's long arc of expansion. The first secretary of state was Thomas Jefferson, who was less than thrilled to take the job — it meant giving up his role as minister to France, a post that would become ambassador in 1893 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/US-Department-of-State)). The early department had fewer than two dozen employees — eight at inception, 23 by 1830, 42 by 1860 — and its first four secretaries, Jefferson, James Madison, James Monroe and John Quincy Adams, all went on to the presidency; six secretaries in total have become president ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/US-Department-of-State)).

The department matured with the country's ambitions. During the Civil War, Secretary William H. Seward worked to keep European powers neutral — and in 1867 negotiated the purchase of Alaska from Russia for $7.2 million, mocked at the time as "Seward's Folly" until Alaska became the 49th state in 1959 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/US-Department-of-State)). The Rogers Act of 1924 professionalized the foreign service; Cordell Hull, the longest-serving secretary (1933–1944), earned the 1945 Nobel Peace Prize for his role in creating the United Nations; and George C. Marshall, serving only two years, architected the Marshall Plan — a humanitarian program that also served as a bulwark against Soviet expansionism, earning him the 1953 Nobel ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/US-Department-of-State)). Later came the era of "shuttle diplomacy," in which Henry Kissinger ping-ponged among combatant Middle Eastern countries seeking peace agreements ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/US-Department-of-State)).

The department has also been the center of some of America's darkest government controversies: Joseph McCarthy's Red Scare hunt for communists (and the parallel Lavender Scare purge of gay employees) targeted State Department staff; the 1971 Pentagon Papers exposed diplomatic decision-making mislead the public about Vietnam; Colin Powell's 2003 UN presentation on Iraqi weapons of mass destruction collapsed when no stockpiles were found; and the 2012 Benghazi attack killed Ambassador Chris Stevens and three other Americans, forcing an overhaul of embassy security ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/US-Department-of-State)).

## The building and its history

The department's physical history carries a quiet irony of its own. The 1930s Foggy Bottom headquarters the War Department built — and then outgrew, prompting the construction of [the Pentagon](https://factsledger.com/organizations/pentagon/) — is now used by the State Department ([Arlington Tours](https://www.arlingtontours.com/pentagon)). "Foggy Bottom," the neighborhood, is itself the department's metonym. Britannica notes that Rubio is the third of Donald Trump's three secretaries of state, after Rex Tillerson and Mike Pompeo, and that his tenure has included navigating foreign policy positions — including on the Russia-Ukraine war — that have put the U.S. at odds with long-term NATO allies ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/US-Department-of-State)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not assert a current total employee count — tertiary sources give roughly 80,000 worldwide, but no such source was read in full, so the figure is not stated. It distinguishes the 1,353 July 2025 layoff notices from the "nearly 3,000" total departures that include voluntary exits; no single final number is claimed. It does not state the Senate vote margin confirming Rubio, which appears in this site's Marco Rubio profile but not in a source read for this profile. The Foggy Bottom-building claim rests on a single tier3 source. And nothing here predicts whether the reorganization will survive legal challenges or a change of administration — the "largest overhaul in decades" is described as what was announced and begun, not as an irreversible outcome.
