## Overview

Insurance Australia Group (IAG) is Australia's largest general insurance group — an umbrella organisation, listed on the ASX, that owns well-known customer brands including NRMA Insurance, CGU, SGIO, SGIC, and WFI rather than selling under its own name ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/companies/iag/53115/)). Its origins lie with the National Roads and Motorists' Association, which began offering motor insurance in New South Wales and the ACT in 1920 ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/companies/iag/53115/)). After a nationwide expansion in the 1990s — including the 1998 purchases of state-owned insurers SGIO (WA) and SGIC (SA) and a 1999 joint venture with Victoria's RACV — NRMA demutualised in 2000, separating road services from insurance and issuing shares to members ([Insurance News](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/analysis/iags-new-australia-division-is-worth-watching)). The listed entity was renamed Insurance Australia Group in 2002 ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/companies/iag/53115/)).

## Built by acquisition

IAG's defining deal came in January 2003, when it completed the acquisition of CGU and NZI — described at the time as a coup, capturing one of Australia's largest commercial insurers ([Insurance News](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/analysis/iags-new-australia-division-is-worth-watching)). The four-year transformation took the group from a NSW-based business of a couple of thousand employees to a listed company of around 11,500 staff, half outside NSW ([HR Director](https://www.hcamag.com/au/specialisation/learning-development/assessing-acquisitions-an-iag-case-study/133003)). Through CGU, IAG inherited an insurance lineage stretching back to the Great Fire of London in the 1660s — a heritage its integration team deliberately made the cultural anchor of the merged business ([HR Director](https://www.hcamag.com/au/specialisation/learning-development/assessing-acquisitions-an-iag-case-study/133003)). Later deals added AMI (2012) and Wesfarmers' insurance underwriting businesses (2014), and in 2015 Berkshire Hathaway took a 3.7% stake — the same era in which IAG launched its $75 million start-up fund, Firemark Ventures ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/companies/iag/53115/)).

## Unifying the empire

A group assembled by acquisition was, by 2015, "a series of independent silos, each of which carried its own systems and cultures" ([Insurance News](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/analysis/iags-new-australia-division-is-worth-watching)). CEO Peter Harmer's answer was the Australia Division — a single unit centralising accountability for customer, product, distribution, and operations — and Federal Court approval to consolidate nine insurance licences into two: Insurance Australia Limited and the RACV-part-owned Insurance Manufacturers of Australia ([Insurance News](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/analysis/iags-new-australia-division-is-worth-watching)). Nick Hawkins, a 12-year CFO of the group, became managing director and CEO in November 2020 ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/companies/iag/53115/)). Today the group counts more than 8.5 million customers and underwrites around $12.6 billion in premiums a year ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/companies/iag/53115/)).
