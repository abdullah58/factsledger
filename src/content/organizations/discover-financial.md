## Overview

Discover Financial Services was an American bank holding company that issued the Discover Card and owned three payment networks — Discover, PULSE and Diners Club International — and it ended its independent life on May 18, 2025, when [Capital One](/organizations/capital-one/) acquired it for $35.3 billion ([Virginia Business](https://virginiabusiness.com/capital-one-discover-merger-2025/)). It began inside Sears, Roebuck and Co., then the largest retailer in the [United States](/places/united-states/), which launched the Discover Card nationally on January 28, 1986 with two features no major card had offered together: no annual fee and cash back on purchases ([companieshistory.com](https://www.companieshistory.com/discover-financial-services/); [GOBankingRates](https://www.gobankingrates.com/credit-cards/advice/history-discover-card/amp/)). Over forty years it passed through three owners — Sears, Morgan Stanley, and independence — built a debit network and bought a global one, sued [Visa](/organizations/visa/) and [Mastercard](/organizations/mastercard/) over exclusionary rules, and closed with a 17-year misclassification scandal that cost it more than $1.4 billion in penalties and restitution ([CUToday](https://www.cutoday.info/Fresh-Today/Discover-To-Pay-Up-To-1.2-Billion-In-Credit-Card-Misclassification-Case)).

## From Sears to independence

Sears built Discover as one piece of a financial-services empire: in 1981 it bought the Dean Witter Reynolds brokerage and the Coldwell Banker real estate franchise, grouped them as the Sears Financial Network, and in 1985 acquired Greenwood Trust Company — a Delaware bank incorporated in 1911 — to provide the banking infrastructure ([companieshistory.com](https://www.companieshistory.com/discover-financial-services/)). The card launched the next year with no annual fee and a cash-back bonus, both industry firsts at the time, and higher credit limits than Visa and Mastercard offered ([GOBankingRates](https://www.gobankingrates.com/credit-cards/advice/history-discover-card/amp/)). The launch was a fight as much as a product: merchants were slow to accept a card from a retailer that had stopped taking competitors' cards in its own stores, and rival networks' rules kept banks from issuing Discover cards at all ([GOBankingRates](https://www.gobankingrates.com/credit-cards/advice/history-discover-card/amp/)).

Ownership changed three times in fourteen years. Sears spun off its financial-services arm as Dean Witter, Discover & Co. in March 1993; that company merged with Morgan Stanley in 1997; and on June 30, 2007 Morgan Stanley spun Discover off as an independent public company, distributing one Discover share for every two Morgan Stanley shares and listing it on the New York Stock Exchange as DFS, with regular trading from July 2 ([companieshistory.com](https://www.companieshistory.com/discover-financial-services/); [Morgan Stanley](https://www.morganstanley.com/press-releases/morgan-stanley-announces-record-and-distribution-dates-for-spin-off-of-discover_4984)). The acceptance fight ended in court: after the Justice Department prevailed in antitrust litigation against Visa and Mastercard's exclusionary rules, Discover sued both networks in 2005 seeking damages, spending more than $101 million on the litigation since then ([Business Travel News](https://www.businesstravelnews.com/Business-Globalization/Discover-Buys-Diners-for-Global-Expansion)).

## Building a network: PULSE and Diners Club

Independence made Discover a network-builder rather than just a card issuer. In January 2005 it closed a $311 million acquisition of the PULSE EFT Association, a Houston-based debit network owned by its 4,100 member banks, credit unions and savings institutions; the deal more than doubled Discover's US card base and transaction volume, combining 50 million card accounts and 4 million merchant locations with a major PIN-debit switch ([Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/discover-seals-its-pulse-acquisition-following-pulse-members-ok/)). Three years later it bought its way abroad: in April 2008 Discover agreed to buy Diners Club International from [Citigroup](/organizations/citigroup/) for $165 million in cash, closing the deal on July 1, 2008 ([Business Travel News](https://www.businesstravelnews.com/Business-Globalization/Discover-Buys-Diners-for-Global-Expansion); [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1393612/000119312508144606/0001193125-08-144606.txt)). Diners, founded in 1950, was the oldest card network against Discover's youngest; the purchase brought more than $30 billion in annual charge volume outside North America, agreements with 44 network licensees, and acceptance at more than 8 million incremental merchant and cash-access locations in 185 countries ([Business Travel News](https://www.businesstravelnews.com/Business-Globalization/Discover-Buys-Diners-for-Global-Expansion)).

## The misclassification scandal and the Capital One ending

Discover's final years were shadowed by a compliance failure that began almost as soon as it became independent. From 2007, Discover's systems misclassified certain consumer credit cards — its "rewards", "premium" and "premium plus" cards — as commercial cards, pushing those transactions into higher-priced interchange categories and causing merchants, acquirers and payment intermediaries to overpay fees through the end of 2023 ([CUToday](https://www.cutoday.info/Fresh-Today/Discover-To-Pay-Up-To-1.2-Billion-In-Credit-Card-Misclassification-Case)). Regulators said the problem lasted roughly 17 years and merchants were overcharged more than $1 billion ([CUToday](https://www.cutoday.info/Fresh-Today/Discover-To-Pay-Up-To-1.2-Billion-In-Credit-Card-Misclassification-Case)). In April 2025 the FDIC ordered Discover Bank to provide at least $1.225 billion in restitution to affected merchants and imposed a $150 million civil money penalty, while the Federal Reserve separately assessed a $100 million penalty ([CUToday](https://www.cutoday.info/Fresh-Today/Discover-To-Pay-Up-To-1.2-Billion-In-Credit-Card-Misclassification-Case)). Discover denied wrongdoing but agreed to settle the class action to avoid continued litigation, and a federal judge granted final approval in May 2026 to a settlement of $540 million to $1.225 billion ([CUToday](https://www.cutoday.info/Fresh-Today/Discover-To-Pay-Up-To-1.2-Billion-In-Credit-Card-Misclassification-Case)).

The scandal surfaced during the regulatory review of Capital One's acquisition, with Capital One executives acknowledging the compliance problems could create substantial additional remediation costs ([CUToday](https://www.cutoday.info/Fresh-Today/Discover-To-Pay-Up-To-1.2-Billion-In-Credit-Card-Misclassification-Case)). The deal closed anyway on May 18, 2025, after Federal Reserve and OCC approval on April 18, 2025; Discover's branded cards continue alongside Capital One's, and the Discover, PULSE and Diners Club networks joined Capital One's suite of offerings ([Capital One](https://www.capitalone.com/about/newsroom/capital-one-completes-acquisition-of-discover/)). Capital One's acquisition program continued into 2026: [Zacks](/organizations/zacks/) reported in April 2026 that Capital One had completed its $5.15 billion acquisition of [Brex](/organizations/brex/), the corporate-card fintech ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2897536/capital-one-completes-brex-buyout-boosts-digital-payments-strategy)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who founded Discover?**
Sears, Roebuck and Co., which built the card inside its Sears Financial Network — Dean Witter Reynolds brokerage plus Coldwell Banker real estate — and acquired Greenwood Trust Company in 1985 for banking infrastructure ([companieshistory.com](https://www.companieshistory.com/discover-financial-services/)).

**What made the Discover Card different at launch?**
It carried no annual fee and offered a cash-back bonus on purchases, both industry firsts in 1986, with higher credit limits than Visa and Mastercard typically offered ([GOBankingRates](https://www.gobankingrates.com/credit-cards/advice/history-discover-card/amp/)).

**When did Discover become independent?**
On June 30, 2007, when Morgan Stanley spun it off as a public company listed on the NYSE as DFS, after owning it through the 1997 Dean Witter merger ([Morgan Stanley](https://www.morganstanley.com/press-releases/morgan-stanley-announces-record-and-distribution-dates-for-spin-off-of-discover_4984)).

**What did Capital One pay for Discover?**
$35.3 billion in an all-stock deal announced February 19, 2024 and closed May 18, 2025, after Federal Reserve and OCC approval on April 18, 2025 ([Virginia Business](https://virginiabusiness.com/capital-one-discover-merger-2025/)).

**What was the card misclassification scandal?**
From 2007 to 2023 Discover classed some consumer cards as commercial, inflating merchant interchange fees; regulators ordered at least $1.225 billion in restitution plus $250 million in penalties, and a class settlement of $540 million to $1.225 billion won final approval in May 2026 ([CUToday](https://www.cutoday.info/Fresh-Today/Discover-To-Pay-Up-To-1.2-Billion-In-Credit-Card-Misclassification-Case)).
