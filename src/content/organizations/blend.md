## Overview

This profile covers **Blend Labs, Inc.** — the San Francisco financial-software company listed on the New York Stock Exchange as BLND — not the various unrelated businesses that share the name. Blend builds a cloud-based digital origination platform for banks, credit unions and mortgage lenders, spanning mortgages, consumer loans and deposit accounts and helping institutions streamline workflows from application to close ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/BLND.N/profile)). Co-founded in 2012 by Nima Ghamsari, a former employee of analytics giant Palantir Technologies, it rode the pandemic-era lending boom to a $360 million IPO in July 2021 ([Reuters](https://mymixfm.com/2021/07/06/mortgage-focused-software-startup-blend-seeks-4-billion-valuation-in-u-s-ipo/)) — then spent the 2022 rate shock unwinding the acquisition that the boom had funded.

## The platform

Blend's software digitizes the banking experience end to end, "from the moment they start an application for a loan or a deposit account to the moment they digitally sign the final documents," as [Reuters](/organizations/reuters/) described it at the IPO ([Reuters](https://mymixfm.com/2021/07/06/mortgage-focused-software-startup-blend-seeks-4-billion-valuation-in-u-s-ipo/)). The company describes itself as a digital origination platform whose suite covers mortgage and consumer banking — home equity, deposit accounts, credit cards, personal lending and auto lending — with Blend Builder letting customers configure custom workflows from pre-built components ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/BLND.N/profile)). The platform also embeds third-party technology, data and service providers, including a marketplace of insurance carriers and settlement agencies ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/BLND.N/profile)). One measure of its institutional standing: housing-finance giant Fannie Mae has integrated data from Blend into its underwriting program, using it to help lenders evaluate borrowers' rental payment history when approving home loans ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/markets/companies/BLND.N/profile)).

## The IPO and the money behind it

Blend filed confidentially for its listing in April 2021, targeting a valuation of nearly $4 billion and seeking to raise up to $360 million; it had last been valued at $3.3 billion after a January funding round ([Reuters](https://mymixfm.com/2021/07/06/mortgage-focused-software-startup-blend-seeks-4-billion-valuation-in-u-s-ipo/)). The offering priced 20 million Class A shares at the upper end of its $16–$18 range, with Goldman Sachs, Allen & Company and Wells Fargo Securities as lead underwriters ([Reuters](https://mymixfm.com/2021/07/06/mortgage-focused-software-startup-blend-seeks-4-billion-valuation-in-u-s-ipo/)). On the first day of trading the shares opened at $20.00 and closed at $20.90 ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/blend-labs-lands-on-nyse-in-360m-ipo/)). The underwriters held a 30-day option to buy another 3 million shares, which could have pushed the offering to $420 million ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/blend-labs-lands-on-nyse-in-360m-ipo/)).

The IPO capped a fast pre-public funding sprint: Salesforce Ventures invested in May 2019, Temasek and General Atlantic co-led a $130 million Series E the following month, a $75 million Series F in August 2020 valued the company at $1.7 billion, and Coatue Management and Tiger Global co-led a $300 million Series G in January 2021 — with earlier backing from Greylock, Emergence Capital, 8VC, Lightspeed, Founders Fund, Formation 8, Nyca Partners and Andreessen Horowitz ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/blend-labs-lands-on-nyse-in-360m-ipo/)). Timothy Mayopoulos, the former chief executive of Fannie Mae, served as president through the listing ([Reuters](https://mymixfm.com/2021/07/06/mortgage-focused-software-startup-blend-seeks-4-billion-valuation-in-u-s-ipo/)).

## The refi bust

Just before going public, Blend paid $422 million to acquire Title365, a national title insurance and settlement services provider, from Mr. Cooper Group ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/04/21/blend-labs-laying-off-200-employees-as-mortgage-refinancings-plummet/)). The deal supercharged 2021 revenue — up 144% to $234.5 million — but operating expenses rose 129% to $313.2 million, and headcount ballooned from 577 at the start of the year to 2,276 by its end, including 587 Title365 employees in Chennai, India ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/04/21/blend-labs-laying-off-200-employees-as-mortgage-refinancings-plummet/)).

Then rates rose. Fannie Mae economists forecast refinancings would fall roughly 80% from 2020's $2.8 trillion, and Blend — whose title business was "driven heavily by refinance," in the words of executive Marc Greenberg — laid off 200 employees, about 10% of its workforce, in April 2022, shedding $34.5 million in annual payroll costs ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/04/21/blend-labs-laying-off-200-employees-as-mortgage-refinancings-plummet/)). Ghamsari told staff the company was "renewing our focus on the technology that got us here" — the software platform — while cutting the operational businesses that had grown up around the refi boom ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/04/21/blend-labs-laying-off-200-employees-as-mortgage-refinancings-plummet/)).
