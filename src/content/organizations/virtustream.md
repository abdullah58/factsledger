## Overview

Virtustream was an enterprise cloud company founded in 2009 by Rodney Rogers and Kevin Reid (president and chief technology officer) in Bethesda, Maryland, selling infrastructure-as-a-service and cloud management built around its xStream platform — aimed squarely at mission-critical, I/O-intensive enterprise workloads such as SAP ([Bloomberg](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/startups-tech/technology/emcs-us12-billion-virtustream-deal-furthers-cloud-strategy), [CRN](https://www.crn.com/news/cloud/300076926/emc-eyes-hybrid-cloud-market-with-1-2-b-acquisition-of-virtustream)). On May 26, 2015, EMC announced it would acquire Virtustream for $1.2 billion in cash, and the deal closed that July ([CRN](https://www.crn.com/news/cloud/300076926/emc-eyes-hybrid-cloud-market-with-1-2-b-acquisition-of-virtustream), [CRN](http://www.crn.com/news/cloud/300077410/watch-out-aws-virtustream-deal-begins-emcs-life-as-a-cloud-provider.htm)). Three months later, Dell announced it would buy EMC for $67 billion — so Virtustream, EMC's property for roughly 14 months, passed to Dell Technologies with the merger's completion on September 7, 2016 ([AP](http://phys.org/news/2015-10-dell-emc-transaction-valued-billion.html), [Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/450303842/Combined-Dell-and-EMC-company-targets-intelligent-things)).

## The IPO that never happened

Rogers told EMC's announcement-day conference call that he had spent about a year exploring taking Virtustream public "when suitors began to gather for an outright acquisition" — and that he chose EMC for its "extraordinary vision for what it intends to accomplish in this space" ([CRN](https://www.crn.com/news/cloud/300076926/emc-eyes-hybrid-cloud-market-with-1-2-b-acquisition-of-virtustream)). EMC chairman and CEO Joe Tucci called the $1.2 billion cash deal "a game changer," positioning Virtustream as the managed-cloud-services arm of EMC's "federation" — alongside EMC Infrastructure, VMware, VCE, Pivotal and RSA — and as the vehicle to move customers' entire application portfolios to the cloud ([CRN](https://www.crn.com/news/cloud/300076926/emc-eyes-hybrid-cloud-market-with-1-2-b-acquisition-of-virtustream)). Virtustream had recently landed in Gartner's 2015 cloud IaaS Magic Quadrant, and its roster of blue-chip customers included Coca-Cola, Hess, Lexmark, Heinz and Kawasaki ([CRN](https://www.crn.com/news/cloud/300076926/emc-eyes-hybrid-cloud-market-with-1-2-b-acquisition-of-virtustream), [Bloomberg](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/startups-tech/technology/emcs-us12-billion-virtustream-deal-furthers-cloud-strategy)).

## Why EMC paid: SLAs down to the transaction

Virtustream's technical pitch went beyond ordinary IaaS. Its xStream platform — already integrated with VMware vSphere — was architected to guarantee service-level agreements not just for infrastructure availability but for application performance and transaction latency, guarantees CRN noted were "unusual in this corner of the IT management business" ([CRN](https://www.crn.com/news/cloud/300076926/emc-eyes-hybrid-cloud-market-with-1-2-b-acquisition-of-virtustream)). The focus was application lifecycle automation for I/O-intensive, mission-critical workloads like SAP's in-memory S/4HANA database ([CRN](https://www.crn.com/news/cloud/300076926/emc-eyes-hybrid-cloud-market-with-1-2-b-acquisition-of-virtustream)). IDC analyst Stephen Elliot told Bloomberg the deal existed because VMware's vCloud Air couldn't yet handle large companies' critical applications: "Many Fortune 1000 organizations are looking to move these workloads, provided the financial model makes sense and that the sense of control, security and compliance are risk mitigated. While VMware would like to be here, they are not" ([Bloomberg](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/startups-tech/technology/emcs-us12-billion-virtustream-deal-furthers-cloud-strategy)).

The acquisition also played into EMC's corporate drama: activist shareholder Elliott Management had been pressuring EMC to spin off VMware, and analysts read the Virtustream deal as evidence EMC would not — FBR's Daniel Ives and James Moore called it evidence EMC intended to "expand its tentacles" into cloud rather than slim down ([Bloomberg](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/startups-tech/technology/emcs-us12-billion-virtustream-deal-furthers-cloud-strategy)). Partners welcomed the simplicity: one EMC partner told CRN that with Virtustream, a customer got "one sku (so to speak) and one maintenance contract all through EMC eliminating the need for AWS or any third party separate contracts" ([CRN](http://www.crn.com/news/cloud/300077410/watch-out-aws-virtustream-deal-begins-emcs-life-as-a-cloud-provider.htm)). EMC said the buy would have no material impact on 2015 financials but would boost revenues and earnings in 2016 ([CRN](https://www.crn.com/news/cloud/300076926/emc-eyes-hybrid-cloud-market-with-1-2-b-acquisition-of-virtustream)).

## "Beat AWS" — and the awkward internal rival

Partners pitched the acquisition as EMC's answer to Amazon Web Services. "They don't want everybody to go to AWS," one EMC partner told CRN; "They're trying to build something to compete with it" ([CRN](http://www.crn.com/news/cloud/300077410/watch-out-aws-virtustream-deal-begins-emcs-life-as-a-cloud-provider.htm)). But the deal also created an awkward overlap: EMC's own federation included VMware's vCloud Air, a public-cloud offering aimed at the same hybrid market. Raymond James analysts warned that an independent Virtustream "could add to confusion" for VMware customers "unless there is full product, brand, and go-to-market integration" ([CRN](https://www.crn.com/news/cloud/300076926/emc-eyes-hybrid-cloud-market-with-1-2-b-acquisition-of-virtustream)). IDC's Stephen Elliot put it more bluntly: large enterprises needed someone to run their critical applications in the cloud, and "while VMware would like to be here, they are not" ([Bloomberg](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/startups-tech/technology/emcs-us12-billion-virtustream-deal-furthers-cloud-strategy)). **Virtustream and VMware's vCloud Air were distinct, potentially competing products** — not the same offering.

## Absorbed into the biggest tech deal ever

The $67 billion Dell–EMC deal — announced October 12, 2015, the largest-ever acquisition between two technology companies at the time — swallowed Virtustream along with the rest of EMC before the ink on its own acquisition had meaningfully dried ([AP](http://phys.org/news/2015-10-dell-emc-transaction-valued-billion.html)). When the merger completed on September 7, 2016, forming Dell Technologies, Virtustream was listed among the businesses operating under the new brand — alongside Dell EMC, VMware, Pivotal, RSA, SecureWorks and Boomi ([Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/450303842/Combined-Dell-and-EMC-company-targets-intelligent-things), [GovCon Wire](https://www.govconwire.com/article/dell-closes-merger-with-emc-unveils-dell-technologies-brand)).
