**The Ministry of Information and Broadcasting is the Government of India ministry that writes the rules for broadcasting, the press, films and digital media — and runs the machinery that carries the government's own message to the public: the Press Information Bureau, the Central Bureau of Communication, the autonomous public broadcaster Prasar Bharati (All India Radio and Doordarshan), and the National Film Development Corporation, which organises the International Film Festival of India ([Ministry of Information and Broadcasting](https://mib.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-05/finalized-induction-material-of-moib-2.pdf)). Its Central Bureau of Communication co-produced the animated series [Krish, Trish and Baltiboy](/creative-works/krish-trish-and-baltiboy/): *Bharat Hain Hum*, whose trailer the then-I&B Minister [Anurag Thakur](/people/anurag-thakur/) launched on 11 October 2023 ([Binged](https://www.binged.com/news/mib-launches-krish-trish-baltiboy-bharat-hain-hum-trailer/)).**

The ministry is one of the early ministries set up after Independence — the ministry's own induction material notes that Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel was India's first Minister for Information and Broadcasting ([Ministry of Information and Broadcasting](https://mib.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-05/finalized-induction-material-of-moib-2.pdf)). Its current charge is held by Ashwini Vaishnaw, named as Union Minister for Information & Broadcasting in a May 2026 government release appointing Prasoon Joshi as Chairman of Prasar Bharati ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2257506&reg=3&lang=2)).

## What the ministry actually runs

The ministry is functionally organised into five wings — Information, Broadcasting, Films, Economic and Finance — and the Allocation of Business Rules give it a sweep that runs from the Cinematograph Act of 1952 (film certification and industry development) to the Cable Television Networks (Regulation) Act of 1995, the Prasar Bharati Act of 1990, print-media legislation, and, through a Digital Media Division, the IT Rules of 2021 covering news publishers and OTT platforms ([Ministry of Information and Broadcasting](https://mib.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-05/finalized-induction-material-of-moib-2.pdf)). Its mandate is unusual in combining regulation with communication: it is both the rulemaker for private broadcasting, cable television and films, and the government's own publisher. It describes itself as the face of the government in reaching out to the masses, tasked with disseminating information about government policies, schemes and programmes across radio, television, press, social media and traditional folk communication ([Ministry of Information and Broadcasting](https://mib.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-05/finalized-induction-material-of-moib-2.pdf)).

## The media-unit map: 7 + 2 + 3 + 2

What the directory-style SERP results never show clearly is the ministry's actual architecture. Its own 2025 induction material lists 7 media units and attached/subordinate offices, 2 autonomous organisations, 3 training institutes and 2 public-sector undertakings ([Ministry of Information and Broadcasting](https://mib.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-05/finalized-induction-material-of-moib-2.pdf)):

| Category | Bodies |
| -------- | ------ |
| Attached & subordinate offices (7) | [Press Information Bureau](/organizations/press-information-bureau/) (PIB), [Central Bureau of Communication](/organizations/central-bureau-of-communication/) (CBC), Press Registrar General of India, Directorate of Publication Division, New Media Wing, Electronic Media Monitoring Centre, Central Board of Film Certification |
| Autonomous organisations (2) | Press Council of India, [Prasar Bharati](/organizations/prasar-bharati/) (Broadcasting Corporation of India) |
| Training institutes (3) | Indian Institute of Mass Communication, Film and Television Institute of India (Pune), Satyajit Ray Film and Television Institute (Kolkata) |
| Public-sector undertakings (2) | Broadcast Engineering Consultants India Ltd, National Film Development Corporation (NFDC) |

The CBC — the unit that co-produced *Bharat Hain Hum* — was itself only created on 8 December 2017, by merging the Directorate of Advertising and Visual Publicity, the Directorate of Field Publicity and the Song & Drama Division; it operates through 23 Regional Offices and 148 Field Offices as the government's 360-degree communication agency ([CBC](https://cbcindia.gov.in/about-us/)). Prasar Bharati, meanwhile, is the statutory autonomous public service broadcaster established under the 1990 Act and operational since 1997, comprising All India Radio and [Doordarshan](/organizations/doordarshan/) — and it has now launched its own free-to-air OTT platform, Waves ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2257506&reg=3&lang=2)).

## From publicity to content: the KTB co-production

Until recently the CBC was an advertising and outreach body — buying print ads, running exhibitions, staging folk-theatre campaigns. Its co-production of *Krish, Trish and Baltiboy: Bharat Hain Hum* with Graphiti Studios was therefore a structural shift: a government publicity agency financing an animated children's series. Union Minister Anurag Thakur launched the trailer on 11 October 2023, confirming the 52-episode, 11-minute format covering the freedom struggle from the 1500s to 1947, created by [Munjal Shroff](/people/munjal-shroff/) and Tilakraj Shetty ([Binged](https://www.binged.com/news/mib-launches-krish-trish-baltiboy-bharat-hain-hum-trailer/)). The series was produced in 12 Indian languages plus 7 international languages, telecast simultaneously for the first time on Doordarshan, [Netflix](/organizations/netflix/) and [Amazon](/organizations/amazon/) Prime Video — and it was the first series from which the CBC would earn revenue ([Binged](https://www.binged.com/news/mib-launches-krish-trish-baltiboy-bharat-hain-hum-trailer/)). Season 2 was formally launched by the ministry at the [International Film Festival of India](/events/international-film-festival-of-india/) in Goa on 23 November 2024, with Nagarjuna at the event, streaming on Prasar Bharati's Waves platform and reaching a reported 150 countries ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/entertainment/second-season-of-animated-series-krish-trish-and-baltiboy-bharat-hain-hum-launched-at-iffi/)). In the 117th episode of [Mann Ki Baat](/creative-works/mann-ki-baat/) in December 2024, Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](/people/narendra-modi/) named the series directly, calling it a "superhit" among children and praising its stories of lesser-discussed freedom fighters ([Prime Minister's Office](https://www.pmindia.gov.in/en/news_updates/pms-address-in-the-117th-episode-of-mann-ki-baat/)).

The stat that matters here is the scale bet: 52 eleven-minute episodes in 19 languages, simultaneously premiered on a public broadcaster and two global OTT platforms — a distribution footprint that put a government co-production alongside commercial children's programming, with the revenue flowing back to a body that had previously only bought advertising ([Binged](https://www.binged.com/news/mib-launches-krish-trish-baltiboy-bharat-hain-hum-trailer/)).

## IFFI and the NFDC umbrella

The International Film Festival of India — founded in 1952 and permanently based in Goa since 2004 — is jointly organised by the NFDC, the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting and the Entertainment Society of Goa ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190314&reg=3&lang=2); [DD India](https://ddindia.co.in/2025/11/goa-set-to-host-56th-international-film-festival-of-india-from-november-20-to-28/)). The 56th edition in November 2025 drew a record 2,314 submissions from 127 countries and screened 240+ films from 81 countries — scale that underpins its standing as South Asia's only FIAPF-accredited competitive feature-film festival ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190314&reg=3&lang=2)). Its companion film market, WAVES Film Bazaar, has run alongside IFFI since 2007 ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2190314&reg=3&lang=2)).

IFFI's organiser is itself a product of the ministry's biggest recent restructuring: in December 2020 the Union Cabinet approved merging the Films Division (established 1948), the Directorate of Film Festivals (1973), the National Film Archives of India (1964) and the Children's Film Society, India (1955) with the NFDC, and in March 2022 the ministry notified the transfer of documentary production, festival organisation and film preservation to the NFDC, a PSU working under the ministry, with a budgetary allocation of Rs 1,304.52 crore up to 2026 ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/todays-paper/tp-national/cabinet-approves-merger-of-four-film-and-media-units/article33406987.ece); [The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-india-several-film-bodies-part-of-nfdc-7848252/)).

| Law | What the ministry does with it |
| --- | ------------------------------ |
| Cinematograph Act, 1952 | Film certification, import and export of films |
| Cable Television Networks (Regulation) Act, 1995 | Cable TV policy and regulation |
| Prasar Bharati (Broadcasting Corporation of India) Act, 1990 | Administration of the public broadcaster |
| Press Council Act, 1978; Press and Registration of Periodicals Act, 2023 | Print-media policy and regulation |
| IT Rules, 2021 (Part III) | Digital news publishers and online curated content (OTT) |

All five subjects are allocated to the ministry under the Allocation of Business Rules, administered through its Films, Broadcasting, Information and Digital Media wings ([Ministry of Information and Broadcasting](https://mib.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-05/finalized-induction-material-of-moib-2.pdf)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who is India's Information and Broadcasting minister?**
Ashwini Vaishnaw is the Union Minister for Information and Broadcasting, as confirmed by a May 2026 government release on the Prasar Bharati chairmanship. He succeeded Anurag Thakur, who held the portfolio during the 2023 *Krish, Trish and Baltiboy: Bharat Hain Hum* trailer launch.

**Which organisations come under the I&B ministry?**
Fourteen, in four categories: seven attached and subordinate offices (led by the Press Information Bureau, the Central Bureau of Communication and the film certification board), two autonomous bodies (the Press Council of India and Prasar Bharati), three training institutes (IIMC, FTII Pune, SRFTI Kolkata) and two PSUs (BECIL and the NFDC).

**What did the ministry's CBC produce?**
The Central Bureau of Communication co-produced the animated series *Krish, Trish and Baltiboy: Bharat Hain Hum* (52 eleven-minute episodes on the freedom struggle) with Graphiti Studios — launched by Anurag Thakur on 11 October 2023, streamed on Doordarshan, Netflix and Amazon Prime Video, and the first series from which the CBC would earn revenue.

**Who organises the International Film Festival of India?**
IFFI is jointly organised by the National Film Development Corporation, the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting and the Entertainment Society of Goa. Founded in 1952 and based in Goa since 2004, it is South Asia's only FIAPF-accredited competitive feature-film festival.
