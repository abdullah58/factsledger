## Overview

Charter Communications is an American telecommunications company whose consumer services use the Spectrum brand ([Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ca/)). Founded in St. Louis in 1993, it became the largest US cable operator with its $34.5 billion Cox Communications merger in August 2026 ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/charter-communications-inc); [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ca/)). It survived a 2009 bankruptcy, absorbed Time Warner Cable in 2016, and drew a bottom-ten grade from Accountable.US's 2022 American Democracy Scorecard ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2012/12/iin-re-charter-communicationsi-driving-the-equitable-mootness-wedge-deeper); [Charter via PRNewswire](https://newswire.telecomramblings.com/2016/05/charter-communications-time-warner-cable-bright-house-networks-complete-transactions/); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression?partner=rss&%3Butm_source=rss&%3Butm_medium=feed&%3Butm_campaign=rss+fastcompany&%3Butm_content=rss)).

Today the company is headquartered in Stamford, Connecticut, led by president and CEO Chris Winfrey, and serves close to 38 million customers in 45 states, with its network passing around 70 million locations ([Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ca/)). Its services span Spectrum TV, Spectrum Internet, Spectrum Voice, Spectrum Mobile, Spectrum Business and Spectrum Reach advertising ([Charter via PRNewswire](https://newswire.telecomramblings.com/2016/05/charter-communications-time-warner-cable-bright-house-networks-complete-transactions/); [Cablefax](https://www.cablefax.com/campaigns/spectrum-mobile)).

## History: from Cencom to cable giant

Charter Communications, Inc. was formed in January 1993 by Howard Wood, Barry Babcock and Jerry Kent, who had been the CEO, COO and CFO of St. Louis-based Cencom Cable Associates ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/charter-communications-inc)). After Cencom was acquired in 1991 by Crown Media, a subsidiary of Hallmark Cards, Crown backed the trio's new venture and took a 51 percent non-voting stake; Babcock became chairman, Kent president, and Wood management-committee chairman ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/charter-communications-inc)). Charter's first acquisition, in February 1994, spent nearly $200 million on ten cable systems serving about 100,000 subscribers ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/charter-communications-inc)).

The company's trajectory changed in 1998, when Microsoft co-founder Paul Allen bought Charter for $4.5 billion and merged it with Marcus Cable, a company he had previously acquired, creating the seventh-largest US cable operator with 2.4 million customers ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/charter-communications-inc)). Allen's funding then powered an acquisition spree — eleven major deals in 1999 — capped by a November 1999 NASDAQ IPO that raised approximately $3.5 billion, leaving Charter with 6.2 million subscribers as the country's fourth-largest multi-system operator ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/charter-communications-inc)).

Debt caught up with that growth: in March 2009 Charter filed a pre-negotiated Chapter 11 case in New York carrying more than $24 billion in debt, then the nation's fourth-largest cable television company ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2012/12/iin-re-charter-communicationsi-driving-the-equitable-mootness-wedge-deeper)). Junior bondholders and Allen steered the reorganization; Allen kept his voting control for $375 million and a liability release, other noteholders recovered 32.7 percent, and other equity holders received nothing ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2012/12/iin-re-charter-communicationsi-driving-the-equitable-mootness-wedge-deeper)). The plan took effect on November 30, 2009 ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2012/12/iin-re-charter-communicationsi-driving-the-equitable-mootness-wedge-deeper)).

Charter's defining transaction closed on May 18, 2016: its combinations with Time Warner Cable and Bright House Networks ([Charter via PRNewswire](https://newswire.telecomramblings.com/2016/05/charter-communications-time-warner-cable-bright-house-networks-complete-transactions/)). TWC shareholders received cash and Charter stock, Bright House's parent Advance/Newhouse got 31 million exchangeable units plus $2 billion in cash, and Liberty Broadband bought $4.3 billion of Charter shares at $195.70 each ([Charter via PRNewswire](https://newswire.telecomramblings.com/2016/05/charter-communications-time-warner-cable-bright-house-networks-complete-transactions/)). The result served over 25 million customers in 41 states — the second-largest US cable operator at the time — led by Chairman, President and CEO Tom Rutledge ([Charter via PRNewswire](https://newswire.telecomramblings.com/2016/05/charter-communications-time-warner-cable-bright-house-networks-complete-transactions/)).

## The 2022 American Democracy Scorecard

On September 16, 2022, voting-rights advocacy organization [Accountable.US](/organizations/accountable-us/) unveiled its American Democracy Scorecard, grading the nation's top 100 corporations on their support for democracy ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression?partner=rss&%3Butm_source=rss&%3Butm_medium=feed&%3Butm_campaign=rss+fastcompany&%3Butm_content=rss)). Companies were rated on 14 criteria covering pro-democracy statements, political-spending transparency, and PAC donations to lawmakers who opposed voting-rights legislation, objected to certifying the 2020 election, or backed "anti-voter" measures ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression?partner=rss&%3Butm_source=rss&%3Butm_medium=feed&%3Butm_campaign=rss+fastcompany&%3Butm_content=rss)). Two-thirds of the companies received failing marks ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression?partner=rss&%3Butm_source=rss&%3Butm_medium=feed&%3Butm_campaign=rss+fastcompany&%3Butm_content=rss)).

Charter Communications landed among the 10 lowest scorers, alongside AT&T — the scorecard's lowest-ranked company — and [Verizon](/organizations/verizon/), with telecommunications receiving the lowest ranking of any industry ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression?partner=rss&%3Butm_source=rss&%3Butm_medium=feed&%3Butm_campaign=rss+fastcompany&%3Butm_content=rss)). ## After 2016: New York's revocation threat and the 2026 mega-deals

Within two years of the Time Warner Cable deal, Charter faced the most dramatic regulatory confrontation of its history. In July 2018, the New York State Public Service Commission voted to revoke its 2016 approval of Charter's TWC purchase, ordering the company to sell the former TWC system in New York and directing its counsel to bring an enforcement action in State Supreme Court over "past failures and ongoing noncompliance" ([Benton Institute](https://www.benton.org/headlines/new-york-orders-charter-out-state-says-it-must-sell-time-warner-cable-system)). State officials said Charter had repeatedly missed the broadband-expansion deadlines tied to merger approval; it was given 60 days to file an orderly-transition plan while keeping customers connected ([Benton Institute](https://www.benton.org/headlines/new-york-orders-charter-out-state-says-it-must-sell-time-warner-cable-system)).

The standoff was resolved a year later: on July 11, 2019, the PSC approved a settlement under which Charter would pass 145,000 residences and businesses — entirely in Upstate New York — by September 30, 2021, and pay an additional $12 million for further broadband expansion, with $2,800-per-address escrow penalties for missed targets ([NY Public Service Commission](https://documents.dps.ny.gov/public/Common/ViewDoc.aspx?DocRefId={15297CA6-63CB-4D11-9E98-D541B5892A89}&DocTitle=Press%20Release%20-%20PSC%20Approves%20Settlement%20with%20Charter%20Communications)). The department estimated compliance would cost Charter more than $600 million; the settlement resolved the dispute without further litigation ([NY Public Service Commission](https://documents.dps.ny.gov/public/Common/ViewDoc.aspx?DocRefId={15297CA6-63CB-4D11-9E98-D541B5892A89}&DocTitle=Press%20Release%20-%20PSC%20Approves%20Settlement%20with%20Charter%20Communications)).

Meanwhile Charter built a mobile business: Spectrum Mobile soft-launched in June 2018 through an MVNO agreement with Verizon, with a full market launch that September, reaching 8.8 million mobile lines by mid-2024; the Spectrum One bundle followed in 2022 ([Cablefax](https://www.cablefax.com/campaigns/spectrum-mobile)).

The largest deal in Charter's history arrived on August 20, 2026, when it closed its $34.5 billion merger with Cox Communications — announced in May 2025 and cleared by the FCC and the California Public Utilities Commission ([Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ca/)). Cox Enterprises received about 33.6 million common units (valued around $5 billion), $6 billion in convertible preferred units and roughly $4 billion in cash — approximately 26 percent of the combined company ([Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ca/)). The combined company will use the Cox Communications name while the consumer brand remains Spectrum, headquartered in Stamford, Connecticut alongside Cox's Atlanta campus ([Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ca/)). The same day, Charter completed its all-stock acquisition of Liberty Broadband, which held a 26 percent Charter stake — retiring about 38.6 million Liberty-held shares and issuing about 33.9 million new ones ([Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ca/)).

## FAQ

**When was Charter Communications founded?** Charter was formed in January 1993 in St. Louis by three former Cencom Cable Associates executives — CEO Howard Wood, COO Barry Babcock and CFO Jerry Kent — with backing from Crown Media, the Hallmark Cards subsidiary that took a 51 percent non-voting stake ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/charter-communications-inc)).

**What does Charter own or operate today?** Charter's consumer services run under the Spectrum brand: TV, Internet, Voice and Mobile, plus Spectrum Business and Spectrum Reach advertising ([Charter via PRNewswire](https://newswire.telecomramblings.com/2016/05/charter-communications-time-warner-cable-bright-house-networks-complete-transactions/); [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ca/)). After the August 2026 Cox merger it serves about 38 million customers in 45 states — the largest US cable operator ([Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ca/)).

**Did Charter file for bankruptcy?** Yes — in March 2009 Charter filed a pre-negotiated Chapter 11 case in New York carrying over $24 billion in debt ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2012/12/iin-re-charter-communicationsi-driving-the-equitable-mootness-wedge-deeper)). The plan took effect November 30, 2009; most old equity was wiped out while Paul Allen kept voting control for $375 million ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2012/12/iin-re-charter-communicationsi-driving-the-equitable-mootness-wedge-deeper)).

**Why was Charter nearly kicked out of New York?** In July 2018 the New York Public Service Commission revoked its 2016 approval of the Time Warner Cable purchase, saying Charter repeatedly missed merger-condition broadband deadlines, and ordered it to prepare to sell the former TWC system statewide ([Benton Institute](https://www.benton.org/headlines/new-york-orders-charter-out-state-says-it-must-sell-time-warner-cable-system)). The dispute settled July 11, 2019 ([NY Public Service Commission](https://documents.dps.ny.gov/public/Common/ViewDoc.aspx?DocRefId={15297CA6-63CB-4D11-9E98-D541B5892A89}&DocTitle=Press%20Release%20-%20PSC%20Approves%20Settlement%20with%20Charter%20Communications)).

**What was Charter's rank on the 2022 American Democracy Scorecard?** Charter was one of the 10 lowest scorers among the 100 corporations graded by Accountable.US — alongside AT&T (the lowest scorer), Verizon, Comcast, Boeing, ExxonMobil, Lockheed Martin, UnitedHealth Group, Energy Transfer and Pfizer — while telecommunications was the lowest-ranked industry ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression?partner=rss&%3Butm_source=rss&%3Butm_medium=feed&%3Butm_campaign=rss+fastcompany&%3Butm_content=rss)).
