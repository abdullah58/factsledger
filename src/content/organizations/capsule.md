## Overview

Capsule is the New York–based digital pharmacy that tried to rebuild the pharmacy experience around same-day delivery and direct pharmacist access — and became one of the flagship venture-backed bets of the pre-2022 digital-health boom before a data breach and the sector's funding freeze tested it. Founder and CEO Eric Kinariwala founded the company in 2015, after a frustrating personal experience at a chain pharmacy, with the stated aim of modernizing pharmacy "not just for consumers, who face the familiar frustrations of conventional retail pharmacies, but also for doctors, hospitals, insurers and manufacturers" ([Chain Drug Review](https://chaindrugreview.com/capsule-setting-stage-for-national-presence/)). This profile covers the pharmacy (capsule.com) — not the unrelated fashion-and-lifestyle trade show also named Capsule, founded in New York in 2007 by the BPMW partners and acquired by Reed Exhibitions in 2014 ([MR Magazine](https://mr-mag.com/capsule-acquired-by-reed-exhibitions/)).

## The model: a holistic, doctor-facing pharmacy

Capsule's pitch was that the pharmacy — "the largest retail category in America" and the highest-frequency healthcare interaction — had gone unchanged for a century ([Chain Drug Review](https://chaindrugreview.com/capsule-setting-stage-for-national-presence/)). Patients received prescriptions by text as soon as their doctor called them in (no account needed), then scheduled free same-day delivery within a two-hour window, while Capsule coordinated refills with the doctor and co-pays with the patient's insurer ([Chain Drug Review](https://chaindrugreview.com/capsule-setting-stage-for-national-presence/)). The platform also served prescribers directly: its software let doctors set up refill preferences and specify alternate drugs when a patient's insurance wouldn't cover the first choice, giving them visibility into patient behavior to improve fill rates ([MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2019/09/capsules-200m-financing-will-help-it-to-expand-to-other-major-cities/)). Beyond delivery, Capsule operated a physical storefront pharmacy in New York's Chelsea neighborhood, and pharmacists were reachable by text, call or chat ([MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2019/09/capsules-200m-financing-will-help-it-to-expand-to-other-major-cities/)).

## Venture capital and the unicorn round

Capsule's funding history is a clean record of the digital-health capital cycle. After a $20m Series A in 2017 led by Thrive Capital (with Virgin Group and Sound Ventures) and a $50m Series B in 2018 led by Thrive and Glade Brook Capital Partners, TCV led a $200m round in September 2019 that funded expansion beyond New York City, where the company then reported about 60,000 customers ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2021/05/04/investors-put-300m-in-capsule/); [MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2019/09/capsules-200m-financing-will-help-it-to-expand-to-other-major-cities/)). In April 2021, Durable Capital Partners led a $300m round joined by Baillie Gifford, T. Rowe Price and Whale Rock, valuing Capsule above $1 billion and bringing total funding past $570 million ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/tech/digital-pharmacy-startup-capsule-clinches-300m-to-invest-one-stop-shop-for-digital-healthcare)). By then the ambition had grown beyond delivery: the company planned a "one-stop-shop" digital-health marketplace — telemedicine and mental-health support curated inside a single app — built on APIs it had already developed for partners including the insurer Oscar and mental-health startup Ginger ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/tech/digital-pharmacy-startup-capsule-clinches-300m-to-invest-one-stop-shop-for-digital-healthcare); [MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2021/04/online-pharmacy-startup-capsule-passes-1b-valuation/)).

## Competition in a crowded market

Capsule entered the national stage as retail pharmacy was being disrupted from several directions. Chain Drug Review framed the 2018 $50m raise as arriving "on the heels of the Amazon/PillPack deal," and by 2019 Capsule was competing against Amazon-owned PillPack and NimbleRx ([Chain Drug Review](https://chaindrugreview.com/capsule-setting-stage-for-national-presence/); [MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2019/09/capsules-200m-financing-will-help-it-to-expand-to-other-major-cities/)). In November 2020 [Amazon](/organizations/amazon/) rolled out its own Amazon Pharmacy service with free two-day delivery for Prime members, while UnitedHealth acquired the startup DivvyDose and Walmart bought medication-management technology from CareZone — alongside independent players such as GoodRx, NowRx, Medly Pharmacy and Alto Pharmacy ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/tech/digital-pharmacy-startup-capsule-clinches-300m-to-invest-one-stop-shop-for-digital-healthcare)). A Penn State healthcare business professor told MedCity News that Capsule could expect to grow but was "unlikely to overtake larger players," predicting it would more likely become an acquisition target ([MedCity News](https://medcitynews.com/2019/09/capsules-200m-financing-will-help-it-to-expand-to-other-major-cities/)).

## The 2022 breach — and the 2022 downturn

In June 2022 Capsule reported a network-server breach affecting more than 27,000 people. The company said the attack was likely an instance of "password spraying" — attackers trying login credentials stolen from other companies' breaches — and a third-party cybersecurity firm concluded the breach was not a result of weaknesses in Capsule's security infrastructure, a company spokeswoman told Crain's New York Business ([KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/morning-breakout/hack-hits-27000-customers-of-pharmacy-app-capsule/)). A proposed class action followed, alleging the breach — dated in the complaint to April 5, 2022 — had exposed patient names, contact details, medical conditions, prescribed medications, order histories, insurance information and chat messages with Capsule agents, and charging that the company had failed to maintain adequate cybersecurity procedures; the suit also said Capsule's breach notice omitted how the attackers got in and how long they had access ([ClassAction.org](https://www.classaction.org/news/online-pharmacy-capsule-hit-with-class-action-over-2022-data-breach)). The breach arrived in the same season as the digital-health funding freeze: an industry roundup reported that Capsule released 13% of its more than 900 staff that summer, amid a wave of layoffs across the sector ([Telecare Aware](https://telecareaware.com/week-end-roundup-of-not-good-news-teladocs-q2-3b-net-loss-shares-down-24-humana-centene-molina-reorg-and-downscale-layoffs-at-included-health-capsule-noom-kry-livi-more/)).
