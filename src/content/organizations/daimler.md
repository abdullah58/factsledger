## Overview

Daimler was the name of the German automaker that is now Mercedes-Benz Group AG — the company behind Mercedes-Benz cars, founded in 1926 as Daimler-Benz from the merger of the firms of Karl Benz and Gottlieb Daimler, the two men who built the first gasoline automobiles. It was called Daimler-Benz until 1998, DaimlerChrysler until 2007, and Daimler until 1 February 2022, when it renamed itself Mercedes-Benz Group AG after spinning off its truck business as Daimler Truck ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Daimler-AG); [Mercedes-Benz Group](https://group.mercedes-benz.com/company/news/daimler-becomes-mercedes-benz-group.html)). In 2024 the renamed group earned €145.6 billion in revenue and sold 1,983,403 cars and 405,610 vans ([Automotive World](https://www.automotiveworld.com/news-releases/mastering-transformation-mercedes-benz-lays-out-its-roadmap-for-profitable-growth/)).

## The inventors: Benz and Daimler

No other carmaker can claim the invention itself. Daimler-Benz was formed in 1926 by the merger of two pioneering German automobile companies, one founded by Karl Benz and the other by Gottlieb Daimler, and both men claimed to have invented the gasoline-powered auto engine ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Daimler-AG)). Benz built his first automobile, powered by a one-cylinder gasoline engine, in 1885 and started selling cars in 1887; Daimler designed a series of gasoline-powered engines in 1883 and received a German patent on a three-wheeled gasoline-powered vehicle in 1885 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Daimler-AG)). In 1890 Daimler formed Daimler-Motoren-Gesellschaft, and in 1901 the company sold its first Mercedes — named after the daughter of investor Emil Jellinek, who feared the German-sounding Daimler name would not sell in France ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Daimler-AG)).

After the 1926 merger, the two firms' engineers worked together on the classic S series Mercedes, and Daimler-Benz became the first company to put diesel engines in passenger cars, with the diesel Mercedes-Benz available from 1936 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Daimler-AG)). During the Second World War its engines were used extensively in German tanks, bombers and fighter planes, and in 1944 the factories were almost destroyed by Allied bombing before being rebuilt ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Daimler-AG)). By the early 1980s, demand had made Daimler-Benz Germany's largest automaker by sales revenue; a mid-1980s diversification into high technology — buying AEG, Dornier and the aircraft-engine maker MTU — mostly lost money, and a mid-1990s restructuring sold off subsidiaries and cut the workforce drastically ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Daimler-AG)).

## DaimlerChrysler: the merger that failed

Daimler's most famous deal is also its biggest failure. In May 1998 Chrysler Corporation and Daimler-Benz announced plans to merge, with Daimler-Benz acquiring the smallest of America's Big Three automakers for more than $35 billion in a stock swap; shareholders approved in September, the merger completed on 12 November 1998, and DaimlerChrysler AG began trading later that month with dual headquarters, dual chairmen and English as its official language ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Daimler-AG)). The company bought 34 per cent of Mitsubishi Motors in 2000, briefly becoming the world's third-largest automaker, but the Japanese stake became a financial drain and was fully divested by 2005 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Daimler-AG)). The Chrysler division lost $1.5 billion in 2006, and in 2007 it was sold to the US private equity firm Cerberus Capital Management; DaimlerChrysler renamed itself Daimler AG that October ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Daimler-AG)).

Daimler's reach into its German rivals is part of the same story. Daimler-Benz took 88 per cent of Auto Union in April 1958 and owned it until Volkswagenwerk AG took over in 1965 — the company that became [Audi](/organizations/audi/) ([Audi AG](https://www.audi.com/en/company-history-17227)). A year later, in late 1959, Daimler-Benz tried to take over [BMW](/organizations/bmw/) itself; small shareholders and the workforce rejected the offer on 9 December 1959, and Herbert Quandt rescued BMW instead ([BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/company/history.html)). After Chrysler, Daimler rebuilt its luxury image with a Maybach revival, a wider AMG performance range, the EQ electric line in 2016 and a 2017 autonomous-driving partnership with Bosch ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Daimler-AG)).

## The split and the rename

The Daimler name ended with a deliberate break-up. In October 2021 shareholders voted to spin the truck and bus division into a separate company, and in December 2021 Daimler Truck Holding began trading on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Daimler-AG)). On 1 February 2022 Daimler AG became Mercedes-Benz Group AG, its stock symbol changing from DAI to MBG, and Daimler Mobility AG became Mercedes-Benz Mobility AG ([Mercedes-Benz Group](https://group.mercedes-benz.com/company/news/daimler-becomes-mercedes-benz-group.html)). The renamed group concentrates on its passenger-car brands — Mercedes-Benz, Mercedes-AMG and Mercedes-Maybach — plus vans, and kept a 35 per cent minority stake in Daimler Truck Holding AG at the time of the split ([Mercedes-Benz Group](https://group.mercedes-benz.com/company/news/daimler-becomes-mercedes-benz-group.html)). Chairman Ola Källenius said the renaming "underlines our renewed strategic focus" on "building the most desirable cars in the world" and taking the lead in electric mobility and vehicle software ([Mercedes-Benz Group](https://group.mercedes-benz.com/company/news/daimler-becomes-mercedes-benz-group.html)).

## Mercedes-Benz Group today

The renamed company remains one of the world's largest luxury carmakers, but 2024 was a difficult year. Group revenue was €145,594 million, down 4.5 per cent; EBIT fell 30.8 per cent to €13,599 million and net profit fell 28.4 per cent to €10,409 million, with free cash flow from the industrial business at €9,152 million ([Automotive World](https://www.automotiveworld.com/news-releases/mastering-transformation-mercedes-benz-lays-out-its-roadmap-for-profitable-growth/)). Mercedes-Benz Cars sold 1,983,403 vehicles, including 185,059 fully electric cars — down 23.1 per cent — on revenue of €107,761 million, with its adjusted return on sales falling to 8.1 per cent on lower volumes in China, negative net pricing and an unfavourable model mix ([Automotive World](https://www.automotiveworld.com/news-releases/mastering-transformation-mercedes-benz-lays-out-its-roadmap-for-profitable-growth/)).

## The voice in the car: Cerence and MBUX

Mercedes-Benz runs one of the industry's most ambitious voice systems, and [Cerence](/organizations/cerence/) has powered it for years. In December 2020 Mercedes-Benz chose Cerence for the second generation of MBUX, its Mercedes-Benz User Experience assistant, woken with "Hey Mercedes" ([Voicebot.ai](https://voicebot.ai/2020/12/18/mercedes-benz-will-keep-using-cerence-to-run-revamped-mbux-virtual-assistant/)). The system uses Cerence voice biometrics to identify the driver and apply their seat position, temperature and media preferences, recognises voices from every seat for multi-seat phone calls, understands implicit commands such as "I'm cold", and extends beyond the car to smart-home control and an interactive "Explore me" manual ([Voicebot.ai](https://voicebot.ai/2020/12/18/mercedes-benz-will-keep-using-cerence-to-run-revamped-mbux-virtual-assistant/)). The partnership continued into the generative-AI era: in June 2025 Cerence's technology powered the fourth generation of MBUX in the all-electric Mercedes-Benz CLA — the first launch of the MB.OS operating system — with speech recognition and natural-language understanding across 25 languages and a "living" avatar in the shape of the Mercedes-Benz star ([Cerence Inc.](https://investors.cerence.com/_assets/_f329982e399c067154903733490b1171/cerence/news/2025-06-26_Cerence_AI_Powers_In_Car_Experience_in_Premier_92.pdf)).

## Two diesel cases, two different endings

Daimler's diesel history produced two opposite outcomes. In the United States, Daimler agreed in August 2020 to pay over $2.2 billion to settle diesel-emissions claims covering about 250,000 diesel cars and vans — about $1.5 billion to US authorities including the Justice Department, the Environmental Protection Agency and California regulators, and about $700 million for a consumer class action in New Jersey ([Associated Press](https://www.manufacturing.net/energy/news/21160293/daimler-reaches-deals-to-settle-us-diesel-emissions-claims)). Daimler denied the allegations and admitted no liability, but the Justice Department had asked it in April 2016 to investigate its own exhaust-emissions certification process, shortly after Volkswagen's cheating emerged ([Associated Press](https://www.manufacturing.net/energy/news/21160293/daimler-reaches-deals-to-settle-us-diesel-emissions-claims)).

In Europe, the same company walked away paying nothing. On 8 July 2021 the European Commission found that Daimler, BMW and the Volkswagen group (Volkswagen, Audi and Porsche) had breached EU antitrust rules by colluding on nitrogen-oxide cleaning technology for diesel cars, fining them €875,189,000 in total ([European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/it/ip_21_3581/IP_21_3581_EN.pdf)). Daimler paid €0: as the whistleblower that revealed the cartel, it received full immunity and avoided a fine of about €727 million, while BMW paid €372,827,000 and the Volkswagen group €502,362,000 ([European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/it/ip_21_3581/IP_21_3581_EN.pdf)). The Commission stressed that the cartel case was separate from defeat-device investigations, with no indication the parties coordinated the use of illegal defeat devices ([European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/it/ip_21_3581/IP_21_3581_EN.pdf)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Daimler still a company?**
Not under that name. Daimler AG renamed itself Mercedes-Benz Group AG on 1 February 2022, and its truck business became the separate, listed Daimler Truck Holding AG ([Mercedes-Benz Group](https://group.mercedes-benz.com/company/news/daimler-becomes-mercedes-benz-group.html)).

**Who invented the automobile?**
Karl Benz built his first gasoline-powered automobile in 1885, while Gottlieb Daimler designed gasoline engines from 1883 and patented a three-wheeled gasoline vehicle in 1885; their companies merged as Daimler-Benz in 1926 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Daimler-AG)).

**What happened to DaimlerChrysler?**
Daimler-Benz bought Chrysler for more than $35 billion in 1998, but the merger never delivered: Chrysler lost $1.5 billion in 2006 and was sold to Cerberus Capital Management in 2007, after which the company renamed itself Daimler AG ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Daimler-AG)).

**Does Mercedes-Benz use Cerence technology?**
Yes. Cerence powers the MBUX voice assistant — the second generation from 2020 and the fourth generation in the electric CLA from 2025 — with voice biometrics, multi-seat recognition and, in the newest version, a star-shaped avatar ([Voicebot.ai](https://voicebot.ai/2020/12/18/mercedes-benz-will-keep-using-cerence-to-run-revamped-mbux-virtual-assistant/); [Cerence Inc.](https://investors.cerence.com/_assets/_f329982e399c067154903733490b1171/cerence/news/2025-06-26_Cerence_AI_Powers_In_Car_Experience_in_Premier_92.pdf)).

**When did Mercedes-Benz get all-wheel drive?**
Mercedes-Benz introduced 4Matic in 1987, seven years after Audi's quattro debuted and two years after BMW offered all-wheel drive on the 325i ([Autoblog](https://www.autoblog.com/news/audi-quattro-40-years-anniversary-history)).

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not pretend Daimler AG still exists: the company renamed itself Mercedes-Benz Group AG on 1 February 2022, and this profile is about the Daimler-named company and its successor, not a current "Daimler" carmaker.
- It does not state an exact merger day for Daimler-Benz in 1926; only the year is asserted.
- It states no group headcount: the ~179,000 figure for 2024 in tertiary tables was not verified from a source read in full.
- It does not present the $2.2 billion US diesel settlement as an admission of guilt: Daimler denied the allegations and admitted no liability.
- It makes no claim about Cerence's spin-off date or total vehicles shipped — those facts were not needed for this profile and are not asserted here.
- The Daimler Truck stake is stated as 35% at the February 2022 rename; Britannica's November 2026 update says 30%, and that difference is flagged in the open items rather than silently resolved.
