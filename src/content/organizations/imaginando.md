## Overview

Imaginando is a music-software studio in Braga, Portugal, founded in 2014 by solo developer [Nuno Santos](/people/nuno-santos/) as, in his own telling, "a manifestation of the will to create" — and one of the few music-tech companies of its size whose whole arc is publicly traceable from a single side project. Santos spent the late 2000s in two parallel lives: working on a team that built touch technology for public installations in places like museums while gigging as a DJ on Native Instruments Traktor and producing with Ableton Live ([Music Connection](https://www.musicconnection.com/up-close-imaginando/)).

The company itself grew out of LIVKONTROL, an Ableton Live controller for mobile devices Santos built and earned money from; he used those earnings to self-fund Imaginando's launch rather than taking outside investment ([Music Connection](https://www.musicconnection.com/up-close-imaginando/)). Twelve years on, the company still describes itself as a small Braga team that "doesn't ship until it sounds right," and claims more than 30,000 active users across 153 countries with an average 4.32-star rating — a self-reported figure that reflects the brand's appeal but has not been independently audited ([Imaginando](https://www.imaginando.pt/)).

## The instruments era: controllers, synths and effects

Imaginando's first official product was TKFX, a full-featured Traktor controller for iOS and Android with decks, looper, mixer, a jog dial, and X/Y pads per effect unit ([Music Connection](https://www.musicconnection.com/up-close-imaginando/)). In 2015 it released LK, which converted the LIVKONTROL side project into a flexible remote application offering tight integration with Ableton Live projects as well as generic MIDI control and sequencing ([Music Connection](https://www.musicconnection.com/up-close-imaginando/)).

The company then moved from control surfaces into sound itself. In 2016 it released DRC, a polyphonic virtual-analog subtractive synthesizer that recreates analog character through circuit modeling across Windows, Mac, iOS and Android, designed to capture what Santos calls "all the great features of '80s synths" ([Music Connection](https://www.musicconnection.com/up-close-imaginando/)). FRMS followed in 2020 as the company's second software synth, combining granular, subtractive and FM synthesis in a flexible four-layer system, with a 100-plus sample library, 150 preset patches, live-audio granulation, and Imaginando's Cloud Sync technology built into every version ([Sonicstate](http://sonicstate.com/news/2020/03/24/multi-platform-granular-synthesizer/)).

FRMS also revealed Imaginando's unusual business model for an independent developer: alongside a one-time EUR 119 desktop license, it offered a rent-to-own plan at EUR 11.90 a month for twelve months, with mobile versions at EUR 14.99 and a 50 percent launch discount ([Sonicstate](http://sonicstate.com/news/2020/03/24/multi-platform-granular-synthesizer/)). K7D, the company's vintage tape delay plugin emulating classic delay hardware with analog-modeled noise, tape distortion and mechanical vibration, received a 1.4.0 update adding VST3 compatibility; Nuno Santos framed the philosophy as "blend[ing] the nostalgic appeal of analog sound with the precision and flexibility of digital innovation" ([Rekkerd](https://rekkerd.org/imaginando-enhances-k7d-tape-delay-plugin-with-vst3-support/)).

## The audio-visual thread and BAM

In 2021 Imaginando released VS (Visual Synthesizer), a tool that turns audio and MIDI into real-time reactive visuals through a multi-layered shader system — the company's first product centered on visual art rather than sound alone ([Music Connection](https://www.musicconnection.com/up-close-imaginando/)). It became the most successful product the company had released to that point, and development continued: the March 2024 1.5.0 update added the "Textural Memories" preset pack from German new-media artist Perplex On, with 31 materials and 84 presets, priced at EUR 9.99, alongside a 50 percent discount on VS itself ([MixOnline](https://www.mixonline.com/the-wire/imaginando-and-perplex-on-unveil-new-textural-memories-preset-pack-in-vs-visual-synthesizer-1-5-0-update)). The company has since shipped VS 2, with VS 2.3.0 bringing the app to Android, plus expansion packs such as Minimal and Granular ([Imaginando](https://www.imaginando.pt/)).

BAM, released in November 2023, is in many ways the sum of these experiences: a complete music-production studio, beat maker and groovebox that gives users every tool they need to turn ideas into tracks quickly ([Music Connection](https://www.musicconnection.com/up-close-imaginando/)). Independent reviewers described it as a 16-track DAW built around a non-linear arranger driven by per-track step sequencers, with no conventional linear tracks — a pattern-focused design similar in concept to Ableton Live's Session View, priced at EUR 149 ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/reviews/digital-audio-workstations/imaginando-bam-review/)). Each track chains modules (trigger, modulators, a sound-generation engine, two effect slots) into an output mixer with two global send effects, and the sound engines range from a generic drum synth and TR-808-inspired units through a sampler and two-oscillator subtractive synth to the Hofmann, a recreation of the classic MC-303 groovebox ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/reviews/digital-audio-workstations/imaginando-bam-review/)). The same review praised the polyrhythmic potential — independent time signatures and groove per pattern, probabilistic triggering — while noting that the iPadOS version, where AUv3 hosting is supported, was clearly the best experience, and that the desktop build lacked expected interactions like tooltips and drag-to-edit values, with no linear recording at all ([MusicTech](https://musictech.com/reviews/digital-audio-workstations/imaginando-bam-review/)). An updated BAM 1.1.0 arrived in April 2024 with audio-loop support and a house-themed sound bank ([Music Connection](https://www.musicconnection.com/up-close-imaginando/)).

The company kept expanding after BAM. In October 2024 it released TV3, an acid bass synth and one-click pattern generator for Mac and Windows with drag-and-drop MIDI export, free until 1 December 2024 at a regular price of EUR 19 ([Sonicstate](http://sonicstate.com/news/2024/10/22/free-acid-bass-synth/)). In April 2025 it released GRFX, a granular effects plugin positioned as a sequel to FRMS, built around a "Harmonic Triangle" pitch controller, four drag-and-drop LFOs and a per-grain routing system, priced at USD 39 (USD 29 introductory) and reviewed as a hands-on budget alternative to pricier granular effects like Output Portal ([We Rave You](https://weraveyou.com/2025/04/imaginando-grfx-might-be-the-most-fun-granular-plugin-youll-use-this-year/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim any verified sales or download figures: the 30,000-plus user count, 153-country reach and 4.32-star average are the company's own website statements, presented as such. It does not assert the exact release months for products whose sources only give years. The rent-to-own pricing described is from FRMS's 2020 launch and may not reflect current offerings. Imaginando's new-media installations — including a 2015 Noite Branca street installation in Braga and a 2017 GNRation digital music-box project — are documented in tertiary references but were not independently verified in a citable source for this rewrite and are not asserted as fact here.
