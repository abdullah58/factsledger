## Overview

The Estonian Business Register — the e-Business Register, or Äriregister — is the official national register of legal entities in [Estonia](/places/estonia/), covering every company, self-employed person, branch of a foreign company, non-profit association and foundation registered in the country ([European e-Justice Portal](https://e-justice.europa.eu/106/EN/business_registers_in_eu_countries?ESTONIA&member=1)). The commercial register and the register of non-profit associations and foundations are maintained by the registration department of Tartu County Court, deliberately kept at the court to ensure the independent status and legal expertise of the registrar, while the central database is maintained by the Centre of Registers and Information Systems (RIK), a state agency under the Ministry of Justice ([European e-Justice Portal](https://e-justice.europa.eu/106/EN/business_registers_in_eu_countries?ESTONIA&member=1)). Its online portal, ariregister.rik.ee, is developed and managed by RIK ([Centre of Registers and Information Systems](https://www.rik.ee/en/e-business-register/e-business-register-portal)).

The register is not a directory. Its entries have strong legal force and exist to ensure legal certainty: an entry in the commercial register is held as correct with regard to a third party, unless the third party knew or should have known that the entry was not correct ([European e-Justice Portal](https://e-justice.europa.eu/106/EN/business_registers_in_eu_countries?ESTONIA&member=1)). Some legal circumstances are only valid if they are entered in the register — for instance, limits on a management board member's signatory authority apply to third parties only if entered — and certain circumstances take effect from the moment of entry, including a share-capital increase, the establishment of a legal entity, an amendment of its articles, and a merger, division or transformation ([European e-Justice Portal](https://e-justice.europa.eu/106/EN/business_registers_in_eu_countries?ESTONIA&member=1)). The register contains information dating back to 1 September 1995, and all data is regularly updated ([European e-Justice Portal](https://e-justice.europa.eu/106/EN/business_registers_in_eu_countries?ESTONIA&member=1)).

## What the register holds

The commercial register covers self-employed persons, companies — public and private limited companies, general and limited partnerships, commercial associations, European Companies, European Cooperative Societies and European groupings of territorial cooperation — and branches of foreign companies located in Estonia ([European e-Justice Portal](https://e-justice.europa.eu/106/EN/business_registers_in_eu_countries?ESTONIA&member=1)). The register of non-profit associations and foundations covers non-profit associations and foundations, with non-profit associations deemed to include political parties, trade unions, churches, congregations, associations of congregations and monasteries, plus the registers of housing associations and of land improvement associations ([European e-Justice Portal](https://e-justice.europa.eu/106/EN/business_registers_in_eu_countries?ESTONIA&member=1)).

For each self-employed person, legal entity and branch, the register opens three things: a registry card, a business file (or public file, for non-profits, foundations and housing associations), and a registry file ([European e-Justice Portal](https://e-justice.europa.eu/106/EN/business_registers_in_eu_countries?ESTONIA&member=1)). The registry card records the business name and registry code, the registered office and address and email, the persons with powers of representation — board members, partners, liquidators, trustees in bankruptcy — with their names and personal identification numbers, the contact person, the procurator, the legal form, the date the articles of association were approved, the share capital, the financial-year dates, bankruptcy information, and notes on merger, division, transformation, dissolution and de-registration ([European e-Justice Portal](https://e-justice.europa.eu/106/EN/business_registers_in_eu_countries?ESTONIA&member=1)). Register matters are reviewed as non-contentious civil cases using written proceedings, and entries are made on the basis of an application for entry or a court decision; applications must bear a digital signature or be in notarial form ([European e-Justice Portal](https://e-justice.europa.eu/106/EN/business_registers_in_eu_countries?ESTONIA&member=1)).

The register's own public example on this site is the [Banned Books Museum](/organizations/banned-books-museum/): Banned Books MTÜ is a non-profit association registered in Estonia on 28 August 2020, with registry code 80584386 and founder [Joseph Maximillian Dunnigan](/people/joseph-maximillian-dunnigan/) and Urve Leemets on its board, its record held at ariregister.rik.ee. That record is exactly the kind of public entry the register exists to provide — a verifiable, court-maintained statement of who runs an organisation and when it was formed.

## Public access and the online portal

Entries in the commercial register and the register of non-profit associations and foundations are public: everyone has the right to examine registry card data and the documents in business or public files and to obtain copies, while registry files may be examined by competent government bodies, courts during proceedings, and other persons with a legitimate interest ([European e-Justice Portal](https://e-justice.europa.eu/106/EN/business_registers_in_eu_countries?ESTONIA&member=1)). Through the e-Business Register, users can examine registry card data, general data and tax-arrears data free of charge, search by name, registry code, registered office or field of activities, view annual reports and articles of association for a fee, monitor procedural information and changes to entries in real time, check trade bans imposed on Estonian persons or entities free of charge, view the list of members of political parties free of charge, and view the links between various companies and persons ([European e-Justice Portal](https://e-justice.europa.eu/106/EN/business_registers_in_eu_countries?ESTONIA&member=1)). Since 1 October 2022, data in the e-Business Register has been accessible to everyone free of charge ([Estonian Business Register](https://ariregister.rik.ee/eng/statistics)).

The portal also lets users submit documents to the registration department themselves — applications to register a new company, amend its registry data, liquidate it and delete it from the register — and to prepare and submit annual reports ([European e-Justice Portal](https://e-justice.europa.eu/106/EN/business_registers_in_eu_countries?ESTONIA&member=1)). Estonian, Finnish, Latvian and Belgian citizens and e-residents of Estonia can log in using ID cards, and Estonian and Lithuanian citizens can log in using Mobile-ID ([European e-Justice Portal](https://e-justice.europa.eu/106/EN/business_registers_in_eu_countries?ESTONIA&member=1)). A business name can be booked on the portal before establishing an association, for up to six months, with one reservation per person at a time ([Centre of Registers and Information Systems](https://www.rik.ee/en/e-business-register/e-business-register-portal)). The portal also carries the commercial pledge register, beneficial-owner data, tax information, business and entrepreneurship bans, and lists of members of political parties and creative associations ([Centre of Registers and Information Systems](https://www.rik.ee/en/e-business-register/e-business-register-portal)).

## History: from paper court register to a 15-minute incorporation

Online filing arrived early. Since February 2007, entrepreneurs have been able to submit data to the Commercial Register through the Company Registration Portal, with documents processed within the next working day at the earliest, identified by the Estonian eID card and digital signature ([European Commission](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Digital_Government_Factsheets_Estonia_2019.pdf)). RIK, the agency behind the portal, was established in 1994 as Estonia's first state IT development centre, and under the Ministry of Justice it develops and administers not only the e-Business Registry but also the e-Notary system, the e-Land Registry, the court information system, the Probation Supervision Registry, the Prisoners Registry, the Criminal Records Database, the e-File and the electronic State Gazette ([European Commission](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Digital_Government_Factsheets_Estonia_2019.pdf); [Estonian Business Register](https://ariregister.rik.ee/eng/statistics)).

The current portal is a consolidation: on 30 August 2021 the new e-Business Register portal at ariregister.rik.ee was launched, bringing all commercial register services into one environment — previously, the Company Registration portal had to be used to submit petitions and annual reports while a separate e-Business Register information system was used to check legal entities ([City of Tallinn](https://www.tallinn.ee/en/news/new-e-business-register-available-use)). The launch updated the sections for founding a company, amending data and searching for legal entities, and added a wholly new tool for comparing legal forms to help an entrepreneur choose the most suitable type ([City of Tallinn](https://www.tallinn.ee/en/news/new-e-business-register-available-use)).

The stat that anchors the speed story comes from the e-Residency programme: e-residents can register a company 100 percent digitally in two to four hours on average with their digital ID — against 24 hours in the UK, five days in Delaware, three to seven days in Ireland, ten days in France, fifteen days in Spain and twenty days in Germany ([Estonian e-Residency programme](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/blog/posts/4-key-ways-e-residency-makes-business-easy-at-every-stage/)). According to the programme, Estonia holds the world record for fastest company incorporation: in 2022, e-resident entrepreneur Dominik Panosch registered a business in Estonia in just 15 minutes 33 seconds, live onstage at London Tech Week ([Estonian e-Residency programme](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/blog/posts/4-key-ways-e-residency-makes-business-easy-at-every-stage/)). The implication is that the register's value is not only legal certainty but speed — a court-maintained register that a founder anywhere in the world can enter in an afternoon.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim an exact current total of registered entities: RIK says only "hundreds of thousands", and the conflicting precise counts from commercial aggregators were not verified from a tier1 or tier2 source. It does not claim a registration fee, since the widely quoted €265 figure for online OÜ registration appears only in commercial guides and was not verified. It does not say the register was founded in 1994 — that year is RIK's founding; the register's data run begins on 1 September 1995. It does not present the 15 minutes 33 seconds incorporation as an independently adjudicated world record; it is attributed to the e-Residency programme's own account. It does not describe the European Business Register beyond noting it as a separate cross-border search system covering 17 countries' registers, and it does not claim anything about any individual company held in the register beyond the Banned Books MTÜ record already published on this site.
