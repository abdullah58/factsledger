## Overview

Atalaya Capital Management is a New York alternative-credit manager founded in 2006 by Ivan Zinn that managed over $10 billion as of 30 June 2024 ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/blue-owl-acquires-atalaya-for-abf-expansion-in-450m-deal)). It led [Alt](/organizations/alt/)'s $200 million debt financing in 2022, and Blue Owl acquired its business for $450 million in 2024 ([ABL Advisor](https://www.abladvisor.com/articles/34590/atalaya-capital-management-leads-200mm-in-new-debt-funding-for-alt)).This profile covers Atalaya as an independent alternative-credit firm — not the later Blue Owl alternative-credit business it became part of — and follows it from its 2006 founding through the Alt debt deal to its acquisition.

## An asset-based lender, not a venture firm

Atalaya's business was private credit, not venture equity, and that distinction explains the Alt deal. PitchBook describes Atalaya as focusing primarily on asset-based credit investments across consumer and commercial finance, corporate and real estate assets, and calls it an early pioneer in private asset-based finance ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/blue-owl-acquires-atalaya-for-abf-expansion-in-450m-deal)). At the time of the Blue Owl agreement it was New York-based, with approximately 115 employees including over 50 investment professionals, and managed over $10 billion of assets under management as of 30 June 2024 ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/blue-owl-acquires-atalaya-for-abf-expansion-in-450m-deal)). Two years earlier, in the Alt announcement, the firm had been described as an approximately $8.5 billion privately held, SEC-registered alternative investment advisory firm ([ABL Advisor](https://www.abladvisor.com/articles/34590/atalaya-capital-management-leads-200mm-in-new-debt-funding-for-alt)). Both figures are stated here with their dates rather than blended, because they come from different moments and the earlier one is company-stated in its announcement.

The firm's founder stayed at its centre throughout. Ivan Zinn founded Atalaya in 2006 and served as its founding partner and chief investment officer, and under the Blue Owl deal he was to join Blue Owl as head of alternative credit, reporting to Craig Packer, Blue Owl's head of credit and co-president ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/blue-owl-acquires-atalaya-for-abf-expansion-in-450m-deal)). That continuity — founder becoming head of the acquirer's alternative-credit arm, and most of Atalaya's 115 employees expected to join Blue Owl and continue managing existing Atalaya funds — is how PitchBook framed the acquisition: an adjacent, scaled capability being absorbed, not a team being wound down ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/blue-owl-acquires-atalaya-for-abf-expansion-in-450m-deal)).

## The Alt debt deal and ALT Advance

On 21 July 2022, Alt announced $200 million in new debt financing led by Atalaya, coming on the heels of ALT Advance, a lending product that lets investors borrow cash against their vaulted assets without having to sell them ([ABL Advisor](https://www.abladvisor.com/articles/34590/atalaya-capital-management-leads-200mm-in-new-debt-funding-for-alt)). ALT Advance provides liquidity against graded collectibles such as trading cards, watches and sneakers, giving those assets a utility similar to that afforded to traditional assets ([ABL Advisor](https://www.abladvisor.com/articles/34590/atalaya-capital-management-leads-200mm-in-new-debt-funding-for-alt)). FinTech Global's independent account of the same deal adds the mechanics: borrowers send graded collectibles to Alt's secure vaulting facility, review terms offered in real time against the portfolio, and then repay on a flexible schedule with the option of early payments ([FinTech Global](https://fintech.global/2022/07/22/investing-platform-alt-raises-200m-in-debt/)).

The deal mattered to both sides for the same reason: it treated a graded collectible as lendable collateral. Alt's head of lending, Mark Ricciardi, said Alt had transformed its data and vaulting infrastructure into a first-of-its-kind productised lending solution for graded collectibles, giving borrowers near-immediate liquidity in a flexible fashion at affordable rates, with an eventual extension into areas such as venture-backed equity interests ([ABL Advisor](https://www.abladvisor.com/articles/34590/atalaya-capital-management-leads-200mm-in-new-debt-funding-for-alt)). Atalaya principal Brian Moore said Alt's team had taken a novel approach to extending the reach of financial services, and that Atalaya's financial-assets practice was well aligned with that goal ([ABL Advisor](https://www.abladvisor.com/articles/34590/atalaya-capital-management-leads-200mm-in-new-debt-funding-for-alt)). For Atalaya, in other words, Alt was not a venture bet on a marketplace; it was an asset-based lending opportunity in which the vaulted, graded, priced asset was the collateral.

## The Blue Owl acquisition

In July 2024 Blue Owl Capital announced it had entered into a definitive agreement to acquire Atalaya's business for $450 million, a price comprising $350 million of Blue Owl equity and $100 million of cash, with up to a further $350 million of equity earnout consideration available ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/blue-owl-acquires-atalaya-for-abf-expansion-in-450m-deal)). Blue Owl's stated rationale, as reported by PitchBook, was expansion into private asset-based finance at a moment when private-credit providers were extending into that area, and its co-chief executives described Atalaya as an early pioneer whose capabilities complemented Blue Owl's leading position in direct lending ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/blue-owl-acquires-atalaya-for-abf-expansion-in-450m-deal)). The deal was expected to close in the second half of 2024, and ABF Journal later recorded that Blue Owl completed its acquisition of Atalaya on 30 September 2024 ([ABF Journal](https://www.abfjournal.com/atalaya-structures-2b-agreement-between-blue-owl-and-upstart/)). That completion date is where this profile's account of Atalaya as an independent firm ends, because no later independent Atalaya activity was verified from the sources read.

## What this profile does not claim

- No current assets under management are claimed: the $8.5 billion figure is from July 2022 and the over-$10 billion figure is from 30 June 2024, and neither is presented as today's figure.
- The acquirer's claim that Atalaya had deployed over $17 billion across more than 600 investments was not used, because it appears in the acquirer's own release and no independent source read confirms it.
- Whether the Atalaya name, funds, or team continued unchanged after 30 September 2024 is not stated, beyond PitchBook's expectation that most employees would join Blue Owl and continue managing existing funds.
- The pricing, covenants, and repayment terms of the Alt debt financing were not disclosed in the sources read, so only its $200 million size and its ALT Advance purpose are stated.
- No Atalaya activity after September 2024 is claimed.
