## Overview

AdExchanger is the trade publication that has covered programmatic advertising and data-driven marketing technology since 2008, when it launched with a focus on exactly that emerging ecosystem ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/admonsters-joins-adexchanger-in-publisher-community-power-play/)). It established itself as a leading voice covering the programmatic ecosystem through editorial coverage, research, and industry events, and it has spent the years since consolidating that position — acquired by Access Intelligence in 2017, organized under the Chief Marketer Network in 2024, and merged with its sister publication AdMonsters in 2025–2026 ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/admonsters-joins-adexchanger-in-publisher-community-power-play/)). By its own account, it now attracts more than 290,000 monthly unique visitors, with newsletters and articles landing in more than 55,000 subscribers' inboxes daily, and produces the Programmatic AI Las Vegas and Programmatic I/O New York conferences ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/about/)).

## A publication built on the programmatic beat

AdExchanger's value as a source is visible in its own archive, which is how it enters this site's AdRoll story. In July 2012 it interviewed [AdRoll](/organizations/adroll/) founders Aaron Bell and Adam Berke at the moment the company pivoted from an ordinary ad network to a retargeting provider, recording the $15 million raise that brought AdRoll's total investment to $19 million, the pivot to retargeting via ad exchanges in 2008–09, its position as one of the first buyers on Google's AdEx, and its scale at the time — 5,000 customers, 500 a month, 85 San Francisco employees, profitable since 2011 with north of 400% growth ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/online-advertising/adroll-retargeting-firm/)). Six years later it covered the other end of the same company's arc: the February 2018 split into [RollWorks](/organizations/rollworks/), focused solely on business-to-business marketing, and the legacy AdRoll business serving small to mid-size eCommerce companies, each with its own profit-and-loss statement under the newly formed AdRoll Group parent ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/ad-exchange-news/adroll-rebrands-splits-b2b-b2c-divisions/)).

That kind of beat continuity — the same publication interviewing the founders in 2012 and explaining their reorganization in 2018 — is what made AdExchanger a reference point for the industry it covered, rather than just a commentator on it. Its editorial director, Sarah Sluis, oversees its news and feature coverage and event content, and has written extensively about publishers, sell-side technology, and Google, with a focus on explaining the business implications of technically complex topics ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/about/)).

## Ownership, network, and the AdMonsters merger

AdExchanger's corporate history is a small map of trade-media consolidation. Access Intelligence acquired it in 2017, expanding its reach in market-leading events and proprietary information, and in 2024 organized its media and marketing brands under the Chief Marketer Network platform, which describes itself as a marketing intelligence hub connecting nine specialized media brands ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/admonsters-joins-adexchanger-in-publisher-community-power-play/)). Access Intelligence itself is Rockville, Maryland-based, and its CEO since 2023, Heather Farley, had spent more than 30 years with the company, most recently as chief operating officer where she led the integration of acquisitions including TradeFair Group, Red7 Media, and AdExchanger ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/admonsters-joins-adexchanger-in-publisher-community-power-play/)).

The AdMonsters chapter closed the loop. The two brands had already merged editorial operations in March 2025, aligning content teams to provide comprehensive coverage of digital media and advertising technology, and on January 14, 2026 the Chief Marketer Network announced that AdMonsters would officially join AdExchanger, unifying content onto a single website at AdExchanger.com while maintaining AdMonsters' practitioner-focused identity as what executives described as a foundational community within the larger platform ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/admonsters-joins-adexchanger-in-publisher-community-power-play/)). The stated rationale was the increasingly intertwined relationship between the publisher and ad-tech communities: publishers had navigated between both brands for different parts of the same conversation, and the integration followed how the community had evolved on its own ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/admonsters-joins-adexchanger-in-publisher-community-power-play/)).
