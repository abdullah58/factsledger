## Overview

A7A5 is a ruble-backed token issued by Kyrgyzstan-registered Old Vector LLC for the Russia-linked A7 Network. The [U.S. Treasury](/organizations/us-department-of-the-treasury/) says the network created A7A5 so sanctioned members could transact internationally and evade sanctions ([U.S. Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0644)). OFAC designated the issuer on August 14, 2025, and in October 2026 Treasury described the token as blocked property ([U.S. Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0225); [U.S. Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0644)). It also surfaced in a September 2025 [Accountable.US](/organizations/accountable-us/) report alleging a [World Liberty Financial](/organizations/world-liberty-financial/) token buyer had used it — unverified ([Accountable.US](https://accountable.us/trump-crypto-venture-wlfl-sold-tokens-to-entities-tied-to-north-korea-iran-a-notorious-money-laundering-platform/)).

This profile is about the A7A5 token itself, not A7 LLC, the Russia-based cross-border settlement company whose network the token served ([U.S. Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0225)). The token trades on the Tron and Ethereum blockchains, though each token was advertised as backed by ruble deposits held at Promsvyazbank (PSB), the Russian bank already under OFAC sanctions ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/a7a5-grinex-russian-crypto-economy-ofac-sanctions-august-2025/); [FinCEN](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-10/FinCEN-Alert-A7-Network.pdf)). A7 and its subsidiaries A71 and A7 Agent are owned by sanctioned Moldovan oligarch Ilan Shor and PSB ([U.S. Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0225)).

## How sanctions caught up with the token

The enforcement record unfolded in four stages. In the August 14, 2025 action, OFAC re-designated the cryptocurrency exchange Garantex, designated its successor Grinex, three Garantex executives — Sergey Mendeleev, Aleksandr Mira Serda and Pavel Karavatsky — and the A7 entities plus Old Vector LLC, all under Executive Order 13694 as amended for cyber-enabled sanctions evasion ([U.S. Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0225)). The press release discusses the A7A5 token at length — it says Garantex allowed customers who lost funds in the March 2025 takedown to regain access using A7A5 — but the designations named the issuer and network entities, not the token itself ([U.S. Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0225)).

Six days later, on August 20, the United Kingdom's Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation sanctioned Grinex LLC, Old Vector LLC, CJSC Tengricoin (operator of the Meer exchange) and others in the Kyrgyz crypto network ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/a7a5-grinex-russian-crypto-economy-ofac-sanctions-august-2025/)). Then, on October 23, 2025, the European Commission adopted its 19th sanctions package against Russia, which included a transaction ban on the A7A5 stablecoin itself alongside sanctions on Old Vector and Grinex ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/a7a5-grinex-russian-crypto-economy-ofac-sanctions-august-2025/)). Finally, on October 1, 2026, as part of Operation Economic Outcast, OFAC designated the A7 Network as a significant transnational criminal organization and Treasury stated flatly: "The A7A5 token is a blocked, ruble-backed token issued by Old Vector LLC," created to evade sanctions while generating revenue for sanctioned infrastructure providers ([U.S. Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0644)). FinCEN added that the token "is to be considered blocked property due to the interest of Old Vector LLC" ([FinCEN](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-10/FinCEN-Alert-A7-Network.pdf)).

## How A7A5 moved money

A7A5 was built as a closed, business-use instrument rather than a retail cryptocurrency. Chainalysis found that trading concentrated on a limited set of exchanges with Russian ties — Grinex, the OFAC-sanctioned peer-to-peer exchange Bitpapa, and Meer — and that trading occurred predominantly on a Monday-to-Friday schedule, consistent with business settlement rather than speculative retail use ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/a7a5-grinex-russian-crypto-economy-ofac-sanctions-august-2025/)). Through the end of July 2025 the token had processed over $51.17 billion, with volumes surging after the March 2025 Garantex takedown, the launch of Grinex, and the token's listing on Bitpapa ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/a7a5-grinex-russian-crypto-economy-ofac-sanctions-august-2025/)). FinCEN later reported that more than 180 entities processed at least $179.1 billion in A7A5 transactions between February 2025 and June 2026, drawing on TRM Labs research — a stat that, paired with the chain's relatively small float, makes the token a case study in high-velocity value transfer through a thinly circulated asset ([U.S. Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0644)). A dedicated decentralized exchange using a centrally controlled swap ratio converted A7A5 into mainstream dollar-backed stablecoins such as Tether's USDT, with $1.46 billion moving through that bridge and downstream funds reaching no-KYC exchanges and payment processors with Russian links ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/a7a5-grinex-russian-crypto-economy-ofac-sanctions-august-2025/)).

The token's origin was inseparable from the Garantex-to-Grinex handoff. TRM Labs' on-chain analysis showed Garantex wallets moving funds into A7A5 as early as January 2025 — the token contracts were created in late January — weeks before the enforcement action, suggesting foreknowledge of the disruption ([TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/garantex-grinex-and-the-a7a5-token-a-deep-dive-into-sanctions-evasion-networks)). After the March 2025 takedown, newly minted A7A5 flowed from Old Vector through Garantex wallets to provision liquidity on Grinex, with one OFAC-labeled Garantex-controlled address anchoring the early transfer chain ([TRM Labs](https://www.trmlabs.com/resources/blog/garantex-grinex-and-the-a7a5-token-a-deep-dive-into-sanctions-evasion-networks)). FinCEN describes the broader mechanism: where banking channels were restricted or unreliable, the A7 Network used digital assets including A7A5 to transfer value between network-linked actors, with sub-agents disguising payments as ordinary commercial activity ([FinCEN](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-10/FinCEN-Alert-A7-Network.pdf)).

## The World Liberty Financial allegation

In September 2025, the nonprofit watchdog Accountable.US published a report on buyers of the WLFI governance token issued by World Liberty Financial, the Trump family's crypto venture ([Accountable.US](https://accountable.us/trump-crypto-venture-wlfl-sold-tokens-to-entities-tied-to-north-korea-iran-a-notorious-money-laundering-platform/)). One key finding: since February 2, 2025, World Liberty Financial had sold user "0x9009" over 10,000 WLFI tokens, and "this same user has also used the A7A5 crypto token — a Russian 'Ruble-backed sanctions evasion tool'" whose creators were sanctioned by the U.S. government in August 2025 ([Accountable.US](https://accountable.us/trump-crypto-venture-wlfl-sold-tokens-to-entities-tied-to-north-korea-iran-a-notorious-money-laundering-platform/)). This is an allegation of wallet-level use, not a claim that World Liberty Financial transacted with the A7 Network.

The report drew a political response. In November 2025, Senators Elizabeth Warren and Jack Reed wrote to Attorney General Pamela Bondi and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent citing the Accountable.US findings, asking whether any inquiries were open into WLFI token sales to North Korea-linked, Russia-linked or other illicit buyers; CNBC first reported the letter ([Bitcoin.com News](https://api.news.bitcoin.com/senators-press-doj-treasury-for-probe-into-trump-linked-crypto-platform-wlfi/)). World Liberty Financial rejected the allegations, saying it performed strict AML and KYC checks on all token purchasers ([Bitcoin.com News](https://api.news.bitcoin.com/senators-press-doj-treasury-for-probe-into-trump-linked-crypto-platform-wlfi/)). Blockchain security researchers, including Nick Bax and MetaMask's Taylor Monahan, publicly disputed the report's on-chain analysis — the documented dispute concerned the report's claim linking a wallet to North Korea's Lazarus Group, which Bax said was a false positive that froze roughly $95,000 of an innocent user's WLFI ([Standing Record](https://standingrecord.com/entries/2025/09/18/federal-self-dealing-6ac10a32/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Was the A7A5 token itself sanctioned in August 2025?** Not directly. The August 14, 2025 U.S. action designated the issuer Old Vector LLC and the A7 companies while discussing the token in the release text. Explicit treatment of the token itself came later: the EU banned transactions involving A7A5 in October 2025, and Treasury called it "a blocked, ruble-backed token" in October 2026 ([U.S. Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0225); [U.S. Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0644)).

**What does "ruble-backed" mean here?** FinCEN says each A7A5 token was advertised as backed by ruble deposits held at Promsvyazbank (PSB), the Russian state defense bank designated by OFAC in February 2022 for issuing billions in financial support to Russia's defense sector ([FinCEN](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2026-10/FinCEN-Alert-A7-Network.pdf)). The profile does not independently audit that backing claim.

**How widely was A7A5 used?** Chainalysis measured over $51.17 billion in processed volume through July 2025, with trading concentrated on Russian-linked exchanges on business days; FinCEN later reported at least $179.1 billion across more than 180 entities from February 2025 to June 2026, and $1.46 billion had moved through the token's dedicated swap bridge into mainstream stablecoins ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/a7a5-grinex-russian-crypto-economy-ofac-sanctions-august-2025/); [U.S. Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0644)).

**Did a World Liberty Financial token buyer really use A7A5?** That is an allegation, not an established fact. Accountable.US asserted that WLFI buyer "0x9009" — buyer of over 10,000 WLFI tokens since February 2025 — had also used A7A5; senators cited the claim in a November 2025 probe request, while World Liberty Financial rejected the report and researchers disputed parts of its on-chain analysis ([Accountable.US](https://accountable.us/trump-crypto-venture-wlfl-sold-tokens-to-entities-tied-to-north-korea-iran-a-notorious-money-laundering-platform/); [Bitcoin.com News](https://api.news.bitcoin.com/senators-press-doj-treasury-for-probe-into-trump-linked-crypto-platform-wlfi/)).

**Why did regulators single A7A5 out?** Treasury's stated basis was purpose-built design: the A7 Network created the token for sanctioned members to transact internationally while evading sanctions and generating revenue for sanctioned infrastructure providers ([U.S. Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0644)). Chainalysis' evidence of a closed, business-hours trading ecosystem and a centrally controlled swap ratio supported that institutional reading ([Chainalysis](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/a7a5-grinex-russian-crypto-economy-ofac-sanctions-august-2025/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim the August 2025 OFAC action designated the A7A5 token itself — it designated the issuer Old Vector LLC and the A7 network entities; the token's blocked-property treatment is dated to October 2026 (U.S.) and its transaction ban to October 2025 (EU). It does not assert that the Accountable.US claim about WLFI buyer "0x9009" is true; it reports it as an allegation from an advocacy source, notes WLFI's denial, and flags the public dispute over the report's on-chain analysis.
