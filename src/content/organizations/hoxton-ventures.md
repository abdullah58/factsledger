## Overview

Hoxton Ventures is a [London](/places/london/)-based early-stage venture capital firm founded in 2013 by Hussein Kanji and Rob Kniaz. It is best known for backing three British unicorns — Deliveroo, Darktrace and Babylon Health — from its first, $40 million fund, and it has since raised two larger funds, of just under $100 million in 2020 and $215 million in 2022 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/06/18/hoxton-ventures-2/); [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2022/03/17/hoxton-ventures-brings-the-heat-with-new-215-million-fund/)). One of its three 2021 listings, Babylon Health, collapsed into administration two years later ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/digital-health/babylon-closes-us-business-lays-employees-after-mindmaze-take-private-deal-collapses)).

## Founding and investment thesis

Kanji and Kniaz arrived at Hoxton from opposite sides of the venture industry. Kanji was an [Accel](/organizations/accel/) alumnus who had also worked at [Microsoft](/organizations/microsoft/), while Kniaz was a Fidelity Ventures alumnus who had been a product manager at [Google](/organizations/google/) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/12/04/hoxton-ventures/)). Both brought operating experience from Silicon Valley — Kanji from Microsoft and companies including Sun, Kniaz from [Intel](/organizations/intel/) and Google — and Kanji argued at launch that the Bay Area ecosystem mattered even for European companies, because "that's where the best and brightest talent lies" and "ultimately that's also where the buyers are" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/12/04/hoxton-ventures/)).

The thesis they announced in December 2013 was deliberately narrow in ambition and broad in sector. Kanji told TechCrunch the firm was "entirely outlier driven, meaning we want to find companies that will become $1b+ companies," adding: "If you present a company to us where we are guaranteed to make 3-5x, but there is no potential of being 30-50x, it's a pass for us" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/12/04/hoxton-ventures/)). The target was entrepreneurs "inventing entirely new industry categories" rather than improving existing ones, and the firm said it would typically invest $1–2 million, make four to six investments a year, and follow on in every subsequent round ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/12/04/hoxton-ventures/)). The strategy has barely changed since: in 2020 Kanji described Hoxton as "deliberately anti-thematic," backing "tomorrow's global tech winners from Europe" across sectors, with cheques then ranging from $500,000 to $5 million at pre-seed, seed and Series A stage ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/06/18/hoxton-ventures-2/)).

Serial entrepreneur Dylan Collins was recruited as a venture partner at launch, adding operating credibility to the new fund: he had founded and sold three companies — Jolt Online Gaming, acquired by GameStop; DemonWare, acquired by Activision Blizzard; and mobile messaging company Phorest, acquired by MBO — and was then building SuperAwesome ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/12/04/hoxton-ventures/)). Before the debut fund even closed, Kanji and Kniaz had already invested in Llustre, later acquired by Fab, and GoCardless; at the announcement the fund held three investments, Campanja, Tizaro and one that had yet to be disclosed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/12/04/hoxton-ventures/)).

## The three funds

Fund I's record, as reported in June 2020, is the foundation of Hoxton's reputation: 17 companies backed, three unicorns, six acquisitions and two restarts, with the first fund holding the highest ratio of unicorns to investments of any fund, according to Dealroom ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/06/18/hoxton-ventures-2/)). The aggregate portfolio was then valued at more than $7 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/06/18/hoxton-ventures-2/)). A year after launch, the firm had already extended its reach with a dedicated Irish vehicle: in December 2014 it set aside €6 million exclusively for Irish startups, with Enterprise Ireland committing €2.5 million to the first closing and government minister Richard Bruton welcoming the non-bank financing ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2014/12/04/hoxton-ventures-fund-enterprise-ireland/)). Collins and Vijay Pandurangan, a former Google software engineer who had sold his startup Mitro to Twitter, were named venture partners for the Irish push ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2014/12/04/hoxton-ventures-fund-enterprise-ireland/)).

Fund II, announced in June 2020 at a little less than $100 million, was more than twice the size of the debut fund — but it was hard won ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/06/18/hoxton-ventures-2/)). Brexit disrupted the raise directly: the European Investment Fund discontinued new fund relationships in the UK after Article 50 was triggered, a setback Kanji said cost the firm at least a year, and commitments timed out and had to be re-raised ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/06/18/hoxton-ventures-2/)). British Patient Capital stepped in as anchor investor and, in the partners' telling, "helped ensure we had some continuity" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/06/18/hoxton-ventures-2/)). The majority of the fund had actually closed, unannounced, in early 2019, and it began investing then; by June 2020 it had made 20 investments, including seed bets on FabricNano, a next-generation enzyme business, Fy!, a homewares marketplace, Kheiron Medical, an AI radiology startup, and Preply, an online education company, with about a sixth of those investments made after the start of the coronavirus crisis ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/06/18/hoxton-ventures-2/)). Rob Ludwig had joined as partner and chief operating officer in 2019 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/06/18/hoxton-ventures-2/)).

Fund III, announced in March 2022, was a step change: $215 million, oversubscribed by $75 million, aimed at European startups intending to expand into the US market through pre-seed and seed investments, with ticket sizes usually between $500,000 and $5 million that the firm now wanted to increase ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2022/03/17/hoxton-ventures-brings-the-heat-with-new-215-million-fund/)). By then Hoxton had made 63 investments, 19 of them through the new fund, counted four investments valued at more than a billion dollars, and put its aggregate portfolio value at over $47 billion ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2022/03/17/hoxton-ventures-brings-the-heat-with-new-215-million-fund/)). Kanji framed the raise as a vindication of the original bet: "When we founded Hoxton eight years ago, there was doubt that Europe could produce winning tech companies. Last year, Europe attracted more capital than China with $116 billion invested, up from $8 billion a decade ago" ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2022/03/17/hoxton-ventures-brings-the-heat-with-new-215-million-fund/)). Charles Seely joined as a partner alongside the announcement, having advised Cambridge Quantum during its merger with Honeywell Quantum Solutions to form Quantinuum; the connection came through a co-led $6 million seed investment in UK grocery delivery company Bother ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2022/03/17/hoxton-ventures-brings-the-heat-with-new-215-million-fund/)).

## Portfolio: unicorns, and the Behavox bet

The three names that define Hoxton's first fund are Deliveroo, Darktrace and Babylon Health — all three went public in 2021, a single-year harvest that turned a $40 million debut fund into a European venture legend ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2022/03/17/hoxton-ventures-brings-the-heat-with-new-215-million-fund/)). But the firm's early bets were not confined to consumer and security names. Its portfolio also spans payments (GoCardless), education (Preply), and deep technology (FabricNano, Kheiron Medical), reflecting the anti-thematic stance Kanji has maintained since 2013 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/12/04/hoxton-ventures/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/06/18/hoxton-ventures-2/)).

The Behavox investment is the firm's most fully documented single bet, and it shows Hoxton's pattern of backing AI applied to unglamorous, regulated work. Behavox had raised $400,000 from unnamed investors in 2014 and another $1 million in 2015 before closing an undisclosed Series A in 2016 co-led by Hoxton Ventures and Promus Ventures, followed by a $20 million Series A1 in 2017 led by Citigroup and backed by [Index Ventures](/organizations/index-ventures/) ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/02/25/behavox-spies-100m-vision-fund-investment/)). The co-lead is confirmed from both sides: Promus Ventures' own July 2016 newsletter says "Alongside our friends Hoxton Ventures, we are privileged to co-lead the Series A for Behavox," a London-based company building compliance-surveillance software using natural-language processing, voice analytics and neural networks for banks, hedge funds, traders and brokers ([Promus Ventures](https://us12.campaign-archive.com/?u=cf5606e10850059e196dadeff&id=26352e1ffa)). The timing was pointed — the investment closed only days after the Brexit referendum vote, and Promus used the announcement to argue that London would remain the European centre of startup investing ([Promus Ventures](https://us12.campaign-archive.com/?u=cf5606e10850059e196dadeff&id=26352e1ffa)). Behavox went on to raise $100 million from SoftBank's Vision Fund 2 before the pandemic, a round TechCrunch noted in 2020 while reporting that Hoxton remained "big fans" of the company ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/06/18/hoxton-ventures-2/)).

## The Babylon reversal

Babylon Health is the counterweight to the unicorn story, and the part of Hoxton's record the celebratory fund announcements do not dwell on. The digital-health company went public in October 2021 through a $4.2 billion SPAC merger with Alkuri Global Acquisition Corp, one of three Hoxton first-fund listings that year ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/digital-health/babylon-closes-us-business-lays-employees-after-mindmaze-take-private-deal-collapses); [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2022/03/17/hoxton-ventures-brings-the-heat-with-new-215-million-fund/)). Within two years it had unravelled: it reported a 2022 net loss of $221 million on $1 billion in revenue, a planned take-private deal with digital neurotherapy company MindMaze collapsed, and in August 2023 its US business entered Chapter 7 bankruptcy proceedings while its UK subsidiary went into administration ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/digital-health/babylon-closes-us-business-lays-employees-after-mindmaze-take-private-deal-collapses)). Restructuring firm Alvarez & Marsal was appointed administrator over Babylon Group Holdings on 30 August 2023, and most of the UK business was sold to eMed Healthcare — a sale that, the company said in an SEC filing, would provide no payment to its Class A shareholders ([Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/digital-health/babylon-closes-us-business-lays-employees-after-mindmaze-take-private-deal-collapses)). The episode is a reminder that the "outlier-driven" strategy cuts both ways: the same first fund that produced three billion-dollar listings also held a company whose equity ended up worthless, and the aggregate portfolio figures Hoxton cites — over $47 billion by 2022 — are snapshots of paper value, not realised returns ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2022/03/17/hoxton-ventures-brings-the-heat-with-new-215-million-fund/); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/digital-health/babylon-closes-us-business-lays-employees-after-mindmaze-take-private-deal-collapses)).
