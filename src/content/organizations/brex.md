## Overview

Brex was a San Francisco fintech founded in 2017 by Brazilian entrepreneurs Henrique Dubugras and Pedro Franceschi, offering corporate credit cards and expense-management software first to startups and later to large enterprises ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/brex-launches-credit-card-for-startups-raises-57-million-from-y-combinator-paypal-founders-others)). After one of the most dramatic arcs in fintech — a $12.3 billion peak valuation in 2022, a controversial mass abandonment of small-business customers, two layoff rounds and the end of its co-CEO structure — it was acquired by Capital One in April 2026 for $5.15 billion, less than half its peak valuation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/); [Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2897536/capital-one-completes-brex-buyout-boosts-digital-payments-strategy)).

## Two teenagers, a payments company, and a startup credit card

Dubugras and Franceschi met as teenagers in Brazil and founded the payments processor Pagar.me at age 16 in 2012; it was later acquired by Stone Pagamentos ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/12/fintech-brex-abandons-co-ceo-model-talks-ipo-cash-burn-and-plans-for-a-secondary-sale/)). They dropped out of Stanford as freshmen to join Y Combinator's winter 2017 cohort and founded Brex, reimagining the corporate credit card: instead of underwriting on credit history and personal guarantees, Brex set credit limits based on who had invested in a startup, the equity held, cash balance and spending patterns ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/brex-launches-credit-card-for-startups-raises-57-million-from-y-combinator-paypal-founders-others)). The concept resonated: after a private launch with 1,000 customers including Flexport, Algolia and Affirm, the card opened to all US startups in mid-2018 with $57 million in announced funding — an April 2017 $7 million round from YC and Ribbit Capital plus a $50 million Series B from Y Combinator Continuity, Peter Thiel, Max Levchin and others ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/brex-launches-credit-card-for-startups-raises-57-million-from-y-combinator-paypal-founders-others)).

## The valuation run-up and the pivots

Brex's valuation trajectory was steep: a $125 million Series C at $1.1 billion in October 2018, then a $100 million Series C extension led by Kleiner Perkins at $2.6 billion in 2019 (total equity funding $315 million), and finally a $300 million Series D-2 in January 2022 at $12.3 billion, with over $1.2 billion raised in total ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/brex-raises-100-million-at-2-6-billion-valuation-for-startup-friendly-credit-card); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/06/19/what-was-really-behind-brexs-decision-to-ditch-smbs/)). Along the way there were stumbles — including a 2019 purchase of San Francisco's South Park Cafe by the then-23-year-old co-CEOs, a hospitality venture that collapsed when COVID-19 shut the city down ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)).

The defining pivot came in June 2022. Three months after announcing a big push into enterprise software, Brex confirmed it would no longer serve small- to medium-sized businesses without "professional" funding — venture capital, angel money or accelerator backing. "Tens of thousands" of businesses were told their accounts would close by August 15, a move that angered founders who felt the company was abandoning the customers who had built it ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/06/19/what-was-really-behind-brexs-decision-to-ditch-smbs/)). Dubugras admitted the criteria might not be "perfect," but argued startup clients needed resources diverted from SMBs, and that servicing small businesses — with its onboarding, fraud and risk costs — was simply uneconomical against a macro backdrop of investors demanding real revenue ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/06/19/what-was-really-behind-brexs-decision-to-ditch-smbs/)).

Cost-cutting followed. Brex laid off 136 people (11 percent of staff) in October 2022 and another 282 (20 percent) in January 2024, leaving about 1,000 workers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/12/fintech-brex-abandons-co-ceo-model-talks-ipo-cash-burn-and-plans-for-a-secondary-sale/)). In June 2024 the founders ended the co-CEO model they had run since inception — Franceschi became sole CEO while Dubugras became board chairman — arguing investors preferred a traditional structure ahead of a potential IPO and that single leadership enabled faster decisions ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/12/fintech-brex-abandons-co-ceo-model-talks-ipo-cash-burn-and-plans-for-a-secondary-sale/)). They said cash burn had been halved over the prior year, with cash-flow positivity targeted for 2025, and the company served more than 30,000 customers including over 130 publicly traded companies such as DoorDash, Roblox and Shein — though the bulk of revenue still came from startups ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/12/fintech-brex-abandons-co-ceo-model-talks-ipo-cash-burn-and-plans-for-a-secondary-sale/)).

## The Capital One exit

On January 22, 2026, the Wall Street Journal broke the news that Capital One would acquire Brex for $5.15 billion in cash and stock — less than half of the $12.3 billion 2022 valuation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)). For the earliest backers it was a triumph anyway: Ribbit Capital's $7 million Series A bet had multiplied roughly 700-fold ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)). For later-stage investors such as TCV, GIC, Baillie Gifford and Madrone — which had invested at a $7.4 billion valuation or higher — the exit was a haircut, but a liquid one ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)).

The deal closed in April 2026 after regulatory approvals: Capital One paid approximately $2.56 billion in cash and issued about 10.7 million of its shares, with $950 million in transaction-related costs over three years and nearly 80 percent of the purchase price allocated to goodwill ([Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/2897536/capital-one-completes-brex-buyout-boosts-digital-payments-strategy)). Capital One — which had itself swallowed Discover Financial in a $35 billion deal the previous May — gained Brex's tech platform, a reported client roster including TikTok, Robinhood and Intel, $13 billion in deposits overseen at partner banks, and a fresh EU operating license covering all 30 EU countries ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)). Franceschi remains CEO post-acquisition ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)).

## What this profile does not claim

- This profile does not present Brex as an independent company: the Capital One acquisition was completed in April 2026 and CEO Pedro Franceschi reports into Capital One.
- Revenue figures were never independently disclosed; the profile states funding, valuations and customer counts from press reporting only.
- The Pagar.me sale price is deliberately omitted because sources disagree (tens of millions per a 2024 founder interview; undisclosed per 2018 coverage); only the Stone Pagamentos acquisition is stated.
- The client names TikTok, Robinhood and Intel are stated as reported by TechCrunch, not as independently verified customer relationships.
- The $13 billion in deposits "oversees at partner banks" figure is TechCrunch's phrasing of reported figures; its exact accounting basis was not verified.
- Brex is not confused with Ramp: Brex was founded by Dubugras and Franceschi in 2017; Ramp is a separate expense-management fintech co-founded by Eric Glyman ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/12/fintech-brex-abandons-co-ceo-model-talks-ipo-cash-burn-and-plans-for-a-secondary-sale/)).
