## Overview

Industry Ventures is a San Francisco-based venture capital platform founded in 2000 by Hans Swildens that made its name in venture capital secondaries — buying stakes in existing venture funds and venture-backed companies from early investors who want liquidity — and in early-stage hybrid funds that blend primary and secondary investing ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures), [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/goldman-sachs-to-acquire-industry-ventures-and-its-7b-portfolio)). It manages $7 billion of assets under supervision and has made more than 1,000 secondary and primary investments since its founding, with a portfolio spanning over 800 venture capital and technology-focused funds ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures)). In October 2025, [Goldman Sachs](/organizations/goldman-sachs/) announced an agreement to acquire the firm for $665 million in cash and equity at closing, plus up to $300 million more tied to performance through 2030 ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures)).

The acquisition price is a measure of how central secondaries have become to venture capital. As companies stay private longer, the investors who backed them early increasingly need a way to sell before an IPO or acquisition — and Industry Ventures spent 25 years building the machinery to buy those positions. Goldman Sachs chief executive David Solomon put it directly in the announcement: "Industry Ventures pioneered venture secondary investing and early-stage hybrid funds, areas that are rapidly expanding as companies stay private longer and investors seek new forms of liquidity" ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures)).

## What a venture secondary actually is

A venture secondary is a purchase of an existing stake rather than a new investment in a company. When a limited partner in a venture fund wants out early, or an early employee or angel wants to sell shares in a still-private startup, a secondary buyer steps in and takes over the position. The seller gets cash years before any exit; the buyer gets exposure to a company or fund at a price that often reflects the seller's urgency rather than the latest headline valuation. Industry Ventures was founded in San Francisco in 2000 with that single focus, at a time when most of the secondary market served buyouts rather than venture capital ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/goldman-sachs-to-acquire-industry-ventures-and-its-7b-portfolio)).

Over time the firm layered complementary strategies on top of the secondary business. It seeds emerging venture capital funds, co-invests directly in early-stage high-growth businesses using pro-rata rights, and invests at the intersection of venture capital and tech buyout ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures)). The combination matters: the fund-investment arm gives the firm a view across more than 800 venture funds and relationships with over 325 venture capital firms as a value-added limited partner, liquidity provider and co-investment partner — and that network in turn generates the deal flow for its secondary and direct deals ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures)). The firm is headquartered in San Francisco, with offices in Boston, Washington, DC, and London ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures)).

The scale of that network shows up in the portfolio names. Industry Ventures has invested in some of the most high-profile venture-backed companies of the last 25 years, including Uber, Airbnb, Alibaba, DocuSign, Cisco, Dropbox, DoorDash and Roblox ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/goldman-sachs-to-acquire-industry-ventures-and-its-7b-portfolio)). In an interview with Bloomberg, Swildens estimated the firm had exposure to roughly a fifth of the US venture capital market ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/goldman-sachs-to-acquire-industry-ventures-and-its-7b-portfolio)). That breadth is the point of the model: rather than betting a fund on a handful of companies, Industry Ventures holds a slice of the venture market itself, and profits when the market's winners compound.

## Performance, as the firm reports it

Industry Ventures calculates its realized performance across its platform as a net internal rate of return of 18% and a net realized multiple on invested capital (MOIC) of 2.2x since inception, measured as of March 31, 2025 ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures)). Those are the firm's own calculations, not independently audited returns, and they cover realized investments — positions where more than half the cost has been returned — rather than the paper value of everything it still holds. The distinction matters in secondaries, where a buyer can manufacture an attractive entry price and still lose money if the underlying company stalls; a realized 2.2x multiple, if accurate, says the entry-price discipline actually converted into cash.

The firm's institutional backing predates the Goldman deal by years. Goldman Sachs Asset Management had been a limited partner in Industry Ventures' funds for over two decades and had offered the firm's strategies to its own clients for a decade, and Petershill Partners — Goldman's GP-stakes business — had owned a minority stake in Industry Ventures on behalf of fund investors since 2019 ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures)). In other words, Goldman was already a customer and a part-owner before it decided to buy the rest. That history also explains the deal's logic from Goldman's side: the acquisition adds a venture-secondaries capability to an alternatives platform that already manages about $540 billion, and places Industry Ventures inside Goldman's external manager platform, the External Investing Group, which has over $450 billion in assets under supervision across traditional and alternative strategies ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures)).

## The Booksy investment — a primary deal, not a secondary

Industry Ventures is best known for secondaries, but it also leads primary rounds, and its most relevant one for this site is [Booksy](/organizations/booksy/). In September 2019, Booksy raised $28.5 million in a round co-led by [Piton Capital](/organizations/piton-capital/), [Enern](/organizations/enern/), Industry Ventures and XG Ventures ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2021/01/29/booksy-slots-in-70m/)). The round sits in the middle of Booksy's funding history: after a $4.2 million Series A in 2017 and a $13.2 million Series B in 2018, and before the $70 million Series C in January 2021 that took total capital raised to $119 million ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2021/01/29/booksy-slots-in-70m/)). For Industry Ventures, the deal is a useful illustration of the hybrid model in practice — a direct primary investment in a European-founded company expanding in the United States, made by a firm whose core business is buying other investors' positions. It also shows the firm's geographic reach beyond its San Francisco base: the London office and the European co-investors on the Booksy cap table (Piton, Enern) are part of the same network.

## The Goldman Sachs acquisition

Goldman Sachs announced on October 13, 2025 that it had entered into an agreement to acquire 100% of Industry Ventures' equity ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures)). The consideration is $665 million in cash and equity payable at closing, plus additional contingent consideration of up to $300 million, payable in cash and equity, based on the firm's future performance through 2030 ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures)). The transaction was expected to close in the first quarter of 2026, subject to regulatory approval and conditions ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures)). Whether it has actually closed was not verified from a fully-read source as of November 18, 2026, and this profile does not state that it has.

The human terms of the deal were spelled out in the announcement: all 45 Industry Ventures employees were expected to join Goldman Sachs, and Swildens, senior managing director Justin Burden and senior managing director Roland Reynolds were to be appointed partners within Goldman Sachs Asset Management ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures)). Swildens framed the combination as a response to a market at "a pivotal inflection point as technology and artificial intelligence reshape the world," arguing that Goldman's global resources plus Industry Ventures' venture expertise would serve entrepreneurs, private technology companies, limited partners and venture fund managers ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures)). For Goldman, the deal fills a specific gap: its External Investing Group already ran a buyout-oriented secondaries platform in Vintage Strategies, and Industry Ventures adds a dedicated venture-secondaries capability alongside it.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does Industry Ventures do?**
It is a venture capital platform that invests across the venture lifecycle through secondaries (buying existing stakes in funds and companies), fund investments, direct co-investments, and hybrid funds that blend primary and secondary investing ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures)).

**Who founded Industry Ventures?**
Hans Swildens founded the firm in San Francisco in 2000 and remained its founder and chief executive through the Goldman Sachs acquisition announcement ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures), [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/goldman-sachs-to-acquire-industry-ventures-and-its-7b-portfolio)).

**How big is Industry Ventures?**
It manages $7 billion of assets under supervision, has made more than 1,000 investments since 2000, and holds positions across more than 800 venture funds ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures)).

**Did Goldman Sachs buy Industry Ventures?**
Goldman Sachs announced an agreement to acquire 100% of the firm on October 13, 2025, for $665 million at closing plus up to $300 million contingent on performance through 2030, with closing expected in the first quarter of 2026; completion was not verified as of November 18, 2026 ([Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2025/goldman-sachs-announces-acquisition-of-industry-ventures)).

**What is Industry Ventures' connection to Booksy?**
It co-led Booksy's $28.5 million September 2019 funding round alongside Piton Capital, Enern and XG Ventures ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2021/01/29/booksy-slots-in-70m/)).
