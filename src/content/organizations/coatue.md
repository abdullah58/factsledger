## Overview

Coatue, formally Coatue Management, is the American technology-focused investment firm that Philippe Laffont founded in 1999 with a $45 million hedge fund, and that grew into a crossover platform managing more than $70 billion across public and private markets ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/famous-investors/philippe-laffont/); [BusinessWire](https://markets.financialcontent.com/pentictonherald/article/bizwire-2026-5-12-coatues-ctek-joins-cais-bringing-public-private-innovation-strategies-to-independent-advisors)). Laffont, a former analyst at Julian Robertson's Tiger Management, built the firm on technology research first — early pre-IPO bets on ByteDance, Snap, and Spotify, then a decisive AI-era concentration in chipmakers and private AI leaders — and in 2025 Coatue co-led [Anysphere](/organizations/anysphere/)'s $2.3 billion Series D, its first investment in the maker of the Cursor coding assistant ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/famous-investors/philippe-laffont/); [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/coatue-joins-23-billion-bet-hot-ai-coding-platform)).

## From a $45 million hedge fund to a crossover platform

Laffont did not start as a stock picker by training. He left France to earn a computer science degree from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 1989, worked as a management consultant in Madrid, and then joined Tiger Management as an analyst covering European telecommunications stocks ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/coatue-management-raises-700-million-dan-rose/)). After three years at Tiger he raised $45 million in 1999 and launched Coatue, named for a beach off the coast of Nantucket, joining the ranks of Robertson's former employees known as "Tiger Cubs" ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/coatue-management-raises-700-million-dan-rose/); [The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/famous-investors/philippe-laffont/)). The timing was brutal: the Nasdaq crashed in the dot-com bust shortly after the first fund launched, but Laffont navigated it by going long on the most stable technology companies and short on companies struggling to compete against internet businesses ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/coatue-management-raises-700-million-dan-rose/)). He then hired his brother Thomas — working at the time as an agent for Hollywood actors — and their father briefly served as the firm's chief financial officer ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/coatue-management-raises-700-million-dan-rose/)).

Coatue survived the dot-com bust and the 2008–2009 financial crisis, the two events that define its early resilience ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/famous-investors/philippe-laffont/)). In the crisis year of 2008 the brothers were managing $2.2 billion, the fund lost more than 14%, and they responded by adding 11 analysts and 4 partners, recovering and raising more money the following year ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/coatue-management-raises-700-million-dan-rose/)). The decisive structural shift came in 2012, when Thomas Laffont began overseeing Coatue's Silicon Valley office, opening the door to early-stage investing in companies such as Box, which Coatue backed at a $1.2 billion valuation ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/coatue-management-raises-700-million-dan-rose/)). From there the firm built out the private side of what became a lifecycle model: late-stage wins in Grab, Lyft, Snap, and Uber, plus investments in DoorDash, Lime, and Instacart, and pre-IPO positions in ByteDance, Snap, Instacart, Lyft, and Spotify ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/coatue-management-raises-700-million-dan-rose/); [The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/famous-investors/philippe-laffont/)). In 2019 the firm reportedly raised $700 million for a dedicated early-stage venture fund and hired Dan Rose, Facebook's former vice president of partnerships, to oversee it, with early bets on AppZen, Persona, and Sigma ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/coatue-management-raises-700-million-dan-rose/)). By then Coatue managed $17 billion, spent over $30 million a year buying data for its algorithms, and counted engineers as about half of its investment team ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/coatue-management-raises-700-million-dan-rose/)).

## The AI-era portfolio and the retail-access experiment

Laffont's public-market positioning in the AI era is concentrated, not diversified. At the end of the second quarter of 2026, nearly 23% of Coatue's capital sat in three AI-related positions: Taiwan Semiconductor at 8.8%, Micron Technology at 7.5% — a position increased by 1,794% in the quarter to more than $3.6 billion — and [SpaceX](/organizations/spacex/) at 6.5%, a private position disclosed at close to $3.2 billion ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/21/billionaire-investor-philippe-laffont-has-nearly-2/)). The firm had also put money into [Nvidia](/organizations/nvidia/), Broadcom, Meta Platforms, and CoreWeave, with CoreWeave its largest holding at the end of the second quarter of 2025 at 8.1% of the portfolio ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/famous-investors/philippe-laffont/)). Laffont's own framing, laid out in a 2025 Coatue presentation, reads technology history as a succession of epochs — IBM mainframes, then PCs, networking, the desktop internet, the mobile internet led by ByteDance and Tencent, cloud and software-as-a-service, and now AI, benefiting Taiwan Semiconductor, Meta, Microsoft, Anthropic, Nvidia, Broadcom, Oracle, and OpenAI — while warning that high prices, tariffs, and budget deficits could curb the AI boom even as it might create a "virtuous flywheel" of productivity gains ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/famous-investors/philippe-laffont/)).

The firm's newer experiment is structural rather than thematic. In 2025 Coatue launched the Coatue Innovation Fund, or CTEK, a tender-offer fund designed to bring qualifying individual investors closer to the technology-driven companies the firm has long backed, seeded with a combined $1 billion in anchor commitments from Bezos Expeditions and DFO Management, the family offices of Jeff Bezos and Michael Dell ([BusinessWire](https://markets.financialcontent.com/pentictonherald/article/bizwire-2026-5-12-coatues-ctek-joins-cais-bringing-public-private-innovation-strategies-to-independent-advisors)). In May 2026 CTEK joined the CAIS platform, which connects more than 62,000 financial advisors at over 2,000 wealth management firms to alternative strategies ([BusinessWire](https://markets.financialcontent.com/pentictonherald/article/bizwire-2026-5-12-coatues-ctek-joins-cais-bringing-public-private-innovation-strategies-to-independent-advisors)). The same year the firm raised $1 billion for Coatue Tech Fund I, its seventh venture capital fund, and by one count completed 39 private deals in 2025, against 43 in all of 2024 ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/coatue-joins-23-billion-bet-hot-ai-coding-platform)). The firm describes itself as a global technology investment platform with more than $70 billion in assets under management, run by an integrated team of investors, technologists, and data scientists ([BusinessWire](https://markets.financialcontent.com/pentictonherald/article/bizwire-2026-5-12-coatues-ctek-joins-cais-bringing-public-private-innovation-strategies-to-independent-advisors)).

## A first bet on Anysphere — as co-lead

Coatue's most visible recent private deal arrived late in 2025, and it was a debut, not a follow-on. In November 2025 Anysphere announced a $2.3 billion Series D financing for its coding automation platform Cursor, valuing the company at $29.3 billion — more than three times the roughly $9 billion valuation of its Series C six months earlier — and Coatue led the financing with [Accel](/organizations/accel/) ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/coatue-joins-23-billion-bet-hot-ai-coding-platform)). It was Coatue's first investment in the company, and it joined existing investors including Nvidia, Google parent Alphabet, Thrive Capital, DST Global, and Andreessen Horowitz ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/coatue-joins-23-billion-bet-hot-ai-coding-platform)). Anysphere said at the time that it had crossed $1 billion in annualized revenue and employed more than 300 engineers, researchers, designers, and operators ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/coatue-joins-23-billion-bet-hot-ai-coding-platform)). For a firm that built its name on public-market technology research before expanding into private markets, co-leading the round of one of the fastest-appreciating private companies of the AI era was the crossover model in its purest form: public-market discipline applied to a private deal at full scale.
