# American Agrisurance, Inc.

**American Agrisurance, Inc. — known inside its group as "AmAg" — was a
non-insurance subsidiary of Acceptance Insurance Companies Inc. that
marketed and serviced the group's federally reinsured crop insurance
business.** It was not the underwriter: the Kentucky Supreme Court
described it as "the marketing and service affiliate for American Growers'
crop insurance business," while American Growers Insurance itself sold the
multiple-peril crop insurance (MPCI) policies reinsured by the Federal
Crop Insurance Corporation (FCIC). It filed for Chapter 7 liquidation in
Nebraska on January 7, 2005, the same day its parent filed Chapter 11.

## Role in the Acceptance insurance group

American Agrisurance's function is defined in two appellate opinions. In
Dailey v. American Growers Insurance and American Agrisurance, Inc. (Ky.
2003), a Kentucky tobacco farmer's MPCI claim dispute, the court
introduced the company as American Growers' "marketing and service
affiliate" — American Growers being, per that opinion, an Iowa
corporation selling MPCI policies reinsured by the FCIC. Three years
later, in FSS Potted Plants LLC v. American Agrisurance Inc. (Mich. Ct.
App. 2006), a frost-coverage dispute, the court recorded that "Growers
issued an insurance policy... American Agrisurance, Inc., marketed the
policy," and treated the plaintiffs' description of Agrisurance as "the
marketing arm of Growers" — the "marketing division for" Growers — as a
principal-agent relationship binding it to the insurer's arbitration.

The Troubled Company Reporter classified American Agrisurance as one of
two "non-insurance company subsidiaries" of [AICI](/organizations/acceptance-insurance-companies-inc/), headquartered in
[Council Bluffs](/places/council-bluffs-iowa/), [Iowa](/places/iowa/), that filed alongside the parent.

## 2005 liquidation

On January 7, 2005, American Agrisurance filed a voluntary Chapter 7
liquidation petition in the [U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of
Nebraska](/organizations/us-bankruptcy-court-district-of-nebraska/) (Case No. 05-80058), the same day sister company Acceptance
Insurance Services, Inc. filed Chapter 7 (05-80056) and parent AICI filed
Chapter 11 (05-80059). AICI listed $33,069,446 in assets and
$137,120,541 in debts — parent-company figures, not Agrisurance's own.

AICI chief executive John E. Martin said the filings by "AICI, AIS and
AmAg" did "not involve Acceptance Insurance Company," which would remain
under the Nebraska Department of Insurance's supervision. The background
was the collapse of the crop business: NDOI had issued an Order of
Supervision to American Growers in November 2002 after the USDA Risk
Management Agency blocked its sale to Rain and Hail LLC.

## FAQ

**Was American Agrisurance an insurance company?**
No — it was a non-insurance subsidiary. The insurer was American
Growers Insurance (the MPCI underwriter); Agrisurance marketed and
serviced that business.

**Is this the same "Acceptance Insurance" as the Nashville auto insurer?**
No. First Acceptance Corporation of Nashville, Tennessee (non-standard
auto insurer, consumer brand "Acceptance Insurance") is an entirely
separate company. American Agrisurance belonged to Acceptance Insurance
Companies Inc. of Council Bluffs, Iowa.

**Why did the group file for bankruptcy?**
The Troubled Company Reporter traced the collapse to the 2002 failure of
the crop insurance business — the NDOI supervision order, the blocked
sale of the crop book, and the resulting Chapter 11/Chapter 7 filings in
January 2005.

**What happened to the Chapter 7 case?**
Case No. 05-80058 was a liquidation petition in the District of Nebraska.
The trustee's disposition and closing date are not in the public sources
read for this profile.
