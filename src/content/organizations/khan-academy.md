## Overview

Khan Academy is an American nonprofit educational organization whose mission is "to provide a free, world-class education to anyone, anywhere," funded by philanthropy rather than fees paid by learners ([FourWeekMBA](http://fourweekmba.com/how-does-khan-academy-make-money/)). Salman "Sal" Khan founded the nonprofit in 2008, converting the tutorial videos he had been posting online into a formal organization ([Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/consumer-tech/khanmigo-ai-tutor); [Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2019/9/13/21121840/how-khan-academy-the-popular-free-tool-for-students-wants-to-play-a-more-official-role-in-america-s/)). The distinctive feature is the nonprofit model itself: donor-funded free access is the business model, not a marketing tier, and the organization treats its nonprofit status as a trust-building asset with schools ([Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2019/9/13/21121840/how-khan-academy-the-popular-free-tool-for-students-wants-to-play-a-more-official-role-in-america-s/)).

## What it is known for

Khan Academy is known for a large free library of short instructional videos, practice exercises and lessons spanning subjects from pre-K reading to college-level calculus, offered online in multiple languages ([Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/consumer-tech/khanmigo-ai-tutor)). It began when Khan, then a hedge-fund analyst, tutored family members and started uploading video tutorials to YouTube, finding the videos engaged even his own cousins more effectively than remote tutoring alone ([FourWeekMBA](http://fourweekmba.com/how-does-khan-academy-make-money/)). It does not collect money from YouTube advertisements; the videos serve as a free distribution channel watched by millions of people daily ([FourWeekMBA](http://fourweekmba.com/how-does-khan-academy-make-money/)). In 2023 it became the most prominent nonprofit deployment of generative AI in education with Khanmigo, an AI tutor and teaching assistant built on [OpenAI's](/organizations/openai/) GPT-4 ([Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/consumer-tech/khanmigo-ai-tutor)).

## The nonprofit model (funding)

Khan Academy's core offering has no price: the organization is funded by donations and grants from individuals, foundations and corporations — historically including the Gates Foundation, Google, Bank of America, Comcast, AT&T, Valhalla, Hyatt, Disney, the Smith Family Foundation, General Motors and the Musk Foundation ([FourWeekMBA](http://fourweekmba.com/how-does-khan-academy-make-money/)). Khan funded the first four years himself; the first major outside donation was a $100,000 grant from Ann Doerr, and roughly $4 million followed in the next twelve months ([FourWeekMBA](http://fourweekmba.com/how-does-khan-academy-make-money/)).

It also charges school districts for implementation services: in 2019, districts paid $10 per student for teacher training and a data dashboard, or $12.50 per student for the full bundle including test-preparation tools — Compton's contract cost $44,000 that year, and by that fall some 218,000 students across seven districts were using the paid offerings ([Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2019/9/13/21121840/how-khan-academy-the-popular-free-tool-for-students-wants-to-play-a-more-official-role-in-america-s/)). Khan has said those fees do not cover the full cost, and the remainder is covered by philanthropy — being a nonprofit does not mean operating at zero margin: the organization ran a $15 million surplus in 2017 and deficits in 2018 and 2019 ([Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2019/9/13/21121840/how-khan-academy-the-popular-free-tool-for-students-wants-to-play-a-more-official-role-in-america-s/)). As Sal Khan put it, "I do think being not-for-profit increases the trust level that folks can have with us" — trust the organization explicitly banks on to sell its classroom services ([Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2019/9/13/21121840/how-khan-academy-the-popular-free-tool-for-students-wants-to-play-a-more-official-role-in-america-s/)).

## Sal Khan

Sal Khan is the founder and chief executive of Khan Academy, a role he has held since the organization's launch ([ITU](https://www.itu.int/initiatives/sdgdigital/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/08/Sal-Khan-bio.pdf)). He holds three degrees from MIT — two Bachelor of Science degrees, in Mathematics and in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science, and a Master of Science in electrical engineering — plus an MBA from Harvard Business School, and before education he worked as a hedge-fund analyst ([ITU](https://www.itu.int/initiatives/sdgdigital/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/08/Sal-Khan-bio.pdf); [FourWeekMBA](http://fourweekmba.com/how-does-khan-academy-make-money/)). His interest in education predates the organization: as an MIT undergraduate he developed math software for children with ADHD and tutored fourth- and seventh-grade public school students in Boston ([ITU](https://www.itu.int/initiatives/sdgdigital/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/08/Sal-Khan-bio.pdf)). He is also the founder of Schoolhouse.world, Khan Lab School and Khan World School — all nonprofits focused on personalized mastery education ([ITU](https://www.itu.int/initiatives/sdgdigital/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2023/08/Sal-Khan-bio.pdf)).

## Scale and reach

Khan Academy's library covers pre-K reading through college-level calculus, offered free online in multiple languages ([Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/consumer-tech/khanmigo-ai-tutor)). Its classroom arm had real penetration by 2019: more than 200 Compton teachers were trained in one year, and 218,000 students across seven districts used its paid offerings that fall ([Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/2019/9/13/21121840/how-khan-academy-the-popular-free-tool-for-students-wants-to-play-a-more-official-role-in-america-s/)). Its AI product scaled quickly too: Khanmigo piloted in grades 3–12 in more than 260 U.S. school districts, with more than 65,000 students reported as users at the time of the Microsoft deal ([Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/consumer-tech/khanmigo-ai-tutor); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/microsoft-to-enhance-khan-academys-ai-bot-with-azure-openai-service-develop-phi-3-math-model)).

## Products and offerings

Khanmigo is the AI-powered personal tutor and teaching assistant built on OpenAI's GPT-4, piloted from March 2023; Khan's team saw the model in summer 2022 after [Sam Altman](/people/sam-altman/) and [Greg Brockman](/people/greg-brockman/) showed it to him ([Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/consumer-tech/khanmigo-ai-tutor)). Its defining design choice is Socratic: rather than handing over answers, Khanmigo asks students what they think the first step is; the companion Writing Coach guides essay drafts without writing for them, flagging copied passages for teacher review ([Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/consumer-tech/khanmigo-ai-tutor)). Teachers can review transcripts, safety issues are flagged, and the data is never used to train a public model ([Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/consumer-tech/khanmigo-ai-tutor)).

Because the models are expensive to run, the rollout mirrors the nonprofit economics: individuals pay $4/month or $44/year, while a 2024 [Microsoft](/organizations/microsoft/) partnership — migrating Khanmigo to Azure OpenAI Service — made it free for teachers in 40+ countries, with districts paying a yearly per-student fee ([Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/consumer-tech/khanmigo-ai-tutor); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/microsoft-to-enhance-khan-academys-ai-bot-with-azure-openai-service-develop-phi-3-math-model)). The Microsoft deal also covers Phi-3-based math tutoring — with no Khan Academy user data used in training — and distribution of lessons through Microsoft Copilot and Teams for Education ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/microsoft-to-enhance-khan-academys-ai-bot-with-azure-openai-service-develop-phi-3-math-model)).

## Related entities

Khanmigo is built on OpenAI's GPT-4, accessed early through OpenAI's Sam Altman and Greg Brockman ([Freethink](https://www.freethink.com/consumer-tech/khanmigo-ai-tutor)). Microsoft is the infrastructure partner: it migrated Khanmigo to Azure OpenAI Service in 2024 so K-12 teachers could use it for free, and collaborates on Phi-3 math tutoring and Copilot/Teams distribution ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/microsoft-to-enhance-khan-academys-ai-bot-with-azure-openai-service-develop-phi-3-math-model)).
