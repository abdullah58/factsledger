## Overview

Equifax is one of the three nationwide consumer reporting agencies in the [United States](/places/united-states/) — the companies whose files decide whether Americans get a mortgage, a car loan or a credit card — and it is the bureau the public knows best, for the worst reason. Founded in Atlanta in 1899 as the Retail Credit Company, it reported $5,681.1 million of revenue in 2024 and employs about 15,000 people across 24 countries ([Equifax](https://investor.equifax.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000033185-25-000009/a2024q48-kexhibit991.htm)). In 2017 a breach the House Oversight Committee later called "entirely preventable" exposed the personal data of about 147 million Americans; the US government indicted four members of China's People's Liberation Army for it, and Equifax agreed to pay up to $700 million to settle ([House Committee on Oversight and Reform](https://oversight.house.gov/report/committee-releases-report-revealing-new-information-on-equifax-data-breach); [learncra](https://www.learncra.com/cfpb-ftc-cfpb-and-states-announce-settlement-with-equifax-over-2017-data-breach/)). Then, in January 2025, the [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](/organizations/consumer-financial-protection-bureau/) fined it $15 million for failing to investigate consumer disputes properly — the same kind of failure at the heart of the breach ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/equifax-inc-and-equifax-information-services-llc/)).

Equifax's files now carry rent as well as loans: [Bilt](/organizations/bilt/) reports members' on-time rent payments to Equifax, [Experian](/organizations/experian/) and [TransUnion](/organizations/transunion/) free of charge, and a TransUnion study cited by Bilt found scores rose by nearly 60 points on average when rent was included ([Bilt](https://newsroom.biltrewards.com/free-rent-reporting-to-renters)). That new data sits on top of an old business — mortgage reporting that combines the three major bureaus' reports — and a newer one, Workforce Solutions, its largest segment ([Equifax](https://investor.equifax.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001104659-07-036085/a07-12736_1ex99d1.htm); [Equifax](https://investor.equifax.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000033185-25-000009/a2024q48-kexhibit991.htm)).

## From Retail Credit to Equifax

Equifax began in 1899, when Cator and Guy Woolford formed the Retail Credit Company in Atlanta to sell credit information about consumers to local merchants ([1library.net](https://1library.net/article/late-s-onwards-consumer-history-of-credit-bureaux.zx83kpwq)). It bought the Retailers Commercial Agency of Brooklyn in 1934, was publicly owned from 1965, listed on the New York Stock Exchange in 1971, and took the name Equifax in 1975 ([Equifax](https://investor.equifax.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001104659-07-036085/a07-12736_1ex99d1.htm); [1library.net](https://1library.net/article/late-s-onwards-consumer-history-of-credit-bureaux.zx83kpwq)). By the late 1970s it and TransUnion had emerged as the industry's leaders, and the business it built still shows in its shape today: mortgage reporting that combines the three major bureaus' reports, a US consumer information business, and Workforce Solutions — income and employment verification — which took $598.1 million of revenue in the last quarter of 2024, more than any other segment ([Equifax](https://investor.equifax.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000033185-25-000009/a2024q48-kexhibit991.htm)). About 85% of its revenue now runs on EFX Cloud, the platform it built after the breach ([Equifax](https://investor.equifax.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000033185-25-000009/a2024q48-kexhibit991.htm)).

## The breach that was "entirely preventable"

The breach began with a known flaw: hackers exploited an Apache Struts vulnerability in Equifax's online dispute portal, publicly announced on 7 March 2017, and first got in around 13 May, running about 9,000 queries against the company's databases and routing traffic through about 34 servers in about 20 countries ([Security Magazine](https://www.securitymagazine.com/articles/91687-chinese-military-hackers-charged-in-equifax-breach)). The House Oversight Committee's 14-month investigation found the failure was managerial, not just technical: more than 300 security certificates had expired, including 79 for monitoring business-critical domains, and one expired certificate went unrenewed for 19 months, leaving Equifax blind to the data leaving its network ([House Committee on Oversight and Reform](https://oversight.house.gov/report/committee-releases-report-revealing-new-information-on-equifax-data-breach)). When the breach was announced on 7 September 2017, the website and call centres set up for affected consumers were overwhelmed ([House Committee on Oversight and Reform](https://oversight.house.gov/report/committee-releases-report-revealing-new-information-on-equifax-data-breach)). In February 2020 a federal grand jury indicted four PLA officers — Wu Zhiyong, Wang Qian, Xu Ke and Liu Lei — for stealing the data of about 145 million Americans plus Equifax's trade secrets ([Security Magazine](https://www.securitymagazine.com/articles/91687-chinese-military-hackers-charged-in-equifax-breach)).

## The bill, and the repeat offence

The settlement announced in July 2019 was the largest of its kind: up to $700 million in relief and penalties from Equifax, the CFPB, the FTC and 48 states, including up to $425 million for consumers and a $100 million civil money penalty to the CFPB, with the balance going to the states ([learncra](https://www.learncra.com/cfpb-ftc-cfpb-and-states-announce-settlement-with-equifax-over-2017-data-breach/)). Affected consumers could claim up to $20,000 each, with at least ten years of free credit monitoring ([learncra](https://www.learncra.com/cfpb-ftc-cfpb-and-states-announce-settlement-with-equifax-over-2017-data-breach/)). Britain's regulator fined the UK unit £11,164,400 in October 2023, and Equifax says it has since spent more than $1.5 billion on security ([FX News Group](https://fxnewsgroup.com/forex-news/regulatory/fca-fines-equifax-ltd-11m-for-role-in-cyber-security-breach/)). But the January 2025 CFPB order showed the dispute failures had not gone away: the Bureau found Equifax failed to investigate disputes properly, reinserted deleted information, and even sold inaccurate credit scores after "test code" reached a production scoring server ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/equifax-inc-and-equifax-information-services-llc/)). Equifax did not show the relief-rate collapse its peers did in 2025 ([ProPublica](https://qoshe.com/propublica/joel-jacobs/credit-bureaus-are-leaving-more-mistakes-on-frustrated-consumers-reports-under-trump-s-cfpb/188133152)) — because, ProPublica reported, it was under a consent order entered just before Trump's inauguration.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many people did the Equifax breach affect?** The honest answer is a range, not one number: 143 million at the initial announcement, about 147 million in the 2019 settlement, 148 million in the House Oversight report, and about 145 million victims in the DOJ indictment ([House Committee on Oversight and Reform](https://oversight.house.gov/report/committee-releases-report-revealing-new-information-on-equifax-data-breach); [learncra](https://www.learncra.com/cfpb-ftc-cfpb-and-states-announce-settlement-with-equifax-over-2017-data-breach/)). About 13.8 million UK consumers were also affected ([FX News Group](https://fxnewsgroup.com/forex-news/regulatory/fca-fines-equifax-ltd-11m-for-role-in-cyber-security-breach/)).

**Who was behind the breach?** In February 2020 a federal grand jury indicted four members of China's People's Liberation Army — Wu Zhiyong, Wang Qian, Xu Ke and Liu Lei of the PLA 54th Research Institute — for stealing the data of about 145 million Americans ([Security Magazine](https://www.securitymagazine.com/articles/91687-chinese-military-hackers-charged-in-equifax-breach)). They exploited a known Apache Struts vulnerability in Equifax's dispute portal.

**What did the $700 million settlement provide?** Up to $425 million in consumer relief and a $100 million civil money penalty to the CFPB, with the balance going to the states, plus at least ten years of free credit monitoring and six free Equifax reports a year for seven years ([learncra](https://www.learncra.com/cfpb-ftc-cfpb-and-states-announce-settlement-with-equifax-over-2017-data-breach/)). Individual consumers could claim up to $20,000 for lost time and money.

**Why was Equifax fined again in 2025?** The CFPB's January 2025 order found Equifax failed to investigate consumer disputes properly, reinserted previously deleted information, and sold inaccurate scores after "test code" reached a production scoring server ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/equifax-inc-and-equifax-information-services-llc/)). The order also found Equifax failed to block reporting of information consumers identified as resulting from identity theft. The penalty was $15 million.

**Can rent payments appear on my Equifax report?** Yes. Bilt reports members' on-time rent payments to Equifax, Experian and TransUnion free of charge, and a TransUnion study cited by Bilt found scores rose by nearly 60 points on average when rent was included ([Bilt](https://newsroom.biltrewards.com/free-rent-reporting-to-renters)). Only on-time payments are reported.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not pick one breach total as settled: it gives the range — 143 million, about 147 million, 148 million, about 145 million — with the source for each, because the sources themselves disagree ([House Committee on Oversight and Reform](https://oversight.house.gov/report/committee-releases-report-revealing-new-information-on-equifax-data-breach); [learncra](https://www.learncra.com/cfpb-ftc-cfpb-and-states-announce-settlement-with-equifax-over-2017-data-breach/)). It does not use the 147.9 million figure or the GAO's "at least 145.5 million", neither of which was re-verified from a full read in this session. It does not date the rename to 1976, the year given by the New Georgia Encyclopedia; it follows the SEC filing and the academic history, which both give 1975 ([Equifax](https://investor.equifax.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0001104659-07-036085/a07-12736_1ex99d1.htm)). It does not use Verdict's "nearly $800 million" for the US settlement, which conflicts with the verified $700 million ([Verdict](http://verdict.co.uk/uk-regulator-fines-equifax-13-4m-for-cyber-breach/)). It does not claim the four indicted PLA officers were ever arrested or tried: the indictment is the verified fact, not an outcome ([Security Magazine](https://www.securitymagazine.com/articles/91687-chinese-military-hackers-charged-in-equifax-breach)). It does not state the Bilt 2.0 migration harms as verified fact: the missed payments and damaged credit scores are reported by the Bilt profile and an ainvest analysis that were not read in full here. And it does not claim Equifax's dispute system is fixed — the January 2025 order found the same failures that the 2019 settlement was supposed to end ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/equifax-inc-and-equifax-information-services-llc/)).
