## Overview

The Harvard Kennedy School — officially the John F. Kennedy School of Government — is the public-policy school of [Harvard University](/universities/harvard-university/) in Cambridge, Massachusetts ([Harvard Kennedy School](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/about)). It states its mission as training exceptional public leaders, producing research, and working directly with policymakers and practitioners to make the world "more safe, free, just, and sustainably prosperous for all" ([Harvard Kennedy School](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/about)). What makes its story unusual is not just what it became, but how close it came to never becoming anything at all: for decades, Harvard's own leadership treated the school as an unwanted experiment, and one president wanted it closed outright ([The Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2011/10/27/GSPA-Kennedy-birthday/)).

## The Littauer gift and a reluctant founding

In 1935, Lucius N. Littauer, a Harvard College alumnus of the Class of 1878, gave Harvard two million dollars to invest in a school he hoped would lead to "the development of training in government as a profession, and not simply to education in government as a branch of learning" ([The Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2011/10/27/GSPA-Kennedy-birthday/)). Littauer imagined public servants working to rebuild America as a major element of Franklin D. Roosevelt's New Deal ([The Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2011/10/27/GSPA-Kennedy-birthday/)). The school itself dates its founding to the 1930s, when it opened as the Harvard Graduate School of Public Administration to provide an education centered on public service, public policy, and public leadership ([Harvard Kennedy School](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/about)).

Harvard president James Bryant Conant took the money and, in 1936, oversaw the establishment of the Graduate School of Public Administration (GSPA) ([The Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2011/10/27/GSPA-Kennedy-birthday/)). The school's home was the Littauer Center, named for the benefactor — and, as the Crimson's history puts it, the university then "rolled its collective eyes" ([The Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2011/10/27/GSPA-Kennedy-birthday/)).

## An unwanted experiment

Resistance was real. Former Harvard president Abbott Lawrence Lowell had explained the skepticism bluntly: Harvard had no interest in training men "for a career that does not exist, and for which, if it did exist, I think our training would very likely not be the best preparation" ([The Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2011/10/27/GSPA-Kennedy-birthday/)). The university refused to grant formal degrees to the school's students at first, and lent it just 12 professors from the Faculty of Arts and Sciences to teach in addition to their usual duties ([The Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2011/10/27/GSPA-Kennedy-birthday/)).

Degrees finally came in 1941, when Harvard began awarding GSPA students formal master's and doctoral degrees in public administration — but the university still considered the whole enterprise ceremonial, and Conant insisted that a GSPA degree was "not worth the paper it was written on" ([The Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2011/10/27/GSPA-Kennedy-birthday/)). His successor, Nathan Pusey, decided the proper remedy was to close the school entirely ([The Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2011/10/27/GSPA-Kennedy-birthday/)). The GSPA endured anyway: through the years of World War II enrollment kept growing, slowly but steadily, and a new dean was hired to spearhead a redefinition of the school ([The Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2011/10/27/GSPA-Kennedy-birthday/)).

## From GSPA to the Kennedy School

The turning point came in 1966, three years after the assassination of President John F. Kennedy. Harvard renamed the school the John F. Kennedy School of Government, and the Kennedy family worked with Harvard to found the Institute of Politics ([The Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2011/10/27/GSPA-Kennedy-birthday/)). During this period the school began its transition from an overlooked offshoot of the Faculty of Arts and Sciences into a separate school, respected in its own right ([The Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2011/10/27/GSPA-Kennedy-birthday/)). The school still frames its identity around Kennedy's famous summons to service: its about page issues "a call to public service" in which faculty, staff, students, and alumni are heard asking "what they can do" to make a positive impact on their communities ([Harvard Kennedy School](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/about)).

## Programs and research today

The modern school offers four master's programs: the two-year Master in Public Policy (MPP), the two-year Master in Public Administration (MPA), the one-year Mid-Career MPA (a summer program plus two semesters of coursework), and the two-year Master in Public Administration in International Development (MPA/ID) ([Harvard Kennedy School](https://hks.harvard.edu/lead-with-hks-masters)). The MPP's first year centers on a cross-disciplinary core — economics, ethics, leadership, negotiation, politics, policy design and delivery, quantitative analysis — and ends with the Spring Exercise, a two-week simulation applying the tools to a real-time policy issue; the second year closes with a Policy Area of Concentration and the Policy Analysis Exercise thesis ([Harvard Kennedy School](https://hks.harvard.edu/lead-with-hks-masters)).

Admission is selective and career-weighted: MPP applicants need a bachelor's degree and demonstrated quantitative proficiency (undergraduate-level economics, statistics, or calculus), and incoming MPP students typically bring two to three years of professional work experience ([Harvard Kennedy School](https://hks.harvard.edu/lead-with-hks-masters)). The MPA asks for three years of work experience plus prior graduate-level coursework; the Mid-Career MPA asks for at least seven years; the MPA/ID demands microeconomics, macroeconomics, and multivariable calculus before enrollment ([Harvard Kennedy School](https://hks.harvard.edu/lead-with-hks-masters)). The school reports that 39 percent of enrolled students received Harvard or HKS financial aid for the 2025–2026 academic year ([Harvard Kennedy School](https://hks.harvard.edu/lead-with-hks-masters)). Beyond teaching, the school is dedicated to advancing public policy and governance through 12 research centers that conduct research, publish findings, and run fellowships and events ([Harvard Kennedy School](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/about)). "Since its founding," the school says, "HKS has stayed faithful to its mission to improve public policy and leadership so people can live in safer, freer, more just, and more prosperous societies" ([Harvard Kennedy School](https://www.hks.harvard.edu/about)).

## A noted alumnus

One graduate who has figured prominently in American public life is [Pete Hegseth](/people/pete-hegseth/), who earned a master's degree in public policy from Harvard University's Harvard Kennedy School of Government, according to his Fox News biography ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/11/13/who-is-pete-hegseth-the-pro-israel-fox-news-host-picked-to-head-pentagon)). Hegseth, a former Fox News host, was chosen by President-elect Donald Trump to lead the Department of Defense ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/11/13/who-is-pete-hegseth-the-pro-israel-fox-news-host-picked-to-head-pentagon)).
