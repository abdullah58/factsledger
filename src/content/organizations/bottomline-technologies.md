## Overview

Bottomline Technologies spent 33 years trying to kill the paper check — and ended as a textbook private-equity take-private. Founded in Exeter, New Hampshire in 1989 by Daniel McGurl, with co-founder Jim Loomis shaping product strategy and culture, the company was built on the then-novel idea that businesses did not need pre-cut paper checks to make payments and could use an electronic automated system instead ([NH Business Review](https://www.nhbr.com/portsmouth-based-bottomline-technologies-sold-to-private-equity-firm/)). It went public in 1999 at $19 a share and spent 23 years as a listed fintech before Thoma Bravo's $2.6 billion all-cash acquisition closed on May 13, 2022, at $57 a share — ending its run on Nasdaq ([NH Business Review](https://www.nhbr.com/portsmouth-based-bottomline-technologies-sold-to-private-equity-firm/); [NH Business Review](https://www.nhbr.com/private-equity-firm-completes-deal-for-bottomline-technologies/)). The take-private is independently verified across multiple reports: announced December 2021, overwhelmingly approved by shareholders on March 8, 2022, and completed that May ([NH Business Review](https://www.nhbr.com/private-equity-firm-completes-deal-for-bottomline-technologies/); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/newsletter/thoma-bravo-takes-bottomline-private-for-26b)).

## What Bottomline sold

By the time of the sale, Bottomline had grown far beyond check replacement into a diversified B2B financial-technology provider: payments and cash management, fraud and financial-crime management, securities management, and financial-documentation automation for financial institutions worldwide ([Electronic Payments International](https://www.electronicpaymentsinternational.com/news/thoma-bravo-to-buy-bottomline/)). Its customer list included UK digital bank Starling, [Revolut](/organizations/revolut/), Hargenant Group and TD Bank, and in November 2021 it had closed the purchase of Bora Payment Systems, adding bank channel relationships to its Paymode-X electronic payment network ([Electronic Payments International](https://www.electronicpaymentsinternational.com/news/thoma-bravo-to-buy-bottomline/)). The company employed roughly 2,000 people worldwide — about a quarter of them at its 100,000-square-foot, four-story headquarters at the Pease International Tradeport in Portsmouth, built in 2000 — with additional offices across the U.S., Europe and Asia-Pacific ([NH Business Review](https://www.nhbr.com/portsmouth-based-bottomline-technologies-sold-to-private-equity-firm/)).

## The deal and the day-one CEO swap

The transaction's shape was classic late-stage private equity. Thoma Bravo — the San Francisco software-focused firm with more than $91 billion under management — agreed to pay $57 in cash per share, a 42% premium to the price Bottomline stock traded at before the company announced it was exploring a sale, and a 41% premium to its 30-day volume-weighted average price on October 19, 2021 ([NH Business Review](https://www.nhbr.com/portsmouth-based-bottomline-technologies-sold-to-private-equity-firm/); [Electronic Payments International](https://www.electronicpaymentsinternational.com/news/thoma-bravo-to-buy-bottomline/)). Bloomberg broke the news of the possible sale and Barron's confirmed it, after Bottomline had hired Deutsche Bank to explore strategic options ([NH Business Review](https://www.nhbr.com/portsmouth-based-bottomline-technologies-sold-to-private-equity-firm/)). At the announcement, Eberle called the deal "an exciting next chapter" that would bring resources to build on the company's leadership position and accelerate go-to-market efforts, while Thoma Bravo managing partner Holden Spaht pointed to the large and growing B2B payments market as the opportunity ([NH Business Review](https://www.nhbr.com/portsmouth-based-bottomline-technologies-sold-to-private-equity-firm/)).

NHBR's account of the closing captures the PE playbook in miniature. Three days before the deal closed, Bottomline filed its last quarterly report as a public company: $795 million in assets ($118 million in cash) against $379 million in liabilities, leaving $417 million in equity; the quarter brought $132 million in revenue — nearly all subscriptions, up 12% year over year — but a $7.5 million net loss, wider than the prior year's $4.8 million ([NH Business Review](https://www.nhbr.com/private-equity-firm-completes-deal-for-bottomline-technologies/)). Fiscal-year-to-date the company had lost $29 million while paying executives $42 million in stock-based compensation; CEO Rob Eberle alone had been paid $6.4 million the prior fiscal year, $5.8 million of it in stock awards ([NH Business Review](https://www.nhbr.com/private-equity-firm-completes-deal-for-bottomline-technologies/)). One of Thoma Bravo's first acts was to replace Eberle — CEO since 2006, at the company since 1998 — with Craig Saks, formerly of ACI Worldwide, whose SEC filing noted an "existing relationship with Thoma Bravo" without elaborating ([NH Business Review](https://www.nhbr.com/private-equity-firm-completes-deal-for-bottomline-technologies/)). Eberle stayed on as a strategic advisor through June 30 for a $34,476 monthly consulting fee ([NH Business Review](https://www.nhbr.com/private-equity-firm-completes-deal-for-bottomline-technologies/)).
