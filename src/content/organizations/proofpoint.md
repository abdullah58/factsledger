## Overview

Proofpoint is a Sunnyvale, California-based cybersecurity company founded in June 2002 by Eric Hahn, the former chief technology officer of Netscape Communications; it launched the following year after raising a $7 million Series A round, initially backed by Benchmark Capital and Stanford University ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2021/04/26/security-vendor-proofpoint-snapped-up-by-private-equity-for-123bn-but-still-in-search-of-profit/1502042?td=keepreading-four_with)). It went public in April 2012, raising more than $80 million, and built its business on "people-centric" email security and compliance — protecting organizations' people, in its framing, rather than just their networks ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2021/04/26/security-vendor-proofpoint-snapped-up-by-private-equity-for-123bn-but-still-in-search-of-profit/1502042?td=keepreading-four_with)).

On August 31, 2021, private equity firm Thoma Bravo completed a $12.3 billion all-cash acquisition of Proofpoint at $176 per share, and the company's stock ceased trading on Nasdaq ([GovCon Wire](https://www.govconwire.com/articles/thoma-bravo-closes-12-3b-cash-buy-of-proofpoint)).

## A $12.3 billion buyout of a company still losing money

The deal's striking feature, as The Register noted in its headline, was that Thoma Bravo paid a 34% premium for a company "still in search of profit." In its first-quarter 2021 results, Proofpoint reported revenue of $287.8 million, up 15% year over year, with gross profits of $214.3 million — but an operating loss of $42.1 million, "not much changed from a year earlier" ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2021/04/26/security-vendor-proofpoint-snapped-up-by-private-equity-for-123bn-but-still-in-search-of-profit/1502042?td=keepreading-four_with)). CEO Gary Steele framed going private as a path to agility: the move would let the firm invest "outside of the glare of Wall Street moneymen and their quarterly expectations," in The Register's gloss ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2021/04/26/security-vendor-proofpoint-snapped-up-by-private-equity-for-123bn-but-still-in-search-of-profit/1502042?td=keepreading-four_with)).

[Reuters](/organizations/reuters/) reported the April 26, 2021 announcement as part of a pandemic-era surge: remote working had boosted demand for network security just as cybersecurity industry funding hit an all-time high ([Reuters](https://ktwb.com/2021/04/26/thoma-bravo-to-take-cybersecurity-firm-proofpoint-private-in-12-3-billion-deal/)). Shareholders were offered $176 per share, about 34% above the prior close, with the transaction expected to close in the third quarter ([Reuters](https://ktwb.com/2021/04/26/thoma-bravo-to-take-cybersecurity-firm-proofpoint-private-in-12-3-billion-deal/)).

## One piece of a private-equity security rollup

Thoma Bravo was not buying its first security vendor. The $35 billion private equity firm had bought British encryption and antivirus company Sophos for £3 billion in February 2020, held a stake in [McAfee](/organizations/mcafee/), owned equity in identity-management vendor SailPoint, and had previously owned certificate specialist Entrust before selling it to Datacard ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2021/04/26/security-vendor-proofpoint-snapped-up-by-private-equity-for-123bn-but-still-in-search-of-profit/1502042?td=keepreading-four_with)). Proofpoint was the largest piece yet in that infosec landgrab — and its credentials had recently been dented: 2018 research by rival Mimecast found that Proofpoint's servers, along with Microsoft's, had missed malware attachments, impersonation attempts, and malicious links in a sample of 15,656 emails drawn from 142 million ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2021/04/26/security-vendor-proofpoint-snapped-up-by-private-equity-for-123bn-but-still-in-search-of-profit/1502042?td=keepreading-four_with)).
