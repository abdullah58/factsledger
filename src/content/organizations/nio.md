## Overview

NIO Inc. is a Chinese maker of premium smart electric vehicles, founded in Shanghai in November 2014 by entrepreneur William Li, initially under the name NextEV ([NIO Inc.](https://www.nio.com/news/nio-inc-announces-pricing-initial-public-offering)). It sells cars under the NIO brand and, since 2024, the family-oriented ONVO brand, with the Firefly brand following in 2025 — and it is defined less by any single model than by its bet that drivers should swap batteries instead of charging them: by the third quarter of 2024 its network had grown from 36 stations to 2,737 ([Motley Fool](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ultimate-ev-stock-buy-500-right-now); [NIO Inc.](https://www.stockwatch.com/News/Item/U-z9398540-U!NIO-20250321/U/NIO)). In 2024 it delivered a record 221,970 vehicles and took in ¥65.7 billion in revenue — and still lost ¥22.4 billion ([NIO Inc.](https://www.stockwatch.com/News/Item/U-z9398540-U!NIO-20250321/U/NIO)).

## Founded 2014, listed 2018

NIO's early line-up centred on the ES8, a seven-seater electric SUV; deliveries began in China in June 2018, and the five-seater ES6 was planned for late that year ([NIO Inc.](https://www.nio.com/news/nio-inc-announces-pricing-initial-public-offering)). Three months later, on 12 September 2018, NIO priced its New York Stock Exchange IPO at $6.26 per American depositary share, raising about $1.0 billion from 160 million shares, and began trading under the symbol "NIO" ([NIO Inc.](https://www.nio.com/news/nio-inc-announces-pricing-initial-public-offering)). The listing put NIO on the map as [China](/places/china/)'s answer to Tesla; the share price later told the rest of the story, rising tenfold from the $6.26 IPO price to a record $62.84 on 9 February 2021, then falling below $5 by late 2024 as growth slowed, losses mounted and interest rates rose ([Motley Fool](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ultimate-ev-stock-buy-500-right-now)).

## The Hefei rescue

NIO's position in 2019–2020 was precarious: deliveries were still small — 20,565 vehicles in 2019 — and the rescue came from a city government rather than the markets ([Motley Fool](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ultimate-ev-stock-buy-500-right-now); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/29/ev-startup-nio-secures-1-billion-investment-from-china-entities/)). On 29 April 2020 NIO secured a $1 billion (¥7 billion) investment from state-owned companies in Hefei — Hefei City Construction and Investment Holding Group, CMG-SDIC Capital and Anhui Provincial Emerging Industry Investment Co. — in return for establishing its headquarters in Hefei's Economic and Technological Development Area and giving up a stake in a business unit ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/29/ev-startup-nio-secures-1-billion-investment-from-china-entities/)). The money went into NIO China, a recently established unit: the investors would hold 24.1% and NIO 75.9%, while NIO put its core Chinese businesses and assets — research and development, supply chain and its power division — into the new company ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/29/ev-startup-nio-secures-1-billion-investment-from-china-entities/)). NIO's factory was already in Hefei, operated with Anhui Jianghuai Automobile Group (JAC); its headquarters and other operations were in Shanghai, about 300 miles away ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/29/ev-startup-nio-secures-1-billion-investment-from-china-entities/)).

## Battery swapping as the differentiator

NIO's answer to range anxiety is to change the battery rather than charge it. A driver pulls into a swap station and leaves minutes later with a fully charged pack, either as a one-off or under a Battery-as-a-Service subscription that separates battery cost from the car's purchase price ([Motley Fool](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ultimate-ev-stock-buy-500-right-now)). The network grew from just 36 stations at the end of 2019 to 2,737 stations at the end of the third quarter of 2024 ([Motley Fool](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ultimate-ev-stock-buy-500-right-now)). The economics are still unproven: stations perform an average of 30–40 swaps per day, and NIO expects them to break even only at 60–70 swaps per day ([Motley Fool](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ultimate-ev-stock-buy-500-right-now)).

Deliveries, meanwhile, grew almost every year — but so did the losses. Vehicle margin, which peaked at a record 20.2% in 2021, fell to 9.5% in 2023 as China's EV price war, sharpened by Tesla's price cuts, squeezed every premium maker ([Motley Fool](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ultimate-ev-stock-buy-500-right-now)).

## Three brands, still loss-making

The 2024 results show a company growing fast and losing money fast in equal measure. Deliveries reached 221,970, up 38.7% on 2023, including 19,929 from the new ONVO brand in the fourth quarter alone; total revenue rose 18.2% to ¥65,731.6 million ($9,005.2 million), vehicle margin recovered to 12.3% from 9.5%, and gross margin nearly doubled to 9.9% ([NIO Inc.](https://www.stockwatch.com/News/Item/U-z9398540-U!NIO-20250321/U/NIO)). Yet the net loss widened 8.1% to ¥22,401.7 million ($3,069.0 million), and NIO ended the year with ¥41.9 billion in cash and equivalents, short-term investments and long-term time deposits ([NIO Inc.](https://www.stockwatch.com/News/Item/U-z9398540-U!NIO-20250321/U/NIO)). Cumulative deliveries reached 698,619 by 28 February 2025 ([NIO Inc.](https://www.stockwatch.com/News/Item/U-z9398540-U!NIO-20250321/U/NIO)).

NIO's own account of its position is that the premium brand held its ground: in 2024 the NIO brand "maintained its position as the leader in China's BEV market for vehicles priced over RMB300,000, capturing a 40% market share", while the ONVO L60 held a top-three position in the RMB200,000–300,000 battery-electric SUV market ([NIO Inc.](https://www.stockwatch.com/News/Item/U-z9398540-U!NIO-20250321/U/NIO)). Firefly, the small-car brand aimed at international expansion, was to launch and begin deliveries in April 2025 ([NIO Inc.](https://www.stockwatch.com/News/Item/U-z9398540-U!NIO-20250321/U/NIO)). The strategy is clear — premium at home, cheaper brands for volume, Firefly for Europe — but the 2024 accounts show the premium business alone still cannot pay for the swap network, the service ecosystem and the three-brand expansion at once.

## The Cerence thread: voice AI from Shanghai to Norway

NIO's link to [Cerence](/organizations/cerence/) is the oldest in this batch and the most directly tied to going abroad. On 8 October 2020 Cerence announced that NIO had selected it for collaboration to deploy voice technologies in its products ([Cerence Inc.](https://investors.cerence.com/_assets/_64418cb86bd93820a0fc3d86daf9e797/cerence/news/2020-10-08_NIO_Selects_Cerence_for_Collaboration_on_Voice_296.pdf)). The deal came a year after Cerence became an independent company, and at the time Cerence described its track record as more than 325 million cars on the road with its technology ([Cerence Inc.](https://investors.cerence.com/_assets/_64418cb86bd93820a0fc3d86daf9e797/cerence/news/2020-10-08_NIO_Selects_Cerence_for_Collaboration_on_Voice_296.pdf)).

The collaboration's first visible product was export-shaped: Cerence's conversational voice technology was integrated into the ES8 full-size SUV for the Norwegian market, running on the Cerence Automotive Platform with natural-language understanding and text-to-speech, on-board and in the cloud ([Mobile ID World](https://mobileidworld.com/nio-brings-cerences-voice-technology-new-electric-suv-011104/)). NIO's own in-car assistant, NOMI, speaks Chinese at home; the Cerence-powered version speaks English and Norwegian, which is what a Chinese premium brand needs if it is going to sell cars in Scandinavia ([Mobile ID World](https://mobileidworld.com/nio-brings-cerences-voice-technology-new-electric-suv-011104/)). The same report notes Cerence had by then supplied voice technology to [Geely](/organizations/geely/) Auto Group, XPeng, Great Wall Motors and Beijing Electric Vehicle as well ([Mobile ID World](https://mobileidworld.com/nio-brings-cerences-voice-technology-new-electric-suv-011104/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was NIO founded?** In November 2014, as NextEV, by William Li; it took the NIO name later and listed in New York in September 2018 ([NIO Inc.](https://www.nio.com/news/nio-inc-announces-pricing-initial-public-offering)).

**What is battery swapping?** NIO's stations replace a depleted battery with a fully charged one in minutes, instead of charging the car; drivers can buy the car without the battery and pay a monthly Battery-as-a-Service subscription instead ([Motley Fool](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ultimate-ev-stock-buy-500-right-now)).

**How did NIO survive 2020?** State investors in Hefei put ¥7 billion (about $1 billion) into NIO China for a 24.1% stake, and NIO moved its Chinese headquarters to Hefei ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/29/ev-startup-nio-secures-1-billion-investment-from-china-entities/)).

**Is NIO profitable?** No. It delivered a record 221,970 vehicles in 2024 on revenue of ¥65.7 billion, but its net loss widened to ¥22.4 billion ([NIO Inc.](https://www.stockwatch.com/News/Item/U-z9398540-U!NIO-20250321/U/NIO)).

**What is NIO's link to Cerence?** NIO selected Cerence for voice-technology collaboration in October 2020, and Cerence's platform powers the NOMI assistant in the Norwegian ES8 ([Cerence Inc.](https://investors.cerence.com/_assets/_64418cb86bd93820a0fc3d86daf9e797/cerence/news/2020-10-08_NIO_Selects_Cerence_for_Collaboration_on_Voice_296.pdf); [Mobile ID World](https://mobileidworld.com/nio-brings-cerences-voice-technology-new-electric-suv-011104/)).
