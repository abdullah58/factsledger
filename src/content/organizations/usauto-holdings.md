# USAuto Holdings

**USAuto Holdings, Inc. was a privately held, Delaware-incorporated
holding company for USAuto Insurance Company — a Tennessee-based
retailer, servicer, and underwriter of non-standard automobile
insurance, the market segment covering drivers standard carriers reject
or price out.** [Liberté Investors, Inc.](/organizations/liberte-investors/) ([NYSE](/organizations/new-york-stock-exchange/): LBI) agreed to acquire it
for $76 million in cash plus stock on December 15, 2003, and the merger
closed April 30, 2004, creating First Acceptance Corporation.

## The company

USAuto's underwriting arm, USAuto Insurance Company, was organized in
Tennessee in 1995, co-founded by [Stephen J. Harrison](/people/stephen-j-harrison/). By the time of
the 2003 deal the business traded through company-owned retail agencies
under the "Acceptance" brand. The voting agreement tied to the merger
identifies USAuto Holdings, Inc. as a [Delaware](/places/delaware/) corporation headquartered
at 3813 Green Hills Village Drive, Nashville, Tennessee.

## The 2003–2004 Liberté merger

On December 15, 2003, Liberté Investors announced a definitive merger
agreement: it would acquire 100% of USAuto's stock for $76 million in
cash and 13.25 million newly issued Liberté shares, with up to 750,000
more shares payable if USAuto hit 2004 operating performance criteria.
The cash was to be funded from a new equity issuance plus existing
cash. Liberté chairman [Gerald J. Ford](/people/gerald-j-ford/) (then a 45% stockholder) agreed to
vote his shares in favor, and [Morgan Joseph & Co.](/organizations/morgan-joseph/) served as financial
advisor, rendering a fairness opinion to Liberté's board.

On closing — April 30, 2004 — Liberté changed its name to First
Acceptance Corporation and moved its headquarters from Dallas to
Nashville. The changing of the guard was wholesale: Stephen J. Harrison
(USAuto's CEO) replaced [Donald J. Edwards](/people/donald-j-edwards/) as CEO; [Thomas M. Harrison](/people/thomas-m-harrison/)
(USAuto's COO) became Executive Vice President; Ford remained Chairman;
and Edwards stayed a director with a four-year advisory relationship.

## FAQ

**Is USAuto Holdings the same as the bankrupt Iowa "Acceptance
Insurance" company?**
No. USAuto Holdings was the Nashville, Tennessee holding company
acquired by Liberté in 2004, forming First Acceptance Corporation.
Acceptance Insurance Companies Inc. of Council Bluffs, Iowa is a
different SEC registrant that filed Chapter 11 in 2005. The shared
"Acceptance" branding is a naming coincidence worth flagging, not a
corporate relationship.

**Is this USAA or "USA Auto Insurance"?**
No. It is not USAA, not the modern low-down-payment "USA Auto
Insurance," not United Automobile Insurance Company (UAIC), and not The
General.

**How big was the deal?**
$76 million in cash plus 13.25 million shares of Liberté, with up to
750,000 additional earn-out shares — a cash-and-stock merger, not a
distressed sale.
