## Overview

EliseAI is a New York-based artificial intelligence company that automates the administrative front desk of two industries — housing and healthcare — founded in 2017 by Minna Song and Tony Stoyanov ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/14/eliseais-chatbots-for-property-owners-nets-it-75m-in-funding/)). Its chatbots text, email and call renters about apartment tours, maintenance requests, lease renewals and delinquencies, and its healthcare tools handle patient intake, scheduling, insurance checks and follow-ups ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/a16z-backed-eliseai-raises-350m-doubles-valuation-to-4b/)). The name invites confusion with ELIZA — the 1964–1966 MIT chatbot by Joseph Weizenbaum, known as the first chatbot in history — but the two are entirely unrelated ([Weizenbaum Institut](http://www.weizenbaum-institut.de/w-100/exhibition-eliza/)).

## The admin-assistant origin

Song and Stoyanov met as undergraduates at Cambridge, but the company came from fieldwork. After graduating, Song moved to New York and took a job as an administrative assistant at a residential real-estate firm — where she watched messaging inefficiencies between management and tenants grind teams toward burnout ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/14/eliseais-chatbots-for-property-owners-nets-it-75m-in-funding/)). "Recognizing this challenge, Stoyanov and I began creating AI software to automate communication," she told TechCrunch — and they founded EliseAI in 2017 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/14/eliseais-chatbots-for-property-owners-nets-it-75m-in-funding/)).

The bet scaled fast. By August 2024 EliseAI had raised $75 million in a Series D led by Sapphire Ventures at a $1 billion valuation, served 350+ customers including 70% of the top 50 US rental-housing operators, and employed around 150 people ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/14/eliseais-chatbots-for-property-owners-nets-it-75m-in-funding/)). In 2023 it had already begun its second act — HealthAI, adapted for clinics' scheduling and billing — betting that the same conversational-AI stack could serve healthcare's front office ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/08/14/eliseais-chatbots-for-property-owners-nets-it-75m-in-funding/)).

## $4 billion for the boring work

On September 29, 2026, EliseAI announced a $350 million raise co-led by Andreessen Horowitz and Bessemer Ventures at a $4 billion valuation — roughly double its Series E of the previous August ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/a16z-backed-eliseai-raises-350m-doubles-valuation-to-4b/)). The company said its software runs in one in six US apartments and that it had passed $200 million in annual recurring revenue that summer ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/a16z-backed-eliseai-raises-350m-doubles-valuation-to-4b/)). Weeks earlier it had launched Apollo, an AI "teammate" built into the EliseAI platform to execute tasks — maintenance requests, lease renewals — inside the property software teams already use ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/a16z-backed-eliseai-raises-350m-doubles-valuation-to-4b/)). Song's pitch: housing and healthcare are "two of the largest expenses for American households," and EliseAI automates the paperwork nobody else wants ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/29/a16z-backed-eliseai-raises-350m-doubles-valuation-to-4b/)).
