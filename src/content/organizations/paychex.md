## Overview

Paychex, Inc. is a Rochester, New York-based human capital management company that sells payroll, HR, benefits and insurance services to about 800,000 mostly small and mid-sized clients. Founded by B. Thomas Golisano in 1971 with $3,000, it pays one out of every 11 American private-sector workers and reported $5.6 billion in revenue in its fiscal year ended May 31, 2025 ([Paychex Form 8-K, 2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000950103-25-006123/dp228939_ex9901.htm); [Paychex Form 10-K, FY2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/section-16-filings/content/0000950170-25-095300/0000950170-25-095300.pdf)).

Paychex is a Delaware corporation, formed in 1979 through the consolidation of 17 corporations that provided computerized payroll accounting services, and its shares trade on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol PAYX ([Paychex Form 10-K, FY2003](http://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000950152-03-007265/l01352ae10vk.htm); [Paychex Form 10-K, FY2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/section-16-filings/content/0000950170-25-095300/0000950170-25-095300.pdf)). Its headquarters remain in Rochester, where Golisano started the business, and its fiscal year ends on May 31 ([Paychex Form 10-K, FY2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/section-16-filings/content/0000950170-25-095300/0000950170-25-095300.pdf)). The company describes its strategy as becoming the digitally driven HR leader for its clients, and more than half of its revenue now comes from services other than payroll processing ([Paychex Form 10-K, FY2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/section-16-filings/content/0000950170-25-095300/0000950170-25-095300.pdf)).

## From a $3,000 start to a public company

Golisano founded the business in 1971 with just $3,000 and the idea of making payroll outsourcing easy and affordable for small businesses, starting with one employee and 40 clients in Rochester ([Rochester Institute of Technology](https://www.rit.edu/archives/golisano/tom-golisano); [Paychex Form 8-K, 2004](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000950152-04-007179/l09766aexv99w1.txt)). The company first grew through partnerships and franchises across the country, and in 1979 Golisano consolidated them into a single private company ([Paychex Form 8-K, 2004](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000950152-04-007179/l09766aexv99w1.txt)). That consolidation — 17 separate corporations folded into one [Delaware](/places/delaware/) company — is the least-told part of the Paychex origin story: most profiles jump straight from the 1971 founding to the public listing. Paychex went public in 1983 and its shares trade on [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/) ([Paychex Form 8-K, 2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000950103-25-006123/dp228939_ex9901.htm)).

The company grew quickly. By the end of fiscal 2003 Paychex served about 490,000 clients and employed about 8,850 people from more than 100 offices, and a year later it passed 500,000 clients with revenue of nearly $1.3 billion ([Paychex Form 10-K, FY2003](http://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000950152-03-007265/l01352ae10vk.htm); [Paychex Form 8-K, 2004](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000950152-04-007179/l09766aexv99w1.txt)). Golisano ran the company as president and chief executive from 1971 until October 2004, when he handed the role to Jonathan J. Judge, a 25-year [IBM](/organizations/ibm/) veteran who had run IBM's Personal Computing Division and then Crystal Decisions ([Rochester Institute of Technology](https://www.rit.edu/archives/golisano/tom-golisano); [Paychex Form 8-K, 2004](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000950152-04-007179/l09766aexv99w1.txt)). Martin Mucci, who joined Paychex in 2002, became chief executive in 2010 and chairman in 2021, and John B. Gibson — at Paychex since 2013 — succeeded him as president and chief executive on October 14, 2022 ([Paychex Form 8-K, 2022](http://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000950170-22-017720/0000950170-22-017720.pdf)). Golisano stayed on the board until July 9, 2025, when he stepped down at age 83, ending a 54-year association with the company he founded ([Paychex Form 8-K, 2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000950103-25-006123/dp228939_ex9901.htm)).

## What Paychex sells, and how big it is

Paychex sells a full suite of human capital management services that follows the employee life cycle from hire to retire: payroll processing and tax administration, HR software and advice, benefits administration, retirement services, insurance, and HR outsourcing through administrative services and professional employer organization (PEO) arrangements, in which Paychex acts as a co-employer ([Paychex Form 10-K, FY2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/section-16-filings/content/0000950170-25-095300/0000950170-25-095300.pdf)). It delivers these through three technology platforms: SurePayroll for digitally driven small businesses that want self-service, Paychex Flex as its proprietary software-as-a-service platform for small and mid-sized businesses, and Paycor as its primary platform for larger businesses with more complex needs ([Paychex Form 10-K, FY2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/section-16-filings/content/0000950170-25-095300/0000950170-25-095300.pdf)). The company says it is the largest 401(k) recordkeeper for small businesses in the United States ([Paychex Form 10-K, FY2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/section-16-filings/content/0000950170-25-095300/0000950170-25-095300.pdf)).

Scale is the story the numbers tell. In fiscal 2025 Paychex reported total revenue of $5,571.7 million, up 6% on the year, with net income of $1,657.3 million and diluted earnings per share of $4.58 ([Paychex Form 10-K, FY2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/section-16-filings/content/0000950170-25-095300/0000950170-25-095300.pdf)). Management Solutions brought in $4,067.1 million and PEO and Insurance Solutions $1,342.9 million, and the company employed about 19,000 people, including roughly 250 compliance experts and more than 650 HR business professionals ([Paychex Form 10-K, FY2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/section-16-filings/content/0000950170-25-095300/0000950170-25-095300.pdf)). The most striking single statistic is the payroll reach: Paychex pays one out of every 11 American private-sector workers, up from one in 12 in 2018 and 2022 — a concrete measure of how much of the country's small-business payroll flows through one Rochester company, and why its client-retention rate of 82–83% matters so much to its results ([Paychex Form 8-K, 2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000950103-25-006123/dp228939_ex9901.htm); [Paychex Form 10-K, FY2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/section-16-filings/content/0000950170-25-095300/0000950170-25-095300.pdf); [Paychex Form 8-K, 2018](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/723531/000072353118000043/payx-20181126xex99_1.htm)).

Distribution leans heavily on referrals: more than half of Paychex's payroll clients come through accountants, benefit brokers and banks, and Paychex is the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants' preferred payroll provider under a partnership running through September 2027 ([Paychex Form 10-K, FY2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/section-16-filings/content/0000950170-25-095300/0000950170-25-095300.pdf)). The company also publishes a monthly Paychex Small Business Employment Watch tracking small-business hiring and wages ([Paychex Form 10-K, FY2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/section-16-filings/content/0000950170-25-095300/0000950170-25-095300.pdf)). On recognition, Paychex was named one of the World's Most Ethical Companies by Ethisphere 17 times consecutively since 2012 — one of only three companies to achieve that — and was inducted into Training magazine's Hall of Fame in 2025 ([Paychex Form 10-K, FY2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/section-16-filings/content/0000950170-25-095300/0000950170-25-095300.pdf)).

## Buying growth: the acquisition trail

Paychex grew first by selling and then, from the 2000s on, by buying. In September 2002 it acquired Advantage Payroll Services for $314 million in cash, and in April 2003 it bought InterPay, a unit of FleetBoston Financial, for $182 million in cash; together the two deals added more than 82,000 clients and opened geographic areas Paychex had not served before ([Paychex Form 10-K, FY2003](http://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000950152-03-007265/l01352ae10vk.htm)). In November 2018 it agreed to buy Oasis Outsourcing, the largest privately owned professional employer organization in the country, for $1.2 billion in cash, taking its HR outsourcing business past 1.4 million worksite employees ([Paychex Form 8-K, 2018](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/723531/000072353118000043/payx-20181126xex99_1.htm)).

The biggest deal came in 2025. On January 7, 2025, Paychex announced it would acquire Paycor HCM, a Cincinnati-based provider of cloud payroll and talent software serving nearly 50,000 small and mid-sized businesses, for about $4.1 billion in cash at $22.50 a share ([Reuters](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025:newsml_L4N3O313Y:0-paychex-to-buy-rival-paycor-in-4-1-bln-deal-to-expand-ai-driven-hr-products/)). The deal closed on April 14, 2025, and chief executive John Gibson said it strengthened Paychex's position upmarket and unlocked new revenue opportunities ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/acquisitions/2025/paychex-acquires-paycor-in-4-1-billion-deal/?ref=biztoc.com)). Reuters called it the largest deal in Paychex's history, aimed at broadening its artificial-intelligence capabilities and consolidating market share ([Reuters](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025:newsml_L4N3O313Y:0-paychex-to-buy-rival-paycor-in-4-1-bln-deal-to-expand-ai-driven-hr-products/)). The market Paychex competes in is highly competitive and fragmented, with one primary national competitor, according to its own 10-K ([Paychex Form 10-K, FY2025](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/section-16-filings/content/0000950170-25-095300/0000950170-25-095300.pdf)). Smaller rival [Asure Software](/organizations/asure-software/) competes against giants like Paychex on small-business relevance and payroll-tax expertise rather than product breadth, according to an [Umbrex](/organizations/umbrex/) strategy profile ([Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/company-profiles/asure-software/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Paychex?**
