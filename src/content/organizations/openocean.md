## Overview

OpenOcean is a pan-European early-stage venture capital firm, based in Helsinki with a [London](/places/london/) office, that invests in data-driven B2B software — the data economy, enterprise software, AI and data infrastructure, and open source. It was founded in 2011 by Michael "Monty" Widenius, Patrik Backman and Ralf Wahlsten, the creators of MySQL and MariaDB, with Tom Henriksson, who had invested in both ([OpenOcean](https://www.openocean.vc/articles/data-ecosystems-pioneer-openocean-raises-eu92m-fund-for-data-solutions-and-software)). OpenOcean led [Booksy](/organizations/booksy/)'s $4.2 million Series A in March 2017, and it is the only Booksy investor founded by the people who built one of the most widely used databases in the world ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2017/03/03/booksy-series-a/)).

## From MySQL to venture capital

The firm's origin is its credential. MySQL, created by Widenius and colleagues, was sold to Sun Microsystems for $1 billion in 2008, and the team then built MariaDB Corporation, now listed on the NYSE ([OpenOcean](https://www.openocean.vc/articles/data-ecosystems-pioneer-openocean-raises-eu92m-fund-for-data-solutions-and-software)). MySQL was part of the LAMP stack and, in the firm's own words, "the first commercially successful open source project and one of the first technology unicorns from Europe" ([OpenOcean](https://www.openocean.vc/articles/data-ecosystems-pioneer-openocean-raises-eu92m-fund-for-data-solutions-and-software)). The founders turned operator experience into an investment thesis: back European companies driving the data economy, and lead or co-lead their Series A rounds ([OpenOcean](https://www.openocean.vc/articles/data-ecosystems-pioneer-openocean-raises-eu92m-fund-for-data-solutions-and-software)).

The firm is unusual in one respect it makes much of: its partners are founders themselves. OpenOcean notes that only 8% of European general partners have founder experience — a claim stated here as the firm's own positioning, not as an independently verified fact ([TechFundingNews](https://techfundingnews.com/openocean-targets-e130m-fund-bridging-europes-ai-investment-gap/)).

## Four funds

OpenOcean's fund ladder is the firm's public record:

| Fund | Vintage | Size | Limited partners |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fund I | 2011 | €45m | Not stated |
| Fund II | 2015 | €80m | Not stated |
| Fund III ("Fund 2020") | 2021 | €92m first close; €130m target | European Investment Fund, Tesi, Elo Mutual Pension Insurance, Fund of Fund Growth, Oxford Corpus Christi College |
| Fund IV | 2024–2025 | €100m first close; €130m target | Isomer Capital, KRR Fund of Funds, ELO, European Investment Fund |

The third fund's €92 million first close was announced in February 2021, targeting a €130 million final close in the first half of that year ([OpenOcean](https://www.openocean.vc/articles/data-ecosystems-pioneer-openocean-raises-eu92m-fund-for-data-solutions-and-software)). The fourth fund's €100 million first close came in 2024, targeting a €130 million final close in early 2025, with first investments in Embeddable (customer-facing analytics) and Dreamfold (AI protein search and design), and a plan for 18–20 companies, primarily at seed ([TechFundingNews](https://techfundingnews.com/openocean-targets-e130m-fund-bridging-europes-ai-investment-gap/)). Ekaterina Almasque was appointed General Partner in February 2021, leading the London team and operations; she had previously worked at the Samsung Catalyst Fund and led investments in IQM and Sunrise.io ([OpenOcean](https://www.openocean.vc/articles/data-ecosystems-pioneer-openocean-raises-eu92m-fund-for-data-solutions-and-software)).

The portfolio named by the firm includes Binalyze, Booksy, IQM, MindsDB, Supermetrics and Truecaller, with early big-data bets in Import.io and RapidMiner, identity in Truecaller, automation in Supermetrics and AppGyver, and DevOps in Bitrise ([OpenOcean](https://www.openocean.vc/articles/data-ecosystems-pioneer-openocean-raises-eu92m-fund-for-data-solutions-and-software), [TechFundingNews](https://techfundingnews.com/openocean-targets-e130m-fund-bridging-europes-ai-investment-gap/)). Exits include Truecaller's IPO and the acquisitions of LoopMe (Mayfield Equity), Passfort (Moody's), AppGyver (SAP), RapidMiner (Altair) and Strong Networks (Citrix) ([OpenOcean](https://www.openocean.vc/articles/data-ecosystems-pioneer-openocean-raises-eu92m-fund-for-data-solutions-and-software)).

## The Booksy Series A

OpenOcean's Booksy role is the concrete case of its thesis. In March 2017 it led Booksy's $4.2 million Series A with [Kai Hansen](/people/kai-hansen/) and angels ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2017/03/03/booksy-series-a/)). Managing partner Patrik Backman drew the contrast that has defined Booksy's positioning ever since: [Treatwell](/organizations/treatwell/) charges a fee per booking, while Booksy charges salons a transparent monthly subscription ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2017/03/03/booksy-series-a/)). A database founder backing a booking platform is not an obvious pairing until the thesis is stated: Booksy is a B2B platform whose value is the data it holds about a salon's customers and schedule, and the subscription model monetises that platform rather than taxing each transaction. OpenOcean also participated in Booksy's $13.2 million Series B in 2018 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/07/06/booksy-the-worldwide-booking-system-raises-13-2-million/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who founded OpenOcean?**
Michael "Monty" Widenius, Patrik Backman and Ralf Wahlsten, the creators of MySQL and MariaDB, with Tom Henriksson ([OpenOcean](https://www.openocean.vc/articles/data-ecosystems-pioneer-openocean-raises-eu92m-fund-for-data-solutions-and-software)).

**What is the MySQL connection?**
MySQL was sold to Sun Microsystems for $1 billion in 2008; the same team then built MariaDB Corporation, and in 2011 founded OpenOcean to invest in the data economy ([OpenOcean](https://www.openocean.vc/articles/data-ecosystems-pioneer-openocean-raises-eu92m-fund-for-data-solutions-and-software)).

**What does OpenOcean invest in?**
European B2B platforms and enterprise software in the data economy — AI, application-driven data infrastructure, intelligent automation and open source — leading or co-leading Series A rounds ([OpenOcean](https://www.openocean.vc/articles/data-ecosystems-pioneer-openocean-raises-eu92m-fund-for-data-solutions-and-software)).

**What is OpenOcean's role in Booksy?**
It led the $4.2 million Series A in March 2017 and participated in the $13.2 million Series B in 2018 ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2017/03/03/booksy-series-a/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/07/06/booksy-the-worldwide-booking-system-raises-13-2-million/)).

**How big are OpenOcean's funds?**
Fund I €45 million (2011), Fund II €80 million (2015), Fund III €92 million at first close (2021) and Fund IV €100 million at first close (2024), both targeting €130 million ([OpenOcean](https://www.openocean.vc/articles/data-ecosystems-pioneer-openocean-raises-eu92m-fund-for-data-solutions-and-software), [TechFundingNews](https://techfundingnews.com/openocean-targets-e130m-fund-bridging-europes-ai-investment-gap/)).
