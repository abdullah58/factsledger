## Overview

This profile is about Relay Financial Technologies, Inc. — relayfi.com — the Toronto-based fintech that sells online business banking and money management to small and medium-sized businesses. It is not Relay Network, the customer-engagement feed platform, nor any other company sharing the Relay name: a software comparison page documents the two as entirely distinct companies, Relay Financial founded in 2018 in Canada and Relay Network founded in 2010 in the United States ([Slashdot comparison](https://slashdot.org/software/comparison/Relay-Financial-vs-Relay-Network/)).

Relay's pitch is cash-flow clarity. Co-founder and CEO Yoseph West's founding claim, as he told *TechCrunch*, is that small-business owners check their bank balances daily but find the information and functions they need missing: most SMBs have only about 27 days of cash on hand, and they need banking that gives them control over inflows and outflows rather than a static balance ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/29/relay-raises-cash-for-its-smb-focused-money-management-platform/)).

## From Vuru to Relay

West studied equity and debt finance in college and co-founded Vuru, a stock-market research app, in 2012. Fintech firm Wave Accounting acquired Vuru later that year; West stayed on and eventually became director of product engagement ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/29/relay-raises-cash-for-its-smb-focused-money-management-platform/)). While at Wave, he developed the idea for Relay and teamed up with Paul Klicnik, an ex-IBM engineer who had previously built the core technical infrastructure at coupon app Flipp, to launch Relay in October 2018 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/29/relay-raises-cash-for-its-smb-focused-money-management-platform/)).

Relay is not itself a bank: it relies on its partner Thread Bank for the banking services it provides, which West says are FDIC-insured ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/29/relay-raises-cash-for-its-smb-focused-money-management-platform/)). The platform lets a business organize income, expenses and reserves across up to 20 checking accounts, automatically set aside cash into savings accounts paying 1%–3% APY, and issue up to 50 physical or virtual Visa debit cards to employees ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/29/relay-raises-cash-for-its-smb-focused-money-management-platform/)). Users can send and receive ACH transfers, wires and check payments like a traditional bank, capture and store receipts, and grant employees access through role-based accounts ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/29/relay-raises-cash-for-its-smb-focused-money-management-platform/)).

The business model is threefold: interest on customer deposits, card interchange fees, and a $30-per-month premium tier, Relay Pro, that adds features like same-day payments ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/29/relay-raises-cash-for-its-smb-focused-money-management-platform/)). The company positions itself against neobanks such as [Bluevine](/organizations/bluevine/) and Mercury — but, West argues, with a deliberately different customer: not tech startups or solo owners, but "heart of America" small businesses with two or more employees and monthly revenue in the tens to hundreds of thousands ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/29/relay-raises-cash-for-its-smb-focused-money-management-platform/)).

## A funding arc through a fintech downturn

Relay raised $19.4 million in May 2021 to build out automation products for financial management, pitching back-office integration that would raise financial visibility while cutting administration time ([ATM Marketplace](https://www.ATMmarketplace.com/news/relay-finishes-194m-funding-round/)). Three years later — in a funding climate where venture investment in fintech had fallen to $43 billion, its lowest in six years, and the banking-as-a-service startup Synapse had collapsed into bankruptcy — Relay closed a $32.2 million Series B led by Bain Capital Ventures, with BTV, Garage, Industry Ventures and Tapestry participating, bringing its total raised to $51.6 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/29/relay-raises-cash-for-its-smb-focused-money-management-platform/)).

The round funded a push into spend management, smart credit products and a financial API marketplace, toward West's stated goal of AI-powered predictive cash-flow analytics ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/29/relay-raises-cash-for-its-smb-focused-money-management-platform/); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/investment-tracker/2024/relay-raises-32-2-million-to-expand-business-banking-platform/)). By then the company served roughly 100,000 businesses, with revenues up 3x in 2022 and nearly 6x in 2023, and planned to grow its workforce from 140 to 200 people ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/29/relay-raises-cash-for-its-smb-focused-money-management-platform/)).

In May 2026, Relay announced a $50 million growth investment from General Catalyst, reporting more than $1.3 billion in managed customer deposits and over 150,000 small-business customers, with a stated target of tripling revenue by the end of 2026 — though that round's independent editorial coverage was limited to one fintech newsletter alongside PR republications of the company's announcement ([Pulse](https://www.pulse.bot/fintech/deals/relay-raises-50m-growth-investment-from-general-catalyst-ec982b6b-e1bd-41a0-9fda-c2eab28ed5ac/)).

## What this profile does not claim

- This profile does not claim Relay is a bank; it is a fintech whose banking services run through partner Thread Bank.
- It does not claim the 2026 General Catalyst round's figures are independently verified — they come from the company's announcement.
- It does not claim Relay has no competitors in SMB banking: TechCrunch explicitly names Bluevine and Mercury in the same space.
- It does not state West's $100M annualized-revenue prediction as an outcome; it was a 2024 projection.
- It does not claim Relay serves consumer customers; its stated target is small businesses with employees.
