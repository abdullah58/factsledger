## Overview

Liquid I.V. is an American hydration drink-mix company that sells electrolyte powder in single-serving sticks, led by its Hydration Multiplier product. Founded in [California](/places/california/) in 2012 by three Loyola Marymount University graduates, it grew from a campus startup into the leading powdered hydration brand in the [United States](/places/united-states/) before Unilever acquired it in 2020 for an undisclosed sum ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2020/liquid-i-v-acquired-by-unilever-for-undisclosed-fee); [Unilever](https://www.unilever.com/news/news-search/2024/liquid-iv-powers-growth-with-partnerships-innovation-and-global-expansion/)).

This profile is about the consumer brand, not a medical treatment: Liquid I.V. is not an intravenous (IV) therapy clinic or IV drip service. Its product is a powder mixed with drinking water, based on the World Health Organization's guidelines for an Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2020/liquid-i-v-acquired-by-unilever-for-undisclosed-fee)). Under Unilever it has quadrupled in size since the acquisition, Unilever says, becoming the biggest brand in its €1.9 billion Health & Wellbeing Collective, with a €1 billion sales goal ([Unilever](https://www.unilever.com/news/news-search/2024/liquid-iv-powers-growth-with-partnerships-innovation-and-global-expansion/)).

## Founding and the oral rehydration idea

Liquid I.V. was founded in 2012 by Brandin Cohen, Hayden Fulstone and Cameron Killeen, three friends who had graduated from Loyola Marymount University (LMU) in 2010 ([LMU Newsroom](https://newsroom.lmu.edu/campusnews/hayden-fulstone-10-cultivates-the-next-generation-of-entrepreneurs/)). The idea came from an observation the three shared: adults were drinking Pedialyte — a rehydration drink marketed for children — for hangovers and dehydration, and no equivalent product was made for adults ([LMU Newsroom](https://newsroom.lmu.edu/campusnews/hayden-fulstone-10-cultivates-the-next-generation-of-entrepreneurs/)). Cohen, who was working in ticket sales for Major League Baseball's Arizona Diamondbacks, had noticed the same pattern among players: they were drinking sugary sports drinks and looking for something better ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/202102/graham-winfrey/liquid-iv-brandin-cohen-unilever-acquisition-sale-expansion.html)).

Cohen became interested in oral rehydration therapy, in which sodium, glucose and potassium mixed at a specific ratio help water absorb into the bloodstream faster — a treatment used in developing countries against deadly dehydration ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/202102/graham-winfrey/liquid-iv-brandin-cohen-unilever-acquisition-sale-expansion.html)). That science became the basis for the Hydration Multiplier: a powder in single-serving sticks, mixed with 16 oz of water, built around a proprietary delivery system the company calls Cellular Transport Technology (CTT) — a specific ratio of sodium, glucose and potassium ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2018/liquid-v-secures-5m-series-b-round/)). The brand says one serving can provide two to three times the hydration of water alone — the company's claim, not an independently verified finding ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2020/liquid-i-v-acquired-by-unilever-for-undisclosed-fee)).

LMU played a direct part in the launch: entrepreneurship professor David Choi invited Liquid I.V. into the inaugural class of the university's Business Incubator, and Fulstone later called the incubator "an integral part of our initial growth" ([LMU Newsroom](https://newsroom.lmu.edu/campusnews/hayden-fulstone-10-cultivates-the-next-generation-of-entrepreneurs/)). The brand did not start as a powder company: it originally launched as a ready-to-drink beverage and quickly switched to the stick format, a move that made direct-to-consumer and Amazon distribution far easier — shipping sticks is cheaper than shipping bottles — and positioned the product as a daily routine rather than an impulse purchase ([New Hope Network](https://www.newhope.com/industry-insights/unilever-s-liquid-iv-acquisition-a-proof-point-for-circleup-investment-fund)).

## Funding: CircleUp, Helio and the celebrity rounds

Liquid I.V.'s funding story has two distinct chapters: a data-driven institutional round, then a celebrity-backed strategic round. In August 2018 the company closed a $5 million Series B led by [CircleUp Growth Partners](/organizations/circleup-growth-partners/), the [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/)-based investment group ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2018/liquid-v-secures-5m-series-b-round/)). [CircleUp](/organizations/circleup/) found the company through Helio, its machine-learning platform for identifying early-stage consumer brands: the data showed Liquid I.V. had doubled revenues in consecutive quarters with strong "cross channel brand power" and social media presence ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2018/liquid-v-secures-5m-series-b-round/)). CircleUp managing director Ben Lee later said Helio's screen surfaced a brand that would otherwise not have been on the firm's radar, and that CircleUp was the only institutional investor the company was talking to at the time ([New Hope Network](https://www.newhope.com/industry-insights/unilever-s-liquid-iv-acquisition-a-proof-point-for-circleup-investment-fund)).

The round's other investors show the brand's entertainment-network lean: TQ Ventures, the group led by SB Projects founder and entrepreneur Scooter Braun, invested alongside Norwegian DJ/producer Kygo and his manager Myles Shear, and Washington Nationals outfielder Adam Eaton ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2018/liquid-v-secures-5m-series-b-round/)). Braun, an investor since 2017, also joined the management team, and Cohen said the funds would support product research and a balanced expansion of distribution, then evenly split between retail and e-commerce ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2018/liquid-v-secures-5m-series-b-round/)). At the time the product was sold online and at more than 20,000 locations, including GNC, Costco and Whole Foods Market ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2018/liquid-v-secures-5m-series-b-round/)).

A year later, in 2019, the brand raised another $5 million in a strategic funding round backed by celebrities including Justin Bieber, Kendall Jenner, Usher and James Corden ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2020/liquid-i-v-acquired-by-unilever-for-undisclosed-fee)). Cohen credited Braun's network for assembling the group: one of the firm's venture capitalists introduced the company to Bieber's manager, and the resulting investor group helped the brand far beyond money, including a Steve Aoki strawberry-cake flavour ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/202102/graham-winfrey/liquid-iv-brandin-cohen-unilever-acquisition-sale-expansion.html)). By 2020 the brand had extended beyond hydration into Sleep and Energy product lines, and more than 35,000 US stores carried its products ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2020/liquid-i-v-acquired-by-unilever-for-undisclosed-fee); [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/202102/graham-winfrey/liquid-iv-brandin-cohen-unilever-acquisition-sale-expansion.html)).

## The Unilever acquisition

At the end of 2019 the business was scaling fast, sold only in the United States, and drew international interest. Cohen said the company chose a strategic buyer because Unilever could plug the brand into its distribution network and make it global: "every kind of buyer was on the table, and Unilever was the perfect fit" ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/202102/graham-winfrey/liquid-iv-brandin-cohen-unilever-acquisition-sale-expansion.html)). BevNET reported the acquisition on September 2, 2020, for an undisclosed fee; the brand stayed based in El Segundo, California, under Cohen ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2020/liquid-i-v-acquired-by-unilever-for-undisclosed-fee)). New Hope Network dates the acquisition to September 1, 2020 ([New Hope Network](https://www.newhope.com/industry-insights/unilever-s-liquid-iv-acquisition-a-proof-point-for-circleup-investment-fund)).

Legal completion came a month later: Unilever's 2020 annual report lists October 1, 2020 as the completion date, describing Liquid I.V. as "a US-based health-science nutrition and wellness company, known for its portfolio of electrolyte drink mixes that enhance rapid hydration," acquired to grow its functional-nutrition presence ([SEC / Unilever](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/217410/000119312521075770/R29.htm)). The gap between the September announcement and the October completion is flagged in the open items rather than smoothed over. No price was ever disclosed, and this profile does not repeat the estimates that circulated in lower-tier roundups.

For CircleUp, the deal proved its model: the first exit for a company in its Growth Partners Fund, which had invested in 2018, and Ben Lee said it validated Helio's data-driven sourcing of early-stage brands ([New Hope Network](https://www.newhope.com/industry-insights/unilever-s-liquid-iv-acquisition-a-proof-point-for-circleup-investment-fund)).

## Growth under Unilever

Under Unilever, Liquid I.V. joined the Health & Wellbeing Collective, the group's portfolio of acquired wellness brands. By Unilever's own account, the brand had 40 people when acquired; by 2023 a team of 160 worked on it, with turnover four times larger ([Unilever](https://www.unilever.com/news/news-search/2023/how-our-1-billion-health-wellbeing-business-keeps-growing/)). By May 2024 Unilever said the brand had quadrupled in size since 2020, becoming the biggest brand in the €1.9 billion Collective, and set a goal of reaching €1 billion in brand sales "in the near future" ([Unilever](https://www.unilever.com/news/news-search/2024/liquid-iv-powers-growth-with-partnerships-innovation-and-global-expansion/)). Unilever also describes it as the No. 1 powdered hydration brand in the US, attributing the ranking to Circana retail data — a company-reported claim, not an independent measurement ([Unilever](https://www.unilever.com/news/news-search/2024/liquid-iv-powers-growth-with-partnerships-innovation-and-global-expansion/)).

Hydration Multiplier Sugar-Free launched in the US in June 2023, answering demand for sugar-free hydration, and in 2023 sampling activations at music and sports events put 1.7 million samples in front of millennial and Gen Z fans ([Unilever](https://www.unilever.com/news/news-search/2024/liquid-iv-powers-growth-with-partnerships-innovation-and-global-expansion/)). International expansion followed the playbook Cohen had wanted from the acquisition: Canada in late 2023, China via cross-border e-commerce in early 2024, Australia and the UK in March 2024, and then Mexico and the Netherlands ([Unilever](https://www.unilever.com/news/news-search/2024/liquid-iv-powers-growth-with-partnerships-innovation-and-global-expansion/)).

Unilever also invested in making the product at home: in March 2024 it announced more than $80 million to add Liquid I.V. production to its Jefferson City, Missouri facility — automated packaging lines, a blending room and heating and cooling work — on top of a separate $25 million warehousing investment from January 2024 ([Missouri Department of Economic Development](https://ded.mo.gov/press-room/unilever-expand-jefferson-city-investing-more-80-million-produce-liquid-iv); [Just Food](https://www.just-food.com/newsletters/unilever-invests-in-production-of-liquid-iv-beverage-brand)). The site, operating since 1966 with more than 450 workers, became the first Unilever facility to manufacture Health & Wellbeing portfolio products ([Missouri Department of Economic Development](https://ded.mo.gov/press-room/unilever-expand-jefferson-city-investing-more-80-million-produce-liquid-iv)). Unilever's chief financial officer told investors in February 2024 that Liquid I.V. grew at a double-digit rate in the fourth quarter of 2023 ([Just Food](https://www.just-food.com/newsletters/unilever-invests-in-production-of-liquid-iv-beverage-brand)).

## Giving back: hydration aid and clean water

Giving was part of the brand from before it was profitable: for every serving purchased, the company donated a serving to someone in need ([Liquid I.V.](https://www.liquid-iv.com/articles/giving-tuesday-is-giving-365-our-impact-initiatives-at-liquid-iv-)). By the end of 2018 it expected to have donated more than 500,000 servings, with product distributed in Haiti and Uganda and to firefighters battling California's wildfires ([BeverageDaily](https://www.beveragedaily.com/Article/2018/08/30/Hydration-beverage-company-lands-5-million-infusion-to-support-rapid-growth/)). During the COVID-19 pandemic the programme scaled sharply: more than 2,000 hospitals reached out, and in a couple of months the company donated more than two million sticks to frontline workers ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/magazine/202102/graham-winfrey/liquid-iv-brandin-cohen-unilever-acquisition-sale-expansion.html)).

The programme has since widened from donating product to funding water access: the company reports over 50 million servings donated by November 2023, with a commitment to 150 million more by 2032 ([Liquid I.V.](https://www.liquid-iv.com/articles/giving-tuesday-is-giving-365-our-impact-initiatives-at-liquid-iv-)). In 2023 it provided $1.3 million in Clean Water Grants to three non-profit partners — a figure Unilever's 2023 account independently confirms — including $500,000 to the International Rescue Committee in drought-affected Ethiopia and $300,000 to MAP International in Liberia and Burkina Faso ([Liquid I.V.](https://www.liquid-iv.com/articles/giving-tuesday-is-giving-365-our-impact-initiatives-at-liquid-iv-); [Unilever](https://www.unilever.com/news/news-search/2023/how-our-1-billion-health-wellbeing-business-keeps-growing/)). The donation totals are company-reported; the grants figure is the part independently corroborated.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who founded Liquid I.V.?**
Liquid I.V. was founded in 2012 by Brandin Cohen, Hayden Fulstone and Cameron Killeen, three Loyola Marymount University graduates from the class of 2010, after noticing adults drinking children's Pedialyte for hangovers ([LMU Newsroom](https://newsroom.lmu.edu/campusnews/hayden-fulstone-10-cultivates-the-next-generation-of-entrepreneurs/)).

**Who owns Liquid I.V. now?**
Unilever owns Liquid I.V. The acquisition was announced in September 2020 and completed on October 1, 2020, for undisclosed terms; the brand sits in Unilever's Health & Wellbeing Collective ([SEC / Unilever](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/217410/000119312521075770/R29.htm); [BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2020/liquid-i-v-acquired-by-unilever-for-undisclosed-fee)).

**What is Liquid I.V.'s main product?**
Its flagship is Hydration Multiplier, an electrolyte powder in single-serving sticks mixed with water, based on WHO oral rehydration solution guidelines and built around a system the company calls Cellular Transport Technology. Sugar-Free, Energy and Sleep lines followed ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2020/liquid-i-v-acquired-by-unilever-for-undisclosed-fee)).

**How much did Unilever pay for Liquid I.V.?**
The price was never disclosed: BevNET reported an "undisclosed fee" and Unilever's annual report gives no price, and unverified estimates are not repeated here ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2020/liquid-i-v-acquired-by-unilever-for-undisclosed-fee)).

**Is Liquid I.V. an IV drip company?**
No. Liquid I.V. is a consumer drink-mix brand — a powder mixed with drinking water — unrelated to intravenous therapy clinics or IV drip services despite the similar name ([BevNET](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2020/liquid-i-v-acquired-by-unilever-for-undisclosed-fee)).
