## Overview

Dubai Taxi Company PJSC (DTC) is [Dubai](/places/dubai/)'s largest taxi operator and a listed mobility company, established on 28 June 1994 and floated on the Dubai Financial Market in December 2023 ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://www.dubaitaxi.ae/en/investor-relations/overview)). It runs taxis, VIP limousines, buses and last-mile delivery bikes, held about 45% of Dubai's taxi market in 2025, and earned AED 2.47 billion in revenue in FY2025 ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://finansia.fra1.digitaloceanspaces.com/finansia/media/publications/publication_file/DTC_PR_E_24_02_2026..pdf)). In 2026 it became [Baidu](/organizations/baidu/)'s local partner for driverless ride-hailing in Dubai, scaling toward more than 1,000 autonomous vehicles ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://www.dubaitaxi.ae/en/post/dubai-taxi-company-and-baidus-apollo-go-launch-fully-driverless-commercial-ride-hailing-in-dubai)). A July 2026 acquisition of National Taxi made DTC the UAE's largest taxi operator, with a combined taxi fleet above 9,500 vehicles ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://finansia.fra1.digitaloceanspaces.com/finansia/media/publications/publication_file/DTC_PR_E_08_07_2026.Pdf.pdf)).

## From 81 taxis to a listed mobility company

DTC began as a taxi company, commencing operations in 1995 with 81 taxi cars ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://www.dubaitaxi.ae/en/investor-relations/overview)). It built its position on exclusive contracts: taxi services at Dubai International Airport from 1997 and at Al Maktoum International Airport from 2010, and limousine pick-ups at landmarks such as Dubai Mall ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://www.dubaitaxi.ae/en/post/dubai-taxi-company-pjsc-announces-its-intention-to-float-on-the-dubai-financial-market-dfm)). Along the way it added a hybrid car programme in 2007, bus services in 2015, a Tesla electric-vehicle programme for limousines in 2017 — the year it passed 1 billion passengers carried since 1994 — its own app and a partnership with Hala in 2019, and a delivery-bike service in 2022 ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://www.dubaitaxi.ae/en/investor-relations/overview)).

Dubai's taxi market is tightly regulated: the Roads and Transport Authority is the sole regulator, controlling the supply of licence plates through auctions open to the five existing taxi franchisees, of which DTC is one ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://www.dubaitaxi.ae/en/post/dubai-taxi-company-pjsc-announces-its-intention-to-float-on-the-dubai-financial-market-dfm)). That scarcity is the foundation of DTC's position. At 30 June 2023 it held about 44% of Dubai's taxi fleet — twice the 22% of its next-largest competitor — operating more than 7,000 vehicles, 5,216 of them taxis, and making 44 million trips in the preceding twelve months ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://www.dubaitaxi.ae/en/post/dubai-taxi-company-pjsc-announces-its-intention-to-float-on-the-dubai-financial-market-dfm)).

In November 2023 Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum issued Law No. (21) of 2023, recognising the company as a public joint stock company, Dubai Taxi Company PJSC, with financial and administrative independence ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://www.dubaitaxi.ae/en/post/dubai-taxi-company-pjsc-announces-its-intention-to-float-on-the-dubai-financial-market-dfm)). The same month it announced its intention to float: 624,750,000 shares, 24.99% of its share capital, all existing shares held by the Department of Finance for the Government of Dubai, with subscriptions running 21–28 November 2023 for retail investors and 21–29 November for professional investors, and admission to the Dubai Financial Market in December 2023 ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://www.dubaitaxi.ae/en/post/dubai-taxi-company-pjsc-announces-its-intention-to-float-on-the-dubai-financial-market-dfm)). The offer price was set by book-building; 5% of the offering was reserved for the Emirates Investment Authority and 5% for the Pensions and Social Security Fund of Local Military Personnel, and Emirates NBD's Sharia committee pronounced the offering compliant with Shariah principles ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://www.dubaitaxi.ae/en/post/dubai-taxi-company-pjsc-announces-its-intention-to-float-on-the-dubai-financial-market-dfm)).

## The listed company: FY2025 results and strategy

DTC's first full years as a listed company were strong. For the year ended 31 December 2025, announced on 23 February 2026, revenue rose 13% to AED 2.47 billion, EBITDA rose 12% to AED 652 million at a 26% margin, and net profit rose 7% to AED 356.1 million ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://finansia.fra1.digitaloceanspaces.com/finansia/media/publications/publication_file/DTC_PR_E_24_02_2026..pdf)). Taxis remained the core, with segment revenue up 11% to AED 2.14 billion, while limousines earned AED 128.9 million, buses AED 124.0 million, and the fast-growing delivery-bike segment AED 78.4 million, up 84% year over year ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://finansia.fra1.digitaloceanspaces.com/finansia/media/publications/publication_file/DTC_PR_E_24_02_2026..pdf)). Its taxis and limousines completed 53 million trips, up 8%, of which 20.8 million were e-hailing trips, up 24% ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://finansia.fra1.digitaloceanspaces.com/finansia/media/publications/publication_file/DTC_PR_E_24_02_2026..pdf)). At December 2025 the operational taxi fleet stood at 6,217 vehicles, including 525 fully electric, within a total operational fleet of 11,126 vehicles ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://finansia.fra1.digitaloceanspaces.com/finansia/media/publications/publication_file/DTC_PR_E_24_02_2026..pdf)).

Shareholders are paid generously by design: DTC's policy is to distribute at least 85% of annual net profit, and total FY2025 dividends were AED 302.7 million, or 12.11 fils per share — a final dividend of AED 142.0 million plus an interim dividend of AED 160.7 million ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://finansia.fra1.digitaloceanspaces.com/finansia/media/publications/publication_file/DTC_PR_E_24_02_2026..pdf)). Growth is organised around a five-year strategy for 2025–2029, launched in 2024, built on fleet expansion, digital channels and sustainability ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://finansia.fra1.digitaloceanspaces.com/finansia/media/publications/publication_file/DTC_PR_E_24_02_2026..pdf)). In 2025 DTC signed a five-year partnership with Dubai Airports as the exclusive taxi operator at both Dubai International and Al Maktoum International airports, deepened its e-hailing partnership with Bolt — including an alliance with Kabi, one of Dubai's largest taxi operators, integrating Kabi's fleet into the Bolt platform — agreed a delivery partnership with Keeta, the international subsidiary of Meituan, and signed a long-term agreement with DEWA to deploy more than 200 ultra-fast EV charging points ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://finansia.fra1.digitaloceanspaces.com/finansia/media/publications/publication_file/DTC_PR_E_24_02_2026..pdf)). The stated goal is to convert 100% of its taxi and limousine fleet to electric vehicles by 2040 ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://finansia.fra1.digitaloceanspaces.com/finansia/media/publications/publication_file/DTC_PR_E_24_02_2026..pdf)).

## Two 2026 moves: National Taxi and [Apollo Go](/organizations/apollo-go/)

DTC's first transformational acquisition since its IPO came in 2026. On 13 May 2026 it signed an agreement to buy 100% of National Taxi LLC — founded in 2000, operating about 2,500 licensed plates and more than 2,700 vehicles across Dubai, [Abu Dhabi](/places/abu-dhabi/) and Al Ain — for an enterprise value of AED 1.45 billion, funded through new bank debt with no equity issuance ([Dubai Media Office](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2026/may/13-05/dubai-taxi-company-signs-agreement-to-acquire-national-taxi)). In the year to 31 July 2025 National Taxi completed 25.4 million trips and generated net revenue of AED 774 million and EBITDA of AED 183 million ([Dubai Media Office](https://mediaoffice.ae/en/news/2026/may/13-05/dubai-taxi-company-signs-agreement-to-acquire-national-taxi)). The deal, completed on 8 July 2026 after approvals from Dubai's RTA and Abu Dhabi's Integrated Transport Centre, lifted DTC's Dubai market share from about 47% to about 59% and gave it a 12% share of the Abu Dhabi taxi market, its first presence there ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://finansia.fra1.digitaloceanspaces.com/finansia/media/publications/publication_file/DTC_PR_E_08_07_2026.Pdf.pdf)). The combined taxi fleet exceeds 9,500 vehicles, the National Taxi brand is being retained, and DTC expects the deal to be earnings accretive from its first full year of ownership ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://finansia.fra1.digitaloceanspaces.com/finansia/media/publications/publication_file/DTC_PR_E_08_07_2026.Pdf.pdf)). Group CEO Mansoor Rahma Alfalasi called it "the first transformational acquisition since our IPO" ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://finansia.fra1.digitaloceanspaces.com/finansia/media/publications/publication_file/DTC_PR_E_08_07_2026.Pdf.pdf)).

The second move is the one that connects DTC to Baidu. In March 2025 Baidu announced a plan to deploy more than 1,000 autonomous vehicles in Dubai over the next few years ([Baidu](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/1090379/baidu-s-apollo-go-commences-fully-driverless-commercial-ride-hailing-in-dubai-partners-with-dubai-taxi-company)). In January 2026 Apollo Go received Dubai's first-ever driverless testing permit for fully autonomous vehicles without a safety driver, and Baidu inaugurated the Apollo Go Park in Dubai, its first overseas operations and management hub ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://www.dubaitaxi.ae/en/post/dubai-taxi-company-and-baidus-apollo-go-launch-fully-driverless-commercial-ride-hailing-in-dubai)). Later in 2026, Apollo Go — in partnership with the RTA and in strategic collaboration with DTC — launched fully driverless commercial ride-hailing in Dubai via the Apollo Go app, the platform's first international app deployment ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://www.dubaitaxi.ae/en/post/dubai-taxi-company-and-baidus-apollo-go-launch-fully-driverless-commercial-ride-hailing-in-dubai)). The rollout starts with 50 vehicles in the first year, expanding in phases to more than 1,000 driverless vehicles over the next few years, with booking to be extended to Bolt and other e-hailing providers as it scales ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://www.dubaitaxi.ae/en/post/dubai-taxi-company-and-baidus-apollo-go-launch-fully-driverless-commercial-ride-hailing-in-dubai)). The launch supports Dubai's Self-Driving Transport Strategy of turning 25% of all trips into smart and driverless journeys by 2030 ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://www.dubaitaxi.ae/en/post/dubai-taxi-company-and-baidus-apollo-go-launch-fully-driverless-commercial-ride-hailing-in-dubai)). DTC's contribution is local operational expertise and a fleet of more than 6,000 taxis and limousines built over three decades; as of February 2026 Apollo Go had completed over 20 million rides worldwide, with weekly rides peaking above 300,000 in the fourth quarter of 2025 and its fleets accumulating more than 300 million autonomous kilometres, including more than 190 million fully driverless ([Dubai Taxi Company](https://www.dubaitaxi.ae/en/post/dubai-taxi-company-and-baidus-apollo-go-launch-fully-driverless-commercial-ride-hailing-in-dubai)).

- It does not state the IPO offer price or proceeds; the price was set by book-building and no figure was stated in any fully-read source.
- It does not state the precise date of the Apollo Go commercial launch; only that it took place in 2026.
- It does not present FY2025 figures as independently audited; they are the company's own reported results.
- It does not resolve the National Taxi utilisation discrepancy (98% at announcement versus 90% at completion); the later figure is used.
- It does not claim DTC operates driverless vehicles itself; the autonomous technology and the Apollo Go app are Baidu's, with DTC providing local operational support.
- It does not state a current market share or fleet size; all such figures in this profile are dated.
