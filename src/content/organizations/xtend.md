## Overview

XTEND is an Israeli defense-technology company that builds human-guided autonomous drone systems — machines a soldier can fly with a VR headset and a handheld controller, with no flight experience, in places too dangerous for people to enter. Founded in Tel Aviv in 2018 as a video-game company, it remade itself as a defense contractor after the October 7, 2023 attacks, and in September 2026 it went public through a $1.5 billion merger that pulled in some conspicuous investors: Eric Trump personally, and [Unusual Machines](/organizations/unusual-machines/), the Florida drone-component maker that counts [Donald Trump Jr.](/people/donald-trump-jr/) as an adviser ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/eric-trump-invests-israeli-drone-maker-xtends-merger-with-florida-construction-2026-02-17/); [The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/defense-and-tech/article-886987)).

## From video games to the battlefield

The founders — brothers Aviv and Matteo Shapira, Rubi Liani, and Adir Tubi — were not defense people. They were extended-reality specialists whose previous company, Replay Technologies, had been acquired by Intel in 2016 for a reported $175 million ([The Drone Girl](https://www.thedronegirl.com/2026/09/09/xtend-drone-company-gaming-eric-trump-jfb-merger-analysis/); [The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/defense-and-tech/article-886987)). XTEND's original business was immersive gaming: making it feel natural to fly a virtual drone using wearable displays and gesture controls ([The Drone Girl](https://www.thedronegirl.com/2026/09/09/xtend-drone-company-gaming-eric-trump-jfb-merger-analysis/)).

October 7 changed the company's direction entirely. CEO Aviv Shapira has said publicly that the Hamas attacks made him realize his technology could keep soldiers out of harm's way, and XTEND developed a new concept of operations — drones enter the battlefield before the soldiers do ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/defense-and-tech/article-886987)). Its drones were used to map tunnels under the Gaza Strip and, the company says, to intercept Hezbollah drones in northern Israel ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/defense-and-tech/article-886987)). Shapira's pitch to buyers is that the systems are battle-proven in a way lab-tested rivals are not: "At XTEND, we've unfortunately been involved in many conflicts around the world. But that experience makes our technology truly battle-proven, something you can't buy. And operating with remotes saves lives" ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/defense-and-tech/article-886987)).

## XOS: the "Android of robotics"

XTEND's core product is not really a drone — it is XOS, an operating system for drones. Where most Western drone makers sell a proprietary drone with a proprietary camera running proprietary software, XOS is open and platform-agnostic: the software sits on top of hardware that can range from a tiny indoor micro-drone to a ground rover to a maritime vessel, and operators download capabilities — license-plate recognition, indoor threat tracking, thermal mapping — the way phone users download apps ([The Drone Girl](https://www.thedronegirl.com/2026/09/09/xtend-drone-company-gaming-eric-trump-jfb-merger-analysis/)). Shapira calls it the Android of the robotics world: "We are that Android of the market. If I'm a police officer and I need a drone that does license plate recognition, I go to the marketplace, the app store, and download that capability" ([The Drone Girl](https://www.thedronegirl.com/2026/09/09/xtend-drone-company-gaming-eric-trump-jfb-merger-analysis/)). Third-party developers can build applications and hardware payloads on top of XOS, and the company says it works with more than 100 partners ([The Drone Girl](https://www.thedronegirl.com/2026/09/09/xtend-drone-company-gaming-eric-trump-jfb-merger-analysis/)). The pitch is that the gaming DNA survived the pivot: software that translates human intention into AI-driven action, so an operator with zero flight experience can fly complex indoor and outdoor missions ([The Drone Girl](https://www.thedronegirl.com/2026/09/09/xtend-drone-company-gaming-eric-trump-jfb-merger-analysis/)).

By mid-2025 XTEND's AI-enabled systems were in use with the U.S. Department of Defense, Singapore, European customers, the United Kingdom, and the Israel Defense Forces ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/eric-trump-invests-israeli-drone-maker-xtends-merger-with-florida-construction-2026-02-17/)).

## The $1.5 billion merger — with a construction company

In February 2026 XTEND announced an all-stock merger with JFB Construction Holdings, a Florida-based, Nasdaq-listed construction firm, valuing the deal at $1.5 billion and aimed at taking XTEND public ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/eric-trump-invests-israeli-drone-maker-xtends-merger-with-florida-construction-2026-02-17/)). The pairing confused observers — until Shapira explained it: "How can I build the most advanced, biggest, and most capable factory in the U.S. without a construction company? JFB is physically building our advanced manufacturing facilities" ([The Drone Girl](https://www.thedronegirl.com/2026/09/09/xtend-drone-company-gaming-eric-trump-jfb-merger-analysis/)). To meet strict NDAA supply-chain mandates and scale American production, XTEND built manufacturing facilities in Tampa, Florida, and Virginia, and the merger gave it direct access to U.S. public capital markets ([The Drone Girl](https://www.thedronegirl.com/2026/09/09/xtend-drone-company-gaming-eric-trump-jfb-merger-analysis/)).

The deal was backed by a $152 million strategic investment round ([The Drone Girl](https://www.thedronegirl.com/2026/09/09/xtend-drone-company-gaming-eric-trump-jfb-merger-analysis/)). The merger closed on September 4, 2026; the combined company was renamed XTEND AI Robotics and began trading under the ticker XTND ([The Drone Girl](https://www.thedronegirl.com/2026/09/09/xtend-drone-company-gaming-eric-trump-jfb-merger-analysis/)). Shapira called it "a significant milestone in a journey that began less than a decade ago in Israel," saying it would let the company accelerate growth and bring its systems to more missions ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/defense-and-tech/article-886987)).

## The Trump-family connection

The investor roster is where XTEND enters this wiki's story. Eric Trump, the president's son, invested personally in the merger round ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/eric-trump-invests-israeli-drone-maker-xtends-merger-with-florida-construction-2026-02-17/)). Unusual Machines — the Florida drone-component maker that put Donald Trump Jr. on its advisory board in November 2024 — is also investing in the merger, according to the JFB announcement ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/eric-trump-invests-israeli-drone-maker-xtends-merger-with-florida-construction-2026-02-17/)). Other named investors include Israel's Protego Ventures, Texas real-estate developer [American Ventures](/organizations/american-ventures/), and Miami-based Aliya Capital ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/eric-trump-invests-israeli-drone-maker-xtends-merger-with-florida-construction-2026-02-17/)), with Agostinelli Group and TAU Ventures also participating ([The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/defense-and-tech/article-886987)). The company's workhorse airframe, the Scorpio 1000 quadcopter, is typical of the ruggedized systems its XOS software flies ([The Drone Girl](https://www.thedronegirl.com/2026/09/09/xtend-drone-company-gaming-eric-trump-jfb-merger-analysis/)).

Reuters framed the deal as part of a broader pattern: the Trump family expanding its business ventures since the president's return to office, at a moment when drones have become a top-selling Pentagon item and Silicon Valley money is pouring into drone and military-AI startups, lifting valuations across firms like Anduril and Shield AI ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/eric-trump-invests-israeli-drone-maker-xtends-merger-with-florida-construction-2026-02-17/)). One precision matters: the queue described XTEND as a company with Donald Trump Jr.'s "investment/advisory ties," but the verified record shows the link running one step removed — Trump Jr. advises Unusual Machines, which invested in XTEND; Eric Trump is the family member who invested directly.
