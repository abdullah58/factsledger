## Overview

Shogakukan Inc. is a Japanese publishing company founded in [Tokyo](/places/tokyo/) on 8 August 1922 by Takeo Ōga, and one of [Japan](/places/japan/)'s three great manga publishers alongside [Shueisha](/organizations/shueisha/) and [Kodansha](/organizations/kodansha/). It began in educational publishing — magazines for elementary-school students and instructional magazines for teachers — and grew into a house that publishes 18 comic magazines, about 100 million comic books a year, and a wide range of dictionaries, encyclopaedias and general magazines ([Publishers Weekly](http://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/71334-global-publishing-leaders-2016-shogakukan-inc.html)). Its flagship manga magazine is *Weekly Shōnen Sunday*, home of *Detective Conan* ([Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2008-03-18/shonen-magazine-shonen-sunday-mark-50th-anniversary)). Shogakukan was the publisher that issued the joint filing release when the four houses sued [Cloudflare](/organizations/cloudflare/) in 2022, and on 19 November 2025 the [Tokyo District Court](/organizations/tokyo-district-court/) ordered Cloudflare to pay it ¥126,500,000 ([Tokyo District Court](https://www.courts.go.jp/ip/eng/assets/ip/eng/chizai_en/chizai_en-pdf-3813.pdf)).

Shogakukan is the oldest of the three houses and the parent of one of the others: it created Shueisha in 1925 as its entertainment-related publishing division, and Shueisha became an independent company the following year ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/71335-global-publishing-leaders-2016-shueisha-publishing-co-ltd.html)). The two companies remain tied together in the Hitotsubashi Group, a keiretsu of interconnected companies with shared business relationships and shareholdings that also includes Hakusensha, and the two are headquartered next to each other in Tokyo ([Publishers Weekly](http://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/71334-global-publishing-leaders-2016-shogakukan-inc.html)).

## From school magazines to a manga major

Shogakukan's catalogue still reflects its educational origins. Beyond manga, it publishes children's books, dictionaries and encyclopaedias, with subjects spanning history, folklore, geography, literature, art, education, medicine, photography and gardening ([Publishers Weekly](http://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/71334-global-publishing-leaders-2016-shogakukan-inc.html)). As comic books grew more popular in the 1980s and 1990s it expanded into fashion and lifestyle magazines such as *Oggi* and *Be-Pal*, while continuing its non-manga publishing ([Publishers Weekly](http://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/71334-global-publishing-leaders-2016-shogakukan-inc.html)). By 2016 it was publishing 64 magazines and an average of 760 new book titles a year, selling about 22 million copies from a backlist of 9,200 titles and roughly 117 million copies a year of its 13,900 manga titles ([Publishers Weekly](http://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/71334-global-publishing-leaders-2016-shogakukan-inc.html)). Revenues had stabilised at about ¥103 billion after losses in 2011 and 2012, with e-book revenues of ¥14 billion ([Publishers Weekly](http://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/71334-global-publishing-leaders-2016-shogakukan-inc.html)).

Its international reach runs through Viz Media, which Shogakukan and Shueisha have owned jointly since 2002: Viz Communications was founded in [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) in 1987, and in March 2005 it merged with the American subsidiary of ShoPro — the distribution platform [Shogakukan](/organizations/shogakukan/) operates with Shueisha and Hakusensha — to form Viz Media ([Publishers Weekly](http://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/71334-global-publishing-leaders-2016-shogakukan-inc.html)). *Weekly Shōnen Sunday*, launched on 17 March 1959 — the same day as Kodansha's *Weekly Shōnen Magazine* — remains its flagship weekly manga magazine, and *Detective Conan* by Gōshō Aoyama is its best-known long-running serial ([Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2008-03-18/shonen-magazine-shonen-sunday-mark-50th-anniversary)).

## The Cloudflare manga-piracy lawsuit

Shogakukan sued Cloudflare, Inc. — headquartered in San Francisco, [California](/places/california/) — together with Shueisha, Kodansha and [Kadokawa](/organizations/kadokawa/) on 1 February 2022 in the Tokyo District Court, and issued the joint English-language filing release ([Shogakukan](https://www.shogakukan.co.jp/storage/files/article/2021/20220201e.pdf)). The release set out the publishers' case bluntly: Cloudflare had been providing CDN services to more than half of the top ten piracy sites with the highest numbers of accesses for a long time, some of those sites drew over 100 million accesses a month and earned advertising revenue, and Authorized Books of Japan estimated the retail value of manga illegally read on the top ten sites at over ¥1 trillion in 2021 alone — a retail-value conversion, not a calculation of lost profit ([Shogakukan](https://www.shogakukan.co.jp/storage/files/article/2021/20220201e.pdf)).

Shogakukan's own stake in the case was the earliest and longest-running of the four. Its print rights for the one work at issue were established on 27 December 2018 and 8 February 2019 by its two co-creators, its infringement notice under the US Digital Millennium Copyright Act reached Cloudflare on 7 April 2020 — the earliest of the four publishers — and the damage period the court assessed ran from 8 May 2020 to 28 February 2022 ([Tokyo District Court](https://www.courts.go.jp/ip/eng/assets/ip/eng/chizai_en/chizai_en-pdf-3813.pdf)). The two piracy sites at the centre of the case posted more than 4,000 manga works without permission and at their peak attracted over 300 million monthly visits combined ([Shueisha](https://www.shueisha.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/notice20251119eng.pdf)); the judgment records them as distributing 4,082 titles comprising 123,631 episodes ([Tokyo District Court](https://www.courts.go.jp/ip/eng/assets/ip/eng/chizai_en/chizai_en-pdf-3813.pdf)).

The figures, stated exactly: at filing, the four publishers put total damages for the four litigated works at about ¥5.6 billion and sought about ¥115 million per company as part of that sum — about ¥460 million in total ([Shogakukan](https://www.shogakukan.co.jp/storage/files/article/2021/20220201e.pdf); [Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2025-11-19/shueisha-kodansha-shogakukan-kadokawa-win-copyright-suit-against-cloudflare/.231199)). By judgment, each publisher's claim had become ¥126.5 million. On 19 November 2025 the Tokyo District Court recognised total damages of about ¥3.6 billion (about US$24 million) for the four works, but because the publishers had claimed only a portion of what they suffered, it ordered payment of about ¥500 million in total: ¥126,500,000 to Shogakukan, the same to Shueisha and Kodansha, and ¥121,400,928 to Kadokawa, plus interest at 3% a year from 21 October 2022 ([Tokyo District Court](https://www.courts.go.jp/ip/eng/assets/ip/eng/chizai_en/chizai_en-pdf-3813.pdf); [Shueisha](https://www.shueisha.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/notice20251119eng.pdf)).

The court dismissed the publishers' primary claim that Cloudflare was itself the transmitter of the pirated manga, but held that its failure to act promptly on infringement notices and its negligent continuation of distribution constituted aiding and abetting copyright infringement ([Shueisha](https://www.shueisha.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/notice20251119eng.pdf)). A legal analysis published in April 2026 states that Cloudflare has appealed, with hearings at the Intellectual Property High Court awaited ([Monolith Law Office](https://monolith.law/en/it/cloudflare-piracy-case-liability)).

## FAQ

**What is Shogakukan?**
Shogakukan Inc. is a Japanese publishing company founded in Tokyo on 8 August 1922 by Takeo Ōga. It began in educational publishing for elementary-school children and is now one of Japan's leading manga publishers, home of *Weekly Shōnen Sunday* and *Detective Conan*.

**What is the Hitotsubashi Group?**
The Hitotsubashi Group is a keiretsu — a set of interconnected companies with shared business relationships and shareholdings — comprising Shogakukan, Shueisha and Hakusensha. Shogakukan founded Shueisha in 1925, and the two jointly own Viz Media in the [United States](/places/united-states/).

**What does Shogakukan publish besides manga?**
Children's books, dictionaries, encyclopaedias, general magazines and fashion and lifestyle magazines such as *Oggi* and *Be-Pal*, alongside 18 comic magazines and about 100 million comic books a year (2016 figures).

**How much did Shogakukan win from Cloudflare?**
The Tokyo District Court ordered Cloudflare to pay Shogakukan ¥126,500,000 plus 3% annual interest from 21 October 2022, in a judgment dated 19 November 2025. About ¥500 million was ordered to the four publishers in total.

**Why was Shogakukan's damage period the longest?**
Its infringement notice reached Cloudflare earliest — 7 April 2020 — and its print rights dated from December 2018 and February 2019, so the court assessed damages from 8 May 2020 to 28 February 2022, against 2021 start dates for the other three publishers.
