## Overview

*Vanity Fair* is Condé Nast's American magazine of culture, politics, business, and celebrity — founded in 1913, folded into *Vogue* in 1936, and revived in 1983 as one of the company's flagship publications ([Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/vanity-fair-100-years?page=0&rel=search&query=Abrams)). In September 2026 it produced one of the year's most consequential pieces of political reporting: [Chris Whipple](/people/chris-whipple/)'s "God And Vance At Yale," a profile of Vice President [J.D. Vance](/people/jd-vance/) that contained both a revelation about his 2016 outreach to Hillary Clinton's campaign and Tucker Carlson's on-the-record claim that President Trump personally ordered the Jeffrey Epstein files covered up ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/npr-news/2026-09-16/vanity-fair-feature-profiles-the-political-development-of-jd-vance-at-yale)). The piece landed early in the tenure of Mark Guiducci, named the magazine's first global editorial director in June 2025, and it reads as a statement of what he wants the magazine to be ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/406559/mark-guiducci-to-serve-as-global-editorial-directo.html)).

## "God And Vance At Yale"

On September 16, 2026, *Vanity Fair* published Chris Whipple's feature on Vance's years at Yale Law School. Vance declined to sit for the piece — despite having given roughly 33 interviews for "Communion," his book-length project — which makes the reported portrait entirely a reconstruction from the people around him ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/npr-news/2026-09-16/vanity-fair-feature-profiles-the-political-development-of-jd-vance-at-yale)).

The piece's human core is not the bombshells but a mentor: Yale Law professor Amy Chua, the "Tiger Mother" author, who "immediately pegged" Vance as someone going places, helped turn his early musings into *Hillbilly Elegy*, and befriended Usha Chilukuri, the classmate who would become his wife ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/npr-news/2026-09-16/vanity-fair-feature-profiles-the-political-development-of-jd-vance-at-yale)). It was at Yale that Vance converted to Catholicism, and Whipple frames the law school's class of roughly 2010–2013 — classmates included Vivek Ramaswamy, Elbridge Colby, and Nate Ellis — as a cohort that is now, in his phrase, "running the country" ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/npr-news/2026-09-16/vanity-fair-feature-profiles-the-political-development-of-jd-vance-at-yale)).

## The two bombshells

The first concerned 2016. After Trump won the Republican nomination, Vance — then a prominent "Never Trump" voice — reached out through an intermediary to the Clinton campaign. His message, as Whipple reports it: "I hate Hillary, but I hate Trump more. He can't be elected." His advice was tactical: "get Hillary to the blue wall Midwestern states. Stop hanging out with Beyonce in Miami." He also contacted Clinton aide Jake Sullivan directly after the "deplorables" remark to warn that it might be a problem ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/npr-news/2026-09-16/vanity-fair-feature-profiles-the-political-development-of-jd-vance-at-yale)). Vance's office pushed back hard: spokesperson Taylor Van Kirk told the magazine, "We 100 percent reject the characterization that he was trying to help Hillary win," and Vance personally denies it — though Whipple, asked about the denial, answered simply, "the reporting speaks for itself" ([thedailybs.com](https://www.thedailybs.com/politics/jd-vance-clinton-2016-vanity-fair-profile); [NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/npr-news/2026-09-16/vanity-fair-feature-profiles-the-political-development-of-jd-vance-at-yale)).

The second came from [Tucker Carlson](/people/tucker-carlson/), on the record. Carlson told Whipple that in the summer of 2025 he pressed Trump to release all the [Epstein](/people/jeffrey-epstein/) files: "My position from the beginning with Trump, I said it to Trump, was, 'I don't understand why you would ever keep any of this stuff secret.'" He got, he said, "a bunch of different answers." But in the end, Carlson said, "it came from Trump directly" — and when Whipple asked what exactly had come directly from the president, Carlson answered: "The order to cover it up came from Trump." Asked why, Carlson said Trump gave "a long, very emotional explanation that this was all a plot against him, like Russiagate" ([NPR](https://www.nprillinois.org/npr-news/2026-09-16/vanity-fair-feature-profiles-the-political-development-of-jd-vance-at-yale); [internewsgroup.com](https://internewsgroup.com/2026/09/tucker-carlson-makes-explosive-trump-claim/)). The White House denied the claim: spokeswoman [Lauren Bis](/people/lauren-bis/) said Trump has been "totally exonerated on anything relating to Epstein," crediting him with releasing thousands of pages of documents, cooperating with a House Oversight Committee subpoena, signing the transparency act, and calling for investigations into Epstein's Democratic associates ([headlinereporter.com](https://headlinereporter.com/2026/09/tucker-carlson-makes-new-explosive-trump-claim/)). The allegation stands as Carlson's account of a private conversation, attributed and disputed — which is how this profile presents it.

## A new editor's magazine

The piece arrived barely a year into a new era. On June 10, 2025, Condé Nast named Mark Guiducci as *Vanity Fair*'s global editorial director — a newly created position, effective June 30 — succeeding Radhika Jones, who had led the magazine for seven years ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/406559/mark-guiducci-to-serve-as-global-editorial-directo.html)). Guiducci had started his career at *Vanity Fair* as an assistant to the managing editor, served as editor in chief of *Garage* magazine from 2017 to 2020, and since 2020 had been creative editorial director of *Vogue*, Condé Nast's largest title, where he launched Vogue World and the publication's TikTok channel ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/406559/mark-guiducci-to-serve-as-global-editorial-directo.html)).

A little over a month into the job, Guiducci put his stamp on the title in a staff memo: *Vanity Fair* would re-center its coverage on entertainment, celebrities, and culture along with "money, politics, and style," while cutting back "news aggregation, reviews and trade coverage" and phasing out editorial verticals including The Hive and Hollywood Daily ([Editor & Publisher](https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/vanity-fair-will-boost-focus-on-core-areas-like-hollywood-while-cutting-back-some-coverage-says,257198)). A long, reported political profile of the sitting vice president — built on original sourcing and on-the-record accusations — is that refocus in action.

## A century of Vanity Fair

The modern magazine is the second incarnation. *Vanity Fair* was founded in 1913 under founding editor Frank "Crownie" Crowninshield and published until 1936, when it was folded into *Vogue*; it was revived in 1983 and became one of Condé Nast's flagship publications, celebrating its 100th anniversary in 2013 ([Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/vanity-fair-100-years?page=0&rel=search&query=Abrams)). Its editors have included Tina Brown and Graydon Carter, and a 1914 mission statement declared: "Take a dozen or so cultivated men and women… sit them down to dinner. What will these people say? *Vanity Fair* is that dinner!" ([Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/vanity-fair-100-years?page=0&rel=search&query=Abrams)).

The talent it has published reads as a roll call of twentieth-century letters and image-making: writers Robert Benchley, Dorothy Parker, Gertrude Stein, P. G. Wodehouse, D. H. Lawrence, Aldous Huxley, Dominick Dunne, and Christopher Hitchens; photographers Cecil Beaton, Man Ray, Edward Steichen, Annie Leibovitz, and Mario Testino; illustrators Miguel Covarrubias, Edward Sorel, Bruce McCall, and Robert Risko ([Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/vanity-fair-100-years?page=0&rel=search&query=Abrams)).

It does not assert that Vance tried to help Clinton win in 2016 — his office rejects that characterization, and both positions are given. It does not present Carlson's Epstein claim as established fact: it is Carlson's attributed account of a private conversation with Trump, which the White House denies. It does not give circulation or digital-audience figures, which were not verified. And it does not claim the [Hillary Clinton](/people/hillary-clinton/) outreach went beyond what Whipple reported — one intermediary conversation, campaign advice, and a direct contact with Jake Sullivan.
