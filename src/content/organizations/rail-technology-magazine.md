## Overview

Rail Technology Magazine is a Manchester-based trade publication covering the United Kingdom's rail industry, published by Cognitive Publishing Ltd from 82 King Street, Manchester ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/terms-2)). It reports news, comment, analysis and best practice for rail professionals, and says it has connected buyers with suppliers since its first issue in 2000 ([Rail Technology Magazine](http://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/about-us-2); [Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/News-archive/empower-your-reader-choose-print-)).

It describes itself as the leading independent news brand for the UK rail industry ([Rail Technology Magazine](http://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/about-us-2)). Its trade-press role is concrete rather than ceremonial: it reported the February 2008 Barrow upon Soar footbridge collision within weeks, and it has covered Office of Rail Regulation prosecutions of [Network Rail](/organizations/network-rail/) for years ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/Rail-News/Train-strikes-footbridge); [Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/Rail-News/orr-fines-network-rail-318125-over-mans-death)). The Audit Bureau of Circulations confirms it as an ABC-audited business magazine under Cognitive Publishing Ltd, with certificates running from 2008 through 2019, though it is no longer ABC-registered ([ABC UK](https://www.abc.org.uk/product/8807-rail)).

## A trade title built on the supplier market

Rail Technology Magazine's pitch has always been aimed at sellers, not passengers. In February 2011 it announced it had become the first and only journal to obtain Link-up Supplier Patron status, in a scheme providing a single qualification process for suppliers shared by Network Rail and over 80 major rail businesses ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/Rail-News/rail-technology-magazine-is-the-first-journal-to-obtain-link-up-approval)). The same article put its readership at approximately 100,000, including senior industry decision makers, and cited its ABC certificate as independent evidence of that reach ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/Rail-News/rail-technology-magazine-is-the-first-journal-to-obtain-link-up-approval)). At Railtex 2009, the industry's trade show, the magazine said it was in its tenth year, claimed the largest readership of any UK bi-monthly and the most advertisers of any rail industry magazine, and handed out 5,000 copies of a special edition ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/Rail-News/rail-technology-magazine-makes-a-splash-at-railtex-2009)). By 2019 its print pitch had scaled the audience claim up to about 290,000 rail industry decision-makers spending over £20 billion a year ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/News-archive/empower-your-reader-choose-print-)). Those figures are the publisher's own; the independent check available is the ABC registry, which confirms audited certificates from July–December 2008 through January–December 2019 and that the title is no longer registered ([ABC UK](https://www.abc.org.uk/product/8807-rail)). The magazine is published by Cognitive Publishing Ltd, registered in England under company number 4011145, with subscription terms offering 6, 12 or 18 issues over 12, 24 or 36 months — six issues a year, a bi-monthly rhythm ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/terms-2)).

## What it actually reports

The magazine's stated remit is news, comment, analysis and best practice for the UK rail industry, produced by liaising with its audience, industry leaders and influencers ([Rail Technology Magazine](http://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/about-us-2)). It presents that reporting as unique and exclusive, aimed at understanding industry challenges and key objectives ([Rail Technology Magazine](http://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/about-us-2)). Its Railtex 2009 presence advertised editorial content including interviews with Geoff Hoon and Sir Moir Lockhead, alongside the claim of the largest readership of any UK bi-monthly ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/Rail-News/rail-technology-magazine-makes-a-splash-at-railtex-2009)). Its accident coverage shows that remit in practice. On 20 February 2008 it reported that a 31-year-old East Midlands Trains driver was in a stable condition at Leicester Royal Infirmary after his train struck the Barrow Old Station footbridge, that the 06:13 Nottingham to Norwich service had derailed but remained upright, and that the Midland Main Line stayed blocked with London services terminating at Leicester ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/Rail-News/Train-strikes-footbridge)). Earlier that month it had reported the [Rail Accident Investigation Branch](/organizations/rail-accident-investigation-branch/) (RAIB) investigation, including its focus on the circumstances of the lorry striking the bridge and the crashworthiness of the train after derailment ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/Rail-News/investigation-into-an-accident-at-barrow-on-soar-on-1-february-2008)). Its prosecution coverage follows the same pattern: in March 2013 it reported that Network Rail and GT Railway Maintenance Ltd had been fined £318,125 over the death of Liam Robinson, a maintenance worker trapped by moving machinery parts, carrying the ORR principal inspector's statement that the death was entirely avoidable ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/Rail-News/orr-fines-network-rail-318125-over-mans-death)). Earlier, in January 2009, it had reported that Maintrain Limited, part of the National Express Group, was fined £75,000 with £8,584 in costs after a maintenance worker received an electric shock at its Soho Light depot in Handsworth, Birmingham, in February 2007, when a train that should have been isolated was electrified ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/News-archive/Maintrain-fined)). The ORR investigation found inadequate risk assessment and a failure to implement safe systems of work, and the magazine quoted the ORR's principal railway inspector warning that had all the axle covers been removed, the full voltage of 25,000 volts could have passed through the worker ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/News-archive/Maintrain-fined)). The current team page lists an unnamed Interim Editor, an online reporter, a multimedia reporter, a business development director and a publisher, Roy V. Rowlands ([Rail Technology Magazine](http://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/about-us-2)).

## The business model

Rail Technology Magazine sells access to buyers. Its 2019 print pitch argues that print advertising is still seen as a safer and more credible channel, that a social-media post may be seen for only three seconds while a print advertisement stays in the customer's hands, and that combining print and digital news brands can deliver a higher return on investment ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/News-archive/empower-your-reader-choose-print-)). The publisher presents itself under the tagline "Magazines That Mean Business," and the magazine operates across print, a digital edition, a website and events ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://railtechnologymagazine.com/Current-Edition); [Rail Technology Magazine](http://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/about-us-2)). The commercial logic is visible in its history: at Railtex 2009 it promoted both the magazine and its advertisers, giving them extra exposure at no extra charge, and launched rtmjobs.com, a rail-industry-specific jobs board that visitors welcomed as overdue ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/Rail-News/rail-technology-magazine-makes-a-splash-at-railtex-2009)). The Link-up Supplier Patron status fits the same model — a credential that positions the title inside the supplier qualification world its advertisers inhabit ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/Rail-News/rail-technology-magazine-is-the-first-journal-to-obtain-link-up-approval)). Subscriptions are sold in 12-, 24- and 36-month terms with a single-user licence and a digital edition alongside print ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/terms-2)). The publisher sits inside a wider stable: Cognitive Publishing Ltd is a subsidiary of Cognitive Business Media Ltd, whose other brands listed on the magazine's site include National Health Executive and Public Sector Executive ([Rail Technology Magazine](https://railtechnologymagazine.com/Current-Edition)). In short, the magazine is a business-to-business title: its readers are the industry's buyers, its advertisers are the industry's suppliers, and its editorial product is the news that connects the two ([Rail Technology Magazine](http://www.railtechnologymagazine.com/about-us-2)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Rail Technology Magazine?**
It is a Manchester-based trade publication covering the UK rail industry, published by Cognitive Publishing Ltd. It reports news, comment, analysis and best practice for rail professionals, and sells subscriptions and advertising to the industry's suppliers, with a jobs board at rtmjobs.com alongside the magazine.

**Who publishes Rail Technology Magazine?**
Cognitive Publishing Ltd, based at 82 King Street, Manchester, and registered in England under company number 4011145. The magazine's team page names Roy V. Rowlands as publisher, with an unnamed Interim Editor leading the editorial side. Cognitive Publishing is a subsidiary of Cognitive Business Media Ltd, whose other brands include National Health Executive and Public Sector Executive.

**How often is Rail Technology Magazine published?**
It is a bi-monthly title: subscription terms offer 6 issues per 12 months, 12 per 24 months, or 18 per 36 months. The magazine itself claimed at Railtex 2009 to have the largest readership of any UK bi-monthly, and its site offers a digital edition and a current issue.

**Is Rail Technology Magazine still ABC-audited?**
No. The Audit Bureau of Circulations registry lists ABC certificates for the title from July–December 2008 through January–December 2019, and states that the magazine is no longer registered with ABC. Its earlier articles cite the ABC certificate as evidence of its readership.
