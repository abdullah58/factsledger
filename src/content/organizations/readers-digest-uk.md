## Overview

Reader's Digest UK was the British edition of the American general-interest magazine *Reader's Digest*, first published in the United Kingdom in 1938 and closed in April 2024 after 86 years ([NDA News UK](https://nda.news/amp/readers-digest-st_583685/news/its-farewell-to-readers-digest-as-british-edition-of-iconic-magazine-closes-down-sn_5803126/)). A monthly built on condensed articles, it sold more than a million copies a month in 2000, then passed through administration, two rescues and three owners before financial pressures ended it ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/readers-digest-uk-closure/)).

The UK edition was the magazine's first publication outside the United States, and for its first 72 years it was part of the American parent company, publishing under the motto "Articles of enduring interest" ([NDA News UK](https://nda.news/amp/readers-digest-st_583685/news/its-farewell-to-readers-digest-as-british-edition-of-iconic-magazine-closes-down-sn_5803126/)). That changed in 2010, when the UK arm went into administration and was sold off; from then on it was a separately owned British business publishing under a licence from the US parent ([Accountancy Age](https://accountancyage.com/2010/04/12/moore-stephens-sells-readers-digest-to-jon-moulton-business/)). The US edition, where the magazine was founded in 1922, continues to be published by Trusted Media Brands ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/readers-digest-uk-closure/)).

## A British edition of an American original

*Reader's Digest* was the brainchild of DeWitt Wallace and Lila Bell Wallace, a married American couple who conceived a monthly magazine made of condensations of articles from other periodicals, an idea Wallace developed while recovering from a World War I injury ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/readers-digest-magazine)). The first edition was sold directly to subscribers attracted through mailed-out publicity, and the formula worked: by the end of the 1920s almost 300,000 Americans subscribed, and by 1940 more than four million did ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/readers-digest-magazine)). In 1940 the magazine was published only in the United States and the United Kingdom, and by 1950 it had more than 15 million readers around the world ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/readers-digest-magazine)).

The British edition, launched in 1938, became a fixture in its own right, and its later decades show how the business around the magazine changed: from the 1950s the magazine added conventional advertising, condensed fiction and sweepstakes contests to the original digest formula ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/history/readers-digest-magazine)). Along with Book Club Associates, Reader's Digest was one of the first UK companies to embrace direct marketing, and its subscription programme led the way in the 1970s and 1980s, although its reliance on prize draws and promotions drew criticism from some quarters ([Decision Marketing](http://www.decisionmarketing.co.uk/news/readers-digest-sold-for-a-quid)). In its final years its front covers featured celebrities including Sharon Osbourne, Ricky Gervais, Idris Elba and Judi Dench ([NDA News UK](https://nda.news/amp/readers-digest-st_583685/news/its-farewell-to-readers-digest-as-british-edition-of-iconic-magazine-closes-down-sn_5803126/)).

The circulation table above tells the commercial story in one glance: from more than a million copies a month in 2000 to 403,458 in the first half of 2010, then 186,802 in the second half of 2013 and 106,335 in July to December 2016, after which ABC stopped publishing a figure at all ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/readers-digest-uk-closure/); [Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/readers-digest-uk-sold-nominal-sum-tv-entrepreneur-four-years-after-it-went-administration/)). The stat that anchors the decline is the 23.7 percent fall in a single half-year to 2013 — a magazine losing nearly a quarter of its sales in six months, with 89.3 percent of the remaining copies paid for, meaning there was no free-distribution cushion left ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/readers-digest-uk-sold-nominal-sum-tv-entrepreneur-four-years-after-it-went-administration/)).

## Administration, rescues and closure

The crisis came in February 2010, when administrators from Moore Stephens were appointed to the UK arm after plans to deal with its pension liability fell through ([Accountancy Age](https://accountancyage.com/2010/04/12/moore-stephens-sells-readers-digest-to-jon-moulton-business/)). The £125m pension liability stayed in the old company, with the Pensions Regulator, the Pension Protection Fund and the administrators left to resolve it, while the business itself was sold on ([Accountancy Age](https://accountancyage.com/2010/04/12/moore-stephens-sells-readers-digest-to-jon-moulton-business/)). In April 2010, Better Capital's BECAP fund backed a management buyout of the business in a deal worth £13m, led by managing director Chris Spratling, with 100 people employed and no bank debt, publishing through a licensing deal with its US counterpart ([Accountancy Age](https://accountancyage.com/2010/04/12/moore-stephens-sells-readers-digest-to-jon-moulton-business/); [InPublishing](https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/future-secured-for-readers-digest-in-the-uk-13240)). The business it bought was far wider than the magazine: a marketer of magazines, books, music, video, financial services and healthcare products, supported by its prize-draw offering ([InPublishing](https://www.inpublishing.co.uk/articles/future-secured-for-readers-digest-in-the-uk-13240)).

The rescue did not hold. Better Capital spent £23m on a direct-marketing division, DM Partners, which was scrapped in January 2013 with the loss of over 100 jobs, and the company began insolvency proceedings the same month ([Decision Marketing](http://www.decisionmarketing.co.uk/news/readers-digest-sold-for-a-quid)). That January it also sacked 95 out of 120 staff after closing its retail business, and it entered a company voluntary arrangement with creditors that let it keep trading while exiting unprofitable activities ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/readers-digest-uk-sold-nominal-sum-tv-entrepreneur-four-years-after-it-went-administration/); [NDA News UK](https://nda.news/amp/readers-digest-st_583685/news/its-farewell-to-readers-digest-as-british-edition-of-iconic-magazine-closes-down-sn_5803126/)). In February 2014 Better Capital sold the magazine for a nominal sum — £1 — to venture capitalist Mike Luckwell, who planned to target the over-50s market and expand the brand into financial services to emulate the success of Saga ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/readers-digest-uk-sold-nominal-sum-tv-entrepreneur-four-years-after-it-went-administration/)). Jon Moulton, who led Better Capital, said he was selling because "from the viewpoint of the fund, the business didn't justify the time and effort for us" ([Decision Marketing](http://www.decisionmarketing.co.uk/news/readers-digest-sold-for-a-quid)). Part of the strain was structural: the US company still owned the name and took a percentage of profits for its use, and protracted negotiations over that licence were thought to be at the heart of the financial woes ([Decision Marketing](http://www.decisionmarketing.co.uk/news/readers-digest-sold-for-a-quid)).

Luckwell did return the magazine to profit and sold it in 2018 to its former chief executive Gary Hopkins, who had worked closely with him on the turnaround ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/readers-digest-uk-closure/)). Vivat Direct Ltd continued to control the UK brand, although Hopkins was no longer involved by the time of the closure ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/readers-digest-uk-closure/)). The end came in April 2024, when editor-in-chief Eva Mackevic — who had contributed to the magazine for nearly eight years and led its team for six — announced on LinkedIn that it had "come to an end" after 86 years ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/readers-digest-uk-closure/); [NDA News UK](https://nda.news/amp/readers-digest-st_583685/news/its-farewell-to-readers-digest-as-british-edition-of-iconic-magazine-closes-down-sn_5803126/)). "Unfortunately, the company just couldn't withstand the financial pressures of today's unforgiving magazine publishing landscape and has ceased to trade," she wrote, adding that insolvency practitioners were working to communicate with writers waiting to be paid ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/readers-digest-uk-closure/)). The last articles on its homepage appeared on Wednesday 24 April 2024, and the May 2024 edition, priced at £4.50, appears to have been its final one ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/readers-digest-uk-closure/); [NDA News UK](https://nda.news/amp/readers-digest-st_583685/news/its-farewell-to-readers-digest-as-british-edition-of-iconic-magazine-closes-down-sn_5803126/)). The Canadian edition had shut down at the end of 2023 for similar reasons, blaming declining advertising revenue and rising production and delivery costs ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/magazines/readers-digest-uk-closure/)).

## Late coverage: Tallinn's Banned Books Museum

In its final years the magazine's travel and lifestyle pages still reached beyond Britain. On 30 June 2022 Reader's Digest UK published David Hudson's feature "How Tallinn's Banned Books Museum Defends Free Speech", covering the small museum of censored books in Tallinn's Old Town ([Reader's Digest UK](https://www.readersdigest.co.uk/lifestyle/travel/how-tallins-banned-books-museum-defends-free-speech)). The piece described the museum's founder and mission and its collection as it stood at that date, and it is one of the independent press features that documented the museum in its first years — alongside coverage by Book Riot, Euronews and others. The museum, the [Banned Books Museum](/organizations/banned-books-museum/), opened in Tallinn in December 2020, so the Reader's Digest feature came about eighteen months into its life.
