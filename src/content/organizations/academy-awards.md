The Academy Awards are presented each year by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences (AMPAS), the nonprofit film-industry organization founded in 1927 — but this profile is about the institution, not the telecast. The Academy is a voting body of roughly 11,300 members across 18 professional branches, headquartered in Beverly Hills, that licenses the Oscars broadcast, runs a film museum in Los Angeles, and since 2024 ties Best Picture eligibility to representation standards.

## Overview

The Academy was spearheaded by MGM head Louis B. Mayer and organized in May 1927 as a nonprofit dedicated to the advancement of the film industry ([HISTORY](https://www.History.com/this-day-in-history/February-18/first-academy-awards-announced)). The awards committee was only one of several committees the new body formed: the young Academy was preoccupied with labor disputes, the industry's tarnished public image, and the exchange of production ideas — the trophies came second ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/Academy-Award)). In May 1928 it approved Academy Awards of Merit in 12 categories, and the first awards — announced in February 1929 and presented on May 16, 1929 in the Blossom Room of the Hollywood Roosevelt Hotel before about 270 guests paying $5 a ticket — honored Janet Gaynor, Emil Jannings, and the World War I drama *Wings* ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/Academy-Award); [HISTORY](https://www.History.com/this-day-in-history/February-18/first-academy-awards-announced)). Douglas Fairbanks, the silent-era star, was the Academy's first president and handed out the first statuettes ([HISTORY](https://www.History.com/this-day-in-history/February-18/first-academy-awards-announced)). The industry club is now a global broadcast business — and has spent a decade rebuilding who votes.

## The business behind the telecast

The Academy's core asset is the vote: only members nominate and vote, with nominees chosen inside each craft's branch and the full membership voting on Best Picture and most winners ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/Academy-Award)). After the June 2026 invite class of 529, the body numbers 11,319 members including emeritus — 10,338 of them voting — up from roughly 6,000 a decade earlier ([Associated Press](https://mb.com.ph/2026/06/25/film-academy-invites-529-new-members-including-jenna-ortega-the-safdie-brothers-and-jacob-elordi)). It is organized into 18 branches, the newest the 2023 Production and Technology Branch for about 400 previously members-at-large professionals ([Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences](http://press.oscars.org/news/academy-establishes-new-production-and-technology-branch)). The Board of Governors elects the president — producer Lynette Howell Taylor, the fifth woman in the role, since July 2025 — alongside CEO Bill Kramer ([AFP](https://www.malaymail.com/news/showbiz/2025/08/01/british-producer-lynette-howell-taylor-named-new-oscars-academy-president/186015)). ABC has carried the Oscars since 1976; in 2025 the Academy announced live YouTube streaming from 2029 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/Academy-Award)), as the telecast drew about 20 million viewers for the March 2026 ceremony, up from a 10.4 million pandemic low ([AFP](https://www.malaymail.com/news/showbiz/2025/08/01/british-producer-lynette-howell-taylor-named-new-oscars-academy-president/186015)).

## The post-#OscarsSoWhite remake

In 2015, all 20 acting nominees were white, and activist April Reign's #OscarsSoWhite hashtag put the Academy's 75%-male, 92%-white membership under a lasting spotlight ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/Academy-Award); [Associated Press](https://mb.com.ph/2026/06/25/film-academy-invites-529-new-members-including-jenna-ortega-the-safdie-brothers-and-jacob-elordi)). The response was structural. Membership nearly doubled, and after the 2026 class the Academy is 64% male and 75% white, with 22% voting from outside the United States; the newest class is 42% women, 56% from underrepresented communities, and 53% non-U.S. ([Associated Press](https://mb.com.ph/2026/06/25/film-academy-invites-529-new-members-including-jenna-ortega-the-safdie-brothers-and-jacob-elordi)). Then came the rules: under Academy Aperture 2025, films must meet two of four inclusion standards — on-screen representation, creative leadership, industry access, audience development — for Best Picture eligibility, effective from the 96th Oscars in 2024, in a framework modeled on the British Film Institute's standards for the BAFTAs ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/oscars-set-new-inclusion-and-diversity-standards-for-best-picture-eligibility/)). The Academy's voting body grew from roughly 6,000 members in 2016 to 11,319 — 10,338 voting — by mid-2026 ([Associated Press](https://mb.com.ph/2026/06/25/film-academy-invites-529-new-members-including-jenna-ortega-the-safdie-brothers-and-jacob-elordi)), meaning the Oscars are now decided by a body nearly twice the size that produced the #OscarsSoWhite-era shortlists, with nearly a quarter voting from abroad.

## The statuette, the secrecy, and the separate honorary dinner

The Gibbons-designed, George Stanley-sculpted statuette stands 13.5 inches tall and weighs 8.5 pounds; cast in bronze and plated with 24-karat gold since 2016, after a wartime plaster detour and a 1982–2015 britannia-metal run ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/Academy-Award)). Its nickname was never settled — Bette Davis, Sidney Skolsky, and Margaret Herrick all get credit for "Oscar" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/Academy-Award)). Sealed envelopes date to 1940, when the *Los Angeles Times* printed the winners early ([HISTORY](https://www.History.com/this-day-in-history/February-18/first-academy-awards-announced)). The ceremony was first televised in the U.S. in 1953, internationally since 1969, and has sat in the Dolby Theatre since 2002 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/Academy-Award)). Since 2009, the honorary, Thalberg, and Hersholt awards have gone out at a non-televised Governors Awards dinner ([TheWrap](http://www.thewrap.com/academy-choose-honorary-oscar-winners-29677/)). The Academy also owns and operates the Academy Museum of Motion Pictures in Los Angeles, opened September 30, 2021 in Renzo Piano's Saban Building ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2021/03/10/renzo-piano-academy-museum-of-motion-pictures-los-angeles-2/)). Its reach is global: a record 92 countries entered the 90th Academy Awards' Foreign Language Film race, including Turkey's [Ayla: The Daughter of War](/creative-works/ayla-the-daughter-of-war/) (Can Ülkay), which was not nominated ([SHOOTonline](https://www.shootonline.com/article/92-nations-vying-foreign-language-film-oscar-haiti-honduras-among-1st-time-entrants/); [Prothom Alo](http://en.prothomalo.com/amp/story/entertainment/Oscar-nominees-in-main-categories)).

## FAQ

**What is the difference between an Academy Award and an Oscar?**
There is none in practice: the prizes are officially the Academy Awards, presented by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, and "Oscar" is the nickname of the gold-plated statuette itself ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/Academy-Award)). Three competing stories explain the nickname — Bette Davis, Sidney Skolsky, and Margaret Herrick's "Uncle Oscar" — and its true origin was never determined.

**Who actually votes for the Oscars?**
Only Academy members. Nominees in each category are chosen by members of the corresponding craft branch — directors nominate directors, writers nominate writers — while the full membership nominates Best Picture candidates and votes on the winners in most categories ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/Academy-Award)). As of mid-2026 there were 10,338 voting members ([Associated Press](https://mb.com.ph/2026/06/25/film-academy-invites-529-new-members-including-jenna-ortega-the-safdie-brothers-and-jacob-elordi)).

**What are the Academy's inclusion standards for Best Picture?**
Since the 96th Oscars in 2024, a film must meet two of four standards to be eligible for Best Picture: on-screen representation and themes; creative leadership and project teams; industry access and opportunities; and audience development ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/oscars-set-new-inclusion-and-diversity-standards-for-best-picture-eligibility/)). They were introduced under the Academy Aperture 2025 program and modeled on the British Film Institute's diversity standards.

**Is this profile about the 90th Academy Awards ceremony?**
No. This profile covers the Academy Awards as an institution — the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, its membership, rules, and history. The 90th ceremony (held March 4, 2018 at the Dolby Theatre) is a separate event; the Academy's institutional role there included administering the Foreign Language Film race that Turkey's *Ayla: The Daughter of War* entered ([SHOOTonline](https://www.shootonline.com/article/92-nations-vying-foreign-language-film-oscar-haiti-honduras-among-1st-time-entrants/)).

**Does the Academy run anything besides the awards?**
Yes. It owns and operates the Academy Museum of Motion Pictures in Los Angeles, which opened September 30, 2021 in Renzo Piano's renovation of the 1939 Saban Building at Wilshire and Fairfax ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2021/03/10/renzo-piano-academy-museum-of-motion-pictures-los-angeles-2/)). It also presents scientific and technical awards and runs the separate Governors Awards dinner for honorary honors ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/art/Academy-Award)).
