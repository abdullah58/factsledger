## Overview

General Motors Company — GM — is an American multinational automaker headquartered in [Detroit](/places/detroit/), [Michigan](/places/michigan/), founded on September 16, 1908 by William C. Durant as a holding company for Buick ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)). It was the world's largest motor-vehicle manufacturer for much of the 20th and early 21st centuries, went bankrupt in 2009 in one of the largest industrial bankruptcies in US history, and was rebuilt with federal rescue money into the four-brand company — Chevrolet, Buick, GMC and Cadillac — that exists today ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)). In 2025 it earned $2.7 billion on $185 billion in revenue, its net income cut by more than half by $7.9 billion in charges for restructuring its electric-vehicle business ([WardsAuto](https://www.wardsauto.com/news/gm-reports-55-billion-decline-net-income-2025-ev-charges-earnings/810616/)).

## From holding company to the world's largest automaker

Durant built GM by acquisition. The company was founded in 1908 to consolidate motorcar companies producing Buick, Oldsmobile, Cadillac, Oakland (later Pontiac), Ewing and Marquette, plus Reliance and Rapid trucks ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)). GM introduced the electric self-starter commercially in its 1912 Cadillac, making the hand crank obsolete ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)). Durant was forced out in 1920 and succeeded by Alfred P. Sloan Jr., who served as president from 1923 to 1937 and chairman until 1956, reorganizing a sprawling collection of units into five divisions — Cadillac, Buick, Pontiac, Oldsmobile and Chevrolet — coordinated by a central office, a decentralized management model that became the template for large American corporations ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)).

The Sloan system delivered dominance. By 1929 GM had surpassed [Ford](/organizations/ford/) to become the leading American passenger-car manufacturer, and in 1931 it became the world's largest manufacturer of motor vehicles; by 1941 it was making 44 percent of all cars in the [United States](/places/united-states/) ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)). The company held 40 to 45 percent of total US automotive sales through the 1950s and 1960s, and the Chevrolet Corvette, introduced in 1953, became one of the most recognizable American sports cars ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)). GM also reached beyond cars: it bought the data-processing company EDS in 1984 and Hughes Aircraft in 1986, then sold both in the 1990s to refocus on the automotive business ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)). In 1984 it opened NUMMI with [Toyota](/organizations/toyota/), resurrecting a closed GM plant in Fremont, California to build vehicles under both brands for 25 years ([EBSCO Research Starters](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/toyota-motor-corporation/)).

## Bankruptcy and rebirth

The decline was slow, then sudden. GM discontinued Oldsmobile in 2004, and in 2008 Toyota surpassed it as the world's largest automaker ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)). In December 2008 President George W. Bush announced an emergency rescue plan making $13.4 billion in Troubled Asset Relief Program loans immediately available to GM and Chrysler; Ford had sufficient funds and did not apply ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)). With debts totaling $173 billion, GM filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in June 2009 and emerged from reorganization the following month as a new company ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)).

The reborn GM was smaller and sharper. In 2010 it discontinued Pontiac and Saturn and sold Saab, leaving four vehicle divisions — Buick, Cadillac, Chevrolet and GMC — and in November 2010 it returned to the stock market with one of the largest initial public offerings in US history; in 2011 it regained its title as the largest automaker in the world ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)). In 2017 it sold Opel and Vauxhall to the PSA Group for €2.2 billion, effectively ending its European operations ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)).

The recovery was shadowed by a reckoning. In 2014 GM became embroiled in a scandal over defective ignition switches that could shut engines off while vehicles were moving, preventing airbags from deploying; the company had identified the problem as early as 2001 but failed to address it for more than a decade ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)). GM recalled millions of vehicles, and a compensation program for victims ultimately recognized 124 deaths and 275 injuries linked to the defect ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)). Mary Barra, who became GM's first female CEO in January 2014, oversaw the response — an independent investigation, changes to vehicle safety procedures, and compensation for victims and their families ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)).

## Strikes, EVs, and the Cruise write-off

Labor shaped GM's recent decade twice. In September 2019 nearly 50,000 United Auto Workers members struck for 40 days over wages, benefits and plant closures ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)). In September 2023 the UAW struck GM, Ford and [Stellantis](/organizations/stellantis/) simultaneously — the first time the union had targeted all three automakers at once — and GM's settlement provided wage increases of 25 percent over four and a half years, restored cost-of-living adjustments, and extended union protections to workers at the Ultium Cells battery joint venture ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)).

Electrification has been GM's most expensive pivot. In 2021 the company set a goal of eliminating tailpipe emissions from new light-duty vehicles by 2035, but the transition stumbled: it recalled all Chevrolet Bolt EVs that year over battery fire risk, and after federal EV purchase tax credits ended for vehicles acquired after September 30, 2025, GM scaled back its EV production plans and investments ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)). The company recorded $7.9 billion in charges related to its EV restructuring in 2025 and another $3.6 billion during the first nine months of 2026, while continuing to rely heavily on gasoline-powered trucks and SUVs for profits ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)).

The autonomous-vehicle bet ended the same way. GM acquired a majority stake in Cruise in 2016, but after a Cruise vehicle struck and dragged a pedestrian in San Francisco in October 2023, driverless operations were suspended; in December 2024 GM said it would stop funding Cruise's robotaxi development, having invested more than $10 billion in the company since 2016 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)). The focus shifted to driver assistance for personal vehicles, including the Super Cruise hands-free system, with a more advanced version planned for 2028 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)). In the cabin, GM shares a supplier with its rivals: [Cerence](/organizations/cerence/), spun off from [Nuance Communications](/organizations/nuance-communications/) on October 1, 2019, counts GM among the nearly all major automakers its voice technology supports, in nearly 300 million vehicles as of 2020 ([CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2020/10/08/nio-cerence-collaboration-on-voice-tech/)).

The gap between the $2.7 billion net income and the $12.7 billion adjusted EBIT is almost entirely the EV reset: $6 billion of the charges landed in the fourth quarter, including $4.2 billion in cash for supplier settlements and contract cancellations ([WardsAuto](https://www.wardsauto.com/news/gm-reports-55-billion-decline-net-income-2025-ev-charges-earnings/810616/)). Yet the core business was strong — combined sales of GMC and Chevrolet pickups reached 940,000 units, the best in 20 years — and GM bought back $6 billion in stock in 2025 while its board authorized another $6 billion for 2026 ([WardsAuto](https://www.wardsauto.com/news/gm-reports-55-billion-decline-net-income-2025-ev-charges-earnings/810616/)). Guidance for 2026 calls for adjusted EBIT of $13 to $15 billion at 8 to 10 percent margins, and GM expects annual US vehicle production to reach 2 million units in 2027 ([WardsAuto](https://www.wardsauto.com/news/gm-reports-55-billion-decline-net-income-2025-ev-charges-earnings/810616/)).

The company also reshaped its corporate footprint in 2026: in January it moved its global headquarters to Hudson's Detroit, a new downtown development, from the Renaissance Center that had housed it since the 1990s — returning to Woodward Avenue, where it established its first headquarters in 1911 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)). In October 2026 GM said it would transfer its stock listing to [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/) from the New York Stock Exchange, effective November 17, a move that would also make it eligible for the Nasdaq-100 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)).

## FAQ

**When was General Motors founded?**
William C. Durant founded the General Motors Company on September 16, 1908, as a holding company consolidating Buick, Oldsmobile, Cadillac and other early automakers ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)).

**What happened to GM in 2009?**
With debts of $173 billion, GM filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in June 2009 after receiving $13.4 billion in emergency federal TARP loans, and emerged the following month as a new company; in 2010 it shed Pontiac, Saturn and Saab and returned to the stock market ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)).

**Who is GM's CEO?**
Mary Barra, chair and CEO, who became GM's first female CEO in January 2014 and oversaw the response to the ignition-switch scandal ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)).

**What brands does GM own?**
Four vehicle divisions: Chevrolet, Buick, GMC and Cadillac; Pontiac, Saturn, Oldsmobile, Saab, Opel and Vauxhall were all discontinued or sold ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/General-Motors-Corporation)).

**How many vehicles did GM sell in 2025?**
GM sold 2.85 million vehicles in the US in 2025, up 6 percent, including 940,000 combined GMC and Chevrolet full-size pickups — the best in 20 years ([WardsAuto](https://www.wardsauto.com/news/gm-reports-55-billion-decline-net-income-2025-ev-charges-earnings/810616/)).
