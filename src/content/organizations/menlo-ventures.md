## Overview

Menlo Ventures is a Menlo Park, California venture capital firm founded in 1976 by H. DuBose Montgomery, a 27-year-old electrical engineer fresh from Bell Labs who opened the firm on Sand Hill Road ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/menlo-turns-50-and-announces-3b-in-fresh-capital-to-go-all-in-on-ai/)). Across five decades it backed Gilead Sciences, Siri, Roku and [Uber](/organizations/uber/) before going all-in on artificial intelligence: its [Anthropic](/organizations/anthropic/) stake is now worth about $14 billion, and in June 2026 it raised $3 billion, the largest fund in its history ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/23/after-betting-the-firm-on-anthropic-menlo-ventures-raises-victorious-3b-fund/)).

## Fifty years of being early

Menlo's founding bet was not on software at all but on biotechnology: in 1987 the firm incubated Gilead Sciences, founded by Menlo associate Michael Riordan, and Gilead went on to raise $86.25 million in a 1992 initial public offering ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/entrepreneur/menlo-ventures-banks-on-tech-driven-life-sciences-adds-greg-yap-as-new-partner)). The firm then stepped away from life sciences in the mid-1990s, judging the sector too capital-intensive — its companies often took 12 to 14 years to show returns, longer than a typical 10-year fund life — and returned only in 2017, hiring Greg Yap as a partner and committing 15 percent of its $450 million Menlo XIV fund to companies at the intersection of biology and technology ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/entrepreneur/menlo-ventures-banks-on-tech-driven-life-sciences-adds-greg-yap-as-new-partner)).

The consumer era produced the firm's signature wins. Partner Shawn Carolan was the first institutional investor in Siri, the voice assistant Apple bought for a little more than $200 million in 2010, and Menlo accumulated a 35.3 percent pre-IPO stake in streaming company Roku ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/04/29/getting-a-piece-of-uber/)). The defining deal came in 2011, when Menlo led Uber's $32 million Series B at a $322 million valuation — stepping in after the company's first-choice investor, [Andreessen Horowitz](/organizations/andreessen-horowitz/), declined the terms — and eventually invested $66.5 million in the company, with expected total returns of up to $3.1 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/04/29/getting-a-piece-of-uber/)). In 2018 a SoftBank-led group bought nearly half of Menlo's Uber stock for $973 million, a 93-times return on shares the firm had paid $10.5 million for, and Roku's IPO plus the Uber sale brought in roughly $2 billion in returns across 2017–2018 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/04/29/getting-a-piece-of-uber/)).

Menlo's fintech record includes leading [BlueVine](/organizations/bluevine/)'s $60 million Series E in 2018, with [SVB Capital](/organizations/svb-capital/) participating, bringing the small-business lender's total financing to $578 million at a point when it had funded more than $900 million in loans to over 10,000 customers ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/bluevine-brings-in-60-million/)). Carolan also invested in [Chime](/organizations/chime/), the mobile banking platform valued at $1.5 billion in March 2019 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/04/29/getting-a-piece-of-uber/)).

## The Anthropic bet — and the all-in turn to AI

Menlo's AI turn began with a single position. The firm first invested in Anthropic in 2023, as an early investor in its Series C, before the company had a product — a bet led by partner Matt Murphy ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/23/after-betting-the-firm-on-anthropic-menlo-ventures-raises-victorious-3b-fund/)). A year later it led Anthropic's Series D, a round of more than $750 million, investing more than $500 million from funds it managed — much of it structured as a special-purpose vehicle pooling outside capital for the single deal, an eye-catching structure in a venture market still recovering from the post-pandemic downturn ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/23/after-betting-the-firm-on-anthropic-menlo-ventures-raises-victorious-3b-fund/)). The round quadrupled Anthropic's valuation to about $18 billion, and Menlo has invested in every Anthropic round since ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/23/after-betting-the-firm-on-anthropic-menlo-ventures-raises-victorious-3b-fund/)).

The scale of the outcome is what reshaped the firm: having put roughly $1 billion into Anthropic over the years, Menlo's stake is now worth approximately $14 billion, according to Bloomberg reporting cited by TechCrunch and TechFundingNews — a paper gain on an unrealized position, not cash returned to investors ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/23/after-betting-the-firm-on-anthropic-menlo-ventures-raises-victorious-3b-fund/); [TechFundingNews](https://techfundingnews.com/the-vc-that-turned-a-750m-anthropic-deal-into-a-14b-stake-just-raised-3b-to-do-it-again/)). Menlo then turned the relationship into an ecosystem: in July 2024 it launched the $100 million Anthology Fund with Anthropic to back pre-seed, seed and Series A AI startups, writing checks from $100,000 and offering founders $25,000 in credits for Anthropic's models ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/17/menlo-ventures-and-anthropic-team-up-on-a-100m-ai-fund/)). By June 2026 the fund had deployed about $250 million across more than 60 companies, with early exits including Graphite, acquired by Cursor, and Astrix Security, which Cisco announced its intent to acquire in May 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/23/after-betting-the-firm-on-anthropic-menlo-ventures-raises-victorious-3b-fund/)).

## Funds and scale

The June 2026 raise capped a steady climb in fund size, with each cycle re-anchoring the firm around the technology wave of the moment:

| Menlo Special Opportunities III | July 2022 | $761.4 million | Special opportunities |
| Menlo XVI + Menlo Inflection III | November 2023 | $1.35 billion | Seed–Series A; Series B and beyond |
| Menlo Ventures XVII + Menlo Inflection IV | June 2026 | $3 billion | Seed–Series A; Series B and beyond |

Fund sizes and dates are from [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/16/menlo-ventures-closes-on-1-35b-in-new-capital-targets-investments-in-ai-startups/) (2020–2023 funds) and [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/23/after-betting-the-firm-on-anthropic-menlo-ventures-raises-victorious-3b-fund/) (2026 funds). With the 2023 close, Menlo reported $5.6 billion under management, more than $3.8 billion raised across eight fund groups, and $5.2 billion distributed to its limited partners ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/16/menlo-ventures-closes-on-1-35b-in-new-capital-targets-investments-in-ai-startups/)). After the $3 billion raise, TechFundingNews reported the firm managing more than $8.5 billion in capital ([TechFundingNews](https://techfundingnews.com/the-vc-that-turned-a-750m-anthropic-deal-into-a-14b-stake-just-raised-3b-to-do-it-again/)). Its stated portfolio record as of November 2023 was 80 exits — 15 public listings and 65 acquisitions, including Tenor's sale to Google and PillPack's to Amazon ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/16/menlo-ventures-closes-on-1-35b-in-new-capital-targets-investments-in-ai-startups/)). Across the AI stack it now backs infrastructure names including Axiom, Chai Discovery, Goodfire, Neon, OpenRouter and Skild AI, and applications including Eve, Higgsfield, Legora, Lovable, OpenEvidence, Semgrep, Suno and Wispr Flow ([Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/menlo-turns-50-and-announces-3b-in-fresh-capital-to-go-all-in-on-ai/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim Menlo has realized any return on its Anthropic stake — the roughly $14 billion figure is a paper valuation of an unrealized position. It does not reconcile the $18 billion versus $18.4 billion reports of the Series D valuation, the $32 million versus $37 million reports of Uber's Series B, or the sharply different exit counts reported in November 2023 and June 2026; each discrepancy is flagged in the open items above. The founding narrative — Montgomery aged 27, fresh from Bell Labs, opening on Sand Hill Road — rests on Menlo's own account, not an independent source. Menlo's enterprise AI spending research and any Anthropic IPO reporting were not verified from fully-read sources and are deliberately not covered here.
