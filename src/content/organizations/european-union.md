## Overview

The European Union (EU) is a unique economic and political union between 27 European countries that together cover much of the continent — the world's largest trade block, its biggest exporter of manufactured goods and services, and the biggest import market for over 100 countries ([European Commission](https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/glossary/european-union)). It is not a country: its member states share institutions and a single market rather than a single national government, and in competition cases it is the European Commission — the EU's executive institution — that acts, as it did when it issued a formal antitrust complaint over [Adobe](/organizations/adobe/)'s proposed $20 billion acquisition of [Figma](/organizations/figma/) in November 2023 ([European Commission](https://ieu-monitoring.com/editorial/eu-commission-sends-adobe-statement-of-objections-over-acquisition-of-figma/421117?utm_source=ieu-portal)).

The EU's main economic engine is its single market, which "enables most goods, services, money and people to move freely" ([European Commission](https://trade.ec.europa.eu/access-to-markets/en/glossary/european-union)). The Commission describes the market as guaranteeing the free movement of goods, capital, services and labour, creating "a borderless economic area where individuals and businesses can operate freely," covering the 27 member states plus Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein through the European Economic Area, with 450 million consumers, 34 million businesses and €18 trillion in GDP ([European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/single-market_en)). That shared market is precisely why a merger between two American software companies fell under EU review: the Commission has the duty to assess mergers above set turnover thresholds and "to prevent concentrations that would significantly impede effective competition" in the European Economic Area ([European Commission](https://ieu-monitoring.com/editorial/eu-commission-sends-adobe-statement-of-objections-over-acquisition-of-figma/421117?utm_source=ieu-portal)).

## The Commission's case against Adobe–Figma

The Adobe/Figma transaction reached the Commission by an unusual route: it was notified on 30 June 2023 after being referred by 16 countries under Article 22 of the EU Merger Regulation, and the Commission opened an in-depth investigation on 7 August 2023 ([European Commission](https://ieu-monitoring.com/editorial/eu-commission-sends-adobe-statement-of-objections-over-acquisition-of-figma/421117?utm_source=ieu-portal)). The investigation drew on internal documents from the parties and views from competing software providers and customers, and the Commission cooperated closely with other competition authorities throughout ([European Commission](https://ieu-monitoring.com/editorial/eu-commission-sends-adobe-statement-of-objections-over-acquisition-of-figma/421117?utm_source=ieu-portal)).

On 17 November 2023 the Commission sent Adobe a Statement of Objections — the formal step in which it informs the companies concerned in writing of its objections ([European Commission](https://ieu-monitoring.com/editorial/eu-commission-sends-adobe-statement-of-objections-over-acquisition-of-figma/421117?utm_source=ieu-portal)). Its preliminary conclusion was that the deal "may significantly reduce competition in the global markets" for two groups of products. First, interactive product design tools — software used mainly to design websites, mobile applications and other digital products — where Figma is "the clear market leader" and Adobe "one of its largest competitors," so the combination would likely "create a dominant player" and discontinue Adobe's own tool, [Adobe XD](/creative-works/adobe-xd/), as well as any successor product: what the Commission called a "reverse killer acquisition" ([European Commission](https://ieu-monitoring.com/editorial/eu-commission-sends-adobe-statement-of-objections-over-acquisition-of-figma/421117?utm_source=ieu-portal)).

Second, vector and raster editing tools, where the Commission argued the deal would eliminate Figma as a potential competitor and thereby strengthen Adobe's dominance: Figma already exerted "a significant constraining influence" on Adobe's vector tool, [Illustrator](/creative-works/illustrator/), and its raster tool, [Photoshop](/creative-works/photoshop/), and "absent the transaction, Figma is significantly likely to enter these markets and grow into an effective competitive force" ([European Commission](https://ieu-monitoring.com/editorial/eu-commission-sends-adobe-statement-of-objections-over-acquisition-of-figma/421117?utm_source=ieu-portal)). The theory was therefore not just about today's rivalry but about tomorrow's: a merger that would remove both a current leader's challenger and a future entrant's launchpad.

Crucially, a Statement of Objections is not a verdict. The Commission states explicitly that sending one "does not prejudge the outcome of the investigation," and Adobe had the opportunity to reply, consult the case file and request an oral hearing, with a final decision due by 5 February 2024 ([European Commission](https://ieu-monitoring.com/editorial/eu-commission-sends-adobe-statement-of-objections-over-acquisition-of-figma/421117?utm_source=ieu-portal)). That decision never came. In parallel with the UK [Competition and Markets Authority](/organizations/competition-and-markets-authority/)'s own provisional findings, the parties concluded there was no clear path to approval and mutually terminated the merger on 18 December 2023 ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/termination-of-adobe-figma-merger/)). Afterwards, Executive Vice-President Margrethe Vestager, who oversaw competition policy, commented that the proposed acquisition "would have terminated all current and prevented all future competition between [the parties]" ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/termination-of-adobe-figma-merger/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim the EU is a country, or that "the EU" and "the European Commission" are interchangeable: the EU is the union of 27 member states, and the Commission is the institution that acted as its competition authority in this case. It does not claim the Commission prohibited the Adobe/Figma merger — the Statement of Objections was a preliminary view, Adobe was entitled to respond, and the deal was abandoned before the 5 February 2024 deadline for a final decision. It does not offer a general history of the EU, its treaties or its enlargement; the institutional sketch here is limited to what the cited Commission sources support, and the Figma case is the profile's substantive core.
