## Overview

Greylock Partners is a venture capital firm founded in October 1965 in the Boston area of the [United States](/places/united-states/), and one of the oldest venture firms in the country ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/greylock-partners-teardown/)). It backs consumer and enterprise software companies at the earliest stages, and its best-known bets — Facebook, LinkedIn, Airbnb and [Blend](/organizations/blend/) — were all made before those companies looked inevitable ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/greylock-partners-teardown/); [Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/announcing-greylock-17/)). Today it invests from Menlo Park, California, out of Greylock 18, a $1.5 billion early-stage fund announced in July 2026 ([Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/introducing-greylock-18/)).

The firm's recent funds show a deliberate refusal to keep getting bigger: Greylock 17 (2023) and Greylock 18 (2026) are $1 billion and $1.5 billion respectively, both aimed at pre-seed, seed and Series A founders rather than later-stage deals ([Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/announcing-greylock-17/); [Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/introducing-greylock-18/)). Each partner makes only one or two new investments a year, a pace the firm says the work demands because it partners deeply rather than broadly ([Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/introducing-greylock-18/)). That selectivity is the through-line of its sixty-year history: few bets, early, with the whole firm behind each one.

## Founding in Boston: the Doriot inheritance

Greylock was founded in October 1965 by Bill Elfers and Dan Gregory, with Charlie Waite joining about six months to a year later and counted as a founder ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)). Elfers had spent eighteen years, from 1947 to 1965, as the number two to Georges Doriot at American Research and Development Corporation, one of America's first venture firms, and left because Doriot, at sixty-five, showed no sign of retiring ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)). The name came from Greylock Road in Wellesley, [Massachusetts](/places/massachusetts/), where Elfers lived — a street named after Mount Greylock — because Elfers refused to name the firm after himself ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)). What he took from Doriot was a rule he repeated for decades: an A management team with a B product beats a B team with an A product, because a good team can reposition when the market shifts ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)).

## The partnership model: flat, aligned, [entrepreneur](/organizations/entrepreneur/)-first

Elfers built Greylock as a deliberate correction of Doriot's firm, which he watched disintegrate as its founder refused to hand over power ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)). He made the organisation flat, passed the managing-partner role to Dan Gregory at the second partnership in 1972 with Charlie Waite to lead the third, and designed the firm to outlast him ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)). He raised the first capital from a group of families rather than one, so no single family could make the firm beholden, and pioneered the successive-partnership fund structure now standard in venture capital ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)). Fees were budget-based, not a percentage of assets, with modest salaries and carry at the high end, so partners profited only when investments did ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)). The operating creed was to be the entrepreneur's best supporting actor, earning the lead-director role every day rather than claiming it by shareholding ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)).

## From minicomputers to the internet

Greylock's first decades tracked Boston's technology cycles: early bets included minicomputer makers Digital Equipment, Data General, Wang Laboratories and Prime Computer ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)). Henry McCance, who joined in 1969 as the firm's first non-founder associate and later became chairman emeritus, pushed it into software, backing American Management Systems, Pansophic, Cullinane, McCormack & Dodge and VM Software, and later Tellabs, Shiva, Manugistics and Trilogy ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)). By 2010 the firm had raised twelve partnerships, with committed capital above $2 billion, and helped build about 300 companies ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)). When the personal computer displaced the minicomputer, Boston overstayed on the old technology while Apple, Intel and Microsoft grew on the West Coast, and Greylock's centre of gravity followed ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)). Its internet-era bets included browser company Spyglass and firewall company Raptor, and partner Bill Kaiser flew to Virginia to invest in Red Hat ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)).

## The consumer decade: Facebook, LinkedIn, Airbnb

The firm's defining run came in consumer internet. David Sze, who joined Greylock in 2000, is widely credited with leading its investment in [Facebook](/organizations/facebook/) in 2006, as well as its investments in LinkedIn and Pandora ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/greylock-partners-teardown/)). Greylock had led LinkedIn's $10 million Series B in 2004, when the startup had no revenue at all — a bet on recruiting as the business model that paid off when Microsoft bought LinkedIn for $26.2 billion in 2016 ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90861033/2004-linkedin-pitch-deck-reid-hoffman); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/linkedin-co-founder-reid-hoffman-step-down-microsofts-board-2026-06-05/)). Reid Hoffman, LinkedIn's co-founder, joined Greylock as a partner in 2009 and led Airbnb's Series A in 2010, again through Greylock ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/greylock-partners-teardown/); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/linkedin-co-founder-reid-hoffman-step-down-microsofts-board-2026-06-05/)). The pattern repeated in security: Palo Alto Networks founder Nir Zuk started the company in 2005 as an Entrepreneur in Residence at Greylock, and it debuted on the NYSE in July 2012, raising $260 million after pricing at $42 and closing its first day at $53.13 ([AllThingsD](http://allthingsd.com/20120723/seven-questions-for-palo-alto-networks-founder-and-cto-nir-zuk/)).

## The Blend bet: fintech's quiet infrastructure

Greylock's investment in Blend shows the same method applied to unglamorous infrastructure. Blend was founded in 2012 by former [Palantir](/organizations/palantir/) employees [Nima Ghamsari](/people/nima-ghamsari/) and Eugene Marinelli with Erin Collard, to rebuild mortgage origination as software ([Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/our-investment-in-blend/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/08/24/blend-100-million/?ncid=rss)). Greylock investor Saam Motamedi met the team, and in 2017 Greylock led Blend's $100 million Series D — at about a $0.5 billion valuation — taking a board seat and bringing Blend's total funding to $166 million ([Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/our-investment-in-blend/); [Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/how-nima-ghamsaris-blend-labs-prevented-the-us-mortgage-market-meltdown/72291/1); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/08/24/blend-100-million/?ncid=rss)). The thesis: mortgages were only the first chapter of a financial-services cloud. Blend worked with lenders controlling 25% of the US mortgage market, including Wells Fargo, US Bank and Movement Mortgage, and processed tens of billions of dollars in applications in the first half of 2017 ([Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/our-investment-in-blend/)). Blend raised $130 million in 2019, hired former [Fannie Mae](/organizations/fannie-mae/) chief [Tim Mayopoulos](/people/timothy-mayopoulos/) as president and went public in July 2021, raising $360 million; Greylock held 4.7% of its class A shares afterwards ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com/amp/article/cross-border/how-nima-ghamsaris-blend-labs-prevented-the-us-mortgage-market-meltdown/72291/1); [Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/blend-labs-lands-on-nyse-in-360m-ipo/)).

## Funds 17 and 18: AI and the return to day one

Greylock 17, announced in October 2023, was a $1 billion fund for pre-seed, seed and Series A founders in enterprise and consumer software, launched with Greylock Edge, a programme taking founders from idea to early revenue and product-market fit ([Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/announcing-greylock-17/)). The firm said over 80% of its previous fund's investments were at those earliest stages, and that it had invested in AI for a decade, naming Abnormal AI, Adept, Aurora Innovation, Cresta, LlamaIndex, Inflection, Nuro, Snorkel and Tome ([Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/announcing-greylock-17/)). Older names include AppDynamics, Okta, Pure Storage, Sumo Logic and Workday alongside Airbnb, LinkedIn, Meta and Palo Alto Networks ([Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/announcing-greylock-17/)). Greylock 18 (July 2026) is a $1.5 billion early-stage fund on the same premise: many defining AI companies do not yet exist ([Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/introducing-greylock-18/)). In August 2023 Hoffman stepped back as a general partner to focus on AI, remaining a strategic partner; he was a founding investor in [OpenAI](/organizations/openai/) and co-founded Inflection AI in 2022 ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2023/report-reid-hoffman-reduces-role-at-greylock-partners-to-focus-on-ai/); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/linkedin-co-founder-reid-hoffman-step-down-microsofts-board-2026-06-05/)).

## The move west

Greylock spent its first twenty-five years as an East Coast firm, with most of its staff in the East, before concluding in the late 1990s that the thought leaders in software, semiconductors, consumer internet and security were on the West Coast ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)). Charlie Waite became its first West Coast representative, and David Strohm and Roger Evans later ran the West Coast office, while the firm de-emphasised biotech to focus on information technology ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)). In May 2009 it moved its headquarters from Boston to Sand Hill Road in Menlo Park, California, after 44 years in Boston, keeping a leaner Boston operation ([Fast Company](https://WWW.fastcompany.com/1674468/greylock-partners-why-you-shouldnt-start-company-boston); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/greylock-partners-joins-the-sand-hill-neighborhood)). Today its offices are in Silicon Valley at 2550 Sand Hill Road, Suite 200, Menlo Park, and in [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) at 140 New Montgomery Street ([Greylock](https://greylock.com/contact-us/)). The move was controversial in Boston, but McCance's argument was simple: fish where the fish are, and the fish were in Silicon Valley ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Greylock Partners?** Greylock Partners is a venture capital firm founded in October 1965, one of the oldest in the United States, now headquartered in Menlo Park, California ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf); [Greylock](https://greylock.com/contact-us/)). It invests at the earliest stages in consumer and enterprise software companies, backing founders from idea to IPO ([Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/introducing-greylock-18/)).

**Who founded Greylock?** Bill Elfers and Dan Gregory founded the firm in 1965, with Charlie Waite joining within a year ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)). Elfers had spent eighteen years as Georges Doriot's deputy at American Research and Development Corporation before leaving to start his own firm ([Computer History Museum](http://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102781068-05-01-acc.pdf)).

**What companies has Greylock backed?** Its best-known early bets include Facebook, LinkedIn, Airbnb, Palo Alto Networks and Workday, plus Blend, whose $100 million Series D it led in 2017 ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/greylock-partners-teardown/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/08/24/blend-100-million/?ncid=rss)). More recent names include Figma, Discord, Abnormal AI, Cato Networks and Rubrik ([Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/announcing-greylock-17/)).

**What is Greylock 18?** Greylock 18 is the firm's $1.5 billion early-stage fund, announced in July 2026 and aimed at AI-first founders from the earliest stages ([Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/introducing-greylock-18/)). Each partner makes only one or two new investments a year, keeping the portfolio deliberately small and concentrated ([Greylock](https://greylock.com/blog/introducing-greylock-18/)).

**Is Reid Hoffman still at Greylock?** Hoffman joined Greylock as a partner in 2009 and led its Airbnb Series A in 2010 ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/greylock-partners-teardown/); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/linkedin-co-founder-reid-hoffman-step-down-microsofts-board-2026-06-05/)).
