## Overview

Atlassian is the [Sydney](/places/sydney/), [Australia](/places/australia/) software company behind [Jira](/creative-works/jira/), Confluence and Trello — founded in 2002 by Mike Cannon-Brookes and Scott Farquhar on $10,000 of credit card debt, listed on the Nasdaq as TEAM since 2015, and reporting $6.57 billion of revenue in fiscal 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/12/10/hipchat-maker-atlassian-begins-trading-at-27-67-valued-at-5-8-billion/); [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037226000031/ex991q4fy26.htm)). It makes the work-tracking and collaboration tools that run software teams — and that rivals such as [ClickUp](/organizations/clickup/) pitch themselves as replacing ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2021/10/27/productivity-platform-provider-clickup-valued-4-billion-raising-400-million-series-c/)).

The company's stated mission is to "unleash the potential of every team," and its software serves over 300,000 companies, including most of the Fortune 500 — among them NASA, Audi, Kiva, Deutsche Bank and Dropbox ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037224000013/ex991q3fy24.htm)). Atlassian is Australian in origin and still Sydney-based, but its listed parent has been a Delaware corporation since 2022, after a redomiciliation from the United Kingdom ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000119312522256215/d583064dex991.htm)). That split identity — a Sydney company with a US parent and a Nasdaq listing — is the first thing to get straight about it.

## From a $10,000 credit card to the Nasdaq

Cannon-Brookes and Farquhar met as classmates at the University of New South Wales and founded Atlassian in Sydney in 2002, financing the startup with $10,000 in credit card debt ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2010/07/14/atlassian-accel-60-million/)). They began by providing customer support for other companies, grew unhappy with the bug-tracking software they were using, and built their own tracker — Jira, a play on "Gojira", the Japanese title for the 1954 Godzilla film — then decided they were better off selling it than providing support services ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/atlassian-founders-20-years-20-lessons&)). Confluence, the team wiki, followed in 2004 ([InformationWeek](https://www.informationweek.com/it-leadership/confluence-4-content-sharing-not-just-for-techies)).

What made Atlassian unusual was how it sold. Instead of a traditional sales team, it built a self-service model — "software should be bought, not sold" — and let the product spread by word of mouth. In 2003 the founders woke to find an order from American Airlines lying on the fax machine: a customer that had found Jira on the website and bought it with no human contact at all ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/atlassian-founders-20-years-20-lessons&)). By 2010 the company had 225 employees, hardly any of them in sales, and 20,000 customers including Facebook, Zynga, Cisco and [Adobe](/organizations/adobe/) — and it had been profitable from its first year ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2010/07/14/atlassian-accel-60-million/)).

It took no outside capital for eight years. Then, in July 2010, Accel Partners invested $60 million for a minority stake — Atlassian's first venture money, with Accel's Rich Wong joining the board — on fiscal 2010 revenue of $59 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2010/07/14/atlassian-accel-60-million/)). Five years later came the IPO: Atlassian priced at $21 a share on December 9, 2015, above the expected $19–20 range, and began trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker TEAM the next day, opening at $27.67 — up 32%, for a $5.8 billion valuation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/12/10/hipchat-maker-atlassian-begins-trading-at-27-67-valued-at-5-8-billion/)). At the time it had about 1,400 employees, 51,000 corporate customers and over 5 million monthly active users, and it was the last tech IPO of 2015 and one of the year's strongest ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/12/10/hipchat-maker-atlassian-begins-trading-at-27-67-valued-at-5-8-billion/)).

## The products — and the acquisitions that built the platform

Jira is Atlassian's flagship: an issue and project tracker built for software teams that has since spread to business teams of every kind. Confluence is its documentation and knowledge companion, and the two products anchor a platform that also includes Jira Service Management for IT teams and Bitbucket for code hosting ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/12/10/hipchat-maker-atlassian-begins-trading-at-27-67-valued-at-5-8-billion/)). But the platform was as much bought as built: Atlassian has made a string of acquisitions, each one extending it into a neighbouring kind of teamwork.

Trello shows the logic. It was a breakout product with about 19 million users and just under 100 employees, half of whose users worked in non-technical functions — finance, HR, legal, marketing and sales — and Atlassian bought it to reach beyond its traditional market of developer teams ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/01/09/atlassian-acquires-trello/)). OpsGenie did the same for IT operations: a $295 million deal, Atlassian's largest since Trello, that gave it incident alerting for the teams that keep software running, packaged with the new Jira Ops incident command centre ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1700620/atlassian-launches-jira-ops-acquires-opsgenie-for-295m.html)). Loom, bought in October 2023 at a steep discount to its $1.53 billion 2021 valuation, added asynchronous video — a bet that recorded video messages were the next evolution of team collaboration ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/12/atlassian-to-acquire-former-unicorn-loom-for-975m/?mrfhud=true)). And The Browser Company, bought for $610 million in cash in September 2025, extends the same bet to the browser itself: an AI-powered browser, Dia, optimised for the SaaS apps where knowledge workers spend their day ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/04/atlassian-to-buy-arc-developer-the-browser-company-for-610m/)).

## The cloud bet, the chat exit, and distributed work

Three decisions in the 2018–2024 period define the modern company. The first was knowing when to quit. Less than a year after launching Stride in September 2017 as a competitor to Slack, Atlassian sold the intellectual property for HipChat and Stride to Slack on July 26, 2018, took a minority equity investment in Slack, and shut both products down in February 2019 ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/startups/atlassian-the-company-behind-jira-and-confluence-cedes-enterprise-chat-to-slack/)). Its own retrospective is blunt: it acquired HipChat early, when group chat was only beginning to go mainstream, but never went big on it, and by the time it built Stride, Slack had taken the market ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/atlassian-founders-20-years-20-lessons&)). Exiting freed it to focus on IT teams instead — the OpsGenie deal followed six weeks later.

The second was the cloud migration. In October 2020 Atlassian announced it would end support for its self-hosted Server products, and support ended on February 15, 2024 ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/company-news/farewell-to-server)). It was a forced march for thousands of on-premises customers, but it worked as a business move: paid seats in Cloud tripled in the three and a half years after the announcement, and by early 2024 the Cloud powered more than 290,000 customers, with 94% of all customers having a Jira Software, Confluence and/or Jira Service Management presence on Cloud ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037224000013/ex991q3fy24.htm); [Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/company-news/farewell-to-server)). The self-hosted era also carried the company's worst security episode: in October 2023 Atlassian disclosed and patched CVE-2023-22515, a critical flaw in self-hosted Confluence Data Center and Server that let unauthenticated attackers create administrator accounts — rated CVSS 10, the highest possible severity — while Cloud sites were unaffected. [Microsoft](/organizations/microsoft/) observed the nation-state actor Storm-0062 exploiting it since September 14, 2023 ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/news/Critical-Atlassian-Confluence-zero-day-flaw-under-attack/837560/)).

The third was how its own people work. When the [COVID-19 pandemic](/events/covid-19-pandemic/) shut Atlassian's offices in March 2020, the company made distributed work permanent under the name Team Anywhere: since 2020 employees have been able to choose where they work every single day, across 12 global offices and more than 10,000 locations, with no in-office mandates ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/distributed-work/distributed-work-report&)). Teams still meet in person about three times a year, and 92% of employees say the policy lets them do their best work ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/distributed-work/distributed-work-report&)). A dedicated Team Anywhere Lab of behavioural scientists runs experiments on how distributed teams actually work — the company treats its own working model as a product to be measured, not a perk to be defended.

## The AI pivot and the 2026 reset

Atlassian's current strategy has three priorities — enterprise, AI, and what it calls the System of Work — and they converge in Rovo, the AI assistant launched at its Team '24 conference on May 1, 2024 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/01/atlassian-launches-rovo-its-new-ai-teammate/); [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037226000031/ex991q4fy26.htm)). Rovo is built on Atlassian's "cloud teamwork graph", the data layer underneath Atlassian Intelligence, its year-old AI effort, and it pulls in data from first- and third-party tools — connectors at launch included [Google](/organizations/google/) Drive, Microsoft SharePoint, Microsoft Teams, GitHub, Slack and Figma — to power AI search, chat, and Rovo Agents that automate workflows in Jira and Confluence, which anyone can build using natural language ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/01/atlassian-launches-rovo-its-new-ai-teammate/)).

The pivot has had a human cost, twice. In March 2023 the company cut about 500 employees, 5% of its workforce, framed as a "rebalancing" toward cloud migrations, IT service management and enterprise customers in the cloud, at an expected cost of $70–75 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/06/atlassian-cuts-5-of-its-workforce/?ref=hackandslash.blog)). Then, in March 2026, it cut about 1,600 roles — roughly 10% of its workforce — explicitly to "self-fund further investment in AI and enterprise sales", with CEO Mike Cannon-Brookes saying it would be "disingenuous to pretend AI doesn't change the mix of skills we need or the number of roles required in certain areas" ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4144218/atlassian-cuts-1600-jobs-to-fund-ai-and-enterprise-expansion.html)). Affected staff received a minimum 16-week separation package globally, and the same announcement disclosed a CTO transition ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4144218/atlassian-cuts-1600-jobs-to-fund-ai-and-enterprise-expansion.html)). Between the two cuts, the leadership also changed: co-founder Scott Farquhar stepped down as co-CEO on August 31, 2024 after 23 years, staying on the board and as a special adviser, leaving Cannon-Brookes as sole CEO ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037224000013/ex991q3fy24.htm)).

The numbers that followed suggest the reset landed. For the fiscal year ended June 30, 2026, Atlassian reported revenue of $6,572 million, up 26% from $5,215 million in fiscal 2025, with subscription ARR of $6,606 million (up 23%) and remaining performance obligations of $4,817 million (up 44%) ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037226000031/ex991q4fy26.htm)). The fourth quarter delivered a 12% GAAP operating margin — the company still reported a $54 million GAAP net loss for the full year, but $1,526 million of non-GAAP net income and $1,319 million of free cash flow ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037226000031/ex991q4fy26.htm)). [Gartner](/organizations/gartner/) named Atlassian a Leader in its 2026 Magic Quadrants for Developer Productivity Insight Platforms, DevSecOps Platforms and IT Service Management Platforms, and its MCP server and Teamwork Graph CLI passed 1 million monthly active users ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037226000031/ex991q4fy26.htm)). The through-line of the whole story is visible in that release: a company that began by selling a bug tracker with no salespeople now sells an AI-orchestrated system of work — and is restaffing itself, twice over, to build it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Atlassian Australian or American?** Both, in different senses. It was founded in Sydney in 2002 and remains Sydney-based, but its listed parent company has been a Delaware corporation since a redomiciliation completed on October 3, 2022, and its shares trade on the Nasdaq as TEAM ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000119312522256215/d583064dex991.htm)).

**Who founded Atlassian?** Mike Cannon-Brookes and Scott Farquhar, two University of New South Wales classmates, founded it in Sydney in 2002 with $10,000 of credit card debt; Farquhar stepped down as co-CEO in August 2024, leaving Cannon-Brookes as sole CEO ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2010/07/14/atlassian-accel-60-million/); [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037224000013/ex991q3fy24.htm)).

**What products does Atlassian own?** Its flagship products are Jira (project and issue tracking), Confluence (team documentation), Trello (kanban boards, acquired 2017), Loom (async video, acquired 2023), Jira Service Management and Bitbucket — plus Rovo, its AI assistant, and Dia, the AI browser from The Browser Company acquisition ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/01/09/atlassian-acquires-trello/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/12/atlassian-to-acquire-former-unicorn-loom-for-975m/?mrfhud=true)).

**Is Jira owned by Atlassian?** Yes. Jira was built by Atlassian's founders in 2002 as their first product, and it remains the company's flagship — the tool rival ClickUp claims to replace ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/blog/announcements/atlassian-founders-20-years-20-lessons&); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2021/10/27/productivity-platform-provider-clickup-valued-4-billion-raising-400-million-series-c/)).

**How much revenue does Atlassian make?** In fiscal 2026 (year ended June 30, 2026) it reported $6,572 million in total revenue, up 26% year over year, with subscription ARR of $6,606 million and free cash flow of $1,319 million ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037226000031/ex991q4fy26.htm)).
