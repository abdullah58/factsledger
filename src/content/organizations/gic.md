## Overview

GIC Private Limited is Singapore's sovereign wealth fund, the state-owned investment manager that invests the country's foreign reserves for long-term returns. Founded in 1981 as the Government of Singapore Investment Corporation, it is one of three entities that manage Singapore's reserves, alongside the Monetary Authority of Singapore and Temasek, and it manages most of the Government's financial assets ([GIC](https://www.gic.com.sg/who-we-are/)). It discloses no asset total, no annual profit and no holdings list; outside estimates of its size run from about US$936 billion to about US$1.16 trillion ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/singapores-gic-banks-hedge-funds-weather-market-volatility-rides-ai-boom-2026-07-23/)). Its headline performance measure is a rolling 20-year real return, 3.4% a year above global inflation for the period ended 31 March 2026 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/singapores-gic-banks-hedge-funds-weather-market-volatility-rides-ai-boom-2026-07-23/)).

## Founding and mandate

GIC was incorporated on 22 May 1981 under Singapore's Companies Act as a private company wholly owned by the Government, set up to manage the country's foreign reserves ([National Library Board, Singapore](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=13ca991b-fef6-4f87-a95a-40d360a66faa)). It was the brainchild of Dr Goh Keng Swee, then First Deputy Prime Minister and chairman of the Monetary Authority of Singapore, who saw reserves growing rapidly and concluded they should be invested in longer-term, higher-yielding assets rather than left in liquid but low-yielding instruments ([National Library Board, Singapore](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=13ca991b-fef6-4f87-a95a-40d360a66faa)). GIC describes itself as the world's first non-commodity-based sovereign wealth fund, founded at a time when the idea of a country managing reserves for long-term returns was unconventional ([GIC](https://www.gic.com.sg/who-we-are/)).

The first board was chaired by Prime Minister Lee Kuan Yew, with Goh as deputy chairman; its members included Lim Kim San, Hon Sui Sen, Goh Chok Tong, Tony Tan, S. Dhanabalan and Tan Teck Chwee ([National Library Board, Singapore](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/article-detail?cmsuuid=13ca991b-fef6-4f87-a95a-40d360a66faa)). Lee remained chairman until 2011, when his son Lee Hsien Loong succeeded him ([GIC](https://www.gic.com.sg/who-we-are/); [Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bszuoki1emx4mjfeurcw/corner-office/singapore-pm-to-chair-gic)). The Government, represented by the Ministry of Finance, mandates GIC to manage the reserves for good long-term real returns, with a board overseeing performance and management making individual investment decisions ([GIC](https://www.gic.com.sg/who-we-are/)). The fund's returns feed directly into public spending: the Net Investment Returns Contribution, which draws on GIC, MAS and Temasek returns, was estimated at S$28.5 billion in Financial Year 2026, funding education, research and development, healthcare and the physical environment ([GIC](https://www.gic.com.sg/who-we-are/)).

## How it invests: the portfolio and the 2026 pivot

GIC invests almost entirely outside Singapore, in more than 40 countries, across public equities, fixed income, private equity, real estate, infrastructure and, increasingly, hedge funds ([SovereignWealthFunds.com](https://www.sovereignwealthfunds.com/funds/gic-singapore/)). At 31 March 2026 its portfolio was 56% equities, 22% fixed income and 22% real assets, with the Americas its largest exposure at 53% ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/singapores-gic-banks-hedge-funds-weather-market-volatility-rides-ai-boom-2026-07-23/)). The fund employs more than 2,500 people across 11 offices in key financial centres ([SovereignWealthFunds.com](https://www.sovereignwealthfunds.com/funds/gic-singapore/)). Its chief executive since January 2017 is Lim Chow Kiat, who joined GIC as a portfolio manager in 1993 and previously served as Group Chief Investment Officer ([GIC](https://www.gic.com.sg/who-we-are/executive-management/lim-chow-kiat/)).

The 20-year real return of 3.4% was GIC's weakest since the 2.7% reported in 2020, down from 3.8% a year earlier, and Lim said the result partly reflected a deliberate decision to take less risk and focus on diversification ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/singapores-gic-banks-hedge-funds-weather-market-volatility-rides-ai-boom-2026-07-23/)). In response, GIC moved from 1 April 2026 to a refreshed investment framework built around a Strategic Portfolio of three groups — equities for growth, fixed income for income, real assets for inflation resilience — and announced plans to deploy an additional US$30 billion into hedge funds over three years, focused on global macro, quantitative and multi-strategy managers ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/singapores-gic-banks-hedge-funds-weather-market-volatility-rides-ai-boom-2026-07-23/)). Group Chief Investment Officer Bryan Yeo said the fund had already tripled its hedge-fund investments over the past decade ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/singapores-gic-banks-hedge-funds-weather-market-volatility-rides-ai-boom-2026-07-23/)).

## Notable investments

GIC's earliest headline deal came in the 2008 financial crisis, when it put US$6.88 billion into Citigroup convertible preferred securities, a stake that would have been about 4% of the bank if converted; it took no board seat and described itself as a financial investor seeking long-term commercial returns ([GIC](https://www.gic.com.sg/newsroom/news/gic-invests-usd-6-88-billion-in-citigroup/)). The same crisis-era playbook showed up in a CHF 11 billion convertible investment in UBS in 2007, converted to shares in 2010 ([SovereignWealthFunds.com](https://www.sovereignwealthfunds.com/funds/gic-singapore/)). In April 2021 GIC joined [Brex](/organizations/brex/)'s US$425 million Series D as a new investor, alongside [TCV](/organizations/tcv/), [Baillie Gifford](/organizations/baillie-gifford/) and [Madrone Capital Partners](/organizations/madrone-capital-partners/), in a round led by Tiger Global that valued Brex at over US$7.4 billion ([Brex](https://www.brex.com/journal/press/series-d-investment)).

The most visible recent deal is artificial intelligence: in February 2026 GIC co-led Anthropic's US$30 billion Series G with Coatue, valuing the AI company at US$380 billion post-money, with [Founders Fund](/organizations/founders-fund/) among the co-leads ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-raises-30-billion-series-g-funding-380-billion-post-money-valuation)). GIC's private-equity chief, Choo Yong Cheen, called Anthropic "the clear category leader in enterprise AI" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-raises-30-billion-series-g-funding-380-billion-post-money-valuation)). GIC says it is investing across the AI value chain — infrastructure, companies that build AI products, and firms using AI to improve their operations — while watching concentration risk in crowded AI stocks closely ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/singapores-gic-banks-hedge-funds-weather-market-volatility-rides-ai-boom-2026-07-23/)).

## The size it will not disclose

GIC's defining feature is deliberate opacity: it publishes no asset total, no annual profit and no holdings list, on the stated view that disclosing the reserves would reveal their size and make the Singapore dollar easier to attack ([SovereignWealthFunds.com](https://www.sovereignwealthfunds.com/funds/gic-singapore/)). That policy leaves outside estimators to disagree by hundreds of billions of dollars. The Sovereign Wealth Fund Institute put the figure at about US$936 billion ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/singapores-gic-banks-hedge-funds-weather-market-volatility-rides-ai-boom-2026-07-23/)), while another outside estimate put it at about US$1.16 trillion at 31 March 2026, up from US$744 billion in August 2021 ([SovereignWealthFunds.com](https://www.sovereignwealthfunds.com/funds/gic-singapore/)). No single "assets under management" figure for GIC is therefore reliable, and this profile does not state one.

What GIC does publish is performance measured against its own mandate. The 20-year real return is its main yardstick because it shows how much the fund earned above global inflation over a rolling two decades ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/singapores-gic-banks-hedge-funds-weather-market-volatility-rides-ai-boom-2026-07-23/)). At 3.4% a year to March 2026, GIC said it had close to doubled the real value of the reserves over 20 years, and tripled them before inflation ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/singapores-gic-banks-hedge-funds-weather-market-volatility-rides-ai-boom-2026-07-23/)). The contrast with Temasek, Singapore's other state investor, is structural: Temasek owns assets on its own balance sheet and publishes a net portfolio value — a record S$518 billion — while GIC manages reserves it does not own and reports only returns and portfolio composition ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/singapores-gic-banks-hedge-funds-weather-market-volatility-rides-ai-boom-2026-07-23/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is GIC?**
GIC is Singapore's sovereign wealth fund, a state-owned company that manages the country's foreign reserves for long-term real returns. It is one of three entities managing Singapore's reserves, alongside the Monetary Authority of Singapore and Temasek ([GIC](https://www.gic.com.sg/who-we-are/)).

**How big is GIC?**
Nobody outside the Singapore Government knows exactly. GIC does not disclose its assets under management, and outside estimates for 2026 range from about US$936 billion to about US$1.16 trillion ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/singapores-gic-banks-hedge-funds-weather-market-volatility-rides-ai-boom-2026-07-23/); [SovereignWealthFunds.com](https://www.sovereignwealthfunds.com/funds/gic-singapore/)).

**How is GIC different from Temasek?**
GIC is a fund manager: it invests foreign reserves owned by the Government and does not own the assets itself. Temasek is an investment company that owns assets on its own balance sheet and publishes a net portfolio value, most recently a record S$518 billion ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/singapores-gic-banks-hedge-funds-weather-market-volatility-rides-ai-boom-2026-07-23/)).

**Who runs GIC?**
Lim Chow Kiat has been Chief Executive Officer since January 2017, after joining GIC in 1993 and serving as Group Chief Investment Officer ([GIC](https://www.gic.com.sg/who-we-are/executive-management/lim-chow-kiat/)). Lee Hsien Loong has chaired the board since June 2011 ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bszuoki1emx4mjfeurcw/corner-office/singapore-pm-to-chair-gic); [SovereignWealthFunds.com](https://www.sovereignwealthfunds.com/funds/gic-singapore/)).

**Did GIC invest in Brex?**
Yes. GIC was a new investor in Brex's US$425 million Series D in April 2021, a round led by Tiger Global that valued Brex at over US$7.4 billion ([Brex](https://www.brex.com/journal/press/series-d-investment)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not state a single assets-under-management figure for GIC, because GIC discloses none and outside estimates disagree by more than US$200 billion; it gives the range instead. It does not state the 2009 partial sale of the Citigroup stake as a verified headline fact, because that detail rests only on a tier3 deal table. It does not claim GIC's 10-year and 5-year nominal returns (6.2% and 3.6%) as tier1-verified, because GIC's own 2025/26 report could not be fetched and those figures come from a tier3 source. It does not present the "world's first non-commodity-based sovereign wealth fund" description as independent fact; that is GIC's own characterisation of itself. And it does not treat GIC and Temasek as interchangeable: they are separate institutions with different mandates, ownership structures and disclosure practices, and this profile keeps them distinct throughout.
