## Overview

TCV (Technology Crossover Ventures) is a Menlo Park-based growth-equity firm founded in 1995 that invests in private and public technology companies, typically once they have proven their business model but before — and beyond — their IPO. It has backed Netflix, Spotify, Airbnb and [Brex](/organizations/brex/), and reports $23 billion in assets under management as of June 2026 ([TCV](https://www.tcv.com/approach)). The firm is formally TCMI, Inc., and it operates from Menlo Park, New York and London ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/tcv-acquisition-corp.-announces-liquidation-2023-03-30)).

The word "crossover" in the name is the whole strategy: TCV invests in late-stage private companies and then keeps investing as they cross into the public market, rather than treating the IPO as the moment to sell. "Crossover stands for investing at the IPO, which we will do in the preponderance of situations," general partner Neil Tolaney told Crunchbase News in 2021 ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tcv-technology-crossover-ventures-investing-strategy-under-the-hood/)). The firm does not view an IPO as a liquidity event at all — it views it as a financing event for the company, and in many cases it participates. That patience is the firm's signature: founding general partner Jay Hoag has sat on Netflix's board since 1999 ([Zillow Group](https://www.zillowgroup.com/people/Jay-Hoag/)), more than two decades after TCV first invested in the company at its Series C.

## Founding and the crossover idea

Jay Hoag co-founded TCV in 1995 and continues to serve as a Founding General Partner, alongside fellow Founding General Partner Rick Kimball ([Zillow Group](https://www.zillowgroup.com/people/Jay-Hoag/); [TCV](https://www.tcv.com/team)). The firm's first fund raised $100 million in 1995 — about 3 percent of the size of the $4 billion fund it would raise a quarter-century later ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tcv-technology-crossover-ventures-investing-strategy-under-the-hood/)). From the start, the charter was late-stage private companies followed across the IPO line into the public market, a structure most venture firms of the era avoided because their own investors wanted them in discrete private or public buckets ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/08/15/stop-listening-to-your-bankers-and-go-public-says-top-late-stage-team/)).

The early bets defined the firm. TCV was one of the first investors in Netflix, at Series C in 1999, and in Zillow, whose Series A it led alongside Benchmark in 2005 ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tcv-technology-crossover-ventures-investing-strategy-under-the-hood/)). Hoag's board seats tell the same story of staying put: Netflix since 1999, Zillow since 2005, Peloton since August 2018 — where he is chairman — and TripAdvisor since February 2018 ([Zillow Group](https://www.zillowgroup.com/people/Jay-Hoag/)). TCV's own statement of purpose has barely changed in thirty years: since its founding in 1995, its mission has been to identify innovators capturing shifts in technology and behaviour that reshape their industries ([TCV](https://www.tcv.com/approach)).

## The growth-equity model

TCV invests behind companies that already lead their market, and it insists on being an active partner rather than a passive cheque. Its stated criteria are threefold: an identifiable trend driving disruption and growth in the market segment, a leading market position, and a management team willing to partner ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tcv-raises-3b-tech-fund/)). It keeps its portfolio deliberately concentrated so it can be, in its own words, the hardest-working investor on each partner company's cap table ([TCV](https://www.tcv.com/approach)). Deals are typically a mix of primary capital, funding a company's growth, and secondary stakes, buying shares from a founder or an earlier investor ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/08/15/stop-listening-to-your-bankers-and-go-public-says-top-late-stage-team/)).

Cheque sizes grew with the funds. From its eleventh fund, TCV typically invested $30 million to $40 million in a deal and up to 10 percent of the fund, or $400 million ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tcv-technology-crossover-ventures-investing-strategy-under-the-hood/)). The firm now describes its range as $10 million to $500 million in equity investments, with flexibility around structure ([TCV](https://www.tcv.com/approach)). The public-market half of the model matters as much as the private half: most of TCV's public investments are in companies it first backed while private, though it sometimes buys public companies it never held privately — in early 2016 it bought LinkedIn shares after they had fallen more than 40 percent ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/08/15/stop-listening-to-your-bankers-and-go-public-says-top-late-stage-team/)). Its largest public offerings include Peloton and LegalZoom, both first invested in during 2018; for LegalZoom's June 2021 IPO, TCV anchored the offering with $90 million, or 15 percent of it, on top of a private investment of more than $100 million ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tcv-technology-crossover-ventures-investing-strategy-under-the-hood/)).

## Funds and scale

TCV's fund sizes trace the growth of the technology market itself, with one long interruption. The staircase ran from $100 million in 1995 to a $2.5 billion ninth fund in 2016, then $3 billion for TCV X in 2019 and a record $4 billion for TCV XI in January 2021 ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tcv-technology-crossover-ventures-investing-strategy-under-the-hood/)). The eleventh fund was raised entirely virtually during the pandemic, from a mix of returning and new investors, and came in above its original $3.25 billion target ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/tcv-closes-record-4b-fund-to-invest-in-e-commerce-fintech-edtech-travel-and-more/)). By 2020 the firm had invested $14 billion across hundreds of startups in 25 years of operations; by March 2023 that figure was over $17 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/tcv-closes-record-4b-fund-to-invest-in-e-commerce-fintech-edtech-travel-and-more/); [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/tcv-acquisition-corp.-announces-liquidation-2023-03-30)).

The firm reports, as of June 30, 2026, more than 350 technology investments, 82 IPOs, 78 strategic exits and $23 billion in assets under management ([TCV](https://www.tcv.com/approach)). Three-quarters of its investments by number have historically been made in Silicon Valley, though it has spent three decades arguing that great companies can be built elsewhere, and its offices in New York (opened 2005) and London (opened 2011) were built to prove it ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tcv-raises-3b-tech-fund/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/08/15/stop-listening-to-your-bankers-and-go-public-says-top-late-stage-team/)). In 2021 it was an investor in 33 private unicorns, 15 of them added since the start of 2020 ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tcv-technology-crossover-ventures-investing-strategy-under-the-hood/)).

## Portfolio: from Netflix to Brex

The portfolio reads as a history of the consumer internet. After Netflix in 1999 and Zillow in 2005 came Spotify, where TCV led a growth round in 2013 that valued the company at $4.25 billion; former Netflix CFO Barry McCarthy, then an executive adviser to TCV, later became Spotify's CFO, and TCV general partner Woody Marshall remains on Spotify's board ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tcv-technology-crossover-ventures-investing-strategy-under-the-hood/)). Airbnb went public in December 2020 in the middle of a pandemic that had halted travel, and TCV bought more shares in the IPO, describing itself as a "big believer in the Airbnb story" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/tcv-closes-record-4b-fund-to-invest-in-e-commerce-fintech-edtech-travel-and-more/)). The 2020–21 fintech wave was the firm's busiest stretch: Revolut at $500 million, [Klarna](/organizations/klarna/) at $650 million, Nubank at $400 million, Mollie at $106 million, Spryker at $130 million and Mambu at $135 million — almost all of those rounds outside the [United States](/places/united-states/) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/tcv-closes-record-4b-fund-to-invest-in-e-commerce-fintech-edtech-travel-and-more/)).

Brex is the most recent fintech landmark in the portfolio. In January 2022, TCV co-led Brex's $300 million Series D-2 round with Greenoaks Capital, at a $12.3 billion valuation; Brex had by then raised $1.2 billion in total ([FFNews](https://ffnews.com/news/brex-appoints-karandeep-anand-as-chief-product-officer-raises-300-million-in-series-d-2-round)). TCV partner David Zhang said Brex was "fundamentally transforming financial operations" and that the firm was honoured to keep joining founders [Henrique Dubugras](/people/henrique-dubugras/) and [Pedro Franceschi](/people/pedro-franceschi/) on their journey — noting the company had accelerated "since our last investment", meaning TCV was already a Brex investor before the round ([FFNews](https://ffnews.com/news/brex-appoints-karandeep-anand-as-chief-product-officer-raises-300-million-in-series-d-2-round)). Europe has been a second home since the London office opened: by 2016 TCV had invested roughly $700 million in Europe, including in Spotify and WorldRemit, arguing that local offices beat "being a tourist" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/08/15/stop-listening-to-your-bankers-and-go-public-says-top-late-stage-team/)).

## The pause and the SPAC

Two episodes break the smooth story, and both are instructive. First, the fundraising gap: TCV raised funds fairly consistently up to its seventh fund in 2008, then, given that fund's size and the financial crisis, did not raise another fund for more than six years, until fund eight was announced in 2014 — and every fund since has grown larger ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tcv-technology-crossover-ventures-investing-strategy-under-the-hood/)). The pause is the one visible scar of the crisis on a firm that otherwise compounded steadily for thirty years. Second, the SPAC: in 2021 TCV formed TCV Acquisition Corp., a blank-cheque company, and listed it on Nasdaq — then in March 2023 announced it would dissolve and liquidate because it could not complete a business combination in the required time, redeeming public shares at about $10.24 each ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/tcv-acquisition-corp.-announces-liquidation-2023-03-30)). The firm's own statement put it plainly: in light of market conditions and available targets, returning the capital held in trust was in shareholders' best interests ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/tcv-acquisition-corp.-announces-liquidation-2023-03-30)). The SPAC episode is the rare case of TCV walking away from a deal structure rather than forcing one — consistent with a firm that tells its portfolio companies to ignore bankers' advice about IPO timing and think in years, not quarters ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/08/15/stop-listening-to-your-bankers-and-go-public-says-top-late-stage-team/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What does TCV stand for?**
Technology Crossover Ventures. The "crossover" refers to investing across the IPO line — backing late-stage private companies and continuing to invest after they go public, treating the IPO as a financing event for the company rather than the moment to sell ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tcv-technology-crossover-ventures-investing-strategy-under-the-hood/)).

**Who founded TCV?**
Jay Hoag and Richard H. Kimball founded the firm in 1995; both remain Founding General Partners, Hoag on the executive committee and Kimball as a senior adviser ([Zillow Group](https://www.zillowgroup.com/people/Jay-Hoag/); [TCV](https://www.tcv.com/team)). Their first fund raised $100 million in 1995 ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tcv-technology-crossover-ventures-investing-strategy-under-the-hood/)).

**What kind of companies does TCV invest in?**
Growth-stage technology companies, private and public, across consumer internet, software, fintech and IT infrastructure — typically market leaders with an identifiable trend driving their growth, where TCV can be an active partner ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tcv-raises-3b-tech-fund/)). From its eleventh fund it typically invested $30 million to $40 million per deal, and up to $400 million ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/tcv-technology-crossover-ventures-investing-strategy-under-the-hood/)).

**How big is TCV?**
The firm reports $23 billion in assets under management as of June 30, 2026, with more than 350 investments, 82 IPOs and 78 strategic exits; its largest fund, TCV XI, closed at $4 billion in January 2021 ([TCV](https://www.tcv.com/approach); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/tcv-closes-record-4b-fund-to-invest-in-e-commerce-fintech-edtech-travel-and-more/)). It had invested $14 billion by 2020 and over $17 billion by March 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/tcv-closes-record-4b-fund-to-invest-in-e-commerce-fintech-edtech-travel-and-more/); [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/tcv-acquisition-corp.-announces-liquidation-2023-03-30)).

**What is TCV's connection to Brex?**
TCV co-led Brex's $300 million Series D-2 round with Greenoaks Capital in January 2022, at a $12.3 billion valuation, having already invested in the company earlier ([FFNews](https://ffnews.com/news/brex-appoints-karandeep-anand-as-chief-product-officer-raises-300-million-in-series-d-2-round)). Brex had raised $1.2 billion in total by then, and TCV partner David Zhang said the firm was honoured to keep backing founders Henrique Dubugras and Pedro Franceschi.
