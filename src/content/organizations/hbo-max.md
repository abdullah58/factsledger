## Overview

Few media brands have cycled through identities the way HBO Max has. In six years the streaming service has gone from HBO Go and HBO Now to HBO Max, then shed the HBO name for the plain "Max," then — after subscriber backlash, industry mockery, and its own research — publicly restored the HBO Max name, with its content chief calling the 2023 change a mistake ([Collider](https://collider.com/hbo-max-rebrand-mistake-streaming-service-netflix-dominance-casey-bloys-explains/)). The rebrand was not cosmetic spin: Warner Bros. Discovery framed it as a strategy shift from volume to prestige, telling advertisers that consumers want better content, not more of it ([Akron Beacon Journal / USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/tv/2025/05/14/max-is-hbo-max-again-streaming-wbd/83619869007/)).

## The 2025 reversal

On May 14, 2025, Warner Bros. Discovery told advertisers in New York that Max would once again become HBO Max that summer ([Akron Beacon Journal / USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/tv/2025/05/14/max-is-hbo-max-again-streaming-wbd/83619869007/)). JB Perrette, the company's president and CEO of streaming, said the move answered what consumer research was telling them: "No consumer today is saying they want more content, but most consumers today say they want better content." CEO David Zaslav added, "We are bringing back HBO, the brand that represents the highest quality in media." Casey Bloys, chairman and CEO of HBO and Max content, joked to the room, "My team is well aware of what the HBO brand means to the industry and our consumers. ... I know you're all shocked. The good thing is I still have stationery."

The reversal was a deliberate repudiation of the 2023 strategy. Two years earlier, fresh off the Warner Bros.–Discovery merger, the company had said the theme was "more is more," with Zaslav calling the combined Max "our rendezvous with destiny" and describing it as a place for "every member of the household" to find whatever they wanted ([Akron Beacon Journal / USA Today](https://www.beaconjournal.com/story/entertainment/tv/2025/05/14/max-is-hbo-max-again-streaming-wbd/83619869007/)). In 2025, Perrette replaced that with a narrower promise: "something distinct and great for adults and families," not everything for everyone. The company's own release said the restored brand would focus on HBO programming, recent box-office movies, docuseries, certain reality series, and Max and local originals — while "de-prioritizing other genres that drive less engagement or acquisition."

Six months later, Bloys went further than the prepared remarks. Speaking to reporters at HBO Max's New York office in November 2025, he admitted the 2023 rebrand had been a mistake driven by the industry's race to match [Netflix](/organizations/netflix/)'s scale ([Collider](https://collider.com/hbo-max-rebrand-mistake-streaming-service-netflix-dominance-casey-bloys-explains/)). "To Netflix's credit, as the first mover, they have become a utility for the consumers," he said. "[Netflix] is the basic cable of today, and in today's world, consumers still want to add to their entertainment portfolio with must-have, truly unique programming that only we can deliver." The key line for the strategy: HBO was never meant to replace Netflix but to complement it as a premium service.

## A migration without a migration

The July 9, 2025 flip was engineered to be uneventful. Unlike the 2023 switch — which forced tens of millions of subscribers to download a new app — the return to HBO Max happened server-side: max.com began redirecting to hbomax.com, the App Store and Google Play listings updated, and existing installations simply renamed themselves ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/07/09/hbo-max-name-returns-after-two-years-max/)). The App Store update carried a note that winked at the whole saga: "Same app. New-ish name. The plot twist everyone has been waiting for: Max is now HBO Max." The black-and-white branding that echoed HBO's 2010s identity replaced the violet-blue Max palette, while the interface, recommendation algorithms and price tiers stayed the same.

It completed the strangest naming journey in streaming: HBO Go, HBO Now, HBO, HBO Max, Max, and back to HBO Max — six identities in little more than a decade ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/07/09/hbo-max-name-returns-after-two-years-max/)).

## What the service airs

HBO Max launched on May 27, 2020, with roughly 10,000 hours of programming — the entire HBO service, Warner Bros. library titles, and a modest original slate headlined by the Anna Kendrick comedy Love Life, the ballroom competition Legendary, and new Looney Tunes cartoons — at $14.99 a month ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1977775); [Warner Bros. Discovery Pressroom](https://press.wbd.com/us/media-release/warnermedia-bolsters-hbo-max-launch-new-distribution-partners)).

Original programming became the brand's backbone. The Max Original comedy Bookie, from Chuck Lorre and Nick Bakay and starring Sebastian Maniscalco as a veteran Los Angeles bookie fighting the legalization of sports gambling, debuted November 30, 2023, ran two seasons and 16 episodes, and was canceled on February 18, 2025 ([The Futon Critic / Warner Bros. Discovery](http://www.thefutoncritic.com/news/2023/11/08/max-original-comedy-series-bookie-executive-produced-by-chuck-lorre-and-starring-sebastian-maniscalco-debuts-november-30-140215/20231108wbd01/); [The Futon Critic](http://www.thefutoncritic.com/showatch/bookie/)).

The clearest proof of the prestige strategy came in 2026. Stuart Fails to Save the Universe, the Big Bang Theory spinoff starring Kevin Sussman as comic-book shop owner Stuart Bloom, premiered on HBO Max in July 2026, topped the platform's global Top 10 TV chart ahead of the critically acclaimed Lanterns, drew a 77% Certified Fresh critics score and an 82% audience score on Rotten Tomatoes, and earned a second-season renewal ([Collider](https://collider.com/big-bang-theory-sci-fi-spin-off-stuart-fails-to-save-the-universe-streaming-success-hbo-max-september-2026/)).

Bloys has described the development philosophy behind the slate as deliberately un-mechanized: no strict quotas across HBO Originals, Max Originals or franchise IP, just the question "What's a good show?" ([Collider](https://collider.com/hbo-max-rebrand-mistake-streaming-service-netflix-dominance-casey-bloys-explains/)). The slate leans on creator-driven series — I Love LA, the returning Euphoria, a six-part Larry David sketch comedy from the Obamas' Higher Ground — and on core IP in Game of Thrones, Harry Potter and DC Studios to fill the gaps left as rival studios keep their films for their own platforms.
