## Overview

Robbins LLP is a San Diego-based law firm founded in 2002 that represents shareholders in securities fraud and shareholder derivative litigation ([Robbins LLP](https://robbinsllp.com/daily-journals-2025-top-boutiques/)). It is a boutique: the Daily Journal named it to its 2025 Top Boutiques in California as "a nationally recognized shareholder rights boutique dedicated to the prosecution of shareholder derivative and class action lawsuits" ([Robbins LLP](https://robbinsllp.com/daily-journals-2025-top-boutiques/)). In September 2026 the firm issued a series of investor notices about a securities class action against [Baidu](/organizations/baidu/), alerting investors who bought Baidu securities between 18 November 2025 and 17 August 2026 to a 13 November 2026 deadline to seek appointment as lead plaintiff ([GlobeNewswire](https://rss.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/18/3364975/32719/en/bidu-class-action-reminder-robbins-llp-reminds-investors-of-the-lead-plaintiff-deadline-in-the-baidu-inc-class-action-lawsuit.html)). One point must be stated plainly at the outset: Robbins LLP did not file that lawsuit. The complaint in *Rosewood Funeral Home Inc. v. Baidu, Inc.*, No. 26-cv-08012 (S.D.N.Y.), was signed and filed on 14 September 2026 by Glancy Prongay Wolke & Rotter LLP ([U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York](https://www.dandodiary.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/893/2026/09/Baidu-SCA-complaint.pdf)). Robbins LLP's role was to publicise the case and invite investors to contact the firm — a common role for plaintiffs' firms, and a different one from filing the complaint.

## A shareholder-rights boutique

Robbins LLP was founded in 2002 by Brian J. Robbins, at a time when, in the firm's words, "shareholder rights as a concept was in its infancy" ([Robbins LLP](https://robbinsllp.com/daily-journals-2025-top-boutiques/)). Its founding principle, in Brian Robbins's formulation, is to "step in when we see mismanagement or unfair dealing," and to give "a powerful legal voice to the people who invest in corporate America" ([Robbins LLP](https://robbinsllp.com/daily-journals-2025-top-boutiques/)). The firm represents individual and institutional shareholders in securities fraud and shareholder derivative cases, and also handles antitrust, consumer, ERISA and whistleblower matters; it works on a contingency fee basis, with no upfront or out-of-pocket fees for investors ([GlobeNewswire](https://rss.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/18/3364975/32719/en/bidu-class-action-reminder-robbins-llp-reminds-investors-of-the-lead-plaintiff-deadline-in-the-baidu-inc-class-action-lawsuit.html)). It says it has represented shareholders seeking better board oversight, legal compliance, transparency and responsiveness at more than 400 Fortune 1000 companies, and helped clients realise more than $1 billion in value — figures that are the firm's own claims ([Robbins LLP](https://robbinsllp.com/daily-journals-2025-top-boutiques/)).

The firm's record in derivative litigation — suits brought on a company's behalf against its own officers and directors — is its calling card. Its early landmark was Cardinal Health: as lead counsel, Robbins LLP challenged Cardinal's acquisition of Syncor International, which was under SEC investigation, forced a renegotiation of the price, and after five years of litigation obtained $70 million for the company, among the largest monetary recoveries ever in a shareholder derivative action, plus governance and accounting-control reforms ([Robbins LLP](https://robbinsllp.com/our-firm/noteworthy-cases/cardinal-health-inc/)). More recently it was co-counsel with Robbins Geller Rudman & Dowd LLP in the Wells Fargo derivative action, in which a $100 million cash payment to Wells Fargo was approved in July 2025 — the largest cash payment made directly to a company in a California state court derivative action ([Robbins Geller Daily News](https://dailynews.rgrdlaw.com/news/20260506-5.6.26media.pdf)). The firm reports a strong 2025: more than $85 million recovered for shareholders in SPAC-related cases, a $10 million Prudential Financial settlement, $6.75 million plus governance reforms at Novavax, and $15 million plus governance reforms at Emergent BioSolutions ([Robbins LLP](https://robbinsllp.com/daily-journals-2025-top-boutiques/)).

## The Baidu case — and what Robbins LLP did and did not do

The Baidu case began on 14 September 2026, when a class action complaint was filed in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York, captioned *Rosewood Funeral Home Inc. v. Baidu, Inc.*, No. 26-cv-08012 ([U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York](https://www.dandodiary.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/893/2026/09/Baidu-SCA-complaint.pdf)). The defendants are Baidu, Inc., chief executive [Robin Yanhong Li](/people/robin-li/), and chief financial officer [Haijian He](/people/haijian-he/), and the claims arise under Sections 10(b) and 20(a) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and SEC Rule 10b-5 ([U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York](https://www.dandodiary.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/893/2026/09/Baidu-SCA-complaint.pdf)). The complaint was signed by Glancy Prongay Wolke & Rotter LLP as counsel for the plaintiff ([U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York](https://www.dandodiary.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/893/2026/09/Baidu-SCA-complaint.pdf)). Robbins LLP's connection to the case is different: beginning 15 September 2026, the day after filing, it issued investor notices — repeated on 18, 24 and 28 September — informing Baidu investors of the case and of the 13 November 2026 deadline to seek appointment as lead plaintiff ([GlobeNewswire](https://rss.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/18/3364975/32719/en/bidu-class-action-reminder-robbins-llp-reminds-investors-of-the-lead-plaintiff-deadline-in-the-baidu-inc-class-action-lawsuit.html)). Several other plaintiffs' firms issued near-identical notices about the same case in the same weeks.

The complaint's allegations, which are the plaintiff's claims and have not been ruled on by any court, concern Baidu's account of its AI transition. The complaint alleges that Baidu overstated the ability of its AI business to mitigate rapid declines in its legacy online marketing business, that its revenue was therefore reasonably likely to decline, and that its positive statements about the business were materially misleading or lacked a reasonable basis ([U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York](https://www.dandodiary.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/893/2026/09/Baidu-SCA-complaint.pdf)). Online Marketing Services, Baidu's core legacy business, represented about 53.1% of total revenues in the third quarter of 2025 and fell 17.6% year over year that quarter — while Baidu assured investors its AI-powered business growth would meaningfully offset the decline ([U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York](https://www.dandodiary.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/893/2026/09/Baidu-SCA-complaint.pdf)). The class period runs from 18 November 2025, the date of Baidu's third-quarter 2025 results announcement, to 17 August 2026 ([U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York](https://www.dandodiary.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/893/2026/09/Baidu-SCA-complaint.pdf)).

The complaint points to two earnings days. On 26 February 2026, it alleges, Baidu reported fourth-quarter and full-year 2025 results showing total revenue down more than 4% in the quarter to RMB32.74 billion and more than 3% for the year to RMB129.079 billion, while management said its AI-Powered Business grew 48% to RMB40 billion; Baidu's American depositary shares fell $7.50, or 5.65%, to $125.15 ([U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York](https://www.dandodiary.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/893/2026/09/Baidu-SCA-complaint.pdf)). On 18 August 2026, it alleges, Baidu reported second-quarter 2026 results showing Baidu General Business revenue down 4% year over year to RMB25.2 billion, Legacy Business revenue down 23% to RMB10.4 billion, and total Online Marketing Services revenue down 19% to RMB13.1 billion — with the Core AI-powered business down 8% quarter over quarter to RMB12.5 billion and its largest component, AI Cloud Infra, down 17% quarter over quarter; the shares fell $13.25, or 12.73%, to $90.87 ([U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York](https://www.dandodiary.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/893/2026/09/Baidu-SCA-complaint.pdf)). As of November 2026 no class had been certified, and lead-plaintiff motions were due by 13 November 2026 ([Glancy Prongay Wolke & Rotter LLP](https://www.wcia.com/business/press-releases/cision/20261020LA76224/whats-next-for-baidu-inc-shareholders-after-12-73-stock-drop-glancy-prongay-wolke-rotter-llp-explains-securities-fraud-class-action-lead-plaintiff-deadline/)). Serving as lead plaintiff is not required to share in any potential recovery ([GlobeNewswire](https://rss.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/18/3364975/32719/en/bidu-class-action-reminder-robbins-llp-reminds-investors-of-the-lead-plaintiff-deadline-in-the-baidu-inc-class-action-lawsuit.html)).

## Not the other Robbins

The name invites confusion, and the confusion is material here. **Robbins LLP** (founded 2002, Brian J. Robbins) is a different firm from **Robbins Geller Rudman & Dowd LLP** (formed in 2010, Darren J. Robbins), a much larger plaintiffs' firm also based in San Diego ([Robbins Geller Rudman & Dowd LLP](http://www.rgrdlaw.com/cases-baidu-class-action-lawsuit-bidu.html)). The two have worked together — Robbins LLP was co-counsel with Robbins Geller on the Wells Fargo derivative case — but they are separate firms, and their Baidu roles differ: Robbins Geller's case page states that it filed the Baidu complaint, while the filed complaint's signature block names Glancy Prongay Wolke & Rotter LLP ([U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York](https://www.dandodiary.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/893/2026/09/Baidu-SCA-complaint.pdf)). Neither Robbins firm filed the Rosewood complaint. Any account that credits the Baidu filing to a firm named Robbins is wrong on the court record.

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not claim Robbins LLP filed the Baidu class action; the filed complaint was signed by Glancy Prongay Wolke & Rotter LLP.
- It does not treat the complaint's allegations as facts. Baidu has not been found to have misled investors; no court has ruled, and no class has been certified.
- It does not present the earnings figures as independently verified financials; they are figures as alleged in the complaint.
- It does not independently verify the firm's claimed recoveries ($1 billion-plus in value, 400+ Fortune 1000 companies); those are Robbins LLP's own figures.
- It does not cover any separate Baidu complaint with a different class period, or any development after the 13 November 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline.
