## Overview

The Chuck Lorre Family Foundation is the philanthropic arm of [Chuck Lorre](/people/chuck-lorre/), the television producer behind *Two and a Half Men*, *The Big Bang Theory* and *Young Sheldon*. Lorre established the foundation in 2013 to expand his philanthropic projects, and it has since become one of the more distinctive celebrity foundations in Los Angeles — not for gala theatrics but for a consistent, healthcare-and-education-focused giving pattern: a $30 million allied health school at Cedars-Sinai, a $10 million research scholars program at Children's Hospital Los Angeles, and, as of February 2026, its name on a commercial kitchen feeding critically ill Angelenos ([Cedars-Sinai](https://www.cedars-sinai.org/newsroom/chuck-lorre-family-foundation-increases-investment-in-yed-program/); [Project Angel Food](https://angelfood.org/our-stories/chuck-lorre-jamie-lee-curtis-richard-ayoub-more-celebrate-project-angel-foods-grand-opening-of-the-chuck-lorre-family-foundation-kitchen-campus)). The foundation's stated mission, in the words of its President and Chief Giving Officer Trisha Cardoso, is "to fund innovative and compassionate organizations in the areas of STEM education, health and the arts" ([Children's Hospital Los Angeles](https://www.chla.org/newsroom/press-release/chuck-lorre-family-foundation-makes-10-million-gift-childrens-hospital-los-angeles)).

## The kitchen opening — and the matching grant nobody saw coming

On Thursday, February 5, 2026, [Project Angel Food](/organizations/project-angel-food/) cut the ribbon on the first building of the Chuck Lorre Family Foundation Kitchen & Campus, expanding the nonprofit's footprint from 922 Vine Street to 960 Vine Street in Hollywood — the opening move in a $51.5 million, multi-year expansion campaign ([Project Angel Food](https://angelfood.org/our-stories/chuck-lorre-jamie-lee-curtis-richard-ayoub-more-celebrate-project-angel-foods-grand-opening-of-the-chuck-lorre-family-foundation-kitchen-campus)). Project Angel Food prepares and delivers medically tailored meals to people living with critical illnesses such as HIV/AIDS, cancer and kidney failure, and the new building more than doubles its culinary footprint, from roughly 7,700 to 16,000 square feet: hot cook lines go from one to three, and annual capacity jumps from 1.5 million meals to more than 4.5 million, with the ability to one day serve more than 10,000 people a year across Los Angeles County ([Project Angel Food](https://angelfood.org/our-stories/chuck-lorre-jamie-lee-curtis-richard-ayoub-more-celebrate-project-angel-foods-grand-opening-of-the-chuck-lorre-family-foundation-kitchen-campus)).

What made the ceremony unusual was what Lorre said about why he was there at all. "They have a policy, which struck me deeply, which was 'food is medicine,'" he told the crowd. "Food that nourishes and brings health, which meant a lot to me because as a much younger man, I was very ill, and nutrition actually turned it around for me. So I know something about how important it is to be fed properly. Not just fed, but fed properly. So when they said those words, 'food is medicine,' we were all in" ([Project Angel Food](https://angelfood.org/our-stories/chuck-lorre-jamie-lee-curtis-richard-ayoub-more-celebrate-project-angel-foods-grand-opening-of-the-chuck-lorre-family-foundation-kitchen-campus)). Project Angel Food CEO Richard Ayoub had credited "transformative gifts from The Chuck Lorre Family Foundation and the S. Mark Taper Foundation" with making the opening possible — "we're opening this kitchen today, instead of still trying to figure out how to make it possible" ([Project Angel Food](https://angelfood.org/our-stories/chuck-lorre-jamie-lee-curtis-richard-ayoub-more-celebrate-project-angel-foods-grand-opening-of-the-chuck-lorre-family-foundation-kitchen-campus)).

Then Ayoub broke some news of his own: upon learning that Project Angel Food was just $2.3 million short of the capital threshold needed to release millions more through a federal program that incentivizes corporate investment in nonprofits, Lorre had stepped forward with a dollar-for-dollar matching grant covering half — $1.15 million — to inspire others to close the gap ([Project Angel Food](https://angelfood.org/our-stories/chuck-lorre-jamie-lee-curtis-richard-ayoub-more-celebrate-project-angel-foods-grand-opening-of-the-chuck-lorre-family-foundation-kitchen-campus)). The Feb. 5 opening completed only the first phase; the campaign continues toward a second, brand-new facility at 960 Vine Street, with construction expected to begin the following summer and completion anticipated by the end of 2027 ([Project Angel Food](https://angelfood.org/our-stories/chuck-lorre-jamie-lee-curtis-richard-ayoub-more-celebrate-project-angel-foods-grand-opening-of-the-chuck-lorre-family-foundation-kitchen-campus)).

## The Cedars-Sinai pillar

The foundation's largest verified commitment is to Cedars-Sinai: $30 million to establish the Chuck Lorre Allied Health School (also styled the Chuck Lorre School of Allied Health), a vocational school aimed at training people from underrepresented and historically marginalized communities in Los Angeles for chronically understaffed hospital roles — respiratory therapy, pharmacy technician training, clinical laboratory science, MRI and radiologic technology, and echo/cardio technology ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ai/cedars-sinai-creates-health-sciences-university-4-notes/); [Cedars-Sinai](https://www.cedars-sinai.org/newsroom/chuck-lorre-family-foundation-increases-investment-in-yed-program/)). Programs run six to 24 months, and the school sits inside the newly established Cedars-Sinai Health Sciences University ([Becker's Hospital Review](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ai/cedars-sinai-creates-health-sciences-university-4-notes/)).

The sources disagree on the gift's date — the Cedars-Sinai newsroom dates the $30 million commitment to 2024, while a December 2024 school update says the school "was established in 2022 with a $30 million donation" ([Cedars-Sinai](https://www.cedars-sinai.org/newsroom/chuck-lorre-family-foundation-increases-investment-in-yed-program/); [Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/cedars-sinai-chuck-lorre-allied-health-school-steps-closer-to-welcoming-first-students)). The most likely reading is that the gift was announced in 2022 and the school took shape through 2024, when it prepared to enroll its first students — but the discrepancy is stated here rather than smoothed over ([Newswise](https://www.newswise.com/articles/cedars-sinai-chuck-lorre-allied-health-school-steps-closer-to-welcoming-first-students)).

Alongside the school, the foundation doubled down on Cedars-Sinai's Youth Employment and Development (YED) Health Careers Academy, pledging an additional $7.5 million to endow the program. YED was created following the 1992 Los Angeles civil unrest to give high school students from under-represented neighborhoods hands-on training and mentoring in the medical field; the two-year program lets students from Fairfax High School earn 10 high school credits as well as a paycheck, and many graduates go on to become Cedars-Sinai employees ([Cedars-Sinai](https://www.cedars-sinai.org/newsroom/chuck-lorre-family-foundation-increases-investment-in-yed-program/)). Foundation support has powered YED since 2014, and at the time of the pledge there were 50 students in the program and 1,400 alumni ([Cedars-Sinai](https://www.cedars-sinai.org/newsroom/chuck-lorre-family-foundation-increases-investment-in-yed-program/)).

## The Children's Hospital Los Angeles pillar

On December 5, 2023, the foundation pledged $10 million to Children's Hospital Los Angeles to support research education for high school and college students from under-resourced communities across greater Los Angeles ([Children's Hospital Los Angeles](https://www.chla.org/newsroom/press-release/chuck-lorre-family-foundation-makes-10-million-gift-childrens-hospital-los-angeles)). The gift created the Chuck Lorre Research Scholars Program, which welcomed its inaugural class in the summer of 2023: Los Angeles-area college students — including alumni of the Samuels Family Latino and African American High School Internship Program (LA-HIP), a high-school internship started by Dr. Emil Bogenmann in 2005 — spend 10 weeks in paid summer laboratory research after their freshman or sophomore year, with mentoring and career guidance from the program director at CHLA ([Children's Hospital Los Angeles](https://www.chla.org/newsroom/press-release/chuck-lorre-family-foundation-makes-10-million-gift-childrens-hospital-los-angeles)).

"When students are fully immersed in research, they can suddenly imagine a future in STEM where they can pursue their dreams," Lorre said at the announcement. "That is what inspired me to establish this program" ([Children's Hospital Los Angeles](https://www.chla.org/newsroom/press-release/chuck-lorre-family-foundation-makes-10-million-gift-childrens-hospital-los-angeles)). The foundation has been supporting Children's Hospital Los Angeles since 2016 ([Children's Hospital Los Angeles](https://www.chla.org/newsroom/press-release/chuck-lorre-family-foundation-makes-10-million-gift-childrens-hospital-los-angeles)).

## A consistent pattern

Across all three pillars, the foundation's giving follows one logic: pay for the pipeline. The YED academy moves Fairfax High students into paid hospital training; the allied health school converts that training into certifications and doubled salaries in understaffed specialties; the Research Scholars Program keeps underrepresented students in the science pipeline through college; and the Angel Food kitchen treats nutrition itself as medicine ([Cedars-Sinai](https://www.cedars-sinai.org/newsroom/chuck-lorre-family-foundation-increases-investment-in-yed-program/); [Children's Hospital Los Angeles](https://www.chla.org/newsroom/press-release/chuck-lorre-family-foundation-makes-10-million-gift-childrens-hospital-los-angeles); [Project Angel Food](https://angelfood.org/our-stories/chuck-lorre-jamie-lee-curtis-richard-ayoub-more-celebrate-project-angel-foods-grand-opening-of-the-chuck-lorre-family-foundation-kitchen-campus)). Lorre himself put the vocational-school version of the argument bluntly when the Cedars gift was announced: the opportunity to provide training and certificates for underserved people "which in some instances would double their salaries" was why he was "all in" ([Cedars-Sinai](https://www.cedars-sinai.org/newsroom/chuck-lorre-family-foundation-increases-investment-in-yed-program/)).
