## Overview

SoftBank Group Corp. is a Japanese investment holding company founded by Masayoshi Son in September 1981 as a software distributor, which grew through telecoms into the manager of the world's largest technology fund — and then staked its future on artificial intelligence, through chip designer Arm, a planned $40 billion investment in [OpenAI](/organizations/openai/), and the $500 billion Stargate data-centre project ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Son-Masayoshi); [SoftBank Group Corp.](https://group.softbank/en/news/press/20250401?ref=wheresyoured.at)). It is not the same company as [SoftBank Corporation](/organizations/softbank-corporation/), the Japanese telecom operator: since 1 July 2015 the Group has been the holding company, and the telecom business has carried the SoftBank Corp. name ([SoftBank Group Corp.](https://group.softbank/en/news/press/20150511_4)).

Son, born on 31 August 1957 in Saga prefecture, Kyushu, [Japan](/places/japan/), studied economics at the University of California, Berkeley, graduating in 1980 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Son-Masayoshi)). While a student he co-developed a sound-translation device that converted Japanese into English and German, sold the technology to Sharp Corporation, and used the proceeds to establish SoftBank's predecessor on returning to Japan in 1981 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Son-Masayoshi)).

## From software distributor to global investor

Son expanded aggressively through the 1990s, financing acquisitions largely through corporate bonds rather than bank loans — buying publishing and convention operations from Ziff-Davis, the rights to host COMDEX, and a majority stake in memory-card maker Kingston Technology ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Son-Masayoshi)). In the late 1990s it moved heavily into internet businesses, including Yahoo Japan, but the dot-com collapse of 2000 cost it an estimated ¥75 billion ($748 million) ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Son-Masayoshi)). In 2001 SoftBank set up a broadband internet service with Yahoo Japan, and in 2006 it bought Vodafone KK, the Japanese arm of Vodafone, entering mobile telecommunications ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Son-Masayoshi)).

In 2000 Son invested $20 million in [Alibaba](/organizations/alibaba/), then a little-known Chinese e-commerce company, hoping it would become "the next Yahoo" ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/off-prem/2022/08/11/softbank-offloads-part-of-alibaba-stake-to-help-stem-losses/491272?td=readmore)). The stake became SoftBank's most valuable asset: in August 2022 the group sold down almost half of it, realising about ¥4.6 trillion ($34 billion) and cutting its holding from 23.7% to about 14.6%, while retaining a stake then worth north of $50 billion ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/off-prem/2022/08/11/softbank-offloads-part-of-alibaba-stake-to-help-stem-losses/491272?td=readmore)). The other was telecoms scale: in 2013 SoftBank acquired a controlling stake in Sprint Corporation, then the third-largest wireless carrier in the [United States](/places/united-states/) ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Son-Masayoshi)).

The 2015 restructuring made the shape of the modern company explicit. On 1 July 2015 SoftBank Corp. became SoftBank Group Corp. "to clearly identify its role as a holding company driving future global development", while its mobile arm was renamed SoftBank Corp. ([SoftBank Group Corp.](https://group.softbank/en/news/press/20150511_4)). Group assets, including Yahoo Japan, Alibaba and the group's US investments, were consolidated under the holding company, with Son continuing as chairman and CEO ([SoftBank Group Corp.](https://group.softbank/en/news/press/20150511_4)). Son framed the change as building a business for sustainable growth "for centuries to come" ([SoftBank Group Corp.](https://group.softbank/en/news/press/20150511_4)).

## The Vision Funds

The first Vision Fund, announced in October 2016, was a break with venture-capital norms: a single fund aiming at $100 billion, with minimum deal checks of $100 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/20/softbank-vision-fund-first-close/?ncid=rss)). Its first major close on 20 May 2017 brought in over $93 billion of committed capital from backers including Saudi Arabia's [Public Investment Fund](/organizations/public-investment-fund-of-saudi-arabia/), Abu Dhabi's Mubadala, [Apple](/organizations/apple/), Qualcomm, Foxconn and Sharp, targeting internet-of-things, AI, robotics, infrastructure, telecoms, biotech, fintech and mobile apps ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/20/softbank-vision-fund-first-close/?ncid=rss)). Deployment was fast: by February 2018 the fund had spent more than $35 billion, including a $7.7 billion placement with [Uber](/organizations/uber/) — $1.2 billion of it a direct investment — and $4.58 billion into Didi Chuxing through the $6 billion Delta Fund, a Vision Fund twin that handled conflicts of interest ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/02/07/softbank-vision-fund-over-35-billion/)). Other early holdings included Arm, [Nvidia](/organizations/nvidia/), Flipkart, Paytm, Oyo Rooms and Improbable ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/02/07/softbank-vision-fund-over-35-billion/)).

Vision Fund 2, announced in July 2019, was meant to be even bigger: about $108 billion of anticipated capital based on memoranda of understanding, with SoftBank itself putting in $38 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/07/25/softbank-announces-ai-focused-second-108-billion-vision-fund-with-lps-including-microsoft-apple-and-foxconn/)). The backer list changed character — Apple, Foxconn, [Microsoft](/organizations/microsoft/), Mizuho, Sumitomo Mitsui, MUFG and Japanese insurers, with no participants from the Saudi Arabian government, whose close ties to the first fund had become a source of investor concern after the murder of journalist Jamal Khashoggi ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/07/25/softbank-announces-ai-focused-second-108-billion-vision-fund-with-lps-including-microsoft-apple-and-foxconn/)). The $108 billion was anticipated, MOU-based capital rather than a closed fund, and no final closed size was verified for this profile.

## WeWork and the 2020 reckoning

[WeWork](/organizations/wework/) was the Vision Fund's defining failure. Valued at $47 billion in January 2019, the office-sharing company shelved its initial public offering on 30 September 2019 after investors balked at its losses, business model and governance, and its co-founder Adam Neumann resigned as chief executive the previous week ([Reuters](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/softbank-to-go-ahead-with-wework-stock-tender-offer-this-week/article30074700.ece/amp/)). SoftBank, already its biggest investor, agreed a $9.5 billion rescue in October 2019: $6.5 billion in debt and equity financing plus a tender offer of up to $3 billion for existing shareholders' stock, taking SoftBank to about 80% control ([Reuters](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/softbank-to-go-ahead-with-wework-stock-tender-offer-this-week/article30074700.ece/amp/)). Neumann gave up voting control and left the board, receiving a $500 million line of credit and a $185 million advisory fee, while WeWork cut about 2,400 of its 12,500 jobs ([Reuters](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/softbank-to-go-ahead-with-wework-stock-tender-offer-this-week/article30074700.ece/amp/)).

The damage spread across the portfolio. In April 2020 SoftBank said it expected to book its biggest ever annual loss, driven by a ¥1.8 trillion ($16.7 billion) reduction in the value of its Vision Fund investments — effectively wiping out the gains the fund had built since its 2017 launch and likely putting its investments at a net negative ([KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/softbank-vision-funds-gains-wiped-out-with-usd-16-7-billion-loss)). The fund then held stakes in 88 enterprises, including WeWork, Uber and ByteDance, and Son's plans to launch Vision Fund 2 were frozen ([KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/softbank-vision-funds-gains-wiped-out-with-usd-16-7-billion-loss)). The group projected a net loss of ¥750 billion for the year ended March 2020, against a ¥1.41 trillion profit the year before — its biggest loss since going public in 1994 ([KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/softbank-vision-funds-gains-wiped-out-with-usd-16-7-billion-loss)). Son responded with a plan to sell $41 billion of assets to pay down debt and buy back shares ([KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/softbank-vision-funds-gains-wiped-out-with-usd-16-7-billion-loss)).

## The Behavox case: the customer who became an investee

Vision Fund 2's early deals show the fund's method at its most distinctive: investing in companies SoftBank already used. In February 2020 the fund invested $100 million in [Behavox](/organizations/behavox/), a compliance-software company founded in 2014 whose AI platform aggregates and analyses employees' internal communications to detect compliance breaches, insider threats and bad conduct ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/02/25/behavox-spies-100m-vision-fund-investment/)). The investment was made in preferred shares, valuing Behavox at about $500 million, with the investment group also gaining a board seat, according to Reuters reporting documented in the sibling Behavox profile ([Reuters](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/24/02/2020/SoftBank-fund-invests-$100-million-in-compliance-software-firm)). What made the round unusual was the diligence: SoftBank Investment Advisers, the group's investment advisory arm, was already a Behavox customer ([Reuters](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/24/02/2020/SoftBank-fund-invests-$100-million-in-compliance-software-firm)).

[Munish Varma](/people/munish-varma/), managing partner at SoftBank Investment Advisers, said the firm had "experienced the incredible insights and sophisticated analysis of the Behavox platform" and believed in its mission "to organise all corporate communications data on earth to add value for successful companies" ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/02/25/behavox-spies-100m-vision-fund-investment/)). The investor was, in effect, converting its own procurement into equity. The deal was also among Vision Fund 2's first, struck at the very moment SoftBank was facing investor scrutiny over the WeWork and Uber setbacks ([Reuters](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/24/02/2020/SoftBank-fund-invests-$100-million-in-compliance-software-firm)).

## The AI pivot: Arm, OpenAI and Stargate

The next chapter ran through chips and AI. SoftBank had taken British chip designer Arm private in 2016 for $32 billion, and a proposed sale to Nvidia later fell through on regulatory challenges ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/156772/softbank-arm-stock-soars-25-ai-chip-designer-ipo-nets-65-billion-valuation); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2023/09/14/arm-ipo-kicks-off-today-with-company-valued-at-545b/1262795)). Arm instead returned to the public markets: its shares were priced at $51, valuing the company at $54.5 billion, and began trading on the Nasdaq on 14 September 2023, raising $4.87 billion in the largest US IPO of the year ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/ipo-etfs-look-hot-in-september:-heres-why)). The shares closed the first day at $63.59, up nearly 25%, for a valuation of about $65 billion, and Arm remained a consolidated subsidiary under SoftBank's control ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/156772/softbank-arm-stock-soars-25-ai-chip-designer-ipo-nets-65-billion-valuation); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2023/09/14/arm-ipo-kicks-off-today-with-company-valued-at-545b/1262795)).

SoftBank had already invested $2.2 billion in OpenAI through Vision Fund 2 since September 2024, and on 31 March 2025 it agreed follow-on investments of up to $40 billion, at a $260 billion pre-money valuation, with $10 billion to be syndicated to co-investors so its effective investment would be up to $30 billion ([SoftBank Group Corp.](https://group.softbank/en/news/press/20250401?ref=wheresyoured.at)). The first $10 billion was due in April 2025 and up to $30 billion more in December, conditional on OpenAI completing a recapitalisation of its for-profit arm ([SoftBank Group Corp.](https://group.softbank/en/news/press/20250401?ref=wheresyoured.at)). SoftBank calls OpenAI its most important partner on the path to artificial super-intelligence ([SoftBank Group Corp.](https://group.softbank/en/news/press/20250401?ref=wheresyoured.at)).

Alongside the equity stake came infrastructure. SoftBank and OpenAI announced the Stargate Project on 21 January 2025, a plan to build dedicated AI infrastructure for OpenAI in the United States ([SoftBank Group Corp.](https://group.softbank/en/news/press/20250401?ref=wheresyoured.at)). OpenAI describes Stargate as its overarching AI infrastructure platform, a $500 billion, 10-gigawatt commitment announced in January at the White House; by September 2025 it had reached nearly 7 gigawatts of planned capacity and over $400 billion of investment over the next three years, with five new US sites including the flagship campus in Abilene, Texas ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/five-new-stargate-sites/?ref=bayarealetters.com)). Son's own summary of the logic: "Stargate is harnessing SoftBank's innovative data center design and energy expertise to deliver the scalable compute that powers AI's future" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/five-new-stargate-sites/?ref=bayarealetters.com)). The scale of the bet carries matching risk: on 9 November 2026 SoftBank said it had recorded a $3.7 billion second-quarter loss on advance payments for US data-centre equipment after cancelling orders, having reserved more than 13 gigawatts of turbines ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/commentary/breakingviews/softbanks-turbine-snafu-spins-ai-trade-around-2026-11-11/)).

## What this profile does not claim

It does not assert the exact day of the 1981 founding, only the month, and it claims no street address for the Tokyo headquarters ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Son-Masayoshi)). It does not present the Behavox valuation, share structure or board seat as freshly re-verified here: those details rest on Reuters reporting documented in the sibling Behavox profile, whose original page could not be re-fetched in this session, while the $100 million investment is corroborated by Global Venturing ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/02/25/behavox-spies-100m-vision-fund-investment/)).
