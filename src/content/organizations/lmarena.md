## Overview

LMArena — renamed Arena in January 2026 — is the crowdsourced platform that ranks artificial-intelligence models by having real users vote on anonymous, side-by-side answers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/21/lm-arena-the-organization-behind-popular-ai-leaderboards-lands-100m/)). It began in 2023 as Chatbot Arena, a side project of researchers at the University of California, Berkeley, and in April 2025 it incorporated as the independent company Arena Intelligence Inc. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/06/lmarena-lands-1-7b-valuation-four-months-after-launching-its-product/); [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/lmarena)). In under two years as a company it raised $450 million across three rounds, reaching a $3.1 billion valuation in October 2026 ([machine.net](https://www.machine.net/news/arena-ai-evaluation-series-b-3-1-billion/)). Its leaderboards have become the scoreboard the AI industry watches — including for [Baidu](/organizations/baidu/)'s models: ERNIE 5.1 appears on its text leaderboard, rated around 1467 as of November 2026 ([Arena](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/chat/text?rankBy=labs)).

## From Berkeley side project to leaderboard company

Chatbot Arena was launched in 2023 by Anastasios Angelopoulos and Wei-Lin Chiang, then PhD students in electrical engineering and computer science at UC Berkeley, as a side project under the LMSYS research organisation, working closely with Berkeley professor Ion Stoica ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/lmarena)). The idea was simple: anonymise model outputs, let users vote on which response they preferred, and aggregate the results at scale — an alternative to static academic benchmarks that, the founders argued, no longer reflected how people actually used language models ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/lmarena)). Votes are aggregated with a Bradley-Terry model into Elo-style scores, and by April 2025 the platform had logged over 3 million head-to-head comparisons across more than 400 models ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/lmarena)).

The platform grew beyond text chat. It added WebDev Arena in December 2024, where models generate full web applications from prompts and users vote on the live result; Copilot Arena for AI coding assistants; RepoChat Arena for questions about real codebases; and Search Arena in March 2025 for search-augmented models ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/lmarena)). Angelopoulos became CEO, Chiang CTO, and Stoica — a serial founder known for co-founding Databricks, Anyscale and Conviva — co-founder and advisor ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/lmarena)).

The commercial turn came fast. On 21 May 2025 LMArena raised $100 million in a seed round valuing the organisation at $600 million, according to Bloomberg, led by [Andreessen Horowitz](/organizations/andreessen-horowitz/) and UC Investments, with [Lightspeed Venture Partners](/organizations/lightspeed-venture-partners/), Felicis Ventures and Kleiner Perkins participating ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/21/lm-arena-the-organization-behind-popular-ai-leaderboards-lands-100m/)). Until then it had been funded by grants and donations, including from Google's Kaggle platform, a16z and Together AI ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/21/lm-arena-the-organization-behind-popular-ai-leaderboards-lands-100m/)). In September 2025 it launched AI Evaluations, a commercial service in which enterprises, model labs and developers hire the company to run evaluations through its community; by December it reported a $30 million annualised run rate, less than four months after launch ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/06/lmarena-lands-1-7b-valuation-four-months-after-launching-its-product/)).

The Series A, announced on 6 January 2026 and led by Felicis and UC Investments, took the total raised to $250 million in about seven months; by then the platform had more than 5 million monthly users across 150 countries and 60 million conversations a month ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/06/lmarena-lands-1-7b-valuation-four-months-after-launching-its-product/)). On 28 January 2026 the company rebranded from LMArena to Arena and moved to arena.ai. The Series B followed on 8 October 2026 — $200 million co-led by Lightspeed and Khosla Ventures, with [Salesforce](/organizations/salesforce/) Ventures, 01 Advisors, Dell Technologies Capital and Endeavor Catalyst joining existing backers a16z and Felicis — valuing the business at $3.1 billion, with annualised revenue cited at more than $100 million ([machine.net](https://www.machine.net/news/arena-ai-evaluation-series-b-3-1-billion/)). Alongside the round Arena introduced the Alignment Index, a leaderboard ranking models on behaviours such as unauthorised actions and deceptive completion ([machine.net](https://www.machine.net/news/arena-ai-evaluation-series-b-3-1-billion/)).

## Criticism and limits

LMArena's influence has drawn scrutiny. In April 2025 a group of competitors published a paper alleging that its partnerships with select model companies — [OpenAI](/organizations/openai/), [Google](/organizations/google/) and [Anthropic](/organizations/anthropic/) — helped those makers game its benchmarks, an allegation LMArena has vehemently denied ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/06/lmarena-lands-1-7b-valuation-four-months-after-launching-its-product/)). The dispute matters because the leaderboard is not a neutral measuring stick in the abstract: labs use it to test and market flagship models, so the design of the test shapes what gets built ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/21/lm-arena-the-organization-behind-popular-ai-leaderboards-lands-100m/)). No independent adjudication of the allegation was found, and this profile records the claim and the denial without resolving them.

The platform's own method has acknowledged limits too: it measures human preference in open-ended chat, which is not the same as correctness, and scores are relative — they drift as models are added and votes accumulate. What it does offer, and what investors paid for, is a large, constantly renewing pool of real user interactions rather than a fixed test set that labs can optimise against once they know it ([machine.net](https://www.machine.net/news/arena-ai-evaluation-series-b-3-1-billion/)).
