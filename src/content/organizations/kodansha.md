## Overview

Kodansha Ltd. is a Japanese publishing house founded in [Tokyo](/places/tokyo/) in 1909 by Seiji Noma, and one of [Japan](/places/japan/)'s three great manga publishers alongside [Shueisha](/organizations/shueisha/) and [Shogakukan](/organizations/shogakukan/). It publishes the weekly manga magazine *Weekly Shōnen Magazine* and hit series including *Attack on Titan*, *Tokyo Revengers* and *Fairy Tail*, as well as literary magazines, dictionaries and light novels from its headquarters in Bunkyō, Tokyo ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/74586-global-publishing-leaders-2017-kabushiki-gaisha-k-dansha-kodansha-limited.html)). Kodansha was also one of the four publishers that sued [Cloudflare](/organizations/cloudflare/) over manga piracy, and on 19 November 2025 the [Tokyo District Court](/organizations/tokyo-district-court/) ordered Cloudflare to pay it ¥126,500,000 in damages ([Tokyo District Court](https://www.courts.go.jp/ip/eng/assets/ip/eng/chizai_en/chizai_en-pdf-3813.pdf)).

Kodansha began as a spin-off of the Dai-Nippon Yūbenkai (Greater Japan Oratorical Society), with the literary magazine *Yūben* as its first publication ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/74586-global-publishing-leaders-2017-kabushiki-gaisha-k-dansha-kodansha-limited.html)). The name Kodansha came from *Kōdan Club*, a magazine the company published, and was first used in 1911 when the publisher formally merged with the Dai-Nippon Yūbenkai; the company's current legal name has been in use since 1958 ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/74586-global-publishing-leaders-2017-kabushiki-gaisha-k-dansha-kodansha-limited.html)). Its motto is *"Omoshirokute tame ni naru"* — "To be interesting and beneficial" ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/74586-global-publishing-leaders-2017-kabushiki-gaisha-k-dansha-kodansha-limited.html)).

## A general publisher built on manga

Kodansha was long considered the largest publisher in Japan, with yearly revenues at one point over ¥200 billion, before the recession and the publishing downturn cut revenues — ¥117.3 billion in fiscal 2016 against ¥116.8 billion in 2015 ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/74586-global-publishing-leaders-2017-kabushiki-gaisha-k-dansha-kodansha-limited.html)). Unlike Shueisha, which is identified above all with *Weekly Shōnen Jump*, Kodansha has always been a general publisher: its manga magazines — *Weekly Shōnen Magazine* (since 1959), *Nakayoshi*, *Morning*, *Afternoon*, *Weekly Young Magazine* and *Bessatsu Shōnen Magazine* — sit alongside literary magazines such as *Gunzō* and *Shūkan Gendai* and the dictionary *Nihongo Daijiten*. *Weekly Shōnen Magazine* debuted on 17 March 1959, the same day as Shogakukan's *Weekly Shōnen Sunday* ([Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2008-03-18/shonen-magazine-shonen-sunday-mark-50th-anniversary)).

The company also owns the Otowa Group, which manages subsidiaries including King Records and Kobunsha, and publishes *Nikkan Gendai*, a daily tabloid ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/74586-global-publishing-leaders-2017-kabushiki-gaisha-k-dansha-kodansha-limited.html)). It sponsors the Kodansha Manga Award, which has run in its current form since 1977 (and under other names since 1960) ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/74586-global-publishing-leaders-2017-kabushiki-gaisha-k-dansha-kodansha-limited.html)). Its books, comics, magazines, television and film adaptations and merchandise are available in more than 40 countries ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/74586-global-publishing-leaders-2017-kabushiki-gaisha-k-dansha-kodansha-limited.html)). The flagship export of recent years is *Attack on Titan*, whose anime premiered on 7 April 2013 and whose final season became the most in-demand anime television show of 2022–23 ([Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/news/2023/10/one-piece-the-record-of-the-mega-popular-manga-series-explained-760171)).

Kodansha's answer to piracy and to the shift to digital reading has been to publish legitimately and simultaneously. On 21 March 2023 it announced K MANGA, an English-language manga service for readers in the [United States](/places/united-states/), launching on 10 May 2023 with about 400 titles — including 70 ongoing series released at the same time as in Japan — after its manga sales in the US tripled in 2022, a company record ([Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/press-release/2023-03-21/attack-on-titan-and-tokyo-revengers-publisher-kodansha-announces-new-manga-platform-k-manga-for-us-/.195976)). The launch line-up included *Attack on Titan*, *Tokyo Revengers*, *Fairy Tail* and *Ghost in the Shell* ([Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/press-release/2023-03-21/attack-on-titan-and-tokyo-revengers-publisher-kodansha-announces-new-manga-platform-k-manga-for-us-/.195976)).

## The Cloudflare manga-piracy lawsuit

Kodansha sued Cloudflare, Inc. — headquartered in [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/), [California](/places/california/) — together with Shueisha, Shogakukan and [Kadokawa](/organizations/kadokawa/) on 1 February 2022 in the Tokyo District Court ([Shogakukan](https://www.shogakukan.co.jp/storage/files/article/2021/20220201e.pdf)). The suit concerned two piracy sites that posted more than 4,000 manga works without permission and at their peak attracted over 300 million monthly visits combined ([Shueisha](https://www.shueisha.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/notice20251119eng.pdf)); the judgment records the sites as distributing 4,082 titles comprising 123,631 episodes ([Tokyo District Court](https://www.courts.go.jp/ip/eng/assets/ip/eng/chizai_en/chizai_en-pdf-3813.pdf)). Kodansha's print right for the one work of its own at issue in the case was established on 4 June 2021, its infringement notice under the US Digital Millennium Copyright Act reached Cloudflare on 12 November 2021, and the damage period the court assessed ran from 14 December 2021 to 28 February 2022 ([Tokyo District Court](https://www.courts.go.jp/ip/eng/assets/ip/eng/chizai_en/chizai_en-pdf-3813.pdf)).

The figures need to be stated carefully, because the amount sought and the amount ordered are different numbers that are often confused. At filing, the four publishers put total damages for the four litigated works at about ¥5.6 billion and sought about ¥115 million per company as part of that sum — about ¥460 million in total ([Shogakukan](https://www.shogakukan.co.jp/storage/files/article/2021/20220201e.pdf); [Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2025-11-19/shueisha-kodansha-shogakukan-kadokawa-win-copyright-suit-against-cloudflare/.231199)). By judgment, each publisher's claim had become ¥126.5 million. On 19 November 2025 the Tokyo District Court held that Cloudflare's failure to act promptly on infringement notices and its negligent continuation of pirated-content distribution constituted aiding and abetting copyright infringement, recognised total damages of about ¥3.6 billion (about US$24 million) for the four works, and ordered payment of about ¥500 million in total — ¥126,500,000 to Kodansha, the same to Shueisha and Shogakukan, and ¥121,400,928 to Kadokawa — plus interest at 3% a year from 21 October 2022 ([Tokyo District Court](https://www.courts.go.jp/ip/eng/assets/ip/eng/chizai_en/chizai_en-pdf-3813.pdf); [Shueisha](https://www.shueisha.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/notice20251119eng.pdf)).

## FAQ

**What is Kodansha?**
Kodansha Ltd. is a privately held Japanese publishing company founded in Tokyo in 1909 by Seiji Noma. It publishes books, magazines, manga and light novels, including *Weekly Shōnen Magazine* and the manga *Attack on Titan*, *Tokyo Revengers* and *Fairy Tail*.

**What manga magazines does Kodansha publish?**
Its manga magazines include *Weekly Shōnen Magazine* (since 1959), *Nakayoshi*, *Morning*, *Afternoon*, *Weekly Young Magazine* and *Bessatsu Shōnen Magazine*, alongside literary magazines such as *Gunzō* and *Shūkan Gendai*.

**What is K MANGA?**
K MANGA is Kodansha's official English-language manga service, launched in the United States on 10 May 2023 with about 400 titles, 70 of them ongoing series released simultaneously with Japan. It followed a year in which Kodansha's US manga sales tripled.

**How much did Kodansha win from Cloudflare?**
The Tokyo District Court ordered Cloudflare to pay Kodansha ¥126,500,000 plus 3% annual interest from 21 October 2022, in a judgment dated 19 November 2025. The court recognised about ¥3.6 billion in total damages across the four publishers' works but ordered about ¥500 million in total.

**Did Kodansha seek ¥500 million from Cloudflare?**
No. About ¥500 million is the total the court ordered all four publishers combined. At filing, Kodansha and each co-plaintiff sought about ¥115 million as part of estimated total damages of about ¥5.6 billion.
