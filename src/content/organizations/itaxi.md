## Overview

iTaxi is Poland's leading taxi-hailing marketplace — the app [Stefan Batory](/people/stefan-batory/) and Konrad Howard built before [Booksy](/organizations/booksy/), launched in February 2012 as a subsidiary of [EO Networks](/organizations/eo-networks/), Batory's software house ([Sramana Mitra](https://www.sramanamitra.com/2020/03/10/bootstrapping-with-services-from-poland-to-a-us-saas-company-stefan-batory-ceo-of-booksy-part-2/)). It was, in Batory's words, "the first application that would allow people to order a taxi via a smartphone" in Poland — and it was deliberately different from the ride-hailing model that would later dominate: it offered only licensed taxi drivers, "and not random drivers with personal cars" ([Sramana Mitra](https://www.sramanamitra.com/2020/03/10/bootstrapping-with-services-from-poland-to-a-us-saas-company-stefan-batory-ceo-of-booksy-part-2/)). By 2020 it had 10,000 cars in 100 cities and was listed among the most prominent Polish startups of the year ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200224005713/en/iTaxi-Launches-Direct-Carrier-Billing-Centili-Play)).

iTaxi's real significance is what it taught its founders. It succeeded in Poland and stayed in Poland — and that was the lesson. "On Day Zero, we already knew that we wanted to make a global company. We wanted to be an Uber, not an iTaxi," Batory told Sifted when Booksy was breaking into America ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/how-booksy-broke-into-america?ref=newsletter.kaya.vc)). iTaxi is the local-only company Booksy was designed not to be.

## The founding fight

The decision came at the end of 2011. Batory, who had spent twelve years building EO Networks into a 200-plus-engineer software house, and Howard, who had joined him to manage that software development house, decided to build a taxi app ([Sramana Mitra](https://www.sramanamitra.com/2020/03/10/bootstrapping-with-services-from-poland-to-a-us-saas-company-stefan-batory-ceo-of-booksy-part-2/); [Sramana Mitra](https://www.sramanamitra.com/2020/03/11/bootstrapping-with-services-from-poland-to-a-us-saas-company-stefan-batory-ceo-of-booksy-part-3/)). It launched in February 2012, and the incumbents fought back immediately: "we actually had problems with the dispatchers. The dispatchers tried to block us. They tried to threaten the drivers" ([Sramana Mitra](https://www.sramanamitra.com/2020/03/10/bootstrapping-with-services-from-poland-to-a-us-saas-company-stefan-batory-ceo-of-booksy-part-2/)).

The fight was not only external. One of the partners in the business wanted to kill iTaxi, and Batory bought the company back with his shares — a buy-back that also saw him detach "the whole classified media division" from EO Networks ([Sramana Mitra](https://www.sramanamitra.com/2020/03/10/bootstrapping-with-services-from-poland-to-a-us-saas-company-stefan-batory-ceo-of-booksy-part-2/)). The licensed-drivers-only rule, the dispatcher threats and the internal buy-back together explain why iTaxi looked the way it did: a marketplace built on the existing, regulated taxi fleet rather than on a new pool of unlicensed drivers — a model that won trust in Poland and, by the same token, could not easily be exported to markets with different taxi regulation.

## Scale and the road to Booksy

By the time Booksy raised its Series A in March 2017, iTaxi was described as Poland's number-one taxi-hailing app, and Booksy's founders were identified in the coverage as "previously of iTaxi" ([Growth Business](https://growthbusiness.co.uk/booksy-raises-3-4m-globalise-mobile-first-bookings-platform-15582/); [tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2017/03/03/booksy-series-a/)). Three years later it was still growing: in February 2020 it launched direct carrier billing with Centili and Play, the biggest Polish telco, and BusinessWire described it as "Poland's leading taxi hailing marketplace" with 10,000 cars in 100 cities, listed among the most prominent Polish startups of 2020 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200224005713/en/iTaxi-Launches-Direct-Carrier-Billing-Centili-Play)).

The contrast with Booksy is the point. iTaxi was a Polish success; Booksy was founded in 2014 by the same two men with the explicit aim of being global from day zero, and it entered the United States in 2017, acquired Lavito in 2018 and merged with Versum in December 2020 to enter Mexico ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/how-booksy-broke-into-america?ref=newsletter.kaya.vc); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/01/27/booksy-raises-70m-war-chest-to-acquire-salon-appointment-apps-expand-internationally/)). The taxi app taught Batory two things he carried into Booksy: that a marketplace built on an existing, regulated supply side can win a local market — and that a local win is not enough.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is iTaxi?**
Poland's leading taxi-hailing marketplace, launched in February 2012 by Stefan Batory and Konrad Howard, which lets people order a licensed taxi via a smartphone app ([Sramana Mitra](https://www.sramanamitra.com/2020/03/10/bootstrapping-with-services-from-poland-to-a-us-saas-company-stefan-batory-ceo-of-booksy-part-2/); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200224005713/en/iTaxi-Launches-Direct-Carrier-Billing-Centili-Play)).

**Who founded iTaxi?**
Stefan Batory and Konrad Howard, the same two founders who went on to found Booksy in 2014 ([tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2017/03/03/booksy-series-a/)).

**Why did iTaxi use only licensed drivers?**
That was the founding rule: Batory described iTaxi as "the first in Poland to offer only licensed taxi drivers, and not random drivers with personal cars" ([Sramana Mitra](https://www.sramanamitra.com/2020/03/10/bootstrapping-with-services-from-poland-to-a-us-saas-company-stefan-batory-ceo-of-booksy-part-2/)).

**How is iTaxi connected to Booksy?**
It is Booksy's predecessor in the founders' story: the local-only success that taught Batory to build Booksy as a global company from day zero — "We wanted to be an Uber, not an iTaxi" ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/how-booksy-broke-into-america?ref=newsletter.kaya.vc)).

**How big is iTaxi?**
By 2020 it had 10,000 cars in 100 cities and was described as Poland's leading taxi-hailing marketplace ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200224005713/en/iTaxi-Launches-Direct-Carrier-Billing-Centili-Play)).
