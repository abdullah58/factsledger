## Overview

Clay is a New York-based AI go-to-market (GTM) platform that helps sales and growth teams turn any growth idea into reality using data enrichment, workflow automation and AI agents ([Business Wire](https://markets.financialcontent.com/franklincredit/article/bizwire-2026-1-28-clay-announces-second-employee-tender-offer-in-nine-months-at-a-5b-valuation)). Founded in June 2017 by Kareem Amin and Nicolae Rusan, it spent its first five years close to zero revenue, then exploded: by January 2026 it served 14,000 customers, had reached $100 million in annual recurring revenue, and was valued at $5 billion in an employee tender offer ([Business Wire](https://markets.financialcontent.com/franklincredit/article/bizwire-2026-1-28-clay-announces-second-employee-tender-offer-in-nine-months-at-a-5b-valuation)). Its platform aggregates people and company data from more than 150 sources — the kind of career-history enrichment that supplied the dates for Dario Amodei's stint at [Baidu](/organizations/baidu/), the subject of this profile series.

## From spreadsheet to sales engine

The idea behind Clay began with an abstract ambition: to make the power of programming accessible to more people ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/clays-path-to-product-market-fit/?ref=the-review-newsletter)). Amin and Rusan met at McGill University in Montreal, worked together at Microsoft, and left in 2010 to build startups — first Frame, a tablet-friendly ecommerce app acquired by Sailthru in May 2012, then roles at Dow Jones, where Amin was VP of Product at *The Wall Street Journal* ([Contrary Research](https://research2.contrary.com/company/clay)). By the end of 2015 both had left Dow Jones to explore how APIs and software tools could help people work better ([Contrary Research](https://research2.contrary.com/company/clay)).

Their first idea was to rebuild the terminal; then they settled on the world's most popular programming environment — the spreadsheet — and asked what it would look like if a spreadsheet could pull data from the internet ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/clays-path-to-product-market-fit/?ref=the-review-newsletter)). The early product scraped dozens of databases to pull information directly into spreadsheet cells, including identifying information such as profiles, ratings and descriptions ([Contrary Research](https://research2.contrary.com/company/clay)). Two product principles guided them: aggregate every data source in the world, and be the most flexible tool in the world — flexibility first, even at the cost of ease of use ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/clays-path-to-product-market-fit/?ref=the-review-newsletter)).

The trouble was that the product could do too many things. Recruiters used it to find candidates, salespeople for outreach, engineers as a low-code backend — and usage was inconsistent. As Amin put it, people would say "Wow, this is so powerful!" and then not come back the next day ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/clays-path-to-product-market-fit/?ref=the-review-newsletter)). The way out was committing to one customer: outbound salespeople. By January 2022 the team had narrowed to outbound data enrichment for growth and sales teams, and in February 2022 it launched the refocused platform on Product Hunt ([Contrary Research](https://research2.contrary.com/company/clay)). Varun Anand, who had joined as an advisor after sending Clay its first paying customers, became co-founder and Head of Operations in September 2021 ([Contrary Research](https://research2.contrary.com/company/clay)).

## The overnight success, seven years in

First Round Capital, Clay's seed investor, calls the company's story a "slow bake" ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/clays-path-to-product-market-fit/?ref=the-review-newsletter)). The startup was still close to $0 in revenue as recently as spring 2022 — but the narrowing worked. By mid-2024 Clay had passed 100,000 users and 2,500 customers, including [Anthropic](/organizations/anthropic/), Intercom, Notion, Vanta and Verkada, and had raised a $46 million Series B round ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/clays-path-to-product-market-fit/?ref=the-review-newsletter)). The funding arc then accelerated: a $46 million Series B at a $500 million valuation led by Meritech Capital in June 2024, a $40 million Series B expansion at a $1.25 billion valuation in January 2025, and a $100 million Series C at a $3.1 billion valuation led by CapitalG — Alphabet's independent growth fund — in August 2025, bringing total funding to $204 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/05/clay-confirms-it-closed-100m-round-at-3-1b-valuation/)). In January 2025 Clay also acquired Avenue, an automation tool for operations teams ([Contrary Research](https://research2.contrary.com/company/clay)).

Growth kept compounding. Revenue grew more than 3.5 times in the year to December 2025, reaching $100 million in ARR, and the company says it was cash-flow positive for parts of the year ([Business Wire](https://markets.financialcontent.com/franklincredit/article/bizwire-2026-1-28-clay-announces-second-employee-tender-offer-in-nine-months-at-a-5b-valuation)). In January 2026 Clay announced its second employee tender offer in nine months — a $5 billion valuation, more than three times the $1.5 billion Sequoia-led tender the previous spring — letting employees sell up to $55 million of shares ([Business Wire](https://markets.financialcontent.com/franklincredit/article/bizwire-2026-1-28-clay-announces-second-employee-tender-offer-in-nine-months-at-a-5b-valuation)). Customers now include [OpenAI](/organizations/openai/), Canva, Ramp and Rippling alongside Anthropic ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/05/clay-confirms-it-closed-100m-round-at-3-1b-valuation/)).

## People data at the core

Clay's product is built on aggregation. Its enrichment tool enhances a customer's first-party data with information from more than 130 data providers (as of July 2025), searched sequentially through a "waterfall" — if one database lacks a prospect's phone number, the next is queried — and its Claygent AI agent browses public sources to answer custom research queries ([Contrary Research](https://research2.contrary.com/company/clay)). The company says the platform now combines data from 150+ sources ([Business Wire](https://markets.financialcontent.com/franklincredit/article/bizwire-2026-1-28-clay-announces-second-employee-tender-offer-in-nine-months-at-a-5b-valuation)). That people-data layer is the connection to this profile series: Clay's aggregated career histories are the source behind the dates of Dario Amodei's Baidu stint — he joined Baidu in November 2014 and left in 2015 — which the Baidu profile documents. A community grew around the product too: a customer Slack channel that started with around 200 people now has more than 11,000 members, and dozens of "Claygencies" — agencies specialising in implementing Clay — have sprung up, some with run rates over $2 million a year ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/clays-path-to-product-market-fit/?ref=the-review-newsletter)).
