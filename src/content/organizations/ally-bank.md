## Overview

Ally Bank is the branchless, online-only banking subsidiary of Ally Financial, offering savings, checking, money-market accounts, certificates of deposit, and IRAs to U.S. customers without physical branches ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/bull-of-the-day:-ally-financial-ally-2019-09-10)). Its deposit base funds its Detroit-based parent's auto-finance and corporate-lending businesses, and the bank held about $156.6 billion in deposits as of mid-2026 ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/ally-bank-ceo-rhodes-focused-strategy/832330/)).

This profile is about the bank, not the parent: Ally Financial Inc. is the Detroit-headquartered holding company, while Ally Bank is its consumer banking arm, with banking announcements issued from Sandy, Utah ([Associated Press](https://legalnews.com/Home/Articles?DataId=1398786); [Ally Financial](https://media.ally.com/2019-05-13-Ally-Bank-Celebrates-10-Years-of-Doing-It-Right-for-Customers?asPDF=1)).

## From GMAC's bank to Ally Bank

Ally Bank's lineage runs through the auto industry. Its parent was founded in 1919 as the General Motors Acceptance Corporation (GMAC), created to provide automobile financing to customers who had never before bought anything as expensive as a new car ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/bull-of-the-day:-ally-financial-ally-2019-09-10)). The GM association became a liability in the financial crisis: GMAC received a $17.2 billion bailout that began in 2008, and the government sold its final stake in December 2014, by which point it had recovered $18.3 billion from the investment ([Associated Press](https://legalnews.com/Home/Articles?DataId=1398786)).

Ally Bank itself launched in May 2009, on the heels of that crisis, as the renamed GMAC Bank — a bank with no brick-and-mortar locations, built on no monthly account fees, 24/7 customer service, and rates it pitched as among the best in the country ([Ally Financial](https://media.ally.com/2019-05-13-Ally-Bank-Celebrates-10-Years-of-Doing-It-Right-for-Customers?asPDF=1)). The parent followed in 2010, rebranding from GMAC to Ally Financial ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/bull-of-the-day:-ally-financial-ally-2019-09-10)), and went public in April 2014, with the [Treasury](/organizations/us-department-of-the-treasury/) selling a chunk of its stake in the offering ([Associated Press](https://legalnews.com/Home/Articles?DataId=1398786)). The branchless model was the point: as a direct bank offering services online, by telephone, by mail, and at ATMs, Ally avoided branch costs and kept customer fees extremely low, in some cases reimbursing ATM fees charged by other banks ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/bull-of-the-day:-ally-financial-ally-2019-09-10)).

## A deposit franchise inside a refocused parent

By the second quarter of 2026, Ally Bank held about $156.6 billion in deposits — roughly 0.82% of the overall FDIC-insured deposit market — with its retail bank accounting for about $144 billion and 3.6 million customers ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/ally-bank-ceo-rhodes-focused-strategy/832330/)). That deposit franchise is now the funding engine for a deliberately narrower parent. Since Michael Rhodes took over as CEO in April 2024, Ally has narrowed its purview to three businesses — consumer banking, corporate finance, and auto dealer financial services — on the argument that doing less, but extraordinarily well, is the winning formula ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/ally-bank-ceo-rhodes-focused-strategy/832330/)).

The narrowing has been concrete: the bank stopped originating mortgages and sold its credit-card program in early 2025, a business with about $2.3 billion in receivables that served a near-prime segment mismatched with the digital bank's higher-earning customers ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/ally-bank-ceo-rhodes-focused-strategy/832330/)). What remains at the center is the consumer bank itself — high-yield savings accounts and certificates of deposit, sold to a growing pool of customers open to a digital-only banking relationship, in a deposit market where rising rates have made those deposits more expensive to hold ([Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/ally-bank-ceo-rhodes-focused-strategy/832330/)).
