## Overview

Penske Media Corporation (PMC) is a privately held American media company founded by [Jay Penske](/people/jay-penske/) — PMC dates its founding to 2004 — that owns [Variety](/organizations/variety/), [Rolling Stone](/organizations/rolling-stone/), Deadline and Women's Wear Daily, operates Billboard through the PMRC joint venture with [MRC](/organizations/mrc/), and controls [Dick Clark Productions](/organizations/dick-clark-productions/), producer of the Golden Globe Awards ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/leadership/jay); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/variety-parent-penske-media-to-take-over-hollywood-reporter-billboard-in-joint-venture-with-mrc/)). Based in Los Angeles and [New York City](/places/new-york-city/), it is one of the largest entertainment-media owners in the [United States](/places/united-states/), with brands that PMC says reach more than 340 million people each month ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/leadership/jay)).

That scale was assembled deal by deal, not built from scratch: PMC began as an internet-services business, bought its way into Hollywood trade publishing with Deadline in 2009 and Variety in 2012, then added fashion, music and live events through a run of acquisitions and joint ventures. Jay Penske, its founder, chairman and chief executive, is the son of automotive tycoon Roger Penske — but PMC is a separate company from his father's Penske Corporation, the transportation business, and was built with outside capital and partners rather than as a subsidiary of it ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/nikki-finke-owner-penske-media-buys-hollywood-trade-variety-58041/)). PMC says it has twice been named one of the Top 100 Private Companies in the United States ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/leadership/jay)).

## From an internet-services startup to a trade-paper buyer

PMC traces its origin to the company Jay Penske founded in 2004, according to PMC itself, though reference works date the business to 2003 as Velocity Services, an affinity-marketing and internet-services firm that later became Mail.com Media after acquiring the Mail.com domain ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/leadership/jay)). The pivot into publishing came in June 2009, when Mail.com Media bought Deadline Hollywood Daily from its founder Nikki Finke for a reported seven-figure payout plus a five-year employment contract ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/nikki-finke-owner-penske-media-buys-hollywood-trade-variety-58041/)). A blog buying a trade paper still sounded improbable three years later, when PMC bought Variety — the 107-year-old Hollywood trade, founded in 1905 — from Reed Elsevier for about $25 million, with the price never officially disclosed ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/nikki-finke-owner-penske-media-buys-hollywood-trade-variety-58041/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/184804/penske-buys-variety-extends-cross-platform.html?edition=)). Reed Elsevier had struggled for months to sell the paper after asking $40 million, and PMC first partnered with Shamrock Capital Advisors before Third Point LLC ultimately funded the purchase ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/nikki-finke-owner-penske-media-buys-hollywood-trade-variety-58041/)).

## Fashion, Rolling Stone and Saudi money

The Variety deal set the template: buy a distressed prestige brand cheaply, then invest in its digital platform. In August 2014 PMC paid $100 million to Condé Nast for Fairchild Fashion Media, owner of Women's Wear Daily, Footwear News and Beauty Inc., while Condé Nast kept Style.com and NowManifest ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/performance-marketing/cond-nast-sells-fairchild-fashion-media-penske-159594/)). In 2017 PMC acquired a controlling 51% stake in Wenner Media, parent of Rolling Stone, in a deal valuing Wenner Media at $100 million, with Jann Wenner staying on as editorial director ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/markets/mergers-and-acquisitions/rolling-stone-stake-acquired-in-100-million-deal-14427722)). BandLab Technologies, which bought a 49% stake in Rolling Stone in 2016 for about $40 million, remained a shareholder ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/markets/mergers-and-acquisitions/rolling-stone-stake-acquired-in-100-million-deal-14427722)). Two months later PMC took a $200 million minority investment from Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund to fund investment in existing properties and future acquisitions, while Penske kept well over 51% ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/penske-media-saudis-jamal-khashoggis-journalist/)). PMC says its brands reach more than 340 million people a month ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/leadership/jay)) — scale that let it absorb outside capital while keeping control.

## PMRC: how Billboard came under Penske's roof

PMC never bought Billboard outright — a distinction most summaries blur. On September 23, 2020, PMC and MRC announced PMRC, a joint venture that put Billboard, The Hollywood Reporter and Vibe under the same operating roof as PMC's Variety, Rolling Stone and [Music Business Worldwide](/organizations/music-business-worldwide/), with PMC leading day-to-day operations ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/variety-parent-penske-media-to-take-over-hollywood-reporter-billboard-in-joint-venture-with-mrc/); [Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)). Both companies hold significant ownership in the ventures and the financial terms were not disclosed ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/variety-parent-penske-media-to-take-over-hollywood-reporter-billboard-in-joint-venture-with-mrc/)). A second joint venture put MRC — owner of Dick Clark Productions — in charge of developing film, television, live-event and other projects from the PMRC brands' stories and intellectual property ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)). The deal ended some 90 years of rivalry between The Hollywood Reporter and Variety: Variety was marking its 115th year in 2020, The Hollywood Reporter dated to 1930, and Billboard had published its first hit parade in 1934 ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)).

## Awards, live events and the Vox roll-up

The PMRC structure also pointed PMC toward live events. In April 2021 its MRC joint venture bought a 50% stake in Austin's SXSW festival, after the pandemic devastated its finances ([CultureMap Austin](https://culturemap.com/austin/news/entertainment/04-20-21-sxsw-drums-up-financial-lifeline-from-publisher-of-irolling-stonei/)). In January 2023 Penske Media Eldridge — the PMC–Eldridge joint venture — acquired Dick Clark Productions, producer of the Golden Globes, the American Music Awards, the Academy of Country Music Awards and the [Billboard Music Awards](/events/billboard-music-awards/) ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/penske-media-eldridge-acquires-dick-clark-productions-producer-of-the-golden-globes-and-billboard-music-awards/?ref=pennyfractions.ghost.io)). That June, Dick Clark Productions and Eldridge acquired the Golden Globes from the Hollywood Foreign Press Association, which wound down ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/dick-clark-productions-and-eldridge-acquire-golden-globes)). In July 2026 the reactivated HFPA sued Penske and PMC, seeking more than $150 million and the unwinding of the Globes deal; a Globes spokesperson called the claims absurd and illegitimate ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/awards/hfpa-penske-golden-globes-lawsuit-explained/)). PMC had already paid $100 million for a 20% Vox Media stake in February 2023, then acquired the remaining brands in June 2026, folding more than 25 brands into new subsidiary PMX ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/vox-media-majority-stake-penske-media-corporation/); [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/dealroom/penske-media-vox-media-brands-pmx/)).

## Frequently asked questions

### Who owns Penske Media Corporation?

PMC is privately held and controlled by its founder, chairman and chief executive Jay Penske, who is the son of automotive tycoon Roger Penske ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/nikki-finke-owner-penske-media-buys-hollywood-trade-variety-58041/)). Outside investors have held minority stakes, including a $200 million Saudi investment in 2018, but PMC said Penske's stake remained well in excess of 51% ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/penske-media-saudis-jamal-khashoggis-journalist/)).

### Does Penske Media own Billboard?

No, not outright. Billboard came under PMC's operational roof through PMRC, the joint venture PMC formed with MRC in September 2020, in which both companies hold significant ownership ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/variety-parent-penske-media-to-take-over-hollywood-reporter-billboard-in-joint-venture-with-mrc/)). PMC leads PMRC's day-to-day operations and moved Billboard onto its technology platforms, but the financial terms were never disclosed.

### What is PMRC?

PMRC is the joint venture formed by Penske Media Corporation and MRC on September 23, 2020, to operate Billboard, The Hollywood Reporter and Vibe alongside Variety, Rolling Stone and Music Business Worldwide ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)). A second joint venture lets MRC develop film, television and live-event projects from the PMRC brands' stories and intellectual property.

### What happened with the Golden Globes?

Dick Clark Productions and Eldridge acquired all Golden Globes assets from the Hollywood Foreign Press Association in June 2023, and the HFPA wound down ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/dick-clark-productions-and-eldridge-acquire-golden-globes)). In July 2026 the reactivated HFPA sued Jay Penske and PMC, seeking more than $150 million and the unwinding of the deal; a Globes spokesperson called the claims absurd and illegitimate ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/awards/hfpa-penske-golden-globes-lawsuit-explained/)).

### Is Penske Media Corporation the same as Penske Corporation?

No. Penske Corporation is the transportation and automotive business founded by Roger Penske, while Penske Media Corporation is the media company founded by his son Jay Penske ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/nikki-finke-owner-penske-media-buys-hollywood-trade-variety-58041/)). They share a family name and a founder's son, not a corporate structure.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim a settled founding year: PMC says 2004 and reference works say 2003, and the difference is flagged rather than resolved ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/leadership/jay)). It does not claim PMC owns Billboard outright — Billboard sits in the PMRC joint venture, whose terms were never disclosed ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/variety-parent-penske-media-to-take-over-hollywood-reporter-billboard-in-joint-venture-with-mrc/)). It does not settle the identity of the Saudi investor beyond TheWrap's October 2018 report naming Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/penske-media-saudis-jamal-khashoggis-journalist/)). It does not treat the HFPA's 2026 allegations as proven facts: they are claims in a lawsuit, which the Globes side denies ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/awards/hfpa-penske-golden-globes-lawsuit-explained/)). It does not state prices or ownership percentages for deals whose terms were undisclosed, and it omits several acquisitions attributed to PMC in reference works that were not verified in a fully-read source in this session.
