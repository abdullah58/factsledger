## Overview

**Pepsi** is the flagship cola of PepsiCo — first sold as "Brad's Drink" from a New Bern, North Carolina drugstore soda fountain in 1893, renamed Pepsi-Cola in 1898 ([NC DNCR](https://www.dncr.nc.gov/blog/2016/08/28/brads-drink-becomes-pepsi-cola-1898)). It is the challenger half of the century-long "cola wars" with Coca-Cola, and the brand behind the most famous celebrity endorsement in history: [Michael Jackson](/people/michael-jackson/)'s record-breaking 1983 partnership — during which a January 1984 commercial shoot left the singer with severe scalp burns ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/michael-jackson-pepsi-made-marketing-history-99789/); [Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/michael-jackson-biopic-fact-fiction-michael-11956448)).

The incident has returned to the screen: the burns and the painkiller use that followed are part of Antoine Fuqua's 2026 biopic [*Michael*](/creative-works/michael-2026-film/), and the 2026 TMZ documentary *Michael Jackson: 30 Fatal Seconds* — linking a 40-year-old advertising accident to the most scrutinized chapter of Jackson's later life ([Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/michael-jackson-biopic-fact-fiction-michael-11956448); [PEOPLE](https://people.com/michael-jackson-pepsi-commercial-accident-what-to-know-11960631)).

## Brad's Drink becomes Pepsi-Cola

In 1893, New Bern pharmacist Caleb Bradham began serving a carbonated drink he called "Brad's Drink" from the soda fountain of his pharmacy at Pollock and Middle Streets; on August 28, 1898, he renamed it Pepsi-Cola, and in 1902 he incorporated the Pepsi-Cola Company under North Carolina law ([NC DNCR](https://www.dncr.nc.gov/blog/2016/08/28/brads-drink-becomes-pepsi-cola-1898)). Bradham, who had some medical training, believed the brew — originally made with the enzyme pepsin and the kola nut — aided digestion and energy; the name is thought to derive from those ingredients, though pepsin was later removed ([NC DNCR](https://www.dncr.nc.gov/blog/2016/08/28/brads-drink-becomes-pepsi-cola-1898)). By 1910 more than 300 companies in 24 states bottled the drink, before post–World War I sugar-price swings and weak marketing forced Bradham to sell ([NC DNCR](https://www.dncr.nc.gov/blog/2016/08/28/brads-drink-becomes-pepsi-cola-1898)). Per the company's own history, Pepsi introduced its red, white and blue bottle cap in 1951 — the ancestor of the globe logo — and the 1965 merger of the Pepsi-Cola Company with Frito-Lay created PepsiCo ([PepsiCo](https://www.pepsico.com/newsroom/stories/2026/pepsi-history-brads-drink-iconic-american-brand)).

## The cola wars: the Pepsi Challenge and New Coke

Pepsi's century-long rivalry with Coca-Cola turned confrontational in the 1970s with the Pepsi Challenge, a blind taste test in which shoppers repeatedly preferred Pepsi's flavor; the aggressive campaign gained market share through the 1970s and early 1980s while Coca-Cola's slipped ([HISTORY](https://www.history.com/articles/why-coca-cola-new-coke-flopped)). Alarmed, Coca-Cola ran 190,000 of its own blind taste tests, concluded taste was the problem, and on April 23, 1985 launched New Coke — a sweeter formula that, the press noted, tasted "more like Pepsi" ([HISTORY](https://www.history.com/articles/why-coca-cola-new-coke-flopped)). The backlash was instant: 5,000 to 8,000 angry calls a day, protesters pouring New Coke into sewers; Pepsi gave its employees the day off and declared victory in ads boasting "the other guy just blinked" ([HISTORY](https://www.history.com/articles/why-coca-cola-new-coke-flopped)). Seventy-nine days later, Coca-Cola restored the original formula as Coca-Cola Classic, which quickly outsold New Coke ([HISTORY](https://www.history.com/articles/why-coca-cola-new-coke-flopped)) — a lesson, the company's own executives conceded, in the emotional attachment that consumer research could not measure ([HISTORY](https://www.history.com/articles/why-coca-cola-new-coke-flopped)).

## The Jackson partnership — and the 1984 fire

In November 1983, a year after *Thriller*, Michael Jackson and his brothers signed a $5 million partnership with PepsiCo — a celebrity-endorsement record brokered by Jay Coleman, who had first offered the deal to Coca-Cola (whose $1 million counteroffer was rejected) before PepsiCo CEO Roger Enrico seized it to launch the youth-targeted "New Generation" campaign ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/michael-jackson-pepsi-made-marketing-history-99789/)). Jackson suggested using "Billie Jean" as the jingle, rewriting its chorus around the Pepsi generation ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/michael-jackson-pepsi-made-marketing-history-99789/)). On January 27, 1984, filming a commercial at [Los Angeles](/places/los-angeles/)' Shrine Auditorium before 3,000 fans, a pyrotechnics malfunction set Jackson's heavily gelled hair ablaze; he kept dancing before crew members rushed in and tackled him to smother the flames ([Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/michael-jackson-biopic-fact-fiction-michael-11956448); [PEOPLE](https://people.com/michael-jackson-pepsi-commercial-accident-what-to-know-11960631)). He suffered severe second- and third-degree scalp burns, was treated at Cedars-Sinai and then Brotman Medical Center, and declined to sue, settling for $1.5 million, which he donated to establish the Michael Jackson Burn Center at Brotman ([Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/michael-jackson-biopic-fact-fiction-michael-11956448); [PEOPLE](https://people.com/michael-jackson-pepsi-commercial-accident-what-to-know-11960631)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What happened to Michael Jackson during the 1984 Pepsi commercial?**
On January 27, 1984, at the Shrine Auditorium in Los Angeles, Jackson was filming a Pepsi commercial before 3,000 fans when a pyrotechnics malfunction set his heavily gelled hair on fire during a "Billie Jean" take. He kept dancing briefly before crew members tackled him to smother the flames. He suffered severe scalp burns, was treated at Cedars-Sinai and Brotman Medical Center, and the accident is recreated in the 2026 biopic *Michael*.

**How much was Michael Jackson's Pepsi deal worth?**
The November 1983 partnership paid Jackson and his brothers $5 million — a record for a celebrity endorsement at the time. A second deal in 1987–88 was worth $10 million and was global, covering 20-plus countries in support of the *Bad* album and world tour. Both figures come from Adweek's marketing-history account of the partnership.

**When and where was Pepsi invented?**
Pharmacist Caleb Bradham created "Brad's Drink" in 1893 at his drugstore soda fountain in New Bern, North Carolina. He renamed it Pepsi-Cola on August 28, 1898, and incorporated the Pepsi-Cola Company in 1902. By 1910 it was bottled by more than 300 companies across 24 states, per the North Carolina Department of Natural and Cultural Resources.

**Why did Pepsi change its logo in 2023?**
The March 2023 redesign was the brand's sixth since 1898 and its first change in 15 years (the previous logo dated to 2008). It restored the Pepsi wordmark inside the globe — evoking the 1990s look — and added black to the palette, tying the master brand to Pepsi Zero Sugar, which the company treats as its future growth focus.

**Did the 2026 film *Michael* show the Pepsi accident?**
Yes. Entertainment Weekly's fact-check of the film confirms the January 1984 commercial fire is recreated on screen, with [Jaafar Jackson](/people/jaafar-jackson/) as Michael. The biopic's production history — including material on the burns and the painkiller use that followed — is documented in this site's profile of the film.
