## Overview

Fitch Ratings is one of the "Big Three" credit rating agencies — the American–British firm that invented the AAA-to-D scale the whole industry still uses, and one of only three agencies the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission first recognized as nationally recognized statistical rating organizations (NRSROs) in 1975 ([U.S. Senate](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/joynt.pdf)). Founded in 1913 by John Knowles Fitch as the Fitch Publishing Company in New York City, it is dual-headquartered in New York and London and has been wholly owned by the Hearst Corporation since April 2018 ([U.S. Senate](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/joynt.pdf); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/317530/hearst-diversifies-revenue-with-fitch-group-acquis.html?mobile-app=true&theme=false)). Fitch's power is simple to state and hard to overstate: its published opinion on a borrower's creditworthiness, distilled into a letter grade, decides what trillions of dollars of debt cost — and in August 2023 it used that power to strip the [United States](/places/united-states/) of its AAA rating.

A credit rating, in Fitch's own definition, is a published opinion as to the creditworthiness of a security, distilled into a simple grading system from AAA to DDD, typically accompanied by explanatory analysis ([U.S. Senate](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/joynt.pdf)). The grades run in a hierarchy — AAA as the safest, then AA+, AA, AA-, A+, A, A-, BBB, down to D — and for regulatory and investment purposes, debt rated BBB- or higher counts as investment grade ([Congressional Research Service](https://d27r7ebsgu0es2.cloudfront.net/reports/IF11916.html)). Investors use ratings as a common benchmark to compare credit risk across diverse investments, and regulators have long baked them into safety-and-soundness and eligible-investment rules for banks, insurers, and other financial institutions ([U.S. Senate](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/joynt.pdf)).

## From a publishing house to a global agency

John Knowles Fitch's original business was not ratings but information: the Fitch Publishing Company published financial statistics on stocks and bonds for the investment community ([U.S. Senate](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/joynt.pdf)). In the 1920s the firm introduced the now-familiar "AAA" to "D" rating scale — the first agency to do so, and the scale every major agency still uses in near-identical form ([U.S. Senate](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/joynt.pdf)). When the SEC began using ratings to enforce its net capital rule for broker-dealers in 1975, it determined that the ratings of the three dominant agencies — Standard & Poor's, Moody's, and Fitch — were nationally used, making them the original NRSROs ([Congressional Research Service](https://d27r7ebsgu0es2.cloudfront.net/reports/IF11916.html)).

Fitch spent most of the twentieth century as the smallest of the three, then grew aggressively. A new management team recapitalized the firm in 1989, and through the 1990s it expanded especially in structured finance, offering investors original research and more rigorous surveillance than its rivals ([U.S. Senate](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/joynt.pdf)). In 1997 Fitch merged with IBCA Limited, another NRSRO headquartered in London, significantly increasing its worldwide presence and its coverage of banks, financial institutions, and sovereigns — and through the merger it became owned by Fimalac, the French holding company that had acquired IBCA in 1992 ([U.S. Senate](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/joynt.pdf)). In April 2000 it acquired Duff & Phelps Credit Rating Co., a Chicago-based NRSRO, and later that year the rating business of Thomson BankWatch ([U.S. Senate](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/joynt.pdf)).

Fitch's own defense of its record, delivered by CEO Stephen W. Joynt to the Senate Banking Committee in 2005, rested on performance data: its default studies showed AAA-rated corporate bonds defaulting at an average annual rate of 0.00% from 1990 to 2003, and AAA structured-finance bonds at 0.00% from 1991 to 2003 ([U.S. Senate](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/joynt.pdf)). The same testimony was candid about the model's limits: rating agencies do not audit or verify the information they rely on, and Fitch maintained that its ratings are opinions entitled to First Amendment protection, not personalized investment advice ([U.S. Senate](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/joynt.pdf)).

## The 2008 crisis and the regulatory reckoning

The limits of that model became the central story of the 2007–2009 financial crisis. The provision of investment-grade ratings by the three dominant agencies — S&P, Moody's, and Fitch — for structured-finance securities was widely seen as a critical part of structuring the residential mortgage-backed securities and collateralized debt obligations that held subprime mortgages; private mortgage-backed securities issuance reportedly grew from $126 billion in 2000 to $1.145 trillion in 2006 ([Congressional Research Service](https://d27r7ebsgu0es2.cloudfront.net/reports/IF11916.html)). The 2011 Financial Crisis Inquiry Report concluded that the three leading agencies fundamentally failed in their ratings of these securities, exacerbating the market collapse — during the housing boom they often gave top-tier AAA ratings to structured securities, only to downgrade many of them later to below investment grade — and called the agencies "key enablers of the financial meltdown" ([Congressional Research Service](https://d27r7ebsgu0es2.cloudfront.net/reports/IF11916.html)).

Critics pointed to the issuer-pays model as the structural cause: rating agencies are typically paid by the issuers of the securities they rate, which many see as a conflict of interest, and "ratings shopping" — issuers seeking out agencies offering more favorable ratings — may have played a role ([Congressional Research Service](https://d27r7ebsgu0es2.cloudfront.net/reports/IF11916.html)). Congress responded in stages: the Credit Rating Agency Reform Act of 2006 established SEC oversight and a registration program for NRSROs, and the Dodd-Frank Act of 2010 created the SEC Office of Credit Ratings, imposed reporting and examination requirements, required analysts to pass qualifying exams, gave the SEC authority to deregister an NRSRO, and required at least half of an NRSRO board's directors to be independent ([Congressional Research Service](https://d27r7ebsgu0es2.cloudfront.net/reports/IF11916.html)). The industry remains highly concentrated: as of March 2024 there were 10 NRSROs, but the three large ones — S&P, Moody's, and Fitch — garnered 91% of NRSRO revenue, and together the Big Three issued about 95% of outstanding ratings ([Congressional Research Service](https://d27r7ebsgu0es2.cloudfront.net/reports/IF11916.html)). The SEC's February 2024 annual report on NRSROs still found material deficiencies in examinations, including nine instances of failures to sufficiently address conflicts of interest ([Congressional Research Service](https://d27r7ebsgu0es2.cloudfront.net/reports/IF11916.html)).

## Hearst ownership and the U.S. downgrade

Hearst bought its first stake in Fitch Group in 2006 and raised it to 80% in 2015; on April 12, 2018, it announced it had acquired the remaining 20% from Fimalac for $2.8 billion, making Fitch Group its largest wholly-owned business ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/317530/hearst-diversifies-revenue-with-fitch-group-acquis.html?mobile-app=true&theme=false)). Fitch Group comprises three core businesses — Fitch Ratings, Fitch Solutions, and Fitch Learning — and by 2018 about 20% of its annual revenue came from data products unrelated to paid ratings ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/317530/hearst-diversifies-revenue-with-fitch-group-acquis.html?mobile-app=true&theme=false)).

Fitch's most consequential single rating action came on August 1, 2023, when it cut the United States' credit rating by one notch, from AAA to AA+, saying rising deficits and political brinkmanship were imperiling the government's ability to pay its debts ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2023-08-01/the-u-s-loses-its-top-aaa-rating-from-fitch-over-worries-about-the-nations-finances)). The downgrade came two months after the Biden administration and House Republicans agreed to suspend the debt ceiling in a last-minute deal, narrowly avoiding a federal default ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2023-08-01/the-u-s-loses-its-top-aaa-rating-from-fitch-over-worries-about-the-nations-finances)). "In Fitch's view, there has been a steady deterioration in standards of governance over the last 20 years, including on fiscal and debt matters," the agency said, adding that "the repeated debt-limit political standoffs and last-minute resolutions have eroded confidence in fiscal management" ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2023-08-01/the-u-s-loses-its-top-aaa-rating-from-fitch-over-worries-about-the-nations-finances)). It was only the second time a major agency had stripped the U.S. of AAA — Standard & Poor's did so in 2011 — leaving Moody's as the only one of the three still giving the United States its top rating ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2023-08-01/the-u-s-loses-its-top-aaa-rating-from-fitch-over-worries-about-the-nations-finances)). Treasury Secretary Janet Yellen called the decision "arbitrary" and based on outdated information, insisting Treasury securities "remain the world's preeminent safe and liquid asset" ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2023-08-01/the-u-s-loses-its-top-aaa-rating-from-fitch-over-worries-about-the-nations-finances)). Government interest payments in the first nine months of that fiscal year had totaled $652 billion, a 25% increase from the same period a year earlier ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2023-08-01/the-u-s-loses-its-top-aaa-rating-from-fitch-over-worries-about-the-nations-finances)).

## Fitch and the new fintech securitizations

Fitch's ratings now reach into the newest corners of consumer finance. In February 2026, [Avant](/organizations/avant/) closed a $200 million personal-loan securitization — its 23rd since founding and its seventh revolving transaction — that became the first of its deals to earn AAA ratings from both Fitch Ratings and Kroll Bond Rating Agency ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/loans/2026/fintech-avant-boosts-financing-capacity-with-new-200-million-dollar-deal/)). The 24-month revolving structure is expected to provide more than $500 million in financing capacity, bringing Avant's long-term funding commitments to about $2.5 billion ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/loans/2026/fintech-avant-boosts-financing-capacity-with-new-200-million-dollar-deal/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Fitch Ratings?**
Fitch Ratings is one of the Big Three credit rating agencies, alongside Moody's and S&P, dual-headquartered in New York and London and wholly owned by Hearst Corporation since 2018 ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/317530/hearst-diversifies-revenue-with-fitch-group-acquis.html?mobile-app=true&theme=false)). It publishes opinions on the creditworthiness of securities and issuers, from corporations and banks to sovereign nations ([U.S. Senate](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/joynt.pdf)).

**What does AAA mean in a Fitch rating?**
AAA is the top of Fitch's rating scale, which runs from AAA down to D, with BBB- and above counting as investment grade ([Congressional Research Service](https://d27r7ebsgu0es2.cloudfront.net/reports/IF11916.html)). Fitch introduced the AAA-to-D scale in the 1920s, and it became the industry standard ([U.S. Senate](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/joynt.pdf)).

**Why did Fitch downgrade the United States in 2023?**
On August 1, 2023, Fitch cut the U.S. from AAA to AA+, citing expected fiscal deterioration, a high and growing government debt burden, and "a steady deterioration in standards of governance over the last 20 years," including repeated debt-limit standoffs ([NPR](https://www.whro.org/2023-08-01/the-u-s-loses-its-top-aaa-rating-from-fitch-over-worries-about-the-nations-finances)).

**What is an NRSRO?**
A nationally recognized statistical rating organization is a credit rating agency registered with and overseen by the SEC; Fitch was one of the three agencies first recognized under the designation in 1975 ([U.S. Senate](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/joynt.pdf)). As of March 2024 there were 10 NRSROs, with the Big Three taking 91% of the revenue ([Congressional Research Service](https://d27r7ebsgu0es2.cloudfront.net/reports/IF11916.html)).

**Who owns Fitch Ratings?**
The Hearst Corporation, which bought the final 20% of Fitch Group from Fimalac for $2.8 billion in April 2018, making Fitch its largest wholly-owned business ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/317530/hearst-diversifies-revenue-with-fitch-group-acquis.html?mobile-app=true&theme=false)). Fitch Group also includes Fitch Solutions and Fitch Learning ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/317530/hearst-diversifies-revenue-with-fitch-group-acquis.html?mobile-app=true&theme=false)).
