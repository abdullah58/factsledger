## Overview

Experian is one of the three nationwide consumer reporting agencies in the [United States](/places/united-states/) — the companies whose files decide whether Americans get a mortgage, a car loan or a credit card — and the largest of the three by revenue: a FTSE 100 information-services group headquartered in Dublin, with $7,523 million of revenue in its 2025 financial year ([Experian](https://cdn.prod.nntech.io/company-events/reports/934b9b8b-4b2c-325e-9fdf-1707fdaa7811/report.pdf)). In January 2025 the [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](/organizations/consumer-financial-protection-bureau/) sued its US arm, alleging it ran sham dispute investigations, and a March 2026 ProPublica investigation found the share of consumer complaints Experian resolved in consumers' favour had collapsed from nearly 20% in 2024 to less than 1% in 2025 ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/experian-information-solutions-inc/); [ProPublica](https://qoshe.com/propublica/joel-jacobs/credit-bureaus-are-leaving-more-mistakes-on-frustrated-consumers-reports-under-trump-s-cfpb/188133152)).

That makes Experian the most consequential test case for the American credit-reporting system: a company that sells accuracy to lenders while, according to the regulator, failing to investigate the errors consumers report. It is the only one of the three bureaus listed in London. Its files also carry new kinds of data now — [Bilt](/organizations/bilt/) reports members' on-time rent payments to Experian, [TransUnion](/organizations/transunion/) and [Equifax](/organizations/equifax/) free of charge, and a TransUnion study cited by Bilt found that adding rent payments to a credit file raised scores by nearly 60 points on average ([Bilt](https://newsroom.biltrewards.com/free-rent-reporting-to-renters)). The same files feed Experian's consumer business: more than 200 million people use its free membership tools, and its 2019 Experian Boost lets consumers add positive payment history to their own files — a product that only works if the underlying dispute system works. It also sells verification services that check a borrower's income and employment in real time, a business it entered in 2021 ([Experian](https://www.experianplc.com/about-us/our-history)).

## From Manchester Guardian to a global bureau

Experian's history is a merger of three lineages. Its own account traces the company to 1826, when London merchants began sharing credit information, and to the Manchester Guardian Society; in the US, Jim Chilton began listing merchants' credit risks in Dallas in 1897, while Abraham Rose founded Universal Stores in 1900 — the business that became GUS ([Experian](https://www.experianplc.com/about-us/our-history)). The modern company took shape when TRW, which had entered credit reporting in 1968 by buying Credit Data Corp., divested its credit interests in 1996 into a new company called Experian, which GUS bought and merged with its own CCN bureau ([1library.net](https://1library.net/article/late-s-onwards-consumer-history-of-credit-bureaux.zx83kpwq)). Experian demerged from GUS and listed in London on 10 October 2006 at £5.60 a share, then bought control of Serasa, Brazil's largest bureau, in 2007 ([Experian](https://www.experianplc.com/about-us/our-history)). Brian Cassin became chief executive in 2014, and the company has since added consumer tools such as Experian Boost in 2019 and the Ascend Platform in 2023 ([Experian](https://www.experianplc.com/about-us/our-history)).

## The dispute system on trial

The CFPB's lawsuit, filed on 7 January 2025, goes to the heart of what a bureau is for. Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, when a consumer disputes an item, the bureau must investigate, correct inaccurate information and tell the consumer the outcome ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/experian-information-solutions-inc/)). The Bureau's second amended complaint, filed 22 August 2025, alleges Experian failed to conduct proper reinvestigations, failed to delete inaccurate, incomplete or unverified information, gave inadequate notice of results, and improperly reinserted information it had deleted — and that it relied excessively and unreasonably on the very furnishers whose data was disputed ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/experian-information-solutions-inc/)). The case has moved slowly: the motion to dismiss was denied in October 2025 and discovery was still under way in early 2026 ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/experian-information-solutions-inc/)). Experian resolved nearly 20% of complaints in consumers' favour in 2024, but that fell to less than 1% in 2025 ([ProPublica](https://qoshe.com/propublica/joel-jacobs/credit-bureaus-are-leaving-more-mistakes-on-frustrated-consumers-reports-under-trump-s-cfpb/188133152)) — a collapse that means a disputed error now almost never gets fixed in the consumer's favour.

## Two breaches, one pattern

Experian's record on protecting the data it holds is also contested. In 2012 the US Secret Service alerted Experian Data Corp. to an identity thief posing as a private investigator, retrieving names, Social Security numbers, addresses and phone numbers from Court Ventures Inc., a database company Experian had bought — the thief had started before the purchase and kept going after it, and Experian never told the affected consumers ([Massachusetts Attorney General](https://www.mass.gov/news/ag-healey-secures-16-million-from-multistate-settlements-with-experian-and-t-mobile-over-data-breaches)). Three years later a hacker reached the part of Experian's network holding T-Mobile applicant data, exposing more than 15 million people who had applied for postpaid services or device financing between September 2013 and September 2015 ([Massachusetts Attorney General](https://www.mass.gov/news/ag-healey-secures-16-million-from-multistate-settlements-with-experian-and-t-mobile-over-data-breaches)). A multistate settlement announced in November 2022 required Experian to pay $13.67 million across the two breaches, adopt an incident-response and breach-notification plan, vet third parties, minimise its use of Social Security numbers and give affected consumers five years of free credit monitoring ([Massachusetts Attorney General](https://www.mass.gov/news/ag-healey-secures-16-million-from-multistate-settlements-with-experian-and-t-mobile-over-data-breaches)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Experian?** Experian is a global information-services company and one of the three nationwide consumer reporting agencies in the United States, alongside TransUnion and Equifax ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-orders-transunion-and-equifax-pay-deceiving-consumers-marketing-credit-scores-and-credit-products/)). Lenders use its files and scores to decide who gets credit and on what terms. Its US arm is Experian Information Solutions, Inc.

**Is Experian being sued?** Yes. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau sued Experian Information Solutions, Inc. on 7 January 2025, alleging violations of the Fair Credit Reporting Act in how it handled consumer disputes ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/experian-information-solutions-inc/)). The case was still in discovery in early 2026, and the allegations are unproven.

**How big is Experian?** In the year ended 31 March 2025 it reported statutory revenue of $7,523 million, with 23,300 people across 32 countries and more than 200 million free Consumer Services members ([Experian](https://cdn.prod.nntech.io/company-events/reports/934b9b8b-4b2c-325e-9fdf-1707fdaa7811/report.pdf)). It is listed in London as EXPN and sits in the FTSE 100. It expects total revenue growth of 9–11% in FY26.

**What happened in the 2012 and 2015 breaches?** In 2015 a hacker accessed Experian's network where T-Mobile applicant data was stored, exposing more than 15 million people who applied for postpaid services or device financing between September 2013 and September 2015 ([Massachusetts Attorney General](https://www.mass.gov/news/ag-healey-secures-16-million-from-multistate-settlements-with-experian-and-t-mobile-over-data-breaches)). Names, addresses, birth dates, Social Security numbers and ID numbers were exposed. The 2012 breach involved an identity thief posing as a private investigator.

**Can rent payments appear on my Experian report?** Yes. Bilt reports members' on-time rent payments to Experian, TransUnion and Equifax free of charge, and a TransUnion study cited by Bilt found scores rose by nearly 60 points on average when rent was included ([Bilt](https://newsroom.biltrewards.com/free-rent-reporting-to-renters)). Reporting covers on-time payments only.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not settle the founding year: it dates Experian to its 1996 formation while noting lineage back to 1826, and treats those as different facts rather than one founding date ([Experian](https://www.experianplc.com/about-us/our-history)). It does not present the CFPB's allegations as proven — the lawsuit is pending, discovery was under way in early 2026, and Experian's defences are not covered by a fully-read source ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/experian-information-solutions-inc/)). It does not resolve the T-Mobile settlement figure, because the Massachusetts Attorney General's own release gives both $2.5 million and $2.43 million ([Massachusetts Attorney General](https://www.mass.gov/news/ag-healey-secures-16-million-from-multistate-settlements-with-experian-and-t-mobile-over-data-breaches)). It does not use the "2.7 million complaints without relief" figure that circulates in secondary write-ups of the ProPublica investigation, because that number was not in the portion of the article read for this profile ([ProPublica](https://qoshe.com/propublica/joel-jacobs/credit-bureaus-are-leaving-more-mistakes-on-frustrated-consumers-reports-under-trump-s-cfpb/188133152)). Finally, it does not claim rent reporting guarantees a higher score: the nearly-60-point figure is an average from a TransUnion study cited by Bilt, not a promise to any individual renter ([Bilt](https://newsroom.biltrewards.com/free-rent-reporting-to-renters)). It does not claim Experian Boost raises any individual score — the company's own description is only that it adds positive payment history to a credit file ([Experian](https://www.experianplc.com/about-us/our-history)).
