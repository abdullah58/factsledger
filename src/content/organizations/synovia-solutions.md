## Overview

Synovia Solutions LLC is an Indianapolis company that put GPS tracking on school buses before most districts knew they needed it, and turned that head start into Here Comes the Bus, a parent-facing app that passed one million downloads ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000156459019011567/camp-ex991_29.htm); [Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/73337-local-transportation-tech-firm-acquired-for-50-million)). Founded in 2001 as Synovia, Inc., it grew into a North American market leader in fleet safety and management for K-12 school bus and state and local government fleets, holding about 30% of the K-12 school bus market when [CalAmp](/organizations/calamp/) bought it for about $50 million in 2019 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000156459020021390/R7.htm)). Five years later it filed for Chapter 11 alongside its parent, as part of the restructuring that handed CalAmp to its lender ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000095017024067683/camp-20240522.htm)).

## From Indianapolis GPS pioneer to the parent app

Synovia started in Indianapolis in 2001, putting GPS on school buses at a time when vehicle tracking was still aimed mostly at over-the-road trucks, and built a business selling fleet management to school officials and municipalities ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/keeping-kids-on-buses-safe-from-sickness)). The company in its current form dates to a 2012 merger with Everyday Solutions, a decade after it originated as Synovia, Inc.; partnerships with Verizon Wireless and Blue Bird followed, offering a factory GPS solution ([School Transportation News](https://stnonline.com/news/calamp-acquires-synovia-solutions-to-assist-with-saas-build-out/)). At the time of its acquisition it had about 120 employees in a 12,000-square-foot headquarters off East 96th Street and North Keystone Avenue, and revenue had climbed from $14 million in 2014 to more than $28 million in 2018 ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/73337-local-transportation-tech-firm-acquired-for-50-million)).

The breakthrough was Here Comes the Bus, launched in 2014 as the company's first business-to-consumer product after a decade selling only to school officials ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/keeping-kids-on-buses-safe-from-sickness)). The free app gives parents and students real-time tracking plus email and push notifications, with alerts based on bus location for pick-ups and drop-offs down to the minute ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000156459019011567/camp-ex991_29.htm)). It was powered by CalAmp telematics — Synovia had used CalAmp hardware since 2001, and the app had run on CalAmp telematics since 2003 ([School Transportation News](https://stnonline.com/news/calamp-acquires-synovia-solutions-to-assist-with-saas-build-out/)). By 2019 the app had been downloaded over one million times, served a daily average of 300,000 student and parent users, and won an IHS Markit Innovation Award in mobile computing at the 2019 Consumer Electronics Show ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000156459019011567/camp-ex991_29.htm)). One transportation expert called it "Uber for kids," and almost 300 school districts used it at acquisition, with new districts joining weekly ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/73337-local-transportation-tech-firm-acquired-for-50-million)).

## The $50 million CalAmp acquisition

CalAmp announced the acquisition on April 15, 2019, for approximately $50 million in cash, and its Form 10-K records completion on April 12, 2019, at a price of $49.8 million net of cash on hand ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000156459019011567/camp-ex991_29.htm); [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000156459020021390/R7.htm)). The deal was the centerpiece of CalAmp's pivot to subscription revenue: Synovia posted over $28 million in software-as-a-service recurring revenue in 2018, with double-digit growth and approximately 30% EBITDA, and CalAmp expected the acquisition — combined with its recent purchases of [LoJack](/organizations/lojack/) Mexico and Tracker UK — to lift recurring SaaS revenue past $30 million a quarter ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000156459019011567/camp-ex991_29.htm)). CalAmp said the deal would push recurring revenue from software and services past 30% of consolidated revenue and expand its reach into municipalities ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000156459019011567/camp-ex991_29.htm)).

The fit was unusually close for an acquisition: Synovia had been a CalAmp customer before the deal, and the two companies had worked together for nearly two decades ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000156459020021390/R7.htm)). "CalAmp has been our go-to telematics technology partner over the years," said Jon King, Synovia's president and CEO ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/73337-local-transportation-tech-firm-acquired-for-50-million)). CalAmp also saw product synergies with its LoJack SureDrive connected-car service, CrashBoxx crash alerts, and SCiOn Command supply-chain tools ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000156459019011567/camp-ex991_29.htm)). Synovia's sales and marketing vice president told trade press that customers could expect business as usual and that the Synovia and Here Comes the Bus brands would live on ([School Transportation News](https://stnonline.com/news/calamp-acquires-synovia-solutions-to-assist-with-saas-build-out/)).

## Bus Guardian and the bankruptcy

Synovia's last independently documented chapter was its fastest. When COVID-19 swept the [United States](/places/united-states/) in spring 2020, the Indianapolis maker of bus- and student-tracking software built Bus Guardian — a platform combining student tracking, driver health checks, and bus hygiene and disinfecting checklists — in about eight weeks, launching it in July 2020 ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/keeping-kids-on-buses-safe-from-sickness)). The trigger was a May 2020 call from the School District of Palm Beach County in Florida, a five-year customer, ordering 1,000 tablet-driven bus-monitoring systems for contact tracing ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/keeping-kids-on-buses-safe-from-sickness)). By August 2020 about 250 of Synovia's 1,000-plus customers had adopted Bus Guardian, the company was monitoring 120,000 vehicles nationwide, and it had signed a $1 million contract with a North Carolina school corporation; the product was even featured on ABC's Good Morning America ([Indianapolis Business Journal](https://www.ibj.com/articles/keeping-kids-on-buses-safe-from-sickness)).

The pandemic pivot did not outrun the parent company's balance sheet. On June 3, 2024, Synovia filed a voluntary Chapter 11 petition with CalAmp Corp., CalAmp Wireless Networks Corporation and LoJack Global LLC in the [U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware](/organizations/us-bankruptcy-court-district-of-delaware/), under the prepackaged plan that converted about $229 million of notes held by [Lynrock Lake Master Fund LP](/organizations/lynrock-lake-master-fund/) into equity and took CalAmp private ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000095017024067683/camp-20240522.htm); [School Transportation News](https://stnonline.com/news/calamp-restructures-debt-via-chapter-11-bankruptcy-going-private/)). Trade press at the time reported the filing as business as usual for customers, with the company intending to emerge promptly with a healthy balance sheet ([School Transportation News](https://stnonline.com/news/calamp-restructures-debt-via-chapter-11-bankruptcy-going-private/)). No independent coverage of Synovia's operations after 2020 was found, so its current headcount, headquarters and product status under private CalAmp are not stated here.

## What this profile does not claim

- This profile does not give a single founding year without qualification: Synovia originated in 2001 as Synovia, Inc., and took its current form after a 2012 merger; some trade-release boilerplates give 2012 alone.
- It does not settle the Here Comes the Bus launch year definitively: most sources say 2014, one 2019 trade release says 2015.
- It does not adopt the local paper's "closed last month" wording for the acquisition: CalAmp's Form 10-K records completion on April 12, 2019, and that date is used.
- It does not describe Synovia's post-2020 operations, headcount or headquarters, because no independent source covering that period was found.
- Synovia Solutions LLC is not the same entity as CalAmp Wireless Networks Corporation or LoJack Global LLC; the three were sister subsidiaries and joint Chapter 11 petitioners, not the same company.
