## Overview

Shein is a Singapore-headquartered ultra-fast-fashion retailer that sells cheap, trend-led clothing online in more than 150 countries, built on an on-demand model that tests new styles in tiny batches and reorders only what sells ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/shein-faces-probes-tariffs-it-seeks-hong-kong-ipo-2026-08-04/)). Founded in [China](/places/china/) in 2008 by Chris Xu, it grew into the world's largest online-only fashion seller, reported revenue of $41.85 billion in 2025, and listed in Hong Kong on 1 September 2026 at a valuation of about $26.5 billion — more than 70% below its 2022 private-market peak ([International Finance](https://internationalfinance.com/markets/if-insights-shein-finally-lists-and-hong-kong-marks-it-down/)). It owns no factories: around 5,800 third-party manufacturers, mostly in China, make its clothes ([Reuters](https://www.hl.co.uk/news/shein-gains-uk-approval-for-london-ipo-awaits-china-nod-sources-say)). Shein is also a named customer of [Brex](/organizations/brex/), the fintech whose profile lists it alongside DoorDash and Roblox among 130-plus public companies.

## From Nanjing wedding dresses to a global retailer

Shein's origins are deliberately low-profile, and even its founding year is disputed (see below). The history that best fits the sources starts in Nanjing in October 2008, when Chris Xu — Xu Yangtian, also called Sky Xu — launched a small cross-border e-commerce shop, Nanjing Dianwei Information Technology, with two partners ([Dao Insights](https://daoinsights.com/opinions/shein-chinas-mysterious-billion-dollar-company/)). In 2012 Xu pivoted the business to a wedding-dress retailer called SheInside.com, sourcing from Guangzhou's wholesale garment markets rather than designing anything itself ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/shein)). The turn to a vertically integrated retailer came in 2014, when the company bought rival online retailer ROMWE and built its own supply chain in Guangzhou, later centred on the Panyu district — the factory cluster the BBC would later call "Shein village" ([Dao Insights](https://daoinsights.com/opinions/shein-chinas-mysterious-billion-dollar-company/)). In 2015 the SheInside name was shortened to Shein, and the company raised more than $50 million in financing ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/shein)).

Growth from there was driven less by stores — Shein has effectively none — than by social media. It began working with influencers as early as 2011, when internet celebrities were cheap to partner with, and its "haul" videos on TikTok later made it a fixture of young shoppers' feeds ([Dao Insights](https://daoinsights.com/opinions/shein-chinas-mysterious-billion-dollar-company/)). By 2021 it counted 100 million active customers, and in early 2022 it moved its headquarters from China to Singapore, a step [Reuters](/organizations/reuters/) linked to regulatory, expansion and listing plans ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/shein-faces-probes-tariffs-it-seeks-hong-kong-ipo-2026-08-04/)).

## The on-demand model: test small, reorder fast

Shein's defining idea is to replace forecasting with measurement. Instead of ordering a season's stock in advance, it launches each new style in a tiny test batch — typically 50 to 100 units — watches real customer response, and scales up only the designs that sell ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/shein)). The company describes this itself as a demand-driven model that bases decisions on customer preferences and purchases rather than trend forecasts, run through a digitalised backend connecting the whole production cycle ([SHEIN Group](https://www.sheingroup.com/our-group)). The system, known as large-scale automated test and reorder, turns inventory over in about 47 days by one measure, and in 36 days in 2025 by the prospectus figures cited after the listing ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/shein); [International Finance](https://internationalfinance.com/markets/if-insights-shein-finally-lists-and-hong-kong-marks-it-down/)). Because a failed design costs only a hundred units, Shein can list thousands of new items a day at prices traditional retailers cannot match — a $10 dress and $12 jeans were Reuters' shorthand for the range in 2025 ([Reuters](https://www.hl.co.uk/news/shein-gains-uk-approval-for-london-ipo-awaits-china-nod-sources-say)).

That speed depends on a supplier base willing to take tiny orders and reorder at short notice, concentrated around Guangzhou. In 2022 Shein opened a Centre of Innovation for Garment Manufacturing to push lean production and automation among those suppliers ([SHEIN Group](https://www.sheingroup.com/our-group)). The same year it launched a marketplace for third-party sellers, widening the catalogue beyond its own labels into homeware and electronics ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/shein-faces-probes-tariffs-it-seeks-hong-kong-ipo-2026-08-04/)). The model's weak point is that it was built on parcels shipped individually by air from China, direct to customers — cheap only while duty-free parcel rules lasted.

## Three stock markets, one listing

Shein's path to the public markets took six years and three attempts. It filed confidentially in the [United States](/places/united-states/) in 2023, then abandoned that route after pushback from US lawmakers concerned about alleged labour practices in its supply chain ([International Finance](https://internationalfinance.com/markets/if-insights-shein-finally-lists-and-hong-kong-marks-it-down/)). London came next: in April 2025 Britain's Financial Conduct Authority approved its draft prospectus, but the listing also needed clearance from China's securities regulator, which did not come ([Reuters](https://www.hl.co.uk/news/shein-gains-uk-approval-for-london-ipo-awaits-china-nod-sources-say)). The Chinese regulator objected largely over how the company described risks tied to its Chinese operations and Xinjiang ([International Finance](https://internationalfinance.com/markets/if-insights-shein-finally-lists-and-hong-kong-marks-it-down/)).

Hong Kong was the door that opened. China's securities regulator completed filing procedures on 10 July 2026, and Shein listed on 1 September 2026, selling about 280 million shares at HK$48.56 and raising about US$1.74 billion ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/shein-faces-probes-tariffs-it-seeks-hong-kong-ipo-2026-08-04/); [International Finance](https://internationalfinance.com/markets/if-insights-shein-finally-lists-and-hong-kong-marks-it-down/)). The shares fell as much as 10% in the first session before closing almost flat at HK$48.50, and the deal left the founders in firm control: the shares sold carry one-tenth the voting rights of founder stock, and the four co-founders control 90% of the votes ([International Finance](https://internationalfinance.com/markets/if-insights-shein-finally-lists-and-hong-kong-marks-it-down/)). [Goldman Sachs](/organizations/goldman-sachs/), Morgan Stanley and JPMorgan sponsored the listing ([International Finance](https://internationalfinance.com/markets/if-insights-shein-finally-lists-and-hong-kong-marks-it-down/)).

The price tells the story of the intervening years. In May 2025 the US ended duty-free treatment for low-value Chinese imports, and Shein raised American prices and said the change hurt sales; in July 2026 the [European Union](/organizations/european-union/) imposed a €3 fee on e-commerce parcels under €150, with a parcel of five different items charged €15 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/shein-ipo-faces-lower-valuation-e-commerce-crackdown-starts-bite-2026-07-16/)). Revenue still grew 8% in 2025 to $41.85 billion, but net income fell almost 39% to $2.06 billion, and the first quarter of 2026 swung to a $99 million loss ([International Finance](https://internationalfinance.com/markets/if-insights-shein-finally-lists-and-hong-kong-marks-it-down/)). Shein has responded by adding suppliers in [Brazil](/places/brazil/) and Turkey and expanding warehouse space in Poland ([Reuters](https://www.hl.co.uk/news/shein-gains-uk-approval-for-london-ipo-awaits-china-nod-sources-say); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/shein-ipo-faces-lower-valuation-e-commerce-crackdown-starts-bite-2026-07-16/)).

## Scrutiny: labour, cotton and regulators

No profile of Shein is complete without the scrutiny that has followed it since 2021. In January 2025 the BBC visited 10 of the roughly 5,000 factories in Panyu, Guangzhou — "Shein village" — and found workers sitting at sewing machines for around 75 hours a week, typically from 8am to after 10pm, with one day off a month, against a Chinese legal maximum average of 44 hours a week and a required weekly rest day ([IOSH Magazine](https://www.ioshmagazine.com/2025/01/17/bbc-investigation-raises-concern-over-labour-conditions-garment-factories-supply-chinese)). Shein declined a BBC interview but said it was committed to ensuring fair and dignified treatment for all workers in its supply chain and was investing tens of millions of dollars in governance and compliance ([IOSH Magazine](https://www.ioshmagazine.com/2025/01/17/bbc-investigation-raises-concern-over-labour-conditions-garment-factories-supply-chinese)). Its own 2023 sustainability report had disclosed two cases of child labour in its supply chain in the first nine months of that year, with the suppliers' orders suspended and policies tightened ([IOSH Magazine](https://www.ioshmagazine.com/2025/01/17/bbc-investigation-raises-concern-over-labour-conditions-garment-factories-supply-chinese)).

The forced-labour question centres on cotton. Laboratory testing commissioned by Bloomberg on two occasions in 2022 found that garments shipped to the US by Shein were made with cotton from Xinjiang, the region where the US State Department has described horrific abuses against Uyghur people; Shein's individual parcels fell below the $800 threshold that triggers US Customs reporting, so they escaped the scrutiny applied to bulk imports ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2022-11-21/shein-s-cotton-clothes-tied-to-xinjiang-china-region-accused-of-forced-labor)). Shein says it has a zero-tolerance policy for forced and child labour, and its senior lawyer repeatedly refused in a January 2025 UK parliamentary hearing to say whether Shein sold products containing Xinjiang cotton ([Reuters](https://www.hl.co.uk/news/shein-gains-uk-approval-for-london-ipo-awaits-china-nod-sources-say); [IOSH Magazine](https://www.ioshmagazine.com/2025/01/17/bbc-investigation-raises-concern-over-labour-conditions-garment-factories-supply-chinese)).

Regulators have acted on several fronts at once: the European Commission designated Shein a very large online platform in April 2024 and opened a Digital Services Act investigation in February 2026; France's consumer watchdog fined it €40 million in July 2025 over pricing and environmental claims, and its privacy regulator fined it €150 million that September over cookie consent; Ireland's data regulator opened an inquiry in April 2026; and Shein disclosed in July 2026 that the US Federal Trade Commission was investigating its American operations ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/shein-faces-probes-tariffs-it-seeks-hong-kong-ipo-2026-08-04/)). France will also bar influencers from promoting ultra-fast-fashion brands from January 2027 ([International Finance](https://internationalfinance.com/markets/if-insights-shein-finally-lists-and-hong-kong-marks-it-down/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was Shein founded?**
In Nanjing, China, in October 2008, according to Dao Insights and the company's early history — though Contrary Research says it was formally founded in 2012, and Reuters' timeline dates the launch to 2012. The profile uses 2008 and flags the disagreement ([Dao Insights](https://daoinsights.com/opinions/shein-chinas-mysterious-billion-dollar-company/); [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/shein)).

**Where is Shein headquartered?**
In Singapore, since it moved its headquarters from China in 2022, while its supply chain and most of its roughly 5,800 contract manufacturers remain in China ([Reuters](https://www.hl.co.uk/news/shein-gains-uk-approval-for-london-ipo-awaits-china-nod-sources-say)).

**How does Shein's on-demand model work?**
Each new style starts as a test batch of typically 50 to 100 units; only designs customers actually buy are reordered at scale, which keeps unsold stock low and lets Shein list thousands of new items a day ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/shein)).

**Is Shein a public company?**
Yes, since 1 September 2026, when it listed in Hong Kong at about $26.5 billion after failed attempts in New York and London — more than 70% below its 2022 private valuation of $98.2 billion ([International Finance](https://internationalfinance.com/markets/if-insights-shein-finally-lists-and-hong-kong-marks-it-down/)).

**Why is Shein accused of using forced labour?**
Bloomberg's 2022 laboratory tests found Xinjiang cotton in Shein garments shipped to the US, and the BBC found supplier workers doing around 75-hour weeks. Shein denies using forced labour and says it has a zero-tolerance policy, but its lawyer declined to answer parliamentary questions on Xinjiang cotton ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2022-11-21/shein-s-cotton-clothes-tied-to-xinjiang-china-region-accused-of-forced-labor); [IOSH Magazine](https://www.ioshmagazine.com/2025/01/17/bbc-investigation-raises-concern-over-labour-conditions-garment-factories-supply-chinese)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not state a single settled founding year, because the sources disagree: Dao Insights and the NDIT history give October 2008, Contrary Research says the company was formally founded in 2012, and Reuters' timeline dates the launch to 2012. It does not state the $100 billion 2022 valuation as a precise figure — Reuters' timeline records $98.2 billion pre-money, and the $100 billion number is the rounded figure used in contemporary press. It does not assert that Shein itself uses forced labour: what is verified is that Bloomberg's lab tests found Xinjiang cotton in its garments in 2022 and that its lawyer declined to answer parliamentary questions on the subject; the forced-labour allegation itself remains contested, and Shein's denial is recorded. It does not state post-listing share performance beyond the first days of trading, current employee or supplier counts, or any 2026 revenue beyond the first quarter, none of which was verified from a fully-read source. The reported 2026 FTC settlement was not read in full and is not stated.
