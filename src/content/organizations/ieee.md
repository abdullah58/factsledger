## Overview

IEEE — the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers — is the world's largest technical professional organization: a U.S. public charity advancing technology for the benefit of humanity [IEEE at a Glance](https://www.ieee.org/about/at-a-glance). It was formed in 1963 from the 1884-founded [American Institute of Electrical Engineers](/organizations/aiee/) (AIEE) — whose founders included Thomas Edison and Alexander Graham Bell — and the 1912-founded Institute of Radio Engineers (IRE) [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/computing/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/institute-electrical-and-electronics-engineers-ieee).

The distinctive fact about IEEE is the precision of its role: it is the standards body behind the technologies that carry almost every wired and wireless bit sent today — Ethernet and Wi-Fi are both IEEE 802 standards [IEEE at a Glance](https://www.ieee.org/about/at-a-glance). But the "Wi-Fi" brand, and product interoperability certification, come from a different organization entirely. IEEE writes the standard; the Wi-Fi Alliance certifies the products — a split most coverage gets wrong [Wi-Fi Alliance](http://www.wi-fi.org/node/1958).

## What it is known for

IEEE does four things at global scale: standards development, technical publishing, conferences, and professional membership [IEEE at a Glance](https://www.ieee.org/about/at-a-glance). It publishes roughly a third of the world's electrical-engineering, computing, and electronics literature, and its Xplore Digital Library holds more than 7 million documents [IEEE at a Glance](https://www.ieee.org/about/at-a-glance). It sponsors more than 2,000 conferences and events in 109 countries each year, and it is a leading authority across aerospace, telecommunications, biomedicine, electric power, and consumer electronics [IEEE at a Glance](https://www.ieee.org/about/at-a-glance) [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/computing/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/institute-electrical-and-electronics-engineers-ieee).

## The standards program (802.11 and others)

The IEEE Standards Association (IEEE SA) — the operating unit that develops the standards — calls itself "a leading consensus building organization" developing standards through open, consensus-based processes [IEEE SA](https://standards.ieee.org/about/). As of March 2026 the portfolio held 1,358 active standards with more than 1,242 projects under development [IEEE at a Glance](https://www.ieee.org/about/at-a-glance).

The most prominent are the IEEE 802 standards for local, metropolitan, and other area networks — which include Ethernet and wireless LAN, commonly referred to as Wi-Fi [IEEE at a Glance](https://www.ieee.org/about/at-a-glance). The division of labor is precise: the Alliance (originally the Wireless Ethernet Compatibility Alliance, WECA) was formed in 1999 to certify interoperability of products built on the IEEE 802.11b standard, awarding the first certifications in April 2000 [Wi-Fi Alliance](http://www.wi-fi.org/node/1958). In its words, "the term Wi-Fi is used in place of IEEE 802.11b HR, much like Ethernet is the term used instead of IEEE 802.3" — the friendly names are industry branding for IEEE specifications [Wi-Fi Alliance](http://www.wi-fi.org/node/1958). When 802.11g arrived, the Wi-Fi Alliance — not IEEE — ran certification, awarding interoperability marks to products from 3Com, Cisco, Compaq, and Texas Instruments [EDN](https://www.edn.com/wi-fi-alliance-certifies-eight-802-11g-wlan-products/). Testing is independent, and Alliance membership alone certifies nothing [Wi-Fi Alliance](http://www.wi-fi.org/node/1958).

## Membership and organization

As of March 2026, IEEE reports over 533,000 members in more than 190 countries, with over 69 percent from outside the United States [IEEE at a Glance](https://www.ieee.org/about/at-a-glance). More than 224,000 — roughly two in five — are student members across 3,759 student branches in over 100 countries [IEEE at a Glance](https://www.ieee.org/about/at-a-glance).

It comprises 39 technical Societies and eight Technical Councils, plus 349 geographic Sections and 2,914 technical Chapters across ten regions; the largest society is the Computer Society [IEEE at a Glance](https://www.ieee.org/about/at-a-glance) [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/computing/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/institute-electrical-and-electronics-engineers-ieee). About 1,200 employees support the volunteer-led organization, headquartered at 3 Park Avenue in New York City with the operations center in Piscataway, New Jersey; further offices span California, Washington D.C., India, Singapore, China, Japan, and Austria [IEEE at a Glance](https://www.ieee.org/about/at-a-glance) [IEEE Annual Report](https://2024.ieeeannualreport.org/ieee-office-locations/).

## What IEEE does vs does not do

IEEE **does** develop the technical standards through voluntary consensus among engineers, companies, and institutions [IEEE SA](https://standards.ieee.org/about/). It is a nonprofit public charity, not a government agency or regulator [IEEE at a Glance](https://www.ieee.org/about/at-a-glance).

IEEE **does not**, as a rule, certify products or police compliance. The "Wi-Fi" logo on a router certifies interoperability through the Wi-Fi Alliance, a separate industry body — it is not an IEEE stamp [Wi-Fi Alliance](http://www.wi-fi.org/node/1958) [EDN](https://www.edn.com/wi-fi-alliance-certifies-eight-802-11g-wlan-products/).

And IEEE is **not** the IET. The Institution of Engineering and Technology (IET) is a separate UK-based engineering institution formed in 2006 from the merger of the UK's Institution of Electrical Engineers and the Institution of Incorporated Engineers, headquartered in London [EDN](https://www.edn.com/global-designer-uk-engineering-organizations-to-merge/). They share electrical-engineering heritage and confusingly similar names, but they are distinct organizations.

## Timeline

- **1884** — The AIEE is founded by inventors and entrepreneurs including Thomas Edison and Alexander Graham Bell [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/computing/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/institute-electrical-and-electronics-engineers-ieee).
- **1912** — Radio practitioners form the Institute of Radio Engineers (IRE) [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/computing/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/institute-electrical-and-electronics-engineers-ieee).
- **1963** — The AIEE and IRE merge to form IEEE [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/computing/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/institute-electrical-and-electronics-engineers-ieee).
- **1999** — WECA (later the Wi-Fi Alliance) is formed to certify interoperability of IEEE 802.11b products [Wi-Fi Alliance](http://www.wi-fi.org/node/1958).
- **2000** — WECA awards the first Wi-Fi interoperability certifications [Wi-Fi Alliance](http://www.wi-fi.org/node/1958).
- **2003** — The Wi-Fi Alliance certifies the first products built on the new IEEE 802.11g standard [EDN](https://www.edn.com/wi-fi-alliance-certifies-eight-802-11g-wlan-products/).
- **2006** — The UK's IEE and IIE merge to form the Institution of Engineering and Technology (IET) — separate from IEEE [EDN](https://www.edn.com/global-designer-uk-engineering-organizations-to-merge/).
- **2026** — IEEE reports over 533,000 members in 190+ countries and 1,358 active standards [IEEE at a Glance](https://www.ieee.org/about/at-a-glance).
