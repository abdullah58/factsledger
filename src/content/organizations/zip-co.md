## Overview

Zip Co Ltd (ASX: ZIP) is an Australian financial technology company in the buy-now-pay-later sector, headquartered in Sydney, Australia, offering point-of-sale credit and digital payment services built on interest-free buy-now, pay-later technology ([The Motley Fool Australia](https://www.fool.com.au/tickers/asx-zip/page/93/)). It is one of Australia's established BNPL brands, selling two consumer products — Zip Money and Zip Pay — and led by chief executive Cynthia Scott, who has been in the role since August 2023 ([The Motley Fool Australia](https://www.fool.com.au/tickers/asx-zip/page/93/)). In 2026 the company withdrew from the New Zealand market to concentrate on its two remaining core markets, Australia and the United States ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/07/17/buy-now-pay-later-provider-zip-withdraws-from-nz-market/)). The company's corporate history is unusual: the business was founded in 2013, but its stock exchange listing dates to 2009, arrived at through a reverse takeover ([The Motley Fool Australia](https://www.fool.com.au/tickers/asx-zip/page/93/)).

## The backdoor listing

Zip was founded in 2013 by Larry Diamond and Peter Gray "with a goal to disrupt the existing credit card model" ([The Motley Fool Australia](https://www.fool.com.au/tickers/asx-zip/page/93/)). That founding premise — interest-free instalment credit at the point of sale, positioned as an alternative to credit cards — defines both of its consumer products: Zip Money, the longer-term instalment offering, and Zip Pay, its shorter-cycle product, both delivered through digital payment services ([The Motley Fool Australia](https://www.fool.com.au/tickers/asx-zip/page/93/)).

Yet the company's chronology runs backwards on the ASX. According to The Motley Fool Australia's company data, "Zip was listed on the ASX in 2009 through a reverse takeover" — meaning the listed entity's market presence predates the business itself by four years ([The Motley Fool Australia](https://www.fool.com.au/tickers/asx-zip/page/93/)). A reverse takeover, in which a private company acquires control of an already-listed shell to reach the public market without a conventional IPO, is a backdoor listing route that leaves the ticker's history older than the operating company behind it. Zip's is a particularly clean illustration: the ZIP ticker on the Australian Securities Exchange has been tradeable since 2009, while the buy-now-pay-later business Diamond and Gray built dates only to 2013 ([The Motley Fool Australia](https://www.fool.com.au/tickers/asx-zip/page/93/)). Anyone reading Zip's listed history therefore needs to distinguish the corporate shell's listing date from the company's actual founding.

## Retrenchment

In July 2026 Zip withdrew from the New Zealand market, a move reported by 1News, TVNZ's national news service ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/07/17/buy-now-pay-later-provider-zip-withdraws-from-nz-market/)). The wind-down of New Zealand operations completed in August 2026 ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/07/17/buy-now-pay-later-provider-zip-withdraws-from-nz-market/)). The stated rationale was focus: exiting New Zealand lets the company concentrate its resources on Australia and the United States, the two markets where it intends to compete ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/07/17/buy-now-pay-later-provider-zip-withdraws-from-nz-market/)). The retreat shrinks Zip's geographic footprint from three countries to two and reads as a consolidation play — a BNPL operator narrowing its market count rather than chasing scale everywhere ([1News](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/07/17/buy-now-pay-later-provider-zip-withdraws-from-nz-market/)).
