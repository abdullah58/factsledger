## Overview

**The Rashtriya Janata Dal (RJD) is a centre-left [Indian](/places/india/) political party based in Bihar, founded in July 1997 by Lalu Prasad Yadav, who split from the Janata Dal after being named in the Central Bureau of Investigation's (CBI) fodder-scam chargesheet ([The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/law/explained-fodder-scam-lalu-prasad-yadav); [Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/politics/rashtriya-janata-dal/articleshow/59790850.cms)).** Built on a base of Yadavs and Muslims — the "MY" (Muslim-Yadav) equation — it ruled Bihar from 1997 to 2005, then spent most of the years since in opposition or as a coalition partner, and was the state's single largest party in 2020 with 75 Assembly seats ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/politics/rashtriya-janata-dal/articleshow/59790850.cms); [CNBC-TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/politics/bihar-assembly-election-results-live-updates-nitish-kumar-tejashwi-yadav-modi-jdu-rjd-bjp-7442331.htm)). Its electoral face today is Tejashwi Yadav, Lalu's younger son, and its symbol is the hurricane lamp ([Rediff](https://www.rediff.com/news/special/election-why-rjd-won-only-4-seats/20240610.htm); [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/eci-allots-hurricane-lamp-symbol-to-rjd-for-west-bengal-and-tamil-nadu-assembly-polls-enn26041104626)).

The party's story has two halves that do not cancel each other out: a social-justice mobilisation that brought long-marginalised castes into power, and a corruption scandal that ended in five convictions of its founder ([Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/elections/lalu-prasad-yadav-politics-of-power-and-paradox-in-bihar); [The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/law/explained-fodder-scam-lalu-prasad-yadav)).

## From Janata Dal breakaway to fifteen years in power

Lalu Prasad Yadav's rise began far from the fodder scam. Born in 1948 in Phulwaria village in Bihar, he was General Secretary of the Patna University Students' Union in 1967–69, was mentored by the socialist leader Jayaprakash Narayan, joined the JP Movement against Indira Gandhi's government, was detained during the Emergency until 1977, and was elected to the [Lok Sabha](/organizations/lok-sabha/) from Chhapra on a Janata Party ticket later that year ([Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/elections/lalu-prasad-yadav-politics-of-power-and-paradox-in-bihar)). He became chief minister of Bihar in 1990, and his politics was, in the words of one chronicler quoted by Outlook, "an angry revolt against sahebdom, against the age-old order appointed by the upper-caste haves" ([Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/elections/lalu-prasad-yadav-politics-of-power-and-paradox-in-bihar)).

The break came in 1997. After Lalu was named in the CBI chargesheet, the Janata Dal objected to his holding the chief minister's chair while accused of a scam; under mounting pressure he formed the RJD in July 1997, resigned, and installed his wife Rabri Devi as his replacement — she won the vote of confidence ([The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/law/explained-fodder-scam-lalu-prasad-yadav)). On 25 July 1997, facing imminent arrest, Lalu announced Rabri — described by The Indian Express as a homemaker and political greenhorn with no legislative or leadership experience — as his successor, after a meeting with RJD MLAs at his residence ([The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-politics/bihar-chief-minister-woman-rise-rabri-devi-10357312/)).

RJD stayed in power for eight more years. It performed well in the 2000 Bihar Assembly elections and formed a coalition government with the Congress, with Rabri Devi again chief minister; in 2004 it won 21 Lok Sabha seats in alliance with the Congress, joined the Congress-led United Progressive Alliance (UPA), and Lalu became Minister of Railways ([Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/elections/lalu-prasad-yadav-politics-of-power-and-paradox-in-bihar); [Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/politics/rashtriya-janata-dal/articleshow/59790850.cms)). The Railways stint became famous as a "turnaround" — and then famously contested: the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) later found that a new "statement of cash and investible surplus" had let Lalu project Rs 88,669 crore for 2004–05 to 2008–09 when the net revenue surplus was only Rs 34,506 crore, endorsing his successor Mamata Banerjee's white paper that called it a matter of presentation, not an accounting change ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/Lalus-railways-turnaround-tale-was-a-cosmetic-exercise-/articleshow/9499268.cms)). In February 2005, RJD won only 75 Assembly seats and lost power in Bihar ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/politics/rashtriya-janata-dal/articleshow/59790850.cms)).

The party's national standing faded with its Bihar fortunes: it received recognised national-party status in 2008 after its performance in north-eastern states, and was derecognised as a national party on 30 July 2010 ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/politics/rashtriya-janata-dal/articleshow/59790850.cms)).

## The fodder-scam convictions, case by case

The fodder scam — *chara ghotala* — was the embezzlement of funds meant for cattle feed and supplies from the Animal Husbandry Department of undivided Bihar. The Comptroller and Auditor General had warned about money laundering in Bihar's treasuries as early as 1985; in 1996, deputy commissioner Amit Khare ordered a raid whose seized documents indicated embezzlement under the pretext of supplying fodder ([The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/law/explained-fodder-scam-lalu-prasad-yadav)). The CBI took up 41 cases already registered by the state police and registered 23 more on intelligence reports and complaints — 64 cases investigated in all — after the Patna High Court ordered it in March 1996 to scrutinise excess withdrawals from 1977–78 to 1995–96 ([The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/law/explained-fodder-scam-lalu-prasad-yadav)). Suppliers submitted forged bills for transporting animals in vehicles ranging from scooters to oil tankers; one bill claimed Rs 15 lakh for 500 kg of mustard oil to polish buffaloes' horns ([NDTV](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/fodder-scam-1990s-lalu-yadav-corruption-case-the-fodder-scam-of-the-1990s-when-corruption-brought-down-bihars-strongman-lalu-yadav-9328587)).

Lalu was jailed in December 2017, moved to the Rajendra Institute of Medical Sciences in Ranchi and later to the All India Institute of Medical Sciences in Delhi as his health deteriorated, and walked out of jail in May 2021 after the Jharkhand High Court suspended his remaining term in the Dumka case ([The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/law/explained-fodder-scam-lalu-prasad-yadav)).

## The MY base and its limits: votes without seats

RJD's social base is the clearest explanation of both its durability and its ceiling. The MY equation — Muslims and Yadavs — took shape as Lalu, who had championed social justice and the empowerment of backward and Dalit communities, came over time to be seen as the leader of a narrower base ([Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/elections/rjds-yadav-arithmetic-the-caste-maths-and-politics-behind-tejashwi-yadavs-bihar-strategy)). The loyalty is measurable: in the Centre for the Study of Developing Societies (CSDS) 2020 post-poll survey, 83 per cent of Yadavs voted for the Mahagathbandhan (mainly RJD) and only 5 per cent for the NDA ([Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/elections/rjds-yadav-arithmetic-the-caste-maths-and-politics-behind-tejashwi-yadavs-bihar-strategy)).

But the base has narrowed in two ways. First, Extremely Backward Classes (EBCs) — 36 per cent of Bihar's population in the 2023 caste survey, the single largest bloc at 4.7 crore people — drifted away as Nitish Kumar carved them out as a distinct constituency after 2005, and as many EBC groups felt marginalised within the broader Other Backward Classes bloc ([Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/elections/rjds-yadav-arithmetic-the-caste-maths-and-politics-behind-tejashwi-yadavs-bihar-strategy)). Second, RJD's own ticket choices leaned harder into its core: for the 2025 Assembly elections it gave Yadavs 52 of 143 tickets (36.36 per cent) though Yadavs are 14.26 per cent of the population, while halving EBC candidates from 26 to 11 ([Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/elections/rjds-yadav-arithmetic-the-caste-maths-and-politics-behind-tejashwi-yadavs-bihar-strategy)).

The 2024 Lok Sabha result is the citation-magnet fact of this profile: RJD polled 22.14 per cent of the vote in Bihar — the highest share of any party in the state — yet won only 4 of the 23 seats it contested, which implies that a concentrated base can pile up votes without converting them into seats ([Rediff](https://www.rediff.com/news/special/election-why-rjd-won-only-4-seats/20240610.htm)). Tejashwi had addressed 251 election meetings in severe heatwave conditions and campaigned on employment, jobs, price rise and poverty, while NDA leaders attacked his parents' rule as "jungle raj" ([Rediff](https://www.rediff.com/news/special/election-why-rjd-won-only-4-seats/20240610.htm)). The seat losses had specific local causes too: in Saran, Rohini Acharya lost to the BJP's Rajeev Pratap Rudy by 12,000 votes while two Independent candidates — one Muslim, one Yadav — took more than 36,000 votes between them, upsetting the MY equation ([Rediff](https://www.rediff.com/news/special/election-why-rjd-won-only-4-seats/20240610.htm)). RJD has not won any of Bihar's six reserved seats since 2004 ([Rediff](https://www.rediff.com/news/special/election-why-rjd-won-only-4-seats/20240610.htm)).

The longer arc shows the same pattern. In 2015 the Mahagathbandhan won 178 of 243 Assembly seats, with Nitish Kumar as chief minister and Tejashwi as deputy; in July 2017 the partnership broke and Nitish returned to the NDA ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/politics/rashtriya-janata-dal/articleshow/59790850.cms)). In 2020, RJD emerged as the single largest party with 75 seats under Tejashwi's campaign, but the NDA's 125 seats kept it out of government ([CNBC-TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/politics/bihar-assembly-election-results-live-updates-nitish-kumar-tejashwi-yadav-modi-jdu-rjd-bjp-7442331.htm)). In August 2022 Nitish jumped back to the RJD-helmed Mahagathbandhan and was sworn in for an eighth time with Tejashwi as deputy, on the support of 164 MLAs from seven parties ([The Print](https://theprint.in/india/nitish-kumar-takes-oath-as-bihar-chief-minister-for-8th-time-rjds-tejashwi-yadav-is-deputy/1076942/?amp)). Through all of it, the party's symbol stayed the same: the Election Commission of India treats the hurricane lamp as RJD's reserved symbol in Bihar and Jharkhand, where it is a recognised state party ([ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/eci-allots-hurricane-lamp-symbol-to-rjd-for-west-bengal-and-tamil-nadu-assembly-polls-enn26041104626)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was the RJD founded?**
The RJD was formed in July 1997 by Lalu Prasad Yadav, who split from the Janata Dal after being named in the CBI's fodder-scam chargesheet. He resigned as chief minister and installed his wife Rabri Devi, who won a vote of confidence, while he led the new party.

**What is the fodder scam?**
The fodder scam, or *chara ghotala*, was the embezzlement of funds meant for cattle feed and supplies from Bihar's Animal Husbandry Department, through forged bills and fraudulent treasury withdrawals. Lalu Prasad Yadav was convicted in five cases tied to specific treasuries — Chaibasa, Deoghar, Chaibasa again, Dumka and Doranda — between 2013 and 2022.

**What is the MY equation?**
The MY equation is RJD's traditional Muslim-Yadav social base. It delivered remarkable loyalty — 83 per cent of Yadavs voted for the Mahagathbandhan in 2020 — but critics inside and outside the party say the base narrowed as Extremely Backward Classes drifted away and ticket distribution leaned ever harder into Yadav candidates.

**Why did RJD win only 4 Lok Sabha seats in 2024 despite the highest vote share?**
RJD polled 22.14 per cent in Bihar, the highest of any party, but won only 4 of 23 contested seats. Commentators point to vote concentration, local spoilers — Independents splitting the MY vote in seats like Saran and Siwan — and the party's weakness among non-Yadav OBC, EBC and reserved-seat voters.

**What is RJD's election symbol?**
RJD's reserved symbol is the hurricane lamp, often called the lantern. It is the party's official symbol in Bihar and Jharkhand, where it is a recognised state party, and the Election Commission has allotted it to RJD candidates in other states on request.
