## Overview

Stone Pagamentos is the Brazilian merchant acquirer behind the Stone brand: the licensed operating company that lets small and medium-sized businesses in [Brazil](/places/brazil/) accept card and Pix payments, and the core of Nasdaq-listed StoneCo. Founded in 2012 by André Street and Eduardo Pontes after Brazil's card-acquiring duopoly was broken open, it grew by buying the loss-making Elavon Brazil business and taking full control of online-payments firm [Pagar.me](/organizations/pagar-me/) in 2016, then listing its parent in New York in 2018 ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/stoneco-ipo-prices-above-range-raises-over-1b/)).

The distinction matters because most coverage uses the names interchangeably. Stone Pagamentos S.A. is the operating company: one of three group entities regulated by Brazil's National Monetary Council, alongside MNLT Soluções de Pagamento S.A. — the former Elavon — and Pagar.me Pagamentos S.A. ([SEC Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)). StoneCo Ltd., incorporated in the Cayman Islands with its principal offices in São Paulo, is the listed holding company above it ([SEC Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)). Stone Pagamentos holds the licences that make the business possible: a Central Bank licence as a payment institution in the acquirer category, granted July 3, 2017, and as an electronic-money issuer, granted April 24, 2018 ([SEC Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)).

## Founding: built on the end of the duopoly

André Street and Eduardo Pontes founded the business from inside Arpex Capital, the investment firm where Pontes had been a founding partner since 2011; Pontes then served as chief executive of Stone Pagamentos from its inception until early 2018 ([SEC Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)). Street had already built and sold an online payments company, Braspag, and the pair set out to attack physical retail payments, modelling the service-led approach on Square in the United States ([Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/2022/02/20/backed-by-buffett-they-were-billionaires-by-40-but-now-reeling/)). The timing was regulatory: in July 2010 the Brazilian Central Bank had removed the ban on one acquirer processing both Visa and Mastercard, breaking a duopoly in which Rede handled Mastercard and Cielo handled Visa exclusively ([Electronic Payments International](https://www.electronicpaymentsinternational.com/features/us-acquirers-target-latin-america-5028639/)).

Stone was born in 2012 in a small office on Quitanda Street in Rio de Janeiro, pitching a more human, less bureaucratic service to small and medium-sized businesses ([StoneCo](https://www.stoneco.com.br/en/about-us/)). It gained acquirer licences from Visa and Mastercard in 2013 — scheme licences, distinct from the Central Bank payment-institution licence that followed in 2017 — and began operating in 2014 after two years of structuring ([StoneCo](https://www.stoneco.com.br/en/about-us/)). Distribution, not price alone, was the bet: instead of selling terminals online, Stone opened local hubs — the first in Cabo Frio in 2015 — staffed by its green-uniformed "Green Angels" field team, whose first service request came on February 19, 2016 ([StoneCo](https://www.stoneco.com.br/en/about-us/)). By the 2018 listing the group said it served 200,000 clients and about 5% of the country's acquirer market ([StoneCo](https://www.stoneco.com.br/en/about-us/)).

## The 2016 acquisitions: Elavon and Pagar.me

The year 2016 remade the company in two deals, both recorded in the IPO prospectus: the Elavon do Brasil acquisition, which added a portfolio of small-business and e-commerce merchants, and full control of Pagar.me, which gave Stone its proprietary payment-service-provider platform, plus joint control of the reconciliation firm Equals ([SEC Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)). The two targets could hardly have differed more.

| Deal | Target | What Stone got | Price / terms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| April 2016 | Elavon do Brasil | A licensed acquirer with about 1–2% of the market and a large-merchant book | Reported R$1, with negative equity; Stone recapitalised it |
| August 2016 | Pagar.me (remaining 40%) | Full ownership of the online PSP platform | About R$4.8 million for the remaining 40% |

Elavon do Brasil was a rescue, not a trophy purchase. Citigroup and U.S. Bancorp sold their money-losing joint venture — Citi held 49.9% and U.S. Bancorp's Americas arm 50.1% — to Stone Pagamentos, whose owners were Arpex Capital and Banco Pan ([Electronic Payments International](https://www.electronicpaymentsinternational.com/features/us-acquirers-target-latin-america-5028639/)). Reuters reported the unit ended 2015 with negative equity of BRL 200 million, about $62 million, and regulators pressed for a prompt capital injection, which Stone agreed to provide alongside funds for growth ([Electronic Payments International](https://www.electronicpaymentsinternational.com/features/us-acquirers-target-latin-america-5028639/)). Media reports put the headline price at R$1, with roughly R$300 million of negative equity ([PPRO](https://www.ppro.com/insights/elavon-brazil-acquired-by-stone/)). The scale gap shows why Stone wanted it anyway: Stone processed R$1.4 billion through its terminals in 2015 for some 7,600 customers, while Elavon processed R$16.4 billion, and the combined business was expected to handle R$18 billion ([PPRO](https://www.ppro.com/insights/elavon-brazil-acquired-by-stone/)). StoneCo says the deal made it the fourth-largest player in Brazil ([StoneCo](https://www.stoneco.com.br/en/about-us/)).

Pagar.me was the opposite kind of deal: buying out partners the group already knew. Stone already held 60% of the online-payments firm, and on August 24, 2016 it bought the remaining 40% for about R$4.8 million, taking its holding to 100% ([SEC Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)). The platform, a payment-service-provider solution with a simple API for marketplaces and omni-channel merchants, gave Stone an online business to match its in-store acquiring ([SEC Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)). Its founders, [Henrique Dubugras](/people/henrique-dubugras/) and [Pedro Franceschi](/people/pedro-franceschi/), had started Pagar.me in Brazil in 2013, and after the sale they moved to California and founded [Brex](/organizations/brex/) in 2017 ([Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/english/reports/bloomberg-linea-characters/henrique-dubugras-y-pedro-franceschi/)). Dubugras himself describes Pagar.me as "one of the largest payment processors in Brazil" ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/qa-with-henrique-dubugras-and-pedro-franceschi-cofounders-of-brex)).

## The 2018 IPO, the credit reversal, and scale today

In 2017 the group gathered its companies under one name and officially became StoneCo, with 2,896 people and more than 130,000 clients ([StoneCo](https://www.stoneco.com.br/en/about-us/)). The following year it listed in the [United States](/places/united-states/): 45.8 million Class A shares priced at $24 each, above the $21–$23 range, in a $1.22 billion offering alongside a $100 million private placement by Ant Financial, Alibaba's financial affiliate ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2018/10/29/stoneco-ipo-tops-1-2bn/)). Berkshire Hathaway agreed to buy almost 14.2 million shares for $340 million, and the shares opened at $32 on the Nasdaq and closed at $31.09 on the first Friday ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2018/10/29/stoneco-ipo-tops-1-2bn/)). The total raised is disputed — $1.1 billion on primary shares alone, $1.4 billion with the underwriters' option, $1.5 billion by Reuters' count — which is why this profile states the $1.22 billion primary figure ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/stoneco-ipo-prices-above-range-raises-over-1b/)).

Success then nearly undid the company. Having mastered payments, Stone moved into lending against merchants' future card receivables, using its view of their takings to underwrite loans ([Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/2022/02/20/backed-by-buffett-they-were-billionaires-by-40-but-now-reeling/)). In June 2021 Brazil switched to a new receivables-registration framework, but the migration was incomplete, settlement priorities were inconsistently applied, and the registries' interoperability was still not working properly, so guarantees leaked and merchants could bypass their collateral by switching acquirers ([SEC teach-in](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000095010321012852/dp156738_ex9901.htm)). Stone temporarily stopped disbursing credit at the beginning of June 2021, tested the system with R$0.8 million in July, and said resumption could take three to six months ([SEC teach-in](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000095010321012852/dp156738_ex9901.htm)). Delinquencies soared, lending halted, and the shares fell 88% over the following year, erasing $25.6 billion of market value ([Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/2022/02/20/backed-by-buffett-they-were-billionaires-by-40-but-now-reeling/)).

The rebuild was built on the original business. By 2024 the group reported total payment volume of R$516 billion, up 17.8% year over year, with micro, small and medium-business volume of R$454 billion, adjusted net income of R$2.2 billion, retail deposits of R$8.7 billion and a relaunched credit portfolio of R$1.2 billion ([StoneCo 4Q24 earnings release](https://www.companiesmarketcap.com/quarterly-reports/1077.qr.en.2024-q4.pdf)). Those are StoneCo group figures, not Stone Pagamentos alone — the operating acquirer's own revenue is not separately disclosed — but they measure the scale of the platform Stone Pagamentos anchors, which reached 4 million clients in the third quarter of 2024 ([StoneCo](https://www.stoneco.com.br/en/about-us/)). The same release argues that Pix, Brazil's instant-payment system, mainly displaces debit transactions rather than credit, which is why rapid Pix adoption did not derail card volumes ([StoneCo 4Q24 earnings release](https://www.companiesmarketcap.com/quarterly-reports/1077.qr.en.2024-q4.pdf)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Stone Pagamentos?**
It is Stone Pagamentos S.A., the Brazilian licensed merchant acquirer behind the Stone brand and the core operating company of Nasdaq-listed StoneCo, serving small and medium-sized businesses in Brazil. It holds a Central Bank licence as a payment institution in the acquirer category, granted in July 2017, and as an electronic-money issuer from April 2018 ([SEC Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)).

**How is Stone Pagamentos different from StoneCo?**
Stone Pagamentos is the operating acquirer, licensed by Brazil's Central Bank in 2017; StoneCo Ltd. is the Cayman Islands-incorporated holding company that owns it and lists on the Nasdaq under the ticker STNE. Stone Pagamentos is the Brazilian company that actually processes merchants' payments, while StoneCo is the listed parent above it ([SEC Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)).

**Who founded Stone Pagamentos?**
André Street and Eduardo Pontes, who built it from inside the investment firm Arpex Capital; Pontes was chief executive of Stone Pagamentos from its inception until early 2018. Pontes had been a founding partner of Arpex Capital since 2011, and Street founded StoneCo, the holding company, while at Arpex ([SEC Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)).

**What did Stone pay for Elavon Brazil and Pagar.me?**
Media reports put the Elavon price at R$1, with Stone agreeing to recapitalise a unit carrying negative equity, while the remaining 40% of Pagar.me cost about R$4.8 million in August 2016. For Elavon, Stone also committed to replenish the unit's capital and provide funds for growth, after regulators pressed for a prompt capital injection ([PPRO](https://www.ppro.com/insights/elavon-brazil-acquired-by-stone/); [SEC Form F-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000119312518289511/d580263df1.htm)).

**Why did Stone stop lending in 2021?**
Brazil's new card-receivables registry, launched June 7, 2021, malfunctioned — migration was incomplete and guarantees leaked — so Stone paused credit disbursements in June 2021 and waited for the system to work properly. Migration of old loan agreements was incomplete, settlement priorities were inconsistently applied, and the registries could not yet interoperate properly ([SEC teach-in](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1745431/000095010321012852/dp156738_ex9901.htm)).

It does not state a single settled figure for Elavon do Brasil's negative equity, because Reuters reported BRL 200 million while PPRO cited media reports of about R$300 million.
