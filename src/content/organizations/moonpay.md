## Overview

MoonPay is a Miami-based crypto payments company that became the emblem of the 2021 NFT boom's commercial machinery: its on-ramp let anyone buy cryptocurrency — and, through its NFT "concierge," Bored Ape Yacht Club NFTs — with a credit card instead of first owning crypto ([CNBC TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/cryptocurrency/explained-what-is-moonpay-and-why-are-so-many-celebrities-rallying-behind-it-13159252.htm)). Launched in 2019 under co-founder and CEO Ivan Soto-Wright, it raised $555 million at a $3.4 billion valuation in November 2021 — but $150 million of that round quietly went to insiders cashing out shares, The Information reported, including Soto-Wright himself ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2023-05-30-moonpay-insiders-pocket-150-million-the-information-232592)). Since 2025 the company has been rebuilding itself as something different: a regulated institutional payments network for stablecoins and tokenized assets, via an acquisition spree and hard-won New York licenses ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/acquiring/2026/moonpay-boosts-tokenization-business-with-north-capital-deal/); [Brave New Coin](https://bravenewcoin.com/insights/moonpay-secures-new-york-trust-charter-joins-elite-crypto-companies-with-dual-licenses)).

## The "[PayPal](/organizations/paypal/) for crypto" years

MoonPay's pitch was that it offered a "gateway" to digital assets: fiat in, crypto out, usable by debit card, credit card, Apple Pay, Google Pay, bank transfer. People were calling it "PayPal, but for crypto," and Soto-Wright leaned into the comparison while insisting on the difference — a future where "it will be frictionless to move any amount of value around anywhere in the world, and costs move as close as possible to zero" ([Bankless Times](https://www.banklesstimes.com/news/2023/01/31/moonpay-hits-3-4-billion-valuation-and-bitcoin-fever-continues-to-surge/)). The firm was profitable from its 2019 launch, Bankless Times reported, and by late 2021 was targeting $150 million in annual revenue after transaction volumes grew 30-fold in a year, with more than 7 million customers ([Bankless Times](https://www.banklesstimes.com/news/2023/01/31/moonpay-hits-3-4-billion-valuation-and-bitcoin-fever-continues-to-surge/)).

The November 2021 Series A — led by Tiger Global and Coatue at $3.4 billion — was the company's first-ever financing round ([Bankless Times](https://www.banklesstimes.com/news/2023/01/31/moonpay-hits-3-4-billion-valuation-and-bitcoin-fever-continues-to-surge/); [CNBC TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/cryptocurrency/explained-what-is-moonpay-and-why-are-so-many-celebrities-rallying-behind-it-13159252.htm)). What was not disclosed at the time: of the $555 million headline figure, only $405 million came from selling new shares to investors; the remaining $150 million represented shares cashed out by insiders in a secondary transaction, The Information reported in 2023, citing an unnamed source — and MoonPay confirmed the round had included secondary sales ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2023-05-30-moonpay-insiders-pocket-150-million-the-information-232592)). A few weeks after the round, Soto-Wright purchased a $38 million Miami mansion, later used to host parties during Art Basel ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2023-05-30-moonpay-insiders-pocket-150-million-the-information-232592)).

## The celebrity round and the NFT concierge

MoonPay's cultural moment came through its NFT business. Its concierge service removed the hardest step of NFT buying — owning crypto first: the customer named a budget, MoonPay bought the required crypto, purchased the NFT, and invoiced the customer. "We've tried to make it as similar as a process as you would be interacting with your private bank," Soto-Wright said ([CNBC TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/cryptocurrency/explained-what-is-moonpay-and-why-are-so-many-celebrities-rallying-behind-it-13159252.htm)). That convenience made it the default checkout for the Bored Ape Yacht Club wave — Post Malone bought two BAYC NFTs through the platform and featured MoonPay in the "One Right Now" music video — and celebrities who had used it then invested in it: an $87 million round from 60 celebrity investors including Justin Bieber, Snoop Dogg, Gwyneth Paltrow, Post Malone, and Ashton Kutcher ([CNBC TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/cryptocurrency/explained-what-is-moonpay-and-why-are-so-many-celebrities-rallying-behind-it-13159252.htm)). Soto-Wright said the outreach had "virtually no rejections. Everyone wanted to be a part of it" ([CNBC TV18](https://www.cnbctv18.com/cryptocurrency/explained-what-is-moonpay-and-why-are-so-many-celebrities-rallying-behind-it-13159252.htm)).

## The 2025 pivot: from on-ramp to institutional rails

The consumer on-ramp company of 2021 looks very different from the company of 2025–2026. In January 2025 MoonPay acquired Helio, a Solana-based crypto payment processor, for $175 million; in March it bought Iron, an API-first stablecoin infrastructure platform, for a reported $100 million-plus; and in September it added Meso to strengthen US banking connections — "the critical components needed to create a single, global payments network," as the company put it ([Brave New Coin](https://bravenewcoin.com/insights/moonpay-secures-new-york-trust-charter-joins-elite-crypto-companies-with-dual-licenses); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/acquiring/2026/moonpay-boosts-tokenization-business-with-north-capital-deal/)).

The regulatory track ran in parallel. On 4 June 2025 MoonPay secured its BitLicense from the New York Department of Financial Services, completing US coverage across all 50 states; on 25 November 2025 NYDFS granted it a Limited Purpose Trust Charter, enabling regulated digital-asset custody and over-the-counter trading ([Brave New Coin](https://bravenewcoin.com/insights/moonpay-secures-new-york-trust-charter-joins-elite-crypto-companies-with-dual-licenses)). With both licenses, MoonPay joined an exclusive group of five — Coinbase, PayPal, Ripple, and NYDIG being the others — holding dual New York approval ([Brave New Coin](https://bravenewcoin.com/insights/moonpay-secures-new-york-trust-charter-joins-elite-crypto-companies-with-dual-licenses)). The company had also become one of the first crypto firms approved under the EU's MiCA regulation in December 2024 ([Brave New Coin](https://bravenewcoin.com/insights/moonpay-secures-new-york-trust-charter-joins-elite-crypto-companies-with-dual-licenses)).

The spree continued into 2026 — Sodot in April, DFlow and Dawn Labs in May, Entendre in June, Glide in July, Rhythm in September — and in September 2026 MoonPay moved on North Capital, a private-markets infrastructure platform whose SEC-registered broker-dealers, alternative trading system, transfer agent, and investment adviser would become MoonPay subsidiaries: the regulatory foundation, in Soto-Wright's words, "to support mass adoption of tokenized real world assets" ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/acquiring/2026/moonpay-boosts-tokenization-business-with-north-capital-deal/)).

## What this profile does not claim

The $150 million insider secondary rests on The Information's unnamed-source reporting as relayed by The Block, with only the company's confirmation that secondary sales occurred — it is presented with that attribution, not as independently audited fact. Founding-year sources disagree (2018 vs 2019 launch); this profile uses 2019 per CNBC TV18. Co-founders beyond Ivan Soto-Wright were not named in the sources read in full and are not asserted. User, volume, and profitability figures cited from press reports are company statements, not independently verified. Reported-but-unread items — the 2025 debt rounds, the 2025 layoffs, the Iron deal's exact price — are deliberately omitted.
