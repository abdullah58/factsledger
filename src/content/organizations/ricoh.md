## Overview

Ricoh Company, Ltd. is a Japanese multinational imaging and electronics company founded in Tokyo, [Japan](/places/japan/), in 1936 as a maker of sensitized photographic paper ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/about/history/1936_1969)). It grew into one of the world's largest makers of copiers and multifunction printers, owns the Pentax camera brand, and in the fiscal year ended March 2025 reported sales of ¥2,527.8 billion with 78,665 employees ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/about/integrated-report/2025/value_creation_1)).

Ricoh began on 6 February 1936, when the sensitized paper division of Rikagaku Kogyo split off as Riken Kankoshi Co., Ltd., under the directorship of founder Kiyoshi Ichimura, with ¥350,000 in capital and a staff of 33 ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/about/history/1936_1969)). Its roots lay in the Institute of Physical and Chemical Research (Riken), which had set up Rikagaku Kogyo in 1927 to commercialise its research ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/about/history/1936_1969)). In 1946 Ichimura formulated the company's Founding Principles, the Spirit of Three Loves — "Love your neighbor", "Love your country", "Love your work" — which Ricoh still cites as its corporate philosophy ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/about/history/1936_1969); [Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/about/integrated-report/2025/value_creation_1)).

## From sensitized paper to the office: 1936–2000

Ricoh's first business was photography. It began making cameras in 1937, renamed itself Riken Optical Co., Ltd. in March 1938, and launched the Ricohflex III in March 1950, a camera that helped popularise photography in Japan ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/about/history/1936_1969)). The company entered the business machine field in November 1955 with its first diazo copier, the Ricopy 101, which became so common in Japanese offices that "make a Ricopy" came to mean "make a copy" ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/about/history/1936_1969)). Its first electrostatic copier, the Ricopy BS-1, followed in September 1965 and was the world's first desktop copier with a fixed-platen structure ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/about/history/1936_1969)).

The company went global early. It founded its first overseas subsidiary, Ricoh Industries, U.S.A., Inc., in December 1962, and changed its name to Ricoh Company, Ltd. in April 1963 after passing ¥10 billion in sales ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/about/history/1936_1969)). In 1973 its Irvine, California subsidiary made Ricoh the first Japanese company to produce copiers in the United States ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences-and-law/economics-business-and-labor/businesses-and-occupations/ricoh-company-ltd)). The 1970s added the Rifax 600S in 1974, described as the world's first high-speed digital facsimile machine, and Ricoh's first plain-paper copier in 1975, the year it won Japan's Deming Prize for quality control ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences-and-law/economics-business-and-labor/businesses-and-occupations/ricoh-company-ltd)).

In the United States, Ricoh at first sold copiers under the Savin and Pitney Bowes labels, then began marketing under its own name in 1981 and held a 7% share of the U.S. market by 1984 ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences-and-law/economics-business-and-labor/businesses-and-occupations/ricoh-company-ltd)). Moving into the $6,000–$13,000 copier range put it in direct competition with [Xerox](/organizations/xerox/) ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences-and-law/economics-business-and-labor/businesses-and-occupations/ricoh-company-ltd)). Around 2000, reference works called Ricoh the world's number one maker of digital copiers, with more than 60% of its revenues coming from copiers and related supplies ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences-and-law/economics-business-and-labor/businesses-and-occupations/ricoh-company-ltd)).

Ricoh also had a hidden role in the living room: the original Nintendo Entertainment System was powered by a custom MOS 6502 sold as the Ricoh 2A03, manufactured by Ricoh with a built-in 5-voice programmable sound generator, and Ricoh also supplied the 2C02 picture processing unit ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/trivia/2-original-nintendo-entertainment-system-8-bit-processor-manufactured/)).

## Acquisitions and the turn to digital services

Ricoh advocated the Office Automation concept in 1977 and built its business on copiers and multifunction printers, but since 2020 it has been driving a structural transformation into a digital services company ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/about/integrated-report/2025/value_creation_1)). The shift shows in the numbers: in the fiscal year ended 31 March 2025, Digital Services contributed ¥1,930.1 billion — 76.4% of Ricoh's ¥2,527.8 billion in sales — while Digital Products contributed only 6.2% ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/about/integrated-report/2025/value_creation_1)). Ricoh cites IDC data ranking it second, with a 17% share, in the global market for A3 multifunction printers ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/about/integrated-report/2025/value_creation_1)).

Acquisitions drove much of that transformation. On 31 October 2008 Ricoh completed its $1.632 billion purchase of IKON Office Solutions ($17.25 per share), a Malvern, Pennsylvania distributor with about 24,000 employees and more than 400 locations in North America and Western Europe ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/-/Media/Ricoh/Sites/com/info/pdf/081101.pdf)). In July 2011 it agreed to buy Hoya's PENTAX Imaging Systems Business for about ¥10 billion (around $124 million), closing the deal on 1 October 2011, while Hoya kept its endoscope and other businesses ([Imaging Resource](https://www.imaging-resource.com/news/ricoh-acquires-pentax-digital-camera-business/)). Ricoh had been one of the first companies to produce digital cameras, in 1995, and saw Pentax's interchangeable-lens technology and sales channels as the way to strengthen its consumer camera business ([Imaging Resource](https://www.imaging-resource.com/news/ricoh-acquires-pentax-digital-camera-business/)).

The PFU deal brought Ricoh the global leader in document scanners: it bought 80% of [Fujitsu](/organizations/fujitsu/)'s PFU subsidiary in 2022 and the remaining 20% in March 2025, making PFU wholly owned ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/-/Media/Ricoh/Sites/com/release/2022/pdf/0428_1.pdf); [Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.Ricoh.com/-/Media/Ricoh/Sites/com/release/2025/pdf/0304_1.pdf)). And in July 2024 Ricoh and Toshiba Tec formed ETRIA Co., Ltd., a joint venture integrating their multifunction printer development and manufacturing, with OKI joining in 2025 and bringing its LED printhead technology ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/-/media/Ricoh/Sites/com/release/2025/pdf/0213_1.pdf?rev=4f68fa595a80473786d176cc06dfe58b&sc_lang=ja-JP)). After OKI's entry the shareholdings stood at Ricoh 80.74%, Toshiba Tec 14.25% and OKI 5.01% ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/-/media/Ricoh/Sites/com/release/2025/pdf/0213_1.pdf?rev=4f68fa595a80473786d176cc06dfe58b&sc_lang=ja-JP)).

## The JPEG patent fight — and an accounting scandal in India

Ricoh's least-known brush with history came from a patent it never owned. U.S. Patent 4,698,672, covering a "coding system for reducing redundancy", was filed by [Compression Labs](/organizations/compression-labs/) in October 1986 and acquired by Forgent Networks in 1997 ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2004/04/23/forgent_jpeg_suit/)). In June 2002 Forgent announced it held the "sole and exclusive right" to license the patent's JPEG claims, and persuaded [Sony](/organizations/sony/) to pay a one-time fee of $15 million — news alarming enough that the [ISO](/organizations/iso/) threatened to withdraw its standards blessing from JPEG, which it can only endorse royalty-free ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2004/04/23/forgent_jpeg_suit/)).

On 22 April 2004 Forgent's Compression Labs subsidiary sued 31 companies for patent infringement, and Ricoh Corp. was among the 30 companies named in the announcement ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/171812/forgent.html)). The defendant list spanned [Apple](/organizations/apple/), [Adobe](/organizations/adobe/), [IBM](/organizations/ibm/), [Dell](/organizations/dell/), [HP](/organizations/hp/), Eastman [Kodak](/organizations/kodak/), [Fuji Photo Film](/organizations/fujifilm/), Canon USA, [Toshiba](/organizations/toshiba/) and Xerox, among others ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/171812/forgent.html)). Forgent said it had already collected about $90 million in licensing revenue from the patent ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2004/04/23/forgent_jpeg_suit/)). The campaign's legal foundation crumbled in May 2006, when the [U.S. Patent and Trademark Office](/organizations/us-patent-and-trademark-office/) rejected 19 of the patent's 47 claims on reexamination requested by the [Public Patent Foundation](/organizations/public-patent-foundation/), overturning its broadest claims ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/180138/jpeg.html)). By then Forgent said it had generated about $105 million in licensing revenue ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/180138/jpeg.html)). The suit was brought by Forgent Networks, the Austin firm later renamed [Asure Software](/organizations/asure-software/).

A decade later, Ricoh faced a scandal of its own making in India. On 19 July 2016 Ricoh India admitted that its accounts appeared to have been "falsified" and its accounting principles violated, with an unaudited estimated loss of ₹1,123 crore for the fiscal year ended 31 March 2016 ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/accounts-falsified-admits-ricoh-india/article8871555.ece/amp/)). The disclosure followed an internal investigation commissioned after auditor PwC's preliminary findings that the financial statements for April to September 2015 "did not reflect a true and fair view of the state of affairs of the company" ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/accounts-falsified-admits-ricoh-india/article8871555.ece/amp/)). Managing director and CEO Manoj Kumar had resigned from the board in April 2016 after being asked to go on leave amid the audit, and market regulator SEBI expanded its probe to include possible fund diversions ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/accounts-falsified-admits-ricoh-india/article8871555.ece/amp/)). The suspected wrongdoing was described as potentially the biggest corporate fraud in India since the Satyam scandal of 2009 ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/accounts-falsified-admits-ricoh-india/article8871555.ece/amp/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What does Ricoh make today?**
Ricoh is a digital-services company built on office printing: multifunction printers, production printing, document scanners and IT services. In the fiscal year ended 31 March 2025, Digital Services contributed ¥1,930.1 billion — 76.4% of its ¥2,527.8 billion in sales ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/about/integrated-report/2025/value_creation_1)).

**When was Ricoh founded, and by whom?**
Ricoh was founded on 6 February 1936 as Riken Kankoshi Co., Ltd., when the sensitized paper division of Rikagaku Kogyo split off under founder Kiyoshi Ichimura, starting with ¥350,000 in capital and 33 staff ([Ricoh Company, Ltd.](https://www.ricoh.com/about/history/1936_1969)).

**Does Ricoh own Pentax?**
Yes. Ricoh bought Hoya's PENTAX Imaging Systems Business for about ¥10 billion in 2011, closing the deal on 1 October 2011, and the Pentax brand continues under Ricoh's camera business ([Imaging Resource](https://www.imaging-resource.com/news/ricoh-acquires-pentax-digital-camera-business/)).

**Was Ricoh sued over the JPEG patent?**
Yes. In April 2004 Forgent Networks sued 31 companies, Ricoh among the 30 named, alleging its U.S. Patent 4,698,672 covered JPEG compression ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/171812/forgent.html)). In May 2006 the U.S. Patent Office rejected the patent's broadest claims on reexamination ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/180138/jpeg.html)).

**What happened at Ricoh India in 2016?**
Ricoh India admitted its accounts appeared to have been falsified, estimating a loss of ₹1,123 crore for the year ended 31 March 2016, after PwC found its financial statements did not reflect a true and fair view ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/companies/accounts-falsified-admits-ricoh-india/article8871555.ece/amp/)).
