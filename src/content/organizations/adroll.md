## Overview

AdRoll is the [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) adtech company that has spent nearly two decades — and two corporate renames — trying to escape the box it built for itself. Founded in 2007 by [Aaron Bell](/people/aaron-bell/) and [Adam Berke](/people/adam-berke/) as a display advertising network, it pivoted into retargeting as ad exchanges rose, became synonymous with the category, then outgrew it: in 2019 the parent company renamed itself NextRoll, and in 2025 it reversed course and reunified everything under the AdRoll brand ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/online-advertising/adroll-retargeting-firm/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/09/10/adroll-nextroll/100); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/adroll-unifies-brand-with-ai-assistant-launch-eliminating-rollworks-complexity/)). It was not, at any point, a merger with a separate company — it is one company that renamed itself twice, six years apart.

## An ad network that survived 2008

AdRoll "began its life in 2007 as an ordinary ad network," as [AdExchanger](/organizations/adexchanger/) put it, founded by CEO Aaron Bell and President Adam Berke with a mission to make display advertising work for everyone — the way Google had scaled AdWords for advertisers large and small ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/online-advertising/adroll-retargeting-firm/)). The timing was brutal: AdRoll closed its Series A in August 2008, weeks before [Lehman Brothers](/organizations/lehman-brothers/) crashed. Rather than expanding sales into the downturn, the founders focused on product, asking their few customers what they would spend money on in a bad economy. The answer was always ROI — and retargeting, then used only by the largest brands, turned out to be the way to deliver it ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/online-advertising/adroll-retargeting-firm/)).

The rise of ad exchanges around 2008–2009 was the unlock: AdRoll became one of the first buyers on Google's AdEx, built a self-service SaaS platform for retargeting, and grew fast — 5,000 customers adding 500 a month, 85 employees in San Francisco, profitable since 2011 with north of 400% growth, backed by [Accel](/organizations/accel/) and angel investor Peter Thiel ([AdExchanger](https://www.adexchanger.com/online-advertising/adroll-retargeting-firm/); [Search Engine Journal](http://www.searchenginejournal.com/ad-retargeter-adroll-cookies-are-the-new-email/32616/)). In 2015 it extended the model into email with SendRoll, bringing retargeting to what [VentureBeat](/organizations/venturebeat/) called the most lucrative channel in digital marketing ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/adroll-launches-sendroll-an-email-retargeting-product-with-huge-potential)). By then the company's identity was firmly "the retargeting company" — the pigeonhole its CEO would later complain about ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/09/10/adroll-nextroll/100)).

## Two renames: NextRoll, then back to AdRoll

The first rename came in September 2019. The AdRoll Group became NextRoll Inc., a change CEO [Toby Gabriner](/people/toby-gabriner/) — who had taken the role in November 2017 — said was designed to reflect the company's moves beyond retargeting: "We have, for the longest time, been pigeonholed as a retargeting company, but the reality is that we have really been evolving" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/09/10/adroll-nextroll/100)). To be clear, the AdRoll retargeting business did not go away — it continued as one business unit. A second, [RollWorks](/organizations/rollworks/), offered B2B account-based-marketing tools (launched about 18 months earlier), and a third, NextRoll Platform Services, launched as a "marketing-technology-as-a-service" offering, selling access to AdRoll's underlying technology through APIs — Channels-as-a-Service and Audiences-as-a-Service ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/09/10/adroll-nextroll/100)).

The second rename came on August 27, 2025 — a reversal. The company announced a "brand transformation" unifying the portfolio under the AdRoll brand: RollWorks officially became the AdRoll ABM product, and the platform was repositioned as an AI-powered connected advertising system with two core offerings — multi-channel advertising and ABM — plus a newly launched AI Assistant for campaign creation and optimization ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/adroll-unifies-brand-with-ai-assistant-launch-eliminating-rollworks-complexity/)). The NextRoll name was effectively retired as a brand — PPC Land's own summary reads "AdRoll (formerly NextRoll)" ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/adroll-unifies-brand-with-ai-assistant-launch-eliminating-rollworks-complexity/)). By then leadership had also changed: the 2025 announcement quotes [Roli Saxena](/people/roli-saxena/) as chief executive officer at NextRoll and [Vibhor Kapoor](/people/vibhor-kapoor/) as chief business officer at AdRoll, where the 2019 era had Gabriner ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/adroll-unifies-brand-with-ai-assistant-launch-eliminating-rollworks-complexity/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/09/10/adroll-nextroll/100)). The company pitches the unified platform at mid-sized businesses, drawing on what it describes as nearly 20 years of advertising data ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/adroll-unifies-brand-with-ai-assistant-launch-eliminating-rollworks-complexity/)).
