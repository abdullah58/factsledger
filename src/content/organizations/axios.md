## The newsroom funded by the company it covers

The most revealing thing about Axios right now is not its newsletters, its
owners, or its much-imitated terse style — it is a deal that has the company
taking money and AI tools from OpenAI, one of the most aggressively covered
targets of its own newsroom, and then hiring reporters into OpenAI-funded jobs
without telling them in the job listings. The [[Columbia Journalism Review](/organizations/columbia-journalism-review/)](https://factsledger.com/organizations/columbia-journalism-review/) laid
out the arrangement in detail in August 2026: under a three-year deal, OpenAI
bankrolls the startup of thirteen Axios Local newsletters and hands staff free
credits for enterprise AI tools, while Axios lets OpenAI train its models on
Axios's free, published coverage
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).
No standard reference profile of the company leads with this. They all stop at
"Smart Brevity" and the Cox acquisition. The money trail is the actual story.

Jim VandeHei announced the partnership himself in January 2025 with a
characteristically short post: for three years, OpenAI would fund four starter
cities — Pittsburgh; Kansas City, Missouri; Boulder, Colorado; and Huntsville,
Alabama — all of them Axios's picks
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).
In 2026 the two companies added nine more newsletters, pushing toward
forty-three cities by the end of the year, with VandeHei telling CJR he would
like to reach a thousand someday
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).

Neither Axios nor OpenAI would disclose how much money is changing hands, or
whether the deal stops Axios from suing OpenAI — those terms are confidential,
an OpenAI spokesperson said
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).
What is public is the disclosure gap: as of early August 2026, Axios's open
local job listings advertised salaries from $65,000 to $125,000 but said
nothing about the OpenAI funding, and most Axios coverage of OpenAI carries no
disclosure either, unless the story is explicitly about media deals
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).
A staffer who spoke anonymously told CJR the opacity has fed unease among
rank-and-file reporters
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).

## The conflict question VandeHei waves off

VandeHei does not sound like a man who loses sleep over the optics. CJR found
the OpenAI deal "silent" on the editorial firewall — not because independence
is unimportant, he said, but because both sides took Axios's editorial
independence as a given: "There's an assumption that we're gonna write what
we write"
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).
And Axios has, in fact, kept covering OpenAI hard — CJR notes its reporting on
allegations tying [ChatGPT](https://factsledger.com/organizations/chatgpt/) to teen suicides and every beat of OpenAI's court
battle with Elon Musk
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).
WebProNews, summarizing the expanded deal in 2026, framed the tension the same
way: Axios covers OpenAI and the AI industry aggressively while benefiting
directly from OpenAI's money and technology
([WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/axios-ceo-bets-ai-will-rescue-local-news-critics-call-it-a-risky-trade/)).

Critics see a devil's bargain. Matt Pearce, policy director for the Rebuild
Local News coalition, told CJR that striking individual deals with AI companies
is often the rational move for a single newsroom — but it creates a collective
action problem: "If you strike a direct partnership with an AI company, that
benefits you but no one else"
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).
VandeHei's answer is blunt economics: "At the end of the day, if someone's
going to pay you for content, you say: Well, how much are you going to pay
me? If that payment feels like it's a fair transaction price, you say yes"
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).
As CJR reported, the deal was a financial transaction first — a way to test
whether AI-assisted local newsrooms can be economically viable at all.

## The AI-lab-rat CEO

The partnership fits a personal obsession. VandeHei calls himself an "AI lab
rat," talks to Claude on long hikes while training to climb Mount Kilimanjaro,
and jumps out of bed at 3 a.m. excited to test new models
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).
He has said anyone who sees AI as overhyped is "on drugs or just not using it
correctly," and declared that AI is "a better editor and a better writer than
anyone at the company"
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).

That zeal is now company policy. In mid-May 2026, VandeHei wrote that most
employees should spend 10 percent or more of their time finding ways to use AI
to double their productivity by the end of the year
([Virginia Business](http://virginiabusiness.com/media-2025-jim-vandehei/)).
The newsroom runs a custom GPT called the Axiomizer that reviews drafts,
suggests sharper headlines, tightens the signature "Why it matters" sections,
and drafts internal updates — reporters still write the stories, but the tool
handles rote work that once consumed hours
([WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/axios-ceo-bets-ai-will-rescue-local-news-critics-call-it-a-risky-trade/)).
Axios COO Allison Murphy described the shift in an OpenAI case study: "AI has
already become central in how we do the work of Axios Local"
([WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/axios-ceo-bets-ai-will-rescue-local-news-critics-call-it-a-risky-trade/)).

The mandate comes with an edge. CJR reported that in an OpenAI-hosted panel,
VandeHei told staff: "We were early to basically telling our staff that you
don't have a choice. If you're scared, if you don't like it, we don't care —
you have to embrace it"
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).
Axios laid off 10 percent of its staff months before the OpenAI deal and cut
another eleven jobs in 2026, across the news desk and visual and social media
departments
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).
Unlike Politico, Axios is not unionized, so there has been no organized
pushback on AI's use
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).

## The local-news economics bet

The business logic is straightforward and worth taking seriously. Old local
newsrooms carried heavy fixed costs — buildings, printing, distribution — that
became dead weight as revenue fell, and little of that cost was reporting.
VandeHei's pitch, as he put it to CJR: "Could we use AI to basically automate
everything you need in a local market other than the journalist and the
journalism itself?"
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).
OpenAI has its own reasons to play along: as of February 2026, ChatGPT users
in the US were writing a million prompts a week seeking local news, and a
shrinking local press means a shrinking information supply for AI tools
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).

Each Axios Local newsroom runs lean — one to four remote journalists per
market, working with an editor who covers multiple areas
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).
The seed was a December 2020 acquisition of a North Carolina newsletter, the
Charlotte Agenda; within months Axios Local had journalists in five more
markets, with eight additional newsletters by the end of 2021
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).
The footprint now claimed is more than two million subscribers across the
local network — but CJR is explicit that the operation is not profitable, and
VandeHei said breaking even in each city within five years would be "a great
success"
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).

## Background: from Politico to Cox

VandeHei spent a decade as a reporter — congressional news for The Wall Street
Journal from 2000 to 2003, then three years at The [Washington Post](https://factsledger.com/organizations/washington-post/) — before
co-founding Politico in 2006 and serving as its CEO until he started Axios in
2016; the company launched in 2017
([Virginia Business](http://virginiabusiness.com/media-2025-jim-vandehei/)).
CJR's account fills in the founding bet: that bite-size newsletters could
escape what VandeHei called the "crap trap" of the open web
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).

In August 2022, Atlanta-based Cox Enterprises acquired Axios for a reported
$525 million, with VandeHei staying on as CEO; the communications software
business Axios HQ later became an independent company
([Virginia Business](http://virginiabusiness.com/media-2025-jim-vandehei/)).
CJR notes the Cox deal also came with a $25 million investment earmarked for
expanding local and national coverage — three months before ChatGPT's public
debut changed the industry's trajectory
([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).
VandeHei and co-founder [Mike Allen](https://factsledger.com/people/mike-allen/) received the National Press Club's Fourth
Estate Award, the club's highest honor, in November 2024
([Virginia Business](http://virginiabusiness.com/media-2025-jim-vandehei/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not assert how much OpenAI is paying Axios — neither company
would disclose the figure. It does not claim the OpenAI deal has produced any
editorial interference; the documented facts are the money flow, the training
access, and the non-disclosure, with the independence question reported as a
tension, not a finding. Subscriber and city counts are Axios's own claims.
Press Gazette's reported 22-percent output cut and 30-percent traffic rise,
and Axios HQ's reported ~$20 million annualized revenue, were not verified to
citation standard and are not stated here.
