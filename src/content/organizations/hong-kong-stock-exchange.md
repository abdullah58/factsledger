## Overview

The Hong Kong Stock Exchange is the stock exchange of [Hong Kong](/places/hong-kong/), formally established in 1891 as the Association of Stockbrokers in Hong Kong and renamed in 1914 ([Corporate Finance Institute](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/equities/hong-kong-stock-exchange/)). It is operated by Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited (HKEX), a holding company that is itself listed on the exchange under stock code 388 ([Corporate Finance Institute](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/equities/hong-kong-stock-exchange/)). The exchange is the main gateway between mainland Chinese capital markets and global investors — a role built through the 2014 Stock Connect link with Shanghai and cemented by the wave of US-listed Chinese companies returning home to list in Hong Kong ([Charltons](https://www.charltonslaw.com/shanghai-hong-kong-stock-connect-to-commence-monday/)). [Baidu](/organizations/baidu/) is one of them: it completed its Hong Kong secondary listing on the exchange on 23 March 2021 under stock code 9888, raising about $3.08 billion ([Reuters](https://www.tbsnews.net/world/global-economy/chinas-baidu-raise-31-billion-hong-kong-listing-sources-218002)), and in early January 2026 its AI chip unit [Kunlunxin](/organizations/kunlunxin/) submitted a confidential listing application to the same exchange ([Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/baidu-s-ai-chip-spin-off-strategy-could-ignite-39-upside-in-the-stock-says-jefferies/cmxVE2vRE6y)). In 2025 Hong Kong ranked as the world's leading venue for IPO fundraising ([Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing](https://www.hkexgroup.com/Investor-Relations/Regulatory-Disclosure/Announcements/2026/20260226-2025-Final-Results?sc_lang=en)).

## From four rival exchanges to one unified market

Hong Kong's securities trading began informally in the 1860s, but the first formal exchange was the Association of Stockbrokers in Hong Kong, established in 1891 and renamed The Hong Kong Stock Exchange in 1914 ([Corporate Finance Institute](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/equities/hong-kong-stock-exchange/)). Membership was non-Chinese until 1921, when the all-Chinese Hong Kong Stockbrokers' Association was formed; after the Second World War the two merged under the Hong Kong Stock Exchange name ([Corporate Finance Institute](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/equities/hong-kong-stock-exchange/)). Hong Kong's rapid growth then spawned three more exchanges — Kam Ngan, the Far East Exchange and the Kowloon Stock Exchange — and in 1980 they were unified into the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong, which began trading on 2 April 1986 ([Corporate Finance Institute](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/equities/hong-kong-stock-exchange/)).

The 1987 market crash forced reform: the Hong Kong Securities Clearing Company was created in 1989 to run central clearing and settlement, and the Securities and Futures Commission was established the same year as the statutory regulator ([Corporate Finance Institute](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/equities/hong-kong-stock-exchange/)). The decisive structural change came on 6 March 2000, when the Stock Exchange, the Hong Kong Futures Exchange and the clearing company demutualised and became wholly-owned subsidiaries of a new holding company, Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing Limited, which listed its own shares on the Main Board on 27 June 2000 — making HKEX one of the first exchanges in the world to be publicly listed ([Corporate Finance Institute](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/equities/hong-kong-stock-exchange/)). The exchange remains regulated by the Securities and Futures Commission, which oversees listing, enforcement and market supervision ([Corporate Finance Institute](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/equities/hong-kong-stock-exchange/)).

## The LME acquisition and the Stock Connect bridge

HKEX's expansion beyond equities began with commodities. On 6 December 2012 it completed the acquisition of the London Metal Exchange, the world's leading exchange for trading base-metals futures and options ([Australian Securities and Investments Commission](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/1344320/rep354-published-3-July-2013.pdf)). HKEX said the LME's role "enhances HKEx's position as [China](/places/china/)'s link to international markets and investors" and gave it new multi-asset capabilities in commodities, currency and fixed income ([Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing](https://www.hkex.com.hk/News/Regulatory-Announcements/2012/1212065news?sc_lang=en)). At the time, HKEX was the world's second-largest exchange group by market capitalisation, with 1,540 listed companies worth US$2.7 trillion, including 714 mainland enterprises ([Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing](https://www.hkex.com.hk/News/Regulatory-Announcements/2012/1212065news?sc_lang=en)).

Two years later came the reform that defines the exchange today. Shanghai-Hong Kong Stock Connect, jointly announced by the mainland and Hong Kong regulators on 10 April 2014, launched on 17 November 2014 ([Charltons](https://www.charltonslaw.com/shanghai-hong-kong-stock-connect-to-commence-monday/)). For the first time, global investors could trade Shanghai-listed shares through Hong Kong, while mainland investors could trade Hong Kong-listed shares directly; the programme started with daily quotas of RMB 13 billion northbound and RMB 10.5 billion southbound ([Charltons](https://www.charltonslaw.com/shanghai-hong-kong-stock-connect-to-commence-monday/)). By 2025 the link had grown enormously: average daily northbound turnover reached RMB 212.44 billion and southbound turnover HK$121.14 billion ([Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing](https://www.hkexgroup.com/Investor-Relations/Regulatory-Disclosure/Announcements/2026/20260226-2025-Final-Results?sc_lang=en)). Shenzhen Connect followed in 2016, and the Connect family has since extended to bonds, ETFs and swaps.

## Baidu's homecoming listing — and Kunlunxin's next step

The exchange's role as the homecoming venue for US-listed Chinese companies is where its story meets Baidu's. Baidu priced its Hong Kong secondary listing at HK$252 per share, selling 95 million shares to raise about $3.08 billion, with trading beginning on 23 March 2021 ([Reuters](https://www.tbsnews.net/world/global-economy/chinas-baidu-raise-31-billion-hong-kong-listing-sources-218002)). One Baidu American depositary share equals eight Hong Kong shares, and Baidu was the 15th US-listed Chinese company to carry out a Hong Kong homecoming listing since [Alibaba](/organizations/alibaba/) began the trend in November 2019 ([Reuters](https://www.tbsnews.net/world/global-economy/chinas-baidu-raise-31-billion-hong-kong-listing-sources-218002)). Its advisers confirmed the listing under stock code 9888, with net proceeds of about HK$23,682 million ([King & Wood Mallesons](https://www.kingandwood.com/cn/en/about-us/media-center/kwm-advises-baidu-on-its-hk-secondary-listing.html)).

Five years later the exchange became the venue for the next stage of Baidu's capital-markets story. In early January 2026 Baidu's AI chip unit Kunlunxin submitted a confidential main-board listing application as a carve-out spin-off, with Baidu retaining about 59% and control of the unit ([Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/baidu-s-ai-chip-spin-off-strategy-could-ignite-39-upside-in-the-stock-says-jefferies/cmxVE2vRE6y)). The confidential route was itself new: on 6 May 2025 HKEX and the Securities and Futures Commission introduced a confidential filing option for specialist technology and biotech companies ([Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing](https://www.hkexgroup.com/Investor-Relations/Regulatory-Disclosure/Announcements/2026/20260226-2025-Final-Results?sc_lang=en)). Kunlunxin began in 2012 as Baidu's internal chip team and became an independent entity in 2021; its third-generation P800 chip handles the majority of Baidu's inference work ([China AI Dispatch](https://chinaaidispatch.com/companies/kunlunxin/)). A confidential filing puts no prospectus, terms or audited financials on the public record, and no listing date has been set ([China AI Dispatch](https://chinaaidispatch.com/companies/kunlunxin/)).

## A record 2025

The exchange closed 2025 at the top of the global IPO league table. Hong Kong hosted 119 new listings that raised HK$286.9 billion, up 226% from 2024, with two listings among the world's top five IPOs of the year ([Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing](https://www.hkexgroup.com/Investor-Relations/Regulatory-Disclosure/Announcements/2026/20260226-2025-Final-Results?sc_lang=en)). HKEX itself reported record revenue and other income of HK$29,161 million, up 30%, and record profit attributable to shareholders of HK$17,754 million, up 36% ([Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing](https://www.hkexgroup.com/Investor-Relations/Regulatory-Disclosure/Announcements/2026/20260226-2025-Final-Results?sc_lang=en)). Headline average daily turnover reached HK$249.8 billion, up 90% year on year, and the number of listed issuers grew to 2,686 by year-end ([Charltons](https://www.charltonslaw.com/hong-kong-stock-exchange-publishes-listing-committees-2025-report/)). Chief executive Bonnie Y Chan described 2025 as the year HKEX "regained its position as the world's leading venue for IPOs" ([Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing](https://www.hkexgroup.com/Investor-Relations/Regulatory-Disclosure/Announcements/2026/20260226-2025-Final-Results?sc_lang=en)). As 2026 began, the IPO pipeline held more than 400 active applications — including, at its most watched end, a chipmaker whose parent already trades on the exchange as 9888 ([Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing](https://www.hkexgroup.com/Investor-Relations/Regulatory-Disclosure/Announcements/2026/20260226-2025-Final-Results?sc_lang=en)).
