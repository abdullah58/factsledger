## Overview

**SpaceX (Space Exploration Technologies Corp.) is the rocket and spacecraft company Elon Musk founded in 2002 to cut the cost of reaching orbit so sharply that humanity could become multiplanetary — and it is the company that turned that pitch into routine practice: reusable orbital boosters, commercial crew flights for [NASA](/organizations/nasa/), the Starlink satellite internet constellation, and the Starship vehicle NASA chose to return astronauts to the Moon.** ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-spacex-timeline-major-milestones-elon-musk/)) Founded on in-house manufacturing and reusability at a time when orbital launches were dominated by government agencies and traditional contractors, SpaceX spent its first decade proving it could reach orbit at all, then spent the second making reuse, crew transport and satellite broadband ordinary — before closing its private-company era with a record June 2026 public offering ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-spacex-timeline-major-milestones-elon-musk/)).

## From near-bankruptcy to the workhorse rocket

SpaceX's first rocket, Falcon 1, failed on its first three launches. ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-spacex-timeline-major-milestones-elon-musk/)) The fourth attempt succeeded on September 28, 2008, making Falcon 1 the first privately developed, liquid-fueled rocket to reach Earth orbit — a success that arrived just as the company was running low on funding. ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-spacex-timeline-major-milestones-elon-musk/)) Months later, NASA selected SpaceX for its Commercial Resupply Services program to deliver cargo to the International Space Station, giving the startup both financial stability and credibility as a NASA partner. ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-spacex-timeline-major-milestones-elon-musk/))

Falcon 9, which debuted successfully in June 2010, became the backbone of everything that followed: commercial satellites, national security payloads, NASA missions and eventually Starlink satellites all flew on it. ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-spacex-timeline-major-milestones-elon-musk/)) In 2012, Dragon became the first privately built spacecraft to deliver cargo to the space station, proving SpaceX could operate spacecraft in orbit, not just launch rockets. ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-spacex-timeline-major-milestones-elon-musk/))

## Reusability: the bet that changed launch economics

Before December 2015, orbital rockets were largely treated as disposable hardware. ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-spacex-timeline-major-milestones-elon-musk/)) On December 21, 2015, a Falcon 9 first stage landed upright on a converted launch pad at Cape Canaveral — Landing Zone 1 — about 10 minutes after liftoff, having delivered 11 small satellites to orbit. ([Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/spacex-takes-revolutionary-step-toward-reusable-rockets-180957609/)) It was the first time a conventional rocket returned to Earth intact after delivering payloads to orbit; [Blue Origin](/organizations/blue-origin/)'s New Shepard had made a similar soft landing the previous month, but it was not an orbital vehicle. ([Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/spacex-takes-revolutionary-step-toward-reusable-rockets-180957609/)) Two earlier attempts to land on a floating barge had ended in explosions, and Musk had put the landing's odds at only 60 or 70 percent. ([Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/spacex-takes-revolutionary-step-toward-reusable-rockets-180957609/))

The economics were the point: Musk noted the Falcon 9 sold for a little over $60 million but only about $200,000 of that was propellant, and argued that routine recovery could cut costs by "in excess of a factor of 100." ([Smithsonian Magazine](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/spacex-takes-revolutionary-step-toward-reusable-rockets-180957609/)) In March 2017, SpaceX launched and landed a previously flown Falcon 9 booster, proving reuse could become routine operations rather than a stunt. ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-spacex-timeline-major-milestones-elon-musk/)) Falcon Heavy's February 2018 debut extended the trick to heavy lift: then the world's most powerful operational rocket, it launched Musk's Tesla Roadster while two side boosters landed simultaneously. ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-spacex-timeline-major-milestones-elon-musk/))

## Crew, constellation, and the Moon

On May 30, 2020, a Falcon 9 lifted off from Pad 39A at Kennedy Space Center at 3:22 p.m. EDT carrying Crew Dragon on Demo-2, with NASA astronauts Bob Behnken and Doug Hurley aboard. ([Space.com](https://www.space.com/spacex-demo2-nasa-astronaut-launch-success.html)) It was the first crewed orbital launch from U.S. soil since the Space Shuttle retired in 2011, and the final test before NASA could validate Crew Dragon and Falcon 9 for human spaceflight. ([Space.com](https://www.space.com/spacex-demo2-nasa-astronaut-launch-success.html)) The mission capped a decade-long commercial-crew effort: in 2014, SpaceX and Boeing had won contracts worth a combined $6.8 billion to finish their spacecraft and fly six operational crewed missions apiece to the station. ([Space.com](https://www.space.com/spacex-demo2-nasa-astronaut-launch-success.html)) An uncrewed test flight, Demo-1, had visited the station in March 2019. ([Space.com](https://www.space.com/spacex-demo2-nasa-astronaut-launch-success.html))

Meanwhile, SpaceX launched its first large batch of Starlink satellites in 2019, beginning construction of what became the world's largest satellite internet constellation, later expanding beyond residential broadband into aviation, maritime, enterprise networking, emergency communications and direct-to-cell connectivity. ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-spacex-timeline-major-milestones-elon-musk/)) Starlink gave SpaceX a recurring-revenue business beyond launches — the financial base, the timeline argues, for its most ambitious project. ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-spacex-timeline-major-milestones-elon-musk/))

That project is Starship. NASA selected SpaceX's Starship Human Landing System for Artemis in 2021 and later exercised an option for a Starship landing on Artemis IV. ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-spacex-timeline-major-milestones-elon-musk/)) But the plan changed materially in 2026: NASA announced in October that it and its two lander providers, Blue Origin and SpaceX, had streamlined designs and concepts of operations, redefining Artemis III as a low-Earth-orbit demonstration of rendezvous and docking between test versions of each lander and the Orion spacecraft. ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/nasa-partners-accelerate-lander-development-for-2028-moon-missions/)) Under the original contracts, Starship was assigned to Artemis III and IV and Blue Origin's Blue Moon Mark 2 to Artemis V; under the realignment, neither provider is assigned to a specific lunar landing mission, and NASA will choose which company first delivers astronauts to the surface in 2028 based on lander readiness. ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/nasa-partners-accelerate-lander-development-for-2028-moon-missions/)) SpaceX's approach depends on in-space propellant aggregation — tanker Starships transferring cryogenic propellant to a depot Starship that fuels the lander — and the revised plan has Starship wait for Orion in Earth orbit, dock, then fire a trans-lunar injection burn to send the combined stack toward low lunar orbit. ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/nasa-partners-accelerate-lander-development-for-2028-moon-missions/)) Both providers must fly an uncrewed demonstration and be certified by NASA before any crewed landing. ([NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/nasa-partners-accelerate-lander-development-for-2028-moon-missions/))

SpaceX's corporate geography moved with its ambitions. On July 16, 2024, Musk announced the headquarters would move from Hawthorne, California — where the company occupied a roughly million-square-foot former Boeing factory — to Starbase, Texas, the commercial spaceport it had been developing near Brownsville. ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/office/elon-musk-says-space-x-x-moving-headquarters-out-of-california-125112)) And in June 2026, the private-company era ended: SpaceX priced shares at $135, raising $75 billion through about 555.56 million shares at a $1.77 trillion valuation, the largest IPO on record, with shares trading on Nasdaq. ([TradingView News](https://www.tradingview.com/news/moodys:036acab54b874:0-spacex-ipo-vs-upcoming-anthropic-ipo-will-claude-maker-beat-elon-musk-s-75-bn-record/))

A lesser-known thread ties SpaceX to the engineering-software world: [Altair Engineering](/organizations/altair-engineering/) founder and CEO James Scapa said in 2017 that Altair works with "well-known companies like Blue Origin, SpaceX and Facebook," naming SpaceX among its customers. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/11/01/altair-engineering-soars-41-in-public-debut/1024))
