## The oldest governing body in American sport

American sports history usually starts with baseball. Rowing got there first. The United States Rowing Association traces its lineage to the National Association of Amateur Oarsmen, founded in 1872 — the first national governing body for any sport in the United States, and the organisation USRowing says was simply renamed the United States Rowing Association in 1982. It remains the sport's national governing body, headquartered at 1 S Post Road in West Windsor, New Jersey.

The depth of that history shows up in the details USRowing itself publishes. Rowing was one of the original sports of the modern Olympics, and Baron Pierre de Coubertin was a rower. It was the first intercollegiate sport contested in the United States — the first race was between Harvard and Yale in 1852, and Yale founded the first collegiate boat club in 1843. The country's first rowing club was the Narragansett Boat Club (1838), and the first amateur sport organisation of any kind was another rowing club, Philadelphia's Schuylkill Navy (1858). The international federation, FISA, founded in 1892, was the first international sports federation.

## A medal record page 1 doesn't share

USRowing's most striking claims are competitive. From 1920 until 1956, the United States won the gold medal in the men's eight at every Olympic Games — a 36-year stranglehold on rowing's marquee event. The 1924 crew included a young oarsman who would become far more famous ashore: Dr. Benjamin Spock, the baby doctor, won his gold in the eight that year. (Gregory Peck rowed at the University of California in 1937, but apparently did not pursue the sport professionally.)

The modern streak belongs to the women's eight: Olympic gold in 2008, gold again in London in 2012, and gold a third consecutive time in Rio in 2016, where the women's single sculls added a silver. That 2016 victory extended the crew's world-title winning streak to eleven consecutive years. Individual milestones punctuate the record: in 1997, Jamie Koven became the first American to win the men's single sculls at the world championships since 1966; in 1999, the men's eight won its third straight world championship gold, a first in U.S. history; and the 2004 Olympics brought men's eight gold again.

Paris 2024 added three notable results. The men's four won gold — the first in that event since 1960. The men's eight took bronze, its first medal in the event since winning gold in 2004 and bronze in Beijing 2008. And at the Paralympics, the mixed four won silver for the third consecutive Games. The 2024 Games were also the last to field lightweight rowing events, closing the chapter on categories where athletes like [Elaine Tierney](/people/elaine-tierney/) — who won the lightweight women's pair at the USRowing Summer National Championships on Mercer Lake — had made their mark.

Rowers remain the third-largest U.S. delegation to the Olympic Games, at 48 athletes.
