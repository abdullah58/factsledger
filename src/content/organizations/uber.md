## Overview

Uber Technologies, Inc. is an American ride-hailing and delivery company headquartered in [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/), California, that connects riders and eaters with drivers and couriers through a smartphone app ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/personal-finance/111015/story-uber.asp)). Founded in 2009 as UberCab by Travis Kalanick and Garrett Camp, it grew from a black-car service into a platform operating in more than 70 countries and 15,000 cities, completed 13.6 billion trips in 2025, and turned its first operating profit in 2023 after years of heavy losses ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/personal-finance/111015/story-uber.asp); [Uber](https://marketwirenews.com/stock/uber/news/uber-announces-results-for-fourth-quarter-and-full-y-7544631266100302.html)). In 2026 it added a new chapter: [Baidu](/organizations/baidu/)'s fully driverless [Apollo Go](/organizations/apollo-go/) robotaxis began carrying Uber riders in [Dubai](/places/dubai/), the first deployment of a multi-year partnership between the two companies ([Baidu](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/baidus-apollo-go-goes-live-on-uber-in-dubai-offering-a-new-way-for-users-to-hail-fully-driverless-rides-302856106.html)).

## From a Paris taxi queue to a global platform

The company says the idea was born in 2008, when Kalanick and Camp, attending a conference in Paris, were unable to hail a taxi ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/personal-finance/111015/story-uber.asp)). Camp initially wanted a high-end black-car service, but Kalanick pushed for an app-based model in which the company would not own the cars. The two founded UberCab in San Francisco in 2009, received the first ride request in July 2010, and began publicly using the name Uber that October ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/personal-finance/111015/story-uber.asp)). Expansion was rapid: New York and Paris in 2011, the cheaper UberX tier in 2012 — which became the company's most popular offering — operations in 21 countries by 2013, and 300 cities by 2015 ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/personal-finance/111015/story-uber.asp)). Food delivery followed, with UberEats debuting in New York and Chicago in 2015 ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/personal-finance/111015/story-uber.asp)).

Growth came with retreats as well as advances. Uber sold its [China](/places/china/) operations to Didi in 2016 and its Southeast Asia operations to Grab in 2018 ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/personal-finance/111015/story-uber.asp)). The same period brought the crises that defined its reputation: in 2017 a former engineer, Susan Fowler, alleged in a blog post that her sexual-harassment complaints had been largely ignored, an investigation of 215 staff complaints led to 20 firings, and Kalanick stepped down as CEO, replaced by Dara Khosrowshahi ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/personal-finance/111015/story-uber.asp)). Also in 2017, [Alphabet](/organizations/alphabet/)'s self-driving subsidiary [Waymo](/organizations/waymo/) sued Uber alleging trade-secret theft; the case settled in 2018 for $245 million ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/personal-finance/111015/story-uber.asp)). In February 2021 the UK Supreme Court ruled that Uber drivers are workers rather than self-employed, entitled to minimum wage and holiday pay, with working time counted from the moment they log on to the app until they log off ([Silicon UK](https://www.silicon.co.uk/e-management/incomes/uk-court-rules-uber-drivers-are-workers-384054)).

Uber went public on May 10, 2019, pricing its shares at $45 on the New York Stock Exchange, raising $8.1 billion at an $82.4 billion valuation — but the shares closed 7.6% below the IPO price on the first day ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/personal-finance/111015/story-uber.asp)). Early backers included [BlackRock](/organizations/blackrock/) and [Google](/organizations/google/) Ventures, which participated in a 2014 funding round, and [Amazon](/organizations/amazon/) founder Jeff Bezos, who invested in 2011 ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/personal-finance/111015/story-uber.asp)).

## The long road to profit

For most of its life Uber lost money. It burned through tens of billions of dollars capturing market share, and its unprofitability became something of a punch line ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/01/uber-profitable-earnings-analysis/)). Under Khosrowshahi the company sharpened its focus on its core ride-hailing and delivery businesses, shed non-core units, and leaned on advertising and grocery delivery ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/personal-finance/111015/story-uber.asp)). The turn came in the second quarter of 2023, when Uber reported its first-ever GAAP operating profit, $326 million against a $713 million operating loss a year earlier, on revenue of $9.23 billion and gross bookings of $33.6 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/01/uber-profitable-earnings-analysis/)). It went on to record its first full-year operating profit in 2023, about $1.1 billion, and a first annual net profit as a public company of $1.89 billion ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/articles/personal-finance/111015/story-uber.asp)).

The scale of the business by 2025 shows how far that turnaround travelled. For the full year Uber reported 13.6 billion trips, $193.5 billion in gross bookings and $52.0 billion in revenue, with GAAP income from operations of $5.6 billion and free cash flow of $9.8 billion ([Uber](https://marketwirenews.com/stock/uber/news/uber-announces-results-for-fourth-quarter-and-full-y-7544631266100302.html)). More than 200 million people used the platform in an average month of the fourth quarter of 2025, and the company reported operating in more than 70 countries and 15,000 cities, with 46 million members of its Uber One programme ([Uber](https://marketwirenews.com/stock/uber/news/uber-announces-results-for-fourth-quarter-and-full-y-7544631266100302.html); [Expanded Ramblings](https://expandedramblings.com/index.php/uber-statistics/)).

## From building robotaxis to hosting them

Uber's relationship with driverless cars has come full circle. It began developing its own self-driving technology in 2015, but the effort brought controversy and heavy costs: a test vehicle with a human safety driver struck and killed a pedestrian in March 2018, and Uber halted all testing ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/12/07/uber-sells-self-driving-unit-uber-atg-in-deal-that-will-push-auroras-valuation-to-10b/)). In December 2020 Uber sold its self-driving unit, Advanced Technologies Group, to Aurora Innovation, investing $400 million for a 26% stake in the combined company ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/12/07/uber-sells-self-driving-unit-uber-atg-in-deal-that-will-push-auroras-valuation-to-10b/)). Rather than building robotaxis itself, Uber became the marketplace other companies' driverless cars ride on — a strategy its chief executive has described as aiming to make Uber the largest facilitator of autonomous-vehicle trips in the world ([Uber](https://marketwirenews.com/stock/uber/news/uber-announces-results-for-fourth-quarter-and-full-y-7544631266100302.html)).

The Waymo partnership came first. In September 2024 Waymo and Uber announced an expanded partnership to bring Waymo's fully autonomous, all-electric Jaguar I-PACE vehicles to Austin and Atlanta, available only on the Uber app from early 2025, with Uber managing and dispatching the fleet ([Waymo](https://waymo.com/blog/2024/09/waymo-and-uber-expand-partnership/)). The Baidu deal followed the same template on a global scale. First announced in July 2025, the multi-year strategic partnership aims to bring thousands of Baidu Apollo Go autonomous vehicles onto Uber's platform across multiple markets ([Baidu](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/baidus-apollo-go-goes-live-on-uber-in-dubai-offering-a-new-way-for-users-to-hail-fully-driverless-rides-302856106.html)). Dubai was the first market where it came to life: on August 20, 2026, Baidu's fully driverless Apollo Go vehicles became available to Uber riders there, with New Horizon Luxury Transport operating the fleet ([Baidu](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/baidus-apollo-go-goes-live-on-uber-in-dubai-offering-a-new-way-for-users-to-hail-fully-driverless-rides-302856106.html)). Riders in Umm Suqeim and [Jumeirah](/places/jumeirah/) who request UberX or Uber Comfort may be matched with a driverless Apollo Go RT6 — a purpose-built electric robotaxi carrying up to three passengers and more than 30 sensors — or select the "Autonomous" option directly ([Just Auto](https://www.just-auto.com/news/uber-launches-baidus-fully-driverless-apollo-go-in-dubai/)). Dubai is also the first city where Apollo Go runs both its own app and a partner platform in parallel, and Apollo Go's fleets have logged more than 350 million autonomous kilometres, over 240 million of them fully driverless ([Baidu](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/baidus-apollo-go-goes-live-on-uber-in-dubai-offering-a-new-way-for-users-to-hail-fully-driverless-rides-302856106.html)). Uber says it now works with more than 30 autonomous-vehicle partners ([Just Auto](https://www.just-auto.com/news/uber-launches-baidus-fully-driverless-apollo-go-in-dubai/)).
