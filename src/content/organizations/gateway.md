## Overview

Gateway Inc. — originally Gateway 2000 — was an American personal-computer company founded in 1985 in a farmhouse on a cattle ranch outside Sioux City, [Iowa](/places/iowa/). It grew by selling custom-built PCs directly to customers by mail order and phone, shipping them in trademark cow-spotted boxes, and at its peak employed nearly 25,000 people before Acer acquired it for $710 million in 2007 ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/article/2087-gateway-2000/); [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/2334025/acer-completes-gateway-acquisition.html)).

The company was the product of a chance meeting in the fall of 1984 between Ted Waitt, a University of Iowa sophomore, and Mike Hammond, an employee at a Des Moines computer store ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/article/2087-gateway-2000/)). Less than a year later, on 5 September 1985, their mail-order business, TIPC Network, was running from a two-story farmhouse on Waitt's father's cattle ranch, launched with a $10,000 loan secured by Waitt's grandmother Mildred Smith's $15,000 certificate of deposit ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/article/2087-gateway-2000/)). Its first products were peripherals and software for Texas Instruments personal computers, and it took $100,000 in revenue in its first four months ([Gateway 2000, Inc.](http://gateway2000.com/home/aboutgw/ir_relations/)).

What followed was one of the fastest rises in the PC industry: revenue grew from $1.5 million in 1987 to $5.04 billion in 1996 — a more than 3,000-fold increase in nine years — before Acer bought the company for $710 million in 2007, roughly a tenth of the $7 billion Compaq had discussed paying a decade earlier ([Gateway 2000, Inc.](http://gateway2000.com/home/aboutgw/ir_relations/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/off-prem/2007/08/27/acer-to-grab-gateway-for-710m/401102)).

## From a cattle ranch to a billion dollars

The direct-sales model was the whole business. Waitt and Hammond sold by placing ads in computer magazines and shipping straight to the buyer, cutting out the retail markup and the limited choice of the shop floor ([Gateway 2000, Inc.](http://gateway2000.com/home/aboutgw/ir_relations/)). In February 1986 Waitt's brother Norm joined as a full partner to handle the finances, and the trio soon began selling custom-built PCs for less than enthusiasts could build them at home, changing the company name to Gateway 2000 ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/article/2087-gateway-2000/)). By 1987 Gateway was designing and assembling its own fully-configured PC-compatible systems, and a full-page Computer Shopper advertisement that year offered its A12 model — a 12 MHz 80286 processor, 1 MB of RAM, a 40 MB hard drive and a 14-inch monitor — for $1,995 ([Gateway 2000, Inc.](http://gateway2000.com/home/aboutgw/ir_relations/); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/article/2087-gateway-2000/)).

The cow-spotted boxes arrived in 1991 and became the most recognisable shipping mark in the PC business, a deliberate nod to the company's Iowa cattle-ranch roots that also saved money because it used only two colours of ink ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/article/2087-gateway-2000/)). Sales climbed from $12 million in 1988 to $275 million in 1990 and past $1 billion in 1992, and the company went public in December 1993 on Nasdaq under the symbol GATE, moving to the New York Stock Exchange in May 1997 under the ticker GTW ([Gateway 2000, Inc.](http://gateway2000.com/home/aboutgw/ir_relations/)). In 1997 Compaq was in talks to buy Gateway 2000 for about $7 billion, a deal that was nearly closed before it fell apart at the last minute ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/off-prem/2007/08/27/acer-to-grab-gateway-for-710m/401102)).

## Country stores, the slump, and the eMachines rescue

In 1996 Gateway opened its first Gateway Country stores — barn-like showrooms where customers could try machines before ordering by phone or online — a retail concept that predated [Apple](/organizations/apple/)'s standalone stores by half a decade and [Microsoft](/organizations/microsoft/)'s by more than a dozen years ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/article/2087-gateway-2000/)). At their peak in 2001 there were just over 320 stores across the country ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1701502/update-gateway-to-close-all-retail-stores-april-9.html)). But the expansion coincided with the company's turn for the worse: in October 1999 Gateway announced an $800 million, two-year partnership with AOL to market its online service on every new Gateway computer, and around the same time Waitt stepped down as chief executive in favour of Jeffrey Weitzen ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/article/2087-gateway-2000/)). The fourth quarter of 2000 brought a $94.3 million loss as the dot-com bubble burst, and Waitt returned as chief executive in early 2001 ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/article/2087-gateway-2000/)).

The recovery never came. Gateway posted losses in 12 of the 13 quarters before April 2004, and its workforce had been cut from nearly 25,000 in 2000 to about 6,500 ([InformationWeek](https://www.informationweek.com/it-leadership/gateway-to-close-retail-stores-shed-2-500-jobs)). In a last attempt to save the company, Gateway announced on 30 January 2004 that it would buy budget PC maker eMachines for $30 million in cash plus 50 million Gateway shares, completing the deal on 11 March at about $289 million, with eMachines chief Wayne Inouye taking over as Gateway's chief executive and Waitt remaining chairman ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/c/a/Past-News/Gateway-Closes-on-EMachines-Purchase)). Weeks later, on 9 April 2004, Gateway closed all 188 of its remaining Country stores and cut about 2,500 jobs — 38% of its headcount, leaving about 4,000 employees — despite having spent $35 million remodelling the stores the year before ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1701502/update-gateway-to-close-all-retail-stores-april-9.html); [InformationWeek](https://www.informationweek.com/it-leadership/gateway-to-close-retail-stores-shed-2-500-jobs)).

## Sued over the JPEG patent

The same month Gateway closed its stores, it was named in one of the tech industry's most notorious patent cases. In April 2004 Forgent Networks — the Austin, [Texas](/places/texas/)-based company now known as [Asure Software](/organizations/asure-software/) — sued 31 companies, Gateway Inc. among them, through its [Compression Labs](/organizations/compression-labs/) subsidiary, alleging they infringed U.S. Patent No. 4,698,672, a "coding system for reducing redundancy" that Forgent said was integral to JPEG image compression ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/171812/forgent.html)). Forgent had been asserting the patent since 2002, saying it had already collected about $90 million from more than 30 licensees, including a $16 million one-time fee from [Sony](/organizations/sony/) ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/171812/forgent.html); [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1702489/forgent-sues-pc-makers-over-jpeg-patent-claims.html)).

The claim was contested from the start: the Independent JPEG Group said in 2002 that Forgent's patent covered a different type of compression than the one used in the JPEG standard, and Microsoft — whose Windows and Internet Explorer were among the most prominent users of JPEG — was notably not among the companies sued ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1702489/forgent-sues-pc-makers-over-jpeg-patent-claims.html)). The litigation ended in November 2006, when Forgent settled with about 30 companies for about $8 million — far short of the $1 billion it had hoped to collect — after the [U.S. Patent and Trademark Office](/organizations/us-patent-and-trademark-office/) rejected the patent's broadest claims in an initial review that May ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/forgent-settles-jpeg-patent-litigation/)). Over the life of the patent, Forgent said it had collected more than $110 million from over 60 companies ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/forgent-settles-jpeg-patent-litigation/)).

## Acer and the afterlife of the brand

On 27 August 2007 Acer announced a definitive agreement to acquire Gateway for $1.90 a share, about $710 million in cash, in a deal that would create a combined company with over $15 billion in revenue shipping more than 20 million PCs a year — the world's third-largest PC vendor ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/off-prem/2007/08/27/acer-to-grab-gateway-for-710m/401102)). At the time Gateway was the third-largest PC vendor in the United States, with 5.6% of the market in the second quarter of 2007, and alongside the announcement it moved to exercise its right of first refusal over European PC maker Packard Bell ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/off-prem/2007/08/27/acer-to-grab-gateway-for-710m/401102)). Acer completed the acquisition in October 2007 after winning approval from U.S. regulators including the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States, Gateway's stock ceased trading on the New York Stock Exchange, and Gateway continued under its own name as an Acer subsidiary ([InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/2334025/acer-completes-gateway-acquisition.html)).

The brand itself outlived the company. Co-founder Mike Hammond died in 2015, aged 53, but the cow-spotted name returned in September 2020, when a new line of Gateway laptops and tablets went on sale exclusively at Walmart.com — laptops from $179 and tablets from $69, in Ultra Thin and Creators series, shipped in the brand's distinctive cow-print packaging ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/article/2087-gateway-2000/); [Chain Store Age](https://chainstoreage.com/walmart-resurrects-gateway-pc-brand)). Acer owns the Gateway brand, and Walmart became its exclusive provider of Gateway notebooks and tablets ([Chain Store Age](https://chainstoreage.com/walmart-resurrects-gateway-pc-brand)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What happened to Gateway computers?**
Acer acquired Gateway for about $710 million in October 2007, and the company continued as an Acer subsidiary under its own name before the brand went largely dormant ([InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/2334025/acer-completes-gateway-acquisition.html)). The Gateway name returned in September 2020 as a line of laptops and tablets sold exclusively through Walmart ([Chain Store Age](https://chainstoreage.com/walmart-resurrects-gateway-pc-brand)).

**Who founded Gateway?**
Ted Waitt and Mike Hammond founded the company as the TIPC Network mail-order business on 5 September 1985, running it from a farmhouse on Waitt's father's cattle ranch outside Sioux City, Iowa ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/article/2087-gateway-2000/)). Waitt's grandmother put up her $15,000 certificate of deposit as collateral for the $10,000 loan that got it started ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/article/2087-gateway-2000/)).

**Why did Gateway ship computers in cow-spotted boxes?**
The black-and-white boxes mimicked the markings of Holstein dairy cows, a deliberate tribute to the company's Iowa cattle-ranch roots, and they also saved money because they used only two colours of ink ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/article/2087-gateway-2000/)). Introduced in 1991, the pattern became as synonymous with Gateway as the Nike swoosh is with Nike ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/article/2087-gateway-2000/)).

**Was Gateway sued over the JPEG patent?**
Yes. In April 2004 Forgent Networks sued 31 companies, Gateway Inc. among them, alleging infringement of U.S. Patent No. 4,698,672, which Forgent said covered the compression at the heart of the JPEG format ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/171812/forgent.html)). Forgent settled the litigation in November 2006 for about $8 million from about 30 companies ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/forgent-settles-jpeg-patent-litigation/)).

**Is Gateway still in business?**
Not as an independent company: Acer bought it in 2007 and it continued only as an Acer subsidiary ([InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/2334025/acer-completes-gateway-acquisition.html)). The brand survives as a Walmart-exclusive line of laptops and tablets, relaunched in September 2020 with prices starting at $179 for laptops and $69 for tablets ([Chain Store Age](https://chainstoreage.com/walmart-resurrects-gateway-pc-brand)).
