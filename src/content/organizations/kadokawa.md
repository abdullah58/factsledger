## Overview

Kadokawa Corporation is a Japanese media conglomerate based in Fujimi, Chiyoda, [Tokyo](/places/tokyo/), whose business spans publishing, film, video games, web services and education, including IP experiential facilities such as TOKOROZAWA SAKURA TOWN ([Kadokawa Corporation](https://group.kadokawa.co.jp/global/information/news_release/2025111901_en.html)). It traces its origins to Kadokawa Shoten, founded by Genyoshi Kadokawa on 10 November 1945 to, in the company's own founding phrase, revitalise Japanese culture through publishing in the postwar era. Kadokawa was the fourth plaintiff in the manga publishers' suit against [Cloudflare](/organizations/cloudflare/) — and the odd one out in the result: on 19 November 2025 the [Tokyo District Court](/organizations/tokyo-district-court/) ordered Cloudflare to pay Kadokawa ¥121,400,928, less than the ¥126,500,000 awarded to each of [Kodansha](/organizations/kodansha/), [Shueisha](/organizations/shueisha/) and [Shogakukan](/organizations/shogakukan/) ([Tokyo District Court](https://www.courts.go.jp/ip/eng/assets/ip/eng/chizai_en/chizai_en-pdf-3813.pdf)).

Kadokawa Shoten was established on 10 November 1945 by Genyoshi Kadokawa; its first publication imprint, Kadokawa Bunko, appeared in 1949, and the company went public on 2 April 1954 ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/66985-global-publishing-leaders-2015-kadokawa-shoten-publishing-co-ltd.html)). From the start it was a broader media business than a book publisher: it has been active in film production since the founding of Kadokawa Haruki in 1976, and an internal merger of Kadokawa Shoten and Kadokawa Pictures integrated publishing with film ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/66985-global-publishing-leaders-2015-kadokawa-shoten-publishing-co-ltd.html)).

## From publisher to conglomerate

Kadokawa's modern shape comes from a decade of restructuring. In autumn 2013 it reorganised on a "one company" model, integrating nine subsidiaries — including ASCII Media Works, Enterbrain, Fujimi Shobo and Media Factory — into Kadokawa Corporation ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/66985-global-publishing-leaders-2015-kadokawa-shoten-publishing-co-ltd.html)). On 1 October 2014 Kadokawa Corporation merged with Dwango Co., Ltd. — owner of Niconico, one of [Japan](/places/japan/)'s largest video-sharing services — to form Kadokawa Dwango Corporation ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/66985-global-publishing-leaders-2015-kadokawa-shoten-publishing-co-ltd.html)). The deal married a publishing house to a video platform, and the combined group defined its strategy as becoming a "mega software publisher" and platform provider for the digital era ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/66985-global-publishing-leaders-2015-kadokawa-shoten-publishing-co-ltd.html)).

The economics of that strategy ran on light novels and digital distribution. Sales of anime-based light novels represented 42.2% of the book business's sales in 2011, and the Media Factory division drove the light-novel value chain ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/66985-global-publishing-leaders-2015-kadokawa-shoten-publishing-co-ltd.html)). Kadokawa launched the BookWalker e-book platform in 2012, with 7,000 titles available at launch, and the ComicWalker distribution platform in spring 2014 ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/66985-global-publishing-leaders-2015-kadokawa-shoten-publishing-co-ltd.html)). Total revenue for the corporation was ¥151 billion in 2014, down from ¥162 billion in 2013, with Kadokawa Shoten's sales at ¥94.66 billion ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/66985-global-publishing-leaders-2015-kadokawa-shoten-publishing-co-ltd.html)).

## The Cloudflare manga-piracy lawsuit

Kadokawa sued Cloudflare, Inc. — headquartered in [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/), [California](/places/california/) — together with Shueisha, Kodansha and Shogakukan on 1 February 2022 in the Tokyo District Court ([Shogakukan](https://www.shogakukan.co.jp/storage/files/article/2021/20220201e.pdf)). The suit concerned two piracy sites that posted more than 4,000 manga works without permission and at their peak attracted over 300 million monthly visits combined, according to a survey by Authorized Books of Japan ([Shueisha](https://www.shueisha.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/notice20251119eng.pdf)); the judgment records the sites as distributing 4,082 titles comprising 123,631 episodes ([Tokyo District Court](https://www.courts.go.jp/ip/eng/assets/ip/eng/chizai_en/chizai_en-pdf-3813.pdf)). Kadokawa's print right for the one work of its own at issue was established on 1 December 2018, its infringement notice under the US Digital Millennium Copyright Act reached Cloudflare on 12 November 2021, and the damage period ran from 14 December 2021 to 28 February 2022 ([Tokyo District Court](https://www.courts.go.jp/ip/eng/assets/ip/eng/chizai_en/chizai_en-pdf-3813.pdf)).

The figures, stated exactly: at filing, the four publishers put total damages for the four litigated works at about ¥5.6 billion and sought about ¥115 million per company as part of that sum — about ¥460 million in total ([Shogakukan](https://www.shogakukan.co.jp/storage/files/article/2021/20220201e.pdf); [Anime News Network](https://www.animenewsnetwork.com/news/2025-11-19/shueisha-kodansha-shogakukan-kadokawa-win-copyright-suit-against-cloudflare/.231199)). By judgment, the amount claimed had become ¥126.5 million per publisher. On 19 November 2025 the Tokyo District Court recognised total damages of about ¥3.6 billion (about US$24 million) for the four works, but because the publishers had claimed only a portion of what they suffered, it ordered payment of about ¥500 million in total ([Shueisha](https://www.shueisha.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/notice20251119eng.pdf)). Kadokawa's share was the exception: ¥121,400,928, against ¥126,500,000 for each of the other three — a total of ¥500,900,928 — plus interest at 3% a year from 21 October 2022, and the judgment dismissed Kadokawa's remaining alternative claims and made Kadokawa bear one-twentieth of its own and the defendant's litigation costs ([Tokyo District Court](https://www.courts.go.jp/ip/eng/assets/ip/eng/chizai_en/chizai_en-pdf-3813.pdf)). The operative part of the judgment as read does not explain why Kadokawa's figure is lower, and this profile does not guess.

The court dismissed the publishers' primary claim that Cloudflare was itself the transmitter of the pirated manga, but held that its failure to act promptly on infringement notices and its negligent continuation of distribution constituted aiding and abetting copyright infringement ([Shueisha](https://www.shueisha.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/notice20251119eng.pdf)). Kadokawa, issuing its own victory release the same day, said it expected the ruling to lead to the prevention of abuse of CDN services by piracy sites and to the maintenance of a content-creation cycle that returns appropriate compensation to creators ([Kadokawa Corporation](https://group.kadokawa.co.jp/global/information/news_release/2025111901_en.html)). A legal analysis published in April 2026 states that Cloudflare has appealed, with hearings at the Intellectual Property High Court awaited ([Monolith Law Office](https://monolith.law/en/it/cloudflare-piracy-case-liability)).

## FAQ

**What is Kadokawa?**
Kadokawa Corporation is a Japanese media conglomerate based in Fujimi, Chiyoda, Tokyo, founded in 1945 as the publisher Kadokawa Shoten. Its business spans publishing, film, video games, web services, education and IP-based facilities such as TOKOROZAWA SAKURA TOWN.

**How did Kadokawa become a conglomerate?**
Through restructuring: nine group companies were integrated into Kadokawa Corporation in 2013, and in 2014 it merged with Dwango, owner of the Niconico video service, combining publishing with video distribution and games.

**What are Kadokawa's best-known digital platforms?**
BookWalker, its e-book platform launched in 2012, and ComicWalker, launched in spring 2014. Light novels — anime-based paperbacks — were 42.2% of its book-business sales in 2011.

**How much did Kadokawa win from Cloudflare?**
The Tokyo District Court ordered Cloudflare to pay Kadokawa ¥121,400,928 plus 3% annual interest from 21 October 2022, in a judgment dated 19 November 2025 — less than the ¥126,500,000 awarded to each of the other three publishers.

**Why did Kadokawa get less than the other publishers?**
The judgment does not explain the difference in the operative part read for this profile. Kadokawa's remaining alternative claims were dismissed and it was made to bear one-twentieth of its own and the defendant's litigation costs, but the reason for the lower award is not stated and is flagged as an open item.
