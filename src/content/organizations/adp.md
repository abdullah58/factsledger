## Overview

Automatic Data Processing, Inc. (ADP) is the world's largest human capital management company, paying more than 42 million workers for over 1.1 million clients in more than 140 countries and territories ([ADP, Inc.](https://www.investors.adp.com/files/doc_presentations/2025/08/ADP_Investor_Overview_August.pdf)). Founded in Paterson, New Jersey, in 1949 as a manual payroll service run from a room above an ice-cream parlor, it reported $20.6 billion in revenue and $4.1 billion in net earnings in fiscal 2025 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0000008670/581e9519-c723-47af-a280-cd2aaf76776f.pdf)). ADP is a [United States](/places/united-states/) company headquartered in Roseland, New Jersey, and listed on [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/) under the symbol ADP ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0000008670/581e9519-c723-47af-a280-cd2aaf76776f.pdf)).

ADP sells payroll, human resources, benefits, time and attendance, talent and compliance software and services, and it also runs a professional employer organization (PEO) that co-employs client workers ([ADP, Inc.](https://www.investors.adp.com/files/doc_presentations/2025/08/ADP_Investor_Overview_August.pdf)). More than 80% of the Fortune 500 are clients, and ADP moves $3.3 trillion in client funds every month ([ADP, Inc.](https://www.investors.adp.com/files/doc_presentations/2025/08/ADP_Investor_Overview_August.pdf)). Its smaller rival [Asure Software](/organizations/asure-software/) competes against it by focusing on small and midsize employers' payroll-tax expertise rather than product breadth ([Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/company-profiles/asure-software/)).

## From a Paterson payroll room to a public company

The company began with a missed payday. Henry Taub, who had graduated from New York University with an accounting degree at 19, was working for an accounting practice when a client — a clothing business — failed to issue paychecks one week because of an illness, causing an employee walkout ([The New York Times](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2011-09-15/html/CREC-2011-09-15-pt1-PgS5681.htm)). In 1949, at 21, Taub opened Automatic Payrolls, Inc. in his hometown of Paterson, New Jersey, in an office above an ice-cream parlor and later a hotel basement ([The New York Times](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2011-09-15/html/CREC-2011-09-15-pt1-PgS5681.htm)). The "automatic" in the name described the service from the client's point of view; on the company's side, automation began with little more than an adding machine ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)).

Taub's younger brother Joseph joined in 1951 to run administration, and in 1953 Frank R. Lautenberg — a Paterson textile worker's son, a Second World War veteran and a Columbia economics graduate who had been selling insurance for Prudential — joined as the company's salesman ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)). Taub later recalled the trio's division of labour: "me in accounting, Joe in organization, and Frank in marketing" ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)). Early on the company did not handle clients' money at all; the employer signed the checks or filled the pay envelopes on site ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)).

Growth was slow at first. In the fiscal year to June 1957, operating revenues were $150,000 and full-year profits just $964, after a switch from manual bookkeeping machines to an early IBM computer that Lautenberg later said "damned near killed us" ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)). In 1959 the Taubs set up a separate company, Automatic Tabulating Services, for general data-processing work, and in June 1961 the two companies merged into the newly named Automatic Data Processing, with about 200 payroll clients — including the cast and crew of the Broadway hit *My Fair Lady* — and 30 general-processing customers ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)). Three months later ADP went public, offering 100,000 shares at $3 each ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)).

The public-market record that followed became part of the company's legend: from its first quarter as a public company, ADP posted double-digit earnings-per-share growth for 128 consecutive quarters through fiscal 1993, and each $3 share bought in 1961 had multiplied into 144 shares worth more than $7,000 by 1993 ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)). ADP listed on the New York Stock Exchange in 1970, crossed $1 billion in revenues in 1985, and topped $2 billion in fiscal 1993, when earnings reached a record $294 million ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)). Taub was president from 1949 to 1970, chairman and chief executive from 1970 to 1977, and chairman until 1985 ([The New York Times](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2011-09-15/html/CREC-2011-09-15-pt1-PgS5681.htm)).

## The conglomerate years — and the spin-offs that undid them

Modern profiles describe ADP as a payroll and HR company, but for most of its history it was a conglomerate of data-processing businesses, and the two spin-offs that made it a pure HCM company were the logical end of a policy its own managers had followed for decades. In July 1961 ADP opened a Manhattan office to process brokerage back-office data such as trade confirmations, the start of its second major line ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)). Acquisitions followed from 1965, and by 1972 ADP operated centres in 20 cities and paid over one million people ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)).

Brokerage Services became ADP's second-largest business, producing 23% of fiscal 1993 revenues while processing over one-fifth of the trades executed on Wall Street each day ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)). ADP bought GTE's Telenet stock-quote business in 1983 and the Bunker Ramo quote operations in 1986, becoming the top provider of broker information services by 1991, and entered proxy distribution in 1989 ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)). Dealer Services began with the 1972 acquisition of CSI Computer Systems of Cincinnati and by 1993 served more than 7,000 North American car and truck dealers, about 12% of revenues ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)). A third line, Automotive Claims Services, grew from the 1980 acquisition of Itel's Autadex division and a database cataloguing the components of virtually every car model produced since 1970 ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)).

Management's stated test for any line was demanding: the service had to be mass-marketable, produce long-term repetitive revenues, and require enough specialization to raise barriers to entry ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)). Chief executive Josh Weston, who joined in 1970 and led the company from 1982, added a further condition he called a "silent third force" — a uniform regulatory framework, such as the IRS and wage laws for payroll or the SEC and stock exchanges for securities, within which ADP could design its products ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)). Since the mid-1980s ADP also "pruned" lines that no longer fit, selling a computerized tax-processing business, an ATM electronic-funds-transfer operation, and its interest in a Brazilian payroll company ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/30/Automatic-Data-Processing-Inc.html)).

The pruning culminated in two spin-offs. On 30 March 2007 ADP spun off its brokerage-services business as Broadridge Financial Solutions, distributing one Broadridge share for every four ADP shares; Broadridge began trading on the New York Stock Exchange on 2 April 2007 with nearly $2 billion in revenue ([Broadridge Financial Solutions, Inc.](https://www.broadridge.com/press-release/2007/former-adp-brokerage-services-group-begins-trading-as-an-independent-company-broadridge-lists-on-the-new-york-stock-exchange)). On 1 October 2014 ADP completed the spin-off of its Dealer Services business as CDK Global, distributing one CDK share for every three ADP shares and retaining no ownership, so that ADP's ongoing operations would be "squarely focused on our global HCM strategy" ([ADP, Inc.](https://mediacenter.adp.com/2014-10-01-ADP-Completes-Spinoff-of-CDK-Global)).

## ADP today: scale, segments and the client-funds engine

Today's ADP is a cloud HCM business with two reporting segments. Employer Services sells payroll, HR, benefits, time, talent and compliance products — including ADP RUN, ADP Workforce Now and ADP Lyric HCM — while PEO Services provides fully outsourced HR under a co-employment model ([ADP, Inc.](https://www.investors.adp.com/files/doc_presentations/2025/08/ADP_Investor_Overview_August.pdf)). In fiscal 2025 Employer Services signed $2.1 billion in new business bookings and retained 92.1% of client revenue, while PEO average worksite employees rose 3% to about 748,000 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0000008670/581e9519-c723-47af-a280-cd2aaf76776f.pdf)). The fiscal 2024 revenue split shows how the business is weighted:

| Small Business | $3.4 billion | 18% |
| Major Accounts | $2.9 billion | 15% |
| International | $2.6 billion | 14% |
| Complementary Solutions | $1.6 billion | 8% |

Source: [ADP, Inc.](https://www.investors.adp.com/files/doc_presentations/2025/08/ADP_Investor_Overview_August.pdf). Total FY2024 revenue was $19.2 billion.

A less visible profit engine is client funds: because ADP collects payroll taxes and other client money before remitting it, it holds large balances and invests them conservatively ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0000008670/581e9519-c723-47af-a280-cd2aaf76776f.pdf)). In fiscal 2025 interest on funds held for clients was $1.2 billion, up 16%, on average client-funds balances of $37.6 billion at a 3.2% average yield — roughly $1 of every $17 of revenue came from investing clients' money rather than from selling software or services ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0000008670/581e9519-c723-47af-a280-cd2aaf76776f.pdf)). ADP also keeps growing by acquisition: on 15 October 2024 it bought WorkForce Software, a Michigan workforce-management provider founded in 1999, to strengthen time, scheduling and leave management for large global employers ([ADP, Inc.](https://mediacenter.adp.com/2024-10-15-ADP-Acquires-WorkForce-Software)).

Leadership changed in 2023, when Maria Black became president and chief executive on 1 January, succeeding Carlos Rodriguez, who had been chief executive since November 2011 and became executive chair of the board ([ADP, Inc.](https://mediacenter.adp.com/2022-10-26-ADP-President-Maria-Black-to-succeed-CEO-Carlos-Rodriguez,-Effective-January-1,-2023)). Black had been president since January 2022 and had spent more than two decades at the company ([ADP, Inc.](https://mediacenter.adp.com/2022-10-26-ADP-President-Maria-Black-to-succeed-CEO-Carlos-Rodriguez,-Effective-January-1,-2023)). ADP's own innovation timeline claims a string of firsts — the first cloud-based payroll solution in 1998, the first HCM mobile app in 2009, the first HCM marketplace in 2014, and ADP Assist and Lyric HCM in 2024 ([ADP, Inc.](https://www.investors.adp.com/files/doc_presentations/2025/08/ADP_Investor_Overview_August.pdf)).

## The National Employment Report: ADP as an economic indicator

ADP's payroll data doubles as a public economic indicator. Since 2006 it has published the monthly ADP National Employment Report, a measure of the change in total U.S. nonfarm private employment derived from actual, anonymous payroll data of ADP client companies, produced by the ADP Research Institute in collaboration with Moody's Analytics ([ADP, Inc.](https://www.investors.adp.com/files/doc_presentations/2025/08/ADP_Investor_Overview_August.pdf); [ADP, Inc.](https://mediacenter.adp.com/2016-02-03-ADP-National-Employment-Report-Private-Sector-Employment-Increased-by-205-000-Jobs-in-January)). The report is distributed free to the public each month and, in the mid-2010s, was based on a matched sample of about 411,000 U.S. clients employing nearly 24 million workers ([ADP, Inc.](https://mediacenter.adp.com/2016-02-03-ADP-National-Employment-Report-Private-Sector-Employment-Increased-by-205-000-Jobs-in-January)). Its methods mirror those the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics uses for its monthly establishment survey, and the report is distributed free to the public each month ([ADP, Inc.](https://mediacenter.adp.com/2016-02-03-ADP-National-Employment-Report-Private-Sector-Employment-Increased-by-205-000-Jobs-in-January)).

The report also illustrates ADP's structural advantage: no survey-based forecaster can match a sample drawn from live payrolls. That same data underpins ADP DataCloud, introduced in 2012 ([ADP, Inc.](https://www.investors.adp.com/files/doc_presentations/2025/08/ADP_Investor_Overview_August.pdf)). Henry Taub's original insight — that payroll is a recurring, deadline-driven workflow every employer must get right — still describes the business 77 years later; the difference is that the data exhaust of that workflow is now itself a product ([The New York Times](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2011-09-15/html/CREC-2011-09-15-pt1-PgS5681.htm)).

In fiscal 2025 ADP reported $20.6 billion in revenue and $4.1 billion in net earnings, with 67,000 associates, more than 80% of the Fortune 500 as clients, and $3.3 trillion in client funds moved every month ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0000008670/581e9519-c723-47af-a280-cd2aaf76776f.pdf); [ADP, Inc.](https://www.investors.adp.com/files/doc_presentations/2025/08/ADP_Investor_Overview_August.pdf)).

**What happened to ADP's brokerage and car-dealer businesses?**
ADP spun both off to focus on human capital management: brokerage services became Broadridge Financial Solutions in March 2007, and Dealer Services became CDK Global in October 2014, with ADP retaining no ownership of CDK ([Broadridge Financial Solutions, Inc.](https://www.broadridge.com/press-release/2007/former-adp-brokerage-services-group-begins-trading-as-an-independent-company-broadridge-lists-on-the-new-york-stock-exchange); [ADP, Inc.](https://mediacenter.adp.com/2014-10-01-ADP-Completes-Spinoff-of-CDK-Global)).

**Who runs ADP now?**
Maria Black has been president and chief executive since 1 January 2023, succeeding Carlos Rodriguez, who led the company from November 2011 and became executive chair. Black had been ADP's president since January 2022 after more than two decades at the company ([ADP, Inc.](https://mediacenter.adp.com/2022-10-26-ADP-President-Maria-Black-to-succeed-CEO-Carlos-Rodriguez,-Effective-January-1,-2023)).
