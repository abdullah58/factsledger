## Overview

MoneyLion was a New York-based digital finance platform — part bank, part personal-finance manager, part broker-dealer, part lender, as its founder put it — that marketed a membership bundle of checking, investing, credit-building and cash advances to middle-income Americans ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com//article/cross-border/dawn-of-the-neobanks/56671/1)). Founded in 2013 by former Wall Street investment banker Dee Choubey, it went public in 2021 through a SPAC merger and listed on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker "ML," per its SEC 10-K ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807846/000121390022046590/f10k2021a1_moneylion.htm)). In April 2025, cybersecurity company Gen Digital (owner of Norton and Avast) completed a roughly $1 billion acquisition of MoneyLion, ending its independent public-company life ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/avast-antivirus-owner-gen-digital-acquires-moneylion-in-1-billion-deal/)). Its regulatory record — a $1.75 million CFPB settlement in 2025 and a New York attorney-general lawsuit over its Instacash cash advances — is now the most documented part of its corporate history ([Reuters](https://wzuu.com/2025/11/21/us-cfpb-settles-with-moneylion-as-enforcement-activity-winds-down/)).

## From rejected pitch to neobank

Choubey walked away from a near-seven-figure investment banking career in 2013, raised $1 million in seed funding, and started MoneyLion offering free credit scores and micro-loans ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com//article/cross-border/dawn-of-the-neobanks/56671/1)). The early years were lean: 40 venture investors turned him down, and the company subsisted on loan interest, credit-card ad revenue and consumer-behavior data until Edison Partners led a $23 million investment in 2016 ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com//article/cross-border/dawn-of-the-neobanks/56671/1)). That round funded a robo-advisor letting users invest as little as $50, and in 2018 MoneyLion added a free checking account and debit card issued through Iowa-based Lincoln Savings Bank ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com//article/cross-border/dawn-of-the-neobanks/56671/1)). By late 2019 the app had 5.7 million users, about a million of them paying roughly $20 a month for the membership, and Choubey expected $90 million in annual revenue — triple the prior year ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com//article/cross-border/dawn-of-the-neobanks/56671/1)).

MoneyLion rose alongside a wave of U.S. neobanks that Forbes profiled in the same 2019 survey — including [Chime](/organizations/chime/), which was then approaching a $5 billion valuation ([Forbes India](https://www.forbesindia.com//article/cross-border/dawn-of-the-neobanks/56671/1)). Like its peers, MoneyLion was not itself a chartered bank; it operated through bank partners while selling the customer relationship.

## The SPAC listing and the Gen Digital sale

On September 22, 2021, MoneyLion completed a business combination with Fusion Acquisition Corp., a special purpose acquisition company: Fusion was renamed MoneyLion Inc., the legacy business became a subsidiary called MoneyLion Technologies Inc., and the Class A shares began trading on the NYSE as "ML" ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807846/000121390022046590/f10k2021a1_moneylion.htm)). The company filed its financial reports with the [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](/organizations/us-securities-and-exchange-commission/) as a public company through early 2025 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1807846/000121390022046590/f10k2021a1_moneylion.htm)).

In December 2024, Gen Digital agreed to buy MoneyLion for $82 per share in cash — a 6.5% premium — plus one contingent value right per share entitling holders to $23 in Gen stock tied to Gen's future share performance, valuing the deal at about $1 billion ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/avast-antivirus-owner-gen-digital-acquires-moneylion-in-1-billion-deal/)). Reuters framed the acquisition as Gen's push to add financial wellness to its cyber-safety portfolio, citing MoneyLion's personal-finance platform with more than 18 million users ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/avast-antivirus-owner-gen-digital-acquires-moneylion-in-1-billion-deal/)). The deal closed on April 17, 2025, according to Gen's 10-Q, making MoneyLion a unit of Gen Digital ([SEC 10-Q](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/GEN/10-q-gen-digital-inc-quarterly-earnings-report-47b9b6b4b119.html)).

## The regulatory reckoning: CFPB and the states

MoneyLion's membership model became its biggest legal liability. In September 2022 the CFPB sued, alleging MoneyLion charged active servicemembers and their families more than the 36% cap the Military Lending Act allows — by combining interest rates and membership fees for a program offering access to low-cost loans — and refused to let borrowers cancel memberships until loans were paid off ([Reuters](https://wzuu.com/2025/11/21/us-cfpb-settles-with-moneylion-as-enforcement-activity-winds-down/)). In November 2025 the parties settled for $1.75 million in consumer restitution, distributed mostly pro rata to borrowers between December 1, 2017 and October 11, 2024; MoneyLion agreed to let borrowers cancel memberships [even](/organizations/even/) with unpaid balances or fees, and did not admit or deny wrongdoing ([Reuters](https://wzuu.com/2025/11/21/us-cfpb-settles-with-moneylion-as-enforcement-activity-winds-down/)). Reuters noted the settlement came as possibly one of the CFPB's final actions while the agency's enforcement work was being wound down under the Trump administration ([Reuters](https://wzuu.com/2025/11/21/us-cfpb-settles-with-moneylion-as-enforcement-activity-winds-down/)).

Separately, in April 2025 New York Attorney General Letitia James sued MoneyLion and DailyPay, accusing the two earned-wage-access providers of operating as predatory payday lenders ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/new-york-sues-dailypay-and-moneylion-alleging-predatory-payday-lending/)). The AG alleged that MoneyLion's $8.99 fee on a $100 two-week "Instacash" advance carries an effective annual rate of 234%, with some industry fees topping 750% annualized, and sought restitution, civil fines and an end to the practices ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/new-york-sues-dailypay-and-moneylion-alleging-predatory-payday-lending/)). The dispute turns on whether a paycheck advance is a loan at all — a question dividing state regulators, with Nevada and Wisconsin treating advances as non-loans while Maryland classifies them as loans ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bgov.com/banking-law/fintechs-cash-advances-for-workers-draw-suits-as-states-diverge)).

MoneyLion sits inside a broader earned-wage-access enforcement wave documented by Bloomberg Law: the FTC sued Dave Inc. in late 2024 over hidden fees, the D.C. attorney general sued [EarnIn](/organizations/earnin/) (ActiveHours) with similar allegations, and Baltimore sued MoneyLion itself in state court in 2025 ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bgov.com/banking-law/fintechs-cash-advances-for-workers-draw-suits-as-states-diverge)). Because advocates calculate the fees can translate to APRs averaging nearly 400%, the industry's fee-based, "0% interest" framing has become the central legal battleground ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bgov.com/banking-law/fintechs-cash-advances-for-workers-draw-suits-as-states-diverge)).
