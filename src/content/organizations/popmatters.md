## Overview

**PopMatters** is an international online magazine of cultural criticism founded in 1999 by **Sarah Zupko** — an independently owned outlet built, in its own telling, "without corporate sponsorship or financial backing" ([PopMatters](https://www.popmatters.com/pm-at-5-index-2496026450.html)). It publishes reviews, interviews and essays across music, film, TV, books, comics, games and more ([Editor & Publisher](https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/partnership-for-mcclatchy-and-popmatterscom,37535)). In May 2021 it published the in-depth interview "Director Adam Rehmeier on His Abrasive Punk Comedy with a Sweet Heart, 'Dinner in America,'" the source later used for Rehmeier's filmography ([PopMatters](https://www.popmatters.com/adam-rehmeier-interview-dinner-america)).

## Founding and independent ownership

PopMatters was founded in 1999 by Sarah Zupko, whom **Longreads** identifies as having "founded *PopMatters* in 1999 to cover all forms of culture," placing it alongside **Pitchfork** (founded 1996 by **Ryan Schreiber**) among the digital-native publications that captured the readership migrating online in the late 1990s ([Longreads](https://longreads.com/2018/12/27/where-have-all-the-music-magazines-gone/?curator=MusicREDEF)). Zupko — a former Executive Producer for **Tribune Media Services**, with a BA from the **University of Chicago** (musicology, film, art) and an MA from the **University of Texas** (media theory, journalism) — calls west London home and was still publishing bylines on the site in June 2026 ([PopMatters](https://www.popmatters.com/author/sarahzupko)). Ownership is the point: the site's fifth-anniversary retrospective describes PopMatters as beginning "as the singular vision of its founder, Sarah Zupko" and built "from the ground up by her dedication and drive, without corporate sponsorship or financial backing" ([PopMatters](https://www.popmatters.com/pm-at-5-index-2496026450.html)). No parent company or outside owner appears in any source read for this profile.

## Editorial scope and reach

The magazine's tagline is "essaying the pop culture that matters since 1999," and its scope is deliberately wide: music, television, films, books, video games, comics, sports, theater, visual arts, travel and the internet, in reviews, interviews and detailed essays ([PopMatters](https://www.popmatters.com/); [Muck Rack](https://muckrack.com/media-outlet/popmatters)). After five years it had published over 12,000 articles, reviews, interviews and columns and reached upwards of 750,000 monthly visitors — a remarkable scale for an outlet built without corporate backing ([PopMatters](https://www.popmatters.com/pm-at-5-index-2496026450.html)). Its distribution extended beyond the site itself through a content-and-distribution partnership with **McClatchy-Tribune Information Services**: PopMatters stories ran on **MCT Campus**, a wire service reaching more than 1,000 college and high school newspapers, while MCTIS distributed content from 60-plus newspapers to about 1,200 clients worldwide ([Editor & Publisher](https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/partnership-for-mcclatchy-and-popmatterscom,37535)). The 1,000-newspaper campus wire is the number that counts: it put PopMatters' criticism in front of student readers nationally, a reach few independent culture sites of the era achieved.

## The 2021 Rehmeier interview

In May 2021 PopMatters published "Director Adam Rehmeier on His Abrasive Punk Comedy with a Sweet Heart, 'Dinner in America'" — a long-form interview in which Rehmeier discusses his filmmaking process, his musician-first creative identity, and the DIY ethic of his home-recording years ([PopMatters](https://www.popmatters.com/adam-rehmeier-interview-dinner-america)). The interview's framing establishes his filmography: *Dinner in America* (2020), written, directed and edited by Rehmeier and produced by **Ben Stiller** and **Ross Putman**, followed his feature debut, the horror film *The Bunny Game* (2011), plus *Jonas* (2013) and the documentary *Land Grab* (2016), which he produced and edited ([PopMatters](https://www.popmatters.com/adam-rehmeier-interview-dinner-america)). Rehmeier calls himself a filmmaker rather than merely a director or writer — "I do all of the elements" — quoting **François Truffaut** on the three films every project becomes (the written, the shot, the edited), and traces the Simon character to a 2006 walk in the snow ([PopMatters](https://www.popmatters.com/adam-rehmeier-interview-dinner-america)). The piece is why the interview later served as a filmography source: it is one of the few places his full credit list appears in his own words.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who founded PopMatters and when?**
Sarah Zupko founded PopMatters in 1999 as an online magazine of cultural criticism. Longreads places it among the late-1990s digital-native publications that captured readers migrating online, and the site's own retrospective calls it the singular vision of its founder, built by her dedication without corporate sponsorship.

**What does PopMatters cover?**
It publishes reviews, interviews and detailed essays on music, television, films, books, video games, comics, sports, theater, visual arts, travel and the internet. Its tagline is "essaying the pop culture that matters since 1999," and it describes itself as an international magazine of cultural criticism.

**Who owns PopMatters?**
It is independently owned. The site's fifth-anniversary retrospective states it was built "without corporate sponsorship or financial backing" as founder Sarah Zupko's singular vision. No parent company or outside owner appears in any source read for this profile, and Zupko remains active on the site.

**What was the 2021 Rehmeier interview?**
"Director Adam Rehmeier on His Abrasive Punk Comedy with a Sweet Heart, 'Dinner in America'" — a May 2021 long-form interview covering his filmmaking process, his musician-first creative identity and DIY home-recording background. Its framing lays out his filmography from *The Bunny Game* (2011) through *Dinner in America* (2020).

**How widely is PopMatters distributed?**
Beyond the site, it partnered with McClatchy-Tribune Information Services: PopMatters stories ran on MCT Campus, a wire reaching more than 1,000 college and high school newspapers, and in MCTIS packages distributed to about 1,200 clients worldwide, per Editor & Publisher.
