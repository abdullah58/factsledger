## Overview

Moonshot AI is a Beijing-based artificial intelligence company founded in March 2023 by Yang Zhilin, Zhou Xinyu and Wu Yuxin that builds the Kimi family of large language models and the Kimi chatbot ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/21/moonshot-ai-funding-china/)). It rose from a long-context research bet to one of China's most valuable private AI companies, raising about $2 billion at a roughly $20 billion valuation in May 2026, and it released the trillion-parameter open-weight model Kimi K2 in July 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/07/chinas-moonshot-ai-raises-2b-at-20b-valuation-as-demand-for-open-source-ai-skyrockets/)). Its models compete with [OpenAI](/organizations/openai/)'s ChatGPT, [Google](/organizations/google/)'s Gemini and [Anthropic](/organizations/anthropic/)'s Claude, as well as Chinese rivals including ByteDance's Doubao, Alibaba's Qwen, Zhipu's Z.ai and DeepSeek ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/07/chinas-moonshot-ai-raises-2b-at-20b-valuation-as-demand-for-open-source-ai-skyrockets/)).

## The long-context bet

Moonshot's founding idea was narrow and technical: build large language models that can process unusually long inputs of text and data, an area its founders believed had long outfoxed other labs ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/21/moonshot-ai-funding-china/)). The company launched in March 2023 — timed to the 50th anniversary of Pink Floyd's *The Dark Side of the Moon*, founder Yang Zhilin's favourite album and the inspiration for the company's name — with a 100-billion-parameter large language model ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/21/moonshot-ai-funding-china/)). Yang brought a research pedigree to the bet: a computer science PhD from Carnegie Mellon University, work at [Google Brain](/organizations/google-brain/) and Meta AI, and a key authorship role on Transformer-XL, an architecture for understanding language beyond a fixed-length context ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/21/moonshot-ai-funding-china/)).

The first product arrived fast. In October 2023 Moonshot launched Kimi, its first chatbot, which it said could process 200,000 Chinese characters in a single conversation — presented at the time as roughly eight times what OpenAI's GPT-4-32K could manage ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/21/moonshot-ai-funding-china/)). The long-context focus was aimed at use cases other chatbots handled poorly, such as legal documents, fiction writing and deeper financial analysis ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/21/moonshot-ai-funding-china/)). Early capital followed the same logic: before its big Series B, Moonshot had already raised $200 million from HongShan and ZhenFund at a $300 million valuation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/21/moonshot-ai-funding-china/)).

## Kimi K2 and the open-weight pivot

Moonshot's defining moment came in July 2025, when it released Kimi K2, a one-trillion-parameter open model that outperformed OpenAI's GPT-4.1 on several agentic-coding benchmarks ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/11/openai-delays-the-release-of-its-open-model-again/)). The model uses a mixture-of-experts architecture with 32 billion active parameters and 1 trillion total parameters, was pre-trained on 15.5 trillion tokens using the MuonClip optimizer, and was released with its base and post-trained checkpoints openly available ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20534)). Its published benchmarks put it at the top of open models for agentic work: 66.1 on Tau2-Bench, 65.8 on SWE-Bench Verified and 47.3 on SWE-Bench Multilingual, alongside 53.7 on LiveCodeBench v6, 49.5 on AIME 2025 and 75.1 on GPQA-Diamond ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20534)).

The K2 line kept moving. On 27 January 2026 Moonshot released Kimi K2.5, a natively multimodal model — understanding text, image and video — trained on 15 trillion mixed visual and text tokens, which the company said beat Gemini 3 Pro on the SWE-Bench Verified coding benchmark and GPT-5.2 and Claude Opus 4.5 on video understanding ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/27/chinas-moonshot-releases-a-new-open-source-model-kimi-k2-5-and-a-coding-agent/)). Alongside it came Kimi Code, an open coding tool for terminals and editors such as VSCode, Cursor and Zed, pitched against Anthropic's Claude Code and Google's Gemini CLI ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/27/chinas-moonshot-releases-a-new-open-source-model-kimi-k2-5-and-a-coding-agent/)). By May 2026 the follow-up Kimi K2.6 was the second-most used large language model on the distribution platform OpenRouter ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/07/chinas-moonshot-ai-raises-2b-at-20b-valuation-as-demand-for-open-source-ai-skyrockets/)). The next model, Kimi K3, was reported by the *Financial Times* in July 2026 to be expected to perform at par with or better than Anthropic's Opus 4.8 and to be the largest open-weight model from China, at between 2 trillion and 3 trillion parameters ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/16/moonshots-upcoming-kimi-3-is-expected-to-close-the-gap-with-anthropics-opus-4-8/)).

## Funding: the fastest climb in China's AI race

No Chinese AI startup raised money faster than Moonshot in its first three years. In February 2024, less than a year after founding, it raised over $1 billion in a Series B at a $2.5 billion valuation — then the largest single funding round for a Chinese large language model developer on public record ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/21/moonshot-ai-funding-china/)). E-commerce giant Alibaba and HongShan co-led the round, according to the *South China Morning Post*, with Meituan and Xiaohongshu also participating; Bloomberg later reported that Alibaba led the round with Monolith Management participating ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/21/moonshot-ai-funding-china/)). The investor list read as a map of China's internet giants spreading bets across the country's large language model contenders: Alibaba was also backing Baichuan, Zhipu AI and 01.AI, while its rival [Tencent](/organizations/tencent/) backed Baichuan, Zhipu, MiniMax and Light Years Beyond ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/21/moonshot-ai-funding-china/)).

The repricing then accelerated sharply. Moonshot was valued at $4.3 billion at the end of 2025, and by early 2026 that figure had more than doubled to $10 billion following a $700 million raise ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/07/chinas-moonshot-ai-raises-2b-at-20b-valuation-as-demand-for-open-source-ai-skyrockets/)). In May 2026 the company raised about $2 billion at a $20 billion valuation, led by Meituan's venture arm Long-Z Investments, with Tsinghua Capital, China Mobile and CPE Yuanfeng participating — taking its fundraising over the previous six months to $3.9 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/07/chinas-moonshot-ai-raises-2b-at-20b-valuation-as-demand-for-open-source-ai-skyrockets/)). Annual recurring revenue had topped $200 million in April 2026, driven by paid subscriptions and API usage ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/07/chinas-moonshot-ai-raises-2b-at-20b-valuation-as-demand-for-open-source-ai-skyrockets/)). Its backers by then included Alibaba, Tencent, HongShan, ZhenFund, IDG Capital and 5Y Capital ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/07/chinas-moonshot-ai-raises-2b-at-20b-valuation-as-demand-for-open-source-ai-skyrockets/)). Every one of these figures is a press report about a private company, not a disclosure from audited financials — and later rounds were reported at still higher valuations that disagree between outlets, as the open items below record.

## Scrutiny and the road to listing

Moonshot's rise has drawn the kind of scrutiny that follows any lab closing on the frontier. In February 2026, Anthropic accused Moonshot AI, DeepSeek and MiniMax of setting up more than 24,000 fake accounts with its Claude model to improve their own models, generating more than 16 million exchanges through a technique called distillation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/23/anthropic-accuses-chinese-ai-labs-of-mining-claude-as-us-debates-ai-chip-exports/)). Moonshot specifically was tracked at more than 3.4 million exchanges targeting agentic reasoning and tool use, coding and data analysis, computer-use agent development and computer vision ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/23/anthropic-accuses-chinese-ai-labs-of-mining-claude-as-us-debates-ai-chip-exports/)). Anthropic framed the practice as a national-security concern, arguing that models built through illicit distillation were unlikely to retain the safeguards of the originals ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/23/anthropic-accuses-chinese-ai-labs-of-mining-claude-as-us-debates-ai-chip-exports/)). The allegation remains exactly that — an allegation by a competitor — and no response from Moonshot is recorded in the source reporting it.

At the same time, Moonshot moved toward the public markets. In September 2026 it confidentially filed for a [Hong Kong](/places/hong-kong/) initial public offering that could raise about $3 billion, sources told Reuters, as competition for capital intensified among China's fast-growing AI developers ([Reuters via Invezz](https://invezz.com/ie/news/2026/09/03/chinas-moonshot-ai-files-for-hong-kong-ipo-seeks-dollar3-billion-report/)). TechNode separately reported that Moonshot had lodged a confidential A1 application with the [Hong Kong Stock Exchange](/organizations/hong-kong-stock-exchange/) that week ([Reuters via Invezz](https://invezz.com/ie/news/2026/09/03/chinas-moonshot-ai-files-for-hong-kong-ipo-seeks-dollar3-billion-report/)). The filing followed the release of Kimi K3, and came after a July financing reported at about $30 billion, with further fundraising talks said to be targeting as much as $50 billion ([Reuters via Invezz](https://invezz.com/ie/news/2026/09/03/chinas-moonshot-ai-files-for-hong-kong-ipo-seeks-dollar3-billion-report/)). Whether public investors will pay a multibillion-dollar price for Kimi's technical momentum is the open question the listing will answer.
