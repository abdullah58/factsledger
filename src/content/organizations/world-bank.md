## Overview

The World Bank is the international development lender created at the Bretton Woods Conference in 1944 — strictly, the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD) and the International Development Association (IDA), which together lend to governments of developing countries. Headquartered in Washington, D.C., [United States](/places/united-states/), it is the largest source of financial assistance to developing countries, and it also provides technical assistance and policy advice ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/World-Bank)). It is affiliated with the [United Nations](/organizations/united-nations/) but is not accountable to the General Assembly or the Security Council ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/World-Bank)).

The wider World Bank Group adds three more institutions — the International Finance Corporation (IFC), the Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency (MIGA) and the International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes (ICSID) — and the Group's stated vision is "a world free of poverty on a livable planet" ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2024/12/05/donors-and-world-bank-group-boost-ida-development)). It is not the International Monetary Fund: the two were born at the same conference but do different jobs, and this profile keeps them apart throughout.

## From reconstruction to development

In July 1944, delegates from 44 countries met at the Mount Washington Hotel in Bretton Woods, New Hampshire, to build a postwar economic order, and the intellectual leaders were John Maynard Keynes, adviser to the British Treasury, and Harry Dexter White of the US Treasury ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/about/history)). The conference created two institutions, but the Bank was almost an afterthought: most of the time went to the IMF's commission, and the Bank's work took only the last few days, its Articles of Agreement primarily drafted by Keynes ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/about/history)). The Bank began operations in June 1946, its first loans geared toward rebuilding western Europe, and from the mid-1950s it turned to infrastructure in developing countries — roads, hydroelectric dams, water and sewage systems, ports and airports ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/World-Bank)).

The Bank's character changed under Robert S. McNamara, president from 1968 to 1981. By the 1970s over 40% of people in developing countries lived in absolute poverty; McNamara coined the term in his 1973 Annual Meeting speech and set the twin goals of accelerating growth and reducing poverty ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/about/history)). Lending rose twelve-fold between 1968 and 1981 and spread into environment, rural development, water, sanitation and education — the first environment loan, for pollution control in [Brazil](/places/brazil/), came in 1971 ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/about/history)). The 1980s debt crisis then pushed the Bank into structural adjustment loans with policy conditions such as fiscal discipline and trade liberalisation, whose effectiveness was debated inside the Bank and among its clients ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/about/history)). In the 1990s it helped former Soviet nations redirect their economies, established the Global Environment Facility in 1991, and approved the Heavily Indebted Poor Countries initiative in 1996 ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/about/history)).

## Five institutions — and who governs them

"World Bank" means the IBRD and IDA; "World Bank Group" means all five institutions, each with a distinct job. The IBRD lends at market rates to middle-income and creditworthy lower-income countries, raising most of its money on world capital markets, while the IDA, founded in 1960, gives interest-free long-term loans, technical assistance and policy advice to low-income countries, funded by contributions from developed countries ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/World-Bank)). The IFC finances private business in developing countries alongside private investors, MIGA insures foreign investors against noncommercial risks, and ICSID settles investment disputes between foreign investors and host countries by conciliation or arbitration ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/World-Bank)).

| Institution | Founded | What it does | Who it serves |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IBRD | 1944 | Loans, guarantees and advice at market rates | Middle-income and creditworthy low-income governments |
| IDA | 1960 | Concessional loans and grants | The poorest countries (78 countries) |
| IFC | 1956 | Loans, guarantees and equity for business | Private sector in developing countries |
| MIGA | 1988 | Political-risk insurance and guarantees | Foreign investors and lenders |
| ICSID | — | Conciliation and arbitration | Foreign investors and host countries |

Governance is weighted by money, not one-country-one-vote. The Board of Governors — every member, usually its finance minister or central bank governor — meets annually, but day-to-day power sits with 25 Executive Directors ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11361)). The six largest contributors — [China](/places/china/), [France](/places/france/), Germany, [Japan](/places/japan/), the [United Kingdom](/places/united-kingdom/) and the United States — each appoint their own director ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11361)). Major policy decisions, including amending the Articles of Agreement, need 85% of the vote, and the United States holds 16.07% of IBRD votes — the only member above 15%, giving it a veto over such decisions ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11361)). The president is chosen by the IBRD Board for a five-year, renewable term and has always been a US citizen nominated by the United States; Ajay Banga began his term in June 2023 ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11361)).

## Today's scale, the IDA21 record — and what the Bank is not

In fiscal 2025 the IBRD and IDA approved $40.8 billion and $39.9 billion respectively in new projects, with Africa, Europe and Central Asia, and Latin America and the Caribbean the top recipient regions ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11361)). The IBRD's firepower rests on $327 billion of capital — $304 billion of it callable guarantees from member governments, $23 billion paid in — and it has never drawn on its callable capital ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11361)). The scale matters because it is what lets the Bank lend where private capital will not go: in December 2024 donors agreed IDA21, a record $100 billion replenishment in which $23.7 billion of pledges from 59 countries was leveraged into $100 billion of financing for 78 low-income countries, home to 1.9 billion people ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2024/12/05/donors-and-world-bank-group-boost-ida-development)). Since 1960 IDA has provided $533 billion to 115 countries ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2024/12/05/donors-and-world-bank-group-boost-ida-development)).

In fiscal 2025 the IBRD and IDA approved $40.8 billion and $39.9 billion in new projects ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11361)) — and because IDA21 turns each donor dollar into roughly four dollars of financing, the replenishment, not the annual lending alone, is the measure of how far donor money now stretches. The Bank's reach also shows up in unexpected places: [Colleges and Institutes Canada](/organizations/colleges-and-institutes-canada/)'s funding diversified through the World Bank from 1984, alongside the [Asian Development Bank](/organizations/asian-development-bank/). But the Bank is not the IMF, and confusing the two misstates both: the IMF gives short- and medium-term loans to countries in balance-of-payments trouble, funded mainly by member quotas, while the Bank gives long-term finance for schools, water, electricity, disease control and environmental protection, funded by contributions and bonds ([International Monetary Fund](https://www.imf.org/en/About/Factsheets/Sheets/2022/IMF-World-Bank-New)). Nor is it a commercial bank, or a charity: it lends, it charges interest, and its effectiveness is contested — critics say its bureaucracy burdens borrowers, its projects are insufficiently evaluated and its lending crowds out private investment, while defenders point to basic necessities the private sector would not finance and to the research and data it produces ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11361)).

## FAQ

### What is the World Bank?

The World Bank is the international development lender founded at Bretton Woods in 1944 — strictly, the IBRD and IDA, which lend to governments of developing countries. Headquartered in Washington, D.C., it is the largest source of financial assistance to developing countries, and also gives technical assistance and policy advice.

### What is the difference between the World Bank and the World Bank Group?

"World Bank" means just two institutions, the IBRD and IDA, which lend to governments. The "World Bank Group" adds three more: the IFC, which finances private business; MIGA, which insures investors against political risk; and ICSID, which settles investment disputes.

### How is the World Bank different from the IMF?

Both were created at Bretton Woods in 1944, but the IMF handles short- and medium-term balance-of-payments crises and macroeconomic stability, funded by member quotas. The World Bank finances long-term development — schools, water, electricity, disease control — funded by member contributions and bonds.

### Who runs the World Bank, and who holds the power?

A Board of Governors meets annually, but 25 Executive Directors run it day to day. Voting is weighted by financial contribution: the United States holds 16.07% of IBRD votes, the only member above 15%, giving it a veto over major decisions, which need 85%. The president has always been a US citizen nominated by the United States.

### What was the IDA21 replenishment?

In December 2024 donors agreed a record $100 billion replenishment of IDA, the Bank's fund for 78 low-income countries. Donors pledged $23.7 billion, which leveraging turned into $100 billion of financing, with policy requirements cut from 1,100 to 500.

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not settle the founding year: Britannica and the World Bank say 1944, while the Congressional Research Service says the Bank was established in 1945. The profile uses 1944 and records the disagreement above.
- It does not assert a founding year for ICSID, a first-loan figure for France in 1947, or an ordinal ("14th president") for Ajay Banga — none was verified from a fully-read source.
- It does not claim the United States' 16.68% share of IBRD capital is its voting share; the voting share is 16.07%, a different measure, and the profile keeps them separate.
- It does not present the structural-adjustment record as settled: the Bank's own history says the effectiveness of those loans was debated, and Britannica records that they frequently increased poverty and unemployment in borrowing countries.
- It does not claim IDA membership or the US voting share are fixed figures: the latest Congressional Research Service edition gives 175 IDA members and a 16.07% US IBRD vote, against 174 and 15.84% in its January 2025 edition.
- It does not treat the Bank as a commercial bank, a charity, or part of the IMF: it is a member-owned development lender, and the IMF is a separate institution with a different mandate.
