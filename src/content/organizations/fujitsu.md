## Overview

Fujitsu is a Tokyo-headquartered Japanese IT services and technology company, established in June 1935 as Fuji Tsushinki Manufacturing, a subsidiary of Fuji Electric ([ZDNet](https://www.zdnet.com/article/fujitsu/)). It grew into one of Japan's defining technology firms — systems integration, servers, enterprise computing. But for a quarter-century its name in Britain has been inseparable from the Post Office Horizon scandal: the flawed branch-accounting software it built and ran sat at the heart of the most widespread miscarriage of justice in British history, first exposed by [Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366643515/Fujitsu-finally-thrown-out-of-Post-Office-in-500m-Horizon-replacement-deals) in 2009.

## The Horizon contract: £2.5 billion over 25 years

The Post Office Horizon contract is believed to have been Fujitsu's most lucrative ever contract in the UK, earning the company more than £2.5 billion over its 25-plus-year duration ([Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366643515/Fujitsu-finally-thrown-out-of-Post-Office-in-500m-Horizon-replacement-deals)). Between 1999 and 2015 the Post Office prosecuted almost 1,000 subpostmasters based on unreliable data from Horizon, brushing aside concerns about the faulty software until a 2019 High Court case exposed the scandal; hundreds of convictions have since been overturned ([The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/crime/article/post-office-horizon-fujitsu-sub-postmasters-funding-j2kmztj0z)). Nearly 1,000 subpostmasters were wrongly convicted after Horizon recorded phantom accounting errors caused by software bugs, and a further 10,000 are eligible to claim compensation after the Post Office forced them to make good on those phantom losses out of their own pockets ([Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366643515/Fujitsu-finally-thrown-out-of-Post-Office-in-500m-Horizon-replacement-deals)).

Remarkably, the scandal never stopped the cheques. After a failed in-house replacement whose budget ballooned from £180 million to £1.1 billion, the Post Office kept extending Fujitsu's contract — a £41 million one-year bridging extension for April 2026 to March 2027 was awarded in November 2025 ([Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366634300/Post-Office-extends-controversial-Fujitsu-contract-in-41m-deal)). Horizon continued to produce erratic figures the Post Office could not explain: in two years subpostmasters identified more than 16,000 discrepancies, and the taxpayer-owned business wrote off £11.6 million and £10.4 million in unidentified shortfalls in consecutive years ([Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366634300/Post-Office-extends-controversial-Fujitsu-contract-in-41m-deal)). Fujitsu's own European boss told the public inquiry that parts of Horizon were so old he did not want to turn them off for fear of what would happen.

## The exit and the unpaid bill

In May 2026 the Post Office finally signed £500 million in contracts ending Fujitsu's quarter-century on Horizon: Accenture won a £322.8 million contract to take over running the system on a "walk in, take over" basis, while US retail specialist One View Commerce won £169.2 million to replace Horizon with cloud-hosted software across the branch network ([Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366643515/Fujitsu-finally-thrown-out-of-Post-Office-in-500m-Horizon-replacement-deals)). The Post Office's chief technology officer said Fujitsu would be out eight to twelve months after signing, and that by 2030 "we will have eradicated what we now know as Horizon completely from our estate" ([Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366643515/Fujitsu-finally-thrown-out-of-Post-Office-in-500m-Horizon-replacement-deals)).

Meanwhile the financial reckoning is unresolved. UK taxpayers have funded over £1.5 billion in financial redress to subpostmasters alone, with more to come — yet Fujitsu has not committed to any amount toward the costs and has made no interim payment, despite its then-European boss telling a parliamentary committee in January 2024 that the company was "morally obligated" to contribute ([Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366642879/Fujitsu-UK-pays-staff-bonuses-as-it-sits-on-Post-Office-scandal-contribution)). In May 2026 it emerged Fujitsu would pay bonuses to UK staff for the second year running while still sitting on that contribution ([Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366642879/Fujitsu-UK-pays-staff-bonuses-as-it-sits-on-Post-Office-scandal-contribution)). The criminal investigation, Operation Olympos, has identified at least 53 "persons of interest" at the Post Office and Fujitsu — which installed and ran Horizon — with detectives considering charges including perjury and perverting the course of justice, though funding shortfalls may push charging decisions to 2033 ([The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/crime/article/post-office-horizon-fujitsu-sub-postmasters-funding-j2kmztj0z)). The public inquiry's chairman found postmasters suffered "disastrous consequences" from the "wholly unacceptable behaviour" of the Post Office and Fujitsu ([The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/crime/article/post-office-horizon-fujitsu-sub-postmasters-funding-j2kmztj0z)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not state any individual Fujitsu employee's criminal liability — Operation Olympos has named suspects under caution but no charges have been brought, and the profile reports only what the cited investigations state. It does not give a precise figure for Fujitsu's unpaid contribution, because none has been committed or disclosed. The £2.5 billion lifetime Horizon earnings figure is reported as Computer Weekly's estimate ("believed to be"), not an audited total. It does not cover Fujitsu's Japanese domestic business or its Uvance/AI strategy, which the citable sources did not address.
