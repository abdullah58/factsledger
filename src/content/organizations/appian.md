## Overview

Appian Corporation (Nasdaq: APPN) is a low-code automation platform headquartered in McLean, Virginia, that lets enterprises build applications and automate processes with minimal hand-coding. It is the anti-startup of enterprise software: founded in the 1999 dot-com boom by Matt Calkins, it stayed private for 18 years while deliberately avoiding venture capital — raising just $10 million of primary capital — then went public in May 2017 as the first low-code IPO ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/24/appian-prices-application-software-ipo-at-12/); [Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/business-transformation/appian-ceo-sees-low-code-as-digital-transformation-key)). A quarter-century in, the AI wave has finally carried it to GAAP profitability: Q3 2025 brought $187 million in revenue and $7.8 million in net income ([TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/tradingview:3374d93eaccd1:0-appian-corp-sec-10-q-report/)).

## Eighteen years private, by design

Calkins was a 26-year-old "paper millionaire" working at a hot startup when he left — before the bubble burst — to found Appian with co-founders in the late 1990s ([Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/business-transformation/appian-ceo-sees-low-code-as-digital-transformation-key)). For the next 18 years he kept the company private and largely shunned venture capital: Novak Biddle Venture Partners' $10 million Series A in 2008 was the only primary venture round, while New Enterprise Associates' $37.5 million investment in 2014 was technically a secondary transaction — shares bought directly from employees ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/24/appian-prices-application-software-ipo-at-12/)).

The company grew the old-fashioned way, from 173 employees at the end of 2011 to 753 by March 2017, serving about 280 customers including Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport, Sprint, Major League Baseball, UPS, AIG and the Defense Department's Defense Information Systems Agency ([Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/business-transformation/appian-ceo-sees-low-code-as-digital-transformation-key)). Calkins describes low-code as working like Lego blocks: IT creates the objects, and anyone in the enterprise can assemble applications from them, while the CIO keeps central control — "dispersed authorship and central management" ([Light Reading](https://www.lightreading.com/business-transformation/appian-ceo-sees-low-code-as-digital-transformation-key)).

## The first low-code IPO — at a down valuation

Appian priced its IPO at $12 a share on May 24, 2017, raising $75 million at the midpoint of its $11–$13 range; shares closed the first day above $15, up about 25%, valuing the company at roughly $900 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/25/app-maker-appian-soars-25-on-first-day-as-a-public-company/)). But the pricing was a comedown: the Wall Street Journal had reported a valuation above $1 billion the prior year — unicorn territory — while the IPO valued Appian closer to $700 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/24/appian-prices-application-software-ipo-at-12/)). Calkins kept the largest stake at 54.7%; Novak Biddle held 21.5% and NEA 11.7% ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/24/appian-prices-application-software-ipo-at-12/)). In its S-1, Appian named [Salesforce](/organizations/salesforce/) and [ServiceNow](/organizations/servicenow/) as its biggest competitors, with IBM and Oracle in adjacent spaces ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/24/appian-prices-application-software-ipo-at-12/)).

## Profitability arrives with AI

Appian spent its public years growing revenue while booking losses — $132.9 million in 2016 revenue against a $12.5 million loss — but the mix shifted toward high-margin cloud subscriptions and AI ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/24/appian-prices-application-software-ipo-at-12/)). Q4 2024 delivered $166.7 million in revenue (up 14.7%), $98.9 million in cloud subscriptions (up 19%), and the company's first GAAP operating income of $5 million ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/data-news/2025/02/19/appian-revenue-exceeds-expectations/?source=iedfolrf0000001)).

The November 2025 10-Q confirmed the turn: Q3 revenue of $187 million (up 21.4%), gross margin of 75.8%, operating income of $13.1 million against a $7.2 million loss a year earlier, and net income of $7.8 million — $0.10 per diluted share ([TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/tradingview:3374d93eaccd1:0-appian-corp-sec-10-q-report/)). Government remained central: agencies contributed 32.4% of revenue, with U.S. federal agencies alone at 25%, and nearly 40% of revenue came from outside the United States ([TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/tradingview:3374d93eaccd1:0-appian-corp-sec-10-q-report/)). The platform now bundles data fabric, robotic process automation, intelligent document processing, generative AI and AI agents with its low-code core, sold partly through consulting partners including Accenture, Deloitte, EY, KPMG and PwC ([TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/tradingview:3374d93eaccd1:0-appian-corp-sec-10-q-report/)).
