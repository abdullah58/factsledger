## Overview

HUM Nutrition is a [Los Angeles](/places/los-angeles/)-based vitamin and supplement company that sells personalized "beauty from within" products — vitamins, minerals, probiotics and botanicals aimed at skin, body and mood — mainly by online subscription. Founded in 2012 by Walter Faulstroh and Chris Coleridge, it was one of the first investments of [CircleUp Growth Partners](/organizations/circleup-growth-partners/)' machine-learning-driven fund, raised a $15 million Series B in 2019, and passed $50 million in revenue in 2021 ([McKinsey](https://uat.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/are-personalized-vitamins-the-future-of-wellness); [LA TechWatch](https://www.latechwatch.com/2019/07/la-startup-funding-q2-2019-top-most-vc/18/)).

HUM is a privately held supplement brand, not a public company. It is not Humana Inc., the Louisville health insurer whose stock trades on the New York Stock Exchange as HUM ([US Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/49071/000004907120000028/form8k02202020.htm)). The shared "HUM" name is a coincidence of branding, not a corporate relationship. HUM sells in the [United States](/places/united-states/) through its own website, [Amazon](/organizations/amazon/), and retailers including Sephora, Nordstrom and Target ([McKinsey](https://uat.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/are-personalized-vitamins-the-future-of-wellness); [NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/01/15/HUM-Nutrition-expands-into-Target-s-Natural-Beauty-aisles/)).

## Founding: from acne to "beauty from within"

The company grew out of Faulstroh's own skin problems. He struggled with acne and breakouts well into his 20s and 30s — three courses of the prescription drug Accutane each cleared his skin until he stopped taking it — and then, while running his first company, a nutritionist helped him bring his skin under control through diet ([The Quality Edit](https://www.thequalityedit.com/articles/walter-faulstroh-hum-nutrition-supplements-interview); [McKinsey](https://uat.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/are-personalized-vitamins-the-future-of-wellness)). That first company was V Water, a vitamin-infused water Faulstroh built into the leading functional water brand in the UK and Ireland before selling it to Pepsi in 2008 ([The Quality Edit](https://www.thequalityedit.com/articles/walter-faulstroh-hum-nutrition-supplements-interview)). The lesson he took from the experience — that nutrition could change his skin, energy and digestion — became the premise of HUM: supplements formulated for specific skin and body concerns, sold with personal guidance rather than off a shelf ([McKinsey](https://uat.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/are-personalized-vitamins-the-future-of-wellness)).

Faulstroh moved to the United States in 2012 to start the company with Coleridge, and built it first as a direct-to-consumer business in Los Angeles, [California](/places/california/) ([The Quality Edit](https://www.thequalityedit.com/articles/walter-faulstroh-hum-nutrition-supplements-interview); [LA TechWatch](https://www.latechwatch.com/2019/07/la-startup-funding-q2-2019-top-most-vc/18/)). The name, by Faulstroh's own account, comes from humming — he says he always feels good when he hums — and from the hummingbird ([The Quality Edit](https://www.thequalityedit.com/articles/walter-faulstroh-hum-nutrition-supplements-interview)). The founders' positioning was deliberate: most vitamins were complicated and generic, so HUM would start from the consumer's concern and have nutrition experts formulate a product around it, with playful names such as Flatter Me and Daily Cleanse ([VC News Daily](https://www.vcnewsdaily.com/access/getarticle.php?aid=kzfwtgpfkr); [McKinsey](https://uat.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/are-personalized-vitamins-the-future-of-wellness)).

## The business: personalization, subscriptions, and retail

HUM's model has three linked parts: a quiz, a nutritionist, and a subscription. A customer takes an online quiz about diet, lifestyle and concerns; HUM recommends products and assigns a personal nutritionist or registered dietitian who can be contacted with questions ([McKinsey](https://uat.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/are-personalized-vitamins-the-future-of-wellness)). The company describes this as a reflection of Faulstroh's own story — he could not have solved his skin concerns, he says, without an expert holding his hand — and it began by offering the nutritionist connection for free ([The Quality Edit](https://www.thequalityedit.com/articles/walter-faulstroh-hum-nutrition-supplements-interview)). A team of registered dietitians supports the model, and Faulstroh describes a three-level approach to education: a high-level message, more detail, and a deep dive for customers who want it ([Glossy](https://www.glossy.co/podcasts/hum-nutritions-walter-faulstroh-we-dont-dumb-down-the-message/)).

That education-first stance also shaped where HUM chose to sell. About two years into the business it partnered with Sephora, which Faulstroh credits with helping educate consumers and build the ingestible-beauty category alongside HUM ([McKinsey](https://uat.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/are-personalized-vitamins-the-future-of-wellness)). By contrast, HUM deliberately stayed out of GNC and Vitamin Shoppe, where Faulstroh said stores offered no education and sold ingredients on price ([Glossy](https://www.glossy.co/podcasts/hum-nutritions-walter-faulstroh-we-dont-dumb-down-the-message/)). The channel strategy is deliberately split by customer habit: the website sells subscriptions only, while Amazon serves Prime members and buyers who are not attached to a subscription ([McKinsey](https://uat.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/are-personalized-vitamins-the-future-of-wellness)).

| Channel | Model | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| humnutrition.com | Subscription only | Quiz-led personalization; personal nutritionist included |
| Amazon | One-off purchase | Targets Prime members and non-subscription buyers |
| Sephora | Prestige retail | Partner since about 2014; category-building role |
| Nordstrom | Prestige retail | Listed among HUM's retail partners by McKinsey |
| Target Natural Beauty | Mass retail | Entered January 2024, in the beauty aisle rather than supplements |

On ingredients, HUM's stated standard is that every ingredient must have clinical research behind it, ideally double-blind, placebo-controlled and randomized — a standard Faulstroh himself calls expensive ([Glossy](https://www.glossy.co/podcasts/hum-nutritions-walter-faulstroh-we-dont-dumb-down-the-message/)). For its Skin Heroes product the company ran a 60-day dermatologist-supervised study and reported that 89% of consumers saw improvement ([Glossy](https://www.glossy.co/podcasts/hum-nutritions-walter-faulstroh-we-dont-dumb-down-the-message/)). Faulstroh has also said HUM was the first brand to conduct double-blind placebo-controlled studies on hair gummies, and that its products are tested by independent third parties such as the Clean Label Project, which pulls products off shelves at random ([NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/01/15/HUM-Nutrition-expands-into-Target-s-Natural-Beauty-aisles/)). These are the company's claims, reported as such; this profile makes no independent claim that any HUM product delivers a particular health effect.

## Funding and the CircleUp connection

HUM's funding story is inseparable from [CircleUp](/organizations/circleup/), the consumer-investment platform whose Growth Partners fund picked HUM as one of its first bets. CircleUp announced the $125 million fund on October 31, 2017, saying its Helio machine-learning platform — which analyzed data on 1.2 million consumer companies — could identify breakout brands before other investors found them ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/10/31/circleup-announced-125-million-venture-fund/)). HUM, described by [TechCrunch](/organizations/techcrunch/) as "beauty vitamins sold at Sephora," was one of the fund's first two investments, alongside meat-snack maker [4505 Chicharrones](/organizations/4505-meats/) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/10/31/circleup-announced-125-million-venture-fund/); [BevNet](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2017/circleup-closes-125m-fund-powered-machine-learning/)). The fund's limited partners included [Temasek](/organizations/temasek/), Euclidean Capital and Annie's Homegrown co-founder John Foraker, and its general partners were Alison Ryu, Ben Lee and CircleUp co-founder and CEO [Ryan Caldbeck](/people/ryan-caldbeck/) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/10/31/circleup-announced-125-million-venture-fund/); [BevNet](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2017/circleup-closes-125m-fund-powered-machine-learning/)). The existing FactsLedger profile of CircleUp records the same sequence, so the two profiles agree.

| Round | Date | Amount | Investors |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Angel rounds | Before 2017 | Undisclosed | Angel investors who had backed the founders' earlier ventures |
| Series A | November 2017 | $5 million | CircleUp Growth Partners, Imaginary Ventures, Strand Equity Partners |
| Series B | April 2019 | $15 million | Sonoma Brands Capital (lead), CircleUp Growth Partners, Imaginary Ventures, Strand Equity Partners |

The Series A, announced in November 2017, was HUM's first institutional round; until then it had been funded by angel investors who had backed the founders in previous ventures ([VC News Daily](https://www.vcnewsdaily.com/access/getarticle.php?aid=kzfwtgpfkr)). The participants were CircleUp Growth Partners, Natalie Massenet and Nick Brown's Imaginary Ventures, and Strand Equity Partners, whose investment was led by co-founder Seth Rodsky ([VC News Daily](https://www.vcnewsdaily.com/access/getarticle.php?aid=kzfwtgpfkr)). The company said at the time that it had already reached profitability and grown at least 30% quarter over quarter for the previous 12 quarters — a company claim, not an audited figure ([VC News Daily](https://www.vcnewsdaily.com/access/getarticle.php?aid=kzfwtgpfkr)). One contemporaneous report gives a different Series A figure: BevNet's fund-launch coverage says HUM "closed a $4 million dollar round with CircleUp Growth Partners as the lead investor" ([BevNet](https://www.bevnet.com/news/2017/circleup-closes-125m-fund-powered-machine-learning/)). This profile uses the company's announced $5 million and flags the $4 million figure in openItems.

The Series B followed in April 2019: $15 million led by Sonoma Brands Capital, joined by the three Series A investors ([LA TechWatch](https://www.latechwatch.com/2019/07/la-startup-funding-q2-2019-top-most-vc/18/)). LA TechWatch, ranking the largest Los Angeles startup rounds of the second quarter of 2019, put HUM's total reported equity funding at $20 million — exactly the two institutional rounds combined, excluding the earlier angel funding ([LA TechWatch](https://www.latechwatch.com/2019/07/la-startup-funding-q2-2019-top-most-vc/18/)). Sonoma still lists HUM Nutrition as a current brand partner, describing it as "a pioneer in the emerging 'beauty from within' trend" selling "personalized vitamins, minerals and botanical offerings" ([Sonoma Brands Capital](https://www.sonomabrands.com/investments/)). No later funding round, acquisition or ownership change appears in the sources read for this profile.

## Growth, revenue, and the Target expansion

By 2022 HUM's customer base had settled into a clear shape: millennials and Gen Z as the core demographic, about 90% female and 10% male, with many customers buying for their whole household ([McKinsey](https://uat.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/are-personalized-vitamins-the-future-of-wellness)). Faulstroh told McKinsey that HUM's revenues passed the $50 million mark in 2021, with online subscriptions making up the bulk of sales ([McKinsey](https://uat.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/are-personalized-vitamins-the-future-of-wellness)). That revenue figure is the founder's statement as reported by McKinsey, not an audited filing — but it is the only revenue figure any independent source attaches to the company. The assortment by then ran to dozens of products across skin, body, mood, hair, digestion, sleep, stress, energy and women's health, typically priced around $26 a bottle ([The Quality Edit](https://www.thequalityedit.com/articles/walter-faulstroh-hum-nutrition-supplements-interview)).

The most recent verified milestone is the move into mass retail. In January 2024 HUM expanded into Target's Natural Beauty aisles — deliberately placed in the beauty section rather than the supplement aisle — with bestsellers Flatter Me, Daily Cleanse and Private Party plus the newly launched Hormone Balance ([NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/01/15/HUM-Nutrition-expands-into-Target-s-Natural-Beauty-aisles/)). Faulstroh framed the move as a data decision: HUM's direct-to-consumer data showed a strong overlap between its customers and Target's beauty shoppers ([NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/01/15/HUM-Nutrition-expands-into-Target-s-Natural-Beauty-aisles/)). He described the brand's direction as women's wellness "from first period to conception, to perimenopause and menopause," with continued retail expansion planned ([NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/01/15/HUM-Nutrition-expands-into-Target-s-Natural-Beauty-aisles/)). Sustainability runs alongside the growth story: HUM bottles use Prevented Ocean Plastic, and Faulstroh said in January 2024 that the switch had kept the equivalent of more than 23 million water bottles from reaching the ocean ([NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2024/01/15/HUM-Nutrition-expands-into-Target-s-Natural-Beauty-aisles/); [McKinsey](https://uat.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/are-personalized-vitamins-the-future-of-wellness)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who founded HUM Nutrition?** Walter Faulstroh and Chris Coleridge founded HUM Nutrition in Los Angeles in 2012. Faulstroh, the chief executive, had previously built and sold the UK vitamin-water brand V Water to Pepsi in 2008; the idea for HUM came from a nutritionist helping him control his adult acne through diet ([LA TechWatch](https://www.latechwatch.com/2019/07/la-startup-funding-q2-2019-top-most-vc/18/); [The Quality Edit](https://www.thequalityedit.com/articles/walter-faulstroh-hum-nutrition-supplements-interview)).

**What does HUM Nutrition sell?** Personalized vitamins, minerals, probiotics and botanical supplements for skin, body, mood, hair, digestion, sleep, stress, energy and women's health. Customers take an online quiz, receive product recommendations, and are assigned a personal nutritionist; the products come in gummy and pill form ([McKinsey](https://uat.mckinsey.com/industries/consumer-packaged-goods/our-insights/are-personalized-vitamins-the-future-of-wellness); [The Quality Edit](https://www.thequalityedit.com/articles/walter-faulstroh-hum-nutrition-supplements-interview)).

**Who invested in HUM Nutrition?** CircleUp Growth Partners, Imaginary Ventures and Strand Equity Partners backed the $5 million Series A in November 2017, and Sonoma Brands Capital led the $15 million Series B in April 2019, joined by the same three investors. HUM was one of the first two investments of CircleUp's $125 million Growth Partners fund ([VC News Daily](https://www.vcnewsdaily.com/access/getarticle.php?aid=kzfwtgpfkr); [LA TechWatch](https://www.latechwatch.com/2019/07/la-startup-funding-q2-2019-top-most-vc/18/)).

**Is HUM Nutrition the same company as Humana (NYSE: HUM)?** No. HUM Nutrition is a privately held Los Angeles supplement brand. The ticker HUM belongs to Humana Inc., a Louisville, Kentucky health insurer listed on the New York Stock Exchange ([US Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/49071/000004907120000028/form8k02202020.htm)).
