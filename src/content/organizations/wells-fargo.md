## Overview

Wells Fargo & Company is a San Francisco-based multinational financial services company and the fourth-largest bank in the [United States](/places/united-states/) by total assets, with about $1.9 trillion in assets at the end of 2024 ([Wells Fargo & Company](https://stocklight.com/stocks/us/nyse-wfc/wells-fargo/annual-reports/nyse-wfc-2025-10K-25664055.pdf)). Founded in 1852 as a Gold Rush express and banking business, it grew out of the stagecoach era, survived the 2016 fake-accounts scandal and a seven-year Federal Reserve asset cap, and led [Axoni](/organizations/axoni/)'s 2016 blockchain Series A.

The bank's story is really two stories spliced together: a 19th-century express company whose Concord stagecoach became an American icon, and a 20th-century Minnesota bank holding company — Norwest — that bought the Wells Fargo name in 1998 and kept it ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/69/Wells-Fargo-Company.html)). Today it employs about 217,000 people, holds $1.4 trillion in deposits, and runs its business through four segments: consumer banking and lending, commercial banking, corporate and investment banking, and wealth and investment management ([Wells Fargo & Company](https://stocklight.com/stocks/us/nyse-wfc/wells-fargo/annual-reports/nyse-wfc-2025-10K-25664055.pdf)).

## From gold dust to stagecoaches

Henry Wells and William George Fargo, who had earlier helped establish the American Express Company, founded Wells, Fargo & Company in March 1852 to serve the California Gold Rush, buying, selling and transporting gold dust, bullion and specie from the West Coast east by ship across the Isthmus of Panama ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Wells-Fargo-American-corporation)). Organized March 18, 1852 with $300,000 of capital, it opened its first San Francisco office that July ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/wells-fargo-company-0)). It survived the 1855 California banking panic, reopening days after a run cost a third of its net worth ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/wells-fargo-company-0)). After 1855 it expanded into staging, ran the western leg of the Pony Express from Salt Lake City to San Francisco during its final six months in 1861, and in 1866 consolidated nearly every western stagecoach line under its name, creating the world's largest stagecoach empire ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Wells-Fargo-American-corporation)). The horse-drawn Concord stagecoach became its enduring symbol, and its green treasure box, guarded by an armed agent — once Wyatt Earp — became legend ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Wells-Fargo-American-corporation)).

## Becoming a national bank: Norwest, Wachovia and the crisis years

In 1905 the banking business split from the express operation and merged with Nevada National Bank, beginning a chain of mergers — Union Trust in 1923, American Trust in 1960 — that produced the modern Wells Fargo Bank, while the express side faded after its domestic operations were absorbed by American Railway Express in 1918 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Wells-Fargo-American-corporation)). The bank grew by acquisition: it bought Crocker National for about $1.1 billion in 1986 ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/wells-fargo-company-0)) and First Interstate Bancorp in 1996 ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/69/Wells-Fargo-Company.html)). The decisive turn came in 1998, when Minneapolis-based Norwest Corporation acquired the original Wells Fargo and took its name, so today's bank is legally Norwest renamed ([Reference for Business](https://www.referenceforbusiness.com/history2/69/Wells-Fargo-Company.html)). A decade later, on October 3, 2008, Wells Fargo agreed to buy Wachovia for about $15.1 billion in stock, with no government assistance, beating Citigroup's $2.16 billion FDIC-backed bid; the deal closed at year-end and more than doubled the bank's size ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/36995/000089882208000944/exhibit991.htm); [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/story/12019698/1/wells-fargo-sails-through-the-storm.html)).

## Scandal, the asset cap and the road back

The modern bank's defining episode began in September 2016, when the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau fined it $100 million — the largest penalty the bureau had ever imposed — after employees secretly opened more than two million deposit and credit card accounts to hit sales targets; $35 million went to the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency and $50 million to Los Angeles ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/consumer-financial-protection-bureau-fines-wells-fargo-100-million-widespread-illegal-practice-secretly-opening-unauthorized-accounts/)). In February 2018 the Federal Reserve imposed an unprecedented $1.95 trillion cap on the bank's assets ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/fed-lifts-wells-fargos-asset-cap-citing-progress-from-sales-scandal/)). The cap held for seven years, during which [JPMorgan Chase](/organizations/jpmorgan-chase/)'s assets grew by nearly $2 trillion — a measure of the growth the cap cost Wells Fargo — until the Fed lifted it unanimously on June 3, 2025, citing substantial progress on governance and risk controls; CEO Charlie Scharf called the decision a pivotal milestone ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/fed-lifts-wells-fargos-asset-cap-citing-progress-from-sales-scandal/)). In December 2016 the bank had also led Axoni's $18 million Series A alongside ICAP's [Euclid Opportunities](/organizations/euclid-opportunities/) to fund blockchain post-trade technology ([Financial Adviser Magazine](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/vc-deal-of-the-day-axioni-raises-18m-series-a-46944.html?print)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was Wells Fargo founded?**
Wells Fargo was organized on March 18, 1852, in San Francisco by Henry Wells and William George Fargo, with $300,000 of capital, to provide express and banking services during the California Gold Rush ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/wells-fargo-company-0)). Its first office opened that July.

**Why is Wells Fargo associated with stagecoaches?**
After the Gold Rush the company expanded into staging, ran the western leg of the Pony Express in 1861, and in 1866 consolidated nearly all western stagecoach lines under its name, creating the largest stagecoach empire in the world ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Wells-Fargo-American-corporation)). The Concord stagecoach remains its symbol.

**What was the fake-accounts scandal?**
Spurred by sales targets and bonuses, Wells Fargo employees secretly opened more than two million deposit and credit card accounts without customers' knowledge, sometimes charging fees on them ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/consumer-financial-protection-bureau-fines-wells-fargo-100-million-widespread-illegal-practice-secretly-opening-unauthorized-accounts/)). The CFPB fined the bank $100 million in September 2016.

**Is the Federal Reserve asset cap still in place?**
No. The Fed imposed the $1.95 trillion cap in February 2018 and lifted it on June 3, 2025, after seven years, voting unanimously and citing substantial progress on governance and risk management ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/fed-lifts-wells-fargos-asset-cap-citing-progress-from-sales-scandal/)). Some additional Fed oversight from the 2018 order remains.
