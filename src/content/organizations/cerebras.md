## Overview

Cerebras Systems is an American semiconductor company based in Sunnyvale, California, that builds the largest computer chip ever made: the Wafer Scale Engine (WSE), a processor that occupies nearly an entire silicon wafer instead of being cut from one ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/company-nvidia-killer-what-know-about-cerebras-ipo)). Founded in 2016 by chief executive Andrew Feldman and former colleagues from SeaMicro, it spent its first years selling AI supercomputers, then pivoted to selling fast AI inference as a cloud service — and after a two-year IPO odyssey involving a US national-security review, it listed on [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/) in May 2026 ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/company-nvidia-killer-what-know-about-cerebras-ipo); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/cerebras-prices-ipo-185-per-share-raise-555-billion-sources-say-2026-05-13/)).

Cerebras is not the same company as Cerence, the automotive voice-AI firm that trades on Nasdaq as CRNC — the similar names invite confusion, but the two companies are unrelated and operate in different industries. Feldman's team came from SeaMicro, a maker of efficient high-bandwidth microservers that Advanced Micro Devices acquired in 2012 ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/company-nvidia-killer-what-know-about-cerebras-ipo)). Cerebras sold its first AI chips in 2019, the year its first wafer-scale engine debuted ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/company-nvidia-killer-what-know-about-cerebras-ipo); [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/cerebras-chip-cs3)).

## The wafer-scale bet

Conventional chipmakers slice a silicon wafer into hundreds of small dies and discard the defective ones. Cerebras does the opposite: it makes the whole wafer a single processor ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/company-nvidia-killer-what-know-about-cerebras-ipo)). The bet is that doing more processing on one chip removes the networking that strings hundreds of GPUs together, so AI models train and answer faster ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/company-nvidia-killer-what-know-about-cerebras-ipo)). The trade-off is defects: where a rival can throw away a bad die, Cerebras must live with flaws across the whole wafer, so it builds redundant cores and interconnects into the chip — "flaws are designed to be recognized, shut down, and routed around," its filing says ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/company-nvidia-killer-what-know-about-cerebras-ipo)).

The third-generation chip, the WSE-3, unveiled on March 13, 2024, is the current flagship: built on TSMC's 5-nanometer process, it packs 4 trillion transistors and 900,000 AI-optimized cores into 46,225 square millimeters of silicon, with 44 gigabytes of on-chip memory and 125 petaflops of peak AI performance ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/cerebras-chip-cs3)). It powers the CS-3 AI supercomputer, which Cerebras says can train models of up to 24 trillion parameters ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/cerebras-chip-cs3)). The first WSE debuted in 2019 on TSMC 16-nanometer technology and the WSE-2 followed in 2021 on 7-nanometer technology, so the transistor count has more than tripled across three generations ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/cerebras-chip-cs3)).

## G42, Condor Galaxy, and the concentration problem

Cerebras's biggest customer has also been its biggest risk. Abu Dhabi-based G42 accounted for 87% of the company's revenue in the first half of 2024, and the same share in 2023 ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/company-nvidia-killer-what-know-about-cerebras-ipo)). Revenue jumped from $8.7 million in the first half of 2023 to $136.4 million in the first half of 2024, almost entirely on G42's orders ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/company-nvidia-killer-what-know-about-cerebras-ipo)). G42 had agreed to buy $1.43 billion of Cerebras equipment through the end of 2025, and in July 2023 the two companies announced a $900 million G42 investment to build an AI platform in the US using Cerebras chips ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/company-nvidia-killer-what-know-about-cerebras-ipo); [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/cerebras-systems-mulls-1bn-private-funding-round-in-move-that-could-delay-planned-ipo/)).

The partnership's flagship is Condor Galaxy, a network of AI supercomputers built for G42. Condor Galaxy 1 and 2 together delivered 8 exaFLOPs of AI computing, and Condor Galaxy 3 — 64 CS-3 systems in Dallas, Texas — added another 8 exaFLOPs, bringing the network to 16 exaFLOPs ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/cerebras-chip-cs3)). G42's founder is the UAE's national security advisor and its largest shareholder, a link the S-1 itself flagged as a geopolitical risk ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/company-nvidia-killer-what-know-about-cerebras-ipo)).

## The two-year road to the stock market

Cerebras confidentially filed for an IPO in August 2024 and filed its public S-1 the following month, applying to list on Nasdaq under the ticker CBRS ([Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/cerebras-systems-mulls-1bn-private-funding-round-in-move-that-could-delay-planned-ipo/); [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/public/ipo/cerebras-systems-halts-ipo-ai-semiconductors/)). The listing then stalled on a review by the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (CFIUS) of G42's investment, amid US concerns that Middle Eastern firms could give China indirect access to advanced American AI technology ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/cerebras-raises-5-5b-kicking-off-2026s-ipo-season-with-a-bang/)). In March 2025 Cerebras obtained CFIUS clearance to sell 22 million nonvoting shares to Group 42 in an IPO, a stake worth $355 million, and Feldman said in May that the company aimed to go public that year ([Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/cerebras-systems-mulls-1bn-private-funding-round-in-move-that-could-delay-planned-ipo/)).

Instead, the company raised more private money and pulled the filing. On September 30, 2025 it announced a $1.1 billion Series G round at an $8.1 billion post-money valuation, led by Fidelity and Atreides Management; three days later, on October 3, it asked the [US Securities and Exchange Commission](/organizations/us-securities-and-exchange-commission/) to withdraw the S-1 ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/public/ipo/cerebras-systems-halts-ipo-ai-semiconductors/)). Feldman later apologized on LinkedIn for the unexplained withdrawal and said the filing had gone stale: the inference service launched in August 2024 had become a far bigger business than expected, and the company would refile with updated financials and strategy ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2025/10/07/cerebras-ceo-explains-why-ipo-was-pulled/537488)). By late 2025 Cerebras claimed API speeds topping 3,000 tokens a second for models like OpenAI's gpt-oss-120B, and counted Mistral AI, AWS, Meta, IBM, Cognition, AlphaSense, Notion and Perplexity among its inference customers ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/software/2025/10/07/cerebras-ceo-explains-why-ipo-was-pulled/537488)).

Two developments set up the second attempt. In June 2025, [Reuters](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2025:newsml_L4N3SE180:0-us-ipo-market-revival-takes-root-as-chime-soars-in-debut/) named Cerebras among the marquee names in the IPO pipeline as fintech firm [Chime](/organizations/chime/) climbed 59% in its Nasdaq debut, with bankers saying a strong Chime debut could push "waiting room" candidates to file. Then on January 14, 2026, OpenAI signed a multi-year agreement for 750 megawatts of Cerebras compute, rolling out through 2028, in a deal worth over $10 billion according to sources cited by [TechCrunch](/organizations/techcrunch/) and [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/cerebras-prices-ipo-185-per-share-raise-555-billion-sources-say-2026-05-13/) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/14/openai-signs-deal-reportedly-worth-10-billion-for-compute-from-cerebras/)). OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman was already an investor in Cerebras, and OpenAI had once considered acquiring the company ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/14/openai-signs-deal-reportedly-worth-10-billion-for-compute-from-cerebras/)).

Cerebras refiled in April 2026 reporting 2025 revenue of $510 million, up 76% from $290.3 million in 2024, and a swing to $237.8 million in net income from a loss of nearly half a billion dollars the year before ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/cerebras-prices-ipo-185-per-share-raise-555-billion-sources-say-2026-05-13/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/cerebras-raises-5-5b-kicking-off-2026s-ipo-season-with-a-bang/)). On May 13, 2026 it priced its IPO at $185 a share — above a range already raised twice, from $115–$125 to $150–$160 — selling 30 million shares for $5.55 billion at a $56.43 billion fully diluted valuation, the largest US listing of 2026 to that date, with orders for more than 20 times the shares available ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/cerebras-prices-ipo-185-per-share-raise-555-billion-sources-say-2026-05-13/)). [CNBC](/organizations/cnbc/) first reported the pricing, according to [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/cerebras-prices-ipo-185-per-share-raise-555-billion-sources-say-2026-05-13/) ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/cerebras-prices-ipo-185-per-share-raise-555-billion-sources-say-2026-05-13/)). Trading began on May 14 under the ticker CBRS; shares opened at $385, up 108%, and closed at $311.07, valuing the company at about $66 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/cerebras-raises-5-5b-kicking-off-2026s-ipo-season-with-a-bang/)). At the IPO price, Feldman's stake was worth nearly $1.9 billion and co-founder and CTO Sean Lie's about $1 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/cerebras-raises-5-5b-kicking-off-2026s-ipo-season-with-a-bang/)).
