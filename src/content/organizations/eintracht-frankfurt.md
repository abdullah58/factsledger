On August 29, 2026, Eintracht Frankfurt tied a centre-back to the club until 2031. The player in question was not an established international but [Jeremiaha Maluze](/people/jeremiaha-maluze/), a 21-year-old who had signed from Rot-Weiß Erfurt just a year earlier and spent his first season in Frankfurt with the U21 squad — scoring three goals and assisting ten more in 26 Hessenliga appearances as the side earned promotion to the Regionalliga ([arunfoot/Eintracht press release](https://www.arunfoot.com/jeremiaha-maluze-signs-eintracht-frankfurt-contract-extension/)). His first-team debut had come only days before the announcement, replacing captain Robin Koch midway through the second half of the DFB-Pokal first-round tie at SC St. Tönis ([arunfoot](https://www.arunfoot.com/jeremiaha-maluze-signs-eintracht-frankfurt-contract-extension/)). Sporting director Timmo Hardung explained the logic: Maluze had "quickly adapted to the higher level and made a good impression with the first team... willing to learn, works on himself in a very focused way" — the long-term deal secures the prospect while the club watches his next steps ([arunfoot](https://www.arunfoot.com/jeremiaha-maluze-signs-eintracht-frankfurt-contract-extension/)).

It is a story the club has told before. Sebastian Rode came through the Eintracht academy, left for Bayern Munich and Borussia Dortmund, returned — and captained the side that lifted the Europa League in 2022 ([1BoxOffice](https://www.1BoxOffice.com/en/eintracht-frankfurt-tickets)). The Maluze extension shows the pipeline still operating: find the prospect in the lower tiers, prove him with the U21s, blood him in the cup, tie him down. That working model underpins a club whose nickname, *Launische Diva* — the moody diva — was earned by a side "capable of beating anyone and losing to anyone inside the same fortnight" ([1BoxOffice](https://www.1BoxOffice.com/en/eintracht-frankfurt-tickets)).

## One title, and the most famous defeat in European Cup history

The club that becomes Eintracht Frankfurt was founded on March 8, 1899, and the Waldstadion opened in the Frankfurt city forest in 1925 ([1BoxOffice](https://www.1BoxOffice.com/en/eintracht-frankfurt-tickets)). Its single German championship came in 1959, won by beating Kickers Offenbach in the final — a result that still animates the club's oldest and bitterest rivalry, with Offenbach sitting barely ten kilometres east across the Main ([1BoxOffice](https://www.1BoxOffice.com/en/eintracht-frankfurt-tickets)). That title carried Frankfurt into the European Cup, and what followed was defeat so spectacular it became more famous than the title: the 1960 final at Hampden Park, lost 7-3 to Real Madrid in front of more than 127,000 people, with Ferenc Puskás scoring four and Alfredo Di Stéfano a hat-trick — "still regularly cited among the finest the competition has produced" ([1BoxOffice](https://www.1BoxOffice.com/en/eintracht-frankfurt-tickets)). In 1963 the club was selected as a founder member of the new Bundesliga ([1BoxOffice](https://www.1BoxOffice.com/en/eintracht-frankfurt-tickets)).

## Cups, relegations, and Seville

Frankfurt is a cup club. Back-to-back DFB-Pokal wins in 1974 and 1975 were followed by further victories in 1981 and 1988, and the 1980 UEFA Cup — won on away goals in an all-German final against Borussia Mönchengladbach — remains one of only two European titles in the cabinet ([1BoxOffice](https://www.1BoxOffice.com/en/eintracht-frankfurt-tickets)). Bernd Hölzenbein and Jürgen Grabowski, both one-club men in essence, were in the West Germany side that won the 1974 World Cup ([1BoxOffice](https://www.1BoxOffice.com/en/eintracht-frankfurt-tickets)). The 1992 Bundesliga title race is still discussed in the city: it was lost on the final afternoon at Hansa Rostock, when a draw would have been enough ([1BoxOffice](https://www.1BoxOffice.com/en/eintracht-frankfurt-tickets)).

Then came the decades that forged the diva: four relegations in fifteen years (1996, 2001, 2004, 2011), financial difficulty, and a 2016 relegation play-off survived over two legs against 1. FC Nürnberg — described as the lowest point and "arguably the moment the modern club began, because what followed was built by people who had watched from the bottom" ([1BoxOffice](https://www.1BoxOffice.com/en/eintracht-frankfurt-tickets)).

What followed was Seville. The 2018 DFB-Pokal final win over Bayern Munich (3-1) started the revival; in 2022 came the night at the Camp Nou when roughly 30,000 Frankfurt supporters filled home sections and watched their side win 3-2 at Barcelona, and then the Europa League final on May 18, 2022 — 1-1 with Rangers after extra time, won 5-4 on penalties with Kevin Trapp saving from Aaron Ramsey ([1BoxOffice](https://www.1BoxOffice.com/en/eintracht-frankfurt-tickets)). Something close to 100,000 supporters had travelled to Seville for a final the club had 30,000 tickets for ([1BoxOffice](https://www.1BoxOffice.com/en/eintracht-frankfurt-tickets)).

## The ground and the people

Deutsche Bank Park — the Waldstadion by its original name — is a fully enclosed two-tier bowl with a retractable roof, holding 59,500 for league fixtures, the seventh-largest football ground in Germany ([1BoxOffice](https://www.1BoxOffice.com/en/eintracht-frankfurt-tickets)). Against it stand roughly 155,000 members, the fourth-largest membership in German football — a ratio that means the member priority window absorbs much of the best inventory before general sale ([1BoxOffice](https://www.1BoxOffice.com/en/eintracht-frankfurt-tickets)). The Nordwestkurve, the home terrace with safe standing from around €13, is the noisiest section and the first to sell out ([1BoxOffice](https://www.1BoxOffice.com/en/eintracht-frankfurt-tickets)). And the ground's profile will rise further: Deutsche Bank Park hosts the 2027 Europa League final ([1BoxOffice](https://www.1BoxOffice.com/en/eintracht-frankfurt-tickets)).
