## Overview

Liberté Investors, Inc. was a Dallas-based holding company — a Delaware corporation headquartered at 200 Crescent Court, Suite 1365, Dallas, Texas, its stock traded on the [New York Stock Exchange](/organizations/new-york-stock-exchange/) under the ticker LBI — chaired by banker [Gerald J. Ford](/people/gerald-j-ford/), who owned about 45% of it ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1017907/000104746903040833/a2124990z8-k.htm); [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1017907/000104746903040833/a2124990zex-99_1.htm)). The company matters for exactly one reason: on December 15, 2003, it announced a definitive agreement to acquire USAuto Holdings, a Tennessee-based non-standard auto insurer, and on closing it renamed itself [First Acceptance Corporation](/organizations/acceptance-insurance/) and moved to Nashville, Tennessee — the insurer profiled on this site ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1017907/000104746903040833/a2124990zex-99_1.htm)). There is no plain-language profile of Liberté Investors anywhere else; every competing result is a filing fragment.

## The $76 million merger

The December 15, 2003 announcement is the whole story in one document. Liberté would acquire 100% of USAuto's stock for $76 million in cash plus 13.25 million newly issued Liberté shares, with up to 750,000 more shares payable if USAuto hit 2004 operating targets; "Gerald J. Ford, Liberté's Chairman and 45% stockholder, has agreed to vote his shares in favor of the acquisition" ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1017907/000104746903040833/a2124990zex-99_1.htm)). [Morgan Joseph & Co.](/organizations/morgan-joseph/) served as Liberté's financial advisor and rendered a fairness opinion to its board ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1017907/000104746903040833/a2124990zex-99_1.htm)). The cash portion was to be funded partly through a $50 million rights offering to existing stockholders, filed the same day ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1017907/000104746903040833/a2124990z8-k.htm)). Antitrust clearance came fast: the FTC granted early termination of the Hart-Scott-Rodino waiting period on January 2, 2004 ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/early-termination-notices/20040325)).

The closing carried a wholesale changing of the guard. Liberté would change its name to First Acceptance Corporation and relocate its headquarters to Nashville, Tennessee; [USAuto](/organizations/usauto-holdings/) CEO [Stephen J. Harrison](/people/stephen-j-harrison/) would replace [Donald J. Edwards](/people/donald-j-edwards/) as chief executive; USAuto COO [Thomas M. Harrison](/people/thomas-m-harrison/) would become executive vice president; Ford would remain chairman; and Edwards would stay on as a director under a four-year advisory agreement ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1017907/000104746903040833/a2124990zex-99_1.htm)). The transaction behind today's First Acceptance Corporation — and, two corporate lifetimes later, its April 2018 voluntary delisting from the NYSE (NYSE: FAC) to trade over the counter as FACO — starts here ([First Acceptance](https://www.advfn.com/stock-market/USOTC/FACO/stock-news/76977389/first-acceptance-corporation-announces-intent-to-v)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What was Liberté Investors?**
A Dallas-based holding company (Delaware corporation, NYSE: LBI), chaired by Gerald J. Ford, which in 2003–04 acquired USAuto Holdings and renamed itself First Acceptance Corporation.

**What did Liberté Investors pay for USAuto?**
$76 million in cash plus 13.25 million newly issued Liberté shares, with up to 750,000 additional shares tied to USAuto's 2004 operating performance.

**What happened to Liberté Investors after the merger?**
It ceased to exist under that name: on closing it became First Acceptance Corporation and moved its headquarters from Dallas to Nashville, Tennessee.

**Is Liberté Investors related to the French "Liberté" brands?**
No. The name collides in search results with French retail, fashion, and other "Liberté" entities; this was a Texas holding company identified by its NYSE ticker LBI and Dallas address.
