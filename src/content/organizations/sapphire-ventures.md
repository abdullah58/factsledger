## Overview

Sapphire Ventures is a Menlo Park venture capital firm that backs expansion-stage enterprise software companies from Series B through to IPO. Founded in 1996 as the venture-capital arm of German software company SAP, it spun out as an independent firm in 2011 and took the Sapphire name in 2014. It now manages over $11 billion in assets, and in August 2025 co-led [Blue J](/organizations/blue-j/)'s $122 million Series D.

Sapphire describes itself as a "global software venture capital firm" and says it has invested in more than 170 companies globally, producing more than 30 public listings and 45 acquisitions ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)). Its team works from offices in Menlo Park, [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/), [Austin](/places/austin-texas/) and [London](/places/london/) ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)). A dated trail of fund announcements tracks its growth from $1.4 billion under management in 2014 to more than $11 billion by 2025 — the spine of this profile.

## From SAP's venture arm to an independent firm

Sapphire began in 1996 as the venture-capital arm of SAP, the German software conglomerate, investing as a corporate venturing unit for its first fifteen years ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/sapphire-ventures-1-billion)). In 2011 it spun out as an independent company — one of the earliest corporate venture arms to cut loose from its parent. "The best investors don't want to work in the constraints of fund that is linked to the strategic aims of a corporation," chief executive Nino Marakovic said ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/sapphire-ventures-london)).

When the firm rebranded from SAP Ventures to Sapphire Ventures in October 2014, SAP was still its sole limited partner, and Marakovic said the new name deliberately kept "the clear reference to SAP" in it ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2014/10/16/sap-ventures-rebrands-as-sapphire-ventures/)). At that point it managed $1.4 billion and had invested in more than 125 businesses as well as funds ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2014/10/16/sap-ventures-rebrands-as-sapphire-ventures/)). Nearly a decade later SAP was still described as one of the firm's key limited partners, valued for customer introductions as well as capital ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/sapphire-ventures-london)). That history explains a quirk of the record: directories variously give the firm's founding year as 1996 or 2011 — 1996 is when the investing operation began inside SAP, and 2011 is when it became an independent firm.

## How Sapphire invests: three strategies, one thesis

Sapphire invests through three strategies spanning early through growth stages, direct and fund investments ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/sapphire-ventures-closes-on-1-4b-to-invest-in-late-stage-startups/)). The direct strategy is the core: high-growth, expansion-stage technology companies, entered at Series B and held through to IPO in the [United States](/places/united-states/), Europe and Israel, focused on enterprise SaaS, infrastructure software, fintech, healthcare IT and crypto infrastructure ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/30/sapphire-ventures-secures-largest-capital-raise-to-date-across-two-new-funds/)).

| Strategy | What it does | Stage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sapphire Ventures | Direct investment in expansion-stage technology companies | Series B to IPO |
| Sapphire Partners | Invests as a limited partner in early-stage venture funds | Seed / Series A funds |
| Sapphire Sport | Early-stage startups at the intersection of sports, media and entertainment | Series A / Series B |

The cheque sizes grew with the funds. In 2016 the firm said it typically invested around $10 million to $20 million as an initial investment in an expansion-stage company ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/sapphire-ventures-1-billion)). Its entry filter, described in 2020, was a company with a $5 million to $10 million revenue run rate that was doubling and tripling year on year ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/sapphire-ventures-london)). Sapphire Partners, the fund-of-funds arm, puts money into other early-stage venture funds, a programme that already had a $250 million vehicle by 2013 ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2014/10/16/sap-ventures-rebrands-as-sapphire-ventures/)). The point most often misunderstood about the Sapphire name: Sapphire Partners is not a separate outside firm but Sapphire Ventures' own limited-partner investing arm, one of its three strategies ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)). That three-strategy structure held until November 2025, when Sapphire Sport spun out as the fully independent firm 359 Capital (see "Sapphire Sport" below).

## The fundraising arc: $1.4 billion to over $11 billion

Sapphire's growth is best read as a sequence of fundraises, each roughly doubling the firm's scale. In September 2016 the firm closed its third fund at $1 billion, taking total managed assets past $2.4 billion, with 38 exits since the spin-out — 15 IPOs and 23 mergers and acquisitions ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/sapphire-ventures-1-billion)).

December 2019 brought $1.4 billion in new capital — Growth Fund IV, a $150 million Opportunity Fund and extensions of previous funds — taking assets under management past $4 billion ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/sapphire-ventures-closes-on-1-4b-to-invest-in-late-stage-startups/)). By then the firm counted more than 55 exits since independence, including 21 IPOs ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/sapphire-ventures-closes-on-1-4b-to-invest-in-late-stage-startups/)). Then came the two-step leap of 2021: $1.7 billion across multiple strategies in February, followed in November by $2 billion across Fund VI and Opportunity Fund III — the firm's largest raise to date — which took assets under management past $8.8 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/30/sapphire-ventures-secures-largest-capital-raise-to-date-across-two-new-funds/)). In all, Sapphire brought in $3.7 billion of capital in twelve months ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/30/sapphire-ventures-secures-largest-capital-raise-to-date-across-two-new-funds/)). By then it had 165 portfolio companies and more than 70 exits, including 30 IPOs and 45 acquisitions; in 2021 alone it invested $990 million and added 26 new companies ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/30/sapphire-ventures-secures-largest-capital-raise-to-date-across-two-new-funds/)).

## Portfolio Growth: the "more than money" pitch

Sapphire's stated differentiator is what happens after the cheque, not its size: Marakovic told TechCrunch in 2021 that the firm prides itself on "being an active adviser, and that is our differentiator" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/30/sapphire-ventures-secures-largest-capital-raise-to-date-across-two-new-funds/)). Its Portfolio Growth division makes introductions to potential customers, partners and new hires and hosts events and executive briefings ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/sapphire-ventures-closes-on-1-4b-to-invest-in-late-stage-startups/)).

Marakovic's rule, stated in 2020: an investment decision "should be slightly controversial": "If everyone votes in favour of something it is a sign we shouldn't do it. It's too consensus then. Over the years we have seen that it is the controversial calls that perform the best" ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/sapphire-ventures-london)). Notable investments include DocuSign, LinkedIn, Fitbit, Box, Sumo Logic and 23andMe ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/30/sapphire-ventures-secures-largest-capital-raise-to-date-across-two-new-funds/)).

## Europe and the London office

Sapphire's team has always had a strongly European background, but roughly 80% of its investments were in the US ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/sapphire-ventures-london)). In 2020 it returned to its European roots by opening a London office, a planned 10-person team led by Andreas Weiskam, and hiring Annalise Dragic from Atomico ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/sapphire-ventures-london)). Marakovic's reasoning was that Europe had finally matured: "Europe is creating unicorns and decacorns now, so this is a good moment to come back" ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/sapphire-ventures-london)).

At the time the firm had 12 European investments out of just over 100, including Adverity, Contentful, Cazoo, TransferWise and Currencycloud ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/sapphire-ventures-london)). It leaned on the same enterprise network it used in the US: alongside SAP, it worked with companies such as Amazon Web Services and [Salesforce](/organizations/salesforce/), which could offer strategic help to portfolio companies — and act as potential buyers ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/sapphire-ventures-london)).

## Blue J and the vertical-AI bet

On 4 August 2025 Blue J, a generative-AI tax research platform, announced a $122 million Series D co-led by [Oak HC/FT](/organizations/oak-hc-ft/) and Sapphire Ventures, with participation from Intrepid Growth Partners and previous investors Ten Coves Capital and CPA.com ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)). The round came seven months after Blue J's Series C; in the first half of 2025 the company had more than doubled its revenue and customer base ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)). Sapphire partner Cathy Gao joined Blue J's board as its newest board member ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)).

Gao's rationale is the clearest statement of Sapphire's current filter: "Blue J is exactly what we look for in vertical AI: deep domain expertise, proprietary data and a product that drives meaningful business impact" ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)). The deal followed Sapphire's July 2023 commitment of more than $1 billion to AI-powered enterprise technology, drawn from existing funds holding about $10 billion under management with about $3 billion undeployed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/11/sapphire-ventures-plans-to-invest-over-1b-in-enterprise-ai-startups/)). Most of it was earmarked for direct investments, the rest for AI-focused funds through Sapphire Partners, targeting business-to-business software that makes AI easily accessible by using data to predict outcomes better, including verticals such as manufacturing and healthcare ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/11/sapphire-ventures-plans-to-invest-over-1b-in-enterprise-ai-startups/)). Blue J's platform answers complex tax questions across US federal, state and local tax as well as Canadian and [United Kingdom](/places/united-kingdom/) tax law, built on a curated database of tax rulings ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)). The funding was earmarked for team expansion, product development and market reach ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)). The bet was followed up fifteen months later: on 10 November 2026 Blue J raised a $176 million Series E led by JMI Equity at a valuation of more than $700 million, with Sapphire Ventures participating again as an existing investor alongside Oak HC/FT and Intrepid Growth Partners, after annual recurring revenue grew 165% in the fifteen months since the Series D ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/blog/blue-j-announces-series-e-funding)).

## Sapphire Sport: a fund built on team owners

The firm's third strategy, Sapphire Sport, was launched in January 2019 as an oversubscribed $115 million-plus funds platform for early-stage startups at the intersection of sports, media and entertainment ([PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/sapphire-ventures-raises-115m-to-launch-sapphire-sport-funds-platform-300784859.html)). Its anchor investor was City Football Group, owners of Manchester City, and its backers included owners, investors and family offices from the National Football League, Major League Baseball, the National Basketball Association, the National Hockey League and Major League Soccer, alongside adidas, AEG, SAP, Sinclair Broadcast Group and Madison Square Garden ([PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/sapphire-ventures-raises-115m-to-launch-sapphire-sport-funds-platform-300784859.html)). It was co-led by Doug Higgins, a Sapphire co-founder and managing director, and Michael Spirito, who joined from 21st Century Fox ([PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/sapphire-ventures-raises-115m-to-launch-sapphire-sport-funds-platform-300784859.html)).

The launch portfolio of five startups — Tonal, mycujoo, Overtime, Fevo and Phoenix Labs — showed the thesis ([PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/sapphire-ventures-raises-115m-to-launch-sapphire-sport-funds-platform-300784859.html)). ## Frequently asked questions

**What is Sapphire Ventures?** Sapphire Ventures is a Menlo Park-based venture capital firm investing in expansion-stage enterprise software companies from Series B to IPO, with over $11 billion in assets under management as stated by the firm in August 2025 ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)). It was founded in 1996 as SAP's venture-capital arm, became independent in 2011 and rebranded from SAP Ventures in 2014 ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2014/10/16/sap-ventures-rebrands-as-sapphire-ventures/)).

**How is Sapphire Ventures connected to SAP?** Sapphire began as SAP's corporate venture arm in 1996 and spun out as an independent company in 2011 ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/sapphire-ventures-1-billion)). SAP was still its sole limited partner at the 2014 rebrand and remained a key limited partner years later, valued for customer introductions as well as capital ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2014/10/16/sap-ventures-rebrands-as-sapphire-ventures/); [Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/sapphire-ventures-london)).

**What does Sapphire Ventures invest in?** Primarily high-growth, expansion-stage technology companies — enterprise SaaS, infrastructure software, fintech, healthcare IT and crypto infrastructure — entered at Series B and held to IPO, in the US, Europe and Israel ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/30/sapphire-ventures-secures-largest-capital-raise-to-date-across-two-new-funds/)). It also invests as a limited partner in early-stage funds through Sapphire Partners; its former sports, media and entertainment arm, Sapphire Sport, spun out as the independent firm 359 Capital in November 2025 ([The AI Journal](https://aijourn.com/sapphire-sport-becomes-359-capital/)).

**How big is Sapphire Ventures?** Its assets under management grew from $1.4 billion in 2014 to over $11 billion on the firm's own statement in August 2025 ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2014/10/16/sap-ventures-rebrands-as-sapphire-ventures/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/30/sapphire-ventures-secures-largest-capital-raise-to-date-across-two-new-funds/); [Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)). It reports more than 170 portfolio companies, more than 30 public listings and 45 acquisitions ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)).

**What was Sapphire's role in Blue J's Series D?** Sapphire Ventures co-led Blue J's $122 million Series D in August 2025 alongside Oak HC/FT, and Sapphire partner Cathy Gao joined Blue J's board ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)). Gao described Blue J as "exactly what we look for in vertical AI: deep domain expertise, proprietary data and a product that drives meaningful business impact" ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)).
