## Overview

Kleiner Perkins is an American venture capital firm founded in Menlo Park, California, in 1972 by Eugene Kleiner and Tom Perkins. An early backer of Genentech, Amazon and Google, it rebuilt itself under Mamoon Hamid and Ilya Fushman after its growth team spun out in 2018, and in March 2026 it raised $3.5 billion for AI investing — its largest fundraise ever ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/24/with-3-5b-in-fresh-capital-kleiner-perkins-is-going-all-in-on-ai/)).

The firm is headquartered at 2750 Sand Hill Road in Menlo Park, in the [United States](/places/united-states/), according to its own regulatory filings ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2115014/0002115014-26-000001-index.htm)). Its reported assets under management passed $21 billion with the March 2026 raise, a figure that comes from press reporting rather than an audited disclosure ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/kleiner-perkins-3-5-billion-raised-for-ai-focused-venture-and-growth-funds/)). And its most-cited recent deal — leading [Brex](/organizations/brex/)'s $100 million Series C extension in June 2019 at a $2.6 billion valuation — was led through the firm's growth fund by a partner who had already left to start a rival firm ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/06/11/brex-series-c2/)).

## Founding and the bets that made the name

Eugene Kleiner was one of the eight engineers who left William Shockley's laboratory to found Fairchild Semiconductor in 1957, and Tom Perkins was a Hewlett-Packard computer-division executive ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/articles/2003/12/eugenekleiner.html)). In 1972 the two formed one of the world's first venture capital firms, raising an $8 million first fund and pioneering the practice of incubating companies — building them from inside the firm rather than merely funding them — with Genentech, the biotech pioneer, among the first companies incubated this way ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/articles/2003/12/eugenekleiner.html)).

The bets that made the firm's name followed. In June 1996 Kleiner Perkins put $8 million into Amazon for a 13 percent stake at a $60 million valuation, with partner John Doerr joining Amazon's board as a condition of the deal ([HistoryLink](https://www.historylink.org/File/23230)). Three years later, Doerr and Sequoia's Mike Moritz each put $12.5 million into Google's $25 million Series A, after both men had insisted on leading the round and a compromise split it between them ([Electronics Weekly](https://www.electronicsweekly.com/blogs/mannerisms/yarns/funding-google-by-tom-perkins-2008-07/)). Netscape, Sun Microsystems and Compaq filled out a portfolio that made Kleiner Perkins the defining venture firm of the internet's first wave ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/11/kleiner-perkins-starts-off-50th-year-with-1-8b-in-two-new-funds/)).

## Crisis, spin-out and reboot

The firm's standing cracked in the 2010s. Ellen Pao, a former junior partner, sued in May 2012 alleging gender discrimination and retaliation, and after a closely watched five-week trial a [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) Superior Court jury ruled against her on all four claims on March 27, 2015 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/03/27/the-jury-has-reached-a-verdict-in-ellen-pao-vs-kleiner-perkins/?ncid=rss)). She had sought about $16 million in lost income, plus up to $144 million in punitive damages, and was ordered to pay part of Kleiner Perkins' trial bills ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/03/27/the-jury-has-reached-a-verdict-in-ellen-pao-vs-kleiner-perkins/?ncid=rss)).

In 2016 John Doerr stepped down as managing partner, replaced by Ted Schlein, and departures followed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/09/14/mary-meeker-author-of-the-internet-trends-report-is-leaving-kleiner-perkins/)). In September 2018 Mary Meeker — the former Morgan Stanley analyst behind the annual Internet Trends Report — left to start a new fund, taking the firm's entire growth team with her, in what the firm described as an intentional spin-off of its growth-investing unit ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/09/14/mary-meeker-author-of-the-internet-trends-report-is-leaving-kleiner-perkins/)). The reboot came in January 2019: Mamoon Hamid, who had joined from Social Capital in 2017, and Ilya Fushman, from Index Ventures in 2018, raised a $600 million early-stage fund, KP18, explicitly "going back to the future" as a small, hands-on, early-stage firm ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/01/31/kleiner-perkins-early-stage/); [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/checking-in-with-kleiner-perkins-as-it-goes-back-to-the-future/)).

## The fund machine — and the Brex round

KP18's predecessor, KP17, had been $440 million, so the reboot fund was already a step up in size, and the firm planned to put about 70 percent of its dollars into Series A rounds ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/checking-in-with-kleiner-perkins-as-it-goes-back-to-the-future/)). The cycle after that shows a firm that kept its early-stage flagship but let growth capital do the growing: a $750 million growth fund, Select I, arrived in April 2021, then KP20 ($800 million) and Select II ($1 billion) in January 2022 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/11/kleiner-perkins-starts-off-50th-year-with-1-8b-in-two-new-funds/)). KP21 ($825 million) and Select III ($1.2 billion) closed in June 2024, and in March 2026 KP22 ($1 billion) plus $2.5 billion of growth capital brought the total raise to $3.5 billion, framed squarely around artificial intelligence ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/28/kleiner-perkins-announces-2-billion-in-fresh-capital-showing-that-established-firms-can-still-raise-large-sums/); [Kleiner Perkins](https://www.kleinerperkins.com/perspectives/our-new-funds-kp22-select-iv-flex/)).

The growth fund also produced the firm's most-cited recent deal — and its strangest. In June 2019, Brex closed a $100 million Series C extension at a $2.6 billion valuation, led by the Kleiner Perkins Digital Growth Fund, with [Y Combinator Continuity](/organizations/y-combinator-continuity/) and [Ribbit Capital](/organizations/ribbit-capital/) among the participants, taking Brex's total equity funding to $315 million ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/brex-raises-100-million-at-2-6-billion-valuation-for-startup-friendly-credit-card)). But TechCrunch reported the round was led via former general partner Mood Rowghani, who had left the firm the previous year to co-found Bond with Meeker ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/06/11/brex-series-c2/)). Brex, a [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/) graduate whose private launch had about 1,000 customers including [Affirm](/organizations/affirm/), [Algolia](/organizations/algolia/) and [Flexport](/organizations/flexport/), called the round a "repricing event": co-founder Henrique Dubugras said the startup had not yet touched its Series C money ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/06/11/brex-series-c2/); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/brex-raises-100-million-at-2-6-billion-valuation-for-startup-friendly-credit-card)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was Kleiner Perkins founded?**
In 1972, in Menlo Park, California, by Eugene Kleiner — a co-founder of Fairchild Semiconductor — and Tom Perkins, a former Hewlett-Packard executive. Their first fund was $8 million, and the firm pioneered incubating companies, with Genentech among the first ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/articles/2003/12/eugenekleiner.html)).

**What is Kleiner Perkins best known for investing in?**
Its early bets on Genentech, Amazon in 1996 and Google in 1999, plus Netscape, Sun Microsystems and Compaq. Later winners included Airbnb, Slack, Uber, Instacart and Robinhood, and its recent AI stakes include Anthropic, Together AI, Harvey and OpenEvidence ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/28/kleiner-perkins-announces-2-billion-in-fresh-capital-showing-that-established-firms-can-still-raise-large-sums/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/24/with-3-5b-in-fresh-capital-kleiner-perkins-is-going-all-in-on-ai/)).

**What happened in the Ellen Pao lawsuit?**
Pao, a former junior partner, sued in May 2012 alleging gender discrimination and retaliation. After a five-week trial, a San Francisco jury ruled against her on all four claims on March 27, 2015. She had sought about $16 million, plus up to $144 million in punitive damages ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/03/27/the-jury-has-reached-a-verdict-in-ellen-pao-vs-kleiner-perkins/?ncid=rss)).

**Who leads Kleiner Perkins today?**
Mamoon Hamid and Ilya Fushman have led the firm since the January 2019 reboot, when they raised the $600 million KP18 fund. As of March 2026 it operates with a lean team of five partners, after Ev Randle left for Benchmark and Annie Case moved to an advisory role ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/01/31/kleiner-perkins-early-stage/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/24/with-3-5b-in-fresh-capital-kleiner-perkins-is-going-all-in-on-ai/)).

**How much money has Kleiner Perkins raised lately?**
In March 2026 it raised $3.5 billion: $1 billion for KP22, its 22nd early-stage fund, and $2.5 billion for growth funds, all framed around artificial intelligence. The raise took its reported assets under management past $21 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/24/with-3-5b-in-fresh-capital-kleiner-perkins-is-going-all-in-on-ai/)).

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not settle the size of the Google check: it follows co-founder Tom Perkins' own account ($12.5 million each from Kleiner Perkins and Sequoia), while noting that his book also says "$20 million was invested for about 24 per cent" and that other secondary accounts give $11.8 million.
- It does not claim a 19th fund was announced in January 2019: the contemporaneous TechCrunch report calls that day's $600 million fund the 18th, and some tertiary sources disagree.
- It does not state whether the Digital Growth Fund was dissolved or still active when it led the Brex round in June 2019 — only that TechCrunch reports the round was led via former partner Mood Rowghani, who had left to co-found Bond.
- It does not claim Shanghai or Beijing offices: older sources list them, but only the Menlo Park headquarters and the San Francisco office are verified here.
- It claims no returns or IRR figures, and the portfolio names given are as reported in 2018–2026 coverage, not a complete current portfolio.
- The Brex round is called both a "Series C extension" (VentureBeat) and a "Series C-2" (TechCrunch); both names describe the same June 2019 round.
