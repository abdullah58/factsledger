## Overview

Global Corporate Venturing (GCV) is a [London](/places/london/)-based trade publication, data provider and events organiser for the corporate venture capital industry, founded in 2010 by journalist James Mawson and published by his company Mawsonia Ltd ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/about-us)). It tracks which large companies invest in startups, ranks the industry's leaders in its annual Powerlist, and convenes them at its London Symposium — a combination of journalism, proprietary data and live events that has sustained it for more than fifteen years ([TwoFourSeven Strategy](https://www.twofourseven.co.uk/blog/25/6/2026/corporate-venture-capital-the-human-innovation-gap)).

Corporate venture capital (CVC) is the practice of large companies taking minority stakes in startups, usually for strategic as well as financial reasons. GCV covers the companies, deals, people and investment trends of that world through its website, newsletter, social channels and reports, and it sells the underlying deal data to subscribers ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/about-us)). Its own tagline is "independent news, data and analysis for the global corporate venture capital community" ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/about-us)). The business is deliberately narrow: one industry, covered as a trade press, a data vendor and a conference organiser at the same time.

## Origins: Mawson and Mawsonia

James Mawson spent nearly four years as editor of *Private Equity News*, part of Dow Jones and the [*Wall Street Journal*](/organizations/wall-street-journal/) in London, before leaving in May 2010 to launch Global Corporate Venturing as an independent title from his own publishing company, Mawsonia ([SETsquared](https://www.setsquared.co.uk/setsquared-invest-2022-speaker-bio/); [Innovate UK](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a808c7840f0b6230269427a/Williams_InnovateUK_Web.pdf)). The company record matches the story: Mawson was appointed a director of Mawsonia Ltd on 13 January 2010, and the company is registered at 124 City Road, London ([Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/07123963/officers)). Mawsonia's company number is 07123963 ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/about-us/terms-conditions/)).

Mawson came to the launch with a long reporting career behind him. He had freelanced for the BBC, the [*Financial Times*](/organizations/financial-times/), the *Economist*, the *Independent on Sunday*, the *Sunday Express* and Dow Jones Newswires, worked as a foreign correspondent in central and eastern Europe, and served as international editor for FT Business; his first job was at the technology publisher ComputerWire, after graduating from King's College, London ([SETsquared](https://www.setsquared.co.uk/setsquared-invest-2022-speaker-bio/)). He had also coordinated leveraged-buyout and venture-capital coverage for other Dow Jones and News Corporation titles, acted as a spokesman on BBC radio and television, and chaired awards and conferences, including the BVCA awards ([SETsquared](https://www.setsquared.co.uk/setsquared-invest-2022-speaker-bio/)).

The venture grew quickly into three titles. Global University Venturing, aimed at helping academic researchers launch spinouts and work with industry, followed in January 2012, and Global Government Venturing launched in May 2014; the third title was rebranded Global Impact Venturing in February 2019 ([SETsquared](https://www.setsquared.co.uk/setsquared-invest-2022-speaker-bio/)). In November 2013 Mawsonia welcomed Dominic Riley as chairman; Riley said he had joined after examining "three years of rapid growth of more than 30% in both revenues and staff per year" ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2013/11/23/gcv-publisher-appoints-riley-chairman-the-importance-of-people/)). Companies House records Riley as a director from 7 March 2014 ([Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/07123963/officers)). The company's stated founding mission comes from the philosopher David Hume: "Truth springs from argument among good friends" ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2013/11/23/gcv-publisher-appoints-riley-chairman-the-importance-of-people/)).

## What GCV publishes and measures

GCV's editorial output is built on a proprietary dataset, GCV Analytics, which tracks corporate investors and their deals worldwide ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2019/05/23/gcv-powerlist-2019-introduction/)). The data business is old enough to show the sector's growth in its own numbers. Bloomberg reported in May 2016 that there were 801 corporate venture capital units in 2015, up 79% from 2011, according to Global Corporate Venturing, which it described as "a research group" ([Bloomberg](https://www.gadgets360.com/internet/news/hewlett-packard-enterprise-makes-a-100-million-bet-on-startups-838969/amp)). The same article quoted Toby Lewis, then GCV's chief analytics officer, on the small size of most units: three-fourths of corporate firms had about five people or fewer devoted to venture capital ([Bloomberg](https://www.gadgets360.com/internet/news/hewlett-packard-enterprise-makes-a-100-million-bet-on-startups-838969/amp)). Companies House records Lewis as a Mawsonia director from 2012 to 2016 ([Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/07123963/officers)).

The scale of the sector GCV tracks kept rising. By 2024 it was tracking 7,500 companies recorded as having done some kind of corporate venturing activity, with 2,273 corporates actively investing ([SETsquared](https://www.setsquared.co.uk/setsquared-backed-companies-seek-corporate-investment-at-the-gcv-symposium/)). Its annual *World of Corporate Venturing* report put the 2025 figure higher still: 3,068 corporations invested in startups in 2025, a record, and about one in five startup funding rounds now involves a corporate backer, with the money in those rounds up 75% to $233.8bn ([Global Venturing](https://www.worldofcorporateventuring.com/)). The same report found 41% of the dollars in corporate-backed rounds went to AI startups, and that 95% of corporate investors target early-stage (series A and B) rounds ([Global Venturing](https://www.worldofcorporateventuring.com/)). The report rests on an annual benchmarking survey of nearly 400 corporate investment arms ([Global Venturing](https://www.worldofcorporateventuring.com/)).

That data work is also what other publications cite. GCV's reporting has covered individual companies' financing in detail: its February 2020 article on [Behavox](/organizations/behavox/) recorded the compliance-software firm's funding history from its 2014 founding through its $20m Series A1 round in 2017, ahead of [SoftBank](/organizations/softbank-group/)'s $100m investment — the reporting on which the sibling FactsLedger profile of Behavox rests.

## The Powerlist

GCV's best-known product is the Powerlist, its annual ranking of the top 100 heads of corporate venturing units, chosen from the 2,200 units it covers globally (2019 figure) ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2019/05/23/gcv-powerlist-2019-introduction/)). Selection draws on metrics from the GCV Analytics data platform and on strategic and leadership measures, including thought leadership, team expansion and acquisitions of portfolio companies; the winners are celebrated at an awards reception at the Terrace Pavilion of the House of Commons ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2019/05/23/gcv-powerlist-2019-introduction/)). Rising Stars, the next generation of leaders, are selected in January at the GCVI Summit in California ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2019/05/23/gcv-powerlist-2019-introduction/)).

The 2019 edition shows what the ranking measures — and who dominates it. GCV counted 2,472 corporate venturing leaders that year, of whom just 5% were women, though more than a third of the GCV Rising Stars 2019 were women ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2019/05/23/gcv-powerlist-2019-introduction/)). [Tencent](/organizations/tencent/) was placed first, SoftBank second, Naspers third and [Alibaba](/organizations/alibaba/) tenth ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2019/05/23/gcv-powerlist-2019-introduction/)). Groups in the top 25 "can invest hundreds of millions of dollars or even billions annually — more than almost any independent VC" ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2019/05/23/gcv-powerlist-2019-introduction/)). The same introduction noted that the largest CVCs were changing shape: [Intel](/organizations/intel/) Capital's position as the biggest and best-resourced unit had passed, but its president, Wendell Brooks — then chairman of the GCV Leadership Society — still set the tone by asking his team "not what the portfolio companies can do for us, but what we can do for the portfolio companies" ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2019/05/23/gcv-powerlist-2019-introduction/)).

## Events and community

GCV is as much an events and membership business as a publisher. Its services are organised as News & Analysis, Community & Events, the GCV Institute and membership, with the Institute providing professional development and certification informed by GCV Keystone's operational benchmarking ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/about-us)). The flagship is the GCV Symposium, an annual London event at which the Powerlist is run each May ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2019/05/23/gcv-powerlist-2019-introduction/)). SETsquared, the university enterprise partnership, calls it "Europe's leading corporate venturing and innovation event" ([SETsquared](https://www.setsquared.co.uk/setsquared-backed-companies-seek-corporate-investment-at-the-gcv-symposium/)).

The convening reaches beyond London. GCV "publishes analysis, runs the global data, and convenes the community across cities from London to [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/), Tokyo to São Paulo," with events also in Monterey, California, and Tokyo ([TwoFourSeven Strategy](https://www.twofourseven.co.uk/blog/25/6/2026/corporate-venture-capital-the-human-innovation-gap)). A first-hand account of the June 2026 London symposium describes it as "one of the most concentrated rooms in the world for corporate venture capital," opened by founder Jim Mawson, with leaders in European innovation policy — including a keynote from the European Investment Fund and a conversation with the European Innovation Council — among the speakers ([TwoFourSeven Strategy](https://www.twofourseven.co.uk/blog/25/6/2026/corporate-venture-capital-the-human-innovation-gap)). Figures shared at that symposium, as reported by the same account, put CVC investments' financial returns at approximately $55bn against parent-company net income in the first quarter of 2026 alone, with exits across corporate-backed ventures reaching a record $258bn in a single month; the global venture ecosystem had grown from around $60bn in 2010 to more than $600bn at peak ([TwoFourSeven Strategy](https://www.twofourseven.co.uk/blog/25/6/2026/corporate-venture-capital-the-human-innovation-gap)).

Ownership of the publisher has also shifted recently. Companies House records show Mawsonia Holdings Limited became the person with significant control of Mawsonia Ltd on 25 May 2025, the same day James Mawson ceased to hold that status ([Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/07123963/filing-history)). The company filed unaudited abridged accounts made up to 31 January 2026 ([Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/07123963/filing-history)).
