## Overview

*El País* is [Spain's](/places/spain/) leading daily newspaper, a Madrid-based Spanish-language daily founded on 4 May 1976, six months after the death of dictator Francisco Franco, and owned by the media group PRISA ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/); [Fundación Princesa de Asturias](https://www.fpa.es/en/princess-of-asturias-awards/laureates/1983-diario-el-pais/?texto=trayectoria)). It became the newspaper of Spain's transition to democracy, won the Prince of Asturias Award for Communication and Humanities in 1983, and in 2026 leads Spain's digital news subscriptions with 451,000 subscribers ([Fundación Princesa de Asturias](https://www.fpa.es/en/princess-of-asturias-awards/laureates/1983-diario-el-pais/?texto=trayectoria); [Reuters Institute for the Study of Journalism](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/spain)).

The paper was founded by José Ortega Spottorno, the son of exiled philosopher José Ortega y Gasset, who wanted it to be the leading progressive voice in the country's nascent free press ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)). Its first editor was Juan Luis Cebrián, who led the paper from its launch in May 1976 until November 1988 ([Instituto Cervantes](https://cultura.cervantes.es/londres/en/di%C3%A1logos-blanco-white%3A-juan-luis-cebri%C3%A1n-/131226)). Fifty years on, it employs about 400 journalists, runs newsrooms in Brussels, Mexico City and Washington, publishes six digital international editions, and reaches a daily online audience of about four million ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)). Only the Spanish edition is still printed, and the paper has more than 490,000 subscribers across all editions ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)).

## Born with Spanish democracy

*El País* published its first issue on 4 May 1976, as Spain embarked on its transition to democracy ([Fundación Princesa de Asturias](https://www.fpa.es/en/princess-of-asturias-awards/laureates/1983-diario-el-pais/?texto=trayectoria)). Its first editorials defined it as an independent, quality newspaper and an advocate of pluralist democracy, with a European vocation ([Fundación Princesa de Asturias](https://www.fpa.es/en/princess-of-asturias-awards/laureates/1983-diario-el-pais/?texto=trayectoria)). Two years later a new constitution established Spain as a democratic state ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)).

On the evening of 23 February 1981, members of the Civil Guard and the Spanish Army attempted a coup, holding parliamentarians hostage for 18 hours while tanks took to the streets ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)). *El País* was the country's only paper to go to press that night, headlined "*El País*, con la constitución" — "*El País*, with the constitution" ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)). The current editor in chief, Jan Martínez Ahrens, keeps a framed copy of that front page on his office wall; it was the first thing he brought in when he took the top job in 2025 ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)).

In May 1983 the jury of the Prince of Asturias Award for Communication and Humanities unanimously granted the prize to the newspaper *El País* of [Madrid](/places/madrid/), praising its reporting, the high quality of its cultural work, its committed defence of democracy and freedom, and its international presence; the citation also called the paper a symbol of modern Spain ([Fundación Princesa de Asturias](https://www.fpa.es/en/princess-of-asturias-awards/laureates/1983-diario-el-pais/?texto=trayectoria)).

*El País* also built quality controls that were new to Spanish journalism: a published style guide, the figure of the reader's ombudsman, and a Statute of the Editorial Board, approved in 1980, governing relations between editorial staff, management and the publishing company ([Fundación Princesa de Asturias](https://www.fpa.es/en/princess-of-asturias-awards/laureates/1983-diario-el-pais/?texto=trayectoria)). Its European vocation showed in 1989, when it joined a joint news network with *The Independent*, *La Repubblica* and *Le Monde* through the European Newspapers Association ([Fundación Princesa de Asturias](https://www.fpa.es/en/princess-of-asturias-awards/laureates/1983-diario-el-pais/?texto=trayectoria)). Cebrián stayed at the helm until November 1988, when he became editor-in-chief and chief executive of the newspaper and chief executive of Grupo PRISA ([Instituto Cervantes](https://cultura.cervantes.es/londres/en/di%C3%A1logos-blanco-white%3A-juan-luis-cebri%C3%A1n-/131226)).

## From print leader to subscription business

In 2019, the first year it generated more money through digital advertising than print, it had 21 million monthly readers in January 2020, according to Comscore, out of 34.5 million internet users in Spain ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/spain-publishers-warily-pivot-subscriptions/)). The market it was selling subscriptions into was cautious: only 7% of Spaniards had made an ongoing payment for news in the previous year, according to the Reuters Digital News Report ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/spain-publishers-warily-pivot-subscriptions/)).

The paper had already been burnt once by charging online: in 2002 it introduced a hard paywall for its electronic edition that drastically reduced visits to the site, and it was pulled down in 2005, by which time *El País* had lost the top spot to *[El Mundo](/organizations/el-mundo/)*, largely because search-engine traffic was throttled behind the wall ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/spain-publishers-warily-pivot-subscriptions/)). The second attempt was designed to avoid that mistake. A metered paywall was announced in March 2020 — ten articles a month free, then €10 a month for unlimited access — and launched on 1 May 2020, after the pandemic delayed the planned 1 March start by three months ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/spain-publishers-warily-pivot-subscriptions/); [International Symposium on Journalism / Knight Center for Journalism in the Americas](https://isoj.org/borja-echevarria-talks-about-the-success-of-digital-subscriptions-and-the-transformation-of-spanish-newspaper-el-pais/)).

In April 2020, at the pandemic peak, *El País* recorded 185 million unique visitors and more than 1 billion page views ([International Symposium on Journalism / Knight Center for Journalism in the Americas](https://isoj.org/borja-echevarria-talks-about-the-success-of-digital-subscriptions-and-the-transformation-of-spanish-newspaper-el-pais/)). It had been prepared for over a year, including a registration model introduced in 2019 that gathered reader data before anyone was asked to pay ([International Symposium on Journalism / Knight Center for Journalism in the Americas](https://isoj.org/borja-echevarria-talks-about-the-success-of-digital-subscriptions-and-the-transformation-of-spanish-newspaper-el-pais/)). It worked faster than expected: the paper reached 100,000 digital subscribers in less than a year and was expected to reach 200,000 by May 2022 ([International Symposium on Journalism / Knight Center for Journalism in the Americas](https://isoj.org/borja-echevarria-talks-about-the-success-of-digital-subscriptions-and-the-transformation-of-spanish-newspaper-el-pais/)).

## El País today: a global Spanish-language paper

The paper that was once a Madrid daily now publishes six digital international editions covering Latin America, Mexico, Colombia, Chile, Argentina and the US, in separate Spanish- and English-language versions ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)). Its newsrooms sit in Brussels, Mexico City and Washington, with correspondents elsewhere ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)).

Parent company Grupo Prisa carries debt of about €779 million, which it has agreed to repay by 2029, mostly accumulated just before the global financial crisis of 2007 and 2008 through overspending on television and radio projects ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)). In March 2026 the group announced a plan to lift its media arm's revenue to €520 million over three years, from €430 million in 2025, with subscribers rising to 800,000 and further expansion into Latin America ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)). Governance changed in step: PRISA's majority shareholder, Joseph Oughourlian, became Chairman of *El País* in March 2025 after Carlos Núñez resigned, when plans for a new digital terrestrial television channel were rejected ([Reuters Institute for the Study of Journalism](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2025/spain)).

The paper calls itself independent and progressive; its editor says it answers not to a particular party but to its convictions ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)). Safeguards include a code of conduct, journalists from the paper on its board, and the editor in chief's veto over everything published, including ads ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)). It is also adapting verification to the AI era through a collaboration with Maldita.es and other fact-checkers ([Reuters Institute for the Study of Journalism](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/spain)). It operates in a polarised climate — trust in news overall in Spain is 33% ([Reuters Institute for the Study of Journalism](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/spain)).

## Setbacks and scrutiny

Two setbacks mark the paper's modern history. The first was commercial. The hard paywall of 2002 throttled search traffic, cost the paper its leading position to *El Mundo*, and was abandoned in 2005 ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/spain-publishers-warily-pivot-subscriptions/)). The second was editorial, and far more public. On 24 January 2013 *El País* ran a large, blurry front-page photograph captioned as Venezuelan President Hugo Chávez on an operating table, billed as a "global exclusive" under the headline "The Secret of Chavez' Illness" ([WAMC / NPR](https://www.wamc.org/2013-01-25/spains-el-pais-apologizes-for-photo-that-was-not-of-cancer-stricken-chavez?_amp=true)). The photograph was not of Chávez at all: it was a screenshot from a video of another man, uploaded to YouTube in 2008 ([WAMC / NPR](https://www.wamc.org/2013-01-25/spains-el-pais-apologizes-for-photo-that-was-not-of-cancer-stricken-chavez?_amp=true)). The paper pulled the photograph from its website within hours, posted an apology, and said it had received the image from the Spanish photo agency Gtres Online; *El Mundo* and the Associated Press said they had also been offered the photograph but turned it down ([WAMC / NPR](https://www.wamc.org/2013-01-25/spains-el-pais-apologizes-for-photo-that-was-not-of-cancer-stricken-chavez?_amp=true)). Couriers were sent to collect the incorrect copies from newsstands and halt delivery across Spain, Europe and the Americas, and a second print edition was dispatched without the photograph or the article ([WAMC / NPR](https://www.wamc.org/2013-01-25/spains-el-pais-apologizes-for-photo-that-was-not-of-cancer-stricken-chavez?_amp=true)). Venezuela vowed to sue.

The paper also faces a standing political charge: critics accuse it of aligning with the centre-left Spanish Socialist Workers' Party (PSOE), currently in government ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)). Martínez Ahrens rejects the charge, saying the answer is on the paper's front page and website, and that it does not work to confirm its readers' biases: "We don't work to confirm the biases of our readers. A newspaper's duty is to do good journalism" ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)). The paper marked its 50th anniversary in 2026, its editor attributing its longevity in part to a refusal to publish "información basura" — low-quality information he compares to junk food ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was El País founded?**
*El País* published its first issue on 4 May 1976, six months after the death of dictator Francisco Franco and at the start of Spain's transition to democracy. It was founded by José Ortega Spottorno, with Juan Luis Cebrián as its first editor, and it marked its 50th anniversary in 2026 ([Fundación Princesa de Asturias](https://www.fpa.es/en/princess-of-asturias-awards/laureates/1983-diario-el-pais/?texto=trayectoria); [Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)).

**Who owns El País?**
The paper is owned by the Spanish media group PRISA, the country's largest media group. Its majority shareholder, Joseph Oughourlian, became Chairman of *El País* in March 2025, after Carlos Núñez resigned when plans for a new digital terrestrial television channel were rejected ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/); [Reuters Institute for the Study of Journalism](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2025/spain)).

**How many subscribers does El País have?**
It leads Spain with 451,000 digital subscribers, according to the Reuters Institute's Digital News Report 2026, ahead of *El Mundo* on 181,000. Monocle reported more than 490,000 subscribers across all editions in October 2026, and the group plans to reach 800,000 within three years ([Reuters Institute for the Study of Journalism](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/spain); [Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)).

**What did El País do during the 1981 coup attempt?**
On the night of 23 February 1981, while parliamentarians were held hostage for 18 hours and tanks took to the streets, *El País* was the only Spanish paper to go to press, with the front-page headline "*El País*, con la constitución" — "*El País*, with the constitution" ([Monocle](https://monocle.com/culture/media-industry/el-pais-editor-in-chief-jan-martinez-ahrens-on-defending-democracy/)).
