## Overview

AOL is the American internet company that, as America Online, put millions of households online in the 1990s — and outlived its own dial-up business to survive as an email and web-portal brand now owned by Italy's [Bending Spoons](/organizations/bending-spoons/) ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL); [Reuters](https://srnnews.com/italys-bending-spoons-to-acquire-aol-secures-2-8-billion-in-debt-financing/)). Founded in 1985 as Quantum Computer Services, it merged with Time Warner, was spun off, sold to [Verizon](/organizations/verizon/), folded into Yahoo, and sold to Bending Spoons in a deal reported at about $1.4 billion that closed in January 2026 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL); [Reuters](https://srnnews.com/exclusive-yahoo-nears-deal-to-sell-aol-to-italys-bending-spoons-for-1-4-billion-sources-say/); [TechFundingNews](https://techfundingnews.com/after-aol-and-eventbrite-bending-spoons-lands-first-post-ipo-deal-with-1-3b-airtable-acquisition/)).

That needs care: "AOL" now names two different things — the historic dial-up company, which no longer exists independently, and the surviving portal and email brand, which is what Bending Spoons bought.

## From Quantum Computer Services to America's on-ramp

The company began in 1985 as Quantum Computer Services, a small online-services provider in Vienna, Virginia, catering to [Apple](/organizations/apple/) II and Macintosh computer users ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL)). Four years later it changed its name to America Online, and in 1992–93 it expanded to [Microsoft](/organizations/microsoft/) Windows-based personal computers, a shift that dramatically accelerated subscriber growth ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL)). Steve Case, Quantum's cofounder and vice president of marketing, became chief executive officer in 1991, and on 19 March 1992 America Online completed its initial public offering, becoming one of the earliest consumer internet companies to trade publicly ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL)).

Case drove growth through easy-to-use software, accessible pricing, and one of the most extensive direct-mail marketing campaigns in corporate history — millions of floppy disks and compact discs promising hours of free internet access ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL)). The bundle was self-contained — email, chat rooms, news, weather, and games in one place — and by the end of the 1990s AOL had surpassed 20 million subscribers ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL)). In November 1998 it announced the $4.2 billion stock purchase of Netscape Communications ([Computer History Museum](https://www.computerhistory.org/tdih/November/24/)).

## The Time Warner merger — and the long unwind

Case negotiated a merger with media giant Time Warner valued at $106 billion, announced in January 2000 and, at the time, the largest corporate merger in U.S. history; the deal closed in January 2001, creating AOL Time Warner, with Case as chair ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL)). He stepped down as chief executive when the merger was completed and resigned as chair in 2003, as the company struggled with the fallout from the dot-com crash and a sharp decline in advertising revenue ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL)). The unwind took the rest of the decade. The merged company dropped AOL from its name in 2003, and by late 2009 Time Warner spun AOL off as an independent company, then worth about $2 billion, with Tim Armstrong — a former Google advertising executive hired in March 2009 — as chair and chief executive ([Associated Press](https://phys.org/news/2009-12-aol-regains-independence-warner.html?loadCommentsForm=1); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL)). Under Armstrong, AOL shifted toward digital content and advertising, acquiring The Huffington Post in 2011 for $315 million ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL)).

## What people actually remember: dial-up, AIM, and the bundle

For many users in the 1990s, AOL's dial-up service was the internet: connecting meant listening to beeps and static as the modem negotiated with AOL's servers, sometimes followed by disconnection if someone picked up a phone extension ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL)). At its peak in the early 2000s, AOL served more than 30 million subscribers worldwide, while its dial-up base alone peaked at 26.7 million in 2002 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL); [Associated Press](https://phys.org/news/2009-12-aol-regains-independence-warner.html?loadCommentsForm=1)). The shift to broadband and wireless access gradually eroded that base: by 2015 just 2.1 million dial-up subscribers remained, and the U.S. Census Bureau estimated just 163,401 U.S. households still used dial-up in 2023 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL)). In August 2025 AOL said it would discontinue the service on 30 September, ending one of the last major dial-up offerings in the country ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL)).

The cultural residue outlasted the access business. The "You've got mail" alert became lodged in popular culture and inspired a Hollywood romantic comedy of the same name in 1998, and AOL pioneered online community-building through buddy lists and AOL Instant Messenger (AIM), which operated from 1997 until it was shut down in 2017 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL)). Beyond access, AOL developed TMZ.com (in partnership with [Warner Bros.](/organizations/warner-bros/)), AOL Video, MapQuest, and Advertising.com, one of the largest third-party online advertising networks in the U.S. in the mid-2000s ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL)). Even at the 2025 sale, the economics were not just advertising: AOL earned revenue from advertising and from subscriptions including LifeLock identity theft protection, LastPass password management, and McAfee Multi Access malware protection ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/exclusive-yahoo-nears-deal-to-sell-aol-to-italys-bending-spoons-for-1-4-billion-sources-say/)).

## A new owner in 2026: Bending Spoons

In June 2015 Verizon agreed to acquire AOL for about $4.4 billion, completed later that month, to gain its advertising technology and digital content properties ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL)). Two years later Verizon purchased Yahoo's core internet business for $4.48 billion and merged it with AOL under a new name, Oath, renamed Verizon Media Group in 2018 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL)). In May 2021 Verizon sold the media division — including both AOL and Yahoo — to private equity firm Apollo Global Management for $5 billion, retaining a 10% stake, and as of 2025 the business operated under the Yahoo name ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/AOL)).

The final chapter so far began in October 2025, when Milan-based Bending Spoons said it would acquire AOL from Apollo-backed Yahoo, securing a $2.8 billion debt financing package to support the acquisition as well as research, development, and future deal-making — while disclosing no price ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/italys-bending-spoons-to-acquire-aol-secures-2-8-billion-in-debt-financing/)). Sources familiar with the matter had put the price at about $1.4 billion when Reuters reported the talks on 1 October ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/exclusive-yahoo-nears-deal-to-sell-aol-to-italys-bending-spoons-for-1-4-billion-sources-say/)). Bending Spoons described AOL as "an important and beloved business with a brand that has stood the test of time," citing high subscriber retention and around 30 million monthly and 8 million daily active users, and ranking it among the top 10 most used email apps globally ([Reuters](https://srnnews.com/italys-bending-spoons-to-acquire-aol-secures-2-8-billion-in-debt-financing/)). The acquisition closed in January 2026, putting AOL in the same Bending Spoons portfolio as [Airtable](/organizations/airtable/), alongside Evernote, WeTransfer, Vimeo, and Eventbrite ([TechFundingNews](https://techfundingnews.com/after-aol-and-eventbrite-bending-spoons-lands-first-post-ipo-deal-with-1-3b-airtable-acquisition/)). Bending Spoons' stated model is to buy established digital products, restructure them, and hold them rather than flip them ([TechFundingNews](https://techfundingnews.com/after-aol-and-eventbrite-bending-spoons-lands-first-post-ipo-deal-with-1-3b-airtable-acquisition/)).
