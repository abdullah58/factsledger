## Overview

Partners Capital is a London-founded outsourced investment office (OCIO) that manages money for
endowments, foundations, senior investment professionals and prominent families, investing client
assets through external fund managers rather than running its own branded funds
([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2cz096w6yb7a4zm07ydj4/corner-office/partners-capital-found-a-partner-of-its-own-in-general-atlantic)).
The firm was incorporated in England on 29 August 2001 — first as Law 2338 Limited, then briefly as
Partner Wealth Management Limited, before settling on its current name by March 2002
([UK Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/04277937)).
By its own September 2025 statement it oversaw more than $70 billion in assets
([Business Wire via Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20260211784861/partners-capital-closes-1-billion-merlin-iv-co-investment-fund-strengthening-global-platform)).

The firm's origin story carries an unusual symmetry that no competing profile leads with. Co-founder
Paul Dimitruk — who co-founded the firm in 2001, served as its chairman from 2001 to 2020 and is
now its vice chairman — was previously Group Managing Director of [General Atlantic](/organizations/general-atlantic/)
([Partners Capital](https://partners-cap.com/team/paul-dimitruk/)). In 2024, General Atlantic
bought a minority stake in the firm Dimitruk co-founded
([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2cz096w6yb7a4zm07ydj4/corner-office/partners-capital-found-a-partner-of-its-own-in-general-atlantic)).
His co-founder, Stan Miranda, spent his earlier career at Bain & Company, rising to director and
chairman of its Worldwide Executive Committee in 1997–98, and holds a Harvard MBA and a CPA
designation; he led Partners Capital as CEO from 2001 to 2020 and as chairman until July 2024, when
he became Chairman Emeritus
([Partners Capital](https://partners-cap.com/team/stan-miranda/)).

## A clientele drawn from the investment industry itself

One of Partners Capital's distinctive traits, reported in 2022, is that a large share of its private
clients are themselves owners and partners of investment firms — clients drawn from Blackstone, KKR,
Bain Capital, TA Associates and Summit Partners, among others. "If you're a successful partner in
an investment firm you often want to diversify your wealth and sources of return beyond what your
own firm offers," Dimitruk told Family Wealth Report, adding that the firm builds "family endowments"
for these professionals, many of them first-generation wealth creators
([Family Wealth Report](https://www.familywealthreport.com/article.php?id=195259)).

Its institutional side covers the classic endowment roster: the endowment funds of Oxford and
Cambridge in the UK and Syracuse University in the US, plus arts, science and healthcare
institutions such as the Metropolitan Opera Company and the Cancer Research Institute
([Family Wealth Report](https://www.familywealthreport.com/article.php?id=195259)). Dimitruk has
argued that family wealth should be treated "as a long-term endowment much like a college
endowment… that frames everything," and pointed to one multi-generational family client spanning
seven generations and more than 250 members
([Family Wealth Report](https://www.familywealthreport.com/article.php?id=195259)).

## Succession by design: the General Atlantic deal

In February 2024, General Atlantic acquired a minority stake in Partners Capital through one of its
funds rather than using balance-sheet capital, with the firm's investment operations remaining
separate ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2cz096w6yb7a4zm07ydj4/corner-office/partners-capital-found-a-partner-of-its-own-in-general-atlantic)).
CEO Arjun Raghavan said the deal's purpose was succession, not consolidation: "We were trying to
provide liquidity for the first generation of shareholders and founding partners," many of them in
their seventies and eighties, after a roughly two-year search for the right investor
([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2cz096w6yb7a4zm07ydj4/corner-office/partners-capital-found-a-partner-of-its-own-in-general-atlantic)).

Early backers, including the Rothschild Foundation and Sir Ronald Cohen, a founding partner at Apax
Capital, took some money off the table but remained shareholders
([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2cz096w6yb7a4zm07ydj4/corner-office/partners-capital-found-a-partner-of-its-own-in-general-atlantic)).
Current leadership received equity incentives carrying at least a six-year lockup, with shares also
set aside for future partners — "reset[ting] the economics" to "more easily achieve the generational
transfer," as Raghavan put it
([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2cz096w6yb7a4zm07ydj4/corner-office/partners-capital-found-a-partner-of-its-own-in-general-atlantic)).

The deal followed the amicable departures of two chief investment officers: Colin Pan, who left in
2021 to launch his own firm, and Suzanne Streeter, who left in 2023 for a family office. Pan had
joined the firm in April 2010 and became CIO in 2016, succeeding founder Miranda
([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswn8kfmcaiqcfpqdm9s/corner-office/partners-capital-cio-colin-pan-steps-down));
Streeter and Alex Band had been named co-CIOs from July 2021, and Band remains in the role
([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2cz096w6yb7a4zm07ydj4/corner-office/partners-capital-found-a-partner-of-its-own-in-general-atlantic)).

## Growth, offices and the co-investment platform

The firm's scale has grown steadily on its own statements: $38 billion at the end of 2020, $48
billion in mid-2022, over $50 billion in late 2023, over $60 billion in early 2025, and over $70
billion as of September 2025
([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bswn8kfmcaiqcfpqdm9s/corner-office/partners-capital-cio-colin-pan-steps-down),
[Family Wealth Report](https://www.familywealthreport.com/article.php?id=195259),
[PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/partners-capital-goes-open-source-with-two-decades-of-research-supporting-their-institutional-investment-approach-301997433.html),
[Dallas Innovates](https://s24806.pcdn.co/london-based-global-investment-firm-partners-capital-opens-dallas-office/),
[Business Wire via Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20260211784861/partners-capital-closes-1-billion-merlin-iv-co-investment-fund-strengthening-global-platform)).

Its footprint reached eight offices with the January 2025 opening of a Dallas office — the fourth in
the US — led by Olivia Newell as Head of Texas alongside co-founder Dimitruk, responding to demand
from regional endowments, foundations, family offices and private clients
([Dallas Innovates](https://s24806.pcdn.co/london-based-global-investment-firm-partners-capital-opens-dallas-office/)).
At the time the firm counted more than 380 people, of which a 256-strong team covered investment and
client relations; by early 2026 it reported more than 400 employees across Boston, New York, Dallas,
London, San Francisco, Paris, Singapore and Hong Kong
([Dallas Innovates](https://s24806.pcdn.co/london-based-global-investment-firm-partners-capital-opens-dallas-office/),
[Business Wire via Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20260211784861/partners-capital-closes-1-billion-merlin-iv-co-investment-fund-strengthening-global-platform)).

Beyond its OCIO business, the firm runs the Merlin co-investment platform, launched in 2019, which
invests alongside high-conviction private equity managers in single-asset transactions — typically
lower-middle-market and middle-market buyouts, investing $25 million to over $100 million per
transaction
([Business Wire via Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20260211784861/partners-capital-closes-1-billion-merlin-iv-co-investment-fund-strengthening-global-platform)).
Merlin IV, closed in February 2026 at over $1 billion, was the platform's fourth and largest fund,
backed by family offices and institutional investors across North America, Europe, Asia and Africa;
since the platform's launch it has deployed over $2 billion across more than 60 transactions
([Business Wire via Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20260211784861/partners-capital-closes-1-billion-merlin-iv-co-investment-fund-strengthening-global-platform)).

In November 2023 the firm also launched its Intellectual Capital Library, opening to the public more
than two decades of investment research — including 15 years of its previously client-exclusive
*Insights* annual report and over 150 pieces of insight organized around its five-layer investment
framework
([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/partners-capital-goes-open-source-with-two-decades-of-research-supporting-their-institutional-investment-approach-301997433.html)).
The launch explicitly paid tribute to David Swensen's *Pioneering Portfolio Management*, the book
that shared the Yale endowment model with the world
([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.co.uk/news-releases/partners-capital-goes-open-source-with-two-decades-of-research-supporting-their-institutional-investment-approach-301997433.html)).
