## Two arenas, one holding company

TKO Group Holdings, Inc. (NYSE: TKO) is the premium sports-and-entertainment company Endeavor built by combining the UFC and WWE — the world's premier mixed martial arts organization and the global leader in sports entertainment ([Endeavor](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/EDR/endeavor-announces-close-of-ufc-r-and-wwe-r-transaction-to-create-m56f9xlz8qr6.html)). The deal closed on September 12, 2023, and TKO shares began trading on the New York Stock Exchange the same day ([Endeavor](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/EDR/endeavor-announces-close-of-ufc-r-and-wwe-r-transaction-to-create-m56f9xlz8qr6.html)).

At launch the combined enterprise was valued at $21.4 billion — $12.1 billion for the UFC, $9.3 billion for WWE. Endeavor took a 51% controlling interest and existing WWE shareholders kept 49%. The leadership stacked up as expected: Endeavor CEO Ari Emanuel as TKO CEO, Vince McMahon as executive chairman, Mark Shapiro in a dual executive role, [Dana White](/people/dana-white/) as UFC CEO, and Nick Khan as WWE president, over an 11-member board — six appointed by Endeavor, five by WWE ([Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/article/endeavor-deal-combines-wwe-and-ufc-form-214b-sports-entertainment-company/)).

## The McMahon exit

Four months after the close, the company lost its most famous figure. On January 26, 2024, Vince McMahon resigned as TKO executive chairman — one day after former WWE employee Janel Grant filed a federal lawsuit in Connecticut accusing him and former executive John Laurinaitis of serious sexual misconduct, including allegations that McMahon forced Grant into a sexual relationship to keep a job and offered her to a star wrestler for sex ([Associated Press](https://www.knkx.org/2024-01-26/wrestling-icon-vince-mcmahon-resigns-from-wwe-parent-company-after-sex-abuse-suit)). McMahon denied the allegations — calling the suit "replete with lies, obscene made-up instances that never occurred" — and said he was leaving "out of respect" for WWE and TKO ([Associated Press](https://www.knkx.org/2024-01-26/wrestling-icon-vince-mcmahon-resigns-from-wwe-parent-company-after-sex-abuse-suit)). McMahon had bought the company that became WWE from his father in 1982 ([Associated Press](https://www.knkx.org/2024-01-26/wrestling-icon-vince-mcmahon-resigns-from-wwe-parent-company-after-sex-abuse-suit)).

## Beyond UFC and WWE

Leading write-ups of TKO tend to stop at the 2023 merger, but the company's current shape is bigger. On February 28, 2025, TKO closed its $3.25 billion all-equity acquisition from Endeavor of the IMG, On Location, and Professional Bull Riders (PBR) businesses, with Endeavor receiving roughly 26.54 million TKO operating-company units plus an equal number of Class B shares ([TKO](https://files.quartr.com/reports/2df6e-2025-11-05-09-05-15.pdf?ref=cHVibGljLmNvbQ==)). The company now reports three operating segments — UFC, WWE, and IMG (IMG plus On Location) — alongside "Corporate and Other," which carries PBR, boxing management and promotional fees, and unallocated corporate costs ([TKO](https://files.quartr.com/reports/2df6e-2025-11-05-09-05-15.pdf?ref=cHVibGljLmNvbQ==)).

Its own description of scale: one billion households across 210 countries and territories, more than 500 live events a year, and more than three million fans attending ([TKO](https://files.quartr.com/reports/2df6e-2025-11-05-09-05-15.pdf?ref=cHVibGljLmNvbQ==)).

## The 2025 media-rights flywheel

August 2025 was TKO's loudest month. On August 11, TKO and [Paramount](/organizations/paramount/) — the Skydance-owned studio — announced a seven-year U.S. media-rights agreement for UFC events beginning in 2026, at an average annual value of $1.1 billion ($7.7 billion total), weighted toward the back end of the term ([TKO](https://files.quartr.com/reports/2df6e-2025-11-05-09-05-15.pdf?ref=cHVibGljLmNvbQ==)). It ends the U.S. pay-per-view model: all 13 numbered events and 30 Fight Nights move to Paramount+, with select cards simulcast on CBS ([BusinessWorld](https://bworldonline.com/sports/2025/08/12/690983/paramount-wins-exclusive-us-rights-for-ufc-in-7-7-billion-deal/)).

Five days earlier, on August 6, TKO and ESPN had announced a five-year agreement bringing WWE's premium live events in the U.S. to ESPN's new direct-to-consumer offering, beginning in September 2025 at an average annual value of $325 million ([TKO](https://files.quartr.com/reports/2df6e-2025-11-05-09-05-15.pdf?ref=cHVibGljLmNvbQ==)). CEO Ariel Emanuel's Q3 comment tied the arc together: "Having secured landmark multiyear media rights deals for UFC, WWE, and Zuffa Boxing, our conviction in TKO has never been stronger" ([TKO](https://files.quartr.com/reports/2df6e-2025-11-05-09-05-15.pdf?ref=cHVibGljLmNvbQ==)). TKO discloses little else about Zuffa Boxing beyond the management and promotional fees it books in Corporate and Other ([TKO](https://files.quartr.com/reports/2df6e-2025-11-05-09-05-15.pdf?ref=cHVibGljLmNvbQ==)).

For the third quarter of 2025 TKO reported $1.120 billion in revenue — $325.2 million from UFC, $402.1 million from WWE, $336.7 million from IMG, $63.3 million from Corporate and Other — with $106.8 million in net income and $360.2 million in adjusted EBITDA. It raised full-year 2025 guidance to $4.69–$4.72 billion in revenue and $1.57–$1.58 billion in adjusted EBITDA ([TKO](https://files.quartr.com/reports/2df6e-2025-11-05-09-05-15.pdf?ref=cHVibGljLmNvbQ==)).
