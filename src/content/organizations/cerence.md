## Overview

Cerence Inc. is an American conversational-AI company focused on the automotive industry, headquartered in Burlington, Massachusetts ([CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2020/10/08/nio-cerence-collaboration-on-voice-tech/)). It was created on October 1, 2019, when Nuance Communications spun off its automotive division as an independent company, carrying with it two decades of in-car voice-recognition work and contracts with nearly every major automaker ([CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2020/10/08/nio-cerence-collaboration-on-voice-tech/)).

The scale of that inheritance is the core of the Cerence story: as of 2020 the company's technology powered nearly 300 million vehicles on the road in more than 70 languages, supporting automakers including Audi, BMW, Daimler, Toyota, Ford, Geely, SAIC, and GM ([CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2020/10/08/nio-cerence-collaboration-on-voice-tech/)). By 2026, about 50% of worldwide auto production featured Cerence technology — roughly 11.3 million cars in a single quarter ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/07/cerence-crnc-q2-2026-earnings-transcript/); [StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/CRNC/cerence-ai-reports-third-quarter-results-revenue-up-12-yo-y-1cj6zaaey1e3.html)).

## Winning back automakers from the hyperscalers

Cerence's pitch to automakers is independence: a customizable, deeply integrated in-car AI stack that does not lock a carmaker into a single Big Tech ecosystem ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/07/cerence-crnc-q2-2026-earnings-transcript/)). That positioning is producing wins against the hyperscalers it competes with — including a multiyear, multiplatform contract with a major Japanese automaker described as a "win-back from a hyperscaler," and BYD as a new customer for Cerence's next-generation xUI interface, with BYD vehicles already rolling off production lines ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/07/cerence-crnc-q2-2026-earnings-transcript/)). In August 2026 the company added a multi-brand xUI award from Stellantis and the first customer for its Mobile Work Agent ([StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/CRNC/cerence-ai-reports-third-quarter-results-revenue-up-12-yo-y-1cj6zaaey1e3.html)).

The business is shifting from one-time license sales toward recurring connected services: connected-services revenue grew more than 20% year over year in Q3 FY2026, and newly signed xUI programs carry price-per-unit economics above the company's current $5.09 trailing run rate ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/07/cerence-crnc-q2-2026-earnings-transcript/); [StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/CRNC/cerence-ai-reports-third-quarter-results-revenue-up-12-yo-y-1cj6zaaey1e3.html)).

## Suing Big Tech over voice patents

Cerence is also pressing its intellectual-property portfolio in court. In 2026 the company filed a patent-infringement action against Amazon and confirmed ongoing patent matters with Apple, Sony, and TCL, with court dates set for late 2026 — though management expects no near-term IP revenue from the cases ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/07/cerence-crnc-q2-2026-earnings-transcript/)). CEO Brian Krzanich framed the litigation as a long-term strategy to protect technology embedded across the company's voice and conversational-AI products ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/07/cerence-crnc-q2-2026-earnings-transcript/)).

## A maturing business: first buyback in 2026

Cerence's financial profile in FY2026 reflects a company stabilizing after volatile years. Q3 FY2026 brought revenue of $69.6 million (up 12% year over year), adjusted EBITDA of $13.5 million (up 50%), and free cash flow of $19.6 million ([StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/CRNC/cerence-ai-reports-third-quarter-results-revenue-up-12-yo-y-1cj6zaaey1e3.html)). The board authorized the company's first-ever share repurchase program — up to $30 million over 12 months, funded from existing cash and operating free cash flow — and guided full-year FY2026 revenue to $310–$314 million ([StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/CRNC/cerence-ai-reports-third-quarter-results-revenue-up-12-yo-y-1cj6zaaey1e3.html)). The five-year backlog stood at $971 million as of Q2 FY2026 ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/07/cerence-crnc-q2-2026-earnings-transcript/)).
