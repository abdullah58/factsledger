## Overview

C-nario was an Israeli digital-signage software company founded in Tel Aviv in
2002
([TV Technology](https://www.tvtechnology.com/news/graphics-firm-teams-with-digital-signage-company),
[Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/c-nario)), and its most
unusual distinction is what happened after it stopped being
independent: on 27 October 2011 it was acquired by YCD Multimedia in an all-stock
deal whose price was never disclosed
([M&A Navigator](https://www.thefreelibrary.com/YCD+Multimedia+takes+over+digital+signage+firm+C-nario.-a0270878595)),
yet its flagship product refused to die with the company name. YCD folded
C-nario Messenger — the company's multi-display playback engine — into its own
Retail Advertising and Merchandising Platform (RAMP), presenting the combination
as the first step of an integrated product roadmap
([AVNetwork](https://www.avnetwork.com/avnetwork/sweat-the-small-stuff)), and
trade press kept reporting new C-nario-branded deployments at major airports
afterward
([SignageInfo](https://www.signageinfo.com/digital-signage/4478/c-nario-signage-at-milan/?amp=1)).
At its peak the company sold display, distribution and management software for
digital-signage networks across banking, transportation, retail, advertising and
entertainment, with deployments its partners put at 40 countries
([TV Technology](https://www.tvtechnology.com/news/graphics-firm-teams-with-digital-signage-company)).

## Rise: from a Tel Aviv startup to the world's airports

C-nario was founded in 2002 in [Israel](/places/israel/) by Rami Bahar, and was
described in company histories as a spin-off of Disk-In
([Crunchbase](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/c-nario)).
Its early backers were Carmel Ventures of Herzliya and Opus Capital of Menlo
Park, California
([TV Technology](https://www.tvtechnology.com/news/graphics-firm-teams-with-digital-signage-company));
industry editor Adrian Cotterill later reported the investment scale as $5
million from Carmel and $3.5 million from Opus
([DailyDOOH](http://www.dailydooh.com/archives/690)).
The product line grew into a four-part suite: C-nario Messenger, the flagship
display, distribution and management platform; C-nario Advertiser, an
advertising management system; C-nario Entertainer, for complex video, audio and
data displays; and C-Sign, a web-based product for small and medium businesses
([SignageInfo](https://www.signageinfo.com/digital-signage/5783/brazils-inter-soccer-club-selects-c-nario-for-new-multimedia-displays-at-team-museum/)).

The company's breakthrough came in transportation. JCDecaux, the world's largest
airport advertising company, hired C-nario to build the advertising network for
the new Terminal 9 at JFK International Airport: a mile-long run of forty
70-inch LCD screens along the moving walkway, all driven by C-nario's Messenger
platform ([DailyDOOH](http://www.dailydooh.com/archives/690)).
JCDecaux and C-nario then collaborated on the Prestige Digital Network at Los
Angeles International Airport, where C-nario's Advertiser 4 platform managed
more than fifty advertising content channels across pairs of back-to-back
70-inch screens, synchronized and monitored through C-nario software
([TV Technology](https://www.tvtechnology.com/news/los-angeles-international-airport-adds-digital-advertising-network)).
In Shanghai, C-nario's Messenger and Universal Player systems were installed
across Pudong and Hongqiao airports — more than 200 displays including a
64-screen video wall — with JCDecaux Momentum Shanghai operating the network
after selecting C-nario over several rival vendors
([AVNetwork](https://www.avnetwork.com/avnetwork/rdl-introduces-hr-mp2-amp-hr-mp2a-dual-microphone-preamplifiers)).

By the late 2000s the customer list trade press attributed to the company
included [CNN](/organizations/cnn/), Nike, McDonald's, Disney, Comcast,
Motorola and Barclays, and the company had formed an alliance with Norway's
Vizrt, which supplied 3D graphics for C-nario's global display business
([TV Technology](https://www.tvtechnology.com/news/graphics-firm-teams-with-digital-signage-company)).
A 2007 year-in-review noted installations at McDonald's in Singapore, Manpower
and Club Med sites in France, and London's O2 Arena, and had the company
exhibiting at the ISE trade show in Amsterdam that January
([DailyDOOH](http://www.dailydooh.com/archives/690)).

## The 2011 acquisition

On 27 October 2011, YCD Multimedia — itself an Israeli-rooted digital-signage
firm, founded in 1999 and headquartered by then in New York — announced it was
acquiring C-nario in a stock transaction
([Digital Signage Today](https://www.digitalsignagetoday.com/news/ycd-picks-up-c-nario/)).
The deal was accompanied by a new $6 million fundraising round led by Carmel
Ventures, Opus Capital, Pitango Venture Capital and Plenus, with other existing
shareholders participating
([Digital Signage Today](https://www.digitalsignagetoday.com/news/ycd-picks-up-c-nario/)).
YCD said the combined company would deliver an end-to-end product spanning
content management to measurement and analytics, and claimed it would serve
more than 2,000 brands, including Fortune 500 names, in over 40 countries
([Digital Signage Today](https://www.digitalsignagetoday.com/news/ycd-picks-up-c-nario/)).

Independent trade commentary greeted that customer figure with open skepticism.
Dave Haynes, writing at Sixteen:Nine, noted drily: "2,000 brands? Well, YCD says
it has in excess of 1,500 customers, much of that in retail. Hmmm."
([Sixteen:Nine](https://www.sixteen-nine.net/?p=3090)).
Haynes still called the merger logical — two technically strong companies with
shared Israeli roots and complementary strengths in video walls — and a
heartening sign that investors were putting serious money into signage software
during a lingering recession
([Sixteen:Nine](https://www.sixteen-nine.net/?p=3090)).
YCD co-founder and CEO Noam Levavi continued to lead the combined entity from
New York, while C-nario CEO Tamir Ginat's role in making the transaction happen
was publicly credited by C-nario chairman Avi Zeevi
([Sixteen:Nine](https://www.sixteen-nine.net/?p=3090)).

## Afterlife: the product outlives the company

What makes C-nario historically interesting is that the acquisition did not end
the product story. YCD announced the integration of its RAMP platform with
C-nario Messenger, letting YCD partners and customers combine Messenger's
multi-display playback engine with RAMP's graphical interface for retail
campaign management ([AVNetwork](https://www.avnetwork.com/avnetwork/sweat-the-small-stuff)).
YCD chief technology officer Dani Zeevi described it as only the first step of
an integrated roadmap toward a full suite of RAMP-based products
([AVNetwork](https://www.avnetwork.com/avnetwork/sweat-the-small-stuff)).

The C-nario name also kept winning deployments on its own. Trade press reported
C-nario Messenger driving new networks at Milan's Malpensa and Linate airports
— forty 57-inch totems in Malpensa's Terminals 1 and 2, forty-one at Linate —
plus sixty-seven more across Milan's subway stations, in a project led by
Italian outdoor advertiser IGPDecaux with screens built by Hantarex
([SignageInfo](https://www.signageinfo.com/digital-signage/4478/c-nario-signage-at-milan/?amp=1)).
C-nario marketing chief Yael Elstein framed the win as confirmation of the
company's standing in transportation, alongside its existing airport footprint
of twelve US airports and the Shanghai airports
([SignageInfo](https://www.signageinfo.com/digital-signage/4478/c-nario-signage-at-milan/?amp=1)).
Earlier, Brazil's Sport Club Internacional had chosen C-nario Messenger as the
engine behind the multimedia displays at its Porto Alegre stadium museum —
including a kaleidoscopic mosaic of thirty-six screens called "The Torch" —
with C-nario CEO Tamir Ginat calling sports a strategic market for the firm
([SignageInfo](https://www.signageinfo.com/digital-signage/5783/brazils-inter-soccer-club-selects-c-nario-for-new-multimedia-displays-at-team-museum/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not assert the Disk-In spin-off as independently verified
fact: it comes from company-provided history, and trade press read for this
rewrite corroborates the founders and backers but not the spin-off mechanics.
It does not present the "2,000 brands in 40 countries" figure as verified —
that was the merging companies' own claim, questioned at the time by
independent commentary. It does not document YCD Multimedia's later fate or the
exact date the C-nario brand was retired; business databases list the company
as closed while trade press continued to report C-nario-branded deployments,
and the sources read do not resolve that tension. Early milestones repeated in
tertiary mirrors — such as a 2003 Philips agreement, a 2005 Bank Hapoalim
rollout figure, or a 2008 Digi Award — were not independently verified and are
not asserted here; a first-party YCD case study does confirm a Cnario-suite
deployment at Bank Hapoalim's digital branches, without a date
([YCD Multimedia](https://ycdmultimedia.com/case-studies/bank-hapoalim/)).
