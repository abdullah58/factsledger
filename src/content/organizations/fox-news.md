## Overview

Fox News is usually described as a ratings machine or a political lightning rod. Less often told is the business story underneath: at its 1996 launch the channel could not get onto cable in New York City at all. Time Warner — the country's second-largest cable operator and the owner of CNN, the very channel Fox was built to challenge — refused to carry it on its New York systems, and Fox charged that Time Warner was using its vertically integrated cable-and-content holdings to keep a competitor off the air in the country's largest media market ([European Audiovisual Observatory](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/download/605/pdf)). The mayor's office sided with Fox and put the channel on one of the city's public-access channels, arguing that more news outlets served the public interest; Time Warner sued, and on November 6, 1996, a federal judge blocked the city's move, accepting Time Warner's argument that it violated the company's free-speech rights ([European Audiovisual Observatory](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/download/605/pdf)). The city kept fighting — in February 1997 New York was still urging a federal appeals court to overturn the order keeping Fox News off the city-run channel ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/specials/new-york-fights-time-warner-in-murdoch-dispute-197022101027_1.html)). A decade later the power dynamic had flipped: in 2006 Fox News was demanding $1 per subscriber per month from cable and satellite operators — roughly triple the 25 to 35 cents it was then collecting, and about double what CNN averaged — with a Fox executive arguing the network had "a significant advantage over 90% of the industry" while being "paid as if we're at the bottom" ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2006/fox-news-wants-1month-per-subscriber-from-cable-firms/)). The channel that once battled in federal court just to appear on the dial had become one of the most expensive tolls on it.

It went on the air on October 7, 1996, built by Rupert Murdoch and founding chief executive Roger Ailes as a direct challenger to CNN, and it is the most-watched cable news network in the United States, a spot it has held for most of the time since 2002 ([Associated Press](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2017/05/18/roger-ailes-former-fox-news-ceo-and-political-strategist-dies-77); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/dominions-defamation-case-against-fox-poised-trial-after-delay-2023-04-18/?utm_campaign=Above%20the%20Law%20Daily&utm_source=hs_email&utm_medium=email&utm_content=254906729&_hsenc=p2ANqtz-87dYGqCIlq5a06GJspaY8XfOXBOPlSOEukzJydzSATeKCXAES7N5DJDsvD-GWLJZ0jU9HN7-GMru4w60y-qi7eviu8Bpk6IkYjePxAIcUTKrqw-F0); [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/morning-media-newsfeed/fox-news-turns-25/)).

Today the channel sits inside Fox News Media, a unit of Fox Corporation — the company that started operating on its own on March 19, 2019, after Disney bought most of 21st Century Fox's entertainment assets ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Fox-Corporation)). Its chief executive is Suzanne Scott, in the job since May 2018 and the first woman to hold it ([Fox Corporation](https://www.foxcorporation.com/news/business/2018/suzanne-scott-named-chief-executive-officer-of-fox-news/)).

The last decade of Fox News has been defined as much by the courtroom as by the ratings chart. In April 2023 the network paid $787.5 million to settle Dominion Voting Systems' defamation suit over false claims that Dominion's machines rigged the 2020 election — the largest known U.S. media defamation settlement ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/dominions-defamation-case-against-fox-poised-trial-after-delay-2023-04-18/?utm_campaign=Above%20the%20Law%20Daily&utm_source=hs_email&utm_medium=email&utm_content=254906729&_hsenc=p2ANqtz-87dYGqCIlq5a06GJspaY8XfOXBOPlSOEukzJydzSATeKCXAES7N5DJDsvD-GWLJZ0jU9HN7-GMru4w60y-qi7eviu8Bpk6IkYjePxAIcUTKrqw-F0)). As of September 2026, a $2.7 billion suit from Smartmatic was still moving through the courts, and a shareholder case accusing Fox's board of failing its oversight duties had just produced a court order for hundreds of internal documents ([NPR](https://www.ksfr.org/npr-news/2026-09-20/judge-orders-fox-to-hand-over-hundreds-of-documents-in-shareholders-case)).

## How it started

Murdoch came into the project with a working template. He had already launched Sky News in Britain, and in early 1996 he asked Roger Ailes — a former Republican political strategist who had been running CNBC — to build an American equivalent aimed squarely at CNN ([Associated Press](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2017/05/18/roger-ailes-former-fox-news-ceo-and-political-strategist-dies-77)). The channel launched on October 7, 1996, under the slogan "Fair and Balanced" ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/morning-media-newsfeed/fox-news-turns-25/); [Associated Press](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2017/05/18/roger-ailes-former-fox-news-ceo-and-political-strategist-dies-77)).

Getting onto cable was its first real battle. Time Warner — the country's second-largest cable operator and the owner of CNN — refused to carry Fox News on its New York City systems, and Fox charged that Time Warner was using its vertically integrated cable-and-content holdings to keep a competitor off the air in the country's largest media market ([European Audiovisual Observatory](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/download/605/pdf)). The mayor's office sided with Fox and put the channel on one of the city's public-access channels, arguing that more news outlets served the public interest; Time Warner sued, and on November 6, 1996, a federal judge in the Southern District of New York blocked the city's move, accepting Time Warner's argument that forcing it to air the channel violated its First Amendment rights ([European Audiovisual Observatory](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/download/605/pdf)). The city kept fighting into the next year: in February 1997 New York was still urging a federal appeals court to overturn the order keeping Fox News off the city-run channel ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/specials/new-york-fights-time-warner-in-murdoch-dispute-197022101027_1.html)).

A decade later the power dynamic had flipped completely. In 2006 Fox News was demanding $1 per subscriber per month from cable and satellite operators — roughly triple the 25 to 35 cents it was then collecting, and about double the roughly 50 cents CNN averaged — with a Fox executive arguing the network had "a significant advantage over 90% of the industry" while being "paid as if we're at the bottom" ([Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2006/fox-news-wants-1month-per-subscriber-from-cable-firms/)). The channel that had once battled in federal court just to appear on the dial had become one of the most expensive tolls on it.

The bet paid off faster than most people expected. By 2002 Fox News had overtaken CNN as the cable-news ratings leader, and it has kept that position through most of the years since ([Associated Press](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2017/05/18/roger-ailes-former-fox-news-ceo-and-political-strategist-dies-77)). Nielsen's big-data-plus-panel measurement put its Q2 2026 prime-time audience at 2.493 million viewers — first not just among cable news channels but among all cable networks in total viewers, ahead of MS NOW's 1.063 million and CNN's 711,000 in prime time ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/tvnewser/here-are-the-2nd-quarter-of-2026-cable-news-ratings/)). Its most-watched program that quarter was The Five, averaging 3.575 million viewers, followed by Jesse Watters Primetime, Gutfeld!, Hannity, and Special Report with Bret Baier ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/tvnewser/here-are-the-2nd-quarter-of-2026-cable-news-ratings/)).

## Who owns and runs it

Fox News is part of Fox News Media, which also includes Fox Business Network, [Fox Nation](/organizations/fox-nation/), and FOX Weather, plus digital, audio, podcast, and book operations ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Fox-Corporation); [Fox Corporation](https://www.foxcorporation.com/management/business-unit-leaders/suzanne-scott/)). The company says the group reaches nearly 200 million people a month across its platforms ([Fox Corporation](https://www.foxcorporation.com/management/business-unit-leaders/suzanne-scott/)).

Suzanne Scott has run the network since May 17, 2018, when she was named CEO of Fox News and Fox Business Network — the first woman to hold the top job — with Jay Wallace becoming president and executive editor of Fox News ([Fox Corporation](https://www.foxcorporation.com/news/business/2018/suzanne-scott-named-chief-executive-officer-of-fox-news/)). Above her sits Fox Corporation itself: Lachlan Murdoch is its executive chair and CEO, and Rupert Murdoch stepped down as chair in 2023 to become chairman emeritus ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Fox-Corporation)).

## The scandals that reshaped it

The network's modern history turns on two crises, one about its workplace and one about its coverage. On July 6, 2016, anchor Gretchen Carlson sued Roger Ailes alleging sexual harassment, and more women followed with similar claims ([Associated Press](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2017/05/18/roger-ailes-former-fox-news-ceo-and-political-strategist-dies-77)). Ailes left the network on July 21, 2016, while denying the allegations, and Rupert Murdoch took over as interim head ([Associated Press](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2017/05/18/roger-ailes-former-fox-news-ceo-and-political-strategist-dies-77)). Ailes died on May 18, 2017, at 77 ([Associated Press](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2017/05/18/roger-ailes-former-fox-news-ceo-and-political-strategist-dies-77)).

Less than a year after Ailes's exit, the network cut ties with its biggest star. On April 19, 2017, Fox severed ties with Bill O'Reilly after a New York Times report that he and Fox had paid five women $13 million over 15 years to settle harassment claims — a story that had already driven advertisers away. O'Reilly called the claims "completely unfounded" ([Agence France-Presse](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/companies-markets/consumer-healthcare/fox-news-dumps-embattled-host-over-harassment-allegations)).

Then came the 2020 election. On April 18, 2023, Fox settled Dominion Voting Systems' defamation suit for $787.5 million — Dominion had sought $1.6 billion — with Fox acknowledging the court's rulings that certain claims broadcast about Dominion were false ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/dominions-defamation-case-against-fox-poised-trial-after-delay-2023-04-18/?utm_campaign=Above%20the%20Law%20Daily&utm_source=hs_email&utm_medium=email&utm_content=254906729&_hsenc=p2ANqtz-87dYGqCIlq5a06GJspaY8XfOXBOPlSOEukzJydzSATeKCXAES7N5DJDsvD-GWLJZ0jU9HN7-GMru4w60y-qi7eviu8Bpk6IkYjePxAIcUTKrqw-F0)). Less than a week later, on April 24, the network announced that [Tucker Carlson](/people/tucker-carlson/) was leaving immediately; his last show had aired April 21, Fox's statement gave no reason, and NPR reported the decision came from Suzanne Scott and Lachlan Murdoch ([NPR](https://www.wuot.org/2023-04-24/tucker-carlson-ousted-at-fox-news-following-networks-787-million-settlement)).

The legal fallout is still running. Smartmatic's $2.7 billion defamation suit was active as of September 2026 ([NPR](https://www.ksfr.org/npr-news/2026-09-20/judge-orders-fox-to-hand-over-hundreds-of-documents-in-shareholders-case)). That same month, a Delaware judge ordered Fox Corporation to hand over hundreds of documents in a shareholder suit challenging whether the board exercised proper oversight after the Dominion settlement — including roughly 700 documents referencing "phone hacking" and a dispute over Rupert Murdoch's text-message retention ([NPR](https://www.ksfr.org/npr-news/2026-09-20/judge-orders-fox-to-hand-over-hundreds-of-documents-in-shareholders-case)).

## Where it stands in 2026

By the audience numbers, Fox News's dominance is intact. It finished 2025 as the highest-rated network in all of cable for the tenth consecutive year — its highest-rated non-election year on record, according to the company ([Fox Corporation](https://www.foxcorporation.com/management/business-unit-leaders/suzanne-scott/)) — and Q2 2026 put it first among all cable networks in both prime-time and total-day total viewers ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/tvnewser/here-are-the-2nd-quarter-of-2026-cable-news-ratings/)). AFP has described its position as "unique and powerful if controversial," watched by a "deep and committed conservative fan base" that largely overlaps with Trump voters ([Agence France-Presse](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/companies-markets/consumer-healthcare/fox-news-dumps-embattled-host-over-harassment-allegations)).

At the corporate level, Fox is trying to pair that linear-TV strength with streaming. On June 15, 2026, Fox Corporation announced a definitive agreement to acquire Roku for about $22 billion in cash and stock — $160 per share, made up of $96 in cash plus 0.9693 Fox shares — with the deal expected to close in the first half of 2027 if shareholders and regulators approve ([Fox Corporation](https://investor.foxcorporation.com/news/corp-press-releases/2026/fox-corporation-to-acquire-roku-inc/)). Lachlan Murdoch framed it as a way to combine Fox's live news and sports with one of the biggest streaming platforms; as of September 2026 the acquisition had not closed ([Fox Corporation](https://investor.foxcorporation.com/news/corp-press-releases/2026/fox-corporation-to-acquire-roku-inc/)).
