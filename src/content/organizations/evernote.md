## Overview

Evernote is a note-taking and task-management app that began in Stepan Pachikov's 2000 venture, became a defining smartphone-era productivity brand after its 2008 relaunch, and was sold to Milan-based [Bending Spoons](/organizations/bending-spoons/) in early 2023 after a decade of retrenchment ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/bending-spoons-acquires-evernote-marking-the-end-of-an-era/)). Under Bending Spoons it shed staff, moved its centre of operations to Europe, and capped its free plan at 50 notes and one notebook ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/27/bending-spoons-lays-off-129-evernote-staffers/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2635822)).

The original product was built around an idea Pachikov had pursued since his handwriting-recognition work: notes stored on an "infinite roll of paper" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/bending-spoons-acquires-evernote-marking-the-end-of-an-era/)). Under Phil Libin, who joined the company in 2007, Evernote shifted its focus to the web, smartphones and Mac, starting with Evernote 3.0 in 2008 — and the timing worked: its web service reached 11 million users within its first three years ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/bending-spoons-acquires-evernote-marking-the-end-of-an-era/)). Between 2010 and 2015 it raised hundreds of millions of dollars in venture capital from investors including Sequoia, Meritech Capital and Japanese media company Nikkei, and launched a business in China, Yinxiang Biji, as it sought to expand rapidly ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/bending-spoons-acquires-evernote-marking-the-end-of-an-era/)).

## From smartphone-era hit to retrenchment

Evernote was reportedly valued at nearly a billion dollars in 2013 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/bending-spoons-acquires-evernote-marking-the-end-of-an-era/)). But the expansion that followed did not stick. Libin pursued partnerships with physical-goods brands like Moleskine and Pfeiffer, launching Evernote-branded desk accessory lines that failed to catch on in a major way ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/bending-spoons-acquires-evernote-marking-the-end-of-an-era/)). In July 2015, former [Google](/organizations/google/) Glass executive Chris O'Neill replaced Libin as CEO, and that October Evernote laid off 18% of its staff and closed three of its 10 global offices ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/bending-spoons-acquires-evernote-marking-the-end-of-an-era/)).

The pattern repeated in 2018. In August that year Evernote lost its chief technical officer, chief financial officer, chief product officer and head of HR, as it sought funding in a potential down-round from the unicorn valuation it had hit in 2012 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/29/ian-small-former-head-of-tokbox-takes-over-as-evernote-ceo-from-chris-oneill/)). Weeks later it laid off 54 people, about 15% of its workforce, with O'Neill justifying the cut as a correction for overexpansion and "inefficiency" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/bending-spoons-acquires-evernote-marking-the-end-of-an-era/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/29/ian-small-former-head-of-tokbox-takes-over-as-evernote-ceo-from-chris-oneill/)). In October 2018, former TokBox head Ian Small replaced O'Neill as CEO ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/10/29/ian-small-former-head-of-tokbox-takes-over-as-evernote-ceo-from-chris-oneill/)). Under Small, Evernote reached $100 million in annual recurring revenue, millions of paying customers and more than 250 million users — but, TechCrunch reported, it largely failed to keep pace with competitors like Notion, relying heavily on a consumer-focused freemium model while eschewing the collaboration features its rivals embraced ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/bending-spoons-acquires-evernote-marking-the-end-of-an-era/)).

## Under Bending Spoons: cuts, relocation, and a smaller free tier

Bending Spoons announced its acquisition of Evernote on 16 November 2022, with terms undisclosed and the deal expected to close in early 2023; the buyer, fresh off a $340 million venture round, said it would apply its "proprietary technologies" to augment Evernote's usefulness and strengthen its reach ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/bending-spoons-acquires-evernote-marking-the-end-of-an-era/)). The deal was finalised in early 2023, and the cuts followed quickly ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/05/what-is-bending-spoons-everything-to-know-about-aols-acquirer/)). On Friday 17 February 2023, Evernote laid off 129 people across product design, engineering, HR, sales, customer service and marketing; a spokesperson told TechCrunch the company "has been unprofitable for years and the situation was unsustainable in the long term" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/27/bending-spoons-lays-off-129-evernote-staffers/)).

Months later came the relocation. On 23 June 2023 Evernote announced most operations would move to Europe, and layoff communication to employees followed on 5 July; the company blog said the transition required that most of its Chile- and US-based employees be laid off ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/evernote-is-laying-off-staff-and-relocating-to-europe)). The one confirmed number for that round is narrower than the headlines: a Bending Spoons spokesperson confirmed to TechRadar that 98 people were impacted by the 5 July layoff, with some invited to join the Bending Spoons selection process ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/evernote-is-laying-off-staff-and-relocating-to-europe)). Departing staff were typically offered 16 weeks' salary, up to a year of health insurance coverage, and a performance bonus paid pro-rata ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/evernote-is-laying-off-staff-and-relocating-to-europe)).

Then came the monetisation squeeze. From 4 December 2023, new and existing free users were capped at a maximum of 50 notes and one notebook per account, after the plan had been tested on under 1% of free users; existing users above the limit could still view, edit, export, share and delete their notes, but could not add beyond the cap without deleting content ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2635822)). The company said most free users fell below the new limit, while acknowledging the change might push some customers towards "reconsidering" their relationship with Evernote ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2635822)). The through-line is Bending Spoons' stated model: it "aims to hold forever, and has never sold an acquired business," making its brands more financially successful through technology and AI but also through price hikes and layoffs that have caused controversy ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/05/what-is-bending-spoons-everything-to-know-about-aols-acquirer/)). Its co-founder and chief product officer Matteo Danieli told TechCrunch that despite all the changes, customer retention at its acquired products has been "remarkably stable" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/05/what-is-bending-spoons-everything-to-know-about-aols-acquirer/)). By March 2026 the wider Bending Spoons portfolio served over 500 million monthly active users, with 2025 revenue of $1.31 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/05/what-is-bending-spoons-everything-to-know-about-aols-acquirer/)).
