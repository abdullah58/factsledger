## Overview

ICAP was a London interdealer broker — a firm that matches buyers and sellers of financial products in markets with no central exchange — founded in 1986 by Michael Spencer as Intercapital, the first brokerage devoted primarily to over-the-counter derivatives trading ([Euromoney](https://www.euromoney.com/article/27bjsstsqxhkmh1v3mebt/opinion/macaskill-on-markets-nexs-michael-spencer-a-title-for-the-billion-dollar-broker/)). In 2016 it split in two: its voice-broking business and the ICAP name went to Tullett Prebon, now TP ICAP, while its electronic markets became [NEX Group](/organizations/nex-group/), bought by CME Group for £3.9 billion in 2018.

At its peak ICAP was the world's largest interdealer broker, according to the UK's Financial Conduct Authority ([Financial Conduct Authority](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/icap-europe-limited-fined-%C2%A314-million-significant-failings-relation-libor)).

This is not the same ICAP as the International Capital Market Association, and the name itself moved in the split — which is why "ICAP" today is a brand inside TP ICAP, not a standalone listed company ([TP ICAP](https://tpicap.com/tpicap/media/press-releases/2020/tp-icaps-data-analytics-division-launches-ndf-premium)). ICAP also matters to the blockchain story: through its fintech investment arm, [Euclid Opportunities](/organizations/euclid-opportunities/), it co-led [Axoni](/organizations/axoni/)'s $18 million Series A in December 2016 ([Financial Adviser Magazine](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/vc-deal-of-the-day-axioni-raises-18m-series-a-46944.html?print)).

## Intercapital and the rise of the interdealer broker

Michael Spencer founded the business in 1986 as Intercapital, which later morphed into ICAP, and it was the first interdealer brokerage devoted primarily to over-the-counter derivatives trading — a new breed of City business that depended on derivatives specialists with advanced degrees and mathematical ability ([Euromoney](https://www.euromoney.com/article/27bjsstsqxhkmh1v3mebt/opinion/macaskill-on-markets-nexs-michael-spencer-a-title-for-the-billion-dollar-broker/)). Interdealer brokers sit between banks and other dealers, matching trades in bonds, currencies, swaps and other products that trade off-exchange, where there is no central order book. ICAP grew into a markets operator and provider of post-trade risk mitigation and information services, running electronic platforms such as EBS for foreign exchange and BrokerTec for government bonds, plus post-trade businesses TriOptima and Traiana ([Financial Adviser Magazine](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/vc-deal-of-the-day-axioni-raises-18m-series-a-46944.html?print); [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/institutional-forex/execution/ebs-brokertec-revenues-2-icap-will-rename-nex/)). It also built a fintech investment arm, Euclid Opportunities, to back firms in post-trade risk management, data and financial-market applications ([Financial Adviser Magazine](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/vc-deal-of-the-day-axioni-raises-18m-series-a-46944.html?print)). By 2013 the FCA described the ICAP Group as the world's largest inter-dealer broker ([Financial Conduct Authority](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/icap-europe-limited-fined-%C2%A314-million-significant-failings-relation-libor)).

## LIBOR: the £14 million fine

ICAP's reputation was badly damaged in September 2013, when the Financial Conduct Authority fined ICAP Europe Limited £14 million for misconduct relating to the London Interbank Offered Rate, or LIBOR — the benchmark rate banks charge each other, used to price trillions of dollars of derivatives, loans and mortgages ([Financial Conduct Authority](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/icap-europe-limited-fined-%C2%A314-million-significant-failings-relation-libor)). Between October 2006 and November 2010, ICAP brokers colluded with traders at UBS to manipulate Japanese yen LIBOR, emailing skewed rate suggestions to panel banks and asking them to make specific submissions ([Financial Conduct Authority](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/icap-europe-limited-fined-%C2%A314-million-significant-failings-relation-libor)). UBS made at least 330 written requests to ICAP brokers for inappropriate submissions — a number that shows the manipulation was systematic, not a one-off lapse — and one broker received corrupt bonus payments for his help ([Financial Conduct Authority](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/icap-europe-limited-fined-%C2%A314-million-significant-failings-relation-libor)). ICAP Europe was the first broking firm fined over LIBOR, and the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission imposed a further $65 million penalty in the same coordinated action ([Financial Conduct Authority](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/icap-europe-limited-fined-%C2%A314-million-significant-failings-relation-libor)).

## The 2016 split, NEX and the road to CME

In November 2015 ICAP announced it would sell its global hybrid voice-broking business to rival Tullett Prebon, including its iSwap and Fusion platforms, and that the remaining business would be renamed NEX Group ([Financial Adviser Magazine](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/vc-deal-of-the-day-axioni-raises-18m-series-a-46944.html?print)). In May 2016 the company confirmed the plan: from day one after completion it would trade as NEX Group, focused on electronic markets and post-trade business, while the ICAP name transferred to Tullett Prebon ([The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/icap-to-become-nex-after-tullett-deal-completes/)). The deal completed on December 30, 2016, worth £1.28 billion in shares, and Tullett Prebon renamed itself TP ICAP; its chief executive, John Phizackerley, called it a deal that "creates the leading interdealer broker in the world" ([FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/final-ma-deal-2016-tullett-prebon-adds-voice-brokerage-buying-nex-1-1-billion/)). Two years later the electronic half ended too: CME Group agreed to buy NEX for £3.9 billion in March 2018 and completed the deal that November, retiring the NEX name while keeping BrokerTec, EBS, Traiana and TriOptima as sub-brands, with Spencer joining the CME board as a special advisor ([The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/cme-completes-3-9-billion-nex-group-acquisition/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is an interdealer broker?**
An interdealer broker matches buyers and sellers of financial products — bonds, currencies, swaps — in over-the-counter markets where there is no central exchange or order book ([Financial Conduct Authority](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/icap-europe-limited-fined-%C2%A314-million-significant-failings-relation-libor)). ICAP was the first such broker devoted mainly to OTC derivatives trading.

**What happened to ICAP in 2016?**
ICAP split in two. Its voice-broking business and the ICAP brand went to Tullett Prebon in a £1.28 billion all-share deal completed on December 30, 2016, while the remaining electronic-markets and post-trade business was renamed NEX Group ([FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/final-ma-deal-2016-tullett-prebon-adds-voice-brokerage-buying-nex-1-1-billion/)).

**Does ICAP still exist?**
Yes, as a brand. ICAP is now one of the core broking brands inside TP ICAP, alongside Tullett Prebon and PVM, and its FUSION platform carries both voice and electronic liquidity ([TP ICAP](https://tpicap.com/tpicap/media/press-releases/2020/tp-icaps-data-analytics-division-launches-ndf-premium)). It is no longer a standalone listed company.

**What was ICAP's role in the Axoni investment?**
Through Euclid Opportunities, its fintech investment business, ICAP co-led Axoni's $18 million Series A in December 2016 alongside Wells Fargo, backing the blockchain firm's post-trade technology for capital markets ([Financial Adviser Magazine](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/vc-deal-of-the-day-axioni-raises-18m-series-a-46944.html?print)).
