## Overview

Eurex is Europe's leading derivatives exchange — the German marketplace for futures and options on interest rates, equities, equity indices and volatility, created in June 1998 from the merger of the Deutsche Terminbörse (DTB) and Switzerland's SOFFEX. Headquartered in Eschborn near Frankfurt and wholly owned by Deutsche Börse since 2012, it traded 2,080.5 million contracts in 2024, with its Euro-Bund futures the benchmark for eurozone government debt ([Eurex](https://www.eurex.com/ex-en/find/about-us); [Deutsche Börse](https://deutsche-boerse.com/dbg-en/media/news-stories/press-releases/Full-year-and-December-2024-figures-at-Eurex-4250262)).

Eurex is more than a trading venue: with Eurex Clearing as central counterparty and Eurex Repo for secured funding, it runs an integrated trading-and-clearing chain on its T7 platform ([Eurex](https://www.eurex.com/ex-en/find/about-us)). Its history is the story of how electronic trading beat open outcry — the Battle of the Bund — and its volumes still ride on eurozone rates, the market it won from London's LIFFE in 1998 ([University of Houston](https://bauer.uh.edu/spirrong/dtbtip2.pdf)).

## From the DTB to Eurex

Eurex's roots are legal as much as technical. Until 1989, German law treated futures as unenforceable gambling contracts, which made a German derivatives exchange impossible; reforms that year to the German Exchange Act gave exchange-traded derivatives legal protection ([MarketsWiki](https://MarketsWiki.com/wiki/Eurex_Frankfurt_AG)). The Deutsche Terminbörse was created at the start of 1990 — first trading day 26 January — founded by seventeen German banks as one of the world's first fully electronic exchanges, offering equity index futures and stock options, adding Bund futures in November 1990 ([Deutsche Börse](https://deutsche-boerse.com/dbg-en/about-us/deutsche-boerse-group/company-history); [MarketsWiki](https://MarketsWiki.com/wiki/Eurex_Frankfurt_AG)). In February 1998 Deutsche Börse, [SBF–Bourse de Paris](/organizations/euronext-paris/), the Swiss exchange and Dow Jones introduced the STOXX equity indices, and in June 1998 Deutsche Börse and the Swiss Stock Exchange merged the DTB and SOFFEX to create Eurex, the world's first cross-border futures exchange ([Deutsche Börse](https://deutsche-boerse.com/dbg-en/about-us/deutsche-boerse-group/company-history)). The parents ran it jointly — Deutsche Börse holding 50% of the voting rights and 85% of the share capital — until it bought out SIX in January 2012 ([MarketsWiki](https://MarketsWiki.com/wiki/Eurex_Frankfurt_AG)).

## The Battle of the Bund

Eurex's defining episode came before it existed, in the fight for Bund futures — the contract on ten-year German government bonds. At the start of 1997 about 65% of Bund futures trading sat on London's open-outcry LIFFE, with the DTB holding the rest. Over the next 21 months the market tipped completely: Eurex's volume passed LIFFE's in January 1998, it held over 95% of the market by July and over 99.9% by October ([University of Houston](https://bauer.uh.edu/spirrong/dtbtip2.pdf)). The academic account credits fee cuts and operational efficiency rather than spreads — Eurex slashed fees in January 1998 while LIFFE did not respond immediately, and automated trading cost roughly a third of open-outcry trading ([University of Houston](https://bauer.uh.edu/spirrong/dtbtip2.pdf)). German banks' sponsorship of the electronic platform mattered as much as the technology: building a cheaper market was not enough; the order flow had to follow. The episode made electronic trading the default for European derivatives and left Eurex with a near-monopoly in German government-bond futures.

## Trading, clearing and the CNP settlement

Today Eurex trades futures and options across interest rates, equities, equity indices and volatility, led by Euro-Bund futures — the benchmark for euro-denominated government debt — and EURO STOXX 50 futures and options ([Eurex](https://www.eurex.com/ex-en/find/about-us)). In 2024 it traded 2,080.5 million contracts, up 9% on 2023: interest rate derivatives led with 972.2 million, up 26%, ahead of index derivatives at 784.6 million and equity derivatives at 312.6 million ([Deutsche Börse](https://deutsche-boerse.com/dbg-en/media/news-stories/press-releases/Full-year-and-December-2024-figures-at-Eurex-4250262)). Every trade is cleared through Eurex Clearing, the central counterparty, on the T7 trading platform launched in 2013 and the C7 clearing architecture launched in 2014 ([MarketsWiki](https://MarketsWiki.com/wiki/Eurex_Frankfurt_AG)). That machinery is visible when a listed company is taken over: after [La Banque Postale](/organizations/la-banque-postale/)'s bid for [CNP Assurances](/organizations/cnp-assurances/) succeeded on 3 June 2022, Eurex settled existing positions in CNP derivatives at theoretical fair value, using the €20.90 ex-dividend tender price — the €21.90 offer less the €1 dividend — as the basis ([Eurex](https://www.eurex.com/ex-en/rules-regs/corporate-actions/corporate-action-information/CNP-Assurances-S.A.-Takeover-Updated-06-June-2022--2827330)).

## FAQ

### What is Eurex?

Eurex is the German derivatives exchange for futures and options on interest rates, equities, equity indices and volatility, created in June 1998 from the merger of the Deutsche Terminbörse and Switzerland's SOFFEX. Headquartered in Eschborn near Frankfurt, it is wholly owned by Deutsche Börse and describes itself as Europe's leading derivatives exchange.

### Who owns Eurex?

Deutsche Börse AG owns Eurex outright. The exchange was jointly operated by Deutsche Börse and the SIX Swiss Exchange from its 1998 founding, with Deutsche Börse holding 50% of the voting rights and 85% of the share capital, until Deutsche Börse acquired the remaining shares in January 2012.

### What are Eurex's most important products?

Its flagship contracts are Euro-Bund futures, the benchmark for euro-denominated government debt, and EURO STOXX 50 futures and options. In 2024 interest rate derivatives were its largest business at 972.2 million contracts, ahead of index derivatives at 784.6 million and equity derivatives at 312.6 million.

### What was the Battle of the Bund?

It was the 1997–1998 contest for Bund futures — contracts on ten-year German government bonds — between London's open-outcry LIFFE and the electronic DTB, Eurex's predecessor. LIFFE held about 65% of the market at the start of 1997; by October 1998 Eurex held over 99.9%, won on lower fees and cheaper electronic trading.

### How did Eurex handle the CNP Assurances takeover?

When La Banque Postale's €21.90-a-share bid for CNP Assurances succeeded on 3 June 2022, Eurex settled existing positions in CNP futures and options at theoretical fair value, using the €20.90 ex-dividend tender price as the calculation basis under its contract specifications for Eurex Deutschland.
