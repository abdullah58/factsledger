## Overview

Canaan Partners is an [American](/places/united-states/) early-stage venture capital firm founded in 1987 after spinning out of General Electric's venture capital unit ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/canaan-capital-brings-in-675m-for-its-tenth-fund); [TechCircle](https://www.techcircle.in/2012/01/10/canaan-partners-raises-600m-global-fund-to-invest-in-tech-healthcare-cos/)). It invests in technology and healthcare startups, backing them from seed and Series A rounds and following on as they grow, and it managed $6.8 billion in assets as of April 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/06/in-a-slowing-market-venture-investor-canaan-still-closes-850m-across-two-new-funds/)). Among its best-known portfolio bets is [CircleUp](/organizations/circleup/), the consumer-brands funding platform, whose $14 million Series B round Canaan led in 2014 ([New Hope Network](https://www.newhope.com/industry-news/circleup-closes-14-million-series-b-funding)).

Canaan is not the same company as Canaan Inc., the bitcoin-mining hardware maker listed on Nasdaq as CAN. That company was established in 2013, a quarter-century after Canaan Partners began investing, and builds ASIC mining machines under the Avalon brand ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/canaan-inc-provides-january-2026-bitcoin-production-and-mining-operation-updates-2026)). The shared name is a coincidence of two unrelated firms in different industries. Canaan Partners operates from offices in Silicon Valley and [New York City](/places/new-york-city/), with an office in Herzliya, [Israel](/places/israel/), and an affiliate fund there ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/canaan-capital-brings-in-675m-for-its-tenth-fund); [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article.aspx?did=1000039960)).

## Origins: the General Electric spinout

Canaan's history starts inside General Electric. Its founder, Harry Rein, had joined GE in 1979 and run its venture capital arm as president and CEO of GE Venture Capital Corporation before founding Canaan and serving as its founder and managing partner ([Allied Minds](http://alm.ir-data.com/pdf/item/newsArticle/154)). In 1987 the GE venture unit was spun out as an independent firm, and the new firm took the Canaan name ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/canaan-capital-brings-in-675m-for-its-tenth-fund)). TechCircle's 2012 fund announcement dates the firm's investing record from that year, writing that "since 1987, Canaan Partners has invested in technology start-ups and healthcare companies" ([TechCircle](https://www.techcircle.in/2012/01/10/canaan-partners-raises-600m-global-fund-to-invest-in-tech-healthcare-cos/)).

The 1987 date is corroborated by the firm's age as reported at different points: VentureBeat called it almost 20 years old in October 2014, Fierce Biotech described a 30-year history in July 2017, and TechCrunch called it a 35-year-old firm in April 2023 ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/canaan-capital-brings-in-675m-for-its-tenth-fund); [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/canaan-unveils-800m-fund-for-fresh-wave-biotech-bets); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/06/in-a-slowing-market-venture-investor-canaan-still-closes-850m-across-two-new-funds/)). Rein later moved into healthcare investing full-time as a general partner at Foundation Medical Partners, a firm he helped form ([Allied Minds](http://alm.ir-data.com/pdf/item/newsArticle/154)). The dual technology-and-healthcare mandate he set at Canaan has survived him: the firm still splits its capital between the two sectors nearly four decades on.

## Investment strategy

Canaan is an early-stage investor by design. It focuses on first through third rounds, invests in the seed stage with the intent of following on as a company grows, and puts about 80 percent of its investments into these early stages ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/canaan-capital-brings-in-675m-for-its-tenth-fund)). Including follow-on capital, its average investment runs about $15–20 million per company ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/canaan-capital-brings-in-675m-for-its-tenth-fund)). Roughly 40 percent of its capital goes to biotech and medical technology, with the balance going to technology companies ([Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/canaan-unveils-800m-fund-for-fresh-wave-biotech-bets)). On the technology side its 2023 fund targeted enterprise software, consumer companies, frontier technology and fintech; on the healthcare side it named immunology, neurobiology and cardio as areas of interest ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/06/in-a-slowing-market-venture-investor-canaan-still-closes-850m-across-two-new-funds/)). The firm also sometimes builds companies itself rather than waiting for founders to arrive: it co-founded Day One Biopharmaceuticals, which later went public, after spotting an opportunity in oncology it thought it could start on its own ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/06/in-a-slowing-market-venture-investor-canaan-still-closes-850m-across-two-new-funds/)).

Canaan has experimented with who gets to write its checks. In 2018 it launched Canaan Beta, setting aside $20 million of its $800 million fund for its two youngest employees — Hootan Rashidifard, 28, and Adina Tecklu, 27 — to invest as a duo with full decision-making power ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/podcast/canaan-partners-gives-20-million-to-its-two-youngest-employees-to-invest-in-consumer-startups/)). By June 2018 the pair had made five seed-stage investments, writing checks of $250,000 to $500,000 into blockchain, gaming, digital media, social and digital health startups ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/podcast/canaan-partners-gives-20-million-to-its-two-youngest-employees-to-invest-in-consumer-startups/)). The bet behind the program was demographic: as the cost of starting a company fell and the US population diversified, the firm's youngest staffers might spot consumer opportunities its partners would miss ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/podcast/canaan-partners-gives-20-million-to-its-two-youngest-employees-to-invest-in-consumer-startups/)).

Geography followed the same early-stage logic. In December 2005 Canaan opened its first office outside the US, in Herzliya, Israel, managed by venture partner Izhar Shay, as part of a new strategy of opening offices abroad; an India office was expected to follow ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article.aspx?did=1000039960)). Its early Israeli bets included Mobilitec, UltraGuide and Business Layers, in which it owned 20 percent when Netegrity acquired the company for $42.5 million ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article.aspx?did=1000039960)). By 2014 the firm had offices in New York City, Silicon Valley and India, plus an affiliate fund in Israel ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/canaan-capital-brings-in-675m-for-its-tenth-fund)). The partnership itself is long-tenured: general partner Maha Ibrahim had been at the firm for 23 years when its 2023 funds closed, and Julie Grant and Nina Kjellson were general partners as of 2020 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/06/in-a-slowing-market-venture-investor-canaan-still-closes-850m-across-two-new-funds/); [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/canaan-partners-reels-800m-healthcare-and-tech-fund-again)).

## Funds and exits

Canaan has raised a new flagship fund roughly every three years since 2012, growing from $600 million to $850 million in total commitments. The sequence below comes from fund announcements read in full; the 2023 total includes a separate $200 million vehicle for backing breakout portfolio companies, not just the flagship.

| Fund | Year | Size | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canaan IX | 2012 | $600 million | Global fund; two-thirds technology, one-third healthcare |
| Canaan X | 2014 | $675 million | Tenth fund; digital health and marketplaces a growing focus |
| Canaan XI | 2017 | $800 million | Largest to date at the time; about 40% to biotech and medical technology |
| Canaan XII | 2020 | $800 million | Equal to Fund XI; raised during the COVID-19 pandemic |
| Canaan XIII | 2023 | $650 million flagship + $200 million | Oversubscribed flagship for seed and Series A; $850 million total; AUM $6.8 billion |

The fund sizes are from [TechCircle](https://www.techcircle.in/2012/01/10/canaan-partners-raises-600m-global-fund-to-invest-in-tech-healthcare-cos/), [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/canaan-capital-brings-in-675m-for-its-tenth-fund), [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/canaan-unveils-800m-fund-for-fresh-wave-biotech-bets), [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/canaan-partners-reels-800m-healthcare-and-tech-fund-again) and [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/06/in-a-slowing-market-venture-investor-canaan-still-closes-850m-across-two-new-funds/).

The exit record grew with the funds. By December 2005 Canaan had invested in 225 companies, of which 46 had reached the public markets and 55 had merged or been acquired ([Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article.aspx?did=1000039960)). By January 2012 it counted more than 85 acquisitions and 53 IPOs, with $3.5 billion under management ([TechCircle](https://www.techcircle.in/2012/01/10/canaan-partners-raises-600m-global-fund-to-invest-in-tech-healthcare-cos/)). In the five years to April 2023 it recorded ten IPOs, four public listings and eight M&A exits, generating roughly $1.7 billion in returns ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/06/in-a-slowing-market-venture-investor-canaan-still-closes-850m-across-two-new-funds/)).

Named exits show the range. In technology, Canaan backed DoubleClick before Google acquired it, Match.com before IAC acquired it, and SandForce before LSI acquired it, alongside SuccessFactors, Acme Packet and Active Network ([TechCircle](https://www.techcircle.in/2012/01/10/canaan-partners-raises-600m-global-fund-to-invest-in-tech-healthcare-cos/)). In healthcare, its companies were bought by large acquirers: Advanced BioHealing by Shire, AdvancePCS by Caremark, BiPar Sciences by Sanofi, Calixa Therapeutics by Cubist, Cerexa Pharmaceuticals by Forest Labs and Peninsula Pharmaceuticals by Johnson & Johnson ([TechCircle](https://www.techcircle.in/2012/01/10/canaan-partners-raises-600m-global-fund-to-invest-in-tech-healthcare-cos/)). Its biotech exits included Civitas Therapeutics, bought by Acorda Therapeutics for $525 million in cash; Labrys Biologics, bought by Teva for $200 million upfront; and Novira Therapeutics, bought by Johnson & Johnson for an undisclosed sum ([Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/canaan-unveils-800m-fund-for-fresh-wave-biotech-bets)). More recent outcomes include the IPOs of Day One Biopharmaceuticals in May 2021, The RealReal in June 2019 and Arvinas in September 2018, and the acquisitions of Kustomer by Meta for $1 billion in February 2022 and Axis Security by Hewlett Packard Enterprise in March 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/06/in-a-slowing-market-venture-investor-canaan-still-closes-850m-across-two-new-funds/)).

## CircleUp: the marketplace bet

Canaan's most documented single-company relationship in this profile is with CircleUp, the equity-crowdfunding platform for consumer brands. Canaan led CircleUp's $14 million Series B round, announced on March 25, 2014, alongside [Google Ventures](/organizations/gv/), [Union Square Ventures](/organizations/union-square-ventures/), Maveron and Rose Park Advisors ([New Hope Network](https://www.newhope.com/industry-news/circleup-closes-14-million-series-b-funding)). It was then the largest raise for an equity crowdfunding platform, and Canaan general partner Dan Ciporin joined CircleUp's board as part of the deal ([New Hope Network](https://www.newhope.com/industry-news/circleup-closes-14-million-series-b-funding)). Ciporin framed the bet as a repeat of an earlier one: "We saw that with Lending Club in the credit marketplace space in 2007, and we see that with CircleUp in the equity marketplace sector in 2014," he said, arguing that equity fundraising was poised for disruption on the scale of what LendingClub was doing to consumer credit ([New Hope Network](https://www.newhope.com/industry-news/circleup-closes-14-million-series-b-funding)).

Canaan stayed in for the next round. When CircleUp raised a $30 million Series C in November 2015, led by Collaborative Fund, Canaan participated as a previous investor, and the round brought CircleUp's total funding to $53 million ([Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2015/11/77146-marketplace-lender-circleup-raises-30-million-in-series-c-funding/)). The LendingClub parallel Ciporin drew was grounded in Canaan's own history: the firm had backed LendingClub in the credit marketplace space in 2007, and Ciporin sat on LendingClub's board ([New Hope Network](https://www.newhope.com/industry-news/circleup-closes-14-million-series-b-funding)). CircleUp's chief executive and co-founder [Ryan Caldbeck](/people/ryan-caldbeck/) welcomed Canaan to the board in 2014, saying the financing would build out the platform for consumer and retail brands and help investors identify companies they understood ([New Hope Network](https://www.newhope.com/industry-news/circleup-closes-14-million-series-b-funding)).

## FAQ

**When was Canaan Partners founded?** In 1987, as a spinout of General Electric's venture capital unit. Founder Harry Rein had run GE Venture Capital before founding the independent firm, and the firm's investing record is dated from 1987 in its own fund announcements.

**What does Canaan Partners invest in?** Early-stage technology and healthcare companies, from seed through Series C, with about 80 percent of investments at the early stages. Its average investment is about $15–20 million per company including follow-on capital, and roughly 40 percent of its capital goes to biotech and medical technology.

**What was Canaan's role in CircleUp's funding?** Canaan led CircleUp's $14 million Series B in March 2014, with general partner Dan Ciporin joining CircleUp's board, and participated as a previous investor in the $30 million Series C in November 2015, which brought CircleUp's total funding to $53 million.

**Is Canaan Partners the same company as Canaan Inc.?** No. Canaan Inc. (NASDAQ: CAN) is a separate company established in 2013 that designs ASIC chips and builds bitcoin-mining machines under the Avalon brand. Canaan Partners is a venture capital firm founded in 1987; the two share only a name.

**How much money does Canaan manage?** $6.8 billion in assets under management as of April 2023, when it closed $850 million across two new funds: the $650 million flagship Canaan XIII and a $200 million fund for breakaway portfolio companies. No later AUM figure was verified for this profile.
