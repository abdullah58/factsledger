## Overview

The Press Information Bureau is usually introduced the way reference pages introduce it: the Government of India's nodal agency for getting official information to the press, a century-old institution issuing press releases in a dozen languages. That description is accurate and incomplete. The more complete picture is a paradox: PIB runs the government's official Fact Check Unit — the body that flags fake news *about* the government — and in May 2025, independent fact-checkers caught the agency itself displaying misattributed footage at a wartime press briefing ([Alt News](https://www.altnews.in/political-misinformation-in-2025-who-shared-who-was-targeted-how-did-it-shape-narratives/)). A year earlier, the Bombay High Court had struck down the legal provision that would have given that same Fact Check Unit the power to compel social-media takedowns, calling it unconstitutional ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/bombay-high-court-fact-check-unit-it-rules-kunal-kamra-270722)). The agency that authenticates is itself under authentication — and that is the profile.

## What PIB is, on paper

PIB describes itself as the nodal agency of the Government of India for disseminating information to print and electronic media on government policies, programmes, initiatives and achievements — and, notably, as an interface that also feeds media reaction back to the government on "people's reaction as reflected in the media" ([Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/aboutpibn.aspx?reg=3&lang=2)). It issues press releases, press notes, explainers, factsheets, feature articles, photographs, videos and infographics, first in English, Hindi and Urdu and subsequently translated into other Indian languages to reach about 8,400 newspapers and media organisations, and it organises press conferences, briefings, ministerial interviews and press tours to project sites ([Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/aboutpibn.aspx?reg=3&lang=2)).

Its institutional roots are colonial. In June 1919, the British administration in Shimla set up a temporary cell under the Home Department — its first head, Dr L.F. Rushbrook Williams of Allahabad University, designated officer on special duty — to prepare a yearly report on India for presentation to the British Parliament ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/press-information-bureau-pib-celebrates-100-years-of-accurate-government-information-dissemination-in-india-101685645096566.html)). By the end of 1920 it had become the Central Bureau of Information, on 1 June 1923 it was formalised as the Bureau of Public Information, and in 1941 J. Natarajan became the first Indian to head it as Principal Information Officer; the organisation was renamed the Press Information Bureau in 1946, and after independence was reconstituted with the added, "more delicate" task of interpreting government facts and policies rather than merely reporting them ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/press-information-bureau-pib-celebrates-100-years-of-accurate-government-information-dissemination-in-india-101685645096566.html); [Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/aboutpibn.aspx?reg=3&lang=2)).

## The Fact Check Unit — checker and checked

In 2019, PIB established its Fact Check Unit to tackle fake news pertaining to the government and its organisations ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/press-information-bureau-pib-celebrates-100-years-of-accurate-government-information-dissemination-in-india-101685645096566.html)). The scale is considerable: addressing the Lok Sabha on 26 March 2025, I&B Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw said the unit had flagged 1,575 fake-news cases since 2022 — 338 in 2022, 557 in 2023, 583 in 2024 and 97 in 2025 as of 19 March — and had received around 5,200 queries in 2025, of which 1,811 were actionable ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/press-information-bureau-flags-1575-fake-news-cases-since-2022-ministry-of-ib-60317.htm)). The unit acts on its own cognisance as well as on complaints received through its website or WhatsApp hotline, verifies against government open sources — websites, notices, circulars, e-gazettes — and publishes corrections through its social-media handles ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/press-information-bureau-flags-1575-fake-news-cases-since-2022-ministry-of-ib-60317.htm)).

But the unit has been checked as well as checking. [Alt](/organizations/alt/) News found that during PIB's own briefing on Operation Sindoor on 7 May 2025, the bureau displayed footage of a blast claiming it depicted the 2019 Pulwama attack — when the clip was actually from a blast in Iraq in 2007, a video Alt News had fact-checked years earlier ([Alt News](https://www.altnews.in/political-misinformation-in-2025-who-shared-who-was-targeted-how-did-it-shape-narratives/)). The same analysis exposed a bias in the unit's work: between 7 and 16 May 2025, PIB debunked 69 false claims relating to Operation Sindoor but "consistently avoided fact-checking false claims made by the mainstream media" during the India-Pakistan conflict ([Alt News](https://www.altnews.in/political-misinformation-in-2025-who-shared-who-was-targeted-how-did-it-shape-narratives/)). Alt News also found a PIB press release misleading in its claim that India was the world's fourth most equal country per World Bank data — it had compared India's consumption-based Gini coefficient with other countries' income-based Gini scores ([Alt News](https://www.altnews.in/political-misinformation-in-2025-who-shared-who-was-targeted-how-did-it-shape-narratives/)).

## The takedown power the courts refused

The more serious challenge to the Fact Check Unit came from the courts. The 2023 amendments to the IT Rules, 2021, would have empowered the Central Government to establish Fact Check Units to identify "fake and false" information about government business on digital platforms — with intermediaries required to take flagged content down or risk losing their safe-harbour immunity ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/bombay-high-court-fact-check-unit-it-rules-kunal-kamra-270722)). Comedian Kunal Kamra, the Editors Guild of India, the Association of Indian Magazines and the News Broadcasters & Digital Association challenged the rules, and after a split division-bench verdict was resolved by a tie-breaker judge, the Bombay High Court on 24 September 2024 declared Rule 3(1)(v) unconstitutional and struck it down ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/bombay-high-court-fact-check-unit-it-rules-kunal-kamra-270722)).

The reasoning was pointed. Justice Patel held that allowing a government agency to unilaterally declare online content related to government business "fake, false or misleading" was itself a form of "censorship," directly infringing fundamental rights — and found the government's position contradictory: welcoming debate and dissent while arguing that speech-restricting rules were necessary ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/top-stories/bombay-high-court-fact-check-unit-it-rules-kunal-kamra-270722)). PIB's Fact Check Unit survived as a publisher of corrections; its power to compel platforms did not.

## The press release at the origin of an education bill

The bureau's daily function — the press release — remains how the government first speaks on record. When Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan introduced [the Viksit Bharat Shiksha Adhishthan Bill, 2025](/events/viksit-bharat-shiksha-adhishthan-bill-2025/) in the Lok Sabha, it was PIB's 15 December 2025 press release that recorded the introduction date, the 12 December Cabinet approval, the three new councils' names and the government's stated purpose — the first-party record that the bill's FactsLedger profile draws on ([Viksit Bharat Shiksha Adhishthan Bill, 2025](https://factsledger.com/events/viksit-bharat-shiksha-adhishthan-bill-2025)). A century after Rushbrook Williams's cell was told to watch for "questions on which public opinion is exercised," PIB still sits at the exact junction he described: between the government and the press — announcing, interpreting, correcting, and now itself being corrected.
