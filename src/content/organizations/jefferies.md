## Overview

Jefferies is an American independent investment bank and financial services company headquartered in New York City ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/jefferies-group-inc)). Founded in 1962 by Boyd Jefferies with $30,000 of borrowed capital, it began as a trading firm that pioneered the "third market" — off-exchange trading of listed stocks between institutions — and grew into a full-service global investment bank ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/jefferies-group-inc)). It survived a founder's criminal conviction in 1987, rebuilt itself by hiring bankers from the collapsed Drexel Burnham Lambert, and in 2013 merged with Leucadia National Corporation, which later renamed itself Jefferies Financial Group Inc. (NYSE: JEF) ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1084580/000119312513087969/d495163d8k.htm)). In fiscal 2025 it reported net revenues of $7.34 billion ([Jefferies Investor Relations](https://ir.jefferies.com/news/news-details/2026/Jefferies-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-2025-Financial-Results/default.aspx)), and in January 2026 its analyst Thomas Chong raised [Baidu](/organizations/baidu/)'s price target to $181, reiterating a Buy rating ([Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/baidu-s-ai-chip-spin-off-strategy-could-ignite-39-upside-in-the-stock-says-jefferies/cmxVE2vRE6y)).

## From a telephone booth to the third market

Jefferies was started in 1962 in a telephone booth at the Pacific Coast Stock Exchange by Boyd Jefferies, who had been a partner at Noble, Tulk, Marsh & Jefferies and borrowed $30,000 to launch his own operation ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/jefferies-group-inc)). The firm made its name in the "third market": trading listed stocks over the counter, away from the exchange floor, which gave institutional clients anonymity and negotiated commissions instead of the exchange's fixed rates ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/jefferies-group-inc)). It also pioneered the split commission, or "give-up," in 1964 — charging a client full commission and sharing it with other brokers who had worked with that client — which helped it build market share ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/jefferies-group-inc)). Jefferies joined the [New York Stock Exchange](/organizations/new-york-stock-exchange/) in 1967 with a five-person office, and as third-market volume surged 40% in 1967–68 it became the seventh-largest firm in size and trading on the exchange ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/jefferies-group-inc)).

The exchange establishment fought back: a 1969 study by former Federal Reserve chairman William McChesney Martin recommended eliminating the third market, and the SEC held hearings on the issue ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/jefferies-group-inc)). That same year Jefferies was acquired by Minneapolis-based Investors Diversified Services (IDS), then the second-largest financial services company in the United States — a deal that forced it to resign its NYSE membership under Exchange Rule 318 ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/jefferies-group-inc)). The marriage failed: IDS competed with Jefferies for clients and never delivered the expected capital, and in August 1973 Boyd Jefferies bought his company back over dinner for $2.5 million ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/jefferies-group-inc)). The firm went public on 13 October 1983, offering 1.75 million shares at $13 per share, and by 1984 BusinessWeek counted it among the ten most profitable publicly held brokerages ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/jefferies-group-inc)).

## The Boesky crisis and the Drexel rebuild

In 1987, after 25 years in business, Jefferies faced a potentially fatal crisis. Boyd Jefferies was charged by the government and the SEC with two securities violations: "parking" stock for customer Ivan Boesky and a customer margin violation ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/jefferies-group-inc)). He pleaded guilty, was fined and barred from the securities industry for five years, and resigned; the company itself was not charged, though its brokerage unit was censured by the SEC ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/jefferies-group-inc)). Weeks later the stock market crashed on 19 October 1987, costing Jefferies $6.5 million on its principal holdings ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/jefferies-group-inc)). Frank Baxter, the new chief executive, retained the company's name, paid 20% bonuses to its 180 traders — not one left — and cut costs 15% through back-office automation; the firm earned net income of $6.1 million in 1987 and $16 million in 1988 ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/jefferies-group-inc)).

The rebuild accelerated when Drexel Burnham Lambert collapsed in 1990. Jefferies hired former Drexel bankers and traders — including Richard B. Handler, who had worked in Drexel's high-yield bond department — and entered the high-yield markets and investment banking ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/jefferies-group-inc)). Handler joined as a salesman and trader in April 1990, was appointed chief executive on 1 January 2001, and became chairman in 2002, succeeding Baxter ([BBN Times](https://www.bbntimes.com/financial/rich-handler-wall-street-s-longest-serving-ceo-investment-banking-builder-and-chairman-and-ceo-of-jefferies-financial-group)). Under Baxter and then Handler, Jefferies moved into electronic trading, corporate finance, convertible sales and derivatives, and by 1997 revenues approached $800 million ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/books/politics-and-business-magazines/jefferies-group-inc)). Euromoney, naming Jefferies best independent investment bank in 2013, noted that at the start of the 1990s it had been little more than a cash-equities trading firm with about $30 million of capital and $130 million of annual revenue — and that after the 2008 financial crisis it "played massive offence," hiring UK equity staff from Bear Stearns and teams from Lehman, and buying Hoare Govett, Prudential Bache and a municipal bond business ([Euromoney](https://www.euromoney.com/article/27bjsstsqxhkmh0s7wvt2/awards/awards-for-excellence-2013-best-independent-investment-bank/)).

## The Leucadia merger and the firm today

Jefferies' near-death experience came in 2011, when MF Global collapsed and short sellers turned on Jefferies next; Handler fought back by disclosing the firm's offsetting short positions and minimal outright exposure ([Euromoney](https://www.euromoney.com/article/27bjsstsqxhkmh0s7wvt2/awards/awards-for-excellence-2013-best-independent-investment-bank/)). His answer to the vulnerability was structural: on 12 November 2012 Jefferies announced a merger with Leucadia National Corporation, its largest shareholder, in an all-stock deal valuing Jefferies at about $2.7 billion, or roughly $17.66 per share — a 24% premium — with Jefferies shareholders receiving 0.81 Leucadia shares each ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2012/11/12/why-jefferies-group-shares-popped.aspx)). Shareholders approved the deal on 28 February 2013, and it closed on 1 March 2013, with Jefferies Group LLC becoming a wholly-owned subsidiary of Leucadia and Handler becoming chief executive of both companies ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1084580/000119312513087969/d495163d8k.htm)). Handler told Euromoney the merger was preferable to becoming a bank holding company, with its "cumbersome regulation," and that Leucadia's $10 billion book value "protects our bondholders and creates long-term stability" ([Euromoney](https://www.euromoney.com/article/27bjsstsqxhkmh0s7wvt2/awards/awards-for-excellence-2013-best-independent-investment-bank/)). In May 2018 Leucadia renamed itself Jefferies Financial Group Inc., reflecting that the investment bank had become its principal business ([BBN Times](https://www.bbntimes.com/financial/rich-handler-wall-street-s-longest-serving-ceo-investment-banking-builder-and-chairman-and-ceo-of-jefferies-financial-group)).

The combined firm kept growing. In April 2013 Jefferies was lead adviser on First Quantum's $5.1 billion hostile takeover of Inmet Mining, and in May 2013 it advised Inergy on its $7.3 billion merger with Crestwood Midstream ([Euromoney](https://www.euromoney.com/article/27bjsstsqxhkmh0s7wvt2/awards/awards-for-excellence-2013-best-independent-investment-bank/)). For fiscal 2025, ended 30 November 2025, Jefferies reported total net revenues of $7.34 billion and fourth-quarter net revenues of $2.07 billion, with investment banking net revenues of $1.19 billion in the quarter — up 20% year over year — including advisory revenues of $634 million, its second-best quarter ever ([Jefferies Investor Relations](https://ir.jefferies.com/news/news-details/2026/Jefferies-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-2025-Financial-Results/default.aspx)). On 2 January 2026, analyst Thomas Chong raised Baidu's price target to $181 from $159 while reiterating a Buy rating, arguing that spinning off the [Kunlunxin](/organizations/kunlunxin/) AI-chip unit would unlock hidden value; Jefferies estimated Kunlunxin could be worth $16–23 billion as a standalone listing, translating to roughly $9–13 billion of attributable value for Baidu on its 59% stake ([Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/baidu-s-ai-chip-spin-off-strategy-could-ignite-39-upside-in-the-stock-says-jefferies/cmxVE2vRE6y)). The $181 target implied about 39% upside to Baidu's closing price ([Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/baidu-s-ai-chip-spin-off-strategy-could-ignite-39-upside-in-the-stock-says-jefferies/cmxVE2vRE6y)).
