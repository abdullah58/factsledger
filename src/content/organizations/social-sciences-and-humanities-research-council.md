## Overview

The Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council of Canada (SSHRC) is [Canada](/places/canada/)'s federal funding agency for research and training in the social sciences and humanities, one of the country's three federal granting agencies alongside the Canadian Institutes of Health Research and the [Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council](/organizations/natural-sciences-and-engineering-research-council/). Created by an Act of Parliament in 1977, it planned $1.33 billion in spending for 2025-26 — and it also administers the tri-agency programs that fund research chairs and institutional support across every discipline ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/about-sshrc/planning-and-performance-reporting/corporate-reports/departmental-plans/2025-2026/at-a-glance.aspx)).

SSHRC officially began operations on 1 April 1978, taking over the programs previously run by the Canada Council's humanities and social sciences division, and awarded its inaugural research grants that year ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/news_room-salle_de_presse/latest_news-nouvelles_recentes/2018/40th_anniversary-40e_anniversaire-eng.aspx)). Its first president was André Fortier; the current president is Ted Hewitt, and the agency reports to Parliament through the Minister of Innovation, Science and Industry — Mélanie Joly in the 2025-26 Departmental Plan ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/about-sshrc/planning-and-performance-reporting/corporate-reports/departmental-plans/2025-2026/full-plan.aspx)). Its mandate is to promote and assist research and scholarship in the social sciences and humanities, and to advise the minister on such research ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/about-au_sujet/publications/dp/2021-2022/operating_context-raison_d_etre-contexte_operationnel-eng.aspx)). A governing council appointed by order in council sets its strategic priorities and allocates its budgets, and its grants are awarded through independent peer merit review — through its regular programs it provides over 5,000 awards a year and engages more than 3,600 researchers as reviewers ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/about-sshrc/planning-and-performance-reporting/corporate-reports/departmental-plans/2025-2026/at-a-glance.aspx)). The community it serves numbers more than 23,900 full-time university, college and CEGEP professors and close to 48,000 graduate students and postdoctoral researchers ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/about-sshrc/planning-and-performance-reporting/corporate-reports/departmental-plans/2025-2026/full-plan.aspx)).

## Talent, Insight and Connection: what SSHRC funds

SSHRC's own programs run in three streams. Talent supports students and postdoctoral researchers through scholarships and fellowships, including the Canada Graduate Scholarships — master's awards of $27,000 a year and doctoral awards of $40,000 a year — and postdoctoral awards of $70,000 a year ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/funding/opportunities.aspx)). A new harmonised tri-agency training suite, the Canada Research Training Awards Suite, starts in 2026-27 to replace the separate agency scholarships with one system ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/about-sshrc/planning-and-performance-reporting/corporate-reports/departmental-plans/2025-2026/details-transfer-payment-programs.aspx)). Insight funds investigator-led research: Insight Grants support research initiatives of up to five years, currently worth $10,000 to $500,000, while Insight Development Grants support research in its initial stages at $10,000 to $100,000 over one to two years ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/funding/opportunities.aspx)). Connection funds knowledge mobilisation — events and outreach that carry research findings to audiences beyond the university — through Connection Grants of $10,000 to $50,000 over one year ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/funding/opportunities.aspx)). Partnership Grants, the largest of the research grants at up to $2.5 million over four to seven years, fund formal partnerships between researchers and organisations in the public, private and not-for-profit sectors, with partners contributing at least 35% of the cost ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/funding/opportunities.aspx); [University of Calgary](https://research.ucalgary.ca/research-services/find-and-manage-funding/external-grants/grants-tri-council)).

## The tri-agency administrator

SSHRC's less visible but larger role is as administrator of the programs shared by all three federal granting agencies. Through the Tri-agency Institutional Programs Secretariat it runs the Canada Research Chairs Program, the Canada Excellence Research Chairs, the Canada First Research Excellence Fund, the New Frontiers in Research Fund and the Research Support Fund on behalf of itself, the Canadian Institutes of Health Research and the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/about-au_sujet/publications/dp/2021-2022/operating_context-raison_d_etre-contexte_operationnel-eng.aspx)). The Canada Research Chairs Program alone invests approximately $311 million a year to support 2,285 research chairs across the country, attracting and retaining world-class researchers in every field ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/about-sshrc/planning-and-performance-reporting/corporate-reports/departmental-plans/2025-2026/full-plan.aspx)). The Research Support Fund, started in 2003, reimburses universities, colleges and affiliated research hospitals and institutes for a share of the indirect costs of federally funded research — the buildings, equipment and administration that grants themselves do not cover ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/about-sshrc/planning-and-performance-reporting/corporate-reports/departmental-plans/2025-2026/details-transfer-payment-programs.aspx)). The New Frontiers in Research Fund, started in 2018, backs international, interdisciplinary, high-risk and high-reward research that the regular agency programs cannot easily support ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/about-sshrc/planning-and-performance-reporting/corporate-reports/departmental-plans/2025-2026/details-transfer-payment-programs.aspx)).

## Budget 2024 and the 2025-26 plan

Budget 2024 was the largest single funding increase in the granting agencies' recent history: $1.8 billion over five years and $748.3 million ongoing for core research grants across the three agencies, a 30% top-up to existing programs ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/news_room-salle_de_presse/latest_news-nouvelles_recentes/2024/research_grants_budget_2024_bourses_subventions_recherche-eng.aspx)). SSHRC's share was $315,493,311 over five years and $136,273,247 ongoing for its Insight Research and Research Partnerships programs, to raise both the values and the number of Insight Grants, Insight Development Grants, Connection Grants, Partnership Grants, Partnership Development Grants, Partnership Engage Grants and Knowledge Synthesis Grants ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/news_room-salle_de_presse/latest_news-nouvelles_recentes/2024/research_grants_budget_2024_bourses_subventions_recherche-eng.aspx)). In 2023-24 SSHRC actually spent $1.16 billion — $688.8 million on research and training and $452.2 million on institutional support ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/about-sshrc/planning-and-performance-reporting/corporate-reports/departmental-plans/2025-2026/full-plan.aspx)) — and its 2025-26 planned spending rises to $1.33 billion, which means the Budget 2024 uplift is now the main driver of the agency's growth. Planned spending keeps rising after that, to $1.37 billion in 2026-27 and $1.38 billion in 2027-28 ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/about-sshrc/planning-and-performance-reporting/corporate-reports/departmental-plans/2025-2026/full-plan.aspx)). The money follows the research community's priorities: in 2023 alone SSHRC invested about $44 million in environmental and climate change research, more than $34 million in business and economic research, $18 million in AI and cybersecurity, and over $14 million on housing and homelessness, while about 10% of its funding supported research by and with Indigenous peoples ([openparliament.ca](https://openparliament.ca/committees/science-and-research/44-1/110/dr-ted-hewitt-1/only/)). The same budget also funded a $26.9 million tri-agency grants management solution — one harmonised portal for applying to any of the three agencies ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/about-sshrc/planning-and-performance-reporting/corporate-reports/departmental-plans/2025-2026/full-plan.aspx)).

## Imagining Canada's Future, Indigenous research and the college connection

SSHRC's longest-running strategic initiative is Imagining Canada's Future, launched in 2013 after a foresight exercise that identified six future challenge areas as most critical for Canada: new ways of learning, the quest for energy and natural resources, the experiences and aspirations of Aboriginal Peoples, the implications of global peak population, leveraging emerging technologies, and the knowledge Canada needs to thrive in an interconnected world ([Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/research-coordinating-committee/priorities/indigenous-research/strategic-plan-2019-2022/appendix-5.html); [University of Victoria](https://onlineacademiccommunity.uvic.ca/ages/wp-content/uploads/sites/642/2014/11/SSHRC-Proposal_Fall-2015.pdf)). Knowledge Synthesis Grants fund researchers to synthesise existing knowledge on those challenges and identify gaps, and in 2025-26 SSHRC launches a new competition on "The Arts Transformed", in partnership with the Canada Council for the Arts ([SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/about-sshrc/planning-and-performance-reporting/corporate-reports/departmental-plans/2025-2026/at-a-glance.aspx)). Indigenous research is a deliberate priority: between 2008 and 2017 SSHRC invested $360 million in research on Indigenous themes through 3,200 grants and scholarships, roughly 10% of its total research funding, and Budget 2024 added $30 million to strengthen the capacity of First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities to lead their own research ([Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/research-coordinating-committee/priorities/indigenous-research/strategic-plan-2019-2022/appendix-5.html); [SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/about-sshrc/planning-and-performance-reporting/corporate-reports/departmental-plans/2025-2026/full-plan.aspx)). SSHRC's history is also tied to Canada's colleges: for most of its history federal research money was earmarked almost exclusively for universities, and colleges could not access the granting councils apart from limited SSHRC funding — until the [Canada Foundation for Innovation](/organizations/canada-foundation-for-innovation/) included colleges in 1997, a Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council pilot let them apply directly in 2002-03, and the Tri-Council College and Community Innovation Program formed in 2008, a program SSHRC co-delivers and which is managed by the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council ([Colleges and Institutes Canada](/organizations/colleges-and-institutes-canada/); [SSHRC](https://sshrc-crsh.canada.ca/en/about-sshrc/planning-and-performance-reporting/corporate-reports/departmental-plans/2025-2026/details-transfer-payment-programs.aspx)). The agency's future shape is itself an open question: in 2024 the ministers mandated the three agencies to consult the research community on a proposed single capstone research funding organisation, and President Hewitt told Parliament that SSHRC welcomed harmonisation but urged that the social sciences and humanities be recognised as a fundamental ingredient of any consolidated entity ([openparliament.ca](https://openparliament.ca/committees/science-and-research/44-1/110/dr-ted-hewitt-1/only/)).

## FAQ

### What is SSHRC?

SSHRC is Canada's federal funding agency for research and training in the social sciences and humanities, one of three federal granting agencies alongside the Canadian Institutes of Health Research and the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council. Created by an Act of Parliament in 1977, it funds researchers, students and postdoctoral fellows through grants, fellowships and scholarships awarded by peer merit review.

### How is SSHRC different from NSERC and CIHR?

The three agencies divide research by field: SSHRC funds the social sciences and humanities, NSERC funds the natural sciences and engineering, and CIHR funds health research. SSHRC is distinct again in that it administers several tri-agency programs — including the Canada Research Chairs and the Research Support Fund — on behalf of all three agencies.

### How much money does SSHRC distribute?

In 2023-24 SSHRC actually spent $1.16 billion: $688.8 million on research and training and $452.2 million on institutional support. Its planned spending for 2025-26 is $1.33 billion, including $824.0 million for research and training and $483.7 million for the Research Support Fund, with 415 full-time equivalent staff.

### What are SSHRC's biggest grants?

Insight Grants, its main investigator-led awards, are currently worth $10,000 to $500,000 over two to five years. Partnership Grants go up to $2.5 million over four to seven years for formal research partnerships. For students, doctoral scholarships pay $40,000 a year for three years and master's scholarships $27,000 for one year.

### Does SSHRC fund colleges as well as universities?

Yes, in a limited way — and historically SSHRC was the only granting council colleges could access at all, before the Canada Foundation for Innovation included colleges in 1997 and the College and Community Innovation Program was formed in 2008. SSHRC also co-delivers that program, which helps colleges work with local companies on applied research.
