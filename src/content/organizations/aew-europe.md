## Overview

AEW Europe is the European arm of AEW, one of the world's largest real estate asset managers, and the property-investment platform of [Natixis](/organizations/natixis/) Investment Managers inside [Groupe BPCE](/organizations/groupe-bpce/). Created as part of a group founded in 1981, it manages €34.8 billion of European real estate for funds and separate accounts, employs more than 500 people in 11 European offices, and runs Core, Value-Add and Opportunistic strategies from its main offices in Boston, [London](/places/london/), [Paris](/places/paris/) and [Hong Kong](/places/hong-kong/) ([Investment & Pensions Europe](https://hub.ipe.com/asset-manager/aew-real-estate-europe/415389.supplier)). It became a wholly owned Natixis business in 2022, when Natixis bought out [La Banque Postale](/organizations/la-banque-postale/)'s 40% stake as part of a wider swap of French financial assets ([Groupe BPCE](https://newsroom-en.groupebpce.fr/download-pdf/627e80c308de164c260fe401)).

## From a Boston firm to a European platform

AEW's own account, carried by the IPE Reference Hub, dates the group to 1981 and describes a business built around creating and implementing real estate investment and asset management strategies for institutional and retail investors ([Investment & Pensions Europe](https://hub.ipe.com/asset-manager/aew-real-estate-europe/415389.supplier)). Together with AEW Capital Management in the United States and Asia, AEW Europe and its regulated subsidiaries form a global platform; the two are commonly owned by Natixis Investment Managers but operate independently of each other ([Investment & Pensions Europe](https://hub.ipe.com/asset-manager/aew-real-estate-europe/415389.supplier)). By mid-2016 that global platform held over €48 billion in assets under management, €18.5 billion of it in Europe, with 500 employees in 14 locations and head offices in Boston, London, Paris and Singapore ([La Banque Postale](https://www.labanquepostale.com/content/dam/lbp/documents/communiques-de-presse/en/2016/20160729_rapprochement-aew-ciloger_PR.pdf)). The European arm's scale has roughly doubled since: at 30 June 2026 the group reported €73.2 billion under management worldwide and €34.8 billion in Europe, with more than €13 billion of European property bought and sold over the previous five years ([Investment & Pensions Europe](https://hub.ipe.com/asset-manager/aew-real-estate-europe/415389.supplier)).

## The Ciloger merger and the French retail push

The decisive step in AEW Europe's growth was its 2016 merger with Ciloger, the retail real estate fund manager founded in 1984 and owned 90% by La Banque Postale and 10% by [CNP Assurances](/organizations/cnp-assurances/). Talks that began in early 2016 produced signed agreements on 21 July, and the merger completed that October, combining AEW Europe's roughly €19 billion with Ciloger's roughly €5 billion into a business managing over €24 billion. The ownership was re-engineered in the same move: Natixis Global Asset Management first bought the [Caisse des Dépôts](/organizations/caisse-des-depots-et-consignations/)' 40% stake in AEW Europe to hold 100%, then La Banque Postale took a 40% stake, leaving Natixis with 60%. PropertyEU's Top 100 Investors had ranked Paris-based AEW Europe 26th in Europe; the new €24 billion total would lift it eight places, ahead of Blackstone at €23.3 billion and almost level with UBS Global Asset Management at €24.1 billion ([Real Assets IPE](https://realassets.ipe.com/propertyeu/aew-ciloger-merger-creates-24b-european-real-estate-powerhouse/10121975.article)).

The merged French retail platform — AEW Europe's existing NAMI–AEW Europe business combined with Ciloger's — became the third-largest manager of retail real estate funds in France, with access to the distribution networks of Groupe BPCE and La Banque Postale ([Real Assets IPE](https://realassets.ipe.com/propertyeu/aew-ciloger-merger-creates-24b-european-real-estate-powerhouse/10121975.article)). Rob Wilkinson stayed on as chief executive, Isabelle Rossignol moved from running Ciloger to heading the French retail platform, and Serge Bataillie became group chief financial officer at the start of 2017 ([Real Assets IPE](https://realassets.ipe.com/propertyeu/aew-ciloger-merger-creates-24b-european-real-estate-powerhouse/10121975.article)). Wilkinson said the deal would increase the size and profile of the business, particularly in the retail investor market, where French savers were investing more in real estate as life-insurance products changed ([Property Week](https://www.propertyweek.com/finance/aew-europes-aum-to-rise-to-more-than-23bn-after-ciloger-merger)). The merger also lifted headcount from about 270 to about 330, and Ciloger brought €5.2 billion of assets managed at 30 June 2016, mostly through real estate funds sold via the French post office and savings-bank networks ([La Banque Postale](https://www.labanquepostale.com/content/dam/lbp/documents/communiques-de-presse/en/2016/20160729_rapprochement-aew-ciloger_PR.pdf)).

## Debt, scale and full Natixis ownership

AEW Europe's second growth engine was real estate debt. In June 2021 it fully integrated the European debt platform it had launched almost a decade earlier in a 50/50 partnership with [Ostrum Asset Management](/organizations/ostrum-asset-management/), consolidating a team of ten professionals led by Cyril Hoyaux ([AEW](https://www.aew.com/site-assets/documents/10-06-2021_PR_AEW-consolidates-real-estate-debt-platform.pdf)). The platform then held about €4.5 billion of real estate debt worldwide, over €2.5 billion of it in Europe across seven funds and mandates, after more than doubling in three years. Raphaël Brault, AEW's head of institutional business, said the move positioned AEW as a major global player in real estate debt, responding to growing investor appetite for yield and exposure to the real economy ([AEW](https://www.aew.com/site-assets/documents/10-06-2021_PR_AEW-consolidates-real-estate-debt-platform.pdf)). Clients increasingly wanted a single global offering from their investment managers, and the consolidation was designed to serve that demand while remaining selective about market trends ([AEW](https://www.aew.com/site-assets/documents/10-06-2021_PR_AEW-consolidates-real-estate-debt-platform.pdf)).

At 31 March 2021 AEW managed €72.8 billion worldwide and €35.7 billion in Europe, with over 770 employees and more than 430 of them in 12 European locations, and €21 billion of European property bought and sold over the previous five years ([AEW](https://www.aew.com/site-assets/documents/10-06-2021_PR_AEW-consolidates-real-estate-debt-platform.pdf)). The ownership question was settled a year later: on 13 May 2022 Natixis Investment Managers completed its acquisition of La Banque Postale's 40% interest in AEW Europe — and its 45% interest in Ostrum — to own 100% of both, extending the groups' industrial partnerships to the end of 2030 ([Groupe BPCE](https://newsroom-en.groupebpce.fr/download-pdf/627e80c308de164c260fe401)). The two stakes were the other side of La Banque Postale's purchase of Groupe BPCE's 16.1% holding in CNP Assurances at €21.90 a share, a 36.2% premium valuing the insurer at about €15 billion ([Boursorama / Le Revenu](https://www.boursorama.com/bourse/actualites/opa-des-primes-attractives-pour-cnp-assurances-prodware-et-s-t-dupont-739164beaf965dd9e1850c864ef9dcd1)).

## FAQ

### What is AEW Europe?

AEW Europe is the European real estate investment and asset management arm of AEW, one of the world's largest real estate managers. It manages €34.8 billion of European property for funds and separate accounts, employs more than 500 people in 11 European offices, and is the real estate platform of Natixis Investment Managers.

### Who owns AEW Europe?

Natixis Investment Managers owns 100% of AEW Europe. It acquired La Banque Postale's 40% stake in May 2022, completing a swap in which La Banque Postale bought Groupe BPCE's 16.1% stake in CNP Assurances. Before that, Natixis held 60% and La Banque Postale 40%.

### What did the Ciloger merger change?

The 2016 merger combined AEW Europe's roughly €19 billion with Ciloger's roughly €5 billion, creating a manager of over €24 billion and the third-largest French retail real estate fund manager. It gave AEW Europe access to the Banque Populaire, Caisse d'Epargne and La Banque Postale distribution networks.

### What strategies does AEW Europe run?

It runs Core, Value-Add and Opportunistic strategies across European markets, offering commingled funds, separate accounts and securities mandates to institutional and retail investors, plus a real estate debt platform holding over €2.5 billion in Europe. The debt platform, consolidated in 2021, is led by Cyril Hoyaux and spans seven funds and mandates.

### How big is AEW overall?

At 30 June 2026 AEW managed €73.2 billion worldwide, with €34.8 billion of that in Europe, and employed more than 815 people globally. Its main offices are in Boston, London, Paris and Hong Kong. In the previous five years it bought and sold more than €13 billion of European property.
