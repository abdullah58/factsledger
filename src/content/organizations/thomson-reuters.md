## Overview

Thomson [Reuters](/organizations/reuters/) is a Canadian information company headquartered in Toronto that sells specialised software and content to legal, tax, accounting, corporate and government professionals — and owns the Reuters news agency ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Thomson-Reuters)). It was formed on 17 April 2008, when Canada's Thomson Corporation bought Britain's Reuters Group for about $17 billion, and it is controlled by the Thomson family's Woodbridge Company ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1056084/000104746908009269/a2187403zex-99_1.htm)). This profile is about the parent company, not the news agency: Reuters has its own profile, covering the newswire founded in London in 1851.

Today the company is organised around three customer segments it calls the "Big 3" — Legal Professionals, Corporates, and Tax & Accounting Professionals — which together made up 82% of revenues in 2024, alongside the Reuters News division ([Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/press-releases/2025/february/thomson-reuters-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024-results)). Its best-known products are Westlaw for legal research, Practical Law, the CoCounsel AI assistant and Checkpoint for tax professionals, and its chief executive is Steve Hasker ([Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/press-releases/2025/february/thomson-reuters-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024-results)).

## From a pigeon-and-telegraph newswire to a Toronto information company

The Reuters half of the company began with Paul Julius Reuter, a former bank clerk who ran a prototype news service in 1849 using electric telegraphy and carrier pigeons, then launched Reuter's Telegram Company in London in 1851 to serve banks, brokerage houses and business firms ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Thomson-Reuters)). Its first newspaper client, the London *Morning Advertiser*, subscribed in 1858, and Reuters built its reputation on speed — breaking the news of Abraham Lincoln's assassination in 1865 hours before its competitors ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Thomson-Reuters)). The Thomson half was a Canadian publishing and information company; the 2008 combination joined Thomson's North American professional strength with Reuters' European and Asian reach under a dual-listed-company structure

Reuters turned early to financial technology, using computers to transmit data in the 1960s, offering foreign-exchange rates on computer terminals from 1973, and enabling electronic transactions from 1981, before listing in London and on NASDAQ in 1984 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Thomson-Reuters)). The 2008 combination joined Thomson's North American professional strength with Reuters' European and Asian reach under a dual-listed-company structure — two listed parents, Thomson Reuters Corporation of Ontario and Thomson Reuters PLC of England, run as one group ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1056084/000104746908009269/a2187403zex-99_1.htm)). At formation it operated in 93 countries and served customers in more than 140 ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1056084/000104746908009269/a2187403zex-99_1.htm)).

## The Refinitiv split and the professional-software pivot

The defining deal of the modern company was the disposal of its financial-data arm. In October 2018 Thomson Reuters sold a majority interest in its Financial & Risk business to a consortium led by Blackstone; the business was renamed Refinitiv, with Blackstone funds holding 55% and Thomson Reuters 45% ([Thomson Reuters](http://www.newswire.ca/en/releases/archive/January2021/29/c6334.html); [LSEG](https://www.lseg.com/en/media-centre/press-releases/2019/statement-regarding-press-speculation)). In August 2019 the owners agreed to sell Refinitiv to the [London Stock Exchange Group](/organizations/london-stock-exchange-group/), and the all-share sale closed on 29 January 2021 ([Thomson Reuters](http://www.newswire.ca/en/releases/archive/January2021/29/c6334.html)). At closing Thomson Reuters indirectly held 82.5 million LSEG shares worth about $9.8 billion, and about $700 million of tax became payable ([Thomson Reuters](http://www.newswire.ca/en/releases/archive/January2021/29/c6334.html)).

The sale remade the company: it kept the legal, tax and accounting businesses plus Reuters News, and turned from a financial-data giant into a professional-software company. Reuters News kept a 30-year content agreement with Refinitiv, scheduled to run to 2048 ([Thomson Reuters](http://www.newswire.ca/en/releases/archive/January2021/29/c6334.html)). Thomson Reuters finished monetising its LSEG stake during 2024, leaving a cleaner portfolio built around recurring subscriptions ([Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/press-releases/2025/february/thomson-reuters-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024-results)).

## Generative AI and the "Big 3"

The company's next pivot is generative AI for professionals. In August 2023 it closed a $650 million cash acquisition of Casetext, founded in 2013, whose key product CoCounsel is a GPT-4-powered AI legal assistant that drafts research memos, reviews documents, prepares depositions and analyses contracts ([Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/press-releases/2023/august/thomson-reuters-completes-acquisition-of-casetext-inc)). Casetext served more than 10,000 law firms and corporate legal departments, and Thomson Reuters committed more than $100 million a year to weaving AI into its flagship products, including work with Microsoft on a 365 Copilot plugin ([Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/press-releases/2023/august/thomson-reuters-completes-acquisition-of-casetext-inc)).

The economics show why the pivot matters: in 2024 the "Big 3" segments grew 9% organically and supplied 82% of total revenues, while total company revenues rose 7% ([Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/press-releases/2025/february/thomson-reuters-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024-results)). That stat, source and implication is the profile in miniature — a subscription business whose growth now comes from selling AI tools to lawyers, accountants and corporate teams, not from selling market data. Thomson Reuters also invested beyond its core, joining the $18 million Series A of blockchain firm [Axoni](/organizations/axoni/) in December 2016 alongside [Goldman Sachs](/organizations/goldman-sachs/), J.P. Morgan and Andreessen Horowitz ([Financial Adviser Magazine](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/vc-deal-of-the-day-axioni-raises-18m-series-a-46944.html?print)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Thomson Reuters the same as Reuters?**
No. Reuters is the news agency inside the group, founded in London in 1851. Thomson Reuters is the Toronto-headquartered parent company formed in 2008 when Thomson Corporation bought Reuters Group for about $17 billion ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Thomson-Reuters); [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1056084/000104746908009269/a2187403zex-99_1.htm)).

**Who owns Thomson Reuters?**
The Thomson family's Woodbridge Company is the controlling shareholder; it held about a 55% voting interest at the time of the 2008 merger, according to the company's SEC filing ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1056084/000104746908009269/a2187403zex-99_1.htm)).

**What does Thomson Reuters sell?**
Mainly subscription software and content for professionals: Westlaw and Practical Law for lawyers, Checkpoint and UltraTax for tax and accounting professionals, and corporate compliance tools, plus the Reuters news service ([Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/press-releases/2025/february/thomson-reuters-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2024-results)).

**What happened to Refinitiv?**
Refinitiv was Thomson Reuters' Financial & Risk business. A majority stake went to a Blackstone consortium in 2018, and the whole business was sold to the London Stock Exchange Group in a deal that closed in January 2021 ([Thomson Reuters](http://www.newswire.ca/en/releases/archive/January2021/29/c6334.html)).

**What is CoCounsel?**
CoCounsel is an AI legal assistant from Casetext, which Thomson Reuters bought for $650 million in 2023. Powered by GPT-4, it does document review, legal research memos, deposition preparation and contract analysis ([Thomson Reuters](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/press-releases/2023/august/thomson-reuters-completes-acquisition-of-casetext-inc)).
