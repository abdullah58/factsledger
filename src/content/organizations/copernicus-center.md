## Overview

The Copernicus Center is a 1,852-seat performing arts and cultural center at 5216 W. Lawrence Avenue in [Chicago](/places/chicago/)'s [Jefferson Park](/places/jefferson-park/) neighborhood, run by the [Copernicus Foundation](/organizations/copernicus-foundation/) ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/about-us/)). It occupies the former Gateway Theater, a 1930 movie palace designed by Mason Rapp of Rapp and Rapp, which the Foundation bought in 1979 and converted into a Polish-American cultural center while preserving the atmospheric auditorium, renamed the Mitchell P. Kobelinski Theater in 1997 ([Historic Theatre Photos](https://historictheatrephotos.com/Theatre/Copernicus-Center-Chicago.aspx); [Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/theater-history/)).

The Center grew out of Chicago's campaign for a [Nicolaus Copernicus monument](/places/nicolaus-copernicus-monument/): the Foundation raised funds for the statue, dedicated in 1973 before the [Adler Planetarium](/organizations/adler-planetarium/), then used surplus money to buy the Gateway in 1979 ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument); [Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/about-us/)). Today the former cinema rarely shows films; it is used an average of 48 weeks a year for concerts, theater, dance, graduations and community programs, and hosts the annual [Taste of Polonia Festival](/events/taste-of-polonia-festival/) ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/about-us/)).

## From Gateway movie palace to cultural center

The Gateway Theater opened on June 27, 1930, as a Balaban and Katz movie palace in Jefferson Park, designed by Mason Rapp of the firm Rapp and Rapp in the atmospheric style, with an Italian courtyard theme ([Historic Theatre Photos](https://historictheatrephotos.com/Theatre/Copernicus-Center-Chicago.aspx)). Its opening night mixed the old and the new: Henri A. Keates at the "Mighty Grande Organ," a sound newsreel, comedy shorts including She Who Gets Slapped and an Our Gang short, and The First Seven Years as the main feature ([Historic Theatre Photos](https://historictheatrephotos.com/Theatre/Copernicus-Center-Chicago.aspx)). Built with only a minimalist stage a few feet deep — a full stagehouse could be added later if the new "talkies" failed — it was the first movie theater in Chicago built exclusively for sound film ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/about-us/); [Historic Theatre Photos](https://historictheatrephotos.com/Theatre/Copernicus-Center-Chicago.aspx)). In 1977 the Foundation began searching for a permanent home for a Polish cultural center, and in 1979 it bought the Gateway with surplus funds from the monument campaign, preserving the theater while remodeling around it ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/about-us/)).

## The theater today: rebuilding and renaming

The remodeling turned the Gateway into a multi-use center: the first stage, finished in 1981, divided the original 40-foot entry lobby and added three floors of offices, meeting rooms and classrooms around the auditorium; a second stage followed in 1984, and in 1985 the Solidarity Tower — a scale replica of the clock tower of [Warsaw](/places/warsaw/)'s Royal Castle — was erected atop the building, its facade remodeled to resemble the castle ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/about-us/)). The Foundation took over the theater in 1985, and in 1997 renamed the auditorium the Mitchell P. Kobelinski Theater for a founder and its longest-serving president ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/theater-history/)). After falling into disrepair in the late 1990s, a ten-year renovation redid its floors, ceiling, seats and facades and added a thrust stage and modern sound and lighting ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/theater-history/)). In 2011 the Center added the Annex, a loft-style event space that also houses offices of local chambers of commerce and Polish organizations ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/about-us/)).

## Programming, presidents and a second theater

Today the Center hosts concerts, theatrical productions, dance showcases, comedy shows, graduations and community programs, and the Foundation says it is alive virtually every night with dance groups, classes and theatrical activities ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/about-us/)). Its best-known event is the Taste of Polonia Festival, held every year since 1980 and described by the Center as the largest Polish festival in the country, visited by more than 40,000 people annually ([Copernicus Center](https://copernicuscenter.org/about-us/)). The venue's civic weight showed on November 25, 2014, when President [Barack Obama](/people/barack-obama/) spoke there about immigration reform and the Ferguson grand jury decision, opening by thanking the Copernicus Center and calling it "a Polish community center" ([GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/DCPD-201400888/html/DCPD-201400888.htm)). Former president [George H.W. Bush](/people/george-h-w-bush/) also hosted an event at the theater ([Block Club Chicago](https://blockclubchicago.org/2021/07/26/jefferson-parks-copernicus-foundation-marks-50-years-with-history-book-this-place-is-a-legacy/)). [Pope John Paul II](/people/pope-john-paul-ii/) did not enter the theater: in 1979 he drove by the Center from O'Hare airport while the Polish community waited in the parking lot with a mural of him ([Block Club Chicago](https://blockclubchicago.org/2021/07/26/jefferson-parks-copernicus-foundation-marks-50-years-with-history-book-this-place-is-a-legacy/)). In 2023 the Foundation's reach grew beyond Jefferson Park: on July 14 the owners of Park Ridge's 1928 Pickwick Theatre announced the Foundation would operate it as "Copernicus @ The Pickwick," with Center staff programming its main auditorium for live theater and entertainment ([Newcity](https://www.newcity.com/2023/07/17/today-in-the-culture-july-17-2023-destinos-sets-lineup-copernicus-to-operate-pickwick-owen-engine-shutting-down/)).

## FAQ

### What is the Copernicus Center?

The Copernicus Center is a performing arts and cultural center at 5216 W. Lawrence Avenue in Chicago's Jefferson Park, run by the Copernicus Foundation. It occupies the former Gateway Theater, a 1930 movie palace converted in 1979 into a Polish-American cultural center, and its auditorium is the Mitchell P. Kobelinski Theater.

### When did the Gateway Theater become the Copernicus Center?

The Copernicus Foundation bought the Gateway Theater building in 1979, using surplus funds from its Nicolaus Copernicus monument campaign. The first remodeling stage was completed in 1981, the Solidarity Tower was added in 1985, and the Foundation took over running the theater itself that same year.

### Why is the theater called the Mitchell P. Kobelinski Theater?

The auditorium was renamed in 1997 for Mitchell P. Kobelinski, one of the Copernicus Foundation's founders and its longest-serving president, who spearheaded the renovation and modernization of the theater. Before that it was called the Copernicus Theater from 1985, and the Gateway Theater before that.

### What happens at the Copernicus Center today?

The Center hosts concerts, plays, dance showcases, comedy, graduations and community programs, and is used an average of 48 weeks a year. It also runs classes, farmers markets and health fairs, and hosts the annual Taste of Polonia Festival, the largest Polish festival in the United States.

### Does the Copernicus Foundation run any other theater?

Yes. Since 2023 the Foundation has operated the Pickwick Theatre in Park Ridge, Illinois, as "Copernicus @ The Pickwick," programming its main auditorium for live theater and entertainment while its smaller screens continue showing films. The Pickwick's owners, Dino Vlahakis and Dave Loomos, kept ownership of the building.
