## Overview

Baillie Gifford & Co is an independent investment manager based in Edinburgh, founded in 1908 and still wholly owned by the partners who work there — 59 of them as of October 2025 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251010100579/en/Baillie-Gifford-Names-Jamie-McGregor-to-Lead-ETF-Initiative)). It manages over £189 billion in assets as of 30 September 2026 and describes itself as the UK's only large-scale independent, unlimited-liability investment partnership ([Baillie Gifford](https://www.bailliegifford.com/en/israel/qualified-investor/about-us/our-history/)). Its best-known vehicle is the Scottish Mortgage Investment Trust, the FTSE 100 fund behind early bets on Tesla, Amazon and SpaceX — and behind the firm's defining test, the 2022–2023 crash that halved the trust before it recovered.

## Founding and the partnership structure

The firm began as a legal partnership in 1908, when Colonel Augustus Baillie and the younger T J Carlyle Gifford spotted an opportunity in the booming rubber industry and launched an investment trust just as the Ford Model T drove demand for rubber tyres ([Baillie Gifford](https://www.bailliegifford.com/en/israel/qualified-investor/about-us/our-history/)). The trust thrived, expanded into US land, Chilean railways and UK theatres, and was renamed Scottish Mortgage in 1913 — a name it still carries ([Baillie Gifford](https://www.bailliegifford.com/en/israel/qualified-investor/about-us/our-history/)). In 1927 the founders created Baillie Gifford & Co to manage the trusts independently of their legal practice, laying the foundations for the firm as it exists today ([Baillie Gifford](https://www.bailliegifford.com/en/israel/qualified-investor/about-us/our-history/)).

The structure has barely changed since. Baillie Gifford is owned and run by its partners, all of whom work at the firm — 58 partners at 31 March 2025, 59 by October of that year — with around 1,700 staff and offices in Edinburgh, Amsterdam, Dublin, Frankfurt, Hong Kong, London, New York, Shanghai, Singapore, Toronto and Zurich ([ShareSoc](https://www.sharesoc.org/sharesoc-news/press-release-baillie-gifford-partners-with-sharesoc-to-back-individual-investors/)). Because no outside shareholders demand quarterly results, the firm can hold companies for a decade or longer; its stated mission is to find "game-changing companies (both public and private) that can sustain growth and provide returns for clients over five to ten years and sometimes longer" ([ShareSoc](https://www.sharesoc.org/sharesoc-news/press-release-baillie-gifford-partners-with-sharesoc-to-back-individual-investors/)). The firm says the partnership is unlimited-liability, meaning the partners' own capital is on the line ([Baillie Gifford](https://www.bailliegifford.com/en/israel/qualified-investor/about-us/our-history/)). Growth has been steady rather than explosive: assets under management rose from £300 million in 1979 to £3.5 billion in 1989, £16.2 billion by the end of the 1990s, and over £55 billion by the end of 2007 ([Baillie Gifford](https://www.bailliegifford.com/en/israel/qualified-investor/about-us/our-history/)).

## Scottish Mortgage and the long-term growth bets

Scottish Mortgage is where the philosophy is most visible. Established in 1909 and a member of the FTSE 100, the trust aims for long-term growth by investing in the world's most exciting companies, public and private ([Hargreaves Lansdown](https://www.hl.co.uk/shares/investment-trusts/investment-trust-research/scottish-mortgage-investment-trust-june-2026-update)). James Anderson took over as lead manager around 2000 and shifted it from a conservative, dividend-focused UK fund toward technology, backing Amazon, Google, Tesla and Alibaba long before most large investors took them seriously, and buying into private companies such as SpaceX, Stripe and ByteDance ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/60280-baillie-gifford-scottish-mortgage-how-long-term-thinking-beat-the-market/)). Tom Slater, joint manager from 2015, became lead manager when Anderson retired in 2022, with Lawrence Burns as deputy from March 2021 ([Hargreaves Lansdown](https://www.hl.co.uk/shares/investment-trusts/investment-trust-research/scottish-mortgage-investment-trust-june-2026-update)). At the end of April 2026 the trust held 98 companies, including 53 private ones making up 40.5% of its assets, with 59.7% invested in North America and some holdings — Amazon and Netflix among them — held for over ten years ([Hargreaves Lansdown](https://www.hl.co.uk/shares/investment-trusts/investment-trust-research/scottish-mortgage-investment-trust-june-2026-update)).

The Tesla bet shows both the payoff and the discipline. Scottish Mortgage started buying heavily in 2013, when the shares changed hands at about $6, and by January 2021 the firm's Tesla investments had made $29 billion for investors including pension funds and charities — $14.8 billion realised from shares sold and $19.5 billion in retained shares ([Scottish Financial News](https://www.scottishfinancialnews.com/articles/baillie-gifford-wins-big-on-tesla-investment)). The stake grew so large that the managers had to sell to stay within the trust's single-stock limit; Anderson said at the time that the underlying battery, solar and self-driving technologies were "progressing and would continue to do so" and that "what we needed was time" ([Scottish Financial News](https://www.scottishfinancialnews.com/articles/baillie-gifford-wins-big-on-tesla-investment)). The same patience applied to private markets: Brex's April 2021 Series D, led by Tiger Global, brought Baillie Gifford in as a new investor alongside TCV, GIC and Madrone Capital Partners, with [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/) Continuity among the existing backers ([IBS Intelligence](https://ibsintelligence.com/ibsi-news/brex-raises-425mn-series-d-funding-launches-new-all-in-one-financial-solution-for-businesses/)).

But patience is not immunity from volatility. As interest rates rose in 2022 and 2023, Scottish Mortgage fell by over 50%, and its share price dropped 32.32% in the year to May 2022 and a further 17.07% the following year ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/60280-baillie-gifford-scottish-mortgage-how-long-term-thinking-beat-the-market/), [Hargreaves Lansdown](https://www.hl.co.uk/shares/investment-trusts/investment-trust-research/scottish-mortgage-investment-trust-june-2026-update)). The recovery was sharp: from January 2015 to the end of May 2026 the trust's share price returned 555.97%, against 210.57% for the average global investment trust, and in the year to May 2026 alone it rose 55.24% ([Hargreaves Lansdown](https://www.hl.co.uk/shares/investment-trusts/investment-trust-research/scottish-mortgage-investment-trust-june-2026-update)).

## Controversy, climate groups and the 2026 wind-down

The firm's long history as a cultural sponsor ended abruptly in 2024. After more than 50 authors threatened to boycott the Edinburgh International Book Festival over the firm's fossil-fuel investments, the festival and Baillie Gifford ended their 20-year partnership on 30 May 2024, citing "intolerable pressure" on the team ([The Bookseller](https://www.thebookseller.com/news/edinburgh-international-book-festival-and-baillie-gifford-end-partnership-citing-intolerable-pressure)). The Hay Festival had already suspended its sponsorship, and within days Baillie Gifford withdrew from all its remaining literary festival sponsorships, including Cambridge, Stratford, Wigtown, Henley and Wimbledon ([Scottish Financial News](https://www.scottishfinancialnews.com/articles/baillie-gifford-withdraws-all-remaining-literary-festival-sponsorships)). The campaign group Fossil Free Books had demanded divestment from fossil fuels and from companies linked to Israel; the firm replied that it was "not a significant fossil fuel investor" and that only 2% of clients' money was invested in companies with some business related to fossil fuels ([Scottish Financial News](https://www.scottishfinancialnews.com/articles/baillie-gifford-withdraws-all-remaining-literary-festival-sponsorships)). It kept one cultural tie: sponsorship of the Baillie Gifford Prize for nonfiction, contracted until the end of 2025 ([Scottish Financial News](https://www.scottishfinancialnews.com/articles/baillie-gifford-withdraws-all-remaining-literary-festival-sponsorships)).

The same year, Baillie Gifford withdrew from the Net Zero Asset Managers' Initiative and Climate Action 100+, saying membership had become contested and risked distracting from its core responsibilities — a step Hargreaves Lansdown called "a disappointing backward step", while noting the firm said its approach to climate risk and engagement had not changed ([Hargreaves Lansdown](https://www.hl.co.uk/shares/investment-trusts/investment-trust-research/scottish-mortgage-investment-trust-june-2026-update)). The firm was also defending its trusts against a US activist campaign in the same period; its head of global marketing credited individual investors with coming "out in force to protect their investments in UK Trusts from a US activist" ([ShareSoc](https://www.sharesoc.org/sharesoc-news/press-release-baillie-gifford-partners-with-sharesoc-to-back-individual-investors/)).

The year 2026 then closed the Anderson-era arc. Scottish Mortgage sold its remaining Tesla shares in April, ending a 13-year investment: about $470 million invested, about $6.2 billion in realised profit, a 13-fold return — with the trust saying the share price had come to reflect most of Tesla's promise in autonomy and robotics, and pointing to intensifying competition in China ([Daily Chronicle](https://dailychronicle.news/a-long-term-tesla-backer-is-cashing-out-and-cutting-its-spacex-exposure/)). SpaceX, first bought in December 2018 at about a $31 billion valuation, had grown to roughly a quarter of the trust by its June 2026 stock-market debut; the trust has since sold shares as post-IPO lock-ups lifted, cutting the weight from 15% at the end of September to 11.1%, while saying its conviction remains high ([Daily Chronicle](https://dailychronicle.news/a-long-term-tesla-backer-is-cashing-out-and-cutting-its-spacex-exposure/)). The trust's net asset value rose 31.5% in the six months to 30 September 2026, against 16% for the FTSE All-World Index ([Daily Chronicle](https://dailychronicle.news/a-long-term-tesla-backer-is-cashing-out-and-cutting-its-spacex-exposure/)). Meanwhile the firm is moving into new territory: in October 2025 it filed for five actively managed US ETFs and hired Jamie McGregor from Goldman Sachs to lead the entry, 25 years after launching mutual funds in North America ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251010100579/en/Baillie-Gifford-Names-Jamie-McGregor-to-Lead-ETF-Initiative)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Baillie Gifford?**
An independent investment manager founded in Edinburgh in 1908 and wholly owned by its working partners — 59 of them as of October 2025 — managing over £189 billion in assets as of 30 September 2026, with around 1,700 staff and offices in eleven cities ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251010100579/en/Baillie-Gifford-Names-Jamie-McGregor-to-Lead-ETF-Initiative), [Baillie Gifford](https://www.bailliegifford.com/en/israel/qualified-investor/about-us/our-history/)).

**What is Scottish Mortgage?**
The firm's flagship investment trust, launched in 1909 and renamed Scottish Mortgage in 1913. Despite the name it has nothing to do with mortgages: it is a FTSE 100-listed global growth trust that invests in public and private companies, led by Tom Slater since James Anderson retired in 2022 ([Hargreaves Lansdown](https://www.hl.co.uk/shares/investment-trusts/investment-trust-research/scottish-mortgage-investment-trust-june-2026-update)).

**Why did Baillie Gifford end its literary festival sponsorships?**
After authors threatened to boycott the 2024 Edinburgh International Book Festival over the firm's fossil-fuel investments, the festival and the firm ended their 20-year partnership, and Baillie Gifford then withdrew from all remaining festival sponsorships in June 2024 ([Scottish Financial News](https://www.scottishfinancialnews.com/articles/baillie-gifford-withdraws-all-remaining-literary-festival-sponsorships)).

**Did Baillie Gifford sell its Tesla shares?**
Scottish Mortgage sold its remaining Tesla stake in April 2026, ending a 13-year investment that returned about 13 times the roughly $470 million invested; the trust said Tesla's share price already reflected most of its promise in autonomy and robotics ([Daily Chronicle](https://dailychronicle.news/a-long-term-tesla-backer-is-cashing-out-and-cutting-its-spacex-exposure/)).

**How is Baillie Gifford owned?**
It is an independent, unlimited-liability partnership owned and run by its partners, all of whom work at the firm — a structure the firm says leaves it free of outside shareholders and able to invest for decades rather than quarters ([ShareSoc](https://www.sharesoc.org/sharesoc-news/press-release-baillie-gifford-partners-with-sharesoc-to-back-individual-investors/)).

## What this profile does not claim

It does not state a single partner count: firm-sourced figures say 58 in March 2025 and 59 in October 2025, while a Dealroom article says 47. It does not state the campaigners' figures for the firm's fossil-fuel or Israel-linked holdings (£2.5–5 billion and about £10 billion respectively), which were not verified from a fully-read independent source; it states only the firm's 2% response. The April 2026 Tesla exit and the SpaceX weighting figures rest on one tier3 secondary report of the trust's half-year statement, and are flagged as such. It does not claim the firm has left the Baillie Gifford Prize, whose sponsorship was contracted to the end of 2025. The name "Scottish Mortgage" has nothing to do with mortgages; it is the 1913 name of the firm's original investment trust.
