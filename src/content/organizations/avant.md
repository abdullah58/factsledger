## Overview

Avant, LLC is a Chicago-based consumer financial technology company founded in 2012 as AvantCredit by CEO Al Goldstein with co-founders John Sun and Paul Zhang ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/avantcredit-secures-20m-to-make-financial-loans-more-accessible-with-big-data)). It offers personal loans, credit cards and personal financial management tools aimed at middle-income American borrowers ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/loans/2026/fintech-avant-boosts-financing-capacity-with-new-200-million-dollar-deal/)). The identity that matters most for understanding the company is structural: Avant is not a bank. Its branded credit products are originated by WebBank, a Utah-chartered industrial bank, under a lending-program agreement; WebBank makes the loans to consumers who apply through Avant's website and sells them — including to Avant — within two days, while Avant services the loans ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=b4560b61-7957-4ba9-baab-abd52db8ad48), describing the federal court record in *Meade v. Avant*).

## The near-prime bet

Sun and Zhang had come through Y Combinator with Debteye, a startup that automated credit counseling, and saw what they described as a "huge product-gap" for near-prime customers — borrowers with below-average credit scores whose access to credit had tightened since the 2008 recession ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/avantcredit-secures-20m-to-make-financial-loans-more-accessible-with-big-data)). AvantCredit's platform used machine learning to determine lending risk from dozens of data sources captured during the application itself, searching thousands of data attributes for patterns, and could approve and fund a loan of up to $10,000 in near real-time ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/avantcredit-secures-20m-to-make-financial-loans-more-accessible-with-big-data)). The company launched in July 2013 and that year raised a $20 million round led by August Capital and Victory Park Capital, on top of an earlier $9 million in equity and $25 million in debt ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/avantcredit-secures-20m-to-make-financial-loans-more-accessible-with-big-data)).

That bank-partnership structure later became a legal battleground. In *Meade v. Avant*, decided March 1, 2018, the U.S. District Court for the District of Colorado addressed claims by Colorado's consumer-credit regulator that Avant had charged excess finance and delinquency charges in violation of state law ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=b4560b61-7957-4ba9-baab-abd52db8ad48)). Avant argued that because the loans were originated by a state-chartered bank, federal law completely preempted the state claims; the court rejected that argument, holding that the federal statute governed state-chartered banks and was not intended to displace regulation of lenders who are not banks ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=b4560b61-7957-4ba9-baab-abd52db8ad48)). The ruling left open the "true lender" question — whether the bank or the fintech partner is the real party in interest — a fault line that has defined the rent-a-bank debate across online lending ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=b4560b61-7957-4ba9-baab-abd52db8ad48)).

## The FTC enforcement arc

In April 2019 the Federal Trade Commission sued Avant, LLC, alleging the company had engaged in deceptive and unfair loan servicing practices — "systemic issues with the company's loan servicing platform," in the words of the FTC's Bureau of Consumer Protection director ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/04/online-lending-company-agrees-settle-ftc-charges-it-engaged-deceptive-unfair-loan-servicing)). The complaint alleged Avant withdrew money from consumers' accounts or charged their credit cards without authorization — in some cases debiting a monthly payment twice or more, with one consumer's payment debited 11 times in a single day — and that despite hundreds of consumer complaints and internal documents acknowledging the problem, the unauthorized charging continued ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/04/online-lending-company-agrees-settle-ftc-charges-it-engaged-deceptive-unfair-loan-servicing)). The FTC further alleged Avant falsely advertised that it accepted credit- and debit-card payments (rejecting them in practice), gave inaccurate payoff quotes, collected more than quoted payoffs, failed to credit check payments properly, and required borrowers to agree to recurring automatic bank debits as a condition of getting a loan, violating the Telemarketing Sales Rule and the Electronic Fund Transfer Act ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/04/online-lending-company-agrees-settle-ftc-charges-it-engaged-deceptive-unfair-loan-servicing)).

The stipulated order, filed in the Northern District of Illinois on April 15, 2019 and approved by a 5–0 Commission vote, imposed a $3.85 million judgment to be returned to harmed consumers, and barred Avant from taking unauthorized payments and from collecting payment by remotely created check ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/04/online-lending-company-agrees-settle-ftc-charges-it-engaged-deceptive-unfair-loan-servicing)). In January 2022 the FTC mailed more than $3.7 million in refund checks to 17,367 consumers, averaging $216 each ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/01/ftc-returns-more-37-million-consumers-harmed-online-lender-avant)).

## The securitization engine

Behind the consumer-facing brand sits a capital-markets funding machine. In February 2026 Avant closed a $200 million personal-loan securitization — its 23rd since founding and its seventh revolving transaction — that became the first of its deals to earn AAA ratings from both Fitch Ratings and Kroll Bond Rating Agency ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/loans/2026/fintech-avant-boosts-financing-capacity-with-new-200-million-dollar-deal/)). The 24-month revolving structure, which lets Avant reinvest principal as loans are repaid, is expected to provide more than $500 million in financing capacity over the deal's life; combined with existing asset funding commitments, the company reported about $2.5 billion in long-term funding commitments supporting its personal-loan and credit-card businesses ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/loans/2026/fintech-avant-boosts-financing-capacity-with-new-200-million-dollar-deal/)). The company says it uses data and machine learning to serve middle-income Americans with credit cards, personal loans and financial-management tools, and reports having connected more than 4 million unique customers to $13.2 billion in personal loans since 2013 and 2.9 million credit cards since 2017 — figures that are company-reported and have not been independently verified ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/loans/2026/fintech-avant-boosts-financing-capacity-with-new-200-million-dollar-deal/)).
