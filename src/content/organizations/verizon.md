## Overview

Verizon Communications is one of America's largest telecommunications companies — a wireless-first carrier with 2025 revenue of $138.2 billion and about 147 million wireless retail connections, formed in 2000 from the $60 billion merger of Bell Atlantic and GTE ([Verizon Fact Sheet](https://www.verizon.com/about/our-company/verizon-fact-sheet); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Verizon-Communications)). It spent 22 years in the Dow Jones Industrial Average before Alphabet replaced it in June 2026, ranked second-lowest on Accountable.US's 2022 American Democracy Scorecard, and is now rebuilding around fiber and 5G under CEO Dan Schulman ([Tech Times](https://Www.techtimes.com/articles/319350/20260630/alphabet-joins-dow-jones-record-day-index-closes-above-52000-first-time.htm); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression); [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/verizon-ceo-were-no-longer-a-hunting-ground-for-rivals/)).

The company is headquartered in New York City, trades on the NYSE and Nasdaq under the ticker VZ, and reports two segments: consumer services (wireless, FiOS broadband and TV, fixed wireless home internet) and business and government services ([Verizon Fact Sheet](https://www.verizon.com/about/our-company/verizon-fact-sheet); [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Verizon-Communications)). It ranked 28th on the Fortune 500 and has increased its dividend for 20 consecutive years ([Verizon Fact Sheet](https://www.verizon.com/about/our-company/verizon-fact-sheet)).

## From the Bell breakup to a national carrier

Verizon's roots run to the 1982 antitrust settlement that broke up AT&T's Bell System, when its local operations were divided among seven regional "Baby Bells" — including Bell Atlantic, serving Pennsylvania, New Jersey, Delaware, Maryland, Virginia, and West Virginia ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Verizon-Communications)). After the Telecommunications Act of 1996 loosened industry rules, Bell Atlantic merged with fellow Baby Bell NYNEX, extending its territory into New York and New England and moving its headquarters from Philadelphia to New York City ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Verizon-Communications)). In 1998 Bell Atlantic agreed to merge with GTE — then one of the largest local phone companies in America, operating in 29 states — and the $60 billion transaction closed in 2000, creating Verizon ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Verizon-Communications)).

Wireless was central from the start: a 1999 Bell Atlantic–Vodafone joint venture combined Bell Atlantic's wireless assets with Vodafone's AirTouch and PrimeCo, under Verizon majority ownership and management control ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Verizon-Communications)). The early 2000s brought the first U.S. 3G launch (2002), the FiOS fiber network (2004, after roughly $23 billion of buildout), the $8.5 billion MCI acquisition that became Verizon Business (2006), and 4G LTE (2010) ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Verizon-Communications)). In 2011 Ivan Seidenberg handed the CEO role to Lowell McAdam, and in 2013 Verizon bought Vodafone's 45% wireless stake for about $130 billion — one of the largest corporate buyouts ever — taking full ownership of Verizon Wireless ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Verizon-Communications)).

## The media detour and the fiber-first return

In the mid-2010s Verizon bet that digital media could offset slowing telecom growth: it bought AOL for about $4.4 billion in 2015 and Yahoo's core internet business for about $4.48 billion in 2017, combining them as Oath and later Verizon Media ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Verizon-Communications)). The bet failed decisively — the Associated Press reported Verizon spent about $9 billion on the two acquisitions and then wrote down Oath's value by roughly the $4.5 billion it had paid for Yahoo, conceding it could not challenge Google and Facebook in digital advertising ([Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/shoot_newsbriefs/internet-trailblazers-yahoo-and-aol-sold-again-5b/)). In May 2021 Verizon sold Verizon Media to Apollo Global Management for $5 billion ($4.25 billion in cash plus $750 million in preferred interests), kept a 10% stake in the renamed Yahoo, and refocused on networks ([Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/shoot_newsbriefs/internet-trailblazers-yahoo-and-aol-sold-again-5b/); [Verizon/GlobeNewswire](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/verizon-media-to-be-acquired-by-apollo-funds-2021-05-03)).

The refocus accelerated under Hans Vestberg (CEO from 2018, who also bought prepaid carrier TracFone for about $6.3 billion) and then Dan Schulman, the former PayPal chief and board member since 2018 who took over on October 4, 2025 ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Verizon-Communications); [SDxCentral](https://www.sdxcentral.com/news/verizon-appoints-dan-schulman-as-ceo-hans-vestberg-steps-down/); [Verizon Proxy](http://www.verizon.com/about/sites/default/files/2026-Proxy-Statement.pdf)). Weeks after arriving, Schulman announced more than 13,000 job cuts — about 13% of the workforce — in a turnaround targeting $5 billion in annual operating-expense reductions by end-2026 ([Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/verizon-ceo-were-no-longer-a-hunting-ground-for-rivals/)).

The full-circle move came in fiber. Having sold much of its rural and exurban wireline business to Frontier Communications in the 2010s, Verizon agreed in September 2024 to buy the entire company for about $20 billion ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Verizon-Communications); [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/retail/verizon-seals-billion-dollar-acquisition-to-lure-customers)). To clear regulators it rolled back its diversity, equity, and inclusion policies for May 2025 FCC approval and promised California 75,000 new fiber locations, 25 rural wireless towers, and free low-income internet for at least a decade ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/retail/verizon-seals-billion-dollar-acquisition-to-lure-customers)). The deal closed in January 2026, giving Verizon roughly 30 million fiber passings across 31 states and Washington, D.C., with a stated goal of 40–50 million over the medium term ([Converge Digest](https://convergedigest.com/verizon-completes-frontier-acquisition-expands-fiber-reach-to-30m-passings/); [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/verizon-ceo-were-no-longer-a-hunting-ground-for-rivals/)). That same year, Alphabet (GOOGL) replaced Verizon in the Dow Jones on June 29, 2026, ending a 22-year run — S&P Dow Jones Indices cited Verizon's low share price, which had shrunk its index weight to about 0.5% — and Schulman's contract was extended through 2028 ([Tech Times](https://Www.techtimes.com/articles/319350/20260630/alphabet-joins-dow-jones-record-day-index-closes-above-52000-first-time.htm); [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202607244883/verizon-ceo-to-stay-for-at-least-another-year)).

## The 2022 American Democracy Scorecard

In September 2022, the watchdog group Accountable.US unveiled the American Democracy Scorecard, grading the top 100 Fortune 500 companies on 14 criteria covering pro-democracy statements, spending-disclosure transparency, and PAC donations to federal lawmakers who opposed voting-rights legislation, backed 2020-election objections, or opposed the January 6 committee — plus contributions to state officials backing restrictive voting measures or 2020 audits ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression)). Two-thirds of the companies failed; AT&T was the lowest scorer, Verizon the second-lowest, with [Charter Communications](/organizations/charter-communications/) and Comcast also in the bottom ten — making telecommunications the worst-ranked industry ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression)).

Its data showed $6.2 million in Fortune 100 donations to state legislators backing restrictive voting laws, $988,059 to state lawmakers supporting post-2020 election audits, $19.2 million to federal lawmakers opposing the January 6 commission, and $5.7 million to members of Congress voting to overturn the election; 33 of the 50 companies that had paused giving to election objectors later resumed it ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression)). [Home Depot](/organizations/home-depot/) was the top donor to federal lawmakers who voted to overturn the election, at more than $350,000 ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression)). Accountable.US president Kyle Herrig wrote directly to CEOs — including AT&T's [John Stankey](/people/john-stankey/) — urging alignment of actions with stated values, while director Lindsey Melki called the scorecard a "living tool" and the group planned a six-figure digital media campaign; no telecom company responded to a comment request before publication ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was Verizon founded, and how did it get its name?**
Verizon was formed in 2000 when the $60 billion Bell Atlantic–GTE merger closed. The brand name, announced that April, blends *veritas* (Latin, "truth") and *horizon* — meant to signal integrity and future possibility, though analysts at the time were unkind about the mash-up ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/Verizon-Communications); [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2013/04/03/two-major-mobile-milestones.aspx)).

**Who is Verizon's current CEO?**
Dan Schulman — former PayPal CEO and a Verizon board member since 2018 — became chief executive on October 4, 2025, succeeding Hans Vestberg, who was named special advisor. Schulman's turnaround playbook — job cuts, fiber expansion, and AI-led efficiency — earned him a contract extension through December 31, 2028 ([Verizon Proxy](http://www.verizon.com/about/sites/default/files/2026-Proxy-Statement.pdf); [SDxCentral](https://www.sdxcentral.com/news/verizon-appoints-dan-schulman-as-ceo-hans-vestberg-steps-down/); [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/202607244883/verizon-ceo-to-stay-for-at-least-another-year)).

**Why was Verizon removed from the Dow Jones Industrial Average in 2026?**
S&P Dow Jones Indices announced on June 23, 2026, that Alphabet (GOOGL) would replace Verizon before the open on June 29, ending a 22-year run. Because the Dow is price-weighted, Verizon's low share price had shrunk its weight to about 0.5% — index arithmetic, not a collapse of the business ([Tech Times](https://Www.techtimes.com/articles/319350/20260630/alphabet-joins-dow-jones-record-day-index-closes-above-52000-first-time.htm)).

**Why did Verizon rank so low on the 2022 American Democracy Scorecard?**
Accountable.US graded the top 100 Fortune 500 companies on 14 democracy criteria, including spending transparency and PAC donations to lawmakers who opposed voting-rights legislation or backed 2020-election objections. Under that methodology Verizon scored second-lowest, after AT&T — the group's assessment, not a legal finding — and the company did not respond to a comment request ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90790098/democracy-scorecard-top-companies-voter-suppression)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not present the American Democracy Scorecard's rankings as anything other than Accountable.US's own methodology applied to public campaign-finance data — a second-lowest score is the group's assessment, not a legal finding, and the group itself described the tool as something companies could improve on. It does not state a current Verizon employee count, because the company does not publish one on its fact sheet. It does not claim the FCC's Frontier-approval conditions (including the DEI-policy rollback) remain unchanged, only that they were the reported terms of approval. It does not cover Verizon's current consumer pricing, plans, or investment merits. And it flags, without resolving, the one source disagreement found: Britannica dates the Frontier closing to 2025, while Verizon's own proxy and contemporaneous trade press date it to January 2026.
