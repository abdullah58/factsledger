## Overview

This profile covers **Guideline, Inc.** — the San Francisco financial-technology company that provides 401(k) and other retirement plans to small and medium-sized businesses — and not other businesses using the name. Guideline built an integrated system for retirement-plan setup, participant enrollment, recordkeeping, investment management and compliance, and grew to more than 65,000 business customers with $20 billion in assets under its plans ([401(k) Specialist Magazine](https://401kspecialistmag.com/gusto-acquires-smb-retirement-plan-provider-guideline-in-landmark-deal/)). Founded by former TaskRabbit co-founder Kevin Busque, it distinguished itself with a flat, per-employee fee instead of the traditional model of charging a percentage of assets under management ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/01/sources-gusto-paid-600m-to-acquire-guideline-plans-to-divest-customers-linked-to-rivals/)). In August 2025 Gusto — the payroll and HR software company that had been Guideline's partner since 2015 — announced it had agreed to acquire it in its biggest-ever acquisition ([401(k) Specialist Magazine](https://401kspecialistmag.com/gusto-acquires-smb-retirement-plan-provider-guideline-in-landmark-deal/)).

## The flat-fee challenger

Guideline's pitch to small employers was simplicity and price. Rather than the asset-based fees traditional 401(k) providers charge, it levied a flat per-employee fee, betting that automation and deep payroll integrations would make serving small plans profitable at volume ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/01/sources-gusto-paid-600m-to-acquire-guideline-plans-to-divest-customers-linked-to-rivals/)). The strategy worked at scale: by 2025 Guideline served over 65,000 customers, helping more than a million people at businesses, startups and franchises save for retirement, with $20 billion in plan assets ([401(k) Specialist Magazine](https://401kspecialistmag.com/gusto-acquires-smb-retirement-plan-provider-guideline-in-landmark-deal/)). CNBC reported its annualized recurring revenue at $140 million as of January 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/01/sources-gusto-paid-600m-to-acquire-guideline-plans-to-divest-customers-linked-to-rivals/)).

Its distribution ran through payroll providers. Gusto was its first fully integrated payroll partner, and Gusto CEO Joshua Reeves recalled meeting Busque in 2016 when "Guideline was just getting started" — calling the company "already the most popular retirement product on Gusto by far" ([401(k) Specialist Magazine](https://401kspecialistmag.com/gusto-acquires-smb-retirement-plan-provider-guideline-in-landmark-deal/)). The relationship was never exclusive: customers could also set up Guideline plans through ADP, Intuit, Paylocity, TriNet and Rippling ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/01/sources-gusto-paid-600m-to-acquire-guideline-plans-to-divest-customers-linked-to-rivals/)). Investors rewarded the growth: a $200 million Series D led by General Atlantic in 2021 valued Guideline at $1.15 billion, bringing total funding to about $340 million from backers including Felicis, Tiger Global and NEA ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/01/sources-gusto-paid-600m-to-acquire-guideline-plans-to-divest-customers-linked-to-rivals/)).

## The Gusto acquisition — and its disputed details

On August 27, 2025, Gusto announced it had signed an agreement for Guideline to join it, calling the combination a single modern product for payroll, retirement benefits, health insurance, HR, time tracking and compliance for small businesses; the terms were not disclosed ([401(k) Specialist Magazine](https://401kspecialistmag.com/gusto-acquires-smb-retirement-plan-provider-guideline-in-landmark-deal/)). Reeves said the combined product would let business owners set up all their benefits in one place, employees onboard and update benefits in a single app, and compliance, taxes and payments be managed on one dashboard ([401(k) Specialist Magazine](https://401kspecialistmag.com/gusto-acquires-smb-retirement-plan-provider-guideline-in-landmark-deal/)). He also pledged that Gusto would keep supporting rival retirement providers on its platform: "We will always provide customers with a choice so they can use the software that best meets their specific needs" ([401(k) Specialist Magazine](https://401kspecialistmag.com/gusto-acquires-smb-retirement-plan-provider-guideline-in-landmark-deal/)).

What Gusto paid is contested. TechCrunch reported, citing a source familiar with the deal, that Gusto paid approximately $600 million — below Guideline's $1.15 billion private valuation, but still a return for early investors including Felicis, Tiger Global and NEA, with General Atlantic expected to earn a small profit ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/01/sources-gusto-paid-600m-to-acquire-guideline-plans-to-divest-customers-linked-to-rivals/)). Guideline's spokesperson characterized the $600 million figure as incorrect without offering another number ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/01/sources-gusto-paid-600m-to-acquire-guideline-plans-to-divest-customers-linked-to-rivals/)). Also contested: TechCrunch reported, citing three sources, that Gusto plans to sell off Guideline accounts associated with rival payroll companies, with proceeds shared among Gusto and Guideline shareholders — while the company's spokesperson said it has no plans to part ways with any customers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/01/sources-gusto-paid-600m-to-acquire-guideline-plans-to-divest-customers-linked-to-rivals/)). Gusto declined to comment on both the price and the divestment plans ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/01/sources-gusto-paid-600m-to-acquire-guideline-plans-to-divest-customers-linked-to-rivals/)).

The rationale for selling also drew attention: a Guideline spokesperson confirmed the company had been profitable for over a year, but it faced intense competition — notably from Human Interest, its closest rival, backed by SoftBank and Baillie Gifford, which grew 70% the prior year ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/01/sources-gusto-paid-600m-to-acquire-guideline-plans-to-divest-customers-linked-to-rivals/)).
