## A Pentagon contract lands on a vice president's portfolio

In late September 2026, the Pentagon awarded a satellite-surveillance contract to True Anomaly, a defense startup backed by a venture fund in which Vice President J.D. Vance holds a stake worth up to $1 million — plus a right to more than half of the investment profits paid to the fund's managers ([Read Sludge](https://readsludge.com/2026/09/26/satellite-company-backed-by-a-jd-vance-fund-wins-pentagon-award/)). True Anomaly was one of two companies selected to build prototype satellites for GHOST-R, a Space Force effort to observe and track other spacecraft in geosynchronous orbit; Northrop Grumman received the other award ([Read Sludge](https://readsludge.com/2026/09/26/satellite-company-backed-by-a-jd-vance-fund-wins-pentagon-award/)). The contract values were not disclosed, but successful prototypes have a path into the Space Force's RG-XX surveillance satellite program ([Read Sludge](https://readsludge.com/2026/09/26/satellite-company-backed-by-a-jd-vance-fund-wins-pentagon-award/)).

That fund is Narya Capital — the Ohio venture firm Vance co-founded in 2019, and the vehicle at the center of his short, sharp career in technology investing ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)).

## Two firms before the firm's own

Vance moved to the Bay Area shortly after graduating from Yale Law School in 2013 to pursue entrepreneurship, and in 2016 he joined [Mithril Capital](/organizations/mithril-capital/), the San Francisco firm co-founded by Peter Thiel, as a junior investor ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)). He worked at Mithril for about a year — one former colleague said he never once saw Vance in the office, while another remembered seeing him around, noting that he traveled a lot to promote his memoir *Hillbilly Elegy*; neither remembered significant deals that Vance drove there ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)).

In March 2017 Vance left Mithril for [Revolution](/organizations/revolution/), the investment firm founded by AOL co-founder Steve Case, where he was hired to focus on the Rise of the Rest initiative — a seed fund hunting for investments outside the typical tech ecosystems of Silicon Valley, New York, and Boston ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)). Over eighteen months there he was lead partner or co-lead on 14 deals, including the defense-tech startup Anduril Industries, the artificial-intelligence startup Pryon, and the agtech company AppHarvest ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)). He left Revolution at the end of 2019 and started Narya with Greenspon ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)).

## Narya itself

Narya Capital was founded in 2019 by [J.D. Vance](/people/jd-vance/) and Colin Greenspon ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)). The pair raised $120 million in their first fund, with backing from Peter Thiel, venture capitalist Marc Andreessen, and former Google CEO Eric Schmidt, according to a person familiar with the fund's financials ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)). That fund generated a multiple on invested capital of 1.67 and an internal rate of return of 24.35% after fees as of March 2023, according to an investor document ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)).

Since its founding, Narya had made 14 investments, one of them in stealth, the same reporting found — including Rumble, the video platform popular with the political right that went public through a special-purpose acquisition company while Vance was running for the Senate, and Hallow, a Catholic meditation and prayer app ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)). Three other investments had ties to Vivek Ramaswamy, the businessman who once vied for the Republican presidential nomination and had been Vance's law-school classmate ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)).

## The AppHarvest arc

The blemish on Vance's record is AppHarvest, the Kentucky-based indoor-farming tech company — one of his bigger investments, made through two different venture firms ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)). Vance invested again in the company through Narya and joined its board in July 2020; other board members included Martha Stewart and investor Jeffrey Ubben ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)). In February 2021 AppHarvest went public through a SPAC, and Vance resigned from the board two months later to make his Senate run ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)).

Months after Vance left the board, AppHarvest was sued by individual investors and a county retirement association in five separate lawsuits alleging the company had misled regulators and investors and failed to disclose adverse facts about its operations, including a lack of employee training and an inability to hire and retain employees ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)). The company filed for bankruptcy in mid-2023 ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)).

## The exit — and what stayed

Vance left the investing world in 2022 after being elected to represent Ohio in the U.S. Senate ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)). His 2022 financials reveal an income of $110,146 from Narya ([Livemint (Wall Street Journal)](https://www.livemint.com/politics/inside-jd-vance-s-short-lived-career-as-a-venture-capitalist-11721907809610.html)). But he kept his financial stake: his latest annual financial disclosure, which covers 2025, lists an investment worth between $500,001 and $1 million in Narya Capital Fund I, in which he is a general partner ([Read Sludge](https://readsludge.com/2026/09/26/satellite-company-backed-by-a-jd-vance-fund-wins-pentagon-award/)).

The disclosure's agreements entitle him to "56.4% for the first $7 million of aggregate carried interest proceeds distributed to the entity, and 55.2% thereafter," plus 60% of the entity's net fee proceeds and a $525,000 "Management Fee Set Off Contribution" ([Read Sludge](https://readsludge.com/2026/09/26/satellite-company-backed-by-a-jd-vance-fund-wins-pentagon-award/)). If the LLC received $7 million in carried interest, Vance's share would be nearly $4 million ([Read Sludge](https://readsludge.com/2026/09/26/satellite-company-backed-by-a-jd-vance-fund-wins-pentagon-award/)).

True Anomaly, meanwhile, kept collecting government work: in April it was named one of 14 companies eligible to compete under the Andromeda satellite-surveillance program, which carries a $1.843 billion shared ceiling, and Space Force named it among the companies awarded agreements to develop space-based interceptors for the Golden Dome missile-defense project ([Read Sludge](https://readsludge.com/2026/09/26/satellite-company-backed-by-a-jd-vance-fund-wins-pentagon-award/)). Its $650 million funding round in April itself had named Narya among the investors ([Read Sludge](https://readsludge.com/2026/09/26/satellite-company-backed-by-a-jd-vance-fund-wins-pentagon-award/)).

Two facts stand alongside the numbers. Federal law generally bars executive-branch officials from taking part in matters affecting their financial interests, but the law does not apply to the president or vice president — Vance must disclose his interests but need not sell his Narya stake or recuse himself ([Read Sludge](https://readsludge.com/2026/09/26/satellite-company-backed-by-a-jd-vance-fund-wins-pentagon-award/)). And there is no evidence that he participated in selecting True Anomaly for the GHOST-R contract; neither Vance nor True Anomaly replied to Sludge's requests for comment ([Read Sludge](https://readsludge.com/2026/09/26/satellite-company-backed-by-a-jd-vance-fund-wins-pentagon-award/)).
