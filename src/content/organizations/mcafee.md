## Overview

McAfee was founded in 1987 by John McAfee and brought the first commercial antivirus product to market ([Reuters](https://www.technologyforyou.org/mcafee-to-be-taken-private-by-advent-led-group-in-14-billion-deal/)). What followed is one of the strangest ownership odysseys in enterprise software: in just over a decade the company went from public to [Intel](/organizations/intel/) subsidiary to Intel Security to TPG-controlled spinout to public company again — and then private once more, in what was at the time the largest pure-play cybersecurity acquisition in history ([CRN](https://www.crn.com/slide-shows/security/mcafee-s-cybersecurity-private-equity-takeover-6-things-to-know)).

## The ping-pong decade

Intel bought McAfee for $7.7 billion in 2010 — by which point the founder had long since left and had no involvement with the company — and folded it into a division it renamed Intel Security ([Reuters](https://www.technologyforyou.org/mcafee-to-be-taken-private-by-advent-led-group-in-14-billion-deal/)). The marriage didn't last. In April 2017 Intel spun the unit back out as McAfee LLC, selling a 51% stake to private-equity firm TPG Capital in a deal valuing the business at $4.2 billion including debt, while keeping 49% for itself; Thoma Bravo later joined as a minority investor ([Reuters](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/reuters/intel-john-mcafee-settle-lawsuits-over-antivirus-pioneer-s-name-117070600114_1.html)).

Three years later McAfee was public again: the October 2020 IPO raised $740 million at a $9.5 billion valuation, then the highest ever for a newly public cybersecurity vendor ([CRN](https://www.crn.com/slide-shows/security/mcafee-s-cybersecurity-private-equity-takeover-6-things-to-know)). In July 2021 it sold its enterprise business to Symphony Technology Group for $4 billion to become a pure consumer play with about 20 million subscribers ([CRN](https://www.crn.com/slide-shows/security/mcafee-s-cybersecurity-private-equity-takeover-6-things-to-know)). Four months later, an investor group led by Advent International — with Permira, Crosspoint Capital, the Canada Pension Plan Investment Board and others — agreed to take the whole company private at $26 a share, a 22.6% premium, in a deal worth over $14 billion on an enterprise basis ([Reuters](https://www.technologyforyou.org/mcafee-to-be-taken-private-by-advent-led-group-in-14-billion-deal/)). The transaction completed in 2022.

## The founder who lost his own name

While the company changed hands, its founder fought a stranger battle: for the right to call himself McAfee. In September 2016 John McAfee sued Intel after the chipmaker warned that renaming his new venture "John McAfee Global Technologies" would infringe its trademarks; Intel countersued for trademark infringement and unfair competition ([Reuters](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/reuters/intel-john-mcafee-settle-lawsuits-over-antivirus-pioneer-s-name-117070600114_1.html)). The July 2017 settlement was amicable but lopsided: McAfee agreed never to use his name, trademark it, or use the phrases "John McAfee Privacy Phone" or "John McAfee Global Technologies" in connection with cybersecurity products — while keeping the right to invoke his name in other contexts, including his history as the man who built and sold McAfee Associates ([Reuters](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/reuters/intel-john-mcafee-settle-lawsuits-over-antivirus-pioneer-s-name-117070600114_1.html)). Neither side admitted wrongdoing.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not cover McAfee's product history or the founder's later legal troubles and death — the research pass focused on the corporate ownership arc and the name dispute. The Intel acquisition is dated to its 2010 announcement; the deal closed in 2011, which is why some sources attach that year. The "$14 billion, largest pure-play cybersecurity acquisition" framing is CRN's at the time of the 2021 announcement; later deals may have surpassed it. The $14 billion is enterprise value including debt repayment; equity value was about $12 billion.
