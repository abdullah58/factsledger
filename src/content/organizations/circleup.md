## Overview

CircleUp was a San Francisco fintech founded in 2012 by investors Ryan Caldbeck and Rory Eakin as an equity crowdfunding marketplace for consumer packaged goods (CPG) brands — a bid to bring transparency and data science to a corner of the market that traditional venture capital largely ignored ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-breakup-lending-fund-sold-vc-fund-in-transit-ai-play-fate-unknown/)). Its signature asset was Helio, a proprietary machine-learning platform that analyzed companies' financials, brand strength, retail distribution, and teams to predict which emerging brands would succeed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/07/26/circleup-credit-advisors/?ncid=rss)). By 2017 the platform had helped 243 companies raise a total of $365 million in equity financing ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/07/26/circleup-credit-advisors/?ncid=rss)).

## The pivot, then the breakup

In 2017 CircleUp remade itself from marketplace into investor. It raised a $125 million CircleUp Growth Partners fund to back brands chosen with Helio's insights plus traditional investment professionals — early bets included supplement brand HUM and meat-snack maker 4505 — and launched CircleUp Credit Advisors, extending $25,000-$600,000 working-capital loans funded through a $20 million facility from Community Investment Management, while sunsetting the original crowdfunding marketplace ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-breakup-lending-fund-sold-vc-fund-in-transit-ai-play-fate-unknown/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/07/26/circleup-credit-advisors/?ncid=rss)).

Six years later, the company took itself apart. In 2023 CircleUp began divesting its financing vehicles: the Credit Advisors loan fund went to Brightflow AI — a venture-backed financial-tracking platform that also absorbed several CircleUp team members — while the Growth Partners VC fund, which had backed nearly 30 brands including Liquid I.V., Nutpods, and Barnana, was put up for sale to an unidentified multi-strategy asset manager ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-breakup-lending-fund-sold-vc-fund-in-transit-ai-play-fate-unknown/)). Sources told NOSH the lending exit was driven by rising interest rates and risk reduction; one former borrower guessed the CPG loan book's unprofitable borrowers had made the portfolio itself unprofitable ([NOSH](https://www.nosh.com/news/2023/circleup-breakup-lending-fund-sold-vc-fund-in-transit-ai-play-fate-unknown/)).

What remained — the company and the Helio engine — was acquired by Brightflow AI for an undisclosed sum, combining Helio's brand analytics with Brightflow's cash-flow intelligence toolkit ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/425249/brightflow-ai-acquires-circleup/?abkw=citybizsanfrancisco)). At the time of the deal, CircleUp was credited with supplying over $1 billion in capital to more than 500 consumer companies, including Beyond Meat, Supergoop!, Liquid I.V., and Halo Top, backed by investors including Union Square Ventures, GV, Canaan Partners, and Temasek ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/425249/brightflow-ai-acquires-circleup/?abkw=citybizsanfrancisco)).
