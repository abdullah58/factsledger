## Overview

CoinTelegraph is a cryptocurrency news outlet founded in 2013, known for pairing its reporting with distinctive cartoon-style illustrations ([CoinDesk](https://Www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/07/crypto-news-site-cointelegraph-seeks-buyer-after-web-traffic-plunges)). It employs more than 200 people, according to its LinkedIn page, and its Middle East and North Africa franchise was acquired by Luna Media Corporation in July 2022 to fund global and regional expansion ([CoinDesk](https://Www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/07/crypto-news-site-cointelegraph-seeks-buyer-after-web-traffic-plunges)). After a Google manual penalty in October 2025 cut its organic traffic by about 80%, the outlet became the subject of a disputed October 2026 report that it was seeking a buyer — a claim it publicly denied ([CoinDesk](https://Www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/07/crypto-news-site-cointelegraph-seeks-buyer-after-web-traffic-plunges)).

## How it covers contested stories: allegation, denial, and rebuttal in one piece

The clearest direct evidence of CoinTelegraph's editorial approach is a November 19, 2025 article, "Senators Call for Probe Into Trump-Linked Crypto Firm Over Security Risks," covering a letter from Senators Elizabeth Warren and Jack Reed to Attorney General Pamela Bondi and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent urging an investigation of World Liberty Financial over alleged ties to sanctioned actors in North Korea and Russia ([CoinTelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/senators-trump-linked-wlfi-national-security-threat)). The article reports the underlying Accountable.US September report's claims — that WLFI tokens were sold to buyers with on-chain links to the Lazarus Group, a sanctioned Russian sanctions-evasion tool, an Iranian crypto exchange, and Tornado Cash — and the senators' warning that the sales "gave adversaries a seat at the table" by granting governance rights in the protocol ([CoinTelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/senators-trump-linked-wlfi-national-security-threat)). It then carries World Liberty Financial's denial to CNBC that it performed "rigorous AML/KYC checks" on all presale buyers and rejected millions of dollars from those who failed screening, and notes that CoinTelegraph had reached out to WLFI for comment but received no response by publication ([CoinTelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/senators-trump-linked-wlfi-national-security-threat)). Unusually, the same article also publishes the rebuttal: blockchain researcher Nick Bax's analysis showing the "Lazarus-linked" transactions were false positives generated by a joke memecoin contract called Dream Cash, which automatically routed tokens from an address labeled as Lazarus Group to anyone who claimed them ([CoinTelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/senators-trump-linked-wlfi-national-security-threat)). According to the article, an independent user, @shryder1337, claimed the tokens as a joke rather than transacting with North Korea — and had roughly $95,000 in WLFI tokens frozen as a result of the false positive ([CoinTelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/senators-trump-linked-wlfi-national-security-threat)). This allegation-denial-rebuttal structure, all inside one article, is the verified shape of how the outlet handles a contested story.

## Accuracy and security incidents on the record

On October 16, 2023, CoinTelegraph posted on X that the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission had approved BlackRock's spot bitcoin ETF — a false claim that spiked bitcoin's price from $27,900 to $30,000 before it reverted to about $28,103.80 ([Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/bitcoin-surges-to-30k-after-false-spot-etf-approval-tweet/)). The outlet deleted the post within about 30 minutes, apologized, and said it was starting an internal investigation, while BlackRock confirmed to multiple outlets that its application had not been approved ([Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/bitcoin-surges-to-30k-after-false-spot-etf-approval-tweet/)). The incident occurred days after the SEC declined to appeal a court ruling that it had inadequately justified denying Grayscale's bid to convert its Bitcoin Trust into a spot ETF, a context of intense market anticipation that amplified the false report's impact ([Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/bitcoin-surges-to-30k-after-false-spot-etf-approval-tweet/)). Separately, CoinDesk's 2026 reporting confirms the outlet's website was compromised by a front-end exploit in June 2025, an incident whose specifics this profile could not verify beyond that confirmation ([CoinDesk](https://Www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/07/crypto-news-site-cointelegraph-seeks-buyer-after-web-traffic-plunges)).

## Traffic collapse and the disputed sale report

CoinTelegraph's business troubles are the most heavily documented part of its recent history. In October 2025, Google issued a manual penalty that caused the site to disappear from search results and wiped out about 80% of its organic traffic ([CoinDesk](https://Www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/07/crypto-news-site-cointelegraph-seeks-buyer-after-web-traffic-plunges)). Similarweb data cited by CoinDesk put the damage in stark terms: more than 12 million monthly visits in December 2024, falling to just above 700,000 by September 1, 2026 ([CoinDesk](https://Www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/07/crypto-news-site-cointelegraph-seeks-buyer-after-web-traffic-plunges)). On October 7, 2026, CoinDesk reported, citing a person familiar with the matter who spoke on condition of anonymity, that the company was shopping itself to potential buyers, without disclosing a price ([CoinDesk](https://Www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/07/crypto-news-site-cointelegraph-seeks-buyer-after-web-traffic-plunges)). After publication, CoinTelegraph posted on X: "We are not for sale," adding that it welcomed scrutiny but would not stay silent when "speculation is presented as fact" ([CoinDesk](https://Www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/07/crypto-news-site-cointelegraph-seeks-buyer-after-web-traffic-plunges)). CoinDesk attributed part of the downturn to a prolonged period of flat crypto prices that pulled reader attention away from digital-asset newsrooms generally ([CoinDesk](https://Www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/07/crypto-news-site-cointelegraph-seeks-buyer-after-web-traffic-plunges)). Whether a sale process exists remains genuinely disputed — both the report and the denial are on the record, and neither side has produced dispositive evidence.

## Terms used in this profile

- **Manual penalty** — a Google search sanction that, per CoinDesk's reporting, caused CoinTelegraph's website to disappear from Google's search results and cost it roughly 80% of its organic traffic beginning in October 2025 ([CoinDesk](https://Www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/07/crypto-news-site-cointelegraph-seeks-buyer-after-web-traffic-plunges)).
- **Front-end exploit** — the June 2025 compromise of CoinTelegraph's website confirmed in CoinDesk's reporting; further details (reported fake airdrop pop-ups) could not be verified by a fully-read source ([CoinDesk](https://Www.coindesk.com/business/2026/10/07/crypto-news-site-cointelegraph-seeks-buyer-after-web-traffic-plunges)).
- **On-chain false positive** — as used by researcher [Nick Bax](/people/nick-bax/) in CoinTelegraph's own coverage: transactions labeled "Lazarus-linked" that were actually automatic payouts from the joke memecoin contract Dream Cash to anyone who claimed them ([CoinTelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/senators-trump-linked-wlfi-national-security-threat)).

**Is CoinTelegraph a reliable news source?**
It is an established specialist outlet, not a general-audience newspaper. On October 16, 2023, CoinTelegraph posted on X that the SEC had approved BlackRock's spot bitcoin ETF. Bitcoin spiked from $27,900 to $30,000, then fell back to about $28,103.80 once BlackRock confirmed to multiple outlets that no approval had been granted. The outlet deleted the post within about 30 minutes, apologized, and announced an internal investigation ([Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/bitcoin-surges-to-30k-after-false-spot-etf-approval-tweet/)).

Disputed. On October 7, 2026, CoinDesk reported, citing an anonymous person familiar with the matter, that the company was seeking a buyer after losing most of its search traffic. **What does CoinTelegraph cover?**
Blockchain technology, crypto assets, and digital-asset markets, per its own coverage record. Its November 2025 reporting on the Warren/Reed probe letter into World Liberty Financial — including the Accountable.US report's sanctions allegations, the firm's AML/KYC denial, and researcher Nick Bax's false-positive analysis — shows its typical mix of breaking news, regulatory coverage, and on-chain technical reporting ([CoinTelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/senators-trump-linked-wlfi-national-security-threat)).
