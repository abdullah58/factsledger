## Overview

**AARP Foundation is the 501(c)(3) charitable affiliate of [AARP](/organizations/aarp/), the membership and advocacy organization for Americans 50 and older. It fights senior poverty through free tax preparation, job training for low-income older workers, tutoring for young children, hunger relief, and legal advocacy for older adults.**

AARP formed the Foundation in 1961 to handle its philanthropic work with low-income Americans 50 and older in a more focused way, and the Foundation still identifies itself by founder Ethel Percy Andrus's dictum that "it is only in the giving of oneself to others that we truly live" ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/advocacy/jenkins-aarp-foundation-turns-60-2021/)). Its 2023 Form 990 confirms the legal facts: EIN 52-0794300, a 501(c)(3) corporation domiciled in Washington, D.C., and it names Claire Casey as president ([AARP Foundation 2023 Form 990](https://www.aarp.org/content/dam/aarp/aarp_foundation/2024/2023-AARP-Foundation-Form-990-public-disclosure.pdf)). That return reported $175.2 million in revenue and 27,465 volunteers; the Foundation says it serves more than 1.15 million low-income older adults a year ([AARP Foundation](https://www.aarp.org/aarp-foundation/about-us/)).

This profile is about the **Foundation**, not AARP itself: the 501(c)(3) charity is barred by IRS rules from intervening in political campaigns, the bright line between its charitable work and its parent's advocacy — its parent AARP, which the Foundation's own IRS filing lists as a 501(c)(4) social-welfare organization dedicated to persons over 50 ([AARP Foundation 2023 Form 990](https://www.aarp.org/content/dam/aarp/aarp_foundation/2024/2023-AARP-Foundation-Form-990-public-disclosure.pdf); [Internal Revenue Service](https://www.IRS.gov/newsroom/tax-exempt-organizations-and-political-activities)).

## Origins and structure

AARP formed the Foundation in 1961 to focus its philanthropic work with low-income Americans 50 and older, per the Foundation's 60th-anniversary account, which traces its guiding idea to AARP founder [Ethel Percy Andrus](/people/ethel-percy-andrus/) ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/advocacy/jenkins-aarp-foundation-turns-60-2021/)). Its 2023 Form 990 confirms the legal facts: AARP Foundation, EIN 52-0794300, formed 1961, a 501(c)(3) corporation domiciled in [Washington, D.C.](/places/district-of-columbia/) and headquartered at 601 E Street NW. The return names Claire Casey as president ([AARP Foundation 2023 Form 990](https://www.aarp.org/content/dam/aarp/aarp_foundation/2024/2023-AARP-Foundation-Form-990-public-disclosure.pdf)). The Foundation is the charitable affiliate, not AARP itself — the parent membership and advocacy organization — and 501(c)(3) status bars it from intervening in political campaigns under IRS rules ([Internal Revenue Service](https://www.IRS.gov/newsroom/tax-exempt-organizations-and-political-activities)). It serves AARP members and nonmembers alike, a point the Foundation's national Tax-Aide director made before a House subcommittee in 2021 ([U.S. House of Representatives](https://waysandmeans.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/L.-Lee-Villanueva-Testimony_AARP.pdf)). Its stated mission: creating and advancing effective solutions to reduce poverty for and with older adults; its current public formulation promises to strengthen financial resilience for and with older adults ([AARP Foundation](https://www.aarp.org/aarp-foundation/about-us/)).

## Signature programs

Tax-Aide is the flagship. In testimony before the House Ways & Means Subcommittee on Oversight in 2021, its national director described the largest free volunteer-based tax assistance program in the nation, run with the Internal Revenue Service: started in 1968 with four volunteers at one site, it had assisted more than 68 million people, fielding 36,000-plus volunteers at some 4,800 sites across the [United States](/places/united-states/) ([U.S. House of Representatives](https://waysandmeans.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/L.-Lee-Villanueva-Testimony_AARP.pdf)). In 2019, 2.5 million taxpayers received $1.4 billion in refunds plus $200 million in Earned Income Tax Credits. Experience Corps pairs trained volunteers over 50 with K-3 struggling readers; the independent Evidence for ESSA review gives it a "Strong" rating — two studies, 1,135 students, average effect size +0.13 ([Evidence for ESSA](https://evidenceforessa.org/program/aarp-foundation-experience-corps-tutoring-book-buddies/)). The Senior Community Service Employment Program, the Foundation's biggest 2023 expense line at $95 million, places unemployed adults 55 and older with nonprofits and public agencies through a Department of Labor grant — a pipeline now threatened by a 2027 White House budget proposal to zero out its funding ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/personal-finance/labor-dept-program-for-older-workers-faces-cuts)).

## Money and accountability

The Foundation's 2023 Form 990 reports $175.2 million in revenue against $191.9 million in expenses, with net assets of $635.6 million and 27,465 volunteers. Workforce programs — dominated by SCSEP — consumed $95.1 million, the largest program line; tax and credit programs $18.5 million; legal advocacy $6.1 million. That legal arm's signature result came in 2002, when the FTC, six states, AARP, and private attorneys settled predatory-lending charges against First Alliance Mortgage Company: nearly 18,000 borrowers could receive as much as $60 million, one of the largest consumer-protection recoveries in FTC history ([Federal Trade Commission](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2002/03/home-mortgage-lender-settles-predatory-lending-charges)). On accountability, the Foundation says 78 cents of every dollar goes to programs and that it meets all 20 of the Better Business Bureau's Standards for Charity Accountability ([AARP Foundation](https://www.aarp.org/aarp-foundation/about-us/)). It also claims top ratings from Candid, the BBB, and Charity Navigator — assertions the Foundation's own materials make but this profile could not verify independently.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the AARP Foundation?**
A nonprofit charity affiliated with AARP, formed in 1961, that works to reduce poverty among Americans over 50. It runs Tax-Aide, Experience Corps, the Senior Community Service Employment Program, hunger relief, and legal advocacy — serving members and nonmembers alike ([AARP Foundation 2023 Form 990](https://www.aarp.org/content/dam/aarp/aarp_foundation/2024/2023-AARP-Foundation-Form-990-public-disclosure.pdf)).

**Is the AARP Foundation the same as AARP?**
No. AARP is the membership and advocacy organization; the Foundation is its separate 501(c)(3) charitable affiliate (EIN 52-0794300). The Foundation's own IRS filing lists its parent AARP as a 501(c)(4) social-welfare organization — and as a 501(c)(3), the Foundation is barred by IRS rules from intervening in political campaigns, and it focuses on charitable programs rather than lobbying ([Internal Revenue Service](https://www.IRS.gov/newsroom/tax-exempt-organizations-and-political-activities)).

**How big is Tax-Aide?**
Tax-Aide is the nation's largest free volunteer-based tax assistance program, run with the IRS since 1968. In testimony to Congress, the Foundation said it has helped more than 68 million people; it fields 36,000-plus volunteers at about 4,800 sites and typically serves over 2 million filers a year ([U.S. House of Representatives](https://waysandmeans.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/L.-Lee-Villanueva-Testimony_AARP.pdf)).

**Where does the Foundation's money go?**
In 2023 it reported $175.2 million in revenue and $191.9 million in expenses; workforce programs (led by SCSEP) took $95.1 million, tax and credit programs $18.5 million, and legal advocacy $6.1 million. It says 78 cents of each dollar funds programs serving low-income older adults ([AARP Foundation 2023 Form 990](https://www.aarp.org/content/dam/aarp/aarp_foundation/2024/2023-AARP-Foundation-Form-990-public-disclosure.pdf)).

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not resolve the Foundation's original pre-rename name: the 2023 Form 990 gives a 1961 formation year (matching the 2021 60th anniversary), but no fully-read source confirmed an earlier name, so none is stated.
- It does not settle whether Experience Corps launched in 1995 or 1996, or whether Drive to End Hunger launched in 2010 or 2011; the Foundation's own materials give both years, and both disagreements are flagged in openItems.
- It does not independently verify the Foundation's claimed ratings from Candid, the Better Business Bureau, or Charity Navigator; those are presented as the Foundation's own statements.
- It does not claim that the Foundation alone won the First Alliance Mortgage case: FTC documents name AARP as the joint plaintiff alongside six states and private attorneys; AARP's timeline credits the Foundation's litigation arm.
- It does not take a position on SCSEP's job-placement rate: the Foundation says a majority of participants reach unsubsidized jobs, while a September 2025 House Appropriations analysis (reported by TheStreet) put the figure under half.
