## Overview

Booking.com is a Dutch online travel agency headquartered in Amsterdam and the flagship brand of Booking Holdings, the US-listed group that also owns Priceline, Agoda, KAYAK and OpenTable ([Booking Holdings](https://www.bookingholdings.com/about/factsheet/)). It began in 1996 as Bookings.nl, a small hotel-booking website built by University of Twente graduate Geert-Jan Bruinsma, and was bought by Priceline in July 2005 for $133 million ([University of Twente](https://www.utwente.nl/en/alumni/inspiring-alumni/ut-canon/stories/geert-jan-bruinsma/); [Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/Travel-News/Online-Travel/Priceline-buys-European-res-firm)). Today it lists about 4.4 million properties in more than 220 countries and territories, and its parent booked 1,235 million room nights worth $186.1 billion in gross bookings in 2025 ([Booking Holdings](https://s201.q4cdn.com/865305287/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/Investor-Presentation-February-2026-_v-Final.pdf); [Booking Holdings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553126000008/q4-25bkngearningsrelease.htm)). It is also Europe's most-regulated travel platform: an EU gatekeeper under the Digital Markets Act, fined €413.24 million in Spain, and sued by more than 10,000 hotels over the price-parity clauses that built its market power ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-designates-booking-gatekeeper-and-opens-market-investigation-x); [CNMC](https://www.cnmc.es/sites/default/files/5555287.pdf); [Travel Weekly](https://travelweekly.co.uk/news/more-than-10000-european-hotels-seek-billions-in-claims-against-bookingcom)).

The distinction that matters most for reading this profile is between the brand and the group. Booking.com is the accommodation marketplace; Booking Holdings is the parent, and its published room-night, gross-booking and revenue figures cover all five of its brands, not Booking.com alone. Wherever this profile gives a number, it says which of the two it belongs to — the group figures are so large precisely because Booking.com dominates them, but they are not Booking.com-only numbers.

## From a server under a desk to a $133 million sale

The company started in July 1996, when Bruinsma — two years out of the University of Twente, where he had studied business information technology — set up the first hotel-booking website with a server under his desk, after his former internship supervisor Jan Willem Smeenk suggested hotel reservations over dinner ([University of Twente](https://www.utwente.nl/en/alumni/inspiring-alumni/ut-canon/stories/geert-jan-bruinsma/)). Working from the university's incubator in Enschede, he and intern Richard de Vries built Bookings.nl, raised starting capital from 18 investors — mostly friends and fraternity members — and by the end of January 1997 had ten hotels signed up. The first hotel transmitted its available rooms to the system, and the first booking followed shortly after ([University of Twente](https://www.utwente.nl/en/alumni/inspiring-alumni/ut-canon/stories/geert-jan-bruinsma/)).

The early growth came from tactics that now look like textbook online marketing: giving each hotel its own page so the site could be found on the search engine AltaVista, building an affiliate system that eventually drew thousands of websites, and licensing the model abroad before buying the licences back ([University of Twente](https://www.utwente.nl/en/alumni/inspiring-alumni/ut-canon/stories/geert-jan-bruinsma/)). In 2002 Bruinsma stepped back and sold most of his shares, choosing family and travel over the risks of further growth ([University of Twente](https://www.utwente.nl/en/alumni/inspiring-alumni/ut-canon/stories/geert-jan-bruinsma/)). The decisive turn came in 2003, when [Google](/organizations/google/) AdWords arrived: suddenly Bookings.nl could place precisely targeted ads and see exactly what each one earned, and it became Google's largest customer — at one point Google asked it to slow down, because its system was creating new campaigns faster than Google's servers could process them ([University of Twente](https://www.utwente.nl/en/alumni/inspiring-alumni/ut-canon/stories/geert-jan-bruinsma/)).

That same advertising engine nearly sank the company. By 2005 Bookings.nl had bought millions of dollars of Google ads but was only paid after guests actually stayed, leaving a cash-flow gap — and "just at that moment," Bruinsma recalled, Priceline took it over, giving it room to pay the Google bill ([University of Twente](https://www.utwente.nl/en/alumni/inspiring-alumni/ut-canon/stories/geert-jan-bruinsma/)). Priceline had bought Cambridge-based Active Hotels for about $161 million in September 2004; in July 2005 it paid $133 million in cash for Bookings B.V., kept its management, and merged the two into the business now called Booking.com ([Hospitality Net](https://www.hospitalitynet.org/news/4020702.html); [Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/Travel-News/Online-Travel/Priceline-buys-European-res-firm); [Skift](https://skift.com/2014/11/19/interview-pricelines-ma-guru-on-the-most-successful-acquisition-in-online-travel-history/)). The deal was driven by Glenn Fogel, then Priceline's head of corporate development, who had spotted both companies running an agency model — hotels paid a commission only when a guest stayed, rather than the platform buying rooms wholesale — that the big US players had dismissed as out of favour ([Skift](https://skift.com/2014/11/19/interview-pricelines-ma-guru-on-the-most-successful-acquisition-in-online-travel-history/)). Skift later called the two acquisitions the foundation of the Booking.com juggernaut and "the most successful acquisitions in online travel history" ([Skift](https://skift.com/2014/11/19/interview-pricelines-ma-guru-on-the-most-successful-acquisition-in-online-travel-history/)). The parent company is now Booking Holdings, and Booking.com is its flagship brand ([Booking Holdings](https://www.bookingholdings.com/about/factsheet/)). Bruinsma himself returned in 2011, applying by email like anyone else, and worked three days a week as a developer until 2016 — arriving to find 2,300 employees where 60 had worked when he left ([University of Twente](https://www.utwente.nl/en/alumni/inspiring-alumni/ut-canon/stories/geert-jan-bruinsma/)).

Leadership changed twice in the decade that followed. Darren Huston ran both Booking.com and the parent until he left over allegations of misconduct with an employee; Gillian Tans, who had joined Booking.com in 2002 as one of its first employees, succeeded him as CEO in 2016 ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/Booking-Holdings-Booking-leadership-change?ctxp=)). In June 2019 Tans stepped aside to become chairwoman and Fogel — by then president and CEO of Booking Holdings — took on the Booking.com CEO role as well, saying he would drive more collaboration and integration across the group's brands ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/Booking-Holdings-Booking-leadership-change?ctxp=)).

## The business: scale, commission, and the Connected Trip

Booking.com earns its money as an intermediary: hotels and other property owners pay it a commission for each reservation made through the platform, while travellers pay nothing to the platform itself ([Court of Justice of the European Union](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2024-09/cp240145en.pdf)). The original agency model — the guest pays the property, and Booking.com invoices its commission afterwards — is now giving way to a merchant model in which Booking.com takes payment at booking; merchant gross bookings were 70% of the group's total in 2025, up from about 63% in 2024 ([Booking Holdings](https://s201.q4cdn.com/865305287/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/Investor-Presentation-February-2026-_v-Final.pdf)). The scale of that take is visible in the group accounts: $26.9 billion of revenue on $186.1 billion of gross bookings in 2025 works out to about 14.5 cents kept from every dollar booked — a figure calculated from the two reported numbers, not a rate the company publishes ([Booking Holdings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553126000008/q4-25bkngearningsrelease.htm)).

Two trends inside the numbers matter. First, alternative accommodation — homes and apartments — was about 36% of Booking.com room nights in 2025 and grew about 10%, putting the platform in direct competition with short-term-rental rivals ([Booking Holdings](https://s201.q4cdn.com/865305287/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/Investor-Presentation-February-2026-_v-Final.pdf)). Second, the group is deliberately selling more than rooms: its "Connected Trip" strategy bundles stays with flights, ground transport, attractions, dining and payments, with AI at the centre. In 2025 it booked 68 million flight tickets and 88 million rental car days, attraction-ticket bookings grew nearly 80%, and connected transactions — trips combining more than one element — grew over 20% ([Booking Holdings](https://s201.q4cdn.com/865305287/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/Investor-Presentation-February-2026-_v-Final.pdf)).

Generative AI is the newest layer of that strategy. On June 28, 2023 Booking.com launched its AI Trip Planner in beta to select American members of its Genius loyalty programme, combining its own machine-learning models with OpenAI's ChatGPT so travellers could describe a trip in plain words and get destination suggestions, accommodation options and daily itineraries back ([Business Traveller](https://www.businesstraveller.com/news/bookingcom-launches-chatgpt-trip-planner/)). Fogel described generative AI as accelerating years of machine-learning work, and envisaged a system that could "instantly fix" a disrupted trip — rebooking a transfer and alerting the hotel when a flight is delayed, without the traveller lifting a finger ([Business Traveller](https://www.businesstraveller.com/news/bookingcom-launches-chatgpt-trip-planner/)).

## Collapse and recovery: COVID-19

The pandemic was the sharpest test the business has faced. In 2020, as the [COVID-19 pandemic](/events/covid-19-pandemic/) closed borders and grounded planes, Booking Holdings' gross travel bookings fell 63% to $35.4 billion and total revenue fell 55% to $6.8 billion; net income collapsed 99% to $59 million and adjusted EBITDA fell 85% to $879 million, with room nights down 58% ([Booking Holdings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553121000016/ex991123120.htm)). The fourth quarter alone saw gross bookings down 65% and room nights down 60% year over year ([Booking Holdings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553121000016/ex991123120.htm)). The recovery was complete well before 2025, when the group reported 1,235 million room nights and $186.1 billion of gross bookings — far past its pandemic-year trough of $35.4 billion in 2020 ([Booking Holdings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553126000008/q4-25bkngearningsrelease.htm); [Booking Holdings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553121000016/ex991123120.htm)). The episode is also why the profile's scale figures are dated: a travel business this cyclical can only be measured honestly with a date attached.

## Europe's most-regulated travel platform

The same market power that made Booking.com valuable made it a regulatory target, and the cases now form a single arc. At its centre are price-parity clauses — contract terms that stopped hotels from offering a lower price elsewhere than on Booking.com. The wide version, which barred lower prices on any channel, applied until 2015; since then only the narrow version, barring lower prices on the hotel's own website, has applied ([Court of Justice of the European Union](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2024-09/cp240145en.pdf)). On September 19, 2024 the Court of Justice of the EU ruled in Case C-264/23 that these clauses cannot, in principle, be classified as "ancillary restraints" under EU competition law: it had not been established that they are objectively necessary for the platform to operate, or proportionate to that aim — even though the Court also said the platform service itself had a neutral or even positive effect on competition ([Court of Justice of the European Union](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2024-09/cp240145en.pdf)). The ruling did not declare the clauses illegal outright; it sent their legality back for case-by-case assessment under Article 101 of the EU treaty, a precision some coverage has lost ([Court of Justice of the European Union](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2024-09/cp240145en.pdf)).

Eight months earlier, on May 13, 2024, the European Commission had designated Booking a gatekeeper under the Digital Markets Act for its Booking.com intermediation service, after Booking's own self-assessment that it met the thresholds ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-designates-booking-gatekeeper-and-opens-market-investigation-x)). Gatekeeper status brought a six-month deadline to comply, including a ban on parity clauses, with fines of up to 10% of worldwide turnover for infringement ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/30/spains-antitrust-watchdog-fines-booking-com-nearly-450m-for-unfair-terms-and-restricting-rivals/)).

Spain moved first and hardest. On July 30, 2024 the CNMC fined Booking.com €413.24 million — two fines of €206.62 million, the largest the authority had ever imposed — for two abuses of a dominant position running from at least January 2019, after complaints from two Spanish hotel associations in 2021 ([CNMC](https://www.cnmc.es/sites/default/files/5555287.pdf); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/30/spains-antitrust-watchdog-fines-booking-com-nearly-450m-for-unfair-terms-and-restricting-rivals/)). The CNMC found Booking.com held a 70-90% share of Spain's online hotel intermediation market in 2019-2022, and condemned both the narrow parity clause — imposed on hotels while Booking.com reserved the right to undercut their prices itself — and the use of a hotel's total Booking.com reservations as a criterion in its default search ranking, which pushed hotels to concentrate their sales on the platform ([CNMC](https://www.cnmc.es/sites/default/files/5555287.pdf)). Booking.com said it strongly disagreed and would appeal, arguing the Digital Markets Act was the right forum for issues that apply across Europe ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/30/spains-antitrust-watchdog-fines-booking-com-nearly-450m-for-unfair-terms-and-restricting-rivals/)). It did appeal, and in February 2025 the Spanish National Court suspended the decision, including payment of the fine, pending the outcome; the company still recorded a liability of $485 million at December 31, 2025 ([Booking Holdings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553126000037/R21.htm)).

The hotels then came for damages. At the end of January 2026 the Amsterdam non-profit Stichting Hotel Claims Alliance launched collective proceedings over the parity clauses, initially for just over 3,000 hotels; by August 2026 the number had reached 10,783, with registration open until September 11, 2026 and an expected total of up to 18,000 ([Travel Weekly](https://travelweekly.co.uk/news/more-than-10000-european-hotels-seek-billions-in-claims-against-bookingcom)). The claimants seek a significant portion of the commissions they paid between 2004 and 2024, plus interest — potentially billions of euros — and the case will be heard by the Amsterdam District Court ([Travel Weekly](https://travelweekly.co.uk/news/more-than-10000-european-hotels-seek-billions-in-claims-against-bookingcom)). Booking.com disputes the claim, arguing the ECJ ruling "does not open the door to damage claims" and noting that the hotels represent only about 0.4% of its 2.3 million European accommodation partners ([Travel Weekly](https://travelweekly.co.uk/news/more-than-10000-european-hotels-seek-billions-in-claims-against-bookingcom)). The action is backed by the European hotel association HOTREC and more than 30 national hotel associations, and HOTREC estimated Booking.com's share of Europe's online travel agency hotel bookings in 2024 at 70% ([Travel Weekly](https://travelweekly.co.uk/news/more-than-10000-european-hotels-seek-billions-in-claims-against-bookingcom)).

The pressure has not let up. In 2025 Switzerland's Price Surveillance Office ordered Booking.com to cut its average commission level for Swiss hotels — a decision Booking.com has appealed and which is suspended — while Greece opened a formal competition investigation in June 2025 and Hungary opened one into potentially misleading practices in August 2025 ([Booking Holdings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553126000037/R21.htm)). German hotels' parity claims are ongoing, two Dutch foundations have filed claims on behalf of European hotels and consumers, and in July 2026 the US Federal Trade Commission's staff told Priceline it intended to recommend a complaint over allegedly unfair or deceptive practices around disclosures, fees, customer support and billing ([Booking Holdings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553126000037/R21.htm)). Two further matters from the same disclosure show the company's other fronts: a Dutch court ruled in January 2024, confirmed by the Supreme Court in March 2025, that Booking.com must join the travel industry's mandatory pension scheme with retroactive effect to 1999; and in April 2026 Booking.com told data protection authorities that unauthorised third parties had accessed certain guests' booking information ([Booking Holdings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553126000037/R21.htm)). None of these is resolved, and the profile asserts no outcome for any of them.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Booking.com?** It is a Dutch online travel agency, headquartered in Amsterdam, that lets travellers compare and book accommodation — hotels, homes and apartments — plus flights, rental cars and attractions, and charges the property a commission on each booking ([Court of Justice of the European Union](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2024-09/cp240145en.pdf); [Booking Holdings](https://www.bookingholdings.com/about/factsheet/)).

**Who owns Booking.com?** Booking Holdings, the US-listed parent (NASDAQ: BKNG), which bought it as Bookings B.V. in July 2005 for $133 million and also owns Priceline, Agoda, KAYAK and OpenTable ([Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/Travel-News/Online-Travel/Priceline-buys-European-res-firm); [Booking Holdings](https://www.bookingholdings.com/about/factsheet/)).

**How big is Booking.com?** At December 31, 2025 it listed about 4.4 million properties and about 32 million reported listings in over 220 countries and territories; its parent booked 1,235 million room nights and $186.1 billion of gross bookings in 2025 ([Booking Holdings](https://s201.q4cdn.com/865305287/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/Investor-Presentation-February-2026-_v-Final.pdf); [Booking Holdings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553126000008/q4-25bkngearningsrelease.htm)).

**How does Booking.com make money?** It takes a commission from accommodation providers on each reservation, and increasingly takes the traveller's payment itself under its merchant model, which was 70% of group gross bookings in 2025; group revenue of $26.9 billion on $186.1 billion booked is about 14.5% ([Court of Justice of the European Union](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2024-09/cp240145en.pdf); [Booking Holdings](https://s201.q4cdn.com/865305287/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/Investor-Presentation-February-2026-_v-Final.pdf)).

**Why is Booking.com facing so many lawsuits in Europe?** Hotels and regulators say its price-parity clauses and dominant position — 70-90% of Spain's online hotel intermediation market, per the CNMC — stopped hotels undercutting it and inflated commissions. The EU Court ruled in 2024 that the clauses are not ancillary restraints, Spain fined it €413.24 million (suspended on appeal), and over 10,000 hotels are now claiming damages for 2004-2024 ([Court of Justice of the European Union](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2024-09/cp240145en.pdf); [CNMC](https://www.cnmc.es/sites/default/files/5555287.pdf); [Travel Weekly](https://travelweekly.co.uk/news/more-than-10000-european-hotels-seek-billions-in-claims-against-bookingcom)).
