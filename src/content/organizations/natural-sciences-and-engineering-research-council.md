## Overview

The Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada (NSERC) is the federal agency that funds natural sciences and engineering research in [Canada](/places/canada/), supporting university researchers, students and companies through grants, scholarships and partnerships. Created on 1 May 1978 under the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council Act, it is one of Canada's three federal granting councils, alongside the [Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council](/organizations/social-sciences-and-humanities-research-council/) (SSHRC) for the social sciences and humanities and the Canadian Institutes of Health Research (CIHR) for health research ([Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/plans-priorities-and-performance/2024-25-departmental-results-report/2024-25-departmental-results)).

NSERC is not a laboratory and does not perform research itself: it distributes federal money, mostly through peer-reviewed competition, to researchers at universities and colleges and to their partners in industry, government and the not-for-profit sector. In 2024–25 it spent $1,468,578,249 and supported over 12,000 researchers, with 7,087 trainees holding its scholarships and fellowships ([Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/plans-priorities-and-performance/2024-25-departmental-results-report/2024-25-departmental-results)). It describes itself as the main natural sciences and engineering research funder in Canada ([Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada](https://www.nserc-crsng.gc.ca/NSERC-CRSNG/NSERC2030-CRSNG2030/Index_eng.asp)).

## From the National Research Council to NSERC

NSERC's roots reach back to 1916, when the National Research Council of Canada (NRC) was created and began supporting university research through studentships, fellowships and "grants for assisted research" — the direct ancestors of today's NSERC scholarships and Discovery Grants ([Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/chronicle-past-events)). The split came from a conflict-of-interest worry: a single agency both running national laboratories and handing out university grants. The Senate Special Committee on Science Policy, chaired by Senator Maurice Lamontagne, examined the system in 1968 and recommended separation in its 1973 final report, a recommendation that led to Bill C-26 ([Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/chronicle-past-events)).

Bill C-26 came into force on 1 May 1978, and NSERC was born with Gordon MacNabb as its first president, taking over the university grants program the NRC had run ([Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/history)). Its first five-year plan backed free, investigator-driven research alongside major national initiatives such as TRIUMF and the Canada-France-Hawaii Telescope. Within two years it launched the Undergraduate Student Research Awards, University Research Fellowships and Industrial Research Fellowships; early holders included V-Chip inventor Tim Collings and [University of Toronto](/universities/university-of-toronto/) zoologist Ian Orchard, while Francesco Bellini held an early industrial fellowship at Ayerst Laboratories before co-founding BioChem Pharma ([Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/chronicle-past-events)).

## What NSERC funds — and how it differs from SSHRC and CIHR

NSERC funds only the natural sciences and engineering — physics, chemistry, biology, mathematics, computing and all branches of engineering — and not the health sciences, which fall to CIHR, nor the social sciences and humanities, which fall to SSHRC. The three councils work as a system: they harmonised their research-security policy, their talent programs and a shared grants-management system, and together administer the College and Community Innovation Program for applied research at colleges ([Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/plans-priorities-and-performance/2024-25-departmental-results-report/2024-25-departmental-results)). Its program inventory has three streams — Discovery Research, Research Partnerships, and Research Training and Talent Development — with Discovery Grants, the long-term grants for ongoing faculty research programs, as the largest single investment ([Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/news/powering-canadas-innovation-engine-nserc-makes-record-breaking-660-million-investment)).

Partnerships are the second pillar. Alliance grants link university researchers with companies, governments and not-for-profits, and the College and Community Innovation Program carries that model into colleges, polytechnics and CEGEPs — a route [Colleges and Institutes Canada](/organizations/colleges-and-institutes-canada/) spent decades opening, lifting NSERC-eligible colleges from 13 in 2005–06 to 108 in 2014–15. In 2024–25 NSERC supported more than 5,400 such partnerships, and its Lab to Market program backed four new national commercialisation networks with $95 million over five years and nearly 250 participating organisations ([Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/plans-priorities-and-performance/2024-25-departmental-results-report/2024-25-departmental-results)).

## 2024–25 in numbers: a record year for discovery

The 2024–25 fiscal year was NSERC's biggest in recent memory, driven by Budget 2024 money that raised core research grants and scholarship values. Total actual spending reached $1,468,578,249, up from $1,383,259,381 the year before, and planned spending rises further to $1,506,621,011 in 2025–26 ([Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/plans-priorities-and-performance/2024-25-departmental-results-report/2024-25-departmental-results)). The headline came in July 2025: a record $660.3 million in new Discovery Research grants and extensions, including $503.7 million in individual Discovery Grants to 2,138 researchers and $70.6 million in one-year extensions for 1,831 existing grant holders ([Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/news/powering-canadas-innovation-engine-nserc-makes-record-breaking-660-million-investment)).

The results tables show a system that mostly hit its targets: 7,087 trainees supported against a target of 6,800, $557 million in trainee funding against a $420 million target, and 2,858 project partners against a 2,500 target. Two measures missed: 45% of funded research involved international collaboration against a 47% target, and Canada ranked 17th among OECD nations on citation scores against a target of at most 18th — technically a pass on the rank, but down from 16th the year before ([Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/plans-priorities-and-performance/2024-25-departmental-results-report/2024-25-departmental-results)). On inclusion, 39.3% of newly funded recipients self-identified as women, 33.4% as visible minorities, 3.0% as Indigenous peoples and 7.9% as persons with disabilities — all above target.

NSERC reported $408 million in non-academic partner funding for its research projects in 2024–25, against a target of $280 million ([Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada](https://nserc-crsng.canada.ca/en/plans-priorities-and-performance/2024-25-departmental-results-report/2024-25-departmental-results)) — and that leverage is the point of the partnership model: companies, governments and not-for-profits put substantial money alongside federal grants, so public funding pulls private and public-sector funding into university research rather than replacing it. The agency's direction is set by NSERC 2030, its 2022 strategic plan, which commits it to investigator-driven discovery, commercialisation, global partnerships and deeper engagement with Indigenous communities, organisations, researchers and trainees ([Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada](https://www.nserc-crsng.gc.ca/NSERC-CRSNG/NSERC2030-CRSNG2030/Index_eng.asp)).

## FAQ

### What is NSERC?

NSERC is Canada's federal funding agency for natural sciences and engineering research. Created in 1978, it awards grants, scholarships and fellowships to university researchers, students and postdoctoral fellows, and funds partnerships with industry, government and not-for-profits. In 2024–25 it spent $1.47 billion and supported over 12,000 researchers.

### How is NSERC different from SSHRC and CIHR?

The three are Canada's federal granting councils, divided by field: NSERC funds the natural sciences and engineering, SSHRC funds the social sciences and humanities, and CIHR funds health research. They cooperate closely, harmonising talent programs, research-security policy and grants management, but each runs its own competitions.

### What is an NSERC Discovery Grant?

A Discovery Grant is NSERC's core award: long-term operating funds for a university faculty member's ongoing research program in the natural sciences or engineering. It is NSERC's largest area of investment — $503.7 million went to 2,138 researchers in the record 2025 competition, part of a $660.3 million Discovery Research total.

### Who runs NSERC?

The president is Prof. Alejandro Adem, and the responsible minister is Mélanie Joly, Minister of Industry. NSERC answers to Parliament through the minister, and its governing council comprises a full-time president and up to 21 members drawn from the private and public sectors and universities, appointed for terms of up to three years.

### Does NSERC fund colleges as well as universities?

Yes. Through the College and Community Innovation Program, run with SSHRC and CIHR, NSERC funds applied research at colleges, polytechnics and CEGEPs. Colleges were long excluded from federal research funding; a 2002–03 pilot first let them apply directly, and NSERC-eligible colleges grew from 13 in 2005–06 to 108 in 2014–15.
