## Overview

Tala is a mobile lending company that extends small loans to people in emerging markets who lack access to traditional banks or credit bureaus, underwriting them instead on data from their smartphones ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/4031208/micro-loan-startup-tala-raises-30-million-in-series-b-funding/)). Founded in 2011 by Shivani Siroya and headquartered in Santa Monica, California, the company has grown to more than 10 million customers globally and originated over $5 billion in credit ([Reuters](https://theprint.in/tech/microlender-tala-lands-150-million-debt-facility-to-expand-mexico-business/2554830/?amp); [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/alexa-von-tobel/how-founder-of-tala-financial-platform-finds-customers.html)). Its core markets are Kenya, the Philippines, Mexico and India, with Mexico emerging as its largest growth engine ([Reuters](https://theprint.in/tech/microlender-tala-lands-150-million-debt-facility-to-expand-mexico-business/2554830/?amp)).

## Underwriting the unbanked with phone data

Tala's founding insight came from Siroya's time at the U.N. Population Fund, where she conducted more than 3,500 interviews with micro-entrepreneurs and saw firsthand how traditional financial institutions overlooked their purchasing power — which led her to found Tala in 2011 ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/alexa-von-tobel/how-founder-of-tala-financial-platform-finds-customers.html)). Instead of credit-bureau files, which most of its customers do not have, Tala's Android app analyzes smartphone data to build an alternative credit score and disburses loans to a mobile wallet, often within minutes ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/4031208/micro-loan-startup-tala-raises-30-million-in-series-b-funding/)).

The loans are small by design: $10 to $500, averaging around $50, at about 11% interest, with a 30-day repayment rate above 90% and roughly one-third of borrowers women, as reported when the company raised its Series B ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/4031208/micro-loan-startup-tala-raises-30-million-in-series-b-funding/)). The model treats repayment behavior itself as the credential that unlocks larger subsequent loans — a self-reinforcing credit history for people the formal system never scored.

## Funding arc: from micro-loans to a financial platform

Tala's capital raises trace its evolution from lender to platform. The $30 million Series B in 2017 funded expansion across South Asia and Latin America ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/4031208/micro-loan-startup-tala-raises-30-million-in-series-b-funding/)). In October 2021, a $145 million Series E led by Upstart — with the Stellar Development Foundation, Kindred Ventures, the J. Safra Group and existing investors including PayPal Ventures participating — took total equity funding to $350 million and set an agenda beyond credit: savings, money management and a crypto product on the Stellar network aimed at emerging markets ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/money-and-banking/fintech-firm-tala-raises-145-m-in-series-e-round/article37001180.ece/amp/)). Upstart co-founder Paul Gu and Stellar CEO Denelle Dixon joined Tala's board in the round ([The Hindu BusinessLine](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/money-and-banking/fintech-firm-tala-raises-145-m-in-series-e-round/article37001180.ece/amp/)).

## Mexico as the growth engine

The newest chapter is Mexico. In March 2025, Tala secured a $150 million debt facility — starting at $75 million and drawable to $150 million — from funds managed by Neuberger Berman, earmarked for its Mexico business ([Reuters](https://theprint.in/tech/microlender-tala-lands-150-million-debt-facility-to-expand-mexico-business/2554830/?amp)). Mexico is Tala's only Latin American market so far, but it already counts more than 3 million customers there, disbursed over $500 million in loans in the country in 2024, and averages loans of about 2,300 pesos ($115) — many borrowers being small-business owners ([Reuters](https://theprint.in/tech/microlender-tala-lands-150-million-debt-facility-to-expand-mexico-business/2554830/?amp)). Siroya told [Reuters](/organizations/reuters/) the capital would fund higher credit limits, more dynamic pricing and new credit products, with further Latin American expansion to come ([Reuters](https://theprint.in/tech/microlender-tala-lands-150-million-debt-facility-to-expand-mexico-business/2554830/?amp)).
