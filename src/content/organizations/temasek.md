## Overview

Temasek Holdings is Singapore's state-owned global investment company, wholly owned by the Singapore Minister for Finance, with a net portfolio value of S$518 billion (US$401 billion) at 31 March 2026. Founded in 1974 to hold the government's commercial assets, it now invests worldwide through Singapore portfolio companies, global direct investments and fund partnerships, and in 2026 split its investing into three entities while targeting AI exposure of up to 15% by 2031.

Temasek is headquartered in Singapore, and it is often called a sovereign wealth fund — but that label fits it loosely. Unlike a conventional sovereign fund, Temasek owns the assets on its balance sheet rather than managing reserves on behalf of the state ([Temasek](https://tr24.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Governance-and-Leadership.pdf)). Its sole shareholder is the Minister for Finance, yet neither the minister nor the government directs its investment decisions ([Temasek](https://tr24.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Governance-and-Leadership.pdf)). The record portfolio was reported in July 2026, the second consecutive year it hit a record, with a one-year total shareholder return of 10.5% in Singapore dollars, or 14.8% in US dollars ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/temasek-targets-big-jump-ai-investments-value-portfolio-hits-record-high-2026-07-08/)).

The portfolio is built from three segments, each with its own role and return record:

| Segment | Share of portfolio | 10-year IRR |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Singapore-based Temasek Portfolio Companies (TPCs) | 43% | 8.1% |
| Global Direct Investments (GDIs) | 38% | 7.6% |
| Partnerships, Funds and Asset Management Companies (PFAs) | 19% | 7.7% |

The split — roughly 40-40-20 — has largely held since 2018 ([Temasek](https://www.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Temasek-Review-2026-Media-Release.pdf)). Over the year to 31 March 2026 Temasek invested S$51 billion and divested S$31 billion, a net investment of S$20 billion, and its long-term returns stayed resilient: 20-year total shareholder return of 6.8% and 10-year of 7.1% ([Temasek](https://www.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Temasek-Review-2026-Media-Release.pdf)).

## From a S$354 million inheritance to a global investor

Temasek was incorporated on 25 June 1974, when Singapore's Ministry of Finance transferred it 35 companies and investments worth S$354 million, and J. Y. Pillay became its first chairman that August ([Temasek](https://storyof.temasek/downloads/ByGenerations_ForGenerations.pdf)). The name was chosen from four candidates — Temasek, Colossal, Merlion and Solar — and recalls the old name for Singapore, thought to come from the Malay word for a body of water ([Temasek](https://storyof.temasek/downloads/ByGenerations_ForGenerations.pdf)). The point was to free the ministry to make policy while a commercial company ran the assets: the government had built businesses to create jobs, from a bird park and a shoemaker to naval yards, a start-up airline and an iron and steel mill. The early portfolio held the names that built Singapore's economy — Singapore Airlines, Keppel, Sembawang and DBS — with Singtel, PSA and Singapore Power added as newly corporatised entities in the 1990s ([Temasek](https://storyof.temasek/downloads/ByGenerations_ForGenerations.pdf)).

## What Temasek is — and what it is not

Temasek is a company incorporated under the Singapore Companies Act, wholly owned by the Singapore Minister for Finance, and designated a Fifth Schedule entity under Singapore's Constitution, with a constitutional duty to safeguard the company's past reserves ([Temasek](https://tr24.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Governance-and-Leadership.pdf)). The board and chief executive must seek the President's approval before any draw on those reserves, and every disposal must be at fair market value ([Temasek](https://tr24.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Governance-and-Leadership.pdf)). At the same time, Temasek says it is "not state-directed": neither the President nor the government directs its investment decisions, except for protecting past reserves, and there are no government nominees on its board ([Temasek](https://tr24.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Governance-and-Leadership.pdf)). It owns its assets and is not a fund manager — it does not manage Central Provident Fund savings, government assets or foreign-exchange reserves ([Temasek](https://tr24.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Governance-and-Leadership.pdf)). That job belongs to [GIC](/organizations/gic/), set up in 1981 to manage Singapore's foreign reserves, which does not invest in Singapore, while Temasek invests in or out of the country ([Temasek](https://storyof.temasek/downloads/ByGenerations_ForGenerations.pdf)).

## The 2026 restructure: three entities, one portfolio

From 1 April 2026 Temasek has operated through three new wholly-owned entities — Temasek Singapore (TSG), Temasek Global Investments (TGI) and Temasek Partnership Solutions (TPS) — with Temasek International kept as the institutional enabler, and the group continuing to operate collectively as "OneTemasek" ([Temasek](https://www.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Temasek-Review-2026-Media-Release.pdf)). The aim is sharper focus: each entity pursues a differentiated strategy and is accountable for distinct outcomes ([Temasek](https://www.temasek.com.sg/en/news-and-resources/news-room/speeches/2026/temasek-review-2026-media-briefing)). TSG builds globally competitive, future-ready Singapore portfolio companies; TGI builds a global portfolio with a higher risk-return profile; TPS builds an alternatives portfolio through partnerships with top-tier general partners ([Temasek](https://www.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Temasek-Review-2026-Media-Release.pdf)). The Singapore companies remain the ballast: together they generate about S$200 billion in revenue and employ over 400,000 people globally ([Temasek](https://www.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Temasek-Review-2026-Media-Release.pdf)). Chief executive Dilhan Pillay has begun revealing each unit's returns — openness Reuters Breakingviews called rare for sovereign investors, while noting the 10-year figures "reflect old scars more than recent successes" ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/commentary/breakingviews/temasek-performance-transparency-gets-lost-fog-2026-07-09/)).

## Global direct investments: the Blend bet and the AI turn

Temasek's global direct investments take minority stakes in emerging and established market leaders, and the [Blend](/organizations/blend/) Series E is a clean example. On 24 June 2019 Blend raised $130 million in Series E funding led by Temasek and [General Atlantic](/organizations/general-atlantic/), with [8VC](/organizations/8vc/), [Founders Fund](/organizations/founders-fund/), [Greylock Partners](/organizations/greylock/) and [Lightspeed Venture Partners](/organizations/lightspeed-venture-partners/) participating, bringing the digital lender's total capital to $310 million ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/blend-raises-130-million-in-series-e/)). Blend was then processing almost $2 billion in US mortgages a day for more than 150 lender clients ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/blend-raises-130-million-in-series-e/)). The same logic now applies to artificial intelligence: in the year to 31 March 2026 Temasek's global investments included [Anthropic](/organizations/anthropic/) and [OpenAI](/organizations/openai/) in the [United States](/places/united-states/), ANE and Luckin Coffee in [China](/places/china/), and Ermenegildo Zegna in Europe, while it exited Schneider Electric [India](/places/india/) ([Temasek](https://www.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Temasek-Review-2026-Media-Release.pdf)). AI exposure is about 6% of the portfolio, targeted at up to 15% by the year ending 31 March 2031, across energy and data centres, semiconductors, cloud providers, foundation models and AI applications ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/temasek-targets-big-jump-ai-investments-value-portfolio-hits-record-high-2026-07-08/)).

## The FTX write-down

In November 2022 Temasek wrote down its entire $275 million investment in the collapsed crypto exchange [FTX](/organizations/ftx/), "irrespective of the outcome of FTX's bankruptcy protection filing" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/singapores-temasek-writes-down-275m-investment-in-ftx/)). It had invested $210 million for about 1% of FTX International and $65 million for about 1.5% of FTX US, across two rounds from October 2021 to January 2022 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/singapores-temasek-writes-down-275m-investment-in-ftx/)). The cost was 0.09% of its S$403 billion net portfolio value at 31 March 2022 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/singapores-temasek-writes-down-275m-investment-in-ftx/)). Temasek said its due diligence had taken about eight months and reviewed audited financials showing FTX profitable, but conceded its belief in Sam Bankman-Fried's "actions, judgement and leadership... would appear to have been misplaced" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/singapores-temasek-writes-down-275m-investment-in-ftx/)). It stressed the investment was in a digital-asset exchange, not in cryptocurrencies, and that it had no direct crypto exposure ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/singapores-temasek-writes-down-275m-investment-in-ftx/)). [SoftBank](/organizations/softbank-group/) also wrote down its $100 million FTX investment ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/singapores-temasek-writes-down-275m-investment-in-ftx/)).

## A new chairman, and a missed climate target

Teo Chee Hean joined Temasek's board as deputy chairman on 1 July 2025 and became its fifth chairman on 9 October 2025, succeeding Lim Boon Heng after 12 years in the role ([Temasek](https://www.temasek.com.sg/en/news-and-resources/news-room/news/2025/chairman-succession-and-board-changes)). Under Lim, net portfolio value grew from S$223 billion in March 2014 to S$389 billion in March 2024, and the firm developed its T2030 strategy and refreshed its Charter in 2024 ([Temasek](https://www.temasek.com.sg/en/news-and-resources/news-room/news/2025/chairman-succession-and-board-changes)). Teo, a former deputy prime minister and coordinating minister for national security, arrived as Temasek conceded a climate miss: it said it was "unlikely to meet" its interim 2030 target of halving net portfolio emissions from 2010 levels, while holding to its 2050 net-zero ambition ([Temasek](https://www.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Temasek-Review-2026-Media-Release.pdf)). Total portfolio emissions stayed at 21 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent, but carbon intensity fell to 50 tonnes per S$ million of portfolio value, from 57 the year before ([Temasek](https://www.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Temasek-Review-2026-Media-Release.pdf)). The firm still invested S$5 billion in Sustainable Living-aligned opportunities, taking that part of the portfolio to S$49 billion ([Temasek](https://www.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Temasek-Review-2026-Media-Release.pdf)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Temasek?** Temasek is Singapore's state-owned global investment company, wholly owned by the Singapore Minister for Finance, with a net portfolio value of S$518 billion at 31 March 2026 ([Temasek](https://www.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Temasek-Review-2026-Media-Release.pdf)). It owns its assets rather than managing government reserves, invests at home and abroad, and is run commercially without government direction of its investments ([Temasek](https://tr24.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Governance-and-Leadership.pdf)).

**How is Temasek different from GIC?** GIC, set up in 1981, manages Singapore's foreign reserves and does not invest in Singapore, while Temasek is an investment company that owns its assets and invests in or out of the country ([Temasek](https://storyof.temasek/downloads/ByGenerations_ForGenerations.pdf)). Temasek reports the value of what it owns — its net portfolio value — in its annual Temasek Review ([Temasek](https://www.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Temasek-Review-2026-Media-Release.pdf)).

**What did Temasek invest in during its latest year?** In the year to 31 March 2026 it invested S$51 billion and divested S$31 billion, including stakes in Anthropic and OpenAI, and sold the remaining 82% of ST Telemedia Global Data Centres to KKR and Singtel for S$6.6 billion ([Temasek](https://www.temasekreview.com.sg/downloads/Temasek-Review-2026-Media-Release.pdf)). Its AI exposure is about 6% of the portfolio, targeted at up to 15% by 2031 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/temasek-targets-big-jump-ai-investments-value-portfolio-hits-record-high-2026-07-08/)).

**Why did Temasek write down its FTX investment?** FTX collapsed in November 2022, and Temasek wrote down the full $275 million it had invested across two rounds in 2021–2022, saying its belief in Sam Bankman-Fried's leadership "would appear to have been misplaced" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/singapores-temasek-writes-down-275m-investment-in-ftx/)). The loss was 0.09% of its net portfolio value, and Temasek said it had no direct exposure to cryptocurrencies ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/singapores-temasek-writes-down-275m-investment-in-ftx/)).
