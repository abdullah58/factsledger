## Overview

**Ain't It Cool News (AICN)** is Harry Knowles' 1996 Austin film-news site — named for a John Travolta line in *Broken Arrow* — that pioneered spoiler-era scoop journalism through a "spy" network publishing scripts and embargo-busting test-screening "reviews" ([Film Comment](https://www.filmcomment.com/article/rnge-wrs-film-criticism-on-the-internet/)). The *Batman & Robin* leaks made it famous; by 2000 Knowles was the media's "go-to guy" for online film culture ([Film Comment](https://www.filmcomment.com/article/rnge-wrs-film-criticism-on-the-internet/)). He stepped away in 2017 amid sexual-assault allegations, which he denied, handing the site to his sister ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/harry-knowles-step-away-aint-cool-news-sexual-assault-claims/)).

## The bedroom site that invented geek scoops

AICN's method was new: Knowles, laid up after a mid-1990s injury, turned a movie obsession into a site that "sidestepped press wranglers and studio publicists to get inside scoops directly from people in the know — folks on sets who leaked details, members of test audiences who saw early cuts" ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/harry-knowles-aint-it-cool-rise-and-fall/)). Film Comment's 2000 account captured the paradox — a 28-year-old Austin showman who had made himself "the go-to guy for the mainstream media whenever they need someone to represent the online film community," appearing across the aisle on Ebert's show, while his test-screening "reviews" made him "a problem to be dealt with and neutralized" by studios whose carefully staged releases he kept spoiling ([Film Comment](https://www.filmcomment.com/article/rnge-wrs-film-criticism-on-the-internet/)). The same account documents the era's ethics criticism — a Film Threat exposé alleging recycled tips, junket perks, and bootleg-tape leaks — which Knowles refuted ([Film Comment](https://www.filmcomment.com/article/rnge-wrs-film-criticism-on-the-internet/)). The model outlasted its founder: AICN's UK desk was still breaking news in 2011, when "Britgeek" reported the BBFC's refusal to classify [The Bunny Game](/creative-works/the-bunny-game/) and the contrasting *Human Centipede 2* 18 certificate ([Ain't It Cool News](http://legacy.aintitcool.com/node/51563)).

## Peak, decline, and the 2017 reckoning

The turn came early: by 2002, Guardian writer John Patterson was arguing that studios had "opened the door, invited young Knowles to step behind the curtain and slowly suckered away his objectivity and credibility," while rivals — JoBlo, CHUD, Dark Horizons, Slashfilm — ate the site's lunch and updates grew late ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/harry-knowles-aint-it-cool-rise-and-fall/)). The money followed the attention out: gross ad revenue of about $700,000 a year in the early-to-mid-2000s had fallen to the low six figures by 2012, and Knowles told The Hollywood Reporter in 2013 the site owed $300,000 in back taxes ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/harry-knowles-aint-it-cool-rise-and-fall/)) — which implies the site's commercial collapse preceded its cultural one by half a decade. In September 2017, IndieWire reported Jasmine Baker's account of two 1999–2000 assaults at Alamo Drafthouse events; Knowles called the claims "100 percent untrue," but more women came forward, longtime writers Eric "Quint" Vespe and Steve "Capone" Prokopy resigned, and Knowles stepped away to "therapy, detox and getting to a better place," handing the site to his sister ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/harry-knowles-step-away-aint-cool-news-sexual-assault-claims/); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/harry-knowles-aint-it-cool-rise-and-fall/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who founded Ain't It Cool News?**
Harry Knowles founded the site in Austin, Texas, in 1996, naming it after a catchphrase from John Travolta's character in the film Broken Arrow. He built it while recovering from a mid-1990s injury, using a network of anonymous industry "spies" to publish casting news, scripts, and early reviews that studios tried to keep under wraps.

**Why was Ain't It Cool News influential?**
It invented a genre: spoiler-era scoop journalism. Its 1997 Batman & Robin preview-screening leaks made it nationally famous and taught studios to fear the internet. At its 2000 peak, Knowles was mainstream media's go-to representative of online film culture, appearing as a guest reviewer on Roger Ebert's show while rivals like JoBlo and Slashfilm copied the model.

**What happened to Harry Knowles in 2017?**
In September 2017, IndieWire reported Jasmine Baker's account that Knowles had assaulted her on two occasions at Alamo Drafthouse events in 1999 and 2000. Knowles categorically denied the claims, but additional women came forward, longtime writers Eric Vespe and Steve Prokopy resigned, and Knowles stepped away from the site, handing operations to his sister.

**What did Ain't It Cool News publish about The Bunny Game?**
On October 12, 2011, the site's UK desk ("Britgeek") reported the BBFC's refusal to classify The Bunny Game — making it illegal to distribute in the UK — and contrasted it with The Human Centipede II (Full Sequence), which had just been passed with an 18 certificate after a four-month distributor campaign. The piece also noted Trinity Films' UK pickup and the film's then-undistributed US status.
