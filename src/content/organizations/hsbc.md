## Overview

HSBC Holdings plc is a British universal bank headquartered in London that began in Hong Kong in 1865 to finance trade between China, India and Europe, and now serves customers from offices in 58 countries and territories with assets of US$3,054 billion ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1089113/000165495425005028/a2384h.htm)). It is one of the world's largest banking and financial services organisations, and in 2024 it reported a record profit before tax of US$32.3 billion ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2025/hsbc-holdings-plc-annual-results-2024)).

The bank's name comes from its founding member, the Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation, which opened in Hong Kong on 3 March 1865, added a Shanghai branch a month later and a London office three months after that ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/890339)). Its modern shape dates to 1991, when it reorganised as a holding company, and 1992, when it bought Midland Bank — then the United Kingdom's largest commercial bank ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/890339)). In 2018 it joined [Axoni](/organizations/axoni/)'s Series B financing round, an early bet by a global bank on blockchain infrastructure for capital markets ([Finadium](https://finadium.com/hsbc-invests-in-enterprise-blockchain-fintech-axoni/)).

## From Hong Kong trade finance to a London holding company

HSBC was established when trade between China, India and Europe was growing fast, and its early geography followed the trade routes: branches in Japan in 1866, India in 1867, the Philippines in 1875 and Singapore in 1877, and by the close of the 19th century it had become a leading banker for governments, including those of Hong Kong and China ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/890339)). The bank weathered two world wars; many Asian offices were closed and staff imprisoned by the Japanese, its Hong Kong headquarters reopened in 1946, and its mainland China branches were later forced to close amid civil war ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/890339)). It then expanded beyond China, setting up a California subsidiary in 1955, buying the British Bank of the Middle East in 1959 and taking an interest in Hong Kong's Hang Seng Bank in 1965 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/890339)).

## The 2012 settlement and the compliance rebuild

In December 2012 HSBC agreed to hand over $1.9 billion to settle a US money-laundering case — then the biggest penalty ever paid by a bank to the US government ([NPR](https://www.wkyufm.org/2012-12-11/u-s-officials-hope-hsbc-penalty-sends-a-message)). The Justice Department said the bank had violated the Bank Secrecy Act and the Trading With the Enemy Act, that Mexican drug cartels had laundered illegal profits through its Mexican subsidiary — one drug baron was taped in 2008 boasting it was the place to launder money — and that it had let Iran, Cuba and Libya do business in the United States in evasion of sanctions ([NPR](https://www.wkyufm.org/2012-12-11/u-s-officials-hope-hsbc-penalty-sends-a-message)). Under a deferred prosecution agreement HSBC overhauled its compliance department while the government stopped short of charging any individuals, a resolution prosecutors defended by weighing the bank's size against the risk of revoking its US licence ([NPR](https://www.wkyufm.org/2012-12-11/u-s-officials-hope-hsbc-penalty-sends-a-message)).

## A blockchain bet and the 2024 simplification

HSBC's technology bets have been deliberate rather than speculative. In November 2018 it joined Axoni's Series B, bringing the round to $36 million and total funding to $59 million, with the money earmarked for AxCore, Axoni's data-synchronisation platform, and AxLang, an Ethereum-compatible smart-contract language ([Finadium](https://finadium.com/hsbc-invests-in-enterprise-blockchain-fintech-axoni/)). It joined a roster that included [Goldman Sachs](/organizations/goldman-sachs/), [Andreessen Horowitz](/organizations/andreessen-horowitz/), Citi, Coatue, J.P. Morgan, Wells Fargo and [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/), and the two firms said they were actively collaborating on deploying the technology across some of finance's widest-reaching infrastructure ([Finadium](https://finadium.com/hsbc-invests-in-enterprise-blockchain-fintech-axoni/)).

Six years later, under new chief executive Georges Elhedery, the bank reported record results and moved to simplify into four businesses ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2025/hsbc-holdings-plc-annual-results-2024)). Profit before tax of US$32.3 billion included a US$4.8 billion gain on selling its Canadian business, while its Argentine sale produced losses — so 2024 was partly a portfolio-shaping year, concentrating on Hong Kong and the UK as home markets ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2025/hsbc-holdings-plc-annual-results-2024); [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1089113/000165495425005028/a2384h.htm)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was HSBC founded?**
HSBC opened for business on 3 March 1865 in Hong Kong as the Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation, with a Shanghai branch a month later and a London office three months after that ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/890339)). The current parent, HSBC Holdings plc, was created in London in 1991 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/890339)).

**How big is HSBC?**
HSBC serves customers from offices in 58 countries and territories and held assets of US$3,054 billion at 31 March 2025, making it one of the world's largest banking and financial services organisations ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1089113/000165495425005028/a2384h.htm)). It returned US$26.9 billion to shareholders for 2024, and its common equity tier 1 ratio was 14.9% ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1089113/000165495425005028/a2384h.htm)).

**What was the 2012 HSBC settlement?**
HSBC paid $1.9 billion to settle US money-laundering and sanctions cases — then the largest bank penalty to the US government — after the Justice Department found cartel laundering through its Mexican arm and sanctions evasion involving Iran, Cuba and Libya ([NPR](https://www.wkyufm.org/2012-12-11/u-s-officials-hope-hsbc-penalty-sends-a-message)).

**Did HSBC invest in Axoni?**
Yes. In November 2018 HSBC joined Axoni's Series B, bringing the round to $36 million and total funding to $59 million, to fund the AxCore data-synchronisation platform and the AxLang smart-contract language ([Finadium](https://finadium.com/hsbc-invests-in-enterprise-blockchain-fintech-axoni/)). It joined Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan, Citi, [Franklin Templeton](/organizations/franklin-templeton/) and others on the investor list ([Finadium](https://finadium.com/hsbc-invests-in-enterprise-blockchain-fintech-axoni/)).

**Who runs HSBC now?**
Georges Elhedery became Group Chief Executive in September 2024 and reorganised the bank into four businesses, targeting a mid-teens return on tangible equity from 2025 to 2027 ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2025/hsbc-holdings-plc-annual-results-2024)). He told shareholders he aimed to build a simpler, more agile bank focused on its core strengths ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1089113/000165495425005028/a2384h.htm)).
