## Overview

Block, Inc. is the American financial-services company that began life as Square, Inc. — the firm whose pocket-sized card reader let almost anyone accept a credit card — and grew into the parent of the Square seller platform, [Cash App](/organizations/cash-app/), [Afterpay](/organizations/afterpay/), and the music service TIDAL. Founded in 2009 by Jack Dorsey and Jim McKelvey, it renamed itself Block in December 2021 to mark how far it had grown beyond its original seller business, bought Afterpay in a deal announced at $29 billion, and now trades on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker XYZ ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2021/12/01/payments-giant-square-is-changing-its-name-to-block)) ([Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/twitters-dorsey-leads-blockbuster-29-billion-buyout-of-australian-bnpl-giant-afterpay/)).

## From a lost sale to a payments platform

Square's origin story is a small merchant's frustration. McKelvey, a glass artist, lost a $2,000 sale of one of his glass faucets because he had no way to accept his customer's credit card, so he, Dorsey, and iOS engineer Tristan O'Tierney built a workaround ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/audacious-companies/issie-lapowsky/square.html)). In 2010 Square introduced the Card Reader, a one-inch white dongle that plugged into a phone's headset jack and turned it into a card processor, charging merchants 2.75 percent per swipe ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/audacious-companies/issie-lapowsky/square.html)). The company went public in November 2015 at $9 a share — below its expected $11–$13 range — but closed its first day of trading at $13, raising $243 million ([UC Berkeley Law](https://sites.law.berkeley.edu/thenetwork/2015/12/14/squares-ipo-and-the-tech-industry/)).

The consumer side arrived in October 2013 with Square Cash, a service that let anyone send money to a contact simply by emailing them and copying cash@square.com, with no account required and no fee at launch ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=899289)). That product, later renamed Cash App, became the company's consumer engine: a place to send, spend, and store money, and eventually to invest in stocks and bitcoin. In March 2021 Square stretched further from payments, agreeing to pay $297 million for a majority stake in TIDAL, with Jay-Z set to join its board and TIDAL continuing to operate independently alongside the Seller and Cash App businesses ([TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/investing/square-to-acquire-stake-in-music-platform-tidal-for-297-million)).

## The rename, and the Afterpay deal

On 1 December 2021 Square announced it was changing its corporate name to Block, with the legal change expected on or about 10 December 2021 ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2021/12/01/payments-giant-square-is-changing-its-name-to-block)). The point was separation: the Square name had become synonymous with the Seller business, and the company wanted that business to own the brand it was built for, while the corporate entity took a name with room for everything else — building blocks, neighborhood blocks, block parties, and blockchain ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2021/12/01/payments-giant-square-is-changing-its-name-to-block)). There were no organizational changes, and Square Crypto, its bitcoin initiative, was renamed Spiral ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2021/12/01/payments-giant-square-is-changing-its-name-to-block)). The announcement came two days after Dorsey stepped down as CEO of Twitter, prompting speculation he would focus more of his efforts on bitcoin ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2021/12/01/payments-giant-square-is-changing-its-name-to-block)).

Months earlier, in August 2021, Square had announced its biggest deal: buying Afterpay for $29 billion in stock, the largest ever buyout of an Australian firm, with Afterpay shareholders receiving 0.375 Square Class A shares for each share they held ([Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/twitters-dorsey-leads-blockbuster-29-billion-buyout-of-australian-bnpl-giant-afterpay/)). The plan was to fold Afterpay into both the Seller and Cash App ecosystems, letting even the smallest merchants offer buy-now-pay-later at checkout and letting Afterpay customers manage instalments inside Cash App. The deal became fully unconditional in January 2022, Afterpay shares were suspended from trading on 19 January 2022, and the implementation date was 1 February 2022 ([The Golden Times](https://www.thegoldentimes.com.au/?p=28418)).

## What Block looks like now

Two things about the modern company routinely trip up directory listings, and both are worth stating plainly. First, Block has no single designated headquarters: it gave up its 470,000-square-foot lease at 1455 Market Street in San Francisco, which expired in September 2023, as it moved to a largely remote work model, keeping smaller San Francisco offices and its Oakland Uptown Station offices ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/san-francisco/office/block-113431)). Its 2025 announcements carry a "distributed-work-model/Oakland" dateline, not a San Francisco headquarters ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250109826641/en/Block-Announces-Ticker-Symbol-Change-to-XYZ-To-Report-Fourth-Quarter-Results/)). Second, the old SQ ticker is gone: on 21 January 2025 the New York Stock Exchange listing moved from SQ to XYZ (the Australian listing followed on 22 January), with no change to the company's CUSIP or ISIN ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250109826641/en/Block-Announces-Ticker-Symbol-Change-to-XYZ-To-Report-Fourth-Quarter-Results/)).

The Afterpay absorption is still playing out inside Cash App. Afterpay co-founder [Anthony Eisen](/people/anthony-eisen/), who ran Afterpay as co-CEO until the sale, joined Block's board in February 2025, and Afterpay Pre-Purchase — which lets a Cash App Card user convert a debit charge into instalments at checkout — reached general availability in 2026, with Cash App commerce enablement volume growing 17% ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/coverage/filings/2026/08/25/the-afterpay-co-founder-on-block-s-board-moved-18-000-shares-under-a-plan-set-in-march-here-s-what-to-know/)). The pressure point is credit: Block's consumer lending originations grew 59%, and its finance chief expects that pace to normalise as borrower cohorts season ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/coverage/filings/2026/08/25/the-afterpay-co-founder-on-block-s-board-moved-18-000-shares-under-a-plan-set-in-march-here-s-what-to-know/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim San Francisco as Block's headquarters — the evidence points to a distributed model with an Oakland dateline, and the San Francisco listing in many directories is stale. It does not claim a definitive final Afterpay deal value: $29 billion is the announcement-era all-stock figure, and the final value moved with Block's share price. It does not state a final closed price for TIDAL beyond the announced $297 million, nor does it claim a current Cash App user count, which could not be confirmed in a fully-read independent source in this pass. It also does not adjudicate whether Block's bitcoin-centred strategy — Spiral, the Bitkey wallet, and the Proto mining products named in its 2025 brand roster — will pay off; that remains an open strategic question.
