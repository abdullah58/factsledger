## Overview

Euronext Paris is [France](/places/france/)'s regulated stock market in [Paris](/places/paris/) — the former Paris Bourse, founded as a market in 1724 and reborn as Euronext Paris when the Paris, Amsterdam and Brussels exchanges merged on 22 September 2000 to create Euronext, Europe's largest exchange group. It is home to the CAC 40 benchmark index, gathers more than 800 listed companies, and sees over €4.1 billion traded on an average day ([Euronext](https://www.euronext.com/company/markets/paris)).

The market lists equities, bonds, ETFs and derivatives on Euronext's Optiq platform, under a single rule book and the supervision of France's Autorité des Marchés Financiers. It spent 2007–2014 inside NYSE Euronext after the merger with the [New York Stock Exchange](/organizations/new-york-stock-exchange/), before Euronext was spun off as an independent company in 2014 ([Euronext](https://www.euronext.com/en/news/building-leading-european-market-infrastructure)). Euronext handles 25% of European equities trading ([Euronext](https://www.euronext.com/en/news/25-years-euronext-celebrating-quarter-century-shaping-capital-markets)) — so a Paris listing plugs a company into a quarter of the continent's share trading, the practical payoff of the Single Order Book. Euronext acquired the London derivatives market LIFFE soon after the 2000 merger, the market whose Bund futures business [Eurex](/organizations/eurex/)'s predecessor had taken in the late 1990s ([Euronext](https://www.euronext.com/en/news/building-leading-european-market-infrastructure); [University of Houston](https://bauer.uh.edu/spirrong/dtbtip2.pdf)).

## From the Paris Bourse to Euronext

The Paris market's conventional starting point is 1724, when a government decree gave it its first permanent regulation — a date historians treat as a useful marker rather than a settled fact. For its first century the exchange had no fixed home, meeting in the Galerie Vivienne and other Paris venues until Napoleon decided in 1808 to build it a definitive house; the foundation stone of the Palais Brongniart was laid on 5 March 1808 and the building was completed by Éloi Labarre between 1813 and 1826, housing open-outcry trading for most of the next 160 years ([Fondation Napoléon](https://www.napoleon.org/en/magazine/places/palais-brongniart-paris-stock-exchange/)). Electronic trading arrived in 1986 with the CAC system, and the CAC 40 index followed in 1988 ([Euronext](https://www.euronext.com/en/news/cac-40-index-0)). The decisive break came on 22 September 2000, when the Paris Bourse joined Amsterdam and Brussels to form Euronext, the first genuinely cross-border exchange in Europe, with unified rules, a Single Order Book and clearing by LCH S.A. ([Euronext](https://www.euronext.com/en/news/building-leading-european-market-infrastructure)).

## The CAC 40 and the market's structure

The CAC 40 is the market's calling card: a free-float market-capitalisation-weighted index of the 40 largest and most actively traded shares on Euronext Paris, its name short for Cotation Assistée en Continu 40. Its base value of 1,000 was fixed on 31 December 1987; it launched on 15 June 1988, created by the Société des Bourses Françaises for the MATIF futures and MONEP options markets; its future is the second most traded national derivatives product in Europe ([Euronext](https://www.euronext.com/en/news/cac-40-index-0)). Around it sits an index family — CAC Next 20, CAC Large 60, CAC Mid 60, SBF 120 and CAC Small — plus a CAC 40 ESG index from 2021 ([Euronext](https://www.euronext.com/en/news/cac-40-index-0); [Euronext](https://www.euronext.com/company/markets/paris)). The market is governed by a Euronext Rule Book — harmonised Rule Book I plus local Rule Book II rules for Euronext Paris, the regulated market operated by Euronext Paris S.A. — and supervised by the Autorité des Marchés Financiers, with Optiq providing a Single Order Book across its venues ([Euronext](https://www.euronext.com/company/markets/paris)).

## A listing, a takeover and a delisting: CNP Assurances

Euronext Paris is where French companies raise capital — and where listings end. The clearest recent example is [CNP Assurances](/organizations/cnp-assurances/), the insurer listed in Paris until [La Banque Postale](/organizations/la-banque-postale/) moved to take it private. On 28 October 2021 the bank announced it would buy [Groupe BPCE](/organizations/groupe-bpce/)'s entire 16.1% stake at €21.90 a share, taking its holding to 78.9%, and file a simplified tender offer for the remaining minorities at the same price, aiming for a squeeze-out and delisting ([CNP Assurances](https://www.cnp.fr/en/the-cnp-assurances-group/newsroom/press-releases/2021/la-banque-postale-and-groupe-bpce-intend-to-streamline-their-shareholding-relationships-and-strengthen-their-industrial-partnerships)). The €21.90 price was a 36.2% premium and valued CNP at about €15 billion ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2021/10/28/639703.htm)). On 3 June 2022 La Banque Postale announced all offer conditions were fulfilled, holding 97.79% of the capital and 98.56% of the voting rights, and moved to squeeze out the minorities; the bid ended in June 2022 and was followed by a delisting, leaving La Banque Postale sole shareholder of 100% of the capital ([Eurex](https://www.eurex.com/ex-en/rules-regs/corporate-actions/corporate-action-information/CNP-Assurances-S.A.-Takeover-Updated-06-June-2022--2827330); [La Poste Groupe](https://www.lapostegroupe.com/en/news/completion-of-the-merger-between-la-banque-postale-and-cnp-assurances-creates-a-major-bancassurance-player)).

## FAQ

### What is Euronext Paris?

Euronext Paris is France's regulated stock market, the former Paris Bourse. It became part of Euronext when the Paris, Amsterdam and Brussels exchanges merged on 22 September 2000, and it is now the French market of Euronext N.V., Europe's largest exchange group, trading equities, bonds, ETFs and derivatives on the Optiq platform.

### What is the CAC 40?

The CAC 40 is the benchmark index of Euronext Paris: a free-float market-capitalisation-weighted index of the 40 largest and most actively traded shares listed there. Launched on 15 June 1988 with a base value of 1,000 fixed at 31 December 1987, it is Euronext's most widely used index and the yardstick for French blue-chip shares.

### How did Euronext Paris become part of Euronext?

On 22 September 2000 the Paris Bourse combined with the Amsterdam and Brussels exchanges to create Euronext, the first genuinely cross-border exchange in Europe, with unified rules and a Single Order Book. Euronext later merged with the New York Stock Exchange in 2007 and became independent again in 2014 after Intercontinental Exchange floated it.

### Why was CNP Assurances delisted from Euronext Paris?

La Banque Postale bought Groupe BPCE's 16.1% CNP stake at €21.90 a share in 2021 and then made a simplified tender offer for the remaining minorities at the same price — a 36.2% premium valuing CNP at about €15 billion. Once it held 97.79% of capital on 3 June 2022, it squeezed out the minorities and delisted the shares.

### Who regulates Euronext Paris?

The [Autorité des Marchés Financiers](/organizations/autorite-des-marches-financiers/) (AMF), France's independent public markets authority, is the regulator named for the Paris market, alongside the French Prudential Supervisory Authority, which operates under the Banque de France. Trading is governed by a single Euronext Rule Book, with local Rule Book II rules for Euronext Paris.
