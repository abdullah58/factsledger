## Overview

The United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware is the federal bankruptcy court for the state of [Delaware](/places/delaware/), sitting in Wilmington, and the busiest Chapter 11 venue in the [United States](/places/united-states/) ([Court homepage](https://www.deb.uscourts.gov/); [Fox Rothschild](https://insolvency.foxrothschild.com/2021/04/the-delaware-bankruptcy-court-appoints-newest-judge/)). It is a unit of the U.S. District Court for the District of Delaware, with eight authorized bankruptcy judgeships — one permanent and seven temporary — and a docket dominated by large corporate reorganizations filed by companies incorporated in Delaware but headquartered elsewhere ([Federal Judicial Center](https://www.fjc.gov/history/courts/us-district-court-district-delaware-authorized-judgeships); [28 U.S.C. § 152](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2023-title28/html/USCODE-2023-title28-partI-chap6-sec152.htm)).

In 2020 the court recorded 1,666 Chapter 11 filings, more than any other bankruptcy court in the country and nearly triple its 2019 total of 611 — a caseload driven in large part by the COVID-19 pandemic, on a bench that already handled the most cases per judge in the nation ([Fox Rothschild](https://insolvency.foxrothschild.com/2021/04/the-delaware-bankruptcy-court-appoints-newest-judge/)). Its courtroom at 824 N Market Street in Wilmington has hosted some of the defining bankruptcies of the last two decades, from FTX and the Boy Scouts of America to [CalAmp](/organizations/calamp/) ([Court homepage](https://www.deb.uscourts.gov/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/22/ftxs-bankruptcy-hearing-details-prior-control-by-inexperienced-and-unsophisticated-individuals/)).

## Structure, judges and appeals

Bankruptcy judges are appointed by the court of appeals for the circuit — here, the Third Circuit — for 14-year terms, and serve as judicial officers of the district court ([28 U.S.C. § 152](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2023-title28/html/USCODE-2023-title28-partI-chap6-sec152.htm)). The Delaware bench grew with its docket: Congress authorized a single bankruptcy judgeship in 1984, a second in 1992, six in 2005, and eight in 2017 — seven of them temporary seats that Congress has repeatedly extended ([Federal Judicial Center](https://www.fjc.gov/history/courts/us-district-court-district-delaware-authorized-judgeships)). The court's first era was defined by one judge: Helen S. Balick, the 13th woman admitted to the Delaware bar and the first female federal judicial officer in Delaware, was the sole bankruptcy judge until 1993 and served from 1974 to 1998, her tenure leading "to the six-member court of today" ([Widener University Delaware Law School](https://delawarelaw.widener.edu/about/news-events/news/article/delaware-law-school-honors-memory-judge-helen-s-balick)).

The current bench is led by Chief Judge Laurie Selber Silverstein, who held the role by July 2022 ([JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/post/contentViewerEmbed.aspx?fid=a056ef2c-4808-4db0-9f77-bb784822c274)). Seven judges were issuing opinions in the court's database in early 2025 — Silverstein, John T. Dorsey, Craig T. Goldblatt, Thomas M. Horan, Karen B. Owens, J. Kate Stickles and Mary F. Walrath — with Brendan L. Shannon named as a sitting judge in the court's March 2020 general order ([Court opinions database](https://www.deb.uscourts.gov/judges-info/opinions?page=15); [Court general order](https://www.deb.uscourts.gov/sites/deb/files/general-orders/Order_Assignment_of_Judges_for_Cases_Under_the_Small_Business_Reorganization_Act_0.pdf)). Stickles, sworn in on April 6, 2021 to fill the seat of the retired Kevin J. Carey, was the seventh member of the bench at the time ([Fox Rothschild](https://insolvency.foxrothschild.com/2021/04/the-delaware-bankruptcy-court-appoints-newest-judge/)).

Appeals from the court go to the U.S. District Court for the District of Delaware under 28 U.S.C. § 158(a), and from there to the Third Circuit — there is no Bankruptcy Appellate Panel in the Third Circuit, which has not created one ([U.S. Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/archives/jm/civil-resource-manual-190-bankruptcy-jurisdiction-appellate-jurisdiction)). That two-step route matters: district judges in Delaware regularly decide nationally watched questions, such as whether bankruptcy courts may approve non-consensual third-party releases, before the Third Circuit weighs in.

## Why companies file in Delaware

Venue is governed by 28 U.S.C. § 1408: a debtor may file where it is domiciled, resides, or has its principal place of business or principal assets for the 180 days before filing, or where an affiliate's case is already pending ([Weil, Gotshal & Manges](https://restructuring.weil.com/real-estate/wherever-you-go-there-you-are-venue-in-real-estate-bankruptcies/?amp=1)). For a corporation, the state of incorporation counts as its domicile — and because most large American companies are incorporated in Delaware, they can file there even when their headquarters, operations and creditors are elsewhere. Transfer to another district is possible under § 1412 in the "interest of justice or for the convenience of the parties," with the Third Circuit applying a twelve-factor test, but a debtor's choice of venue still carries weight ([Weil, Gotshal & Manges](https://restructuring.weil.com/real-estate/wherever-you-go-there-you-are-venue-in-real-estate-bankruptcies/?amp=1)).

The draw is expertise and speed: a bench that handles complex, multi-state and international restructurings every week, deep precedent, and procedures built for emergency filings. A 2026 study of 155,059 corporate Chapter 11 cases found that 22% are filed outside the debtor's headquarters state and roughly half of those land in Delaware — and that Delaware filings are associated with about a 31 percentage-point lower rate of conversion to Chapter 7 liquidation, about 30 percentage points lower establishment closure three years out, and roughly 25% higher post-bankruptcy employment ([Oxford Law Blog](https://blogs.law.ox.ac.uk/oblb/blog-post/2026/07/does-bankruptcy-forum-shopping-hurt-workers-new-causal-evidence)). Delaware's dominance is not unlimited: in May 2026 Judge Goldblatt held in *In re NB Element* that single-asset real estate cases should presumptively be heard where the property sits, formalizing an exception to the usual deference to a debtor's venue choice ([Weil, Gotshal & Manges](https://restructuring.weil.com/real-estate/wherever-you-go-there-you-are-venue-in-real-estate-bankruptcies/?amp=1)).

## Landmark cases on the docket

FTX filed for Chapter 11 on November 11, 2022, and its first-day hearing in this court drew more than 1,100 remote attendees; Judge Dorsey presided while new CEO John J. Ray III told the court there was a "complete absence of trustworthy financial information" at the collapsed crypto exchange ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/22/ftxs-bankruptcy-hearing-details-prior-control-by-inexperienced-and-unsophisticated-individuals/)). The Boy Scouts of America filed on October 18, 2020, and Chief Judge Silverstein's July 29, 2022 opinion approved most of the third-party releases in its plan — a global settlement of 82,209 abuse claims through a settlement trust, with claimants accepting the plan by more than 85% — while declining to approve releases for claims unrelated to scouting ([JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/post/contentViewerEmbed.aspx?fid=a056ef2c-4808-4db0-9f77-bb784822c274)). Judge Dorsey had reached a similar result months earlier in *In re Mallinckrodt* ([JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/post/contentViewerEmbed.aspx?fid=a056ef2c-4808-4db0-9f77-bb784822c274)).

The court's docket also carries the fast, prepackaged restructurings that define modern Delaware practice. On June 3, 2024, CalAmp — the telematics company based in [Irvine](/places/irvine/), [California](/places/california/), whose stock was registered on the [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/) Global Select Market — filed voluntary Chapter 11 petitions here with subsidiaries CalAmp Wireless Networks, [LoJack](/organizations/lojack/) Global LLC and [Synovia Solutions](/organizations/synovia-solutions/), under a restructuring support agreement with [Lynrock Lake Master Fund](/organizations/lynrock-lake-master-fund/) converting about $229 million of secured notes into equity and taking the company private, as disclosed in the Form 8-K it filed with the [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](/organizations/us-securities-and-exchange-commission/) ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/730255/000095017024067683/camp-20240522.htm)). Older landmarks in the court's opinions database include Energy Future Holdings (Case No. 14-10979, Judge Christopher S. Sontchi) and the Yellow Corporation WARN Act litigation before Judge Goldblatt ([Court opinions database](https://www.deb.uscourts.gov/judges-info/opinions?page=15)).

## What this profile does not claim

- This is not the U.S. District Court for the District of Delaware: the bankruptcy court is a unit of that Article III court, and appeals from it go to the district court first.
- It is not the bankruptcy court of any other district — Delaware's eight authorized judgeships and national Chapter 11 docket are distinct from, for example, the District of Nebraska's two-judge court.
- Judge Shannon's status after 2020 was not re-verified from a source read in full, and the appointment date of the eighth sitting judge (Judge Horan) was not verified; the roster reflects court records read in 2025.
- Chief-judge term dates beyond Silverstein holding the role by July 2022 are not stated; the District Court's July 1, 2024 designation notice exists but its PDF text did not extract.
- No filing-count statistics after 2020 are stated; the 1,666 figure is the latest verified caseload number.
- The Oxford study's findings are associations from an instrumental-variables design on 155,059 cases, not proof that filing in Delaware causes better outcomes for any individual company.
