## Overview

The Motley Fool is a private financial and investing-advice company based in Alexandria, Virginia, founded in July 1993 by brothers David and Tom Gardner and Erik Rydholm as a 16-page print newsletter that mixed stock-picking advice with humour ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/motley-fool-inc)). The name comes from Shakespeare's "motley fool" in *As You Like It* — the court jester who could speak truth to the king without losing his head — and the company's stated mission has always been to educate, enrich and amuse individual investors ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/motley-fool-inc)). Today its best-known product is Stock Advisor, a subscription service launched in February 2002 that sends members two stock picks a month and, by the company's own scoreboard, has returned 1,070% since launch against 185% for the S&P 500 ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/famous-investors/david-gardner/)). The Fool also publishes earnings-call transcripts — including [Cerence](/organizations/cerence/)'s second-quarter fiscal 2026 call, which reported $64.2 million in revenue and named [BYD](/organizations/byd/) as a new customer for Cerence's xUI platform ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/07/cerence-crnc-q2-2026-earnings-transcript/)).

## From a newsletter to the early internet

The Gardner brothers, both English majors with no formal finance training, wrote the first newsletter with Rydholm after graduating, mailing early issues to names taken from friends' and family's wedding invitation lists ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/motley-fool-inc)). Their break came online: after answering investment questions on Prodigy, they moved to America Online, and in 1994 AOL invited the Fool to become a featured part of its homepage ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/motley-fool-inc)). Using the contract's funds they moved into an office in Alexandria and established the "Fool Portfolio", a public portfolio readers could track as a learning device ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/motley-fool-inc)).

By 1996 the Fool's AOL forum was drawing 275,000 visitors a month, and in April 1997, when the AOL contract expired, the company moved to its own domain, Fool.com, already reaching more than 500,000 visitors a month ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/motley-fool-inc)). The same year the Gardners published *The Motley Fool Investment Guide* with Simon & Schuster, which became a *New York Times* bestseller, and they added a nationally syndicated newspaper column, a radio show in 1998, and a UK edition, Fool UK, in 1998 ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/motley-fool-inc)). In 1999 the company launched *Motley Fool Monthly* magazine, signed a content alliance with Reuters, and raised $26.5 million in venture capital from Maveron and the Mayfield Fund ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/motley-fool-inc)).

## The Foolish Four and the dot-com bust

The Fool's first serious reputational test came from its own stock method. In the late 1990s it publicised the "Foolish Four", a systematic method adapted from the Dogs of the Dow for picking Dow stocks by high dividend yield, and published a book on it in 1999. That March, academics Grant McQueen and Steven Thorley used the Foolish Four portfolio as a case study in data mining in the *Financial Analysts Journal*, documenting the mistaken inferences that can come from mining historical returns and setting out antidotes — out-of-sample tests and adjusting returns for risk, transaction costs and taxes ([CFA Institute (Financial Analysts Journal)](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/1999/mining-fools-gold)).

The dot-com crash hit the business directly. After raising a second $30 million round in early 2001 from SoftBank, AOL Time Warner Ventures, Maveron and Mayfield, the Fool was forced to lay off 115 employees as advertising revenue fell, then 45 more in June 2001, and split the business into six units including publishing, electronic media and Fool UK ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/motley-fool-inc)).

## The subscription turn: Stock Advisor

In February 2002 the Fool shifted to a subscription-based business model and launched Stock Advisor, offering members monthly stock picks and premium investment education ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/famous-investors/david-gardner/)). The service sends two picks a month — one from its Hidden Gems team and one from its Rule Breakers team — each with a written investment case, tracked on a public scorecard against the S&P 500 from the day it is issued ([TickerNerd](https://tickernerd.com/resources/the-motley-fool-stock-advisor/)). It costs $199 a year at list price, $99 for a new member's first year, and the company says more than half a million members subscribe ([TickerNerd](https://tickernerd.com/resources/the-motley-fool-stock-advisor/)).

The headline record rests on a handful of famous early calls: Amazon, recommended in 2002 at a split-adjusted $0.77 a share; Netflix in 2004 at $1.85; and Nvidia in 2005 at $0.16 ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/famous-investors/david-gardner/)). From launch in 2002 through August 17, 2025, the company says Stock Advisor returned 1,070%, against 185% for the S&P 500 over the same period ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/famous-investors/david-gardner/)). That figure equal-weights every pick and includes dividends, so it describes an investor who bought every pick in equal amounts and held for years; independent reviews note that a few early winners carry the average, and that the average falls sharply once Nvidia, Netflix and Amazon are removed ([TickerNerd](https://tickernerd.com/resources/the-motley-fool-stock-advisor/)). The house rule is to hold for five years or more, and sell recommendations are rare ([TickerNerd](https://tickernerd.com/resources/the-motley-fool-stock-advisor/)).

In May 2021, co-founder David Gardner announced he was shifting his focus away from stock picking after nearly 30 years — 19 with Stock Advisor and 16 with Rule Breakers — handing his picks to analyst teams who had worked alongside him for years ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/a-letter-from-the-motley-fool-co-founder-david-gardner-2021-05-06)). He remains co-chairman and chief Rule Breaker, hosts the weekly *Rule Breaker Investing* podcast, and chairs The Motley Fool Foundation, the company's public charity; his brother Tom runs the company as chief executive ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/a-letter-from-the-motley-fool-co-founder-david-gardner-2021-05-06); [TickerNerd](https://tickernerd.com/resources/the-motley-fool-stock-advisor/)).

## Beyond stock picks: transcripts, advocacy and sub-brands

The Fool's free site is also a publisher of primary financial material in the form of earnings-call transcripts. The transcript of Cerence's second-quarter fiscal 2026 call, published May 7, 2026, is a typical example: it records $64.2 million in revenue and $7.2 million in adjusted EBITDA, both above guidance, BYD named as a new customer for Cerence's xUI in-car AI platform, and Cerence's disclosure that it had recently filed a patent-infringement action against Amazon, with ongoing matters against Apple, Sony and [TCL](/organizations/tcl/) ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/07/cerence-crnc-q2-2026-earnings-transcript/)).

The company has also played a public advocacy role. David Gardner testified before Congress in 1998 on excessive mutual-fund fees, and the Fool campaigned for the Securities and Exchange Commission's Regulation Fair Disclosure, which requires companies to give vital information to the public at the same time as to Wall Street analysts ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/famous-investors/david-gardner/)). Former SEC chairman Arthur Levitt said in 2001 that two-thirds of the comment letters on the regulation came from Fool readers, and that without them it would not have passed ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/famous-investors/david-gardner/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is The Motley Fool?** A private investing-advice company founded in July 1993 in Alexandria, Virginia, that publishes free stock analysis, books and podcasts and sells subscription stock-picking services, led by Stock Advisor ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/motley-fool-inc)).

**How much does Motley Fool Stock Advisor cost?** It lists at $199 a year, with $99 for a new member's first year, renewing at the list price; it sends two stock picks a month and recommends holding each for at least five years ([TickerNerd](https://tickernerd.com/resources/the-motley-fool-stock-advisor/)).

**Has Stock Advisor beaten the market?** By the company's own scoreboard, Stock Advisor returned 1,070% from its 2002 launch through August 17, 2025, against 185% for the S&P 500 — but the figure equal-weights every pick, so it reflects a handful of enormous early winners, above all Amazon, Netflix and Nvidia ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/how-to-invest/famous-investors/david-gardner/); [TickerNerd](https://tickernerd.com/resources/the-motley-fool-stock-advisor/)).

**Who runs The Motley Fool now?** Co-founder Tom Gardner is chief executive; his brother David stepped back from active stock picking in May 2021 but remains co-chairman, chief Rule Breaker and chairman of The Motley Fool Foundation ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/a-letter-from-the-motley-fool-co-founder-david-gardner-2021-05-06)).

**Why is it called "The Motley Fool"?** The name comes from Shakespeare's "motley fool" in *As You Like It* — the jester who could speak truth to the king without losing his head ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/economics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/motley-fool-inc)).
