## Overview

Paycom is an Oklahoma City-based provider of cloud-based payroll and human capital management (HCM) software, founded in 1998 by Chad Richison and listed on the New York Stock Exchange since 2014 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://app.stocklight.com/stocks/us/nyse-payc/paycom-soft/annual-reports/nyse-payc-2025-10K-25645275.pdf)). Its pitch is a single database covering the whole employment lifecycle, from recruitment to retirement, with employees doing their own payroll through its Beti tool ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://app.stocklight.com/stocks/us/nyse-payc/paycom-soft/annual-reports/nyse-payc-2025-10K-25645275.pdf)). In 2025 it earned $2.05 billion in revenue — and then, in March 2026, it was removed from the S&P 500 ([S&P Dow Jones Indices](https://marketwirenews.com/stock/asgn/news/vertiv-holdings-lumentum-holdings-coherent-and-echos-5691265828482943.html)).

The company is headquartered in Oklahoma City, [Oklahoma](/places/oklahoma/), at 7501 W. Memorial Road, and sells directly through a captive sales force, with 58 sales teams in 29 states and a sales office in 41 of the 50 largest US metropolitan areas ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://app.stocklight.com/stocks/us/nyse-payc/paycom-soft/annual-reports/nyse-payc-2025-10K-25645275.pdf)). It targets organizations with 50 to 10,000 or more employees, served about 39,200 clients at the end of 2025, and kept 91% of its revenue year over year ([Business Wire / Paycom](https://files.quartr.com/reports/377ac-2026-02-11.pdf?ref=TWFya2V0QmVhdCBNZWRpYSBMTEM=)). Its named competitors include [ADP](/organizations/adp/), [Paychex](/organizations/paychex/), [Paylocity](/organizations/paylocity/), Dayforce, Workday, Oracle and SAP ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://app.stocklight.com/stocks/us/nyse-payc/paycom-soft/annual-reports/nyse-payc-2025-10K-25645275.pdf)), and Umbrex's strategy profile of [Asure Software](/organizations/asure-software/) names Paycom as a mid-market HCM competitor with strong payroll capabilities ([Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/company-profiles/asure-software/)).

## From Oklahoma City startup to public company

Paycom was founded in 1998 and built its business on a single idea that still defines it: one database for every HR function, so employee data is entered once and every application — payroll, talent acquisition, time and labor, benefits — works from the same record ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://app.stocklight.com/stocks/us/nyse-payc/paycom-soft/annual-reports/nyse-payc-2025-10K-25645275.pdf)). The company argues this removes the integration errors and duplicate records that come from stitching together multiple vendors' systems, a pitch aimed squarely at competitors that grew by acquisition ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://app.stocklight.com/stocks/us/nyse-payc/paycom-soft/annual-reports/nyse-payc-2025-10K-25645275.pdf)).

It went public on 15 April 2014, pricing 6,645,000 shares at $15.00 and listing on the New York Stock Exchange under the symbol PAYC ([Paycom](https://www.paycom.com/about/press-room/paycom-software-inc-prices-initial-public-offering/)). Growth was fast enough that Paycom joined the S&P 500 in late January 2020 — and then fell 24.27% in the three months after inclusion, the worst of that year's entrants, because the window covered the COVID crash of February and March ([TalkMarkets / Bespoke Investment Group](https://talkmarkets.com/article/sp-500-inductees-class-of-2020)). Richison has led the company since its founding, and in 2020 he ranked fourth on the Equilar 200 list of highest-paid CEOs, published with the New York Times, with $211,131,206 in reported compensation — up 899% on the year before, and mostly a multi-year equity grant reported at its grant-date value ([Equilar](https://www.equilar.com/reports/82-equilar-new-york-times-top-200-highest-paid-ceos-2021)).

Paycom stayed rooted in Oklahoma City while it scaled, hosting its software in three data centers it owns in Oklahoma and [Texas](/places/texas/), and buying a facility in Arizona for a fourth ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://app.stocklight.com/stocks/us/nyse-payc/paycom-soft/annual-reports/nyse-payc-2025-10K-25645275.pdf)). In 2024 the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency authorized it to open the Paycom National Trust Bank, a national trust bank chartered under the National Bank Act ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://app.stocklight.com/stocks/us/nyse-payc/paycom-soft/annual-reports/nyse-payc-2025-10K-25645275.pdf)). Its civic profile in the city peaked in July 2021, when it signed a 15-year naming rights deal that turned the Oklahoma City Thunder's downtown arena into Paycom Center; at the time it was described as an S&P 500 member with a market cap of about $23 billion ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1590955/000119312521225060/d196327dex991.htm)).

## Beti and the automation paradox

Paycom's defining product is Beti, launched in July 2021 as the industry's first self-service payroll technology: employees see, check and approve their own paychecks before payroll runs, catching errors before money moves ([Nucleus Research](https://nucleusresearch.com/research/single/paycom-takes-self-service-to-the-next-level/)). Paycom says Beti cuts payroll processing labor by up to 90% and the time spent correcting payroll errors by up to 85% ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/11/paycom-payc-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript/)). By September 2023 about 60% of existing clients had moved to it, and Richison described the migration as "changing out the jet engines on our plane in mid-flight" ([HR Brew](https://www.hr-brew.com/stories/2023/09/11/paycom-expands-its-global-footprint)). In April 2023 Paycom extended the same model internationally with Global HCM, available in more than 180 countries and in 15 languages, and took Beti to Canada ([HR Brew](https://www.hr-brew.com/stories/2023/09/11/paycom-expands-its-global-footprint)).

The paradox is that Beti worked too well for Paycom's own economics. On 1 November 2023 the shares fell 38.3% in a day after third-quarter revenue of $406 million missed expectations, and CFO Craig Boelte told investors that clients were cutting spending on services and purchases because Beti had made them nonessential — the company's growth was being curbed by cannibalization of its own revenue ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2023/11/01/why-paycom-software-stock-is-crashing-today/?source=iedfolrf0000001)). Paycom guided 2024 revenue growth of just 10–12%, a sharp comedown for a company that had grown at a 22% compound annual rate from 2021 through 2024 ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2023/11/01/why-paycom-software-stock-is-crashing-today/?source=iedfolrf0000001); [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://app.stocklight.com/stocks/us/nyse-payc/paycom-soft/annual-reports/nyse-payc-2025-10K-25645275.pdf)). The numbers since then show a profitable company growing more slowly: revenue rose 9% in 2025 while net income fell, as the table below shows.

## IWant, restructuring and the 2025 reset

Paycom's answer to slower growth was more automation. On 30 July 2025 it launched IWant, a command-driven AI engine that lets any user ask a question by voice or text and get an answer drawn from the single database, without navigating the software; Richison called it "the biggest release since our founding in 1998" ([Paycom](https://www.paycom.com/about/press-room/paycom-unveils-iwant-command-driven-ai-engine-with-accurate-rapid-results/)). Paycom cited a Forrester analysis of a composite organization with more than 500 employees that found IWant delivered a return on investment of over 400%, with managers saving up to 600 hours a year and organizations reclaiming 3,600 hours annually ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/11/paycom-payc-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript/)). Alongside IWant and Beti, the GONE tool automates time-off requests ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/11/paycom-payc-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript/)).

The same automation drive reshaped Paycom itself. In 2025 the company announced a restructuring and ended the year with about 5,800 employees, down from 7,306 a year earlier — a cut of roughly a fifth — while revenue grew 9% and adjusted EBITDA reached $882.3 million, a 43% margin ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/11/paycom-payc-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript/); [Business Wire / Paycom](https://files.quartr.com/reports/377ac-2026-02-11.pdf?ref=TWFya2V0QmVhdCBNZWRpYSBMTEM=)). That is the automation paradox in miniature: the tools that made clients need fewer payroll staff also let Paycom run with fewer of its own. The year also ended Paycom's most visible civic asset: on 14 July 2025 it and the Thunder jointly announced the naming rights agreement would be retired when the new downtown arena is completed in 2028, with Paycom Center keeping its name for three more Thunder seasons ([News 9](https://www.news9.com/story/68757c0b7bb3ac4857abc9ca/paycom-no-naming-rights-new-okc-thunder-arena)). The earnings release's own definition of adjusted EBITDA strips out a "gain on modification of the naming rights agreement," confirming the early exit produced a financial gain ([Business Wire / Paycom](https://files.quartr.com/reports/377ac-2026-02-11.pdf?ref=TWFya2V0QmVhdCBNZWRpYSBMTEM=)).

The reset continued into 2026. On 9 March 2026, S&P Dow Jones Indices announced that Paycom would leave the S&P 500, effective before trading on 23 March, replaced in the index by EchoStar ([S&P Dow Jones Indices](https://marketwirenews.com/stock/asgn/news/vertiv-holdings-lumentum-holdings-coherent-and-echos-5691265828482943.html)). Paycom guided 2026 total revenue of $2.175–2.195 billion, growth of 6–7%, and adjusted EBITDA of $950–970 million ([Business Wire / Paycom](https://files.quartr.com/reports/377ac-2026-02-11.pdf?ref=TWFya2V0QmVhdCBNZWRpYSBMTEM=)). It ended 2025 with $370 million in cash and zero debt ([Business Wire / Paycom](https://files.quartr.com/reports/377ac-2026-02-11.pdf?ref=TWFya2V0QmVhdCBNZWRpYSBMTEM=)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Paycom?**
Paycom is an Oklahoma City-based provider of cloud-based payroll and human capital management software, founded in 1998 by Chad Richison and listed on the NYSE under PAYC since 2014 ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://app.stocklight.com/stocks/us/nyse-payc/paycom-soft/annual-reports/nyse-payc-2025-10K-25645275.pdf)). Its software runs on a single database covering the employment lifecycle, from recruitment to retirement, for about 39,200 clients ([Business Wire / Paycom](https://files.quartr.com/reports/377ac-2026-02-11.pdf?ref=TWFya2V0QmVhdCBNZWRpYSBMTEM=)).

**What is Beti, and why did it hurt Paycom's revenue?**
Beti is Paycom's employee-driven payroll tool, launched in July 2021, which lets employees check and approve their own paychecks before payroll runs ([Nucleus Research](https://nucleusresearch.com/research/single/paycom-takes-self-service-to-the-next-level/)). By removing errors and manual work, it also removed services clients used to pay for, so Paycom's own growth slowed — shares fell 38.3% in a day in November 2023 when management admitted the cannibalization ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2023/11/01/why-paycom-software-stock-is-crashing-today/?source=iedfolrf0000001)).

**Is Paycom still in the S&P 500?**
No. Paycom joined the S&P 500 in late January 2020, but S&P Dow Jones Indices removed it effective 23 March 2026, replacing it with EchoStar ([TalkMarkets / Bespoke Investment Group](https://talkmarkets.com/article/sp-500-inductees-class-of-2020); [S&P Dow Jones Indices](https://marketwirenews.com/stock/asgn/news/vertiv-holdings-lumentum-holdings-coherent-and-echos-5691265828482943.html)). Reference pages that still call it a component are out of date.

**Why did Paycom cut jobs in 2025?**
Paycom announced a restructuring in 2025 and ended the year with about 5,800 employees, down from 7,306, while revenue grew 9% ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/11/paycom-payc-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript/)). Management declined to discuss internal employment strategy beyond confirming the figure, but the cuts coincided with its push into full-solution automation through Beti, GONE and IWant ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/11/paycom-payc-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript/)).
