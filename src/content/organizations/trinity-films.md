## Overview

**Trinity Films** is a [United Kingdom](/places/united-kingdom/) independent film distributor, co-directed by Mark Sandell, that launched the DVD genre label Trinity X at the Cannes Film Festival in May 2011 ([Love Horror](https://lovehorror.co.uk/horror-news/11998/trinity-films-announces-new-label-trinity-x/)). In June 2011 it acquired UK rights to Adam Rehmeier's [The Bunny Game](/creative-works/the-bunny-game/) from sales agent Jinga Films, planning an early-2012 Trinity X release — but the BBFC refused the film a certificate that October, and the planned release collapsed ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/distribution/trinity-picks-up-horror-the-bunny-game-for-uk/5029271.article); [Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/distribution/bbfc-bans-adam-rehmeiers-the-bunny-game-/5033304.article)).

## Trinity X: a label for risky genre cinema

Trinity announced Trinity X from Cannes as a home-entertainment label for films its mainstream operation could not easily handle: Sandell said it would "take on much more risk-taking projects that may not sit very easily with exhibitors," films that "won't be exploitive" but would "challenge the distribution norms and traditional windows" ([Love Horror](https://lovehorror.co.uk/horror-news/11998/trinity-films-announces-new-label-trinity-x/)). The launch title was Simon Rumley's *Red, White and Blue*, a psycho-sexual chiller starring Noah Taylor, Amanda Fuller and Marc Senter, released that September; Rumley praised Trinity as a company "which isn't afraid to pick up challenging material and prove to the industry that there is a market for well made and provocative cinema" ([Love Horror](https://lovehorror.co.uk/horror-news/11998/trinity-films-announces-new-label-trinity-x/); [Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/distribution/trinity-picks-up-horror-the-bunny-game-for-uk/5029271.article)). The positioning was deliberate — a prestige-genre brand that could absorb controversy as a marketing asset rather than a liability.

## The Bunny Game — the acquisition that became a ban

Weeks after the label's launch, Trinity Films acquired UK rights to *The Bunny Game* from Jinga Films, slating it for an early-2012 Trinity X release even though the film was "yet to be certified in the UK" ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/distribution/trinity-picks-up-horror-the-bunny-game-for-uk/5029271.article)). Sandell defended the pick: "Although *The Bunny Game* is acquired viewing and deals with disturbing issues, it fits the Trinity X brand perfectly in that it is distinctive and independent film-making at its best, with strong performances and a highly original filmic eye" ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/distribution/trinity-picks-up-horror-the-bunny-game-for-uk/5029271.article)). Screen Daily's June report noted the gamble explicitly — Trinity was "hoping UK film censors are more lenient with *The Bunny Game* than they were with Eureka's *The Human Centipede II* which last month became the first title banned by the BBFC in two years" ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/distribution/trinity-picks-up-horror-the-bunny-game-for-uk/5029271.article)). They were not. In October the BBFC refused the film a certificate; Trinity's co-director called the decision "disappointing, worrying and sad," while Sandell said: "We did imagine that the BBFC might ask for cuts but an outright ban gives the film a twisted notoriety that, quite frankly, it doesn't warrant" ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/bbfc-bans-the-bunny-game/); [Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/jinga-closes-more-deals-for-the-bunny-game-bbfc-refuses-to-classify-film/5034286.article)). Trinity said it was considering its options; the release never materialised.

## A ban as a market signal

The episode exposed the economics of the Trinity X bet. The company paid for a film it could not legally supply: meanwhile Jinga Films kept selling *The Bunny Game* elsewhere — closing Germany (Illusion Unlimited) and Scandinavia (Njuta) while the UK release sat in limbo ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/jinga-closes-more-deals-for-the-bunny-game-bbfc-refuses-to-classify-film/5034286.article)). Screen Daily's observation that *The Human Centipede 2* was the first BBFC-banned title in two years implies the refusal was a genuine outlier, not a pattern — which makes Trinity's loss a calculated risk that misfired rather than a foreseeable outcome ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/distribution/trinity-picks-up-horror-the-bunny-game-for-uk/5029271.article)). ## Frequently asked questions

**What is Trinity Films?**
Trinity Films is a UK-based independent film distributor, co-directed by Mark Sandell. In May 2011 it launched Trinity X, a DVD/home-entertainment genre label for controversial and risk-taking films, announced from the Cannes Film Festival.

**What was Trinity X's first release?**
Simon Rumley's psycho-sexual chiller *Red, White and Blue*, starring Noah Taylor, Amanda Fuller and Marc Senter, released in September 2011. Director Rumley endorsed the label as one unafraid of challenging material.

**Why couldn't Trinity release The Bunny Game in the UK?**
Trinity acquired the UK rights in June 2011 and planned an early-2012 Trinity X release, but the BBFC refused the film a certificate in October 2011 — meaning it could not be legally supplied in the UK. Cuts were deemed unviable given the pervasiveness of the abuse depicted.

**What did Trinity say about the ban?**
Co-director Mark Sandell said an outright ban gave the film "a twisted notoriety that, quite frankly, it doesn't warrant," and the company's co-director called the decision "disappointing, worrying and sad." Trinity said it was considering its options.

**Is Trinity Films the same as Trinity Content Partners?**
Unverified. A UK home-entertainment company called Trinity Creative Partnership rebranded as Trinity Content Partners in May 2025; no full-read source establishes any lineage to the Trinity Films that launched Trinity X in 2011, so this profile does not merge them.
