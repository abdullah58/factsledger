## Overview

The British Council is usually introduced the way its brochures introduce it: founded in 1934, the UK's international organisation for cultural relations, English teaching and IELTS exams in more than 100 countries, a soft-power flagship. That description is accurate and incomplete. The more complete picture starts in June 2026, when the National Audit Office published an investigation into the Council's financial sustainability that reads less like an audit and more like a diagnosis: the institution that projects Britain to the world has cumulative net losses of £184 million since the pandemic, negative free reserves, a £197 million government loan it cannot repay, and auditors flagging "material uncertainty" over whether it can continue as a going concern ([National Audit Office](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/investigation-into-the-financial-sustainability-of-the-british-council-summary.pdf)). The gap between the brand and the balance sheet is the profile.

## A £197 million loan nobody can resolve

The crisis began with the pandemic. The Council's income fell 28% in 2020-21 as it was forced to temporarily close teaching and exam operations across the world, and it has made losses in every year since — the single exception being 2021-22, when it booked £129 million from selling its share of the IELTS exams market in India ([National Audit Office](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/investigation-into-the-financial-sustainability-of-the-british-council-summary.pdf)). To ease the pressure, HM Treasury and the Foreign Office agreed a £60 million revolving credit facility in July 2020 — on commercial terms, with commercial interest rates — which was extended, increased and finally merged into a £197 million fixed-term loan in March 2024 ([National Audit Office](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/investigation-into-the-financial-sustainability-of-the-british-council-summary.pdf)).

Since that conversion, the Council has not made a single capital repayment: both sides agree it cannot afford them, though it has paid £42 million in interest and fees between 2020-21 and 2025-26 ([National Audit Office](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/investigation-into-the-financial-sustainability-of-the-british-council-summary.pdf)). Its accessible free reserves collapsed from £87 million in 2019-20 to negative £111 million by 2024-25, and the turnaround plan — a condition of the loan extension — does not forecast an operating surplus until 2029-30, with free reserves projected to fall to negative £184 million in the meantime ([National Audit Office](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/investigation-into-the-financial-sustainability-of-the-british-council-summary.pdf)). The two sides are deadlocked on the solution: the Council wants loan forgiveness or an asset swap — it offered its art collection in lieu of repayment — while the Foreign Office and Treasury insist on full repayment, with an external review concluding a write-off would not be appropriate ([National Audit Office](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/investigation-into-the-financial-sustainability-of-the-british-council-summary.pdf)).

## "We could disappear within a decade"

In January 2025, chief executive Scott McDonald said the quiet part aloud: without government help, the organisation faced being cut to the point where "I think we would be in real danger of disappearing, probably over a period of a decade" — and, pointedly, "don't think I'm saying this just for headlines, I think it's really true" ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uk/2025/01/25/british-council-could-disappear-within-a-decade-as-debt-woes-mount-warns-chief-executive/)). He described £250 million in budget cuts under review, hundreds of staff going, and the Council's presence in up to 40 countries in jeopardy, calling the annual loan renewal "a very painful experience for us, because each year we're going to be insolvent unless the loan is renewed" ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uk/2025/01/25/british-council-could-disappear-within-a-decade-as-debt-woes-mount-warns-chief-executive/)).

The most striking detail of his pitch was the art collection: works by Barbara Hepworth, Steve McQueen, David Hockney and Rachel Whiteread, which the Council offered to the government in exchange for writing off the loan. The government rejected it ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uk/2025/01/25/british-council-could-disappear-within-a-decade-as-debt-woes-mount-warns-chief-executive/)). McDonald framed the stakes geopolitically: where the Council withdraws, "that vacuum gets filled — maybe by allies of ours but maybe also by some non-allies," explicitly naming Putin's Russia moving in "to teach language and promote culture and capture the minds of young people" ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uk/2025/01/25/british-council-could-disappear-within-a-decade-as-debt-woes-mount-warns-chief-executive/)). In November 2025, the Council's trustees wrote to the Foreign Secretary warning the organisation remained at high risk of insolvency, and the government has committed to extending the loan only to September 2027 while negotiations on a long-term restructuring continue ([National Audit Office](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/investigation-into-the-financial-sustainability-of-the-british-council-summary.pdf)).

## The shrinkage is already underway

The retrenchment is not hypothetical. Between April 2021 and January 2026 the Council cut 2,110 full-time-equivalent staff, sold its Madrid school for £49 million in April 2025, and — as of June 2026 — intends to proceed with closing its offices in 11 countries, the position still to be finalised with the Foreign Office ([National Audit Office](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/investigation-into-the-financial-sustainability-of-the-british-council-summary.pdf)). Its turnaround plan projects £306 million in net benefits by 2029-30, largely from further headcount reductions of about 25% of the business, but external reviewers have noted the revenue-growth assumptions could be ambitious ([National Audit Office](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/investigation-into-the-financial-sustainability-of-the-british-council-summary.pdf)).

All of this sits oddly beside the institution's origin story. Founded in 1934 by the British government as extremist ideologies gained ground — Communism in Russia, Fascism in Germany, Italy and Spain — and incorporated by Royal Charter in 1940, the Council was created precisely to project British culture outward through friendly knowledge and understanding ([The Straits Times](https://www.straitstimes.com/world/europe/the-british-council-an-instrument-of-britains-soft-power)). Britain's own Foreign Affairs Committee once called it "a vital institution supporting UK influence globally" ([The Straits Times](https://www.straitstimes.com/world/europe/the-british-council-an-instrument-of-britains-soft-power)). It has been here before in smaller doses: the Kremlin ordered its offices outside Moscow closed in 2007 amid tax accusations British officials called retaliation, and in March 2018 Russia suspended all its activities in the country during the Skripal fallout, prompting the Council to say it was "profoundly disappointed" ([The Straits Times](https://www.straitstimes.com/world/europe/the-british-council-an-instrument-of-britains-soft-power)). But the current contraction is self-inflicted by balance-sheet arithmetic, not geopolitics — and it is happening to an organisation that still describes itself as the UK's soft-power and cultural-relations agency ([National Audit Office](https://www.nao.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/investigation-into-the-financial-sustainability-of-the-british-council-summary.pdf)).

## The International School Award connection

Beyond teaching English and delivering exams, the Council runs the International School Award, an accreditation programme recognising schools for international curriculum work. In the 2019–20 cohort, British Council Nepal named 23 schools Full Award recipients — among them [[Brihaspati Vidyasadan](/schools/brihaspati-vidyasadan/)](https://factsledger.com/schools/brihaspati-vidyasadan) of Kathmandu and [Golden Peak High School](https://factsledger.com/schools/golden-peak-high-school), entries 4 and 17 on the award list (British Council Nepal press release, 20 October 2020; corroborated by New Spotlight Magazine). For those schools, the British Council's award is a mark of international standing — the same institution that, a few years later, would be offering its art collection to cover its debts.
