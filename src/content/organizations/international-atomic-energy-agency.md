## Overview

The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) is the world's nuclear watchdog: a Vienna-based autonomous international organization with 182 member states that verifies nuclear material is used only for peaceful purposes, sets global nuclear safety and transport standards, and promotes peaceful nuclear science. ([STVR](https://enrsi.stvr.sk/articles/news/461138/slovakia-secures-seat-on-iaea-board-of-governors)) It is not a United Nations specialized agency; under a 1957 agreement it reports to the [UN General Assembly](/organizations/united-nations/) and, when necessary, the Security Council. ([UN–IAEA Agreement](https://www.jus.uio.no/english/services/library/treaties/14/14-05/agreement-un-atomic.html))

That reporting line is what gives the Agency teeth. When its Board of Governors finds a state in non-compliance with safeguards, the Agency must report the case to both the Security Council and the General Assembly. ([UN–IAEA Agreement](https://www.jus.uio.no/english/services/library/treaties/14/14-05/agreement-un-atomic.html)) Its dual mandate — promote the atom's peaceful uses while policing them — comes from its Statute: to "accelerate and enlarge the contribution of atomic energy to peace, health and prosperity" while ensuring that assistance it provides is not used to further any military purpose. ([Cornell](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/international_atomic_energy_agency)) In 2005 the Agency shared the Nobel Peace Prize with its then Director General, Mohamed ElBaradei, for efforts to prevent nuclear energy from being used for military purposes. ([NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/2005/iaea/speedread/))

## An autonomous watchdog, not a UN specialized agency

The IAEA is not a United Nations specialized agency: the 1957 agreement between the UN and the Agency recognizes it as an autonomous international organization working "under the aegis of the United Nations," reporting to the General Assembly and, when necessary, the Security Council. ([UN–IAEA Agreement](https://www.jus.uio.no/english/services/library/treaties/14/14-05/agreement-un-atomic.html)) It traces to U.S. President Dwight Eisenhower's "Atoms for Peace" address to the General Assembly on 8 December 1953, and its Statute defines a dual mandate: to "accelerate and enlarge the contribution of atomic energy to peace, health and prosperity" while ensuring that assistance it provides is not used to further any military purpose. ([Cornell](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex/international_atomic_energy_agency)) The Agency began work in 1957, is headquartered in Vienna, Austria, and is governed by a General Conference, a 35-nation Board of Governors, and a Secretariat led by a Director General. ([NTI](https://www.nti.org/education-center/treaties-and-regimes/international-atomic-energy-agency/); [STVR](https://enrsi.stvr.sk/articles/news/461138/slovakia-secures-seat-on-iaea-board-of-governors)) It has 182 member states. ([STVR](https://enrsi.stvr.sk/articles/news/461138/slovakia-secures-seat-on-iaea-board-of-governors)) Rafael Grossi of Argentina leads it as the sixth Director General. ([NTI](https://www.nti.org/education-center/treaties-and-regimes/international-atomic-energy-agency/))

## Safeguards: how verification actually works

Safeguards — the IAEA's core verification tool — check whether states' nuclear declarations are correct and complete. Under a safeguards agreement, IAEA inspectors visit nuclear facilities to verify records on the whereabouts of nuclear material, to check IAEA-installed instruments and surveillance equipment, and to confirm physical inventories. ([NTI](https://www.nti.org/education-center/treaties-and-regimes/international-atomic-energy-agency/)) For non-nuclear-weapon states party to the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT), comprehensive safeguards are mandatory, built on the INFCIRC/153 model agreement, plus INFCIRC/66, nuclear-weapon-free-zone treaties, and the Brazilian-Argentine ABACC. ([NTI](https://www.nti.org/education-center/treaties-and-regimes/international-atomic-energy-agency/)) A voluntary Additional Protocol allows more intensive inspections, including access for assurances about undeclared sites. ([NTI](https://www.nti.org/education-center/treaties-and-regimes/international-atomic-energy-agency/)) Non-compliance findings go to the Board, which calls for remedy and decides on referral to the UN Security Council and General Assembly. ([NTI](https://www.nti.org/education-center/treaties-and-regimes/international-atomic-energy-agency/)) The system's limits are real: [Iran](/places/iran/) has been found in violation of its safeguards agreement, and in 2003 North Korea announced its withdrawal from the NPT and forced IAEA inspectors to leave. ([NobelPrize.org](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/2005/iaea/speedread/))

| --- | --- |
| Ad hoc | Verify a state's initial nuclear report |
| Routine | The most common, regularly performed inspections |
| Special | Supplementary inspections in unusual circumstances |
| Safeguard visits | Inspect declared facilities to confirm design information |

## Safety standards, transport rules, and a plant at war

Beyond verification, the IAEA writes the nuclear world's rulebook — including transport. Its Regulations for the Safe Transport of Radioactive Material, SSR-6 (Rev. 1, 2018 edition), set the principal requirements for moving radioactive material, incorporated into the [IMDG Code](/events/imdg-code/) for sea, the ICAO Technical Instructions for air, and regional and national land regulations. ([IAEA](https://nucleus.iaea.org/sites/committees/Before%202023%20Archive/TRANSSC/Denials_of_Shipment_Meeting_December_2020/TMDoSInformationSheet-EVT1907111.pdf)) For packaged irradiated nuclear fuel, plutonium, and high-level waste carried by sea, the [INF Code](/events/inf-code/) — a mandatory IMO instrument — sets ship design and operational requirements, while SSR-6 supplies the underlying transport requirements. In wartime: at [Ukraine](/places/ukraine/)'s Zaporizhzhia plant, Europe's largest, shelling in August 2022 disconnected a reactor unit and forced emergency generators into operation, and Director General Grossi told the Security Council that all seven of his "indispensable pillars" of nuclear safety and security had been compromised at the site. ([UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2022/08/1124532)) Grossi had visited the plant that year seeking a protection zone around it. ([AFP](https://www.spacedaily.com/reports/IAEA_board_backs_Grossi_as_head_for_second_term_to_2027_999.html))

| IMDG Code | IMO | Radioactive material carried by sea |
| ICAO Technical Instructions | ICAO | Radioactive material carried by air |
| INF Code | IMO | Ships carrying irradiated fuel, plutonium, high-level waste |

## Frequently asked questions

### What does the IAEA do?

The IAEA verifies that nuclear material is used only for peaceful purposes through inspections called safeguards, sets international nuclear safety and transport standards, and helps member states apply nuclear science in medicine, agriculture, and energy. It is headquartered in Vienna and had 182 member states as of September 2026. ([STVR](https://enrsi.stvr.sk/articles/news/461138/slovakia-secures-seat-on-iaea-board-of-governors); [NTI](https://www.nti.org/education-center/treaties-and-regimes/international-atomic-energy-agency/))

### Is the IAEA part of the United Nations?

No — it is not a UN specialized agency. A 1957 agreement recognizes the IAEA as an autonomous international organization under the aegis of the United Nations. It reports to the General Assembly at each regular session and to the Security Council when questions within the Council's competence arise. ([UN–IAEA Agreement](https://www.jus.uio.no/english/services/library/treaties/14/14-05/agreement-un-atomic.html))

### Who leads the IAEA?

Rafael Mariano Grossi of Argentina, the Agency's sixth Director General, has led the IAEA since 3 December 2019. The Board of Governors reappointed him by acclamation for a second four-year term running from 3 December 2023 to 2 December 2027. He is the first IAEA head from Latin America. ([Czech Mission](https://mzv.gov.cz/mission.vienna/en/latest/iaea_board_of_governors_reappoints.html); [AFP](https://www.spacedaily.com/reports/IAEA_board_backs_Grossi_as_head_for_second_term_to_2027_999.html))

### What are IAEA safeguards?

Safeguards are technical measures verifying that states' nuclear declarations are correct and complete. Inspectors visit facilities, check records and surveillance equipment, and confirm physical inventories of nuclear material. Non-nuclear-weapon states party to the NPT accept comprehensive safeguards; a voluntary Additional Protocol allows more intensive inspections. ([NTI](https://www.nti.org/education-center/treaties-and-regimes/international-atomic-energy-agency/))

### How does the IAEA's transport role relate to the INF Code?

The IAEA's SSR-6 regulations set the base requirements for transporting radioactive material, and they feed into the IMDG Code for sea carriage. The INF Code is a separate, mandatory IMO instrument governing ships that carry packaged irradiated nuclear fuel, plutonium, and high-level radioactive waste. ([IAEA](https://nucleus.iaea.org/sites/committees/Before%202023%20Archive/TRANSSC/Denials_of_Shipment_Meeting_December_2020/TMDoSInformationSheet-EVT1907111.pdf))
