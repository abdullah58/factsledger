## Overview

The Great American Economic Revival Industry Group was not a company, a commission, or a standing body. It was a list. On April 14, 2020, President Donald J. Trump announced in a White House press release that "many of the esteemed executives, economists, scholars, and industry leaders" would form "various Great American Economic Revival Industry Groups" to work with the White House ([The American Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/white-house-press-release-president-donald-j-trump-announces-great-american-economic)). A count of the release's own roster comes to 220 named people across 17 sector groups — from agriculture to thought leaders. The release framed them as "bipartisan groups of American leaders" who would work together with the White House "to chart the path forward toward a future of unparalleled American prosperity," declaring that "the health and wealth of America is the primary goal" and that the groups would "produce a more independent, self-sufficient, and resilient Nation" ([The American Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/white-house-press-release-president-donald-j-trump-announces-great-american-economic)).

What almost no coverage captures is what happened within 48 hours. On April 15, Trump held conference calls with dozens of the named executives — and according to participants who spoke to the Associated Press, the advisers pushed back: expanding testing and contact tracing was "crucial," best-practice guidance was needed on whether to reopen in phases or all at once, and an impending rush on personal protective equipment was coming, since shuttered businesses would need it to keep workers and customers safe ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2020/4/16/trumps-approach-to-reopen-economy-leaves-decisions-to-governors)). One participant said Trump was told that "the economy will look very different and operations will look very different" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2020/4/16/trumps-approach-to-reopen-economy-leaves-decisions-to-governors)).

## The announcement and the roster

The roster read like a cross-section of American corporate leadership in early 2020. Banking brought JPMorgan's Jamie Dimon, Bank of America's Brian Moynihan, Goldman Sachs's David Solomon, and Morgan Stanley's James Gorman; financial services added Stephen Schwarzman of Blackstone, John Paulson, Citadel's Kenneth Griffin, Elliott Management's Paul Singer, Fidelity's Abigail Johnson, and Visa's Al Kelly ([The American Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/white-house-press-release-president-donald-j-trump-announces-great-american-economic)). Tech gathered Apple CEO Tim Cook, Alphabet's Sundar Pichai, Oracle's Larry Ellison and Safra Catz, Salesforce's Marc Benioff, Microsoft's Satya Nadella, and Facebook's Mark Zuckerberg ([The American Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/white-house-press-release-president-donald-j-trump-announces-great-american-economic)). Retail named Amazon's Jeff Bezos, Walmart's Doug McMillon, and Home Depot co-founders Ken Langone and Bernie Marcus; manufacturing named General Motors' Mary Barra, Ford's Bill Ford, and Tesla's [Elon Musk](/people/elon-musk) ([The American Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/white-house-press-release-president-donald-j-trump-announces-great-american-economic)).

The healthcare section, at 27 names the largest, drew from hospital chains, drugmakers, and distributors: Johnson & Johnson's Alex Gorsky, Pfizer's Albert Bourla, Merck's Kenneth Frazier, 3M's Mike Roman, McKesson's Brian Tyler, and UnitedHealth's David Wichmann among them ([The American Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/white-house-press-release-president-donald-j-trump-announces-great-american-economic)). Hospitality listed casino and hotel chiefs including Sheldon Adelson of Las Vegas Sands and Marriott's Arne Sorenson; the food-and-beverage group ran to 23 names, from McDonald's, Coca-Cola, Starbucks, and Darden to chefs Wolfgang Puck, Thomas Keller, and Daniel Boulud ([The American Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/white-house-press-release-president-donald-j-trump-announces-great-american-economic)). The sports section — the one most copied across the internet — named 16 figures: commissioners Adam Silver (NBA), Rob Manfred (MLB), Roger Goodell (NFL), and Gary Bettman (NHL), plus UFC's [Dana White](/people/dana-white), WWE's Vince McMahon, Patriots owner Bob Kraft, Cowboys owner Jerry Jones, and Mavericks owner Mark Cuban ([The American Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/white-house-press-release-president-donald-j-trump-announces-great-american-economic)). The thought-leaders group mixed economists and political figures: Condoleezza Rice, Art Laffer, Steve Forbes, Steve Moore, Scott Gottlieb, Jim DeMint, and Kay Coles James among the 11 names ([The American Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/white-house-press-release-president-donald-j-trump-announces-great-american-economic)). Notably, the construction/labor section put AFL-CIO president Richard Trumka and Teamsters leader Jim Hoffa on the same list as Bechtel and Fluor ([The American Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/white-house-press-release-president-donald-j-trump-announces-great-american-economic)).

## The Wednesday calls: the advisers push back on testing

The AP's reporting on the April 15 calls is the clearest evidence that the roster was more than a prop — the executives used it to tell Trump what he needed to hear. Participants stressed that testing and contact tracing had to expand before businesses could safely reopen, and that clear federal guidance on phased versus immediate reopening was essential ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2020/4/16/trumps-approach-to-reopen-economy-leaves-decisions-to-governors)). The warnings came with a political edge on both sides: Senator Lindsey Graham told ABC's *The View* that "we are struggling with testing at a large scale" and that "you really can't go back to work until you have more tests," while economist Steve Moore — himself a named member of the thought-leaders group — urged Trump to act fast, warning of "a mini Great Depression if we keep the economy shut down" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2020/4/16/trumps-approach-to-reopen-economy-leaves-decisions-to-governors)).

The AP also offered the reading that no roster copier mentions: the panel "could help give him a measure of cover," because if cases surged once restrictions were lifted — as many experts were warning they would — Trump could tell the public he had not acted alone and that "the nation's top minds," from manufacturing to defense to technology, had helped shape the plan ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2020/4/16/trumps-approach-to-reopen-economy-leaves-decisions-to-governors)). The following day, Trump gave governors a phased reopening road map and told them they would decide for their own states — "You're going to call your own shots," he said, according to an audio recording obtained by the AP — a marked shift from his earlier claim of "total" authority over reopening ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2020/4/16/trumps-approach-to-reopen-economy-leaves-decisions-to-governors)). The same week he also convened legislators for a separate congressional advisory task force, while new federal data showed at least 22 million Americans had been thrown out of work in the previous month ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2020/4/16/trumps-approach-to-reopen-economy-leaves-decisions-to-governors)).

## The energy group and the donor pattern

The one systematic outside analysis of who got picked focuses on the roster's 12-person energy section. The watchdog outlet Documented examined the eight oil-and-gas executives named there and found that their companies had been losing investor money before the pandemic — an average stock-price decline of 7.29% from 2015 to 2019 ([Documented](https://documented.org/reporting/oil-execs-on-trumps-opening-the-country-council-lost-millions-for-shareholders-spent-millions-on-politics-and-lobbying)). In the same period, those eight executives had together made $4,267,988 in federal political contributions since Trump announced his candidacy in June 2015, nearly all of it to Republicans ([Documented](https://documented.org/reporting/oil-execs-on-trumps-opening-the-country-council-lost-millions-for-shareholders-spent-millions-on-politics-and-lobbying)). The largest single recipient was the Trump Victory Committee at $619,400, followed by America First Action ($500,000), the National Republican Senatorial Committee ($410,900), and the National Republican Congressional Committee ($407,400) ([Documented](https://documented.org/reporting/oil-execs-on-trumps-opening-the-country-council-lost-millions-for-shareholders-spent-millions-on-politics-and-lobbying)).

The individual totals are the striking part: Continental Resources' Harold Hamm gave $1,770,200 and Hess's John Hess gave $1,707,841 — the two biggest political spenders in the energy group, even though Documented notes their companies are each less than one-tenth the size of ExxonMobil ([Documented](https://documented.org/reporting/oil-execs-on-trumps-opening-the-country-council-lost-millions-for-shareholders-spent-millions-on-politics-and-lobbying)). The outlet's conclusion, presented here as its analysis rather than an established fact, is that "the inclusion of major campaign donors, like Harold Hamm, indicates that political spending played a role in the selection process for the Economic Revival Group" ([Documented](https://documented.org/reporting/oil-execs-on-trumps-opening-the-country-council-lost-millions-for-shareholders-spent-millions-on-politics-and-lobbying)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim the group had any formal existence beyond the announcement and the April 2020 calls — no charter, no officers, no bylaws, and no documented meetings after the April 15-16 period appear in any source read here. It does not say which of the 220 named members actually joined the calls; the AP reported only that executives of "dozens of leading American companies" participated. The 220 figure is a count of the press release's own roster, not a claim about attendance. The donor analysis is a single watchdog outlet's work and is reported as its analysis; its inference about selection motives is not independently verified. Finally, this profile does not claim the group shaped any specific policy decision — the read sources document its announcement, its members' early warnings, and one outside critique, and nothing beyond that.
