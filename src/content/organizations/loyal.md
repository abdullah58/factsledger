## Overview

Loyal is the San Francisco biotech company trying to do something no pharmaceutical company has done before: win FDA approval for a drug whose labeled purpose is extending lifespan itself. Founded in late 2019 by Celine Halioua, a clinical-stage animal health company, Loyal is developing a pipeline of canine longevity drugs — LOY-001, LOY-002 and LOY-003 — with the explicit goal of becoming the first company to market an FDA-approved lifespan-extension drug for any species ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91490365/loyal-startup-250-million-help-dogs-live-longer)).

Halioua came to the problem through the longevity-investment world: before founding Loyal she was chief of staff at The Longevity Fund, a lifespan-focused investment fund founded by Laura Deming that became one of Loyal's early backers. Her thesis, as she told *Fast Company*, was that dog longevity drugs could one day lead to similar treatments for humans — the species are similar in many ways, dogs' short lives make lifespan trials faster to run, and owners plainly want more time with their dogs ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91490365/loyal-startup-250-million-help-dogs-live-longer)).

## A regulatory first: LOY-001 and the FDA

In November 2023, Loyal cleared the milestone that underwrites its entire regulatory strategy: the FDA's Center for Veterinary Medicine accepted the "Reasonable Expectation of Effectiveness" (RXE) section of its conditional approval application for LOY-001, a drug aimed at extending the lifespan of large and giant-breed dogs ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/207748/big-dog-lifespan-canine-longevity-drug-loyal-fda)). As Halioua put it at the time, the company had earned what it believed was "the FDA's first-ever formal acceptance that a drug can be developed + approved for lifespan extension" — not for treating a disease, but for aging itself ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/207748/big-dog-lifespan-canine-longevity-drug-loyal-fda)).

The science behind LOY-001 targets the breed-size paradox of dogs: large breeds can live half as long as small ones — Great Danes and Newfoundlands may live only eight years versus up to 20 for Chihuahuas and Miniature Poodles — and the company links that gap to insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), a growth-associated hormone it says is 28 times more prevalent in large dogs ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/207748/big-dog-lifespan-canine-longevity-drug-loyal-fda)). LOY-001 is a long-acting injection administered by a veterinarian every three to six months, aimed at dampening that overexpression ([Substack analysis](https://doctortanc.substack.com/p/the-puzzle-of-the-pill-loy-002)). Getting there was not quick: Halioua said the efficacy technical section ran over 2,300 pages and took more than four years to assemble ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/207748/big-dog-lifespan-canine-longevity-drug-loyal-fda)).

## LOY-002, the STAY trial, and $250 million

Loyal's lead program is LOY-002, a beef-flavored daily prescription pill designed to extend the healthy lifespan of senior dogs by mimicking some of the metabolic effects of a calorie-restricted diet — without requiring owners to cut their dogs' food or suppress their appetites, which Halioua says would damage the dog-human bond built around shared meals ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91490365/loyal-startup-250-million-help-dogs-live-longer)). The drug targets age-related metabolic dysfunction rather than the growth-hormone pathway, and the company has deliberately kept its exact active ingredient undisclosed ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91490365/loyal-startup-250-million-help-dogs-live-longer); [Substack analysis](https://doctortanc.substack.com/p/the-puzzle-of-the-pill-loy-002)).

By early 2026, LOY-002 had met two of the three milestones for the FDA's Expanded Conditional Approval — target animal safety and reasonable expectation of effectiveness — with the final one, demonstrating consistent manufacturing at scale, still ahead; if approved, it would likely be labeled for dogs at least 10 years old weighing at least 14 pounds ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91490365/loyal-startup-250-million-help-dogs-live-longer)). The efficacy case rests on the STAY study, which completed enrollment of roughly 1,300 dogs across 72 veterinary clinics — which Halioua describes as the largest-ever animal health clinical trial — with the hope of showing at least one additional healthy year ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91490365/loyal-startup-250-million-help-dogs-live-longer)).

Investors have funded the bet at scale. In February 2026 Loyal announced a $100 million Series C led by age1, the longevity venture firm co-founded by Deming and Alex Colville, with Baillie Gifford and existing investors participating — bringing total investment past $250 million, on top of a $27 million Series A led by Khosla Ventures in September 2021 ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91490365/loyal-startup-250-million-help-dogs-live-longer); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/207748/big-dog-lifespan-canine-longevity-drug-loyal-fda)). Halioua told *Fast Company* she hoped to submit the final FDA requirement for LOY-002 in 2026, starting a roughly six-month review; a third program, the daily pill LOY-003 aimed at larger dogs, could follow a year or two behind ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91490365/loyal-startup-250-million-help-dogs-live-longer)).

## What this profile does not claim

- This profile does not claim any Loyal drug is FDA-approved: as of the sources read, none had cleared the final manufacturing milestone.
- It does not state the active ingredients or exact mechanisms of LOY-001 or LOY-002, which the company has not publicly disclosed; the pharmacological analysis cited is explicitly speculative.
- It does not claim LOY-002 will extend any given dog's life by a specific amount — the STAY trial's efficacy readout had not been reported at research time.
- It does not repeat company-issued claims (such as the third 2026 effectiveness acceptance) that lacked independent editorial coverage.
- Financial and enrollment figures are as reported by the outlets cited, not independently audited.
