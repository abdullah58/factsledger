## Overview

**American Commitment** is a conservative 501(c)(4) advocacy nonprofit, founded on April 10, 2012, by free-market organizer Phil Kerpen, that campaigns for free markets, economic growth, constitutionally limited government, property rights, and individual freedom. ([Center for Public Integrity](http://publicintegrity.org/politics/nonprofit-profile-american-commitment/)) Registered with the IRS as a social-welfare nonprofit under Employer Identification Number 45-2600535, it was listed in Washington, D.C. in a 2012 profile, with a current IRS-record address in Haymarket, Virginia ([District of Columbia](/places/district-of-columbia/)), and is not legally required to disclose its donors. ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/452600535)) Since 2025 it has published commissioned research under its "Commitment to Seniors" initiative dissecting [AARP's](/organizations/aarp/) corporate-royalty finances, including the claim that AARP paid $135.8 million more in employee compensation than it collected in member dues. ([American Commitment](https://commitmenttoseniors.org/new-research-and-polling-sounds-alarm-on-aarps-lucrative-financial-partnership-with-major-health-care-conglomerate-unitedhealth-group/); [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/op-eds/4648693/aarp-membership-dues-unitedhealth-group/))

This profile covers the nonprofit advocacy organization at americancommitment.org (EIN 45-2600535), led by Kerpen — a former policy and legislative strategist at Americans for Prosperity who previously worked at the Club for Growth and who chairs the Internet Freedom Coalition. ([Center for Public Integrity](http://publicintegrity.org/politics/nonprofit-profile-american-commitment/)) It is **not** the American College & University Presidents' Climate Commitment — a higher-education climate pledge managed by the nonprofit Second Nature ([UN Department of Economic and Social Affairs](https://sdgs.un.org/partnerships/american-college-university-presidents-climate-commitment)) — nor any other similarly named civic initiative.

## Origins and early election activity

Kerpen, a former policy and legislative strategist at Americans for Prosperity, launched American Commitment in April 2012; by mid-July he said the group had raised $7 million. ([Center for Public Integrity](http://publicintegrity.org/politics/nonprofit-profile-american-commitment/)) In the 2012 election cycle the Center for Responsive Politics found it spent nearly $2 million on ads expressly advocating the election or defeat of federal candidates across the [United States](/places/united-states/) — $1.4 million in Arizona's Senate race supporting Republican Jeff Flake over Democrat Richard Carmona, and $1.1 million against Florida Democratic Senator Bill Nelson. ([Center for Public Integrity](http://publicintegrity.org/politics/nonprofit-profile-american-commitment/)) Early campaigns ran through standalone sites — NoMandateTax.com against the Affordable Care Act, KeystoneXLNow.com for the Keystone XL pipeline, and WarOnCoal.com against Barack Obama's coal-emissions rules — and the group later backed Steve Lonegan in New Jersey's 2013 Senate race and Thom Tillis in North Carolina's 2014 race. ([Center for Public Integrity](http://publicintegrity.org/politics/koch-linked-organization-uses-dark-money-to-fight-political-disclosure/)) The group later opposed Gigi Sohn's 2021 FCC nomination, argued in a 2022 analysis that school mask mandates failed, and pushed to repeal the estate tax. ([InfluenceWatch](https://www.influencewatch.org/non-profit/american-commitment/))

## Funding, donors, and the disclosure fights

Tax filings reviewed by the Center for Public Integrity show early funding was heavily networked: 87% of the $13 million raised between 2011 and 2013 came from three Koch-connected nonprofits — the Freedom Partners Chamber of Commerce ($6.4 million), the Center to Protect Patient Rights ($4.8 million in 2012, headed by consultant Sean Noble), and Free Enterprise America ($103,000 in 2011). ([Center for Public Integrity](http://publicintegrity.org/politics/koch-linked-organization-uses-dark-money-to-fight-political-disclosure/)) Trade associations also gave: the Pharmaceutical Research and Manufacturers of America contributed $50,000 in 2013 (up from $25,000 in 2012), and the National Cable and Telecommunications Association gave $10,000 in each of those years. ([Center for Public Integrity](http://publicintegrity.org/politics/koch-linked-organization-uses-dark-money-to-fight-political-disclosure/)) As a 501(c)(4) the group need not disclose donors; asked by the *Washington Post* whether Charles and David Koch funded it, Kerpen declined, citing donor privacy. ([Center for Public Integrity](http://publicintegrity.org/politics/nonprofit-profile-american-commitment/)) It also co-wrote a letter to the IRS commissioner urging proposed rules for politically active nonprofits be "withdrawn and abandoned," behind the slogan "Don't let the IRS gut the First Amendment!" ([Center for Public Integrity](http://publicintegrity.org/politics/koch-linked-organization-uses-dark-money-to-fight-political-disclosure/))

## The "Commitment to Seniors" reports

On March 26, 2025, American Commitment launched its "Commitment to Seniors" initiative with a Juniper Research Group report on AARP's financing, stating AARP received $1.1 billion in corporate royalties in 2023 and an estimated $9.9 billion from [UnitedHealth Group](/organizations/unitedhealth-group/) since 2007. ([American Commitment](https://commitmenttoseniors.org/new-research-and-polling-sounds-alarm-on-aarps-lucrative-financial-partnership-with-major-health-care-conglomerate-unitedhealth-group/)) An October 2025 special memo by Juniper CEO Chris Jacobs quoted AARP's own financial statements disclosing a one-time $9.062 billion 2024 royalty advance from UnitedHealth — about 31 times AARP's $288.8 million in dues, a ratio the commissioned analysis presents as evidence of financial dependence on the nation's largest insurer — and a *Washington Examiner* op-ed by Jacobs added the Form 990 findings: compensation exceeded dues by over $135 million, royalties neared $17.6 billion over 25 years, and an estimated $10.8 billion since 2007 came from UnitedHealth. ([American Commitment](https://www.americancommitment.org/app/uploads/2025/10/CTS-AARP-Memo-2025.pdf); [Washington Examiner](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/op-eds/4648693/aarp-membership-dues-unitedhealth-group/)) AMAC's news outlet independently reported the headline finding — $9.06 billion from UnitedHealth in 2024, "more than 30 times what AARP made in membership dues." ([AMAC](/organizations/amac/); [AMAC Newsline](https://amac.us/print-article/?slug=aarp-looks-to-cash-in-on-government-shutdown))

## The Form 990 figures and their limits

The $135 million, $17.6 billion, and $10.8 billion figures are the commissioned research's own numbers: no tier1 or major outlet has independently audited the Form 990 arithmetic, so this profile presents them as American Commitment's claims, not independently verified facts. The launch was paired with McLaughlin & Associates polling finding 89% of voters aged 55 and older were concerned about AARP receiving billions in corporate royalties while lobbying on health care. ([American Commitment](https://commitmenttoseniors.org/new-research-and-polling-sounds-alarm-on-aarps-lucrative-financial-partnership-with-major-health-care-conglomerate-unitedhealth-group/)) AMAC's outlet also noted that UnitedHealth pays a 4.95% royalty on Medigap policies while under federal investigation by the [Department of Justice](/organizations/us-department-of-justice/) for deceptive billing practices. ([AMAC Newsline](https://amac.us/print-article/?slug=aarp-looks-to-cash-in-on-government-shutdown))

## Frequently asked questions

**What is American Commitment?**
It is a conservative 501(c)(4) advocacy nonprofit founded in 2012 by Phil Kerpen, dedicated to free markets, economic growth, constitutionally limited government, property rights, and individual freedom. Its charitable sister organization is the American Commitment Foundation. It is unrelated to the American College & University Presidents' Climate Commitment.

**Who funds American Commitment?**
As a 501(c)(4), it is not legally required to disclose its donors. The Center for Public Integrity found that 87% of its $13 million in 2011–2013 funding came from three Koch-connected nonprofits, and trade groups PhRMA and the National Cable and Telecommunications Association also contributed.

**What did its AARP research claim?**
Its commissioned "Commitment to Seniors" research claims AARP's corporate-royalty economy dwarfs its dues income: a $9.062 billion UnitedHealth advance in 2024, $135.8 million more in employee pay than dues, $17.6 billion in royalties over 25 years, and an estimated $10.8 billion from UnitedHealth since 2007.

**Has that research been independently verified?**
No tier1 or major outlet has independently audited the Form 990 arithmetic. The figures come from research commissioned by American Commitment itself (Juniper Research Group) and have been published only by the organization, the research's author, and advocacy outlets such as AMAC.

**Is American Commitment linked to the Koch brothers?**
The Center for Public Integrity traced most of its 2011–2013 funding to three Koch-connected nonprofits, but when the *Washington Post* asked directly, Kerpen declined to confirm or deny Koch funding, saying he takes the privacy of all donors very seriously as a matter of principle.

## What this profile does not claim

- The $135.8 million, $17.6 billion, and $10.8 billion figures are presented strictly as the findings of research commissioned by American Commitment. No independent outlet has audited that Form 990 arithmetic, and this profile's writer did not do so; they should not be treated as independently verified facts.
- AARP's own response to the royalty-economics claims could not be verified from a fully-read source, so none is quoted or characterized here.
- Current revenue, staff size, and full board composition beyond the president could not be verified from a fully-read source, so none is stated.
- A third-party claim that consultant Sean Noble "launched" the organization was not verified through a fully-read tier1/tier2 source; this profile states only his documented role heading the Center to Protect Patient Rights, a major early donor organization.
