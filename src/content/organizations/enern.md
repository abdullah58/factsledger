## Overview

Enern (Enern Investments) is a European venture and growth capital group formed in Prague in 2010 by Petr Šmída, Pavel Mucha, Radek Švec and Radovan Nesrsta, with Tomáš Čupr becoming the fifth partner in August 2015 ([Miton.cz](https://www.miton.cz/en/blog/slevomat-to-be-acquired-by-uk-based-secret-escapes)). It is the least documented of [Booksy](/organizations/booksy/)'s investors — there is almost no standalone English-language coverage of the firm itself — but its deal record is clear: it led DocPlanner's €15 million Series D in 2017, co-led Booksy's $28.5 million round in September 2019, and was an early backer of the online grocer Rohlik ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/23/docplanner-d/), [Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2021/01/29/booksy-slots-in-70m/)). Its venture arm has since evolved into Kaya VC. This is the Prague investment group, not EnerNOC, the defunct US energy software company.

## A firm known through its deals

Enern appears in the record almost entirely inside portfolio-company funding news, which is itself the finding: the firm has no tier1 or tier2 profile of its own. What can be verified is the shape of it. It was established in Prague in 2010 by four founders — Šmída, Mucha, Švec and Nesrsta — and Čupr joined as the fifth partner in August 2015 ([Miton.cz](https://www.miton.cz/en/blog/slevomat-to-be-acquired-by-uk-based-secret-escapes)). Its ET2 fund invested €15 million in companies including Rohlik.cz, Twisto, the Polish-Turkish Lavito, the French Star of Service, Flatio, Joymile, Nestpick and ROI Hunter, and its ET3 fund of €50 million was targeted at fast-growing firms such as Poland-based DocPlanner ([Miton.cz](https://www.miton.cz/en/blog/slevomat-to-be-acquired-by-uk-based-secret-escapes)). The founding facts rest on Miton.cz, a co-investor's release, corroborated by industry listings that describe Enern as "a regional financial group formed in Prague in 2010".

Partech, which led Rohlik's €190 million round in 2021, describes Enern in its own release as a "European venture and growth capital group" and "key partner early on to CEE champions such as Booksy, DocPlanner, and Rohlik" ([Partech](https://partechpartners.com/news/online-grocery-leader-rohlik-raises-190-million-to-conquer-the-european-market)). That sentence is the best one-line summary of the firm: an early partner to the Central and Eastern European champions, rather than a lead investor in the largest rounds.

## The signature deals

Enern's signature deal is DocPlanner. In May 2017 Enern Investments led the healthcare booking platform's €15 million Series D with Target Global and One Peak Partners, when DocPlanner had raised about €46 million in total; the money was for offices in [Brazil](/places/brazil/) and [Mexico](/places/mexico/) and for sales and marketing in Southern Europe and Latin America ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/23/docplanner-d/)). Partner Pavel Mucha said the firm believed "the time has come for uprising SaaS technologies to change the way we interact with doctors" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/23/docplanner-d/)). Two years later Enern participated in DocPlanner's €80 million Series E, led by One Peak Partners and Goldman Sachs Private Capital, "with existing investors Piton Capital and ENERN Investments also participating", bringing DocPlanner's total raised to about €130 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/05/14/docplanner-series-e/?fbclid=IwAR0BCvLdGeOH3NZOMQgPmfsOGuJWraUCT-a5tC5dv72mfe0b-YTalfAcQB8&utm_campaign=Last+week+in+the+Spanish+startup+ecosystem&utm_medium=email&utm_source=Revue+newsletter)).

The Booksy round came four months after the DocPlanner Series E: in September 2019 Booksy collected $28.5 million in a round co-led by Piton Capital, Enern, [Industry Ventures](/organizations/industry-ventures/) and XG Ventures ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2021/01/29/booksy-slots-in-70m/)). Enern also participated in Booksy's $70 million Series C in January 2021 ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2021/01/29/booksy-slots-in-70m/)).

Rohlik is the third leg of the record. Enern, described in the Partech release as "Enern Miton", was an existing investor in Rohlik's €190 million round in March 2021, led by Partech with Index Ventures, the EBRD, Quadrille, J&T Banka and R2G; Rohlik's revenue for 2020 was €300 million, up 101%, with 750,000 customers ([Partech](https://partechpartners.com/news/online-grocery-leader-rohlik-raises-190-million-to-conquer-the-european-market)). An earlier Enern investment was Slevomat, the Czech daily-deals site, where Enern invested in 2011 alongside Miton and In-Bridge and held 36% with Čupr before Slevomat was sold to Secret Escapes ([Miton.cz](https://www.miton.cz/en/blog/slevomat-to-be-acquired-by-uk-based-secret-escapes)). The ET2 fund's other investments — Twisto, the Polish-Turkish Lavito, the French Star of Service, Flatio, Joymile, Nestpick and ROI Hunter — were the same kind of early bet on a regional service company ([Miton.cz](https://www.miton.cz/en/blog/slevomat-to-be-acquired-by-uk-based-secret-escapes)).

## The Kaya VC succession

The reason the name "Enern" fades from later coverage is that its venture arm evolved into Kaya VC, a dedicated successor vehicle that originated as Enern Investments in 2010. The succession is reported in industry listings rather than verified from a fully-read source, so this profile states it only as reported and flags it as unverified in the open items. The practical consequence is that Enern's deal record — DocPlanner, Booksy, Rohlik — now reads as the first chapter of Kaya VC's history rather than as a standalone firm's.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Enern?**
A European venture and growth capital group formed in Prague in 2010, investing in Central and Eastern European technology companies ([Miton.cz](https://www.miton.cz/en/blog/slevomat-to-be-acquired-by-uk-based-secret-escapes)).

**What is Enern's link to Booksy?**
It co-led Booksy's $28.5 million round in September 2019 with Piton Capital, Industry Ventures and XG Ventures, and participated in the $70 million Series C in 2021 ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2021/01/29/booksy-slots-in-70m/)).

**What is Kaya VC?**
The successor vehicle of Enern's venture arm, which originated as Enern Investments in 2010; the date and terms of the succession were not verified from a fully-read source.

**What are Enern's best-known deals?**
Leading DocPlanner's €15 million Series D in 2017 and participating in its €80 million Series E in 2019, co-leading Booksy's 2019 round, and backing Rohlik early ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/05/23/docplanner-d/), [Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2021/01/29/booksy-slots-in-70m/)).

**Is Enern the same as EnerNOC?**
No. EnerNOC was a US energy software company, now defunct. Enern is the Prague investment group.
