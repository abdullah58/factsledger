The most revealing moment in the OPEC Fund for International Development's recent history came at its 50th birthday party. On June 23-24, 2026, the Fund's 47th Ministerial Council convened in Vienna alongside the OPEC Fund Development Forum, and Kuwait's finance minister Dr. [Yaqoub Al-Rifai](/people/yaqoub-al-rifai/) led his country's delegation to mark the milestone. But the substance of the session was not self-congratulation: the council weighed a proposal from Saudi Arabia to commission an independent review of the structure and operational mechanisms of the Fund's own Board of Governors ([Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/minister-al-rifai-champions-kuwaits-development-vision-at-opec-fund-council-meeting/)). Kuwait publicly supported the proposal, which aims to strengthen corporate governance and improve decision-making efficiency in line with international best practices. A development bank auditing its own governance at 50 — that is the kind of detail the generic institutional bios, which stop at founding dates and mission statements, never reach.

The same session showed the Fund's political layer at work. Al-Rifai congratulated the institution on its five decades of development achievements and affirmed Kuwait, as a founding member, takes pride in its continued contribution since establishment ([Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/minister-al-rifai-champions-kuwaits-development-vision-at-opec-fund-council-meeting/)). On the sidelines he met Fund President Abdulhamid Alkhalifa to discuss cooperation on capacity building, developing national competencies, and exchanging technical expertise — and separately met Kuwaiti professionals working at the Fund in Vienna, where he emphasized the government's commitment to empowering national talent in international institutions ([Times Kuwait](https://timeskuwait.com/minister-al-rifai-champions-kuwaits-development-vision-at-opec-fund-council-meeting/)).

## An institution with one exclusive mandate

The OPEC Fund's own description carries a claim few development banks can make: it is "the only globally mandated development institution that provides financing from member countries to non-member countries exclusively" ([opecfund.org](https://opecfund.org/news-events/press-releases/2023/opec-fund-loan-to-access-bank-botswana-will-boost-lending-to-women-owned-businesses)). Established in 1976 by the member states of the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries, it exists to channel oil-exporting countries' resources into development projects for everyone else ([opecfund.org](https://opecfund.org/news-events/press-releases/2023/opec-fund-loan-to-access-bank-botswana-will-boost-lending-to-women-owned-businesses)). Its twelve member countries are Algeria, Ecuador, Gabon, Indonesia, Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Libya, Nigeria, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, and Venezuela; all financing is for the exclusive benefit of non-member countries ([OPEC Fund 2025 factsheet](https://publications.opecfund.org/view/877524320)). The headquarters sits at Parkring 8, 1010 Vienna ([OPEC Fund 2025 factsheet](https://publications.opecfund.org/view/877524320)).

Its work is, in its own words, "people-centered, focusing on financing projects that meet essential needs, such as food, energy, infrastructure, employment (particularly relating to MSMEs), clean water and sanitation, healthcare and education" ([opecfund.org](https://opecfund.org/news-events/press-releases/2023/opec-fund-loan-to-access-bank-botswana-will-boost-lending-to-women-owned-businesses)).

## Scale, instruments, and money-raising

The numbers from the Fund's 2025 factsheet give the institution its weight: more than US$32 billion committed across over 4,000 operations in more than 125 partner countries, with a US$20 billion financing target for 2025-2030 ([OPEC Fund 2025 factsheet](https://publications.opecfund.org/view/877524320)). The Fund is rated AA+ by both Fitch and S&P, and has issued seven benchmark bonds in the international capital markets since January 2023 — meaning it now borrows in its own name, not just deploys members' capital ([OPEC Fund 2025 factsheet](https://publications.opecfund.org/view/877524320)).

Its toolkit spans public and private sector loans, policy-based finance, credit lines, trade finance, guarantees, risk-sharing facilities, equity, and grants, often supported by technical assistance ([OPEC Fund 2025 factsheet](https://publications.opecfund.org/view/877524320)). A concrete example: the June 2023 US$20 million loan to Access Bank Botswana — the Fund's first private-sector loan in Botswana — with at least 30 percent directed to women-owned businesses and 70 percent for on-lending to local companies; Alkhalifa signed it in Vienna during the OPEC Fund Development Forum, noting that South-South cooperation is one of the Fund's founding principles ([opecfund.org](https://opecfund.org/news-events/press-releases/2023/opec-fund-loan-to-access-bank-botswana-will-boost-lending-to-women-owned-businesses)).

## Priorities: climate, small islands, and digital

More than 40 percent of the Fund's new financing goes to climate action ([OPEC Fund 2025 factsheet](https://publications.opecfund.org/view/877524320)). Its flagship climate-adjacent vehicle is the Island Resilience Facility, helping 25 Small Island Developing States finance climate-resilient infrastructure, building on over US$2 billion committed to ports, energy, water, health, and education ([OPEC Fund 2025 factsheet](https://publications.opecfund.org/view/877524320)). On the project level: around 250,000 patients a year in Viet Nam now receive care at two hospitals in Tuyen Quang Province and Hanoi, expanded with 260 new beds and modern equipment; a US$50 million loan to Honduras backs reforms protecting women from gender-based violence; Azerbaijan's 240 MW Khizi-Absheron wind power plant, the country's largest, is driving its energy transition; and in Senegal, more than half a million people and farms are benefiting from agricultural modernization ([OPEC Fund 2025 factsheet](https://publications.opecfund.org/view/877524320)).

The 2026 pipeline is already named and priced: the US$1.5 billion Economic Stability, Trade and Resilience Initiative (E-STAR) for energy, commodity, and trade disruptions; the US$1.5 billion Digital Transformation Action Plan for digital infrastructure and connectivity; and the US$3 billion Green-Blue Connect Initiative channeling climate finance into resilient infrastructure, sanitation, water, and energy access ([OPEC Fund 2025 factsheet](https://publications.opecfund.org/view/877524320)).

## Governance and the Forum

The Ministerial Council, which met for its 47th session in June 2025, convenes annually as the Fund's highest governing body, approving audited financial statements, annual reports on activities, and other items submitted by management ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/finance/al-jadaan-calls-for-stronger-global-partnerships-at-opec-fund-forum-2648523)). Its sessions are held at the Fund's headquarters or wherever the council decides ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/finance/al-jadaan-calls-for-stronger-global-partnerships-at-opec-fund-forum-2648523)).

The companion Development Forum that week ran under the theme "A Transformation That Empowers Tomorrow" ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/finance/al-jadaan-calls-for-stronger-global-partnerships-at-opec-fund-forum-2648523)). Saudi Arabia's finance minister Mohammed Al-Jadaan used his opening remarks to argue that the development challenge "is no longer limited to promoting economic growth alone but also includes building economies that are more resilient to global shocks," and that financing strategies must be anchored in beneficiary countries' own priorities rather than imposed from outside ([Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/finance/al-jadaan-calls-for-stronger-global-partnerships-at-opec-fund-forum-2648523)). That message — solutions from the countries themselves — is the philosophy the institution was founded to embody fifty years ago, and it was still being debated, and governed, on stage in Vienna.
