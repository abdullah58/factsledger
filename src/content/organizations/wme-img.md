## Overview

WME-IMG was a name that lasted about three years — and in that time it pulled off the biggest sports deal anyone had ever seen. The company was formed when Hollywood talent agency William Morris Endeavor bought IMG Worldwide, the global sports, events, media and fashion business, for $2.4 billion in 2014, with Ari Emanuel and Patrick Whitesell running the combined company as co-CEOs ([SportsBusiness Journal](https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Articles/2025/12/08/influence-125-ari-emanuel-and-patrick-whitesell-wme-imgendeavor/)). In October 2017 the WME-IMG name was retired in favor of a new holding company, Endeavor — Emanuel's own explanation was that "WME-IMG represented a pivotal moment in our company's history, but it does not accurately reflect the extent of our diversity or indicate where we're headed" ([SportsBusiness Journal](https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Daily/Issues/2017/10/10/Labor-and-Agents/Endeavor/)). The name dates back further than either merger: Emanuel and his partners had started a Hollywood talent agency called Endeavor back in 1995, which later merged with the William Morris Agency to create WME, which then acquired IMG ([SportsBusiness Journal](https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Daily/Issues/2017/10/10/Labor-and-Agents/Endeavor/)).

## The $4 billion UFC deal

The defining act of the WME-IMG era came on July 11, 2016, when the company announced it would buy the Ultimate Fighting Championship for about $4 billion — a transaction Reuters called the world's biggest-ever sports deal, surpassing Liberty Media's $2.4 billion purchase of the Atlanta Braves in 2007 ([Reuters](https://www.business-standard.com/article/international/talent-agency-wme-img-to-buy-mixed-martial-arts-group-ufc-116071200029_1.html)). WME-IMG didn't buy it alone: private equity firms Silver Lake Partners and KKR joined as strategic investors, while MSD Capital and MSD Partners — the investment firms of billionaire Michael Dell and his family — provided preferred equity financing ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/ufc-sold-to-wme-img-led-buyers-for-4-billion-report/)).

The deal came together fast and in public. It was announced the day after the franchise hosted UFC 200 — one of the biggest events in its history — at the T-Mobile Arena in Las Vegas ([Reuters](https://www.business-standard.com/article/international/talent-agency-wme-img-to-buy-mixed-martial-arts-group-ufc-116071200029_1.html)). WME-IMG hadn't been the only suitor: Reuters reported in May 2016 that the franchise had drawn interest from Chinese firms China Media Capital and Dalian Wanda Group as well as U.S. private equity firm Blackstone Group ([Reuters](https://www.business-standard.com/article/international/talent-agency-wme-img-to-buy-mixed-martial-arts-group-ufc-116071200029_1.html)). ESPN called it the most expensive transaction for an organization in the history of sports, and Dana White — who had spent two months denying the UFC was for sale — called the sale bittersweet while insisting fans shouldn't worry: "Exact opposite," he said. "Sport is going to the next level" ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/ufc-sold-to-wme-img-led-buyers-for-4-billion-report/)). Lorenzo Fertitta, who had run the company day-to-day, said the family was "confident that the new ownership team of WME-IMG, with whom we've built a strong relationship over the last several years, is committed to accelerating UFC's global growth," adding that "most importantly, our new owners share the same vision and passion for this organization and its athletes" ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/ufc-sold-to-wme-img-led-buyers-for-4-billion-report/)).

What they were buying was already a content machine. Founded in 1993, the UFC held more than 40 fights a year, broadcast in more than 156 countries to 1.1 billion households ([Reuters](https://www.business-standard.com/article/international/talent-agency-wme-img-to-buy-mixed-martial-arts-group-ufc-116071200029_1.html)). The Fertitta brothers — casino moguls Frank and Lorenzo — had bought the whole thing for $2 million in 2001, a 2,000-fold return in fifteen years ([Reuters](https://www.business-standard.com/article/international/talent-agency-wme-img-to-buy-mixed-martial-arts-group-ufc-116071200029_1.html)). Under the deal, [Dana White](/people/dana-white/) stayed on as [UFC](/organizations/ufc/) president, Lorenzo Fertitta stepped down from day-to-day operations, and both brothers kept passive minority stakes ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/ufc-sold-to-wme-img-led-buyers-for-4-billion-report/)). "We've been fortunate over the years to represent UFC and a number of its remarkable athletes," Emanuel and Whitesell said in the announcement. "We're now committed to pursuing new opportunities for UFC and its talented athletes to ensure the sport's continued growth and success on a global scale" ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/ufc-sold-to-wme-img-led-buyers-for-4-billion-report/)). The buyers' financing came from Barclays, Credit Suisse, Deutsche Bank, Goldman Sachs and KKR Capital Markets; the sellers were advised by the Raine Group and JPMorgan Chase ([Reuters](https://www.business-standard.com/article/international/talent-agency-wme-img-to-buy-mixed-martial-arts-group-ufc-116071200029_1.html)).

## From WME-IMG to Endeavor to TKO

The rebrand a year later made the structure explicit. Under the new Endeavor holding company, WME and IMG operated as standalone businesses under co-presidents Mark Shapiro and Ioris Francini, and the wholly owned portfolio included the UFC, the Professional Bull Riders tour, the Miss Universe Organization and the arts firm Frieze, alongside assets like ad agency Droga5, Dixon Talent, the Wall Group, Euroleague basketball and ELeague ([SportsBusiness Journal](https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Daily/Issues/2017/10/10/Labor-and-Agents/Endeavor/)). Emanuel became CEO and Whitesell executive chair, both keeping board seats ([SportsBusiness Journal](https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Daily/Issues/2017/10/10/Labor-and-Agents/Endeavor/)).

The UFC bet paid off the way the buyers hoped. SportsBusiness Journal later called the $4 billion acquisition "one of the industry's greatest success stories," noting that at its height Endeavor had tendrils in everything from athlete representation and event management to youth sports and professional bull riding ([SportsBusiness Journal](https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Articles/2025/12/08/influence-125-ari-emanuel-and-patrick-whitesell-wme-imgendeavor/)). Emanuel ended up atop an even bigger structure: as executive chair of the WME Group, he sits over [TKO Group Holdings](/organizations/tko-group-holdings/), the $39 billion parent of both the UFC and [WWE](/organizations/wwe/) ([SportsBusiness Journal](https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com/Articles/2025/12/08/influence-125-ari-emanuel-and-patrick-whitesell-wme-imgendeavor/)). The three-year WME-IMG interlude — a hyphenated name bridging a talent agency and a sports empire — turned out to be the hinge the whole thing swung on.

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim a precise closing date for the UFC acquisition — the fully-read sources give only the July 11, 2016 announcement. It does not resolve the $2.4 billion versus $2.3 billion variance in reported IMG acquisition prices, or the 2014-versus-2015 variance in the acquisition year; it follows SportsBusiness Journal's $2.4 billion/2014 account. It does not assert that the WME-IMG name vanished from every legal entity in 2017. Endeavor's later IPO, the 2023 UFC–WWE combination into TKO, and the subsequent take-private are not covered here beyond what the cited sources state; the TKO profile carries that story.
