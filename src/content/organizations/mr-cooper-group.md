## Overview

Mr. Cooper Group was the largest home-loan servicer in the [United States](/places/united-states/), a Coppell, [Texas](/places/texas/) servicer for more than 6 million homeowners before Rocket Companies bought it in 2025. It began as Nationstar Mortgage, rebranded as Mr. Cooper in 2017, and became Mr. Cooper Group through a 2018 merger with WMI Holdings, the successor to Washington Mutual. Rocket closed its acquisition on 1 October 2025 and said it would retire the Mr. Cooper brand under the Rocket name ([Rocket Companies](https://www.rocketcompanies.com/press-release/rocket-companies-closes-14-2-billion-acquisition-of-mr-cooper/)).

The company's growth was driven by buying servicing rights rather than by originating most of its own loans. Its servicing portfolio stood at $1,239 billion with 5.4 million customers at 30 September 2024, and the Flagstar Bank acquisition weeks later pushed it past 6 million customers ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/mr-cooper-group-reports-third-quarter-2024-results-2024-10-23); [National Mortgage Professional](http://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/mr-cooper-group-serves-6m-customers-after-acquiring-flagstar-bank)). Its brands were Mr. Cooper, Xome and Rushmore Servicing, and its chairman and chief executive was Jay Bray, who moved to lead Rocket Mortgage when the Rocket deal closed ([Rocket Companies](https://www.rocketcompanies.com/press-release/rocket-companies-closes-14-2-billion-acquisition-of-mr-cooper/)).

## From Nationstar to Mr. Cooper

Nationstar Mortgage officially became Mr. Cooper on 21 August 2017, with the new brand covering both mortgage servicing and originations ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/41047-nationstar-no-more-nonbank-is-now-officially-mr-cooper/)). The change had been a long time coming: it was first reported in December 2015, launched internally in January 2016, and was repeatedly delayed because, as chief executive Jay Bray put it, the company was reimagining how it operated rather than simply changing its name ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/41047-nationstar-no-more-nonbank-is-now-officially-mr-cooper/)). The rebrand came with operational changes — customer-service operations moved back to the United States, online transaction fees for on-time payments were eliminated, and staff completed more than 50,000 hours of customer-experience training ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/41047-nationstar-no-more-nonbank-is-now-officially-mr-cooper/)). At the time the company served about 2.7 million customers with about 7,000 team members, and it also unveiled a mortgage-linked credit card whose rewards went toward a borrower's principal balance ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/41047-nationstar-no-more-nonbank-is-now-officially-mr-cooper/)).

## The WMIH merger and the group name

A year later the corporate structure caught up with the brand. WMIH Inc., the successor company to Washington Mutual — the banking giant that failed in the Great Recession in 2008 — formally acquired Nationstar Mortgage in a deal completed in late July 2018, with Nationstar emerging as the nominal survivor of the deal ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/reverse-mortgage-servicer-champion-has-new-corporate-parent/)). Shareholders of both firms had approved the merger at the end of June 2018, and Bray stayed on to lead the combined company ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/reverse-mortgage-servicer-champion-has-new-corporate-parent/)). The combined firm was to adopt the name Mr. Cooper Group Inc., trading on the [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/) under the WMIH symbol in the near term before shifting to the new COOP ticker ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/reverse-mortgage-servicer-champion-has-new-corporate-parent/)). The deal also brought the reverse-mortgage servicer Champion Mortgage, which Nationstar had owned since 2007, under the new corporate umbrella ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/reverse-mortgage-servicer-champion-has-new-corporate-parent/)).

## Building the largest servicer in the country

Mr. Cooper grew into the largest US servicer mainly by acquiring other companies' servicing books. In May 2023 it merged with Home Point Capital, and in July 2023 it acquired all the equity interests of Roosevelt Management Company, including Rushmore Loan Management Services ([National Mortgage Professional](http://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/mr-cooper-group-serves-6m-customers-after-acquiring-flagstar-bank)). The biggest step came in November 2024, when it closed its acquisition of Flagstar Bank's mortgage operations — servicing rights, advances, subservicing contracts and its third-party origination platform — for about $1.3 billion in cash ([National Mortgage Professional](http://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/mr-cooper-group-serves-6m-customers-after-acquiring-flagstar-bank)). The Flagstar deal added roughly 600,000 customers to the 5.4 million already being served, taking the total past 6 million, with integration onto Mr. Cooper's platform expected in early 2025 ([National Mortgage Professional](http://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/mr-cooper-group-serves-6m-customers-after-acquiring-flagstar-bank)). The scale showed in the numbers: in the third quarter of 2024 the servicing portfolio ended at $1,239 billion, up 32% year over year, with a mortgage servicing rights carrying value of $10,035 million, while originations funded 25,582 loans totalling about $6.8 billion ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/mr-cooper-group-reports-third-quarter-2024-results-2024-10-23)).

## The [Title365](/organizations/title365/) round trip

Title365 shows how Mr. Cooper treated non-core businesses: buy cheap, sell dear. Nationstar's Solutionstar Holdings subsidiary bought the title insurance and settlement provider in 2015 for just $36 million in cash ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-breaks-into-title-insurance-with-latest-acquisition/)). In March 2021 [Blend](/organizations/blend/) agreed to acquire a 90% stake in Title365 from Mr. Cooper Group for approximately $422 million, reflecting a $500 million enterprise value, with Mr. Cooper retaining a 9.9% ownership interest and expecting a $350 million after-tax gain ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-breaks-into-title-insurance-with-latest-acquisition/)). Bray said it would let Mr. Cooper focus on its core servicing and originations business ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-breaks-into-title-insurance-with-latest-acquisition/)). Blend financed the deal with a $225 million term loan and $25 million of revolving credit; Title365 had generated $212 million of revenue in 2020 from six of the top 12 US mortgage lenders as clients ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/06/09/covius-inks-agreement-to-acquire-blends-title365-business/)). But rising rates then curtailed title-insurance demand: Title365 generated $46.3 million for Blend in 2024, down 59% from $113.6 million in 2022 ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/06/09/covius-inks-agreement-to-acquire-blends-title365-business/)). In June 2025 Covius Holdings agreed to acquire Title365 from Blend on undisclosed terms ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/06/09/covius-inks-agreement-to-acquire-blends-title365-business/)).

## The 2020 servicing settlement

On 7 December 2020 the [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](/organizations/consumer-financial-protection-bureau/) filed a complaint against Nationstar Mortgage LLC, doing business as Mr. Cooper; the court entered the settlement order the next day ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/nationstar-mortgage-llc-dba-mr-cooper/)). The order required about $73 million in redress to more than 40,000 harmed borrowers plus a $1.5 million civil penalty, in a coordinated settlement with attorneys general from all 50 states and the District of Columbia and bank regulators from 53 jurisdictions ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/nationstar-mortgage-llc-dba-mr-cooper/)). The bureau alleged violations of the Consumer Financial Protection Act, the Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act and the Homeowner's Protection Act, covering conduct from January 2012 to January 2016 ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/consumer-financial-protection-bureau-multiple-states-enter-settlement-nationstar-mortgage-llc-unlawful-servicing-practices/)). Nationstar allegedly failed to identify loans with pending loss-mitigation applications, foreclosed on borrowers it had promised not to foreclose on, mishandled private mortgage insurance cancellation, and paid escrow taxes late ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/consumer-financial-protection-bureau-multiple-states-enter-settlement-nationstar-mortgage-llc-unlawful-servicing-practices/)). Together the orders yielded nearly $85 million in consumer recoveries and over $6 million more in fees and penalties ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/nationstar-mortgage-llc-dba-mr-cooper/)).

## Bought by Rocket

On 31 March 2025 Rocket Companies agreed to acquire Mr. Cooper Group in an all-stock deal valued at $9.4 billion, at 11.0 Rocket shares per Mr. Cooper share — $143.33 per share, a 35% premium to the 30-day volume-weighted average price — plus a $2.00 per-share special dividend ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/933136/000119312525067734/d941832dex992.htm)). The logic: scale — the combined book would exceed $2.1 trillion across nearly 10 million clients, one in every six US mortgages, with about $500 million in expected annual synergies ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/933136/000119312525067734/d941832dex992.htm)). Rocket shareholders would own about 75% of the combined company, Mr. Cooper shareholders about 25%; Bray would lead Rocket Mortgage as President and CEO, reporting to Rocket CEO Varun Krishna, with Dan Gilbert remaining chairman ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/933136/000119312525067734/d941832dex992.htm)). It closed on 1 October 2025 at $14.2 billion — higher than announced, as the fixed share ratio gained value with Rocket's price — which Rocket called the largest independent mortgage deal ever ([Rocket Companies](https://www.rocketcompanies.com/press-release/rocket-companies-closes-14-2-billion-acquisition-of-mr-cooper/)). Mr. Cooper was to be rebranded under Rocket, months after Rocket closed its Redfin acquisition in July 2025 ([Rocket Companies](https://www.rocketcompanies.com/press-release/rocket-companies-closes-14-2-billion-acquisition-of-mr-cooper/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Mr. Cooper Group?** Mr. Cooper Group was the largest home-loan servicer in the United States, a Coppell, Texas company operating under the Mr. Cooper, Xome and Rushmore Servicing brands ([Rocket Companies](https://www.rocketcompanies.com/press-release/rocket-companies-closes-14-2-billion-acquisition-of-mr-cooper/)). It collected mortgage payments and managed escrow, taxes and insurance for more than 6 million homeowners before Rocket Companies acquired it in 2025 ([National Mortgage Professional](http://nationalmortgageprofessional.com/news/mr-cooper-group-serves-6m-customers-after-acquiring-flagstar-bank)).

**Why was it called Mr. Cooper?** Nationstar Mortgage rebranded as Mr. Cooper on 21 August 2017, choosing a person's name to "personify the next generation of home loan servicing and lending" and signal a more personal relationship with borrowers ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/41047-nationstar-no-more-nonbank-is-now-officially-mr-cooper/)). The group name Mr. Cooper Group Inc. followed the 2018 merger with WMI Holdings, the successor to Washington Mutual ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/reverse-mortgage-servicer-champion-has-new-corporate-parent/)).

**How much did Rocket pay for Mr. Cooper?** Rocket announced the all-stock deal at $9.4 billion in March 2025 and closed it at $14.2 billion on 1 October 2025 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/933136/000119312525067734/d941832dex992.htm); [Rocket Companies](https://www.rocketcompanies.com/press-release/rocket-companies-closes-14-2-billion-acquisition-of-mr-cooper/)). The exchange ratio was fixed at 11 Rocket shares per Mr. Cooper share, so the headline value rose with Rocket's share price between announcement and close.

**What happened to Title365?** Mr. Cooper sold a 90% stake in Title365 to Blend in 2021 for about $422 million, keeping a 9.9% interest, after buying the title business for $36 million in 2015 ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/blend-breaks-into-title-insurance-with-latest-acquisition/)). Blend agreed in June 2025 to sell Title365 to Covius Holdings on undisclosed terms after its revenue fell 59% from 2022 to 2024 ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/06/09/covius-inks-agreement-to-acquire-blends-title365-business/)).

**Was Mr. Cooper ever penalised by regulators?** Yes. In December 2020 the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and state regulators settled with Nationstar, doing business as Mr. Cooper, over alleged 2012–2016 servicing failures, requiring about $73 million in borrower redress and a $1.5 million penalty ([Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/nationstar-mortgage-llc-dba-mr-cooper/)). Allegations included missed loss-mitigation handling, wrongful foreclosures and late escrow tax payments.
