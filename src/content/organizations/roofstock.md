## Overview

Roofstock is an Oakland, California-based online marketplace where buyers and sellers trade single-family rental homes in dozens of U.S. markets — often with tenants still residing in them ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/01/07/roofstock-which-makes-it-easier-to-buy-a-home-as-an-investment-property-just-raised-50-million-in-new-funding/)). The company was founded around 2015 — it was described as "four-year-old" in January 2020 — by Gary Beasley, who serves as CEO, and Gregor Watson, its co-founder and chairman ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/01/07/roofstock-which-makes-it-easier-to-buy-a-home-as-an-investment-property-just-raised-50-million-in-new-funding/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/05/26/roofstock-founder-closes-on-90m-venture-fund-to-back-early-stage-proptech-startups/)).

## Turning occupied homes into tradable assets

Roofstock's structural innovation was making tenant-occupied homes listable: both institutional and retail investors can buy and sell homes without forcing renters to leave, and buyers generate rental income from day one — something TechCrunch noted was "difficult to do through traditional channels" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/01/07/roofstock-which-makes-it-easier-to-buy-a-home-as-an-investment-property-just-raised-50-million-in-new-funding/)). The company makes money per transaction — 2.5% from sellers and 0.5% from buyers — and layers on data tools (comparable sales, neighborhood ratings, valuation estimates built partly on CoreLogic, HouseCanary, and Zillow data) to help sellers price listings ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/01/07/roofstock-which-makes-it-easier-to-buy-a-home-as-an-investment-property-just-raised-50-million-in-new-funding/)).

By January 2020 the model had traction: a $50 million Series D led by SVB Capital brought total funding to $133 million, the marketplace spanned more than 70 U.S. markets with over $2 billion in transactions, and a run rate of about 500 home transactions per month ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/01/07/roofstock-which-makes-it-easier-to-buy-a-home-as-an-investment-property-just-raised-50-million-in-new-funding/)). Its users skewed young and new to the asset class — 75% were first-time real estate investors and more than half were under 35 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/01/07/roofstock-which-makes-it-easier-to-buy-a-home-as-an-investment-property-just-raised-50-million-in-new-funding/)).

## The $1.9 billion peak and the layoffs that followed

In March 2022 Roofstock raised $240 million at a $1.9 billion valuation — described by TechCrunch as an online marketplace for investing in leased single-family rental homes ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/05/26/roofstock-founder-closes-on-90m-venture-fund-to-back-early-stage-proptech-startups/)). Within a year the cycle had turned: after a first cut of about 20% of staff, the company laid off roughly another 27% — about 140 employees — in March 2023, with Beasley saying the reduction was "in response to the challenging macro environment" and needed to "reduce cash burn rate" and preserve "adequate capital runway until the market eventually turns" ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/companies/fintech-firm-roofstock-lays-off-27-of-staff-in-second-round-of-job-cuts-123032301130_1.html)).
