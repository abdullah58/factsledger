## Overview

Fairfax Financial Holdings is the Toronto-based insurance conglomerate its founder calls "Canada's Berkshire Hathaway" — and unlike most such comparisons, the resemblance is structural. Like Warren Buffett's company, Fairfax runs on insurance float: premiums collected months or years before claims are paid, which the company invests in undervalued assets, letting underwriting profits and investment returns reinforce each other ([The Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/news/international/business/prem-watsa-of-fairfax-how-an-indian-became-canadas-warren-buffett/amp_articleshow/125296209.cms), [Intelligent Investor](https://www.intelligentinvestor.com.au/recommendations/fairfax-financial-canadas-berkshire-hathaway/152381)). Founded in 1985 by Prem Watsa as a small insurance operation, it now carries roughly $100 billion in assets and held $31 billion of float in 2022 at a 95% combined ratio — meaning the company was, in effect, paid to borrow ([The Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/news/international/business/prem-watsa-of-fairfax-how-an-indian-became-canadas-warren-buffett/amp_articleshow/125296209.cms), [Intelligent Investor](https://www.intelligentinvestor.com.au/recommendations/fairfax-financial-canadas-berkshire-hathaway/152381)).

## The $8 immigrant and the bankrupt truck insurer

Prem Watsa left Hyderabad in the early 1970s with, by his own telling, just $8 in his pocket, sold air conditioners and furnaces to fund his MBA at the University of Western Ontario, and joined Confederation Life Insurance in Toronto as a research analyst in 1974 ([The Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/news/international/business/prem-watsa-of-fairfax-how-an-indian-became-canadas-warren-buffett/amp_articleshow/125296209.cms)). Under the mentorship of John Watson he absorbed the value-investing doctrine of Benjamin Graham — buy undervalued companies with strong fundamentals, hold them for the long term — which became the bedrock of his philosophy ([The Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/news/international/business/prem-watsa-of-fairfax-how-an-indian-became-canadas-warren-buffett/amp_articleshow/125296209.cms)).

In 1984 Watsa struck out on his own with Hamblin Watsa Investment Counsel. A year later came the breakthrough: he took control of Markel Financial, a failing Canadian trucking insurer on the brink of bankruptcy, and in 1987 reorganized and renamed it Fairfax Financial Holdings — short for "fair, friendly acquisitions" ([The Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/news/international/business/prem-watsa-of-fairfax-how-an-indian-became-canadas-warren-buffett/amp_articleshow/125296209.cms)). For the first 15 years he barely spoke to reporters and only began hosting investor calls in 2001, earning the magazine label "the richest, savviest guy you've never heard of" ([The Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/news/international/business/prem-watsa-of-fairfax-how-an-indian-became-canadas-warren-buffett/amp_articleshow/125296209.cms)).

## The call that made him — and the decade it cost

Watsa built his notoriety by predicting the 2008 crash. In 2007 he warned, "when the music stops, it stops very quickly" — and when markets fell 50%, Fairfax's equity hedges and credit default swaps netted it a $4.6 billion portfolio return across 2007–08 despite the tanking market ([Intelligent Investor](https://www.intelligentinvestor.com.au/recommendations/fairfax-financial-canadas-berkshire-hathaway/152381)). He had earlier called the 1987 crash and Japan's 1990 collapse ([The Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/news/international/business/prem-watsa-of-fairfax-how-an-indian-became-canadas-warren-buffett/amp_articleshow/125296209.cms)).

But the triumph seeded a costly mistake. Emboldened by the payoff, Watsa doubled down on hedges in the years that followed, when protection was expensive; when no second crisis arrived, the contracts proved worthless, and poor stock picks — notably BlackBerry — compounded the damage ([Intelligent Investor](https://www.intelligentinvestor.com.au/recommendations/fairfax-financial-canadas-berkshire-hathaway/152381)). Investment returns that had averaged about 9% from 1986 to 2016 fell to roughly 3.5% over the following decade — a "lost decade" ([Intelligent Investor](https://www.intelligentinvestor.com.au/recommendations/fairfax-financial-canadas-berkshire-hathaway/152381)). Watsa has since abandoned the hedging strategy — "will never be repeated," he told an annual meeting — and rebuilt the underwriting machine: nine of the company's ten profitable five-year underwriting periods came in the most recent decade ([Intelligent Investor](https://www.intelligentinvestor.com.au/recommendations/fairfax-financial-canadas-berkshire-hathaway/152381)). By 2025 Fairfax's shares had quadrupled since October 2022, and the 75-year-old Watsa disclosed his succession plan in the book *The Fairfax Way*: his son Ben is slated to become chairman ([The Economic Times](https://m.economictimes.com/news/international/business/prem-watsa-of-fairfax-how-an-indian-became-canadas-warren-buffett/amp_articleshow/125296209.cms)).
