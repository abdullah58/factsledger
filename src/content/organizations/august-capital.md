## Overview

August Capital is a venture capital firm based in Menlo Park, California, in the [United States](/places/united-states/), founded in 1995 by David Marquardt and John Johnston, investing primarily in early-stage information-technology companies ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)). It manages approximately $2 billion and is best known for a small number of very large bets made patiently: Splunk, whose first round it funded in 2004 and which went public eight years later; the $20 billion Seagate privatization of 2000, in which it invested around $135 million; and Atheros, the wireless chipmaker Qualcomm bought for $3.1 billion in 2011 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2011/01/05/qualcomm_buys_atheros/)). In the FactsLedger graph it is the firm that led [Avant](/organizations/avant/)'s $9 million Series A in April 2013 and co-led its $20 million Series B that August with [Victory Park Capital](/organizations/victory-park-capital/) ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/online-lender-avantcredit-secures-34m-in-funding); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/08/14/avantcredit-raises-20m-to-grow-its-machine-powered-online-lending-platform/)).

The firm's identity is inseparable from its founder's first deal. In 1981, fourteen years before August Capital existed, Marquardt's earlier firm invested $1 million for 5% of Microsoft — the software company's only venture investor — and Marquardt sat on Microsoft's board for the next 33 years ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)). August Capital was built to repeat that kind of relationship: a small, flat partnership that backs founders early, stays for a decade, and measures itself by helpfulness rather than by brand. "Our legacy is Microsoft, Splunk, Sun, and Zulily," partner David Hornik said in 2014, "but just because you didn't turn out only billion-dollar-plus businesses doesn't mean that you aren't making a difference and adding value" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)).

## The Microsoft origin

August Capital begins with a phone call. In 1980, David Marquardt was a 30-year-old venture capitalist at Technology Venture Investors (TVI), a firm he had co-founded that year, when he heard through the grapevine that a young entrepreneur named "Billy Gates" was having success with a startup called Microsoft ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)). Marquardt called Steve Ballmer — a former Stanford Business School classmate working for Gates — to get a meeting. He was a rarity in a venture world filled with ex-bankers: a design engineer, deeply technical, who had worked on disk-drive and printer programs at Diablo Systems before business school ([August Capital](https://www.augustcap.com/team/david-marquardt/)). That resonated with Gates and Ballmer, and Marquardt spent the next year travelling to Redmond almost every weekend — to football games, to brainstorming sessions — until he sealed the deal: in 1981 TVI invested $1 million for 5% of Microsoft in its Series A, as the company's sole venture investor ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)).

Marquardt served on Microsoft's board from 1981 until 2014, and the relationship set the template for everything August Capital later did ([August Capital](https://www.augustcap.com/team/david-marquardt/)). He had co-founded TVI in 1980 and been involved in four funds that invested in more than 100 start-up and emerging-growth companies, including the Series B in Sun Microsystems — he had gone to business school with Sun co-founder Vinod Khosla ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)). When Marquardt and fellow TVI colleague John Johnston founded August Capital in 1995, they had already backed Microsoft and Sun; their TVI colleague Bob Kagel went on to found Benchmark ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)). The first August fund closed at around $100 million, and Andy Rappaport joined soon after; Andrew Anker followed in 1998 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)).

## Early wins: Cobalt, Seagate and Atheros

The firm's first fund was carried by Cobalt Networks, a developer of low-cost Linux-based servers and one of August's earliest investments. Cobalt went public and was then sold to Sun Microsystems for $2 billion — a result that, in Marquardt's telling, "drove the firm's first fund, and made it easy for the second fund to be raised" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)). The firm concentrated on enterprise bets and largely sat out the dot-com bubble's consumer excesses, though Marquardt concedes its mistakes "just weren't that consequential to overall performance" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)).

Its most audacious deal came in 2000. After raising a third fund in 1999, August — a firm that generally did not do buyouts — joined a consortium including Silver Lake to take storage company Seagate private for $20 billion, putting around $135 million into the deal ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)). "When we did the deal at Seagate, everyone thought we were crazy. It took courage to put that much money into hardware when everyone was fleeing," Marquardt said. Seagate sold part of the company to Veritas, and the remainder went public again in 2002 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)).

Atheros, the wireless chipmaker the firm cites as a signature investment — "revolutionizing wireless data transmission" — was its next hardware-scale exit ([August Capital](https://www.augustcap.com/about/)). In January 2011 Qualcomm agreed to buy Atheros for $3.1 billion, or $45 a share, in what was then Qualcomm's largest acquisition ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2011/01/05/qualcomm_buys_atheros/)). Atheros, based in San Jose, had booked nine consecutive years of revenue growth, employed more than 1,700 people, and had trailing sales of $885.7 million with net income of $93.2 million ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2011/01/05/qualcomm_buys_atheros/)).

## Splunk and Zulily: the long hold

Splunk is the firm's defining software investment. David Hornik, who joined August in 2000 after working as a lawyer at board meetings for portfolio company Evite, funded Splunk's first round in 2004 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)). "When [Hornik] funded Splunk in 2004, nobody had ever heard of big data," partner Howard Hartenbaum said in 2012. "Now, Splunk is the poster child for big data" ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/entrepreneur/august-capital-sixth-fund)). Splunk priced its IPO at $17 a share on 19 April 2012, valuing the company at about $1.57 billion and raising $230 million; the shares closed the first day at $35.08, up more than 108%, and August Capital's stake was worth, on paper, north of $570 million — tied with Sevin Rosen Funds as the company's largest shareholder ([AllThingsD](https://allthingsd.com/20120419/and-its-off-splunk-rockets-108-percent-in-ipo-debut/)).

Zulily, the e-commerce company for mothers, showed the same patience in consumer investing. Eric Carlborg brought the deal to the firm on his first day on the job; August led the Series B, and Zulily went public a few years later as a $5 billion company ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)). The pattern — eight years from first cheque to IPO for Splunk, a similar arc for Zulily — is the firm's stated model: "I have no time frame, and no target for what is the outcome of a business when I invest," Hornik said. "I go into every business thinking they could be next Microsoft, Splunk or Zulily, and it takes anywhere from 7 to 13 years to build those kinds of businesses" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)).

## The AvantCredit investment

August Capital's fintech turn came through Avant, the Chicago online lender founded in 2012 as AvantCredit by [Al Goldstein](/people/al-goldstein/), [John Sun](/people/john-sun/) and [Paul Zhang](/people/paul-zhang/) — Sun and Zhang were graduates of [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/08/14/avantcredit-raises-20m-to-grow-its-machine-powered-online-lending-platform/)). In April 2013 AvantCredit completed a $34 million round of equity and debt from August Capital and Victory Park Capital: a $9 million Series A equity investment led by August, plus a $25 million credit facility from Victory Park ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/online-lender-avantcredit-secures-34m-in-funding)). At the time the company offered personal loans of $1,000 to $10,000 to near-prime borrowers, had built a loan portfolio of over $4 million, and planned to grow it to over $1 billion within five years ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/online-lender-avantcredit-secures-34m-in-funding)).

Four months later, in August 2013, AvantCredit raised a further $20 million Series B from the same two investors, August Capital and Victory Park Capital, to expand beyond the 16 U.S. states where it operated, hire talent, and improve the machine-learning system that decided loans in near real time ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/08/14/avantcredit-raises-20m-to-grow-its-machine-powered-online-lending-platform/)). The loans ran from $1,000 to $20,000 over one to five years, at rates of 35–95% — pricing that reflected a near-prime audience banks would not serve at better rates ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/08/14/avantcredit-raises-20m-to-grow-its-machine-powered-online-lending-platform/)). Tripp Jones, August's later hire, worked on the AvantCredit investment for the firm ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)). The investment put August at the start of the online-lending wave — and, through the bank-partnership structure Avant used, at one remove from the "true lender" litigation that later engulfed the company.

## Funds, philosophy and The Lobby

August Capital's fund history runs against the grain of its industry. After a fourth fund of $350 million for early-stage investing plus $250 million for special opportunities, it raised $650 million for its fifth fund in March 2009, then $550 million for its sixth in 2012 — $300 million for early-stage and $250 million for special opportunities ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/entrepreneur/august-capital-sixth-fund); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)). Then it deliberately shrank. Its seventh fund, closed in February 2015, was $450 million: Hornik wrote that the partnership had asked whether to "raise a billion dollar fund and jump on the 'ever bigger' band wagon" or "stick to our knitting," and chose the latter, initially targeting just $400 million so each of the five general partners would have $80 million to deploy ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/02/13/august-capital-raises-450-million-for-its-seventh-fund-august-vii/)).

The operating philosophy behind the numbers is relationship-first. Marquardt's rule is that "the venture business is an intensely personal relationship business. And it's not an industry that scales well" — he would never add a value-added services arm, because "companies should do that themselves," and the CEO, not the VC, makes the decisions ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)). The partnership is flat — seven active partners in 2014, each with equal equity and an equal voice — and partners do their own diligence rather than delegating it to junior associates ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)). Founders notice: one chief executive told [TechCrunch](/organizations/techcrunch/) he took a 10–15% valuation cut to work with August, because its partners had met him at a shopping mall to see his product in use when other firms would not take the time ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)).

The relationship philosophy has an institutional form: The Lobby, the curated, off-the-record, invite-only gathering for founders, CEOs, venture capitalists and thought leaders that the partners created about a decade before 2014 and hold in a tropical location, built around small-group "User Generated Conversations" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)). It is not a money-maker — at best it breaks even, and August sometimes covers the loss — but partnerships and fundings are regularly initiated there, and even competing venture firms are invited ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)). In 2014 the firm added a second Lobby conference focused on enterprise entrepreneurs. August also co-hosts an annual summer cocktail party with TechCrunch for the broader tech community ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/14/sand-hill-roads-consiglieres-august-capital/)).

After Fund VII was fully invested, the partnership's story took an unexpected turn: Hornik and Carlborg decided against raising an eighth August fund and instead founded Lobby Capital, named for the conference, investing in late-seed and Series A software companies ([RevPartners](https://blog.revpartners.io/en/revops-articles/podcast-pit-stop-david-hornik-on-the-investors-perspective-on-founding-a-startup)). Hornik had spent 20 years at August, across funds III through VII. Whether August Capital raised any later fund under other partners was not verified in this pass, and this profile does not claim a current fund status beyond Fund VII.
