## A decade in court — and a $375 million payout

The least-covered chapter of the UFC's modern story is the one that cost it the most. In 2014, fighters led by former champions Cung Le and Nate Quarry filed a class-action antitrust suit accusing the promotion of using monopoly-like tactics — including its acquisitions of rivals WEC in 2010 and Strikeforce in early 2011 — to suppress fighter pay ([InsiderSport](https://insidersport.com/2025/02/07/ufc-to-payout-375m-to-fighters-after-decade-long-antitrust-case/)). The case dragged on for more than a decade: an earlier $335 million settlement reached in March 2024 was rejected by the court, and a revised $375 million agreement followed in October 2024 ([InsiderSport](https://insidersport.com/2025/02/07/ufc-to-payout-375m-to-fighters-after-decade-long-antitrust-case/)). On February 6, 2025, Judge Richard Boulware gave the deal final approval, closing the longest-running legal fight in the sport's history ([InsiderSport](https://insidersport.com/2025/02/07/ufc-to-payout-375m-to-fighters-after-decade-long-antitrust-case/)). A second suit, *Johnson v. Zuffa* — filed in 2017 on behalf of fighters from a later period — was still pending at that point ([InsiderSport](https://insidersport.com/2025/02/07/ufc-to-payout-375m-to-fighters-after-decade-long-antitrust-case/)).

## "Human cockfighting" to $2 million

The promotion that paid that settlement barely survived its own birth. Rorion Gracie and Art Davie joined forces to create the UFC, staging the first event in Denver in November 1993 ([Awful Announcing](https://awfulannouncing.com/si/si-tv-mandalay-sports-media-ufc-1-documentary.html)). The early product was so rough that Senator John McCain branded it "human cockfighting," a campaign that pushed the UFC off pay-per-view in its early years ([Associated Press](https://www.juneauempire.com/2016/07/12/ufc-sold-for-4-billion-white-stays-on-to-run-promotion/)). In 2001, casino executives Lorenzo and Frank Fertitta bought the struggling promotion for $2 million through their Zuffa LLC, with [Dana White](/people/dana-white/) — who had managed fighters and trained in boxing — installed to run it ([Associated Press](https://www.juneauempire.com/2016/07/12/ufc-sold-for-4-billion-white-stays-on-to-run-promotion/)). White has led the promotion ever since.

## The Spike TV turnaround

The gamble paid off on a cable channel few sports executives watched. The finale of the first season of *The Ultimate Fighter* aired live on Spike TV on April 9, 2005, and the light-heavyweight slugfest between Forrest Griffin and Stephan Bonnar — the first live MMA card ever shown on television — turned into a breakout ([Bloody Elbow](https://bloodyelbow.com/2009/12/22/the-real-fight-of-the-decade/)). White later called it "the most significant fight in UFC history," and Spike immediately renewed the show for two more seasons ([Bloody Elbow](https://bloodyelbow.com/2009/12/22/the-real-fight-of-the-decade/)). The stars that followed — Chuck Liddell, Randy Couture, [Tito Ortiz](/people/tito-ortiz/), Conor McGregor, Brock Lesnar, Ronda Rousey — made the UFC appointment viewing ([Associated Press](https://www.juneauempire.com/2016/07/12/ufc-sold-for-4-billion-white-stays-on-to-run-promotion/)). The rebuild years included 2001 cards at Atlantic City's [Trump Taj Mahal](/places/trump-taj-mahal/) — the grind before the television breakthrough. The last legal barrier fell in 2016, when New York lifted its ban on mixed martial arts, making the sport legal in all 50 states ([Associated Press](https://www.juneauempire.com/2016/07/12/ufc-sold-for-4-billion-white-stays-on-to-run-promotion/)).

## The $4 billion sale

By 2016 the UFC was the sport's undisputed center: more than 500 athletes on the roster, roughly 40 live events a year, broadcasts in more than 150 countries to 1.1 billion TV households — and a 180,000-square-foot global headquarters campus under construction in Las Vegas ([Associated Press](https://www.juneauempire.com/2016/07/12/ufc-sold-for-4-billion-white-stays-on-to-run-promotion/)). That July it was sold for roughly $4 billion to a group led by talent agency WME-IMG ([Associated Press](https://www.juneauempire.com/2016/07/12/ufc-sold-for-4-billion-white-stays-on-to-run-promotion/)). White confirmed to the AP he would stay on to run the promotion; the Fertitta brothers kept a minority stake, the Abu Dhabi government retained its 10% interest, and the financial backers included Silver Lake Partners, KKR, and Michael Dell's firm ([Associated Press](https://www.juneauempire.com/2016/07/12/ufc-sold-for-4-billion-white-stays-on-to-run-promotion/)). The numbers from UFC 200 told the story of how far it had come: 18,202 fans and a $10.8 million gate, Nevada records at the time ([Associated Press](https://www.juneauempire.com/2016/07/12/ufc-sold-for-4-billion-white-stays-on-to-run-promotion/)). The sale price was roughly two thousand times the $2 million Zuffa had paid fifteen years earlier.

## Media rights: from Fox to ESPN to Paramount

The WME-IMG era was, above all, a media-rights story. Fox's seven-year TV deal was set to expire in 2018 ([Associated Press](https://www.juneauempire.com/2016/07/12/ufc-sold-for-4-billion-white-stays-on-to-run-promotion/)), and in May of that year the UFC signed a five-year, $1.5 billion agreement with ESPN beginning in 2019: 30 Fight Nights a year — 10 on ESPN's networks, 20 on the ESPN+ streaming service — plus preliminary cards for 12 annual pay-per-view events ([ESPN](http://espn.ph/mma/story/_/id/23581729/espn-ufc-reach-five-year-television-rights-deal)). Fox had been the exclusive TV partner since 2011 ([ESPN](http://espn.ph/mma/story/_/id/23581729/espn-ufc-reach-five-year-television-rights-deal)).

Then came the deal that ended an era. In August 2025 the UFC agreed to a seven-year, $7.7 billion U.S. media-rights deal with Paramount: 13 numbered events and 30 Fight Nights per year, ending the American pay-per-view model that had defined the sport ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/paramount-stream-ufc-main-cards-canada-2027-2026-06-04/)). On October 28, 2025, the deal expanded to Latin America and Australia at the same $7.7 billion / $1.1 billion-per-year framing — with White referenced as "UFC President and CEO" ([InsiderSport](https://insidersport.com/2025/10/29/ufc-paramount-skydance-latam-australia/)). By then the UFC had already become part of [TKO Group Holdings](/organizations/tko-group-holdings/), the Endeavor-built company that combined UFC and [WWE](/organizations/wwe/) in 2023, with White as UFC CEO ([Associated Press](https://www.shootonline.com/article/endeavor-deal-combines-wwe-and-ufc-form-214b-sports-entertainment-company/)). The TKO combination closed on September 12, 2023, at a $21.4 billion combined enterprise value — $12.1 billion of it the UFC itself.

## The USADA split

The one partnership that ended badly was the one meant to keep the sport clean. The UFC's eight-year anti-doping partnership with the U.S. Anti-Doping Agency, begun in 2015, ended on December 31, 2023, after more than 27,000 tests ([Sport Resolutions](https://www.sportresolutions.com/news/us-anti-doping-agency-announces-split-from-ultimate-fighting-championship)). The break came days after Conor McGregor re-entered the testing pool on October 8, 2023: on October 9 the UFC informed USADA it was taking the programme in a new direction, and the two sides went public over the rule requiring returning fighters to spend six months in the pool with two negative tests before competing ([Sport Resolutions](https://www.sportresolutions.com/news/us-anti-doping-agency-announces-split-from-ultimate-fighting-championship)).
