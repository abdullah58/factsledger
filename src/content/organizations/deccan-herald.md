## Overview

**Deccan Herald** is an English-language daily newspaper published from Bengaluru, Karnataka, in [India](/places/india/) — launched in June 1948 by businessman K. N. Guruswamy and published by The Printers (Mysore) Private Limited, a family-owned paper that has stayed in the founder's family for over 75 years and built its reputation on sober, regionally rooted reporting ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/legendary-liquor-baron-s-venture-the-story-of-deccan-herald-s-birth-and-enduring-legacy-in-bengaluru-s-history-101687203141885.html); [afaqs!](https://www.Afaqs.com/news/media/32260_deccan-herald-to-debut-in-delhi-on-december-11)).

Its reporting on national politics is among the coverage cited in accounts of the [2020 Deputy Chairman Rajya Sabha election](/events/2020-deputy-chairman-rajya-sabha-election/), which is why this profile exists in the FactsLedger loop. This profile is about *Deccan Herald* the English daily — not its Kannada sister paper *Prajavani*, and not *Deccan Chronicle*, a separate newspaper.

## From excise halls to the newsroom: the founding

The founding story is the part of this paper's history most coverage skips, and it explains the paper's character. Kanekal Nettakallappa Guruswamy was from Ballari, and his family belonged to the toddy-tapping Ediga caste; as the oldest of six siblings he was inducted into excise auction halls at 18, and after his father's unexpected death in 1928, when Guruswamy was 26, the family business grew until he became a wealthy, influential figure in Bengaluru ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/legendary-liquor-baron-s-venture-the-story-of-deccan-herald-s-birth-and-enduring-legacy-in-bengaluru-s-history-101687203141885.html)). In 1948, Guruswamy risked fortune and reputation on a venture nobody in Mysore state had tried before: a first-class English daily for a city that lacked one ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/legendary-liquor-baron-s-venture-the-story-of-deccan-herald-s-birth-and-enduring-legacy-in-bengaluru-s-history-101687203141885.html)).

The paper began life in a building with a past: a space on South Parade — today's MG Road — that had most recently housed a dance hall called Funnel's, whose Irish owners were selling up as the Raj ended ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/legendary-liquor-baron-s-venture-the-story-of-deccan-herald-s-birth-and-enduring-legacy-in-bengaluru-s-history-101687203141885.html)). After last-minute panic — the original printing press failed and a Kannada press had to be pressed into service — an eight-page tabloid costing one anna a copy was launched in June 1948 ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/legendary-liquor-baron-s-venture-the-story-of-deccan-herald-s-birth-and-enduring-legacy-in-bengaluru-s-history-101687203141885.html)). Guruswamy's biggest triumph, in the Hindustan Times columnist's judgment, was recruiting the first editor, the legendary Pothan Joseph, who had been the founding editor of the Pakistani broadsheet *Dawn*, an editor of the Hindustan Times, and a columnist whose "Over a cup of tea" ran for over half a century ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/legendary-liquor-baron-s-venture-the-story-of-deccan-herald-s-birth-and-enduring-legacy-in-bengaluru-s-history-101687203141885.html)). A few months later came the equally iconic Kannada sister publication, *Prajavani* ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/legendary-liquor-baron-s-venture-the-story-of-deccan-herald-s-birth-and-enduring-legacy-in-bengaluru-s-history-101687203141885.html)). Both papers took their time to turn a profit, but Guruswamy never faltered, according to the same account ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/legendary-liquor-baron-s-venture-the-story-of-deccan-herald-s-birth-and-enduring-legacy-in-bengaluru-s-history-101687203141885.html)).

## Family ownership and the Karnataka base

Deccan Herald remains a family paper. The Hindustan Times column records that it is Guruswamy's three grandsons who continue to bring news of their hometown to nostalgic Bengalureans around the world from 75, MG Road ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/legendary-liquor-baron-s-venture-the-story-of-deccan-herald-s-birth-and-enduring-legacy-in-bengaluru-s-history-101687203141885.html)). The publisher is The Printers (Mysore) Private Limited, which also publishes the Kannada daily *Prajavani*, the weekly magazine *Sudha*, and the monthly magazine *Mayura* ([afaqs!](https://www.Afaqs.com/news/media/32260_deccan-herald-to-debut-in-delhi-on-december-11)). That structure — a single family-owned company running one English and one Kannada daily — has kept the paper anchored in Karnataka rather than absorbed into a national chain.

The paper's Karnataka footprint in 2011 ran through Bengaluru, Hubli, Mysore, Belur, Mangalore, and Gulbarga, with the Belur facility serving Bellary, Koppal, Raichur, Bijapur, Bagalkot, Davangere, Belgaum, Haveri, Gadag, and Dharwad; the Mangalore facility serving Chikmagalur, Shimoga, Kodagu, Udupi, Dakshina Kannada, and Kasargod; and the Gulbarga plant serving Raichur, Bidar, parts of Bijapur, and Gulbarga ([afaqs!](https://www.Afaqs.com/news/media/32260_deccan-herald-to-debut-in-delhi-on-december-11)). Its supplement stable at the time included Sunday Herald, Articulations, Economy and Business, Science and Technology, Avenues, Sportscene, MetroLife, Spectrum, Living, and Open Sesame ([afaqs!](https://www.Afaqs.com/news/media/32260_deccan-herald-to-debut-in-delhi-on-december-11)).

## Expansion, and the Delhi gamble

In December 2011 the paper attempted its first move beyond its home region. The Delhi edition was set to launch on December 11, the paper's maiden edition in northern India, with an initial print run of around 15,000 copies ([afaqs!](https://www.Afaqs.com/news/media/32260_deccan-herald-to-debut-in-delhi-on-december-11)). That figure is the stat that frames the gamble: 15,000 copies against an entrenched Delhi field of The Times of India, Hindustan Times, The Hindu, The Pioneer, Asian Age, and The Indian Express — a modest beachhead that implied the paper knew it could not buy its way into India's most competitive newspaper market ([afaqs!](https://www.Afaqs.com/news/media/32260_deccan-herald-to-debut-in-delhi-on-december-11)). Whether the Delhi edition survived and grew was not confirmed from a full-read source in this pass, and this profile does not assert it.

## Digital reinvention and editorial reputation

The paper has reinvented itself twice in recent years. A 2019 revamp brought a new masthead and tagline, a mobile app, and a relaunched e-paper; the 75th-anniversary website relaunch of 2023 extended that work, with online-only opinion pieces, web stories and news shots, dynamic live blogs, and video content ([afaqs!](https://www.afaqs.com/companies/deccan-herald-launches-new-website)). The website was designed, in afaqs!'s account, to reflect a national character as well as the specialised information available from Karnataka and Bengaluru ([afaqs!](https://www.afaqs.com/companies/deccan-herald-launches-new-website)).

On reputation, the paper's own editor stated the ambition plainly at the relaunch: Sitaraman Shankar, editor of Deccan Herald and CEO of The Printers (Mysore), said the team's "top-notch journalism, commitment to speaking truth to power, and generating impact for the common man" had found an attractive digital home ([afaqs!](https://www.afaqs.com/companies/deccan-herald-launches-new-website)). An outside verdict comes from the Hindustan Times column marking the 75th anniversary, which calls the paper one of Bengaluru's "well-loved, much-respected and most enduring institutions, synonymous with the city itself" ([Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/legendary-liquor-baron-s-venture-the-story-of-deccan-herald-s-birth-and-enduring-legacy-in-bengaluru-s-history-101687203141885.html)). The profile does not independently grade the paper's journalism beyond these two attributed assessments; its record of staying family-owned, Karnataka-rooted, and in print for over 75 years is the verifiable core of its standing.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Deccan Herald?**
Deccan Herald is an English-language daily newspaper published from Bengaluru, Karnataka, founded in June 1948 by businessman K. N. Guruswamy and published by The Printers (Mysore) Private Limited. It is a family-owned paper with the Kannada daily Prajavani, the weekly Sudha, and the monthly Mayura as sister publications.

**Who founded Deccan Herald?**
K. N. Guruswamy — Kanekal Nettakallappa Guruswamy of Ballari — founded the paper in 1948, using wealth built in the family's excise business. Its first editor was the veteran journalist Pothan Joseph, previously founding editor of Dawn and an editor of the Hindustan Times.

**Who owns Deccan Herald?**
The Printers (Mysore) Private Limited publishes the paper. It has remained in the founder's family; by the 75th anniversary in 2023, Guruswamy's three grandsons were continuing the business from 75, MG Road, Bengaluru.

**Did Deccan Herald expand beyond Karnataka?**
Yes, once: it launched a Delhi edition on December 11, 2011, its first in northern India, with an initial print run of about 15,000 copies, competing against The Times of India, Hindustan Times, The Hindu, and others. Whether that edition survived long-term was not confirmed in this research pass.

**Is Deccan Herald still in print and online?**
Yes. It underwent a print revamp in 2019 with a new masthead and tagline, a mobile app, and a relaunched e-paper, then relaunched its website for its 75th anniversary in 2023 with online-only opinion, web stories, live blogs, and video.
