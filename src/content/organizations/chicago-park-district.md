## Overview

The Chicago Park District is the independent municipal park authority of [Chicago](/places/chicago/), Illinois, created in 1934 by consolidating 22 separate park districts into a single agency. It stewards more than 8,900 acres of green space across 600-plus parks, 31 beaches and 26 miles of Lake Michigan lakefront, and owns Soldier Field, home of the Chicago Bears. In 2014 it became the first urban park district to win the National Gold Medal Award for Excellence in Park and Recreation Management ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/about-chicago-park-district); [Chicago Park District](https://chicagoparkdistrict.com/about-us/news/mayor-emanuel-announces-chicago-park-district-receives-2014-national-gold-medal-award)).

The District is a separate unit of local government, not a department of the [City of Chicago](/organizations/city-of-chicago/): its budget is set by a seven-member board appointed by the Mayor, not by the City Council ([Chicago Politics](https://www.chicagopolitics.org/on-my-block/park-district-2027-budget-654-8-million-23-million-tif-surplus-vote-dec-9)). Its roots reach back to three park commissions created in 1869, and its best-known parks — Lincoln Park, Jackson Park, Washington Park and Burnham Park — were laid out by figures including Frederick Law Olmsted Sr. and Daniel H. Burnham ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/about-chicago-park-district)). The District also maintains monuments on park land, including the [Nicolaus Copernicus Monument](/places/nicolaus-copernicus-monument/) on Solidarity Drive, funded by the [Copernicus Foundation](/organizations/copernicus-foundation/) ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)).

## From 22 park districts to one

Chicago's park story began in the early 1850s, when Dr. John Rauch led a protest to set aside a 60-acre slice of a public cemetery as parkland, the seed of Lincoln Park ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/about-chicago-park-district)). In 1869 the state created three separate park commissions — Lincoln, South and West — which built the city's great landscapes independently: the Lincoln Park Commissioners established the Lincoln Park Zoo, the West Park Commissioners created Humboldt, Garfield and Douglas Parks, and the South Park Commission hired Frederick Law Olmsted Sr. to lay out Washington and Jackson Parks and the Midway Plaisance ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/about-chicago-park-district)). By 1930, 19 newer districts had joined them, leaving 22 agencies running parks in one city. The Great Depression broke that arrangement, and voters approved the Park Consolidation Act of 1934; the new District held its first board meeting on May 1, 1934 ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/about-chicago-park-district)).

## What the District runs — and the Soldier Field question

Today the District is the largest open space and recreation provider in Chicago, with 8,900-plus acres, more than 600 parks, 31 beaches, 50 nature areas, two world-class conservatories, 27 indoor pools and 50 outdoor pools ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/about-chicago-park-district)). Its most politically charged asset is Soldier Field, which it owns outright: the stadium opened as Municipal Grant Park Stadium in 1924 on land that was once navigable water of Lake Michigan, and the Chicago Bears have played there since 1971 ([Illinois Supreme Court](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/Resources/60e5ab4b-abc7-43d9-b1c9-4083fdd4df86/93852.htm)). When the stadium's renovation was challenged in court, the Illinois Supreme Court upheld the financing law in 2003, noting the District would remain owner and landlord and the Bears' permit agreement would run to 2033 ([Illinois Supreme Court](https://www.illinoiscourts.gov/Resources/60e5ab4b-abc7-43d9-b1c9-4083fdd4df86/93852.htm)). The District's land also hosts cultural landmarks: the Nicolaus Copernicus Monument stands on Solidarity Drive in front of the [Adler Planetarium](/organizations/adler-planetarium/), on park land the District maintains ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/parks-facilities/nicolaus-copernicus-monument)).

## Governance, money and the 2027 budget

The District answers to a seven-member Board of Commissioners appointed by the Mayor of Chicago, and its budget is set by that board rather than the City Council ([Chicago Politics](https://www.chicagopolitics.org/on-my-block/park-district-2027-budget-654-8-million-23-million-tif-surplus-vote-dec-9)). Its chief executive is General Superintendent and CEO Carlos Ramirez-Rosa, a former 35th Ward alderman appointed by Mayor Brandon Johnson who took office on April 1, 2025, succeeding Rosa Escareño ([The Daily Line](https://www.thedailyline.com/chicago-city-council-alderman-carlos-ramirez-rosa-become-park-district-head-mayor-brandon-johnson-35th-ward-appointment)). Money is the pressure point: the approved 2026 budget of $637.6 million closed a $30 million gap, and the proposed 2027 budget of $654.8 million closes a $36 million shortfall through fee increases, savings and a $2.8 million property-tax adjustment, while counting on $23 million of TIF surplus the City has not yet declared ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/about-us/news/chicago-park-district-board-approves-2026-operational-budget-fiscally-responsible); [Chicago Politics](https://www.chicagopolitics.org/on-my-block/park-district-2027-budget-654-8-million-23-million-tif-surplus-vote-dec-9)). In October 2026 the board extended Ramirez-Rosa's contract to December 31, 2029, a move that drew criticism from several alderpersons ([Chicago Politics](https://www.chicagopolitics.org/on-my-block/park-district-2027-budget-654-8-million-23-million-tif-surplus-vote-dec-9)). The 2027 plan also hires 50 Inclusion Aides and puts $100,000 into the Night Out in the Parks series ([Chicago Park District](https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/about-us/news/park-district-superintendent-and-ceo-ramirez-rosa-presents-2027-proposed-budget)).

## FAQ

### What is the Chicago Park District?

It is the independent municipal park authority of Chicago, Illinois, created in 1934 by consolidating 22 separate park districts. It manages more than 8,900 acres of green space, 600-plus parks, 31 beaches and 26 miles of lakefront, and owns Soldier Field. It is governed by a mayor-appointed seven-member board.

### Is the Park District part of the City of Chicago?

No. It is a separate unit of local government, and its budget is set by its own seven-member board, appointed by the Mayor, rather than by the City Council. It works closely with the City — for example, on land transfers for the CTA's Red Line Extension — but the City does not run it.

### Who runs the Chicago Park District?

General Superintendent and CEO Carlos Ramirez-Rosa, a former 35th Ward alderman, has led the District since April 1, 2025, when he succeeded Rosa Escareño. He was appointed by Mayor Brandon Johnson and confirmed by the Board of Commissioners, whose president is Marlon Everett.

### Does the Park District own Soldier Field?

Yes. The District owns Soldier Field and the surrounding Burnham Park land, and the Chicago Bears play there under a permit agreement that runs to 2033. The Illinois Supreme Court confirmed the District's ownership in 2003 when it upheld the law financing the stadium's renovation.

### How is the Park District funded?

Mostly from property taxes, plus program fees, harbor fees and golf fees. Its approved 2026 budget was $637.6 million. The proposed 2027 budget of $654.8 million also counts on $23 million of TIF surplus that the City of Chicago has not yet declared.
