## Overview

**Ansys is the engineering simulation company that spent half a century buying up the tools engineers use to test products before building them — structural, fluid, thermal and electromagnetic simulation in one multiphysics environment — and then, in July 2025, became the bought company, when chip-design giant Synopsys acquired it for $35 billion.** ([HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/2024/02/08/synopsys-eats-ansys-does-hpc-get-indigestion/)) Founded in 1970 in Pittsburgh as Swanson Analysis Systems, Inc. (SASI) by John Swanson, Ansys grew from a finite-element software code into the simulation layer of product development across automotive, aerospace, electronics and energy — and its acquisition closed the loop between designing a chip and simulating the physical system around it. ([EDN](https://www.edn.com/synopsys-plus-ansys-the-making-of-an-eda-giant/))

## From a finite-element code to a multiphysics platform

Ansys began as a single idea: general-purpose software that could do the complex engineering calculations engineers were otherwise doing by hand or not at all. ([EDN](https://www.edn.com/synopsys-plus-ansys-the-making-of-an-eda-giant/)) Based in Canonsburg, Pennsylvania, the company provides simulation software solutions for product design and testing. ([EDN](https://www.edn.com/synopsys-plus-ansys-the-making-of-an-eda-giant/)) Over the years it made its name as a supplier of multiphysics technologies enabling engineers to simulate the interactions between structures, heat transfer, fluids, electronics and optical elements in a unified engineering environment — the point being that a real product fails, heats, vibrates and interferes all at once, not one physics at a time. ([EDN](https://www.edn.com/synopsys-plus-ansys-the-making-of-an-eda-giant/))

The corporate path ran through venture capital and the public markets: founded by John Swanson as SASI in 1970, the company was acquired by venture capital firm T.A. Associates in 1994, then changed its name to Ansys and went public in 1996. ([EDN](https://www.edn.com/synopsys-plus-ansys-the-making-of-an-eda-giant/)) From there it grew the way acquisitive software companies do — by buying best-in-class tools. By the time Synopsys came calling, Ansys had acquired 34 companies since its inception, notably HPC computational fluid dynamics firm Fluent in 2006. ([HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/2024/02/08/synopsys-eats-ansys-does-hpc-get-indigestion/))

| Acquisition | What it added | Why it mattered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fluent (2006) | Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) | Made Ansys a fluids leader alongside structures |
| LSTC / LS-DYNA (2019) | Explicit dynamics, crash simulation | The automotive industry's crashworthiness standard |
| The Synopsys alliance (pre-2024) | Chip signoff integration | RedHawk-SC power/thermal/reliability signoff inside Synopsys' chip-design flow |

The LSTC deal shows the pattern at its clearest. In September 2019, ANSYS entered a definitive agreement to acquire Livermore Software Technology Corporation, maker of LS-DYNA, a highly scalable multiphysics solver the automotive industry had widely adopted to predict a vehicle's behaviour and its occupants' fate during a collision — simulating structure, tires, seats, seatbelts, airbags, accelerometers, sensors and batteries in a fully coupled mathematical framework. ([InsideHPC](https://insidehpc.com/2019/09/ansys-to-acquire-lstc/)) LSTC counted the vast majority of tier-one automotive suppliers among its customers, and had been an ANSYS partner for nearly 25 years under founder John Hallquist. ([InsideHPC](https://insidehpc.com/2019/09/ansys-to-acquire-lstc/)) LS-DYNA's ability to scale on high-performance computers had already carried it beyond cars into aerospace, civil engineering, defence, manufacturing and biomedicine. ([InsideHPC](https://insidehpc.com/2019/09/ansys-to-acquire-lstc/))

## The acquiree: why Synopsys bought Ansys

Synopsys, headquartered in Sunnyvale, California, is an electronic design automation (EDA) company focused on silicon design and verification, silicon intellectual property, software security and quality, supplying tools and services to the semiconductor design and manufacturing industry. ([HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/2024/02/08/synopsys-eats-ansys-does-hpc-get-indigestion/)) The two companies were not strangers: they had formed a strategic alliance in which Ansys integrated its RedHawk-SC family of power-integrity, thermal and reliability signoff products with Synopsys' Fusion Compiler, 3DIC Compiler and PrimeTime platforms, with Ansys signoff solutions certified for all FinFET nodes down to 3 nm. ([EDN](https://www.edn.com/synopsys-plus-ansys-the-making-of-an-eda-giant/))

The $35 billion cash-and-stock deal, announced January 16, 2024, would create a $100 billion software entity in the EDA/CAE space; at announcement, Ansys carried roughly a $30 billion market capitalisation against Synopsys' $73.6 billion. ([HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/2024/02/08/synopsys-eats-ansys-does-hpc-get-indigestion/)) HPCwire's analysis at the time stressed the logic — between the two companies there was no product overlap, and the combination addressed design challenges at 7 nm and below and in multi-die systems, where thermal effects, electromagnetic interference and advanced 3D layout demand simulation at the prototyping stage — while also raising the open question: would Fluent, a leader in CFD, keep its HPC focus inside a company whose revenue leader was chip design, or become a "distant market" to Synopsys leadership? ([HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/2024/02/08/synopsys-eats-ansys-does-hpc-get-indigestion/))

Completion took 18 months. Synopsys completed its acquisition of Ansys on July 17, 2025, after receiving final regulatory approval from China on July 14; the UK's Competition and Markets Authority had flagged concerns in December 2024 that the merger could reduce choice and raise prices, and the companies agreed to sell certain businesses to Keysight Technologies as a remedy, winning UK clearance in March 2025. ([Manufacturing Dive](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/chip-design-software-maker-synopsys-completes-35b-deal-ansys/753321/)) The deal was pitched on synergies of $400 million in costs by the third year and $400 million in revenue by the fourth, and former Ansys president and CEO Ajei Gopal joined Synopsys' board along with board member Ravi Vijayaraghavan. ([Manufacturing Dive](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/chip-design-software-maker-synopsys-completes-35b-deal-ansys/753321/)) Ansys common stock ceased NASDAQ listing, closing a 29-year run as a public company. ([Manufacturing Dive](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/chip-design-software-maker-synopsys-completes-35b-deal-ansys/753321/))

In competitive terms, Ansys was one of the pillars of its market: [Altair Engineering](/organizations/altair-engineering/) founder and CEO James Scapa named Ansys among Altair's main competitors in 2017, alongside Dassault Systèmes, Siemens and MSC Software. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/11/01/altair-engineering-soars-41-in-public-debut/1024)) By 2025, two of those four names — Ansys and Altair — had both been absorbed by larger groups, leaving Dassault Systèmes as the independent incumbent among them.
