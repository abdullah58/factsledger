## Overview

Citigroup Inc., known as Citi, is an American multinational bank headquartered in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/), formed in 1998 from the merger of Citicorp and Travelers Group and tracing its oldest roots to the City Bank of New York, chartered on 16 June 1812 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/118781)). It is one of the four largest banks in the United States by assets, with about 239,000 employees in 160 countries, and it runs five businesses: Services, Markets, Banking, Wealth and U.S. Personal Banking ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/citigroup); [Citigroup](https://www.citigroup.com/rcs/citigpa/storage/public/Earnings/Q42025/2025prqtr4rslt.pdf?linkId=898650521)). In 2025 it earned net income of $14.3 billion on revenue of $85.2 billion, with total assets of $2,657 billion at year end ([Citigroup](https://www.citigroup.com/rcs/citigpa/storage/public/Earnings/Q42025/2025prqtr4rslt.pdf?linkId=898650521)).

Citi's story is one of scale, collapse and deliberate shrinkage: the world's largest financial services organisation in 1998, a ward of the US government a decade later, and since 2021 a smaller, simpler bank being rebuilt around its institutional strengths under chief executive Jane Fraser, the first woman to lead a major US bank ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/citigroup); [Citi](https://www.citigroup.com/global/about-us/heritage)). Its thread in the [Axoni](/organizations/axoni/) story runs through the whole arc — from the 2016 equity-swaps pilot to joining the Veris network in 2021 ([The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/dlt-provider-secures-major-bank-funding/); [Bloomberg](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/crypto/blackrock-joins-blockchain-platform-axoni-for-equity-swap-trades)).

## From City Bank to Citicorp: 1812–1998

Citi's history begins with the failure of America's first central bank: when Congress refused to renew the First Bank of the United States in 1811, some of its New York shareholders secured a state charter for the City Bank of New York on 16 June 1812 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/118781)). The bank grew with New York City's rise as the nation's commercial capital, took a national charter as the National City Bank of New York in 1865, and in 1897 became the first large American bank to open a foreign department ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/118781)). In 1915 it became America's leading international bank by buying the International Banking Corporation, which had 21 overseas offices in 13 countries and territories ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/118781)).

Mergers kept it growing: the Bank of America, N.A. in 1931 and the First National Bank of the City of New York in 1955, after which it became the First National City Bank of New York ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/118781)). The business was reorganised under a holding company in 1967, named Citicorp in 1974, while the bank itself became Citibank in 1976 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/118781)). Citicorp pioneered ATM networks in the late 1970s and bought its way into credit cards with Carte Blanche in 1978 and Diners Club in 1981 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/118781)). By the late twentieth century it was the largest American bank, with about 3,000 branches worldwide ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/118781)).

## The 1998 merger, the crisis and the rescue

In 1998 Travelers Group, the insurance and brokerage empire built by Sandy Weill, bought all of Citicorp's shares for $70 billion — the most expensive corporate merger brokered to that point — creating a company with about $700 billion in assets and the title of the world's largest financial services organisation ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/citigroup)). The combination was so broad that regulators and the firm itself soon began unwinding it: in 2002 Citigroup spun off its property and casualty insurance businesses, though it kept the red umbrella logo that had originated with Travelers ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/118781)). A decade later the whole edifice nearly collapsed. In 2008 Citigroup suffered billions of dollars of losses in the subprime mortgage crisis, and the US government stepped in twice: a $25 billion investment in October under the Emergency Economic Stabilization Act, then a second rescue in November of a further $20 billion plus a guarantee on more than $300 billion of troubled assets ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/118781)).

The all-in tally of federal support was far larger than those two equity injections: EBSCO records a series of loans totalling $476.2 billion, which the corporation repaid over the following years ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/citigroup)). In January 2009 Citigroup announced it would split into Citicorp, for traditional banking, and Citi Holdings, to manage its riskiest assets ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/118781)). The rescue's cost and scale still define Citi: no other major US bank needed as much government support, and the firm has spent the years since shrinking toward businesses it can run profitably.

## Simplification under Jane Fraser

Jane Fraser became Citi's chief executive on 1 March 2021, the first woman to lead a major US bank ([Citi](https://www.citigroup.com/global/about-us/heritage)). Within weeks she announced the bank would exit consumer franchises in 13 markets, including India, Australia, China, Indonesia and Poland, for lack of scale ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/citigroup)). The simplification continued through disposals and a 2024 corporate overhaul, and in February 2026 Citi completed its exit from Russia by selling AO Citibank to Renaissance Capital ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/citigroup)). The same period brought regulatory rebukes: in 2024 US regulators fined Citigroup $136 million for failing to fix longstanding data-management issues identified in 2020, and the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau ordered $24.5 million in fines and $1.4 million in restitution over credit cards illegally denied to Armenian Americans between 2015 and 2021 ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/citigroup)).

The strategy is to concentrate on five businesses — Services, Markets, Banking, Wealth and U.S. Personal Banking — and 2025 showed progress: every one of the five delivered positive operating leverage, Services earned $21.3 billion in revenue, and the group returned about $17.6 billion to shareholders ([Citigroup](https://www.citigroup.com/rcs/citigpa/storage/public/Earnings/Q42025/2025prqtr4rslt.pdf?linkId=898650521)). Citi's technology thread also continued: after joining Axoni's equity-swaps working group in October 2016 and completing a months-long blockchain pilot in 2017, Citi joined the Veris network in September 2021 alongside [BlackRock](/organizations/blackrock/) and [Goldman Sachs](/organizations/goldman-sachs/) ([The TRADE](https://www.thetradenews.com/dlt-provider-secures-major-bank-funding/); [Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/news/industry/working-group-tests-blockchain-system-for-equity-swaps/); [Bloomberg](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/crypto/blackrock-joins-blockchain-platform-axoni-for-equity-swap-trades)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was Citi founded — 1812 or 1998?**
Both dates are true in different senses. Citigroup Inc. itself was formed in 1998 from the merger of Citicorp and Travelers Group, but its oldest predecessor, the City Bank of New York, was chartered on 16 June 1812 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/118781)). Citi dates its own founding to 1812.

**How big is Citi now?**
At the end of 2025 Citi had $2,657 billion in total assets and about $1.4 trillion in deposits, earned $14.3 billion on $85.2 billion of revenue, and employed around 239,000 people in 160 countries. Its five businesses are Services, Markets, Banking, Wealth and U.S. Personal Banking ([Citigroup](https://www.citigroup.com/rcs/citigpa/storage/public/Earnings/Q42025/2025prqtr4rslt.pdf?linkId=898650521); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/citigroup)).

**What happened to Citi in the 2008 financial crisis?**
Citi suffered billions in subprime-mortgage losses and was rescued twice in 2008: a $25 billion government investment in October and a further $20 billion plus a guarantee on over $300 billion of troubled assets in November ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/118781)). Total federal support is tallied at $476.2 billion including guarantees and loans ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/citigroup)).

**Who runs Citi and what are its businesses?**
Jane Fraser has been chair and chief executive since March 2021, the first woman to lead a major US bank ([Citi](https://www.citigroup.com/global/about-us/heritage)). Citi is organised into five businesses: Services, Markets, Banking, Wealth and U.S. Personal Banking, and every one of the five delivered positive operating leverage in 2025 ([Citigroup](https://www.citigroup.com/rcs/citigpa/storage/public/Earnings/Q42025/2025prqtr4rslt.pdf?linkId=898650521)).
