## Overview

Acrew Capital is a San Francisco- and Palo Alto-based venture capital firm founded in 2019 by five partners who had previously invested together at Aspect Ventures: Theresia Gouw, [Lauren Kolodny](/people/lauren-kolodny/), Vishal Lugani, Asad Khaliq, and Mark Kraynak. Its defining investment predates the firm itself — Kolodny's 2016 rescue of [Chime](/organizations/chime/), a $9 million Series A extension that was the neobank's only term sheet after about 100 rejections — and by October 2024 the firm managed $1.7 billion after raising $700 million in fresh funds ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/12/18/theresia-gouws-multigenerational-new-firm-acrew-just-closed-a-250-million-debut-fund/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/18/acrew-capital-hits-five-years-raises-700m/?guccounter=1)).

The firm runs two vehicles: an early-stage Long Term View fund writing $1–15 million checks, and a Diversify Capital Fund that puts $5–15 million into the growth rounds of market leaders while deliberately adding women and underrepresented investors to their cap tables ([Acrew Capital](https://www.acrewcapital.com/funds)). That second fund is not a side project — it is the firm's signature idea, built on the conviction that who owns a company matters as much as who builds it ([Crunchbase News](https://about.crunchbase.com/blog/venture-capital-firms-expand-opportunities-for-diverse-investors)).

## The Chime bet that defined the firm

Acrew's origin story is a rescue. In early 2016, Chime was weeks from running out of cash. Co-founder [Ryan King](/people/ryan-king/) told [TechCrunch](/organizations/techcrunch/) the company had pitched about 100 investors for an extension to its Series A and received about 100 rejections: venture firms looked at a heavily regulated industry and meagre growth and passed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)). The single yes came from Lauren Kolodny, then a partner at Aspect Ventures, who led a $9 million extension — the only term sheet on the table — after reading Chime's dense financial-partner contracts and concluding that slim margins could become meaningful at scale. The check brought Chime's total raise to $21 million, and Kolodny bought in at 26 cents per share, according to the company's disclosure of its private-round share prices ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)).

The bet paid off in public. Chime went public in June 2025, pricing at $27 a share and opening at $42 for a $14.5 billion market capitalization, and Kolodny — by then Acrew's co-founder and managing partner — stood on the podium to ring the opening bell at [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/12/chime-almost-died-in-2016-turned-down-by-100-vcs-today-it-ipod-at-14-5b/)). Kolodny, who had appeared on the Forbes Midas List three years in a row by July 2025, has kept the same thesis since: technology that democratizes financial services for everyday people, most recently a $20 million Series A she led in Alix, a startup using AI to automate estate settlement ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/24/chime-backer-lauren-kolodny-bets-on-ai-to-revolutionize-estate-processing/)).

## A team built as a crew, from Aspect's split

Acrew was born from a breakup. Gouw had co-founded Aspect Ventures in 2014 with Jennifer Fonstad; four months after news broke in 2019 that the two were going separate ways — Fonstad cited different leadership styles — Gouw announced her new firm ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/12/18/theresia-gouws-multigenerational-new-firm-acrew-just-closed-a-250-million-debut-fund/)). She launched it with Kolodny, Kraynak, Lugani, and Khaliq, all former Aspect colleagues, and closed a $250 million debut fund in December 2019 — above the original $175–200 million target — with many of the same limited partners who had backed Aspect, including Melinda Gates ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/12/18/theresia-gouws-multigenerational-new-firm-acrew-just-closed-a-250-million-debut-fund/)). (A 2024 retrospective gives the first fund's size as $258 million; see the open items.)

The name fits the structure. Gouw calls the five founders a team with equal say in investment decisions: any two partners can push a deal through, while a single partner can veto one, and she describes venture capital as "best practiced as a team sport" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/12/18/theresia-gouws-multigenerational-new-firm-acrew-just-closed-a-250-million-debut-fund/)). The team was also deliberately multigenerational — a rarity in venture — chosen, Gouw said, for the business advantages now and because it "positions us well through many funds and many cycles" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/12/18/theresia-gouws-multigenerational-new-firm-acrew-just-closed-a-250-million-debut-fund/)). The senior partners bring complementary records: Gouw spent 15 years at Accel, had been named to the Forbes Midas List eight times by 2019 and nine times since, and counts Trulia, Imperva, and Forescout among her earlier exits; Kolodny came from product marketing at Google, where she led launches for Workspace including Google Drive; Kraynak spent nearly 13 years at Imperva before joining Aspect; Khaliq focuses on data infrastructure, applied machine learning, and security and privacy ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/12/18/theresia-gouws-multigenerational-new-firm-acrew-just-closed-a-250-million-debut-fund/); [Acrew Capital](https://www.acrewcapital.com/team)).

## Two funds, one thesis — and a diversity model

Acrew invests across four thesis areas — fintech, data and security, health and human empowerment, and the future of work — through two funds with different jobs ([Acrew Capital](https://www.acrewcapital.com/funds)). The Long Term View (LTV) fund backs seed and Series A companies with $1–15 million checks, each thesis area led by a senior partner, supported by a community of nearly 500 diverse leaders who advise portfolio companies ([Acrew Capital](https://www.acrewcapital.com/funds)). Kolodny leads the fintech practice, with investments including Chime, Clara, Divvy, Gusto, Klar, Papaya, Pie, and Plaid; Khaliq's data and security bets include Vanta, Ketch, and Cato Networks ([Acrew Capital](https://www.acrewcapital.com/team)).

The Diversify Capital Fund (DCF) is the firm's distinctive second act. It writes $5–15 million checks into the growth rounds of market leaders on a path to going public, with the explicit aim of adding diversity to their teams and cap tables in a single check ([Acrew Capital](https://www.acrewcapital.com/funds)). The fund was created to "bring more diverse shareholders to later-stage investments of companies that are IPO bound, in turn increasing representation of women and PoC leaders in the ownership of such companies" ([Crunchbase News](https://about.crunchbase.com/blog/venture-capital-firms-expand-opportunities-for-diverse-investors)). As of 2021, the Acrew team itself was 63 percent underrepresented minorities, with 88 percent women or people of color ([Crunchbase News](https://about.crunchbase.com/blog/venture-capital-firms-expand-opportunities-for-diverse-investors)). Kolodny's stated hope is that within five years, a venture firm helping companies add diverse independent board members and diverse executives "won't be a unique strategy" ([Crunchbase News](https://about.crunchbase.com/blog/venture-capital-firms-expand-opportunities-for-diverse-investors)). The model has drawn founder endorsements: Finix chief executive Richie Serna said the DCF "aligned all of my priorities in one investment vehicle," and Chime chief executive [Chris Britt](/people/chris-britt/) credited the Acrew team with sourcing executive hires including the company's CFO ([Acrew Capital](https://www.acrewcapital.com/funds)).

## Exits and the road to $1.7 billion

Acrew's first six investments already included Chime — which closed a $500 million round at a $5.8 billion valuation in December 2019 — alongside Klar, a Mexico City challenger bank, and Augtera Networks ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/12/18/theresia-gouws-multigenerational-new-firm-acrew-just-closed-a-250-million-debut-fund/)). The exits followed. Corporate-spending platform Divvy sold to BILL for $2.5 billion in May 2021, and creator platform Superpeer was acquired by Skillshare in 2024 for an undisclosed amount ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/18/acrew-capital-hits-five-years-raises-700m/?guccounter=1)). By October 2024 the portfolio also carried At-Bay, an insurance cybersecurity startup last valued at $1.3 billion, and Pie Insurance, estimated by PitchBook at nearly $1 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/18/acrew-capital-hits-five-years-raises-700m/?guccounter=1)).

That record is what let the firm raise in a hard market. In October 2024, as Acrew turned five, it announced $700 million in fresh funds — bringing assets under management to $1.7 billion — in a year when U.S. venture firms had raised $65.1 billion across 305 funds, a total on track to beat 2023 but still far below any year since 2019 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/18/acrew-capital-hits-five-years-raises-700m/?guccounter=1)). The new money split between the early-stage strategy and a discovery strategy writing $10–20 million checks for Series B and C companies ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/18/acrew-capital-hits-five-years-raises-700m/?guccounter=1)). Five years after five partners left Aspect to build a firm run as a crew, all five were still in the same roles ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/18/acrew-capital-hits-five-years-raises-700m/?guccounter=1)).
