## Overview

The Guardian just posted the best financial results in its history — and almost nobody on the search results page knows. The British newspaper reported record revenue of £282 million for the year to March 2026, powered by 1.4 million recurring digital supporters and a Scott Trust endowment of £1.35 billion ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/media_business/guardian-grows-revenue-to-record-282m-boosted-by-us-operation/)). It did this the year after selling its Sunday sister paper, The Observer, to Tortoise Media — losing roughly £18 million in print revenue and gaining a business now growing fastest in America, where revenue rose 14% to £63.8 million ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/media_business/guardian-grows-revenue-to-record-282m-boosted-by-us-operation/)).

That business sits under one of journalism's most unusual ownership structures. The Guardian is owned by Guardian Media Group, which is owned by the Scott Trust — a trust created in 1936 precisely so that no proprietor could bend the paper's journalism, with an endowment now worth £1.35 billion, up £100 million in a single year ([University of Manchester](https://documents.manchester.ac.uk/display.aspx?DocID=59306); [Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/media_business/guardian-grows-revenue-to-record-282m-boosted-by-us-operation/)). Pre-tax losses have narrowed from £37 million to £25 million to £17 million across three years, while 41% of revenue now arrives from outside the UK ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/media_business/guardian-grows-revenue-to-record-282m-boosted-by-us-operation/)).

## Two centuries of independence

The paper was founded in 1821 in Manchester by the cotton merchant John Edward Taylor, two years after the Peterloo Massacre — the cavalry charge against pro-democracy demonstrators in St Peter's Field that shocked the city ([University of Manchester](https://documents.manchester.ac.uk/display.aspx?DocID=59306)). The first issue of *The Manchester Guardian* appeared on 5 May 1821 as a weekly; it went twice-weekly in 1836 and daily in 1855 ([University of Manchester](https://documents.manchester.ac.uk/display.aspx?DocID=59306)). Ownership passed through the Taylor and Scott families — C.P. Scott edited the paper for 57 years from 1872 and bought it outright in 1904 — before the family transferred ownership to the Scott Trust in 1936 to protect the paper's journalistic independence ([University of Manchester](https://documents.manchester.ac.uk/display.aspx?DocID=59306)).

The title changed to *The Guardian* on 24 August 1959, and the main editorial offices moved from Manchester to London in 1970 ([University of Manchester](https://documents.manchester.ac.uk/display.aspx?DocID=59306)). The tabloid-era Guardian of the 21st century is best known globally for investigations that carried real political weight — and for a transatlantic readership that now funds it directly.

## The reporting that travels

The Guardian's journalism is the through-line connecting several other FactsLedger profiles. In 2018, after [Sean Hannity](https://factsledger.com/people/sean-hannity/) was revealed in court as a client of Donald Trump's lawyer Michael Cohen, the Guardian's Jon Swaine investigated the Fox News host's real estate holdings — finding records linking Hannity to a group of shell companies that spent at least $90 million on more than 870 homes in seven states over the previous decade ([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/the_media_today/hannity-real-estate.php?Daily)). Swaine further found that Hannity had amassed part of the collection with support from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development — a fact he had not disclosed when praising HUD Secretary Ben Carson on his television show ([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/the_media_today/hannity-real-estate.php?Daily)).

That same year, the Guardian published the profile that traced [Stormy Daniels](https://factsledger.com/people/stormy-daniels/)'s pseudonym to its origins: "drawn from the name Mötley Crüe bassist Nikki Sixx gave to his daughter — Storm — and her preference for Jack Daniel's whiskey," as the Indian Express reported the Guardian's piece ([Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/explained-global/who-is-stormy-daniels-porn-star-donald-trump-8530213/lite/)).
