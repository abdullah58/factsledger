## Overview

ChatGPT is the product that turned its chip supplier into the world's first $4 trillion company. OpenAI trained the technology behind the chatbot using a supercomputer powered by 10,000 [NVIDIA](https://factsledger.com/organizations/nvidia/) GPUs — and when word spread, rivals scrambled for GPU access while investors piled into NVIDIA's shares ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/NVIDIA-Corporation)). NVIDIA crossed the $4 trillion market-capitalization milestone in July 2025, a valuation driven, in Britannica's words, by soaring demand for its AI chips "and its central role in powering large language models and data centers worldwide" ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/NVIDIA-Corporation)). By September 2025, the two companies formalized the relationship, announcing a strategic partnership to build at least 10 gigawatts of computing capacity using NVIDIA hardware, with up to $100 billion in hardware and data-center capacity as phases completed ([Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/NVIDIA-Corporation)).

The chatbot itself arrived on November 30, 2022 — a generative AI system built on the GPT-3.5 architecture and fine-tuned with reinforcement learning from human feedback, offered not as a developer API but as a conversational interface anyone could talk to ([Firstpost](https://www.firstpost.com/explainers/history-today-november-30-openai-chatgpt-launch-ai-13954576.html)). Within five days it had passed 1 million users, at the time the fastest growth any consumer platform had recorded; by January 2023 it was estimated at 100 million monthly active users ([Firstpost](https://www.firstpost.com/explainers/history-today-november-30-openai-chatgpt-launch-ai-13954576.html)).

## From API demos to a product anyone could use

Before ChatGPT, OpenAI had built the earlier GPT models — GPT-1 in 2018, GPT-2 in 2019, GPT-3 in 2020 — but released them primarily through APIs, research collaborations and controlled demonstrations ([Firstpost](https://www.firstpost.com/explainers/history-today-november-30-openai-chatgpt-launch-ai-13954576.html)). ChatGPT was the decisive shift: the RLHF training made responses more aligned with user intent, more conversational and better at following instructions, so that for the first time users needed no programming knowledge to talk to a frontier AI system ([Firstpost](https://www.firstpost.com/explainers/history-today-november-30-openai-chatgpt-launch-ai-13954576.html)). The lineage continued with GPT-4 in 2023, offering more advanced reasoning and better reliability, and later multimodal expansions into browsing, image generation and specialized professional versions ([Firstpost](https://www.firstpost.com/explainers/history-today-november-30-openai-chatgpt-launch-ai-13954576.html)).

Growth at that scale collided with regulators almost immediately. In March 2023, Italy's data protection authority, the Garante, ordered OpenAI to temporarily stop processing Italian users' personal information while it investigated a possible data breach, questioning whether there was a legal basis for collecting the massive datasets used to train the chatbot's algorithms ([Associated Press](https://techxplore.com/news/2023-04-openai-chatgpt-italy-watchdog-demands.html)). OpenAI met the regulator's conditions ahead of an April 30 deadline — publishing privacy notices, creating an EU opt-out form for training data, and adding age verification — and ChatGPT was reinstated in Italy on April 28, 2023 ([Associated Press](https://techxplore.com/news/2023-04-openai-chatgpt-italy-watchdog-demands.html)). The episode was a preview of the regulatory scrutiny that generative AI would face worldwide.

## A chatbot entangled with the news business

ChatGPT's relationship with the media has become a story in its own right. In January 2025, [[Axios](/organizations/axios/)](https://factsledger.com/organizations/axios/) and OpenAI debuted a three-year deal under which OpenAI bankrolls the startup of thirteen Axios Local newsletters and gives Axios staff enterprise tool credits, while Axios lets OpenAI train its models on its published coverage ([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)). Axios CEO Jim VandeHei framed it as a test of whether lean local newsrooms armed with AI could be economically viable; critics called it a devil's bargain ([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).

The tension is direct. As of February 2026, ChatGPT users in the United States were writing a million prompts per week seeking out local news — the very journalism dwindling local newsrooms struggle to produce ([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)). Meanwhile Axios has continued covering damning allegations against OpenAI, including ChatGPT's alleged role in teen suicides and "each beat of OpenAI's ugly court battle with Elon Musk" ([Columbia Journalism Review](https://www.cjr.org/feature/axios-wants-to-save-local-news-with-ai-jim-vandehei-tech-newsletters.php)).
