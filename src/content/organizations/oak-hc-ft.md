## Overview

Oak HC/FT is a venture and growth-equity firm that invests exclusively in healthcare and financial technology, founded in 2014 by Annie Lamont, Andrew Adams and Patricia Kemp as a spin-out of Oak Investment Partners. It has raised five flagship funds totalling $5.24 billion, backs more than 100 portfolio companies, and in 2025 co-led the $122 million Series D of AI tax-research platform [Blue J](/organizations/blue-j/).

The firm's name is a literal statement of its mandate: "HC" for healthcare information and services, "FT" for financial services technology ([Oak HC/FT](https://www.oakhcft.com/)). It describes itself as a venture and growth-equity investor that guides companies "from seed to growth", writing checks of $1 million to $100 million ([Oak HC/FT](https://www.oakhcft.com/)). Its current website puts assets under management at about $10 billion, with more than 100 portfolio companies and twelve years of investing behind it ([Oak HC/FT](https://www.oakhcft.com/)) — though releases as recent as August 2025 still stated "over $5.3 billion", a discrepancy this profile flags rather than resolves (see "What this profile does not claim"). The firm is headquartered in Stamford, Connecticut, with an office in [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)).

## The spin-out from Oak Investment Partners

Oak HC/FT was established in 2014 by Annie Lamont, Andrew Adams and Patricia Kemp, who had worked together since 2003 at Oak Investment Partners ([Pennsylvania Public School Employees' Retirement System](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/psers/documents/board3/resolutions/2025/2025-69%20PSERB%20Resolution%20Oak%20HC_FT%20Partners%20VI,%20L.P..pdf)). The three spun out to capitalise on their longstanding specialisation in healthcare and fintech, and the new firm was described at launch as affiliated with Oak Investment Partners but operating separately ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/oak-investment-partners-announces-500m-health-and-fintech-fund)). The parent firm, founded in 1978, was a multi-stage venture investor across information technology, internet and consumer, financial services technology, healthcare and clean energy ([Pennsylvania Public School Employees' Retirement System](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/psers/documents/board3/resolutions/2025/2025-69%20PSERB%20Resolution%20Oak%20HC_FT%20Partners%20VI,%20L.P..pdf)).

The founders brought deep tenure with them. Lamont had joined Oak Investment Partners in 1982 and served as a general partner from 1986 to 2006, spending 28 years there leading its healthcare and fintech practices ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsxkym4nag6hvze9n30g/corner-office/the-fintech-finance-40-patricia-kemp-and-annie-lamont)). Kemp, a former senior executive at marketing firm Cendant, had been hired by Lamont as an Oak venture partner in 2002 ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsxkym4nag6hvze9n30g/corner-office/the-fintech-finance-40-patricia-kemp-and-annie-lamont)). Lamont has been named to the Forbes Midas List multiple times as the top-ranking investor in healthcare, and she has served as First Lady of Connecticut since 2019 ([State of Connecticut](http://portal.ct.gov/governor/about/first-lady-annie-lamont?language=en_US)).

The debut fund was itself a talking point: *Fortune* noted that two of the three founders were women and called the $500 million vehicle "the largest fund ever raised by a female-majority VC firm" ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsxkym4nag6hvze9n30g/corner-office/the-fintech-finance-40-patricia-kemp-and-annie-lamont)). Early coverage placed the firm in Greenwich, Connecticut, with offices in Boston and San Francisco ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/08/01/oak-hc-ft/)); by 2022 its stated headquarters had moved to Stamford, though the date of that move was not verified from a fully-read source.

## What the firm invests in — and what it does not

Oak HC/FT's thesis is that healthcare and fintech are two regulated, multi-trillion-dollar systems that are ripe for the same kind of disruption, and that much of healthcare is, underneath, a payments problem. Lamont told *Institutional Investor* that both are "regulated industries that are ripe for disruption, and a big part of the health care system has to do with payments" ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsxkym4nag6hvze9n30g/corner-office/the-fintech-finance-40-patricia-kemp-and-annie-lamont)). On the healthcare side the firm defines its territory narrowly: pharmaceutical services, alternative-care delivery models and sub-population management companies — explicitly not bioengineering startups or medical devices ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/04/18/just-three-years-old-oak-hcft-closes-its-second-fund-with-600-million/)). On the fintech side it backs payments and billing digitisation, digital consumer payments, fraud and risk identification, and identity technology ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/24/oak-hc-ft-1-4-billion/)).

The overlap between the two sectors is where the firm says it finds its edge: startups that blend healthcare and fintech around payments and the digitisation of billing, and companies betting that value-based care — providers taking financial risk and being paid for outcomes rather than visits — will replace fee-for-service medicine ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/24/oak-hc-ft-1-4-billion/)). In 2021 the firm said it was looking at startups working on the cost of delivering care and the ability to engage with complex patients, and that telehealth remained "unbelievably complicated to pull off at scale" despite the pandemic's acceleration ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/24/oak-hc-ft-1-4-billion/)). By 2025–2026 the emphasis had shifted to artificial intelligence: the firm's site says AI is reshaping both sectors, that it now spends more time evaluating technology products and roadmaps, and that the most durable companies will combine "the velocity of modern AI with a genuine understanding of how these industries work" ([Oak HC/FT](https://www.oakhcft.com/)).

The debut fund closed in June 2014 ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/oak-investment-partners-announces-500m-health-and-fintech-fund)), Fund II followed in April 2017 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/04/18/just-three-years-old-oak-hcft-closes-its-second-fund-with-600-million/)), and the oversubscribed $800 million Fund III launched in August 2019 ([Oak HC/FT](https://www.oakhcft.com/news/launching-oak-hc-ft-fund-iii-celebrating-five-years-of-growth-innovation)). Fund IV, at $1.4 billion in February 2021, was the firm's largest to that point and was to be invested across about 20 companies at average checks of $60 million to $100 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/24/oak-hc-ft-1-4-billion/)). Fund V closed at $1.94 billion in 2024, with the firm saying it would "double down" on automation, value-based care, ecommerce enablement and identity protection ([Oak HC/FT](https://www.oakhcft.com/news/oak-hc-ft-closes-fifth-fund-at-1-94-billion-continuing-our-legacy-while-investing-in-the-future)).

Fund VI is the one fund in the sequence that cannot yet be described as closed. Pennsylvania's public-school pension board (PSERS) committed up to $100 million to it on 23 October 2025, recording a target of about $2.0 billion with no hard cap ([Pennsylvania Public School Employees' Retirement System](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/psers/documents/board3/resolutions/2025/2025-69%20PSERB%20Resolution%20Oak%20HC_FT%20Partners%20VI,%20L.P..pdf)). The same documents say Fund VI is expected to make 8–9 early-stage and 15–17 growth-stage investments, with about 75% of capital going to healthcare and the rest to fintech, investing primarily in the [United States](/places/united-states/) and opportunistically in Europe, Latin America and Israel ([Pennsylvania Public School Employees' Retirement System](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/psers/documents/board3/resolutions/2025/2025-69%20PSERB%20Resolution%20Oak%20HC_FT%20Partners%20VI,%20L.P..pdf)). The documents also record the firm's scale at the time: 9 investment professionals and 41 employees, and about $5.3 billion of invested capital as of January 2025 ([Pennsylvania Public School Employees' Retirement System](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/psers/documents/board3/resolutions/2025/2025-69%20PSERB%20Resolution%20Oak%20HC_FT%20Partners%20VI,%20L.P..pdf)).

## Portfolio and exits

By February 2021 Oak HC/FT had invested in nearly 100 startups, making 35 investments in 2020 alone, 20 of them follow-on rounds ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/24/oak-hc-ft-1-4-billion/)). Its healthcare portfolio has centred on care delivery and Medicare-focused companies — Devoted Health, the Medicare Advantage insurer, and VillageMD, the primary-care platform — alongside care-coordination company Unite Us, in whose $35 million Series B the firm led in 2019 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/08/01/oak-hc-ft/)). On the fintech side, early bets included fraud-detection company Feedzai, asset-management platform Pagaya, and Rapyd, which raised $300 million at a $2.5 billion valuation in January 2021 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/24/oak-hc-ft-1-4-billion/)).

The firm's exit record to early 2021 comprised six notable deals: Anthem's acquisition of Aspire Health, Thermo Fisher Scientific's acquisition of Core Informatics, Diplomat's acquisition of LDI Integrated Pharmacy Services, AXA Group's acquisition of Maestro Health, GoDaddy's acquisition of Poynt, and Limeade's public debut ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/24/oak-hc-ft-1-4-billion/)). The first of these, Core Informatics, came in March 2017 on undisclosed terms; Oak HC/FT had led the company's $17.5 million Series B in 2015 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/04/18/just-three-years-old-oak-hcft-closes-its-second-fund-with-600-million/)). Within its own portfolio, the firm reported in February 2021 that 26% of C-suite and executive leadership roles were held by women and 52% of senior management roles were held by women ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/24/oak-hc-ft-1-4-billion/)).

## Blue J and the AI turn

Oak HC/FT's most visible recent deal is Blue J, the Toronto-based generative-AI tax-research platform. On 4 August 2025 Blue J announced a $122 million Series D led by Oak HC/FT and [Sapphire Ventures](/organizations/sapphire-ventures/), with participation from Intrepid Growth Partners and previous investors Ten Coves Capital and CPA.com, just seven months after its Series C ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)). Blue J had more than doubled its revenue and customer base in the first half of 2025, and Oak HC/FT partner Allen Miller said the platform had "solved" tax research "with an elegant AI solution that dramatically accelerates research while raising the bar for accuracy" ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)). The bet held: on 10 November 2026 Blue J raised a $176 million Series E led by JMI Equity at a valuation above $700 million, with Oak HC/FT participating as an existing backer, in a round combining about $80 million of new primary capital with nearly $100 million of secondary share purchases ([Canadian Lawyer](https://www.canadianlawyermag.com/cl-plus/deals/4411)).

The same AI logic drove the firm's other landmark 2025 healthcare deal. In July 2025 Ambience Healthcare raised a $243 million Series C co-led by Oak HC/FT and [Andreessen Horowitz](/organizations/andreessen-horowitz/), with the OpenAI Startup Fund, Kleiner Perkins and Optum Ventures participating, to scale an ambient-AI platform that automates clinical documentation, coding and workflows across more than 100 medical specialties ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchhealthit/news/366628247/Ambience-Healthcare-secures-243M-for-AI-clinical-documentation)). The round valued Ambience at $1.25 billion, and its platform was already in use at major health systems including Cleveland Clinic, Ardent Health and St. Luke's Health System ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/kaylawebster/the-billion-dollar-ai-startup-solving-doctors-biggest-headache/91269783)). Together, the Ambience and Blue J rounds show the firm's current positioning: backing AI-native companies that sit inside the regulated workflows of healthcare and finance, rather than AI as a standalone theme.

**How much money has Oak HC/FT raised?** Its five flagship funds total $5.24 billion: $500 million (2014), $600 million (2017), $800 million (2019), $1.4 billion (2021) and $1.94 billion (2024) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/24/oak-hc-ft-1-4-billion/); [Oak HC/FT](https://www.oakhcft.com/news/oak-hc-ft-closes-fifth-fund-at-1-94-billion-continuing-our-legacy-while-investing-in-the-future)). A sixth fund was being raised in 2025 with a target of about $2.0 billion ([Pennsylvania Public School Employees' Retirement System](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/psers/documents/board3/resolutions/2025/2025-69%20PSERB%20Resolution%20Oak%20HC_FT%20Partners%20VI,%20L.P..pdf)).

**What are Oak HC/FT's best-known investments?** Verified names include Devoted Health and VillageMD in healthcare, Feedzai, Pagaya and Rapyd in fintech, and the AI-era bets Ambience Healthcare and Blue J ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/24/oak-hc-ft-1-4-billion/); [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchhealthit/news/366628247/Ambience-Healthcare-secures-243M-for-AI-clinical-documentation)). Its notable exits include Aspire Health (to Anthem), Core Informatics (to Thermo Fisher Scientific) and Poynt (to GoDaddy) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/24/oak-hc-ft-1-4-billion/)).

**Who founded Oak HC/FT?** Annie Lamont, Andrew Adams and Patricia Kemp, who had worked together since 2003 at Oak Investment Partners ([Pennsylvania Public School Employees' Retirement System](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/psers/documents/board3/resolutions/2025/2025-69%20PSERB%20Resolution%20Oak%20HC_FT%20Partners%20VI,%20L.P..pdf)). Lamont, the managing partner, joined Oak in 1982 and is Connecticut's First Lady ([State of Connecticut](http://portal.ct.gov/governor/about/first-lady-annie-lamont?language=en_US)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not state a single current assets-under-management figure: releases in August 2025 said "over $5.3 billion" ([Blue J](https://www.bluej.com/ca/blog/blue-j-announces-122m-series-d-financing-led-by-oak-hc-ft-and-sapphire-ventures)), while the firm's current website says "~$10 billion" ([Oak HC/FT](https://www.oakhcft.com/)), and the two have not been reconciled from a fully-read source. It does not claim Fund VI has closed: the only verified facts are its ~$2.0 billion target and the PSERS commitment of up to $100 million in October 2025 ([Pennsylvania Public School Employees' Retirement System](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/psers/documents/board3/resolutions/2025/2025-69%20PSERB%20Resolution%20Oak%20HC_FT%20Partners%20VI,%20L.P..pdf)). It does not state realised returns — the firm declined to share IRR figures in 2021 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/02/24/oak-hc-ft-1-4-billion/)), and the 25% net IRR / 2.5x figures in the PSERS documents are targets, not results. It does not give the date of the headquarters move from Greenwich to Stamford, which was not verified, and it does not repeat the claim that the firm was one of the ten best-performing growth equity firms in the world in 2023 as an independent fact, because the Connecticut state biography that carries it does not name the ranking body ([State of Connecticut](http://portal.ct.gov/governor/about/first-lady-annie-lamont?language=en_US)).
