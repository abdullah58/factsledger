## Overview

The *Taipei Times* is Taiwan's only English-language print daily, founded in 1999 by the Liberty Times Group as the island's third English-language newspaper ([Ohio State University Pressbooks](https://ohiostate.pressbooks.pub/intstds4850sp23/?p=262)). Published in Taipei as the English-language sister paper of the Chinese-language *Liberty Times*, it has been, since the *China Post* ended its print edition in 2017, the sole English-language newspaper still printed in Taiwan — and therefore the main English-language window through which the outside world reads Taiwan ([New Bloom Magazine](https://newbloommag.net/2017/04/24/print-newspaper-taiwan/)). Its editorial stance is center-left and pan-Green, aligned with the pro-independence leaning of its parent group, and its pages mix original reporting with wire copy from agencies including the [Associated Press](/organizations/associated-press/) ([New Bloom Magazine](https://newbloommag.net/2017/04/24/print-newspaper-taiwan/)).

## The last English paper standing

The *Taipei Times* entered a small field. When it launched in 1999 it was Taiwan's third English-language newspaper, after *Taiwan News* and *The China Post* ([Ohio State University Pressbooks](https://ohiostate.pressbooks.pub/intstds4850sp23/?p=262)). Both rivals eventually abandoned print: *Taiwan News* went web-only, and the *China Post* ended its print edition in April 2017, after its digital subscriptions surpassed its paper subscriptions for the first time ([New Bloom Magazine](https://newbloommag.net/2017/04/24/print-newspaper-taiwan/)). "AND THERE WAS only one," wrote *New Bloom* magazine at the time: "With the end of the *China Post*'s print edition, only one English language print newspaper remains in Taiwan, the *Taipei Times*" ([New Bloom Magazine](https://newbloommag.net/2017/04/24/print-newspaper-taiwan/)).

That survival came with a cost. The paper is, in *New Bloom*'s assessment, "fundamentally subordinate to the Chinese-language *Liberty Times*, and so insufficient resources are devoted to the *Taipei Times*" ([New Bloom Magazine](https://newbloommag.net/2017/04/24/print-newspaper-taiwan/)). After the pan-Green camp's victory in the 2016 presidential and legislative elections, the paper saw internal downsizing and an increase in content reproduced from news agencies such as AP ([New Bloom Magazine](https://newbloommag.net/2017/04/24/print-newspaper-taiwan/)). The paradox *New Bloom* identifies is that this thinly resourced paper carries a disproportionate burden: English-language print media "remains disproportionately important for shaping international perceptions of Taiwan, and it would be a significant blow to Taiwan if Taiwan were to have no English language print media in the near future, seeing as the *Taipei Times* is all that is left" ([New Bloom Magazine](https://newbloommag.net/2017/04/24/print-newspaper-taiwan/)).

## The Baidu connection: an AP story from 2005

The *Taipei Times*'s role as a wire carrier is the link to [Baidu](/organizations/baidu/). In August 2005 the Associated Press reported on the hottest IPO of the summer: Baidu.com, the Chinese search engine, had risen 354 percent on its first day of trading on Nasdaq, 5 August 2005, closing at $122.54 from a $27 offering price ([Associated Press](https://archives.starbulletin.com/2005/08/13/business/market.html)). The AP story, by Ellen Simon, used Baidu's debut to examine a longer pattern — University of Florida finance professor Jay R. Ritter's finding that IPO companies underperformed similar companies by 4.1 percent over five years — and warned that one-day wonders such as VA Linux (up 697.5 percent on debut in December 1999) and Globe.com (up 606 percent) had later collapsed ([Associated Press](https://archives.starbulletin.com/2005/08/13/business/market.html)). The *Taipei Times*, which reproduces AP wire content for its international business coverage, carried AP's Baidu reporting to its Taiwan readership — an early example of the paper's function as the island's English-language conduit for global business news.
