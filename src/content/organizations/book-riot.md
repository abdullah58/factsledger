## Overview

Book Riot is an independent book-news and media website launched in October 2011 by Jeff O'Neal and Clinton Kabler, and operated by Riot New Media Group, a Brooklyn-based company the two co-founded ([MediaJobs](https://mediajobs.com/riot-new-media-group-a-start-up-pushing-the-boundaries-of-color-gender-and-content/5152/)). The site publishes book news, reviews, lists, newsletters and podcasts across all genres, and describes itself as "the largest independent editorial book site in North America" ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/about/)). Since July 2021 it has also been one of the most closely watched chroniclers of the [United States](/places/united-states/) book-banning movement, through Kelly Jensen's weekly Censorship News column ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/libraries/article/95938-the-battle-against-book-bans-rages-on-pw-talks-with-kelly-jensen.html)).

Book Riot began as a bet on book blogging. In 2011, O'Neal was teaching English at The New School and writing a book blog, while Kabler was following that blog; both felt the most interesting book coverage was happening in the blogging community, and they set out to bring the best bloggers together with the technology and resources to reach a broader audience ([MediaJobs](https://mediajobs.com/riot-new-media-group-a-start-up-pushing-the-boundaries-of-color-gender-and-content/5152/)). The business itself was founded in July 2011 and privately funded by the founders and their friends and family; the site launched in October 2011 ([MediaJobs](https://mediajobs.com/riot-new-media-group-a-start-up-pushing-the-boundaries-of-color-gender-and-content/5152/)). O'Neal brought a PhD in English from Columbia and a lectureship at The New School; Kabler brought an MBA from Rutgers and a background directing sales and marketing at an enterprise software company, and the two had attended college together at the University of Kansas ([MediaJobs](https://mediajobs.com/riot-new-media-group-a-start-up-pushing-the-boundaries-of-color-gender-and-content/5152/)).

## What Book Riot publishes — and who it is for

Book Riot's stated mission is that "writing about books and reading should be just as diverse as books and readers are", and it says it began with the goal of "leading a new discussion around books, readers, and publishing" ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/about/)). Its staff backgrounds reflect that brief: "Some of us are or have been booksellers, teachers, librarians, and authors" ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/about/)). The site sets hiring and editorial standards aiming to reflect current North American census and population data, and reported in June 2023 that 35% of Riot New Media Group's staff are people of color, as are about 34% of Book Riot's contributors — adding that it is "not satisfied with these numbers" ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/about/)).

From the start, the audience was readers under 35, described by the company as traditionally underserved by books and comics coverage, with a commitment to diverse coverage across all genres and creators ([MediaJobs](https://mediajobs.com/riot-new-media-group-a-start-up-pushing-the-boundaries-of-color-gender-and-content/5152/)). The #ReadHarder reading challenge embodied that editorial stance: for 2015, staff and contributors aimed for at least 33% books by people of color and 50% books by women, and met and exceeded both targets — 33% by people of color and 65% by women ([MediaJobs](https://mediajobs.com/riot-new-media-group-a-start-up-pushing-the-boundaries-of-color-gender-and-content/5152/)). Chief of staff Rebecca Joines Schinsky later wrote that "Book Riot has had a progressive mission from the start" ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/things-ive-learned-at-book-riot/)).

The format has changed repeatedly as distribution has shifted. Around 2012–2013 the site ran Reddit-style forums for sharing book news and ebook deals; those forums are now defunct, but they led to two of its most popular newsletters, Today in Books and Book Riot Deals ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/things-ive-learned-at-book-riot/)). Its comics vertical, Panels, has since been folded back into main Book Riot coverage, and a short-lived culinary vertical, Food Riot, ran from 2014 before closing because, in the words of its editor, "the field was too flooded" ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/15-years-in-books-and-publishing/)). A "Book Riot News" message board, moderated by Kelly Jensen, ran from around 2013 to 2015 ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/15-years-in-books-and-publishing/)). Podcasts now sit alongside the written output, led by Book Riot: The Podcast, hosted by O'Neal and Schinsky ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/libraries/article/95938-the-battle-against-book-bans-rages-on-pw-talks-with-kelly-jensen.html)).

The clearest measure of the site's current shape is email. In early 2013, BookRiot.com averaged 14 visits per month per Facebook follower; by 2024 that figure had fallen to about a tenth of a visit per follower, as social platforms stopped reliably sending readers to publishers ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/starting-book-riot-today/)). Email became the replacement: "30+ newsletters and 3 millions subscriptions later, this is the spinning molten core of what Book Riot does," co-founder Jeff O'Neal wrote in April 2024 ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/starting-book-riot-today/)). The site also runs a paid membership programme, All Access, offering its content archive, a curated new-book discovery tool called the New Release Index, and community features ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/15-years-in-books-and-publishing/)). In October 2026 it marked 15 years "as an independent media company" ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/15-years-in-books-and-publishing/)).

## Censorship reporting and the EveryLibrary surveys

Book Riot's most consequential work in recent years is its coverage of book banning. When a surge in book challenges hit schools and libraries in late 2020, librarian and journalist Kelly Jensen filed the first edition of her weekly Censorship News roundup for Book Riot in July 2021, "offering commentary, analysis, and often unflinching criticism, along with an extensive rundown of local headlines and her own reported book-banning developments from across the country" ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/libraries/article/95938-the-battle-against-book-bans-rages-on-pw-talks-with-kelly-jensen.html)). Publishers Weekly calls Jensen "one of the most important voices chronicling [the] movement to ban books", saying her column has "effectively creat[ed] a historical record as it unfolds" ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/libraries/article/95938-the-battle-against-book-bans-rages-on-pw-talks-with-kelly-jensen.html)). In March 2024, Library Journal recognised her work with a 2024 Mover & Shaker award ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/libraries/article/95938-the-battle-against-book-bans-rages-on-pw-talks-with-kelly-jensen.html)). Jensen herself has said that when she first proposed the column, her boss "questioned whether there would be enough content to do it every week. We both wish there wasn't" ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/libraries/article/95938-the-battle-against-book-bans-rages-on-pw-talks-with-kelly-jensen.html)).

The profession has formally recognised that work. In August 2023 the American Association of School Librarians commended "Book Riot's Weekly Censorship Roundup and Archives by Kelly Jensen", one of twenty outstanding programmes, events and products recognised that year for supporting the school librarian profession ([American Library Association](https://www.ala.org/news/press-releases/2023/08/aasl-commends-book-riots-weekly-censorship-roundup-and-archives-kelly-jensen)). The commendation recognised the roundup "for helping defend the intellectual freedom of all school librarians, students, and community members by curating resources to educate and empower the public to fight censorship", on a resolution from the Louisiana Association of School Librarians co-signed by 25 other AASL chapters ([American Library Association](https://www.ala.org/news/press-releases/2023/08/aasl-commends-book-riots-weekly-censorship-roundup-and-archives-kelly-jensen)).

Book Riot also moved from reporting on book bans to measuring public opinion about them. With the EveryLibrary Institute it ran a series of three surveys of parents and guardians in September–November 2023, gathering 3,206 respondents in total ([EveryLibrary Institute](https://www.everylibraryinstitute.org/parent_perception_public_libraries_2023)). The first survey, on public libraries and book bans, covered 853 parents and guardians with children under 18 in September 2023, with a margin of error of ±4.6% ([EveryLibrary Institute](https://www.everylibraryinstitute.org/parent_perception_public_libraries_2023)). Its headline findings: 92% of respondents felt their children are safe at the library, 74% agreed or somewhat agreed that book bans infringe on their right to make decisions for their children, and 58% thought librarians should be primarily responsible for what books are selected in the public library ([EveryLibrary Institute](https://www.everylibraryinstitute.org/parent_perception_public_libraries_2023)). The stat that captures the survey's tension is the split on punishment: 49% of respondents agreed or somewhat agreed that "Librarians should be prosecuted for giving children access to certain books", even as 92% said they felt their children were safe at the library — a contradiction the survey itself surfaced rather than resolved ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/parent-perception-of-libraries-survey/)).

Book Riot's own reporting on the museum world connects it directly to this site's graph: on 22 January 2021 it profiled the [Banned Books Museum](/organizations/banned-books-museum/) in [Tallinn](/places/tallinn/), [Estonia](/places/estonia/), describing it as "part museum, part bookstore for books that haven't just been banned, but also ones that have been censored, restricted, or even burned", with a collection of over 100 books organised by country and programming including a book club, podcast and discussion groups ([Book Riot](https://bookriot.com/banned-books-museum/)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim a current monthly-visitor or traffic figure: the only verified audience number is from 2015 (1.2 million monthly unique visitors for Book Riot and Panels combined), and aggregator estimates were not used. It does not claim Brooklyn is still the company's headquarters — that is the last verified location, from 2015. It does not present "largest independent editorial book site in North America" as an independently verified ranking; that is Book Riot's own self-description, attributed as such. It does not settle the survey's "waste of time" figure: Book Riot's article says 67% and the EveryLibrary Institute report page says 64% agree or somewhat agree, and both are stated with their sources. It does not resolve whether the parent company is "Riot New Media Group" or "Riot New Media Company" — the site's own materials and the MediaJobs interview say "Group", while Wikipedia's infobox says "Company". It does not claim the 49% prosecution figure and the 92% safety figure come from the same question; they are separate survey findings that sit in tension with each other.
