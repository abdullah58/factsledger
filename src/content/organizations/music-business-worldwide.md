## Overview

Music Business Worldwide (MBW) is a London-based trade publication covering the global music business, founded in 2015 by former *Music Week* editor Tim Ingham. It publishes daily news, analysis, jobs and data for music-industry executives, and is backed by [Penske Media Corporation](/organizations/penske-media-corporation/) (PMC), the owner of *[Rolling Stone](/organizations/rolling-stone/)* and *Variety* ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/who-we-are/); [Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/penske-medias-substantial-equity-investment-in-music-business-worldwide-strengthens-partnership)).

MBW calls itself the leading intelligence and jobs platform for the global music business, and its audience has roughly doubled since PMC invested: over 200,000 unique users a month and 7.3 million page views in 2019, against over 400,000 unique users a month and more than 10 million page views in 2024 ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/penske-medias-substantial-equity-investment-in-music-business-worldwide-strengthens-partnership); [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/what-we-do/)). What sets it apart from other trade titles is not scale but scoops: MBW's reporting on Spotify's playlist practices and streaming economics has repeatedly forced responses from the companies it covers, and its ownership ties it directly to the biggest names in music media — including *Billboard* itself.

## Founding and the Ingham years

Tim Ingham launched Music Business Worldwide in January 2015 after more than three years as editor of *Music Week*, the UK's established music trade paper, stepping down in late 2014 to strike out on his own ([Record of the Day](https://www.recordoftheday.com/news-and-press/tim-ingham-announced-as-non-executive-director-of-impel)). Within its first year the new site was already being described as a leading provider of analysis and news on the global music industry, with expansion plans for 2016 ([Record of the Day](https://www.recordoftheday.com/news-and-press/tim-ingham-announced-as-non-executive-director-of-impel)). Ingham arrived with a national-press pedigree, having written about the music business for *The Guardian*, *The Independent*, *The Observer*, *The Daily Mail* and *NME*, and in 2014 he had been named one of the world's 500 Most Influential Britons — and one of the 20 most influential British music executives — by *The Sunday Times* and Debrett's ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/who-we-are/)).

The early team was built from the same trade-press world. Murray Stassen, now Editor-in-Chief, joined as Deputy Editor in 2018 and became Editor in 2020 after two years as Deputy Editor of *Music Week*; Rhian Jones has written for MBW since 2015, and Dave Roberts, a former *Music Week* publisher, joined in 2017 and is now Head of Publishing & Partnerships ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/who-we-are/)). In 2017 Ingham received a Gold Badge Award from the British Academy of Songwriters, Composers and Authors (BASCA), honouring those who had worked tirelessly to support the British songwriting and composing community ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/who-we-are/)).

## The Penske partnership — and the Billboard connection

MBW's independence lasted three years before the big American media groups came calling. In November 2018 it struck a global content partnership with *Rolling Stone* and its owner Penske Media Corporation: Ingham would write a weekly industry column appearing first on *Rolling Stone*'s website and in its monthly magazine, while news and feature content from *Rolling Stone* and fellow PMC title [Variety](/organizations/variety/) would be shared on MBW's platform ([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/bwwmusic/article/Rolling-Stone-and-Music-Business-Worldwide-Announce-Global-Content-Partnership-20181112)). At the time MBW was described as "a London-based company that remains fully independent" ([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/bwwmusic/article/Rolling-Stone-and-Music-Business-Worldwide-Announce-Global-Content-Partnership-20181112)).

That independence became partial in February 2020, when PMC — headquartered in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) and Los Angeles — announced a strategic equity investment in MBW, bringing it into a 19-brand portfolio alongside *Rolling Stone*, PMC's music analytics platform Alpha Data and Variety.com ([MusicRow](https://musicrow.com/2020/02/penske-media-makes-equity-investment-in-music-business-worldwide/); [Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/penske-medias-substantial-equity-investment-in-music-business-worldwide-strengthens-partnership)). The entanglement deepened that September, when PMC and [MRC](/organizations/mrc/) formed the PMRC joint venture to operate *Billboard*, *[The Hollywood Reporter](/organizations/the-hollywood-reporter/)* and *[Vibe](/organizations/vibe/)* alongside *Variety*, *Rolling Stone* and MBW — and PMRC took an interest in MBW itself ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/companies/billboard/); [Talking Biz News](https://talkingbiznews.com/they-talk-biz-news/variety-parent-to-operate-hollywood-reporter/)). The result is a genuine oddity of trade journalism: MBW, a publication that reports critically on *Billboard* — a trade and consumer music publication based in the [United States](/places/united-states/) that hosts the [Billboard Music Awards](/events/billboard-music-awards/) — is partly owned by the same venture that operates *Billboard* ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/companies/billboard/)).

## The investigations that made its name

MBW's reputation was built on two Spotify investigations that other outlets picked up and Spotify itself had to answer. In August 2016 MBW reported that Spotify was filling mood playlists such as "Piano & Chill" with bespoke music by artists who did not appear to exist outside the platform, with sources saying the company commissioned producers to create tracks under untraceable pseudonyms ([NPR](https://www.wcmu.org/music/2017-07-12/spotify-is-accused-of-creating-fake-artists-but-what-is-a-fake-artist)). When *Vulture* revived the story in July 2017, Spotify denied it to *Billboard*, saying "We do not and have never created 'fake' artists", and MBW fired back with a list of 50 such artists ([NPR](https://www.wcmu.org/music/2017-07-12/spotify-is-accused-of-creating-fake-artists-but-what-is-a-fake-artist)). NPR named nine of them — including Gabriel Parker, Charlie Key and Anna Olgica — and noted that some were traced to Swedish producers Andreas Romdhane and Josef Svedlund, while a Spotify spokesperson insisted the artists "clearly do exist" ([NPR](https://www.wcmu.org/music/2017-07-12/spotify-is-accused-of-creating-fake-artists-but-what-is-a-fake-artist)).

Eighteen months later MBW exposed an alleged streaming scam with harder numbers. In February 2018 it reported that a Bulgarian operation had used two third-party playlists, 'Soulful Music' and 'Music From The Heart', to draw money from Spotify's royalty pool: 'Soulful Music' reached No. 35 on Spotify's global playlist chart in September 2017 despite having only 1,797 followers, its 467 tracks mostly barely over the 30-second minimum for a payable stream ([NME](https://www.nme.com/news/music/playlist-maker-scam-nearly-1-million-spotify-2246568)). MBW estimated the operation could have taken somewhere north of $1 million over four months, using paid premium accounts to generate plays — a scheme that, as MBW itself noted, probably broke no laws, since the accounts were paid for ([NME](https://www.nme.com/news/music/playlist-maker-scam-nearly-1-million-spotify-2246568)). Spotify did not confirm the swindle but said it took artificial manipulation of streaming "extremely seriously" ([NME](https://www.nme.com/news/music/playlist-maker-scam-nearly-1-million-spotify-2246568)).

## What MBW publishes today

Beyond breaking news, MBW is known for turning public filings into headline numbers. Its analysis of the 2019 fiscal figures for Universal Music Group, Sony Music Group and Warner Music Group showed the three majors took in $22.9 million a day from streaming in 2019 — just under $1 million an hour — a figure picked up across the music press ([Consequence](https://consequence.net/2020/02/major-labels-million-streaming-profits/)). That calculation habit is now a product in its own right: the MBW Database offers dynamically updated charts covering the financial and market realities of the global business, sold as part of the MBW+ subscription alongside premium analysis, opinion columns and an archive of more than eight years ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/mbw-plus/)). The subscription tier, launched in 2023, sits beside the free site, the *Music Business UK* magazine, the annual MBW Yearbook, the Music Business Jobs board and the Music Business UK Awards in London ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/what-we-do/)).

The audience figures tell the growth story in one line: MBW went from over 200,000 unique users a month in 2019 to over 400,000 a month in 2024, with page views rising from 7.3 million to more than 10 million over the same period — growth that tracks the PMC investment and the subscription launch, and that explains why the industry's most senior executives, from Atlantic Records' Craig Kallman to Live Nation's Michael Rapino, publicly call it their first read of the day ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/penske-medias-substantial-equity-investment-in-music-business-worldwide-strengthens-partnership); [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/what-we-do/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who founded Music Business Worldwide?**
Tim Ingham, the former editor of *Music Week*, founded MBW and launched the website in January 2015 ([Record of the Day](https://www.recordoftheday.com/news-and-press/tim-ingham-announced-as-non-executive-director-of-impel)). He remains its Founder and Publisher, and between 2018 and 2021 he wrote more than 100 columns analysing the music industry for *Rolling Stone* ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/who-we-are/)).

**Who owns Music Business Worldwide?**
MBW is a private company founded by Tim Ingham. Penske Media Corporation, owner of *Rolling Stone* and *Variety*, made a strategic equity investment in February 2020, and the PMRC joint venture that operates *Billboard* also holds an interest in MBW ([MusicRow](https://musicrow.com/2020/02/penske-media-makes-equity-investment-in-music-business-worldwide/); [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/companies/billboard/)). No exact ownership percentage has been verified.

**What is MBW best known for?**
Its Spotify investigations: the 2016–2017 "fake artists" reporting, which alleged Spotify stocked playlists with music by pseudonymous artists, and the 2018 exposure of an alleged Bulgarian playlist scam worth nearly $1 million ([NPR](https://www.wcmu.org/music/2017-07-12/spotify-is-accused-of-creating-fake-artists-but-what-is-a-fake-artist); [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/music/playlist-maker-scam-nearly-1-million-spotify-2246568)). Both stories were widely picked up and drew responses from Spotify.

**Is Music Business Worldwide the same as Music Week?**
No. *Music Week* is a separate, older UK trade paper that Ingham used to edit before founding MBW in 2015 ([Record of the Day](https://www.recordoftheday.com/news-and-press/tim-ingham-announced-as-non-executive-director-of-impel)). Several MBW staff, including Editor-in-Chief Murray Stassen, previously worked at *Music Week* ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/who-we-are/)).

**How big is MBW's audience?**
Over 400,000 unique users visit the MBW site each month, sharing over 10 million page views in 2024, according to Google Analytics figures published by MBW ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/what-we-do/)). In 2019, the year before PMC invested, the figures were over 200,000 monthly users and 7.3 million page views ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/penske-medias-substantial-equity-investment-in-music-business-worldwide-strengthens-partnership)).
