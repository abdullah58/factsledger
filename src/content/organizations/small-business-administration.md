## Overview

The U.S. Small Business Administration is the federal government's principal agency for supporting small business — the lead federal voice for the sector, delivering services through field offices and partnerships with public and private organizations ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/RL/HTML/RL33243.web.html); [Greater Fort Myers Chamber of Commerce](https://fortmyers.org/blog/south-florida-district-office-news-release-sba-administrator-kelly-loeffler-issues-statement/)). Congress created it in 1953 with the Small Business Act (P.L. 83-163), declaring that the government should "aid, counsel, assist, and protect, insofar as is possible, the interests of small-business concerns" in order to preserve free competitive enterprise ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/RL/HTML/RL33243.web.html)).

The agency's roots run back to the Great Depression: it assumed some functions of the Reconstruction Finance Corporation, which the federal government had created in 1932 to fund businesses of all sizes and later to finance war production, before the RFC was disbanded in the early 1950s following charges of political favoritism in its lending ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/RL/HTML/RL33243.web.html)). Since 1953 the SBA has run a sprawling set of programs — loan guarantees through the 7(a), 504 and microloan programs, disaster loans, venture capital through the Small Business Investment Company program, innovation grants through SBIR/STTR, entrepreneurial training networks, and federal contracting programs meant to reserve a share of government purchases for small firms ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/RL/HTML/RL33243.web.html)).

Former U.S. Senator Kelly Loeffler was sworn in as the 28th Administrator of the SBA on February 20, 2025, at the agency's Washington, D.C. headquarters ([Greater Fort Myers Chamber of Commerce](https://fortmyers.org/blog/south-florida-district-office-news-release-sba-administrator-kelly-loeffler-issues-statement/)). In her statement that day she said she was grateful to President Trump for entrusting her "with the privilege and responsibility of serving America's 34 million small businesses," and pledged to restore the agency to its founding mission of "growing small business, fueling free enterprise, and driving economic resilience" ([Greater Fort Myers Chamber of Commerce](https://fortmyers.org/blog/south-florida-district-office-news-release-sba-administrator-kelly-loeffler-issues-statement/)). The release frames the SBA as "the leading voice for small businesses within the federal government" and "the nation's leading voice for America's 34 million small businesses," underscoring that the agency's statutory role goes beyond lending into advocacy and counseling ([Greater Fort Myers Chamber of Commerce](https://fortmyers.org/blog/south-florida-district-office-news-release-sba-administrator-kelly-loeffler-issues-statement/)).

## The Million Dollar Pitch Competition

The most-covered SBA activity of 2026 is also its least conventional: the Million Dollar Pitch Competition, a televised contest the agency staged under a banner it calls Freedom 250 ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/17/trump-million-dollar-pitch-competition-judges-contestants/91811105007/)). The SBA describes the initiative as "recognizing the entrepreneurs and small businesses that helped build America's first 250 years, and those who will help build its next 250" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/17/trump-million-dollar-pitch-competition-judges-contestants/91811105007/)). The finalists were selected from 1,182 businesses representing all 50 states, evaluated on their ability to strengthen American competitiveness, demonstrate strong performance and innovation, create economic impact and quality jobs, and show sound business fundamentals ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/17/trump-million-dollar-pitch-competition-judges-contestants/91811105007/)).

Five companies made the finale: Compotech Inc., a defense technology company in Brewer, Maine; Plas-Tech Tooling, a plastic mold manufacturer in Garner, Iowa; Red River, a pressure vessel manufacturer in Gillette, Wyoming; VetPowered LLC, a machinery manufacturing firm in San Diego, California; and Winton Machine, a metalworking machinery manufacturer in Suwanee, Georgia ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/17/trump-million-dollar-pitch-competition-judges-contestants/91811105007/)). All five are manufacturing, tooling, defense and aerospace suppliers — no influencer brands among them ([BeezLoop](https://beezloop.com/nicki-minaj-laura-ingraham-sba-million-dollar-pitch/)).

The judging panel was announced at the end of August 2026: rapper [Nicki Minaj](/people/nicki-minaj/), investor and private equity fund manager Grant Cardone, and Airbnb co-founder and U.S. Chief Design Officer [Joe Gebbia](/people/joe-gebbia/), with actor Dean Cain — the 1990s *Lois & Clark* Superman — hosting ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/17/trump-million-dollar-pitch-competition-judges-contestants/91811105007/); [BeezLoop](https://beezloop.com/nicki-minaj-laura-ingraham-sba-million-dollar-pitch/)). The finale taped in Washington on Friday, September 18, 2026, and the SBA said it would be "produced on Sept. 18 for a nationally distributed primetime special premiering in October, with additional details to be announced" ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/17/trump-million-dollar-pitch-competition-judges-contestants/91811105007/)).

One structural detail matters more than the celebrity framing: the prize money is not taxpayer money. The $1 million pool is funded by Clover Network Inc., the payments company — $400,000 for first place, then $250,000, $150,000, $125,000 and $75,000 down the line, so every finalist leaves with growth capital ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/17/trump-million-dollar-pitch-competition-judges-contestants/91811105007/); [BeezLoop](https://beezloop.com/nicki-minaj-laura-ingraham-sba-million-dollar-pitch/)). Loeffler herself likened the event to President Trump's long-running NBC show *The Apprentice*, saying it would be "bringing the boardroom energy" to Washington ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/17/trump-million-dollar-pitch-competition-judges-contestants/91811105007/)). On the day of the taping, Minaj appeared alongside Loeffler on Fox News' *The Ingraham Angle* to promote the special; asked what she would say to critics of the partnership, she answered, "Absolutely nothing" ([BeezLoop](https://beezloop.com/nicki-minaj-laura-ingraham-sba-million-dollar-pitch/)).

## From Depression-era lender to small-business advocate

The SBA's evolution tracks the federal government's shifting theory of how to keep small firms alive. Its 1953 charter gave it loans, loan guarantees, contracts and counseling; the Small Business Investment Act of 1958 later added a licensed venture-capital channel through SBICs ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/RL/HTML/RL33243.web.html)). The agency's disaster loan program became its most direct form of aid — the only SBA loans that go straight to the ultimate borrower, covering homeowners, renters, businesses and nonprofits in declared disaster areas ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/RL/HTML/RL33243.web.html)).

In 2020 the agency was thrust to the center of the COVID-19 economic response when the CARES Act created the $349 billion Paycheck Protection Program, providing forgivable low-interest loans to small businesses; the authorization was later increased to $659 billion and then to $813.7 billion across subsequent legislation ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/RL/HTML/RL33243.web.html)). The Trump administration's second-term posture has been to refocus the agency on its statutory mission while adding showpieces like the pitch competition under the Freedom 250 banner — a departure from the traditional portfolio of guarantees, counseling and contracting that previous profiles of the agency covered exclusively ([USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/17/trump-million-dollar-pitch-competition-judges-contestants/91811105007/); [Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/RL/HTML/RL33243.web.html)).
