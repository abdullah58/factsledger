## Overview

Allianz Australia is the Australian arm of the Munich-based [Allianz Group](/organizations/allianz-group/) — a wholly owned subsidiary serving about 4 million Australians and ranked the country's third-largest non-life insurer behind IAG and [Suncorp](/organizations/suncorp/) ([CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/money/insurance/insurance-advice/insurers/allianz); [Insurance News](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/corporate/westpac-deal-cements-allianzs-position-at-number-3)). Its Australian story began in 1914 as the Manufacturers' Mutual Accident Insurance Association (MMI), a workers' compensation underwriter; MMI acquired Allianz's Australian operation in 1986, and the combined business was rebranded Allianz Australia in 2000 ([CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/money/insurance/insurance-advice/insurers/allianz)).

Much of the company's consumer presence is invisible: Allianz is the underwriter behind insurance brands sold by banks, credit unions and building societies — including [Westpac](/organizations/westpac/), [NAB](/organizations/national-australia-bank/), St.George, Bank of Melbourne, BankSA and TIO ([CHOICE](https://www.choice.com.au/money/insurance/insurance-advice/insurers/allianz)). In July 2021 it completed the $725 million acquisition of Westpac's general insurance arm plus a 20-year exclusive distribution agreement, absorbing more than 350 Westpac staff and taking headcount past 5,100 ([Insurance News](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/corporate/westpac-deal-cements-allianzs-position-at-number-3)).

## A decade under regulators' eyes

No profile of Allianz Australia is complete without its compliance record. In 2019 [APRA](/organizations/australian-prudential-regulation-authority/) imposed an additional $250 million capital requirement after a risk-governance self-assessment — ordered in the wake of the 2017 Prudential Inquiry into [Commonwealth Bank](/organizations/commonwealth-bank/) — exposed longstanding weaknesses in risk culture, risk governance and risk management ([Financial Standard](https://www.financialstandard.com.au/news/150m-capital-overlay-no-more-for-allianz-australia-179795942)). Allianz had also been a case study in the [Hayne royal commission](/events/hayne-royal-commission/), which referred compliance-system issues to APRA ([APRA](https://www.apra.gov.au/news-and-publications/apra-agrees-court-enforceable-undertaking-allianz-australia)).

In March 2021 APRA accepted a court enforceable undertaking in which Allianz acknowledged it had taken a "narrow, legalistic view of compliance" with inadequate escalation processes and low board-level awareness — and its German parent conceded it had "fallen significantly short" of APRA's oversight expectations ([APRA](https://www.apra.gov.au/news-and-publications/apra-agrees-court-enforceable-undertaking-allianz-australia)). After the transformation programs were completed, APRA removed the residual $150 million overlay in 2022, with deputy chair Helen Rowell warning the weaknesses "should not have happened, and we will not tolerate any recurrence" ([Financial Standard](https://www.financialstandard.com.au/news/150m-capital-overlay-no-more-for-allianz-australia-179795942)).

Then, in February 2025, the NSW Supreme Court convicted Allianz Australia and AWP Australia over false or misleading travel-insurance statements, imposing criminal fines totaling $16.8 million ($13.5 million and $3.3 million respectively) — the websites had advertised maximum benefits without stating the sub-limits, terms and exclusions that reduced them ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/australias-asic-wins-case-against-allianz-awp-misleading-claims-2025-02-28/)). Later in 2025, Allianz acquired [RAA](/organizations/raa/)'s general insurance business, with S&P affirming its position as Australia's third-largest property-and-casualty insurer ([Insurance Asia](https://insuranceasia.com/insurance/news/allianz-australia-absorbs-raa-without-eroding-capital)).
