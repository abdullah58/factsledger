## Overview

BMW AG — Bayerische Motoren Werke, or Bavarian Motor Works — is a German luxury automaker and motorcycle maker headquartered in Munich, and the world's leading premium carmaker. It began in 1916 building aircraft engines, survived a near-takeover in 1959 thanks to the Quandt family, and today sells cars under the BMW, MINI and Rolls-Royce brands plus BMW Motorrad motorcycles ([BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/company/history.html)). In 2024 the BMW Group delivered 2,450,804 vehicles and 210,408 motorcycles, earned €142.4 billion in revenue, and kept the BMW brand first in the global premium segment ([BMW Group](https://newsroom.bmwgroup.com/emea/bmw-group-maintains-bev-growth-path-in-challenging-market-situation-in-2024); [BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/report/2024/financial-statements/income-statement/index.html)).

## From aircraft engines to the Quandt rescue

BMW's origins lie in two Munich aviation firms: the aircraft manufacturer Flugmaschinenfabrik Gustav Otto and the aero-engine producer Rapp Motorenwerke ([BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/company/history.html)). In 1916, at the government's request, Otto's firm was reorganised as Bayerische Flugzeugwerke (BFW); a year later Rapp Motorenwerke was renamed Bayerische Motoren Werke, and in 1922 the BMW name and engine operations were transferred to BFW — which is why 7 March 1916, the foundation of BFW, is BMW's official founding date ([BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/company/history.html)). Britannica dates the BMW name to July 1917 and notes the company began producing motorcycles in the 1920s and entered the automobile business in 1928 ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/56801)). During the Second World War BMW built the world's first jet airplane engines, used by the Luftwaffe, and after the war Allied troops requisitioned its plants and dismantled their machinery, leaving the Munich and Berlin works making household appliances as a temporary solution ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/56801); [BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/company/history.html)).

The company's defining moment came in 1959. By then BMW was on the verge of bankruptcy, and its managers planned to sell the firm to [Daimler](/organizations/daimler/)-Benz ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/56801)). At the annual general meeting on 9 December 1959, small shareholders and the workforce rejected the offer; instead, Herbert Quandt expanded his stockholding and, with temporary government financial support, turned BMW around the following year ([BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/company/history.html)). The rescue worked: the BMW 700 succeeded, followed by the 1500, and members of the Quandt family continued to hold a significant stake in the company ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/56801)). The four-cylinder headquarters tower and the bowl-shaped BMW Museum, opened in Munich on 18 May 1973, became the physical symbol of the recovered company ([BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/company/history.html)).

## Three brands, one premium group

BMW grew into a multi-brand group through two very different British deals. In 1994 it bought the Rover Group, lost roughly $4 billion on the venture, and sold Land Rover to [Ford](/organizations/ford/) in 2000 — a failure Britannica records bluntly ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/56801)). The other two British bets succeeded. In July 1998 BMW obtained the brand and naming rights for Rolls-Royce motor cars from Rolls-Royce plc; after Volkswagen held Rolls-Royce until the end of 2002, BMW took full responsibility for Rolls-Royce Motor Cars and built a new plant and headquarters at Goodwood in southern England, with production of newly developed models from the start of 2003 ([BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/company/history.html)). The new MINI launched in 2001 as the first premium vehicle in the small-car segment, and the BMW i3 followed in 2013 as the group's first series-produced fully electric model ([BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/company/history.html)).

The 2024 group picture was mixed: total deliveries of 2,450,804 vehicles fell 4.0 per cent, hit by delivery stops for a supplied Integrated Brake System component and weak demand in China, while fully electric deliveries grew 13.5 per cent to 426,594 and electrified vehicles reached 24.2 per cent of sales ([BMW Group](https://newsroom.bmwgroup.com/emea/bmw-group-maintains-bev-growth-path-in-challenging-market-situation-in-2024)). Group revenue was €142,380 million, profit before tax €10,971 million and net profit €7,678 million, with 159,104 employees at year-end and 33 production locations in 16 countries ([BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/report/2024/financial-statements/income-statement/index.html); [BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/report/2024/management-report/index.html)). M GmbH set another record at 206,582 cars, and chairman Oliver Zipse noted that BMW sold more than twice as many electric vehicles in China in 2024 as two established German competitors combined ([BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/report/2024/to-our-stakeholders/statement-chairman-board-of-management/index.html)).

## BMW today: Neue Klasse and technology-open

BMW's answer to the electric transition is the Neue Klasse, decided in 2020 in the middle of the pandemic ([BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/report/2024/to-our-stakeholders/statement-chairman-board-of-management/index.html)). The first model, the fully electric iX3, debuted at IAA Mobility in Munich in September 2025 with 805 km of WLTP range, 400 kW peak charging, sixth-generation eDrive technology and a new "Heart of Joy" central control unit, and BMW says the platform will underpin 40 new models and updates by 2027 ([BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/company/history.html)). Production started at a new plant in Debrecen, Hungary, with a sedan from the Munich main plant to follow; Munich is to build only electric vehicles from 2027, and at least six Neue Klasse models are planned within 24 months of the start of production ([BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/report/2024/to-our-stakeholders/statement-chairman-board-of-management/index.html)).

Unlike rivals that bet everything on batteries, BMW calls its approach "technology-open": highly efficient combustion engines, plug-in hybrids and all-electric vehicles, plus a production hydrogen fuel-cell vehicle from 2028, a plan for which it deepened its partnership with [Toyota](/organizations/toyota/) in 2024 ([BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/report/2024/to-our-stakeholders/statement-chairman-board-of-management/index.html)). The strategy is backed by heavy spending — research and development above €9 billion and capital expenditure above €11.8 billion in 2024 — and by more than 40 new vehicles across all brands and drivetrains by the end of 2027 ([BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/report/2024/to-our-stakeholders/statement-chairman-board-of-management/index.html)).

## The voice in the car: Cerence

BMW's voice assistant is one of the industry's longest-running AI partnerships. BMW has worked with [Cerence](/organizations/cerence/) since 2018 — before Cerence spun off from [Nuance Communications](/organizations/nuance-communications/) — and Cerence has powered the BMW Intelligent Personal Assistant since its earliest version ([Voicebot.ai](https://voicebot.ai/2022/09/12/bmw-sticks-with-cerence-to-build-next-car-voice-assistant/)). In September 2022 BMW chose Cerence to build a new generation of the assistant for the 7 Series and i7, with improved natural-language understanding: drivers can open the sunroof by saying "I want to see the sky", the system learns their preferences over time, and it can answer spoken questions about car functions while travelling ([Voicebot.ai](https://voicebot.ai/2022/09/12/bmw-sticks-with-cerence-to-build-next-car-voice-assistant/)). The same upgrade lets drivers activate Apple's Siri by voice alone, without pressing the push-to-talk button, and gives drivers in China voice-powered control of WeChat features ([Voicebot.ai](https://voicebot.ai/2022/09/12/bmw-sticks-with-cerence-to-build-next-car-voice-assistant/)). BMW was also the first carmaker to add Cerence's driver-customized wake word, letting owners replace "Hey BMW" with a word of their own ([Voicebot.ai](https://voicebot.ai/2022/09/12/bmw-sticks-with-cerence-to-build-next-car-voice-assistant/)).

## The AdBlue cartel: a €372.8 million fine

BMW's record includes a major antitrust conviction shared with its two German rivals. On 8 July 2021 the European Commission found that Daimler, BMW and the Volkswagen group (Volkswagen, [Audi](/organizations/audi/) and Porsche) had breached EU antitrust rules by colluding on technical development in nitrogen-oxide cleaning for new diesel passenger cars, and imposed fines totalling €875,189,000 ([European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/it/ip_21_3581/IP_21_3581_EN.pdf)). BMW's share was €372,827,000; the Volkswagen group paid €502,362,000, while Daimler paid nothing, having revealed the cartel and received full immunity — avoiding a fine of about €727 million ([European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/it/ip_21_3581/IP_21_3581_EN.pdf)).

Between 25 June 2009 and 1 October 2014 the manufacturers held regular technical meetings on selective catalytic reduction (SCR) technology, which cleans nitrogen oxide from diesel exhaust by injecting urea ("AdBlue"), and agreed on AdBlue tank sizes and ranges and a common understanding of average AdBlue consumption, exchanging commercially sensitive information ([European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/it/ip_21_3581/IP_21_3581_EN.pdf)). All five manufacturers possessed technology that could clean emissions beyond what EU law required, but avoided competing on using its full potential; the Commission called it the first cartel based solely on restricting technical development ([European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/it/ip_21_3581/IP_21_3581_EN.pdf)). The Commission stressed the case was separate from defeat-device investigations and that there were no indications the parties coordinated the use of illegal defeat devices to cheat regulatory testing ([European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/it/ip_21_3581/IP_21_3581_EN.pdf)).

## Frequently asked questions

**When was BMW founded?**
BMW counts 7 March 1916 — the foundation of Bayerische Flugzeugwerke in Munich — as its official founding date; the BMW name itself dates to the 1917 renaming of Rapp Motorenwerke ([BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/company/history.html)).

**Who owns BMW?**
BMW AG is a public company in which the Quandt family — Herbert Quandt rescued it from a Daimler-Benz takeover in 1959 — continues to hold a significant stake, with the rest in public float ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/56801)).

**What brands does BMW own?**
The BMW Group sells cars under the BMW, MINI and Rolls-Royce brands and motorcycles under BMW Motorrad; MINI relaunched in 2001 and Rolls-Royce Motor Cars came fully under BMW in 2003 ([BMW Group](https://www.bmwgroup.com/en/company/history.html)).

**Does BMW use Cerence technology?**
Yes. Cerence has powered the BMW Intelligent Personal Assistant since its earliest version, and built the upgraded assistant in the 7 Series and i7; BMW was the first carmaker to offer Cerence's driver-customized wake word ([Voicebot.ai](https://voicebot.ai/2022/09/12/bmw-sticks-with-cerence-to-build-next-car-voice-assistant/)).

**Does BMW offer all-wheel drive?**
Yes — BMW made all-wheel drive available on the 325i (E30) in 1985, following Audi's quattro, which had debuted at Geneva in 1980 ([Autoblog](https://www.autoblog.com/news/audi-quattro-40-years-anniversary-history)).

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not name a single founder for BMW: the company's own history traces it to two firms, BFW and Rapp Motorenwerke, and no individual founder is asserted.
- It does not state exact Quandt family shareholdings; the percentages circulating in tertiary sources were not verified from a primary source.
- Delivery figures follow BMW's January 2025 press release; the final Group Report gives a slightly different BEV total (426,536 vs 426,594), and that 58-unit difference is flagged rather than silently resolved.
- It does not claim BMW used defeat devices: the European Commission explicitly states its cartel case is separate from defeat-device investigations, with no indication of coordination on them.
- It makes no claim about Cerence's spin-off date or total vehicles shipped — those facts were not verified from sources read for this profile.
