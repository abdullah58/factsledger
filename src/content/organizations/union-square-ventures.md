## Overview

Union Square Ventures (USV) is a New York City venture capital firm founded in 2003 by Fred Wilson and Brad Burnham. It invests early, in small funds, behind a published thesis about networks — and it has one of the best return records in venture capital, built on early bets on Twitter, Etsy, Tumblr, [Coinbase](/organizations/coinbase/). In October 2026 it broke its own twenty-year rule, raising $900 million, its largest fund cycle ever ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/161000-usv-raises-900m-its-biggest-fund-cycle-in-20-years/)).

USV is named for Union Square, the Manhattan neighborhood where the partners set up shop, and its office is in the Flatiron district, a short walk from the square ([Crain's New York Business](https://texoventures.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/For-Fred-Wilson-a-bird-in-the-hand-9-22-13.pdf)). It is unrelated to other "Union Square" businesses, such as Union Square Hospitality Group or Union Square Advisors. The firm invests at the earliest stages, seed and Series A, and has kept its partnership unusually small: it added just four people in fifteen years ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/01/02/union-square-ventures-secures-429m-across-two-new-funds/)).

## From Flatiron's ashes: founding and the first fund

Both founders came to USV from the wreckage of the dot-com bust. Wilson had co-founded Flatiron Partners in 1996 with Jerry Colonna; the firm collapsed with the bubble, and Wilson spent two years "licking his wounds and internalizing the lessons" before starting again ([Crain's New York Business](https://texoventures.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/For-Fred-Wilson-a-bird-in-the-hand-9-22-13.pdf); [The Generalist](https://blakeir.com/union-square-ventures-the-thinkers)). Burnham had been a general partner at AT&T Ventures and co-founded the ad-technology company Tacoda after leaving it; the two had met years earlier on the board of Multex, which Wilson had backed at Euclid Partners ([The Generalist](https://blakeir.com/union-square-ventures-the-thinkers)).

Raising the first fund was hard even with that pedigree: it took the better part of eighteen months to secure $125 million for "Union Square Ventures 2004" ([The Generalist](https://blakeir.com/union-square-ventures-the-thinkers)). A commitment from the University of Texas endowment turned it around, catalyzing interest from roughly two dozen institutions ([The Generalist](https://blakeir.com/union-square-ventures-the-thinkers)). One early investment was Tacoda itself, later sold to AOL for $275 million — nearly returning the fund on one check ([The Generalist](https://blakeir.com/union-square-ventures-the-thinkers)).

The partnership grew slowly. Albert Wenger, a former president of the social-bookmarking site del.icio.us, became a general partner in 2008 after two years as a venture partner ([AVC](https://avc.com/2017/10/rebecca-kaden/)). John Buttrick joined in 2010 to run the newly formed Opportunity Fund, Betaworks co-founder Andy Weissman joined in 2012 ([AVC](https://avc.com/2017/10/rebecca-kaden/)), and Rebecca Kaden joined from Maveron in October 2017 as its first female general partner ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/5-vcs-that-hired-their-first-female-gp-in-the-last-year)).

## A thesis, revised in public

USV's most distinctive habit is publishing what it believes. The original thesis, formulated by Burnham, fits in one sentence: invest in "large networks of engaged users, differentiated by user experience, and defensible through network effects" ([USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/usv-thesis-3-0)). The logic: a service grows more valuable as more people use it, creating a defensible moat, and such networks were the application layer of the internet ([USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/usv-thesis-3-0)). That filter produced Twitter, Etsy, Tumblr, Foursquare, Behance and Kickstarter ([USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/usv-thesis-3-0)).

As consumer networks matured, USV revised the thesis. Thesis 2.0 looked for "less obvious network effects, infrastructure for the new economy, and enablers of open decentralized data" ([USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/usv-thesis-3-0)). Thesis 3.0, published in April 2018 and authored by Kaden, reframed the practice: "USV backs trusted brands that broaden access to knowledge, capital, and well-being by leveraging networks, platforms, and protocols" ([USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/usv-thesis-3-0)). Its financial-services bucket included Lending Club, Funding Circle, [CircleUp](/organizations/circleup/) and Stash, and its blockchain bucket included Coinbase ([USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/usv-thesis-3-0)).

The fourth revision came with the October 2026 fund announcement. USV now argues that AI will "obliterate markets, not automate them" — reshaping them entirely rather than making them incrementally faster — and it funds companies at the application layer that do the reshaping ([USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/the-next-chapter-of-usv)). The other three 2026 theses are "Data at the Edge" (data previously impossible to reach, via physical intelligence, robotics and sensor networks), "The Rebel Alliance" (powerful intelligence with tighter alignment on trust, security, memory and cost), and energy, whose dedicated fund from 2021 now merges into the core fund ([USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/the-next-chapter-of-usv)). PitchBook calls the firm "vehemently generalist": the theses describe how markets change, not which sector to pick ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/union-square-ventures-275-million-fund)).

## The portfolio: small checks, outsized exits

USV's record rests on a few very large outcomes from very small early checks. The 2004 vintage — the first fund — returned about 14 times invested capital as of 2018, and public pension-fund disclosures put its net IRR at 66.9% with a 13.82x multiple ([The Generalist](https://blakeir.com/union-square-ventures-the-thinkers); [Fundraising Fox](https://fundraisingfox.com/investors/union-square-ventures-usv)). The same disclosures show the 2012 vintage at 53.5% IRR and 23.78x, and the 2016 vintage at 48.9% IRR and 7.79x ([Fundraising Fox](https://fundraisingfox.com/investors/union-square-ventures-usv)). PitchBook, citing Los Angeles County pension data, reports flagship IRRs consistently above 25%, with the 2019 vintage at 57.1% ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/union-square-ventures-275-million-fund)).

| Zynga | Early investor, first fund | IPO at a $7B valuation; a 65x return for USV |
| Etsy | Sourced by Albert Wenger | IPO in 2015 at $3.5B; USV owned over 15% |

| Tumblr | Early investor, first fund | Sold to Yahoo in 2013 as part of a $1 billion acquisition |

Figures come from Crain's, [TechCrunch](/organizations/techcrunch/) and The Generalist's reconstruction of the early funds. At their public offerings, USV owned more than 15% of Etsy and at least 5% of Twitter, per the companies' S-1 filings ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/04/29/usv-quietly-announces-625m-in-fresh-funding-for-both-web2-and-web3-teams/)).

The 2013 exit season made the reputation: Indeed and Tumblr both made billion-dollar exits, and Twitter's IPO was expected to deliver USV over $1 billion, per The New York Times via Crain's ([Crain's New York Business](https://texoventures.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/For-Fred-Wilson-a-bird-in-the-hand-9-22-13.pdf)). The firm was not immune to the later valuation reset — in 2023 it marked down seven funds by 26%, per Business Insider's reading of University of Texas and Texas A&M endowment data, as reported by PitchBook ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/union-square-ventures-275-million-fund)).

## Fund history: twenty years of small funds

For two decades USV treated small fund size as a strategy, not a limitation. Wilson said in 2010 that the firm would "never raise a large fund," a position it held even as rivals raised multi-billion-dollar vehicles ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/01/02/union-square-ventures-secures-429m-across-two-new-funds/)). Small funds keep the firm thesis-driven and concentrated in high-conviction bets ([USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/the-next-chapter-of-usv)).

## CircleUp: the marketplace bet that fits the thesis

USV's relationship with CircleUp is a clean example of Thesis 2.0 and 3.0 in action. CircleUp, the [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/)-based equity-crowdfunding platform for consumer brands, raised a $7.5 million Series A in May 2013 led by USV, with [Google Ventures](/organizations/gv/), Rose Park Advisors, Maveron and David Topper participating; USV partner Andy Weissman joined CircleUp's board ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/05/07/circleup-series-a/)). By then twelve companies had used the platform to raise more than $10 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/05/07/circleup-series-a/)).

USV stayed in. In March 2014 it participated in CircleUp's $14 million Series B, led by [Canaan Partners](/organizations/canaan-partners/), which brought CircleUp's total raised to $23 million since its 2011 founding ([NewHope Network](https://www.newhope.com/industry-news/circleup-closes-14-million-series-b-funding)). USV's Thesis 3.0 post (2018) still listed CircleUp among its financial-services vertical networks ([USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/usv-thesis-3-0)). CircleUp was later acquired by Brightflow AI, with USV credited among its long-term backers — a quiet holding rather than one of the firm's famous exits ([citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/article/425249/brightflow-ai-acquires-circleup/?abkw=citybizsanfrancisco)).

## October 2026: the $900 million break with discipline

On October 8, 2026, USV announced $900 million in new funds — its largest fund cycle ever, and a break with the small-fund discipline that had defined it for twenty years ([USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/the-next-chapter-of-usv); [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/161000-usv-raises-900m-its-biggest-fund-cycle-in-20-years/)). Bloomberg reported the split as a $500 million Core Fund and a $400 million Opportunity Fund, up 82% on the $275 million core fund of 2024 ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/161000-usv-raises-900m-its-biggest-fund-cycle-in-20-years/); [TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/business/28400)). USV's own announcement gave only the $900 million aggregate ([TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/business/28400)).

The money came with a restructuring. The active general partnership narrows to four — Wilson, Kaden, Nick Grossman and Michael Mignano, the Anchor co-founder who joined as a GP in 2026 — while Burnham, Wenger, Weissman and John Buttrick move to supporting roles ([USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/the-next-chapter-of-usv); [TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/business/28400)). The firm also added Venture Partner Jared Hecht (GroupMe, Fundera) and Product Advisory Partner Scott Belsky (Behance, ex-Adobe CPO) to bring a builder's mindset ([USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/the-next-chapter-of-usv)).

The reason is arithmetic, not ambition: AI has made products cheaper to build but breakout rounds bigger, faster and more expensive, so competing now takes more capital per company ([USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/the-next-chapter-of-usv)). USV says it will make roughly the same number of investments, lead more rounds, and invest in capital-heavy categories such as robotics, manufacturing and energy ([USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/the-next-chapter-of-usv)). The partners frame it as continuity: "in 2004, Fred and Brad bet that the next great companies would be built on top of the Internet. In 2026, we're making the same bet on intelligence" ([USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/the-next-chapter-of-usv)).

## FAQ

**Who founded Union Square Ventures?**
Fred Wilson and Brad Burnham founded USV in New York City in 2003. Wilson had co-founded Flatiron Partners in 1996; Burnham had been a general partner at AT&T Ventures and co-founded Tacoda ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/01/02/union-square-ventures-secures-429m-across-two-new-funds/); [The Generalist](https://blakeir.com/union-square-ventures-the-thinkers)).

**What is Union Square Ventures' investment thesis?**
The original thesis was to back "large networks of engaged users, differentiated by user experience, and defensible through network effects." Thesis 3.0 (2018) backs "trusted brands that broaden access to knowledge, capital, and well-being by leveraging networks, platforms, and protocols," and the 2026 theses add AI that obliterates markets, data at the edge and energy ([USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/usv-thesis-3-0); [USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/the-next-chapter-of-usv)).

**What are Union Square Ventures' best-known investments?**
Twitter, Etsy, Tumblr, Zynga, Indeed and Coinbase, plus Stripe, MongoDB, Twilio, Duolingo and Carta. The 2004 fund returned about 14x, and the 2012 fund's Coinbase stake was worth $4.6 billion after the first day of trading ([The Generalist](https://blakeir.com/union-square-ventures-the-thinkers); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/union-square-ventures-275-million-fund)).

**What is USV's relationship with CircleUp?**
USV led CircleUp's $7.5 million Series A in May 2013 — partner Andy Weissman joined the board — and participated in the $14 million Series B in March 2014. USV's Thesis 3.0 post lists CircleUp among its financial-services vertical networks, and the firm stayed a backer through the later sale to Brightflow AI ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2013/05/07/circleup-series-a/); [NewHope Network](https://www.newhope.com/industry-news/circleup-closes-14-million-series-b-funding)).

**How big is USV's latest fund, and why did it grow?**
USV announced $900 million in new funds in October 2026 — a $500 million core fund and a $400 million opportunity fund, per Bloomberg — its largest cycle ever, nearly double the $275 million core fund of 2024. The firm says AI rounds are bigger, faster and more expensive, so competing now takes more capital per company ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/161000-usv-raises-900m-its-biggest-fund-cycle-in-20-years/); [USV](https://www.usv.com/writing/the-next-chapter-of-usv)).

It does not claim a settled IRR for the 2019 vintage (57.1% per LACERA data as of Q1 2024 vs 33.7% in a later public-LP compilation). It does not state assets under management or total capital raised — no fully read source verified either. The first fund's $125 million size and ~14x return rest partly on tier3 analysis, though public pension-fund disclosures corroborate the performance. The $500M/$400M split is Bloomberg's reporting, not USV's own announcement.
