## Overview

The Royal Automobile Association of South Australia, known as RAA, is South Australia's member motoring organisation, founded on 30 September 1903 as a social club for early motorists and now a statewide provider of roadside assistance, travel, vehicle inspections and road-safety advocacy ([RAA](https://www.raa.com.au/about-raa/our-story)). Its insurance story changed fundamentally in 2025, when [Allianz Australia](/organizations/allianz-australia/) bought RAA's general insurance business and began underwriting RAA home and motor policies under the RAA brand, under a 20-year exclusive distribution agreement ([Allianz Australia](https://www.allianz.com.au/about-us/media-hub/allianz-enters-strategic-partnership-with-raa-sa.html)). RAA kept control of its brand, marketing, distribution and service, while Allianz took responsibility for product pricing, claims and underwriting ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/breaking-news/execs-get-7-7-million-payout-after-massive-insurance-sale-553317.aspx)). In the annual-report year covered in October 2025, RAA reported 834,000 members ([Insurance News](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/corporate/insurance-buyout-drives-up-raa-bottom-line)).

## From a social motoring club to a statewide member organisation

On the evening of 30 September 1903, 15 men gathered at the meeting rooms of the League of South Australian Wheelmen, a cycling club, and formed the Automobile and Motor Cycling Club of South Australia, later known as the Royal Automobile Association ([RAA](https://www.raa.com.au/about-raa/our-story)). A South Australian history chronology independently records RAA as formed on 30 September under 1903 ([South Australian History](https://www.southaustralianhistory.com.au/chronology6.htm)). The club met in a Waymouth Street clubroom, organised country drives and competitions, and pooled members' help with the mechanical problems that plagued early motoring ([RAA](https://www.raa.com.au/about-raa/our-story)). Those early mechanics were the forerunners of the RAA Patrols seen around South Australia today, and the club soon became a voice for South Australian motorists to local and state government on road safety and motoring rules ([RAA](https://www.raa.com.au/about-raa/our-story)).

Its services later expanded to insurance, travel, vehicle inspections and driver training, alongside advocacy for better roads and fairer fuel prices ([RAA](https://www.raa.com.au/about-raa/our-story)). Its insurance arm followed a longer path than the 2025 deal alone suggests: RAA first offered insurance as an agent for Lumley, obtained its own general insurance licence in 1987, entered a joint venture with [Suncorp](/organizations/suncorp/) Metway in 2002, and bought out that interest in 2010 ([RAA](https://www.raa.com.au/-/media/Project/RAA/Web/Files/AboutRAA/Advocacy/2019-submission-efc-inquiry-motor-insurance.pdf?la=en&hash=12597E40C030C2DDEE3C2F38C6B84CA1)). By 2019, RAA described that arm as South Australia's largest personal lines insurer ([RAA](https://www.raa.com.au/-/media/Project/RAA/Web/Files/AboutRAA/Advocacy/2019-submission-efc-inquiry-motor-insurance.pdf?la=en&hash=12597E40C030C2DDEE3C2F38C6B84CA1)).

## The 2025 Allianz split: brand kept, underwriting transferred

The Allianz deal, valued at $642 million, included the purchase of RAA's general insurance business and a 20-year exclusive distribution agreement for Home and Motor lines ([Glam Adelaide](https://glamadelaide.com.au/raa-announces-20-year-partnership-with-global-leader-allianz/)). Allianz would underwrite general insurance products except travel and manage claims under the RAA brand, while RAA managed marketing and distribution ([Glam Adelaide](https://glamadelaide.com.au/raa-announces-20-year-partnership-with-global-leader-allianz/)). After approvals on 12 June 2025, the partnership commenced on 1 July 2025, with Allianz welcoming around 300 RAA employees, against about 270 estimated at announcement ([Allianz Australia](https://www.allianz.com.au/about-us/media-hub/allianz-enters-strategic-partnership-with-raa-sa.html)).

RAA's stated reason was exposure: as a state-based insurer, it was increasingly difficult to shield members from rising reinsurance and repair costs and more frequent natural disasters ([Insurance News](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/corporate/insurance-buyout-drives-up-raa-bottom-line)). The Murray River floods of 2022-23 had drawn down its capital reserves, and RAA had posted a $53.9 million loss in 2023 after those floods and other extreme weather drove up claims ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/breaking-news/execs-get-7-7-million-payout-after-massive-insurance-sale-553317.aspx)). A later trade-press analysis noted that the competition regulator cleared the acquisition, concluding that competition from national brands would constrain Allianz's pricing ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/breaking-news/how-consolidation-is-reshaping-australian-insurance-573892.aspx)).

Financially, the sale transformed RAA's reported year: after-tax profit jumped to $276.1 million from $2.1 million a year earlier, driven by a $259.2 million accounting profit from the Allianz transaction ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/breaking-news/execs-get-7-7-million-payout-after-massive-insurance-sale-553317.aspx)). Excluding the one-off benefit, underlying profit before tax rose to $24.3 million from $4.1 million, alongside about 15,000 additional members taking the total to 834,000 ([Insurance News](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/corporate/insurance-buyout-drives-up-raa-bottom-line)). RAA said the deal delivered a $450 million cash injection to be invested while it consulted members on its use, with possible cost-of-living relief floated by chief executive Nick Reade ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/breaking-news/execs-get-7-7-million-payout-after-massive-insurance-sale-553317.aspx)). The same annual report showed RAA paid more than $31 million to about 132,500 members under a pricing-promises remediation programme for past instances when insurance discounts were not applied in line with advertising ([Insurance News](https://www.insurancenews.com.au/corporate/insurance-buyout-drives-up-raa-bottom-line)). For Allianz, the deal mattered nationally: S and P Global Ratings' view, reported in September 2026, was that Allianz Australia had become Australia's third-largest property and casualty insurer after the 2025 deal ([Insurance Asia](https://insuranceasia.com/insurance/news/allianz-australia-absorbs-raa-without-eroding-capital)).

## Roadside, advocacy and life after underwriting

What RAA kept is as important as what it sold: roadside assistance remained central, with RAA attending more than 357,000 roadside breakdowns in the reported year ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/breaking-news/execs-get-7-7-million-payout-after-massive-insurance-sale-553317.aspx)). In September 2025 it extended that help beyond mobile coverage, when members broken down without mobile or Wi-Fi coverage became able to connect with the roadside team via Apple's satellite roadside assistance on iPhone 14 or later models ([Glam Adelaide](https://glamadelaide.com.au/raa-and-apple-launch-satellite-roadside-assistance-for-out-of-range-motorists-in-australia/)). The service, free for iPhone users for the first two years after activating a device, applies to vehicles on or near public roads, while off-road recovery is excluded ([Glam Adelaide](https://glamadelaide.com.au/raa-and-apple-launch-satellite-roadside-assistance-for-out-of-range-motorists-in-australia/)). RAA also expanded new ventures in the reported year, including RAA Energy and a statewide electric vehicle charging network ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/au/news/breaking-news/execs-get-7-7-million-payout-after-massive-insurance-sale-553317.aspx)).

RAA's other continuing role is as a publisher of road-safety data and an advocate to government: its quarterly crash report counted 84 lives lost between January and September 2026 and 681 serious injuries, already above South Australia's full-year targets of no more than 70 lives lost and 591 serious injuries ([Glam Adelaide](https://glamadelaide.com.au/sa-records-largest-increase-in-road-deaths-in-australia/)). Four further deaths in October took the 2026 toll to 88 by 25 October, the largest increase of any state over the previous 12 months ([Glam Adelaide](https://glamadelaide.com.au/sa-records-largest-increase-in-road-deaths-in-australia/)). RAA renewed its calls to duplicate the Sturt and Augusta Highways, arguing that separating opposing traffic could reduce serious crash risk ([Glam Adelaide](https://glamadelaide.com.au/sa-records-largest-increase-in-road-deaths-in-australia/)).
