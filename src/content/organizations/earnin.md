## Overview

EarnIn is a Palo Alto-based earned-wage-access app that lets workers draw on wages they have already earned before payday ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1761030)). It was founded in 2012 as Activehours by Ram Palaniappan, who had spent eight years as president of payments company UniRush — where he had informally fronted employees money before payday from his own checking account, then turned the favor into a business ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/commerce/earnin-raises-125-million-for-payday-advance-platform-without-fees), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1761030)). The app launched in 2014, the company rebranded from Activehours to Earnin in late 2017, and it now operates under the EarnIn name ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/commerce/earnin-raises-125-million-for-payday-advance-platform-without-fees)).

## The tip-based model and the $125 million round

EarnIn's model works by connecting a user's bank account, inferring the pay cycle, and debiting the advanced amount on payday — charging no fees or interest, and instead asking users to leave an optional "tip" that funds the next user's withdrawal ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1761030)). VentureBeat noted the tension plainly: a tip-only revenue model was an unusual basis for a startup that had raised nearly $200 million ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/commerce/earnin-raises-125-million-for-payday-advance-platform-without-fees)). In December 2018, Earnin raised a $125 million Series C from DST Global, Andreessen Horowitz, Spark Capital, Matrix Partners, March Capital Partners, Coatue Management, and Ribbit Capital, on the back of more than a million app downloads and usage by employees at over 50,000 companies ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1761030)).

## The regulatory crackdown

The "free" framing eventually drew legal fire. In November 2024, the attorney general for the District of Columbia sued Earnin, alleging it "deceptively marketed and provided illegal high-interest loans": while the service is free for users willing to wait a couple of business days, getting money immediately costs a "Lightning Speed" fee, which the AG alleges equates to an average interest rate of 300% — more than 12 times the District's 24% rate cap — and which Earnin was allegedly collecting without proper licensing ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/20/another-vc-backed-fintech-earnin-faces-crackdown-over-allegedly-predatory-loans/?ref=biztoc.com)). Earnin's lawyer Karl Racine responded that the lawsuit "demonstrates a fundamental misunderstanding of how our product works," arguing DC workers can access earned money at no cost within 1–2 business days ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/20/another-vc-backed-fintech-earnin-faces-crackdown-over-allegedly-predatory-loans/?ref=biztoc.com)). The suit landed amid a wider enforcement wave: the FTC took action against cash-app Dave and the CFPB sued SoLo Funds over similar fee-disclosure allegations ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/20/another-vc-backed-fintech-earnin-faces-crackdown-over-allegedly-predatory-loans/?ref=biztoc.com)).
