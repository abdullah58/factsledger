## Overview

Inman is a US real-estate trade publication and events company founded in 1996 by journalist and entrepreneur Brad Inman, best known for Inman Connect, the conference where the internet's disruption of homebuying has been debated since the start. Owned since 2021 by Toronto private-equity firm Beringer Capital, it serves agents, brokers, executives and technology leaders through daily news, subscriptions, data products and conferences in the [United States](/places/united-states/) ([RealTrends](https://www.realtrends.com/blog/2021/07/26/brad-inman-sells-company-to-beringer-capital/); [Beringer Capital](https://multi-unitfranchise.com/franchise-real-estate/beringer_capital_acquires_inman_group)).

This profile covers the real-estate news organisation behind inman.com — not the city of Inman, South Carolina, or other businesses sharing the name. Inman describes itself as the leading source of news, insights, events and professional development in the industry, operating for more than 30 years and reaching more than one million real-estate professionals ([PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/inman-names-veteran-dow-jones-editor-lucy-cohen-blatter-editor-in-chief-302873956.html)). Its flagship events are Blueprint, Inman Connect and Social Summit, and its offering spans its website, newsletters, events, awards, education, research and subscriptions ([PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/beringer-capital-portfolio-company-inman-acquires-blueprint-to-expand-its-coverage-of-the-entire-built-world-302296160.html)).

## Founding and the first Connect

Brad Inman founded Inman News in 1996 as an early leader in covering the booming real-estate technology industry, and its controversial coverage and opinion pieces — not always favourable to real-estate brokerage — helped the company gain notoriety ([RealTrends](https://www.realtrends.com/blog/2021/07/26/brad-inman-sells-company-to-beringer-capital/)). That same September, 50 real-estate leaders gathered in a redwood grove in Cazadero, Sonoma County, California, for the first Inman Connect, roasting a pig and talking about how the internet would disrupt real estate when only 1 percent of listings were online ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/01/27/30-years-of-disruption-how-inman-connect-became-the-front-row-seat-to-a-changing-industry/)).

The guest list included Joel Singer, then chief executive of the California Association of Realtors, and Richard Janssen, whose firm became Move Inc. and who was building the technology behind Realtor.com ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/01/27/30-years-of-disruption-how-inman-connect-became-the-front-row-seat-to-a-changing-industry/)). The retreat became a full conference the next year: Real Estate Connect 97 at the [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) Hilton in July 1997 ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/01/27/30-years-of-disruption-how-inman-connect-became-the-front-row-seat-to-a-changing-industry/)). Inman was a serial founder alongside his publication, launching HomeGain, later sold to Classified Ventures, plus TurnHere and Vook, and becoming the first investor in Curbed ([Vendor Alley](https://www.vendoralley.com/2021/07/26/brad/)).

## Inman Connect: the industry's stage

Connect grew into two major conferences a year — in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) and San Diego — attracting thousands, plus virtual, niche and local events, and its stage became where the industry's future was argued in public ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/01/27/30-years-of-disruption-how-inman-connect-became-the-front-row-seat-to-a-changing-industry/)). In 2000, television journalist Charlie Rose interviewed Marc Andreessen, co-founder of Netscape; in 2001, [Google](/organizations/google/) co-founder Larry Page told a Connect audience that homebuyers would eventually search for homes online, to open scepticism ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/01/27/30-years-of-disruption-how-inman-connect-became-the-front-row-seat-to-a-changing-industry/)).

The 2007 editions staged two defining confrontations: a [60 Minutes](/organizations/cbs-news-60-minutes/) crew filmed Realtor.com executive Allan Dalton debating Redfin chief executive Glenn Kelman over commission rates, and Zillow founder Rich Barton debated National Association of Realtors chief executive Dale Stinton ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/01/27/30-years-of-disruption-how-inman-connect-became-the-front-row-seat-to-a-changing-industry/)). Later moments kept the pattern: Rupert Murdoch spoke in 2015, two months after acquiring Realtor.com operator Move Inc.; in 2024, independent presidential candidate [Robert F. Kennedy Jr.](/people/robert-f-kennedy-jr/) discussed homeownership with Brad Inman, and The Agency founder Mauricio Umansky announced a rival to the National Association of Realtors ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/01/27/30-years-of-disruption-how-inman-connect-became-the-front-row-seat-to-a-changing-industry/)).

## The Beringer Capital sale and expansion

On 26 July 2021, Beringer Capital announced it had acquired Inman Group, with Brad Inman — then 68 — retaining an ownership stake and an active role, citing his age and grown children, while chief executive Josh Albertson continued to lead the company ([RealTrends](https://www.realtrends.com/blog/2021/07/26/brad-inman-sells-company-to-beringer-capital/)). Beringer, which had bought Adweek in 2016 and exited in 2020, said it would invest significantly in editorial and events, including strategic acquisitions, and contribute day-to-day strategic input; the deal's terms were not disclosed ([RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/?p=221156); [Beringer Capital](https://multi-unitfranchise.com/franchise-real-estate/beringer_capital_acquires_inman_group)).

Emily Paquette succeeded Albertson as chief executive in 2022 and served four years, having spent 12 years at Inman and risen through sales to chief revenue officer ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/03/24/inman-appoints-experienced-media-executive-as-new-ceo/)). Under her, Inman launched the Inman Intel data and research arm in early 2023, added regional Inman On Tour events in Texas and Tennessee, and in November 2024 acquired Blueprint, a three-day conference with a 2,500-strong built-world community, from Connectiv Holdings ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/03/24/inman-appoints-experienced-media-executive-as-new-ceo/); [PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/beringer-capital-portfolio-company-inman-acquires-blueprint-to-expand-its-coverage-of-the-entire-built-world-302296160.html)).

## Covering the downturn: Blend and [Title365](/organizations/title365/)

Inman's mortgage coverage made it a primary chronicler of the post-refinancing-boom shakeout, above all at [Blend](/organizations/blend/), the mortgage-technology provider whose 2022 layoffs it tracked in detail ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/08/16/blend-cuts-another-220-jobs-as-losses-approach-1b/)). In April 2022 Blend cut 200 jobs; that August it cut another 220 positions — 140 employees plus 80 vacant roles — reducing its workforce by about 25 percent in the year and expecting $60 million in annual compensation savings ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/08/16/blend-cuts-another-220-jobs-as-losses-approach-1b/)).

Most of Blend's $477 million second-quarter 2022 net loss was a $391.8 million writedown of Title365, the national title insurance and settlement services business it had acquired for $422 million in July 2021: $240.1 million of goodwill and $151.7 million of customer relationships ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/08/16/blend-cuts-another-220-jobs-as-losses-approach-1b/)). Blend's accumulated deficit reached $995.1 million, yet the quarter also showed Title365 contributing $31.9 million of $65.5 million in revenue, and mortgage servicer [Mr. Cooper](/organizations/mr-cooper-group/) going live on the Blend Title solution ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2022/08/16/blend-cuts-another-220-jobs-as-losses-approach-1b/)). The coverage drew its charts from Blend's own regulatory filings.

## The 2026 reset: new leadership and Inman 2.0

In March 2026 Inman appointed Tom Bohn chief executive, stepping in on 2 April after Emily Paquette's departure, with a mandate to expand the company as an intelligence platform — growing data and enterprise capabilities, strengthening events and innovating at the intersection of technology, AI and the real-estate transaction ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/03/24/inman-appoints-experienced-media-executive-as-new-ceo/)). Bohn had previously led THM Media, parent of TheHomeMag, the Association for Corporate Growth and the North American Veterinary Community ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/03/24/inman-appoints-experienced-media-executive-as-new-ceo/)).

That September, Inman named Lucy Cohen Blatter editor-in-chief, a veteran Dow Jones editor with more than 15 years covering residential real estate, previously managing editor of Mansion Global and of Penta at Barron's ([PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/inman-names-veteran-dow-jones-editor-lucy-cohen-blatter-editor-in-chief-302873956.html)). Bohn has made editorial independence central, writing: "We can't be bought, and we won't be bullied" ([PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/inman-names-veteran-dow-jones-editor-lucy-cohen-blatter-editor-in-chief-302873956.html)). The reset also brings the 1,000 Storytellers initiative, the Inman Media Network and the anticipated launch of Inman 2.0, an overhaul of inman.com, as Connect marked its 30th anniversary ([PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/inman-names-veteran-dow-jones-editor-lucy-cohen-blatter-editor-in-chief-302873956.html); [Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/01/27/30-years-of-disruption-how-inman-connect-became-the-front-row-seat-to-a-changing-industry/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Inman?** Inman is a US real-estate trade publication and events company founded in 1996 by Brad Inman, covering residential real estate, mortgage technology and brokerage for agents, brokers, executives and technology leaders ([RealTrends](https://www.realtrends.com/blog/2021/07/26/brad-inman-sells-company-to-beringer-capital/)). It publishes daily news, runs subscriptions and data products, and stages conferences including Inman Connect ([PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/inman-names-veteran-dow-jones-editor-lucy-cohen-blatter-editor-in-chief-302873956.html)).

**Who owns Inman?** Toronto-based private-equity firm Beringer Capital has owned Inman Group since July 2021, when founder Brad Inman sold the company while retaining an ownership stake and an active role ([RealTrends](https://www.realtrends.com/blog/2021/07/26/brad-inman-sells-company-to-beringer-capital/)). Beringer previously owned the media and events company Adweek from 2016 to 2020 ([RISMedia](https://www.rismedia.com/?p=221156)).

**What is Inman Connect?** Inman Connect is Inman's flagship real-estate conference, held twice a year in New York and San Diego and attracting thousands of attendees ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/01/27/30-years-of-disruption-how-inman-connect-became-the-front-row-seat-to-a-changing-industry/)). It began in September 1996 with 50 leaders in a Sonoma County redwood grove and has hosted keynotes from Larry Page, Marc Andreessen, Rupert Murdoch and Robert F. Kennedy Jr. ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/01/27/30-years-of-disruption-how-inman-connect-became-the-front-row-seat-to-a-changing-industry/)).

**Who leads Inman now?** Tom Bohn became chief executive on 2 April 2026, succeeding Emily Paquette, and Lucy Cohen Blatter was named editor-in-chief in September 2026 ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/03/24/inman-appoints-experienced-media-executive-as-new-ceo/); [PRWeb](https://www.prweb.com/releases/inman-names-veteran-dow-jones-editor-lucy-cohen-blatter-editor-in-chief-302873956.html)). Founder Brad Inman retains an ownership stake and remains active in the company ([RealTrends](https://www.realtrends.com/blog/2021/07/26/brad-inman-sells-company-to-beringer-capital/)).
