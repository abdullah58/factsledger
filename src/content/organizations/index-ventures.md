## Overview

Index Ventures is a transatlantic venture capital firm founded in Geneva in 1996 by Neil Rimer, his brother David Rimer and Giuseppe Zocco, and now run principally from [London](/places/london/) and [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/) ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/neil-rimer-index-startup-stubborn-advice); [Human Rights Watch](https://www.hrw.org/about/people/neil-rimer)). Over 28 years it backed 108 companies that reached unicorn status and 57 that went public, including Skype, Dropbox, [Revolut](/organizations/revolut/) and [Figma](/organizations/figma/), and in 2025 its seed-stage bet on Wiz turned into a $32 billion sale to [Alphabet](/organizations/alphabet/) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/index-ventures-raises-23-billion-for-new-venture-and-growth-funds/); [Reuters](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/bjdoued3jg)).

The firm was founded on a contrarian idea: that great technology founders would emerge in Europe as well as Silicon Valley, and that a European firm could back them from the start. Neil Rimer's aim, as he put it, was to bring Silicon Valley-style investing to Europe at a time when "venture capital" was still a new term outside the United States ([Human Rights Watch](https://www.hrw.org/about/people/neil-rimer)). He recalled starting every early meeting with a slide that literally read "What is VC?" ([Human Rights Watch](https://www.hrw.org/about/people/neil-rimer)). Thirty years on, Index has raised $15 billion in total capital and invests from seed to IPO across Europe, the United States and [Israel](/places/israel/) ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2024/07/10/index-ventures-announces-2-3n-to-back-founders-from-seed-to-ipo/)).

## From Geneva to a transatlantic firm

Index Ventures was born in Geneva in 1996 and expanded to London six years later, setting out to build a European venture firm with a Silicon Valley sensibility ([Index Ventures](https://www.indexventures.com/perspectives/announcing-165b-in-new-funds-to-back-entrepreneurs-with-global-ambitions/)). Neil Rimer, born in [Montreal](/places/montreal/) and educated in Geneva, had studied history and economics at Stanford and earned an MBA from Harvard Business School before working at Montgomery Securities in San Francisco and joining Index Securities — the predecessor of Index Ventures — in 1990 ([Human Rights Watch](https://www.hrw.org/about/people/neil-rimer)). His brother David co-founded the firm with him and Giuseppe Zocco; David oversaw the firm's strategy and operations from the start and left in 2013 to become a high-school history teacher, remaining an adviser to the partnership ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/neil-rimer-index-startup-stubborn-advice); [Index Ventures](https://www.indexventures.com/team/david-rimer/)).

The transatlantic shape followed the portfolio. Index opened its San Francisco office about seven years before 2018, bringing European insight to what it found an introspective market, and later added a New York office ([Index Ventures](https://www.indexventures.com/perspectives/announcing-165b-in-new-funds-to-back-entrepreneurs-with-global-ambitions/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/index-ventures-raises-23-billion-for-new-venture-and-growth-funds/)). By 2024 the firm said it was present in 24 of the world's 30 most vibrant tech ecosystems, investing across all stages in North America, the United Kingdom, Europe and Israel with a single unified team and funds that are not specific to any one vertical ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2024/07/10/index-ventures-announces-2-3n-to-back-founders-from-seed-to-ipo/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/index-ventures-raises-23-billion-for-new-venture-and-growth-funds/)). Neil Rimer stepped down as an active partner in 2021 after more than 25 years at the firm, and now co-chairs the board of Human Rights Watch ([Human Rights Watch](https://www.hrw.org/about/people/neil-rimer)).

## The early bets: Skype and the European wave

Index's first decade produced the exits that proved the European thesis. It backed Betfair, MySQL and Skype early, and later Just Eat, King and Supercell, all of which became successful global businesses ([Index Ventures](https://www.indexventures.com/perspectives/announcing-165b-in-new-funds-to-back-entrepreneurs-with-global-ambitions/)). Skype is the clearest documented case: [eBay](/organizations/ebay/) acquired the internet-telephony company in 2005 for $2.6 billion, and when eBay sold a majority stake in September 2009 to a Silver Lake-led investor group, Index Ventures was part of that group — described at the time as a previous investor in Skype — in a deal valuing Skype at $2.75 billion ([eBay Inc.](https://investors.ebayinc.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2014/Statement-by-eBay-Inc-eBay-Inc-Reiterates-The-Truth-About-Skype/default.aspx)). In May 2011, all of Skype's equity was sold to [Microsoft](/organizations/microsoft/) for $8.5 billion ([eBay Inc.](https://investors.ebayinc.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2014/Statement-by-eBay-Inc-eBay-Inc-Reiterates-The-Truth-About-Skype/default.aspx)).

The firm's deal-makers built their names on those early companies. Neil Rimer personally led Index investments in Revolut, Betfair, Climate, Supercell and Trello, and worked with the founders of Funding Circle, Roblox, Raisin, Pitch, Stack Overflow and Typeform ([Human Rights Watch](https://www.hrw.org/about/people/neil-rimer)). In the United States, Index had rung the bell on six IPOs by 2018, including Etsy and Pure Storage, and had invested in more than fifty companies including Aurora, Bird, Glossier, Good Eggs, Sonos and Robinhood ([Index Ventures](https://www.indexventures.com/perspectives/announcing-165b-in-new-funds-to-back-entrepreneurs-with-global-ambitions/)). The year 2018 itself brought a cluster of portfolio listings — [Adyen](/organizations/adyen/), Dropbox, Zuora and iZettle all went public ([Index Ventures](https://www.indexventures.com/perspectives/announcing-165b-in-new-funds-to-back-entrepreneurs-with-global-ambitions/)).

## Funds: deliberately not ballooning

Index's fundraising record shows a firm that has grown its capital base steadily while resisting the industry's drift toward ever-larger funds. The 2018 raise of $1.65 billion — $650 million for its ninth venture fund and $1 billion for its fourth growth fund — was followed in 2021 by $900 million for Index Ventures XI and $2 billion for Growth VI, plus a $300 million seed fund, Index Origin II, in 2022 ([Index Ventures](https://www.indexventures.com/perspectives/announcing-165b-in-new-funds-to-back-entrepreneurs-with-global-ambitions/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/index-ventures-raises-23-billion-for-new-venture-and-growth-funds/)). In July 2024 the firm announced $2.3 billion in new funds — an $800 million twelfth venture fund and a $1.5 billion seventh growth fund — taking total capital raised since founding to $15 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/index-ventures-raises-23-billion-for-new-venture-and-growth-funds/); [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2024/07/10/index-ventures-announces-2-3n-to-back-founders-from-seed-to-ipo/)).

The 2024 raise took only weeks and came entirely from the firm's existing limited partners, according to partner Nina Achadjian, who told TechCrunch the firm was "very intentional about the size" and had sized the funds from the bottom up against the actual sizes of growth rounds happening at the time ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/index-ventures-raises-23-billion-for-new-venture-and-growth-funds/)). Partner Shardul Shah put the philosophy bluntly: "We don't think about aggregating assets," contrasting Index with firms that "have gotten big" and "moved towards asset accumulation, which is a totally different strategy" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/index-ventures-raises-23-billion-for-new-venture-and-growth-funds/)). Two years later the discipline held: the July 2026 raise of $2 billion across three funds — $400 million for a seed fund, $900 million for a venture fund and $700 million added to the 2024 growth fund — brought total available capital to $3.5 billion, still smaller than many rivals' single funds ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/31/fresh-off-its-wiz-payout-index-ventures-raises-2b-across-three-funds/)).

## The 2025 exit run: Wiz and Figma

The payoff for that patience arrived in 2025, in two exits that together rank among the best years any European-rooted venture firm has had. Wiz, a cybersecurity company founded in 2020, agreed to a $32 billion all-cash buyout by Alphabet, Google's parent ([Reuters](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/bjdoued3jg)). Index had invested from the seed stage and was quick to back the founding team, becoming Wiz's largest outside shareholder with about a 12% stake — a position sources told Reuters could be worth over $3.8 billion in cash once the deal completed ([Reuters](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/bjdoued3jg)). Reuters reported that seed investors in Wiz stood to reap returns as high as 200 times their money ([Reuters](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/bjdoued3jg)). The sale completed in 2026, and the fresh capital Index raised that July followed directly on the payout ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/31/fresh-off-its-wiz-payout-index-ventures-raises-2b-across-three-funds/)).

Figma delivered the second half of the year. The design-software company priced 36.9 million shares at $33.00 in its New York Stock Exchange IPO, raising about $1.2 billion in an offering more than 40 times oversubscribed ([IFR](https://www.ifre.com/equities/2293557/figma-rides-must-own-status-to-ipo-payday)). Index was an early investor in Figma and was among the selling shareholders, with a holding marked at $2.2 billion in IFR's account of the deal ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/31/fresh-off-its-wiz-payout-index-ventures-raises-2b-across-three-funds/); [IFR](https://www.ifre.com/equities/2293557/figma-rides-must-own-status-to-ipo-payday)). The listing was all the more striking because [Adobe](/organizations/adobe/)'s $20 billion bid to acquire Figma had collapsed in 2023 after regulatory scrutiny, leaving the IPO as the route to liquidity ([IFR](https://www.ifre.com/equities/2293557/figma-rides-must-own-status-to-ipo-payday)).

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Skype | 2005 / 2011 | eBay buys for $2.6bn; Microsoft buys all equity for $8.5bn | Previous investor; joined 2009 buyout group |
| Wiz | 2025 | $32bn all-cash sale to Alphabet | Largest outside shareholder, ~12%, worth over $3.8bn per Reuters sources |
| Figma | 2025 | NYSE IPO at $33/share, ~$1.2bn raised | Early investor; $2.2bn holding marked at IPO |

The firm's current bets show where it thinks the next cycle lies. TechCrunch's 2026 fund coverage names AI bets including robotics company Physical Intelligence, inference platform Fireworks AI and [Anthropic](/organizations/anthropic/), an investment made when the AI model maker raised capital at a $183 billion valuation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/31/fresh-off-its-wiz-payout-index-ventures-raises-2b-across-three-funds/)). Partner Nina Achadjian told TechCrunch in 2024 that the firm splits venture rounds into two categories — AI and everything else — because AI funding rounds at seed and Series A are much bigger than average, while non-AI Series A rounds have shrunk ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/index-ventures-raises-23-billion-for-new-venture-and-growth-funds/)).

## Behavox and the fintech thread

Index's connection to [Behavox](/organizations/behavox/) runs through the firm's long fintech thread — the same thread that produced its early backing of Revolut and its work with Funding Circle and Raisin ([Human Rights Watch](https://www.hrw.org/about/people/neil-rimer)). Behavox closed a $20 million Series A1 round in 2017 led by [Citigroup](/organizations/citigroup/) and backed by Index Ventures ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/blog/2020/02/25/behavox-spies-100m-vision-fund-investment/)). Index stayed on the cap table through Behavox's later, much larger rounds: when [HPS Investment Partners](/organizations/hps-investment-partners/), part of BlackRock, put $175 million of preferred equity into Behavox in June 2026, it joined a roster of institutional investors that included [SoftBank](/organizations/softbank-group/), Citigroup, Index Ventures and [Hoxton Ventures](/organizations/hoxton-ventures/) ([Financial IT](https://financialit.net/news/fundraising-news/behavox-raises-175-million-hps-investment-partners-part-blackrock-accelerate)).

## What this profile does not claim

This profile does not claim a current Geneva office: directories list one, but the sources read in full confirm offices only in London, San Francisco and [New York City](/places/new-york-city/), with Geneva as the founding city. It does not claim that Index led Figma's 2013 seed round: IFR says Greylock Partners led the $3.9 million round, and this profile calls Index an early investor only. It does not state a Figma IPO valuation, because IFR's $19.8 billion fully diluted figure at pricing and other reports' far higher first-day closing values disagree. It does not present the "over $3.8 billion" Wiz figure as a realised payout, because Reuters reported it as a sources-based estimate of what the 12% stake could be worth.
