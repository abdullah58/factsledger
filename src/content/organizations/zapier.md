## Overview

Zapier is a San Francisco-based workflow-automation company founded in 2011 by Wade Foster, Bryan Helmig, and Mike Knoop, whose "Zaps" let users connect thousands of web applications — Gmail to Dropbox, Twitter mentions to Slack — without writing code ([Cantech Letter](https://www.cantechletter.com/2024/02/zapier-ipo-an-overview/amp/)). It began as a nights-and-weekends side project in Columbia, Missouri: as Foster told TechCrunch, "side projects can't afford offices," so the three founders worked from coffee shops and apartments, moved to the Bay Area for Y Combinator, and then — when co-founder Mike Knoop moved back to Missouri — simply stayed distributed ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/03/09/zapier-ceo-wade-foster-on-scaling-a-remote-team-up-to-300-employees/)). The company has never had an office since.

## $1.2 million in, $5 billion out — without a second raise

Zapier's capital story inverts the Silicon Valley playbook. Its first fundraising event — a $1.2 million round in 2012 led by Bessemer Venture Partners with DFJ participating — was also its last primary raise ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/03/08/zapier-buys-no-code-focused-makerpad-in-its-first-acquisition/)). By March 2020 the company had grown to 300 people, "all over the globe — two-thirds in the U.S., one-third international... 27 countries and maybe 35 different states," in Foster's words, and by summer 2020 it had crossed $100 million in annual recurring revenue with 400 workers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/03/09/zapier-ceo-wade-foster-on-scaling-a-remote-team-up-to-300-employees/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/03/08/zapier-buys-no-code-focused-makerpad-in-its-first-acquisition/)).

The $5 billion valuation arrived without the company raising a dollar. In January 2021, Sequoia Capital bought Zapier shares from existing holders on the secondary market — The Information reported the sale valued the company at more than $4 billion, while Forbes' subsequent reporting put the figure at $5 billion, with Steadfast Financial also buying in ([Techmeme](https://www.techmeme.com/210114/p53), [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/chris-morris/zapier-ceo-wade-foster-built-a-robot-staff-it-briefs-him-challenges-him-and-drafts-his-emails/91401528)). Because it was a secondary transaction, the cash changed hands between shareholders; the valuation landed on Zapier without a primary funding round. Foster, who had spent years turning aside venture investors, described financing events as "a tool in the tool belt" rather than a hamster wheel, when asked about an eventual IPO: "never say never" ([Cantech Letter](https://www.cantechletter.com/2024/02/zapier-ipo-an-overview/amp/)).

## Remote before it was a strategy

Zapier's distributed structure was necessity before it was philosophy. At five people across three cities with no office, the founders noticed that "product was getting shipped, customers seemed to be happy... none of it seemed to be hindered," and decided to make remote the operating model rather than a constraint ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/03/09/zapier-ceo-wade-foster-on-scaling-a-remote-team-up-to-300-employees/)). The discipline it forced — writing everything down, documenting processes, building an internal Async bulletin board to counter Slack's short half-life — became, in Foster's telling, an advantage: "As a company gets to a certain size, you have to do this anyway, even if you're not remote... Remote just forces you to do it early" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/03/09/zapier-ceo-wade-foster-on-scaling-a-remote-team-up-to-300-employees/)). Twice-yearly company-wide off-sites, a Donut.ai-powered random coffee pairing bot, and "video always on" norms substitute for hallway culture ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2020/03/09/zapier-ceo-wade-foster-on-scaling-a-remote-team-up-to-300-employees/)).
