## Overview

Alloy is a New York–based financial technology company that sells an "identity decisioning" platform to banks and fintech companies — software that automates the know-your-customer (KYC) identity checks, fraud screening, and transaction monitoring required when opening and managing financial accounts ([Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/40906/alloy-raises-52m-for-global-expansion)). The company was founded in 2015 by Tommy Nicholas (CEO), Laura Spiekerman, and Charles Hearn (CTO), and describes itself as infrastructure that lets financial institutions pull customer, credit-bureau, and alternative data through a single point of integration instead of building compliance systems in-house ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2021/09/alloy-identity-verification-transaction-monitoring-platform-financial-services-kyc-laura-spiekerman/)).

This profile covers the New York identity-decisioning company operating at alloy.com — not Alloy Entertainment (the Warner Bros. media company), Alloy Software (the IT-service-management vendor), or other businesses that use the "Alloy" name. Most public coverage of the company is fragmented funding announcements; this profile assembles the full arc from founding through its unicorn rounds and international expansion.

## Founding and the early funding rounds

Alloy was launched in 2015 to solve what co-founder and CRO Laura Spiekerman described as a gap in the market: financial services firms wanted to onboard customers digitally, but assessing risk in that process was still fragmented across outdated, siloed identity databases that pushed roughly half of applicants into manual review ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2021/09/alloy-identity-verification-transaction-monitoring-platform-financial-services-kyc-laura-spiekerman/)). The founders' pitch was that identity decisioning should be installable infrastructure — in Nicholas's words, something businesses "install" rather than figure out themselves ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/alloy-earns-1-35-billion-valuation-after-securing-series-c-investment/)).

The company demonstrated its technology at the FinDEVr Silicon Valley developer conference in 2016 ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/alloy-earns-1-35-billion-valuation-after-securing-series-c-investment/)), and by September 2019 it had raised a $12 million Series A led by Bessemer Venture Partners, with Eniac Ventures and Primary Venture Partners participating ([VC News Daily](https://vcnewsdaily.com/alloy/venture-capital-funding/zkzrpqglyn)). A year later it closed a $40 million Series B led by Canapi Ventures ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/alloy-earns-1-35-billion-valuation-after-securing-series-c-investment/)). Along the way it built data-source partnerships with firms such as Socure and Ekata and distribution partnerships with account-opening software providers and core banking systems ([VC News Daily](https://vcnewsdaily.com/alloy/venture-capital-funding/zkzrpqglyn)).

## The unicorn years: $1.35B, then $1.55B

In September 2021, Alloy raised a $100 million Series C led by Lightspeed Venture Partners' Justin Overdorff, with participation from Canapi Ventures, Bessemer Venture Partners, Avid Ventures, and Felicis Ventures — a round that valued the company at $1.35 billion and made it a fintech unicorn ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/alloy-earns-1-35-billion-valuation-after-securing-series-c-investment/)). By then the platform had evolved beyond onboarding automation: it had added transaction monitoring, processed more than 455,000 identity decisions a day across 120-plus data sources, and claimed a 50% reduction in manual reviews, an 80% automation rate for new account openings, and customer approval rates above 80% for clients including Novo, Brex, and HMBradley ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/alloy-earns-1-35-billion-valuation-after-securing-series-c-investment/)).

Eleven months later, in September 2022, Alloy raised an additional $52 million at a $1.55 billion valuation, co-led by Lightspeed and Avenir Growth with the same existing investors participating ([Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/40906/alloy-raises-52m-for-global-expansion)). Raising an up-round in the middle of a difficult 2022 fundraising environment was notable; Nicholas attributed the demand to rising fraud sophistication and the need for tools that help institutions land more "good" customers while weeding out the "bad" ones ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/regulation/2022/fraud-prevention-fintech-alloy-nets-52m-on-1-55b-valuation/)). The company said the money would fund a global expansion into 40 countries across North America, Latin America, Asia Pacific, Europe, the Middle East, and Africa ([Retail Banker International](https://www.retailbankerinternational.com/news/alloy-garners-52m-funding-international-expansion/)).

## What the platform does and where it expanded

Alloy's core product is an API-based platform that connects to 160 or more third-party data sources, letting banks and fintechs automate identity decisions during account origination and monitor those decisions on an ongoing basis ([Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/40906/alloy-raises-52m-for-global-expansion)). The 2021 addition of transaction monitoring was the key product evolution: it gave clients automated decisioning on high-risk events and transactions, real-time API activity monitoring for Bank Secrecy Act and anti-money-laundering compliance, and case management to act on flagged activity inside Alloy ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2021/09/alloy-identity-verification-transaction-monitoring-platform-financial-services-kyc-laura-spiekerman/)). The company later moved into credit underwriting as well ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/regulation/2022/fraud-prevention-fintech-alloy-nets-52m-on-1-55b-valuation/)).

The platform's positioning has always leaned on access for smaller institutions: Spiekerman argued that Alloy's alternative-data sources let banks verify applicants who lack traditional identification — the underbanked, immigrants, students — and that its typical buyers range from early-stage fintechs to regional banks and top-20 banks ([AlleyWatch](https://www.alleywatch.com/2021/09/alloy-identity-verification-transaction-monitoring-platform-financial-services-kyc-laura-spiekerman/)). Named customers and partners in independent coverage include Ally Bank, Ramp, Evolve Bank & Trust, Carta, Brex, the $8 billion Florida community bank Amerant Bank, Prove (identity verification), and Nova Credit (inclusive lending) ([Finovate](https://finovate.com/alloy-earns-1-35-billion-valuation-after-securing-series-c-investment/); [Retail Banker International](https://www.retailbankerinternational.com/news/alloy-garners-52m-funding-international-expansion/); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/regulation/2022/fraud-prevention-fintech-alloy-nets-52m-on-1-55b-valuation/); [BusinessMole](https://www.businessmole.com/fintech-startup-alloy-launches-in-the-uk/)).

The global expansion began in earnest with the 2022 announcement of 40 new countries, followed by a UK launch with a London office — Alloy's first physical site outside the United States. Former COO Edwina Johnson relocated to London to head global expansion, and James Baston-Pitt, previously a vice president at Onfido, joined as EMEA Director of Growth ([BusinessMole](https://www.businessmole.com/fintech-startup-alloy-launches-in-the-uk/)). By that point more than 350 companies used the platform and total disclosed funding exceeded $210 million ([BusinessMole](https://www.businessmole.com/fintech-startup-alloy-launches-in-the-uk/)).

## What this profile does not claim

- The exact legal entity name and registration were not stated in any fully-read source; "Alloy" is used here as the operating name of the New York identity-decisioning company.
- One Finovate page elsewhere described the company as headquartered in St. Paul, Minnesota, but the Finovate article read in full for this profile says New York City, and Finextra, AlleyWatch, PYMNTS, and VC News Daily all describe it as a New York company — so this profile treats NYC as the verified location and the Minnesota reference as an editorial error.
- Industry recognitions (Forbes Cloud 100, CNBC's top-fintech lists) were mentioned only in material this profile could not read in full, so they are not claimed here.
- The Series A details come from tier3 funding press; the amount, lead, and participants are corroborated by a second funding database but were not confirmed in tier1/tier2 reporting.
- Independent coverage of the company after the UK launch was thin in this research pass; developments since 2023 are deliberately left out rather than guessed at.
