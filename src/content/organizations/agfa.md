## Overview

Agfa-Gevaert N.V. (Agfa) is a Belgian imaging-technology group headquartered in Mortsel, Belgium, whose roots go back to a German aniline-dye factory founded in 1867 ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/agfa-gevaert-group-n-v-history/)). Listed on Euronext Brussels and led since 2020 by Pascal Juéry, it reported revenue of €1,086 million in 2025 from healthcare imaging software, industrial printing and green-hydrogen membranes ([Alabrent](https://alabrent.com/news/32061); [Agfa-Gevaert](https://app.researchpool.com/company/agfb-be0003755692-agfa-gevaert-nv-gs3evzzlUE/press/agfa-gevaert-nv-agfb-agfa-gevaert-group-in-2025-strong-q4-profitability-and-free-cash-flow-J2WYKkqn5R)).

Agfa is a company that has reinvented itself repeatedly: a 19th-century dye maker became a photographic giant, then a medical-imaging and printing group, and now a supplier of membranes for green hydrogen. It left the consumer market in 2004, when it sold its consumer film business — the AgfaPhoto brand most people remember — and it has spent the two decades since selling off legacy divisions and betting on three growth engines: cloud healthcare imaging, digital printing and green hydrogen ([PI World](https://www.piworld.com/article/agfa-comments-insolvency-filing-agfaphoto-gmbh-24940/4/); [Agfa-Gevaert](https://app.researchpool.com/company/agfb-be0003755692-agfa-gevaert-nv-gs3evzzlUE/press/agfa-gevaert-nv-agfb-the-agfa-gevaert-group-in-2024-strong-performance-from-growth-engines-ZDQXc5ScHT)). In September 2026 it agreed to combine its digital printing business with EFI, the largest deal in its recent history ([World Imaging News](https://worldimagingnews.com/efi-and-agfa-dps-to-combine-what-the-deal-means-for-wide-format-and-industrial-inkjet/)).

## From aniline dyes to imaging: 160 years of reinvention

Agfa began in 1867, when Paul Mendelssohn-Bartholdy — brother of the composer Felix Mendelssohn — and Carl Alexander von Martius set up a factory in Germany to produce colour dyes; the company went public in 1873 as the Aktien Gesellschaft für Anilin-Fabrikation, or Agfa ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/agfa-gevaert-group-n-v-history/)). It entered photography early, producing its first photographic materials in 1888, and one of its researchers developed Rodinal in 1891, a black-and-white developer that became an industry standard ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/agfa-gevaert-group-n-v-history/)). In Belgium, the photographer Lieven Gevaert began making photographic papers in Antwerp in 1890, incorporated L. Gevaert & Cie in 1894, and moved the business to Mortsel in 1904 ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/agfa-gevaert-group-n-v-history/)).

The two halves converged through Germany's chemical consolidation: Agfa joined a community of interests with Bayer and BASF in 1905, and in 1925 the group merged with Bayer, BASF, Hoechst and others to form IG Farben ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/agfa-gevaert-group-n-v-history/)). Under Farben, Agfa introduced its first film roll camera, the Billy, in 1928, and launched colour films using the subtractive colour process in 1936 ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/agfa-gevaert-group-n-v-history/)). IG Farben's role in the Nazi regime — including its development of the Zyklon B gas used in the death camps and its use of slave labour — led to the group's dismantling after the war, and the division of Germany cut Agfa off from its main production site in Wolfen ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/agfa-gevaert-group-n-v-history/)). Production resumed at Bayer's Leverkusen site in 1945, Agfa returned to business in 1952, and it became a wholly owned Bayer subsidiary in 1953 ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/agfa-gevaert-group-n-v-history/)).

The post-war decades were a run of firsts: the first fully automatic camera in 1956, the world's first fully automatic 35 mm camera in 1959, and the first European-designed copy machine in 1972 ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/agfa-gevaert-group-n-v-history/)). In 1964 Agfa merged with Gevaert to form Agfa-Gevaert NV, with Bayer keeping a 50% stake, and Bayer took full control in 1981 ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/agfa-gevaert-group-n-v-history/)). The digital turn came in the 1980s — the world's first digital LED printer in 1983, a CCD flatbed scanner in 1985 and the first mini-lab in 1989 — followed by a string of acquisitions: the American pre-press maker Compugraphic in 1988, Hoechst's Ozasol printing-plate business in 1996, DuPont's offset printing operation in 1998, and Krautkramer and Quadrat in 2000 ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/agfa-gevaert-group-n-v-history/)).

Independence came in 1999, when Bayer spun off 75% of Agfa in a public offering on the Frankfurt and Euronext Brussels exchanges and the group's headquarters moved to Mortsel; Bayer sold its remaining stake to Goldman Sachs in 2002 ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/agfa-gevaert-group-n-v-history/)). By then the group was already shrinking towards its digital future: a 2001 restructuring aimed to cut more than €400 million a year in costs, with over 4,000 of its 22,000 jobs slated to go, and in 2003 it sold its non-destructive-testing unit to General Electric ([FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/agfa-gevaert-group-n-v-history/)).

## Agfa today: healthcare software, print and green hydrogen

Agfa's transformation accelerated under activist pressure. In 2018 the Luxembourg fund Active Ownership surfaced with a 13.4% stake, becoming the company's largest shareholder, and its founder Klaus Röhrig became chairman ([Undervalued Shares](https://www.undervalued-shares.com/weekly-dispatches/agfa-gevaert-and-activist-investing-in-europe-a-case-study/)). In 2020 Agfa sold parts of its healthcare IT business to the Italian software group Dedalus for €975 million, completing the deal that May ([Undervalued Shares](https://www.undervalued-shares.com/weekly-dispatches/agfa-gevaert-and-activist-investing-in-europe-a-case-study/)). The same year Pascal Juéry, a former Rhodia and Solvay executive, became chief executive on 1 February, succeeding Christian Reinaudo ([Alabrent](https://alabrent.com/news/32061)). CompuGroup Medical had already tried and failed to take Agfa over in 2016 ([Undervalued Shares](https://www.undervalued-shares.com/weekly-dispatches/agfa-gevaert-and-activist-investing-in-europe-a-case-study/)).

The HealthCare IT division that remains is built on Agfa's Enterprise Imaging platform, and a 2020 case study described it as the number-one market leader for hospital software in Germany ([Undervalued Shares](https://www.undervalued-shares.com/weekly-dispatches/agfa-gevaert-and-activist-investing-in-europe-a-case-study/); [Agfa-Gevaert](https://app.researchpool.com/company/agfb-be0003755692-agfa-gevaert-nv-gs3evzzlUE/press/agfa-gevaert-nv-agfb-the-agfa-gevaert-group-in-2024-strong-performance-from-growth-engines-ZDQXc5ScHT)). Its shift to cloud delivery is the group's clearest growth story: order intake rose 32% in 2024 to €164.8 million, with 27% of it cloud-related, and rose another 14% in 2025 to €187 million as cloud orders grew 38% ([Agfa-Gevaert](https://app.researchpool.com/company/agfb-be0003755692-agfa-gevaert-nv-gs3evzzlUE/press/agfa-gevaert-nv-agfb-the-agfa-gevaert-group-in-2024-strong-performance-from-growth-engines-ZDQXc5ScHT); [Agfa-Gevaert](https://app.researchpool.com/company/agfb-be0003755692-agfa-gevaert-nv-gs3evzzlUE/press/agfa-gevaert-nv-agfb-agfa-gevaert-group-in-2025-strong-q4-profitability-and-free-cash-flow-J2WYKkqn5R)). The division won three KLAS awards in 2024, including best-in-category for its XERO Viewer and its vendor-neutral archive, and signed a cloud Enterprise Imaging deal covering 120 Alliance Medical sites across the UK and Ireland ([Agfa-Gevaert](https://app.researchpool.com/company/agfb-be0003755692-agfa-gevaert-nv-gs3evzzlUE/press/agfa-gevaert-nv-agfb-the-agfa-gevaert-group-in-2024-strong-performance-from-growth-engines-ZDQXc5ScHT)).

Digital Print & Chemicals is the other growth engine: its revenue grew 7.2% in 2024 and 6.5% in 2025, driven by Digital Printing Solutions (up 13% in 2024) and Green Hydrogen Solutions (up 27% in 2024), while ink sales grew 15% in 2024 ([Agfa-Gevaert](https://app.researchpool.com/company/agfb-be0003755692-agfa-gevaert-nv-gs3evzzlUE/press/agfa-gevaert-nv-agfb-the-agfa-gevaert-group-in-2024-strong-performance-from-growth-engines-ZDQXc5ScHT); [Agfa-Gevaert](https://app.researchpool.com/company/agfb-be0003755692-agfa-gevaert-nv-gs3evzzlUE/press/agfa-gevaert-nv-agfb-agfa-gevaert-group-in-2025-strong-q4-profitability-and-free-cash-flow-J2WYKkqn5R)). Agfa launched three new sign-and-display printers in 2024, installed its first water-based SpeedSet Orca packaging printer at The Delta Group in the UK, and signed a multi-year contract with BHS Corrugated for single-pass corrugated printers ([Agfa-Gevaert](https://app.researchpool.com/company/agfb-be0003755692-agfa-gevaert-nv-gs3evzzlUE/press/agfa-gevaert-nv-agfb-the-agfa-gevaert-group-in-2024-strong-performance-from-growth-engines-ZDQXc5ScHT)). Its ZIRFON membranes — gas-separator membranes for alkaline water electrolysers — won Stiesdal Hydrogen as a customer in late 2024 and a first major customer in India, and in September 2025 Agfa inaugurated a new ZIRFON production plant in Mortsel, backed by an €11 million EU Innovation Fund grant ([Agfa-Gevaert](https://app.researchpool.com/company/agfb-be0003755692-agfa-gevaert-nv-gs3evzzlUE/press/agfa-gevaert-nv-agfb-the-agfa-gevaert-group-in-2024-strong-performance-from-growth-engines-ZDQXc5ScHT); [H2-International](https://www.h2-international.com/cover/manufacturing-agfa-commissions-production-facility-ael-membranes)).

The drag on the group is its legacy film business. Radiology Solutions revenue fell 9.8% in 2024 to €383 million and a further 17.1% in 2025 to €317 million as medical-film volumes declined, although Direct Radiography grew 8% in 2024 and Agfa is pushing AI-assisted image processing under the Musica, SmartXR and ScanXR brands ([Agfa-Gevaert](https://app.researchpool.com/company/agfb-be0003755692-agfa-gevaert-nv-gs3evzzlUE/press/agfa-gevaert-nv-agfb-the-agfa-gevaert-group-in-2024-strong-performance-from-growth-engines-ZDQXc5ScHT); [Agfa-Gevaert](https://app.researchpool.com/company/agfb-be0003755692-agfa-gevaert-nv-gs3evzzlUE/press/agfa-gevaert-nv-agfb-agfa-gevaert-group-in-2025-strong-q4-profitability-and-free-cash-flow-J2WYKkqn5R)). A €50 million savings programme to align the film cost base with the shrinking market is due to complete by the end of 2027, and Agfa agreed to close its computed-radiography plate and cassette production at Schrobenhausen in Germany ([Agfa-Gevaert](https://app.researchpool.com/company/agfb-be0003755692-agfa-gevaert-nv-gs3evzzlUE/press/agfa-gevaert-nv-agfb-the-agfa-gevaert-group-in-2024-strong-performance-from-growth-engines-ZDQXc5ScHT)).

In April 2023 it completed the sale of its Offset Solutions division to the Aurelius Group for €49 million; the division was rebranded ECO3, and Agfa still supplies it with film, consumables and services through a Contractor Operations & Services unit ([Agfa-Gevaert](https://financialreports.eu/filings/agfa-gevaert-nv/quarterly-report/2023/3109955/)). From 1 January 2026 Agfa reports through three segments — HealthCare IT, Industrial Solutions (digital printing plus green hydrogen) and Imaging and Chemicals (film, chemicals and digital radiology) ([Agfa-Gevaert](https://app.researchpool.com/company/agfb-be0003755692-agfa-gevaert-nv-gs3evzzlUE/press/agfa-gevaert-nv-agfb-agfa-gevaert-group-in-2025-strong-q4-profitability-and-free-cash-flow-J2WYKkqn5R)). And in September 2026 it signed a definitive agreement to combine its Digital Printing Solutions business with EFI, the industrial inkjet company owned by the private-equity firm Siris: Siris would hold 60% of the combined company and Agfa 40%, with the two acting as equal partners, and the combined business is expected to generate about $625 million in 2026 revenue ([World Imaging News](https://worldimagingnews.com/efi-and-agfa-dps-to-combine-what-the-deal-means-for-wide-format-and-industrial-inkjet/)). ## Two long legal sagas: the JPEG patent and AgfaPhoto

Agfa's name appears in one of the tech industry's most notorious patent campaigns. In April 2004 Forgent Networks sued 30 companies that had rejected its royalty terms for US Patent 4,698,672, covering compression techniques at the heart of the JPEG image format ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2004/04/23/forgent_jpeg_suit/)). The patent had been filed by [Compression Labs](/organizations/compression-labs/) in October 1986 and came to Forgent when it acquired the dormant company in 1997; Forgent had already persuaded [Sony](/organizations/sony/) to pay $15 million for a licence in 2002 and claimed some $90 million in royalties ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2004/04/23/forgent_jpeg_suit/)). The defendant list spanned Japanese electronics giants, camera makers [Fuji](/organizations/fujifilm/), Agfa and [Kodak](/organizations/kodak/), PC makers [IBM](/organizations/ibm/), [Dell](/organizations/dell/), [Apple](/organizations/apple/), [HP](/organizations/hp/) and [Toshiba](/organizations/toshiba/), and software firms including [Adobe](/organizations/adobe/) and Macromedia ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2004/04/23/forgent_jpeg_suit/)). Forgent Networks, the plaintiff in that suit, is the company now profiled as [Asure Software](/organizations/asure-software/).

The second saga is Agfa's own, and it ran for more than twenty years. When Agfa sold its consumer imaging business in a management buyout effective 2 November 2004, the new company, AgfaPhoto GmbH, took the film, finishing and lab-equipment business for a purchase price of €112 million, funded by a secured vendor loan from Agfa ([PI World](https://www.piworld.com/article/agfa-comments-insolvency-filing-agfaphoto-gmbh-24940/4/)). Six months later AgfaPhoto filed for insolvency, and its receiver accused Agfa of undercapitalising the spun-off company ([PI World](https://www.piworld.com/article/agfa-comments-insolvency-filing-agfaphoto-gmbh-24940/4/); [Agfa-Gevaert](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/arbitral-tribunal-acting-under-icc-rules-arbitration-issues-final-award-arbitration)). The dispute finally reached an ICC arbitral tribunal, which on 24 June 2025 dismissed all of the receiver's claims and ordered it to pay Agfa about €38 million plus interest in costs; the receiver paid roughly €45 million ([Agfa-Gevaert](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/arbitral-tribunal-acting-under-icc-rules-arbitration-issues-final-award-arbitration); [Agfa-Gevaert](https://uk.advfn.com/stock-market/TG/AGE/share-news/Agfa-Gevaert-in-Q2-2026-resilient-performance-amid-market-challenges/99192081)). The €38 million cash-in helped Agfa to a positive free cash flow of €35 million in 2025 ([Agfa-Gevaert](https://app.researchpool.com/company/agfb-be0003755692-agfa-gevaert-nv-gs3evzzlUE/press/agfa-gevaert-nv-agfb-agfa-gevaert-group-in-2025-strong-q4-profitability-and-free-cash-flow-J2WYKkqn5R)). But the case is not over: on 29 May 2026 the Frankfurt Higher Regional Court annulled the award, finding that the tribunal had violated the receiver's right to be heard, and remitted the case to the same tribunal; Agfa has appealed to the German Federal Court of Justice, a process expected to take 12 to 15 months ([Agfa-Gevaert](https://uk.advfn.com/stock-market/TG/AGE/share-news/Agfa-Gevaert-in-Q2-2026-resilient-performance-amid-market-challenges/99192081)).

## Frequently asked questions

**What does Agfa do today?**
Agfa sells healthcare imaging software built on its Enterprise Imaging platform, industrial inkjet printers and inks, radiology equipment and medical film, and ZIRFON membranes for green-hydrogen electrolysers. It also supplies specialty films and chemicals for industrial uses, and film and consumables to its former offset division, now ECO3 ([Agfa-Gevaert](https://app.researchpool.com/company/agfb-be0003755692-agfa-gevaert-nv-gs3evzzlUE/press/agfa-gevaert-nv-agfb-agfa-gevaert-group-in-2025-strong-q4-profitability-and-free-cash-flow-J2WYKkqn5R); [H2-International](https://www.h2-international.com/cover/manufacturing-agfa-commissions-production-facility-ael-membranes)).

**Is Agfa still in consumer photography?**
No. Agfa sold its consumer imaging business in a management buyout effective 2 November 2004, and the business became AgfaPhoto GmbH, which filed for insolvency six months later ([PI World](https://www.piworld.com/article/agfa-comments-insolvency-filing-agfaphoto-gmbh-24940/4/)). The sale ended Agfa's own consumer film, camera and photo-equipment business in 2004 ([PI World](https://www.piworld.com/article/agfa-comments-insolvency-filing-agfaphoto-gmbh-24940/4/)).

**What happened to AgfaPhoto?**
AgfaPhoto GmbH filed for insolvency in May 2005, six months after the buyout, and its receiver spent two decades pursuing claims against Agfa ([PI World](https://www.piworld.com/article/agfa-comments-insolvency-filing-agfaphoto-gmbh-24940/4/)). An ICC tribunal dismissed the claims in June 2025, but a Frankfurt court annulled that award in May 2026 and Agfa has appealed ([Agfa-Gevaert](https://uk.advfn.com/stock-market/TG/AGE/share-news/Agfa-Gevaert-in-Q2-2026-resilient-performance-amid-market-challenges/99192081)).

**What is the EFI deal?**
On 28 September 2026 Agfa agreed to combine its Digital Printing Solutions business with EFI, the industrial inkjet company owned by Siris, with Siris holding 60% and Agfa 40% of the combined company ([World Imaging News](https://worldimagingnews.com/efi-and-agfa-dps-to-combine-what-the-deal-means-for-wide-format-and-industrial-inkjet/)). The deal is expected to close by the end of 2026, subject to approvals.

**Who runs Agfa and where is it based?**
Agfa is headquartered in Mortsel, Belgium, and has been led since 1 February 2020 by chief executive Pascal Juéry, who succeeded Christian Reinaudo after a decade in charge. It is listed on Euronext Brussels under the ticker AGFB and reports in euros ([Alabrent](https://alabrent.com/news/32061); [FundingUniverse](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/agfa-gevaert-group-n-v-history/)).
