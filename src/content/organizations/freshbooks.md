## Overview

FreshBooks is a Toronto-based cloud accounting software company that serves small businesses and self-employed professionals with invoicing, expenses, payments, payroll and financial reporting ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/09/freshbooks-secures-130-75m)). Its defining structural fact is unusual in software: founded in 2003, it ran for eleven years without outside capital before raising its first institutional round in 2014 ([Cantech Letter](https://www.cantechletter.com/2014/07/freshbooks-finally-gives-suitors-raises-30-million-series/)). In August 2021 an $80.75 million Series E plus $50 million in debt financing valued the company at more than $1 billion — Toronto's addition to the unicorn roster ([Bloomberg](https://news.bloombergtax.com/financial-accounting/freshbooks-raises-80-75m-in-series-e-funding)).

## Eleven years of bootstrapping

FreshBooks was founded in 2003 by Mike McDerment and Joe Sawada, rising — in Cantech Letter's contemporary account — "from humble origins in McDerment's parents basement" to become a leader in cloud-based invoicing for small businesses ([Cantech Letter](https://www.cantechletter.com/2014/07/freshbooks-finally-gives-suitors-raises-30-million-series/)). The origin story TechCrunch retells is the classic founder-pain-point arc: in 2003 McDerment was running a small design agency and found Word and Excel frustrating for billing clients, so he coded his own invoicing solution — the foundation of FreshBooks ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/09/freshbooks-secures-130-75m)). The company was self-funded until 2014 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/09/freshbooks-secures-130-75m)).

McDerment's resistance to venture money was public and pointed. Late in 2012 he told Bloomberg's Ari Levy he wasn't interested in any investor with a short-term window: "I don't want to hear anything about you needing your funds back until year No. 9," he said — "At which point I will call you and ask you if you still want it back in the next year" ([Cantech Letter](https://www.cantechletter.com/2014/07/freshbooks-finally-gives-suitors-raises-30-million-series/)). When the company finally did raise in July 2014 — $30 million led by Oak Investment Partners with Atlas Venture and Georgian Partners — it employed 122 people serving customers in 120 countries ([Cantech Letter](https://www.cantechletter.com/2014/07/freshbooks-finally-gives-suitors-raises-30-million-series/)). A $43 million Series B followed in 2017, led by Georgian Partners and backed by Accomplice and Oak Investment Partners, and JPMorgan Chase made a strategic investment of undisclosed size in August 2019 ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/freshbooks-reaches-unicorn-status/)).

## The unicorn round and the CEO handoff

January 2021 brought a changing of the guard: founder Mike McDerment stepped down as CEO and became executive chair, with Don Epperson — who had joined FreshBooks as executive director in 2019 — taking over as chief executive ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/09/freshbooks-secures-130-75m)). Seven months later the company announced the round that made it a unicorn: $80.75 million in Series E equity led by existing backer Accomplice, which described it as an "inside round," plus $50 million in debt financing, at a valuation of "over $1 billion" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/09/freshbooks-secures-130-75m); [Bloomberg](https://news.bloombergtax.com/financial-accounting/freshbooks-raises-80-75m-in-series-e-funding)). J.P. Morgan, Gaingels, BMO Technology & Innovation Banking Group and Manulife participated in the equity investment, and Barclays — already a FreshBooks platform partner in the UK — joined as a new investor ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/09/freshbooks-secures-130-75m)). Total lifetime funding stood above $200 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/09/freshbooks-secures-130-75m)).

Epperson framed the raise around regulated markets and compliance-driven digitization: the company would invest in markets becoming more regulated and help owners manage finances through "simplistic workflows," noting that the pandemic had accelerated the shift of small businesses to digital management ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/09/freshbooks-secures-130-75m)).

## Serving the businesses the incumbents ignored

FreshBooks' pitch has always been that small service-based businesses are poorly served by accounting software built for accountants or enterprises. At the 2014 Series A, McDerment argued that small business owners were "grossly under-served when it comes to accounting software," forced to choose between complex tools and running their businesses on Word and Excel — and positioned FreshBooks as "the only company exclusively focused on the needs of service-based businesses — ironically, the largest segment of the small business market" ([Cantech Letter](https://www.cantechletter.com/2014/07/freshbooks-finally-gives-suitors-raises-30-million-series/)). Oak Investment Partners' Ann Lamont framed the bet as growing the cloud accounting market for the millions of owners "that want to run their businesses without having to learn accounting" ([Cantech Letter](https://www.cantechletter.com/2014/07/freshbooks-finally-gives-suitors-raises-30-million-series/)). The product scope TechCrunch described in 2021 — invoicing, expenses, payments, payroll and financial reporting for small business owners, self-employed people and their clients — stayed close to that original thesis ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/09/freshbooks-secures-130-75m)).

The pandemic gave the company a data-driven public role. Analyzing its own proprietary data, FreshBooks found that women-owned businesses were taking three times longer to recover in the U.S. than businesses owned by men — a finding Epperson said laid the foundation for data-sharing partnerships with local governments to help policymakers shape small-business support ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/09/freshbooks-secures-130-75m)). Around the same period, the credit-control automation platform Chaser integrated with FreshBooks to carry out automated, personalized invoice payments through cloud-to-cloud integration, attacking the late-payment problem for small firms ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/partnerships-acquisitions/2021/freshbooks-acquires-fastbill-to-boost-growth-in-canada/)).

## Expansion by acquisition

The funding was earmarked in part for strategic acquisitions, and FreshBooks had already begun buying its way into new markets ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/09/freshbooks-secures-130-75m)). In September 2020 it acquired Facturama, a Mexico-based e-invoicing company, entering the Latin American market and expanding its audience in Spanish-speaking markets ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/09/freshbooks-secures-130-75m)). In October 2021 it announced the acquisition of FastBill, a German fintech with customers in 16 European countries whose software automates invoices, receipts and banking processes ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/partnerships-acquisitions/2021/freshbooks-acquires-fastbill-to-boost-growth-in-canada/)). FastBill founder Rene Maudrich stayed on as CEO and additionally became managing director of FreshBooks Germany, with the FastBill team remaining at its Frankfurt headquarters — making the deal FreshBooks' entry point into Germany and Central Europe ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/partnerships-acquisitions/2021/freshbooks-acquires-fastbill-to-boost-growth-in-canada/)).

Headquartered in Toronto, the company by 2021 employed 500 people across Canada, Croatia, Mexico, the Netherlands and the United States, and said it had served more than 30 million people in over 160 countries ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/partnerships-acquisitions/2021/freshbooks-acquires-fastbill-to-boost-growth-in-canada/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/09/freshbooks-secures-130-75m)).
