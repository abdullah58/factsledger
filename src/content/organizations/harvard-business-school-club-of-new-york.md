## Overview

The Harvard Business School Club of New York is the nonprofit alumni organisation for [Harvard Business School](/universities/harvard-business-school/) graduates living in the Greater [New York City](/places/new-york-city/) area. The club dates itself to 1920 and says it serves approximately 13,000 HBS alumni, running more than 100 events a year — most featuring C-suite speakers — alongside pro bono consulting for local nonprofits, angel investing, and alumni forums ([HBS Club of New York](https://www.hbscny.org/?p=2067)). Its stated mission is "to Make a Difference and to Support Harvard Business School by Engaging Alumni, Impacting Community, Fostering Leadership and Lifelong Learning" ([HBS Club of New York](https://www.hbscny.org/?p=2067)). It is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, legally named The Harvard Business School Club of Greater New York, based at 1460 Broadway, and its tax filings put its annual revenue at about $2 million ([IRS Form 990](https://irs-efile-renderer.instrumentl.com/render?object_id=202310959349301466&form=IRS990&v=1)).

## What the club does

The club describes itself as a platform for HBS alumni "to use their leadership skills to make a meaningful difference with fellow alumni in areas of mutual interest" ([HBS Club of New York](https://www.hbscny.org/?p=2067)). Its activities are conducted by approximately 400 alumni volunteers, and it is governed by a board of directors composed of HBS alumni ([HBS Club of New York](https://www.hbscny.org/?p=2067)). Membership is limited to HBS alumni, although joint membership with its angel-investing arm is open case by case to graduates of other Harvard schools ([HBS Club of New York](https://www.hbscny.org/?p=2067)). The angel group, HBS Alumni Angels of Greater New York, is a separate 501(c)(6) organisation ([HBS Club of New York](https://www.hbscny.org/?p=2067)).

The scale is documented in the club's own tax filing rather than in press coverage. Its Form 990 for the year ending June 2022 lists 39 voting board members, 12 employees, and about 300 volunteers, with gross receipts of $2,097,042, total revenue of $2,020,153, and total expenses of $1,044,091 ([IRS Form 990](https://irs-efile-renderer.instrumentl.com/render?object_id=202310959349301466&form=IRS990&v=1)). The filing breaks programme spending into $516,395 for member and alumni activities — events spanning finance, lifestyle, marketing, media, art, social enterprise, career development, and talks by HBS professors — and $140,000 in contributions to Harvard Business School itself, alongside $155,000 in grants ([IRS Form 990](https://irs-efile-renderer.instrumentl.com/render?object_id=202310959349301466&form=IRS990&v=1)).

## Community Partners: pro bono consulting

The club's most documented public-facing work is Community Partners, its pro bono consulting programme for nonprofits in the greater New York City area, founded in 1998. Harvard Business School's Social Enterprise Initiative reports that the division "provides over $3M in pro bono consulting services to nonprofit organizations in the greater New York City area annually" ([Harvard Business School](http://sei-pantheon.hbs.edu/blog/life-after-rikers-hbs-alumni-volunteers-complete-project-aimed-at-reducing-recidivism-in-nyc/)). Alumni teams take on management problems — strategy, infrastructure, scaling — for organisations that could not otherwise afford that expertise.

One project illustrates the model. A team of seven alumni volunteers worked with Getting Out and Staying Out (GOSO), a nonprofit providing reentry services to young men aged 16 to 24 detained at or released from Rikers Island and other detention centres, helping it build infrastructure responsive to New York City's supervised-release programme and plan how to scale its model geographically ([Harvard Business School](http://sei-pantheon.hbs.edu/blog/life-after-rikers-hbs-alumni-volunteers-complete-project-aimed-at-reducing-recidivism-in-nyc/)). The volunteers included Charley Beever (MBA 1978), Lauren Marrus (MBA 1987), and Efrem Sigel (MBA 1968), who initiated the project after hearing GOSO present at a police-precinct community meeting ([Harvard Business School](http://sei-pantheon.hbs.edu/blog/life-after-rikers-hbs-alumni-volunteers-complete-project-aimed-at-reducing-recidivism-in-nyc/)). GOSO's leadership said the team's plan "has already helped us shape the direction and growth of our agency" ([Harvard Business School](http://sei-pantheon.hbs.edu/blog/life-after-rikers-hbs-alumni-volunteers-complete-project-aimed-at-reducing-recidivism-in-nyc/)).

## The Cloudflare connection — and a correction

The club's best-known brush with a famous startup is often misdescribed, so the correction matters. [Cloudflare](/organizations/cloudflare/)'s founders, [Matthew Prince](/people/matthew-prince/) and [Michelle Zatlyn](/people/michelle-zatlyn/) (both HBS '09), did win a business plan competition in 2009 — but it was Harvard Business School's own 13th Annual Business Plan Contest, run by the school's Arthur Rock Center for Entrepreneurship, not a competition run by the Club of New York ([Harvard Business School](https://www.hbs.edu/news/releases/2009bizplancontest)). The club's actual connection to the story is as host: on 6 October 2020 it staged a virtual event, "How Cloudflare's Founders … Are Helping to Build a Better Internet", moderated by journalist David Pogue, at which Prince and Zatlyn retold the founding story — the [Silicon Valley](/places/silicon-valley/) class trip, the business model sketched on a napkin, Professor Tom Eisenmann recruited as advisor in a hotel lobby, and programmer [Lee Holloway](/people/lee-holloway/) enlisted back on campus ([HBS Club of New York](https://www.hbscny.org/events/register-virtual-how-cloudflareas-founders-matthew-prince-hbs-a09-and-michelle-zatlyn-hbs-a09-are-helping-to-build-a-better-internet/)). The club also collaborates on the school's New Venture Competition as the regional home for New York and Northeast applicants, one of eight regions worldwide ([HBS Club of New York](https://www.hbscny.org/?p=2067)).

## Not the Harvard Club

| | Harvard Business School Club of New York | Harvard Club of New York City |
|---|---|---|
| Members | HBS alumni only | Harvard University alumni, faculty, officers |
| Founded | 1920 (club's account; IRS filing says 1962) | 1865 |
| Legal form | 501(c)(3) public charity | Private social club |
| Base | 1460 Broadway, no clubhouse | 35 West 44th Street clubhouse |

The distinction is one the club itself stresses: asked "Are we affiliated with the Harvard Club?", its site answers, "No, they are an independent organization" ([HBS Club of New York](https://www.hbscny.org/?p=2067)). The Harvard Club of New York City is the older, separate social club founded in 1865 with a clubhouse on West 44th Street; the HBS Club of New York is the business school's regional alumni association, and the two should not be confused.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the HBS Club of New York the same as the Harvard Club of New York City?

No. They are independent organisations. The Harvard Club of New York City is a social club founded in 1865 with a clubhouse at 35 West 44th Street, while the HBS Club of New York is the regional alumni association for Harvard Business School graduates, based at 1460 Broadway. The HBS Club states plainly on its own site that the two are not affiliated.

### Who can join the HBS Club of New York?

Membership is limited to Harvard Business School alumni. Joint membership with the club's angel-investing arm, HBS Alumni Angels of Greater New York, is open case by case to graduates of other Harvard schools. The club's alumni data comes from the HBS Alumni Directory and is treated as confidential.

### What does Community Partners do?

Community Partners is the club's pro bono consulting programme, founded in 1998, which pairs teams of HBS alumni volunteers with New York-area nonprofits on business and management problems. HBS reports it delivers over $3 million a year in pro bono consulting, for example helping the reentry nonprofit GOSO plan its growth.

### Did Cloudflare win the club's business plan competition?

No — a common misattribution. Cloudflare won Harvard Business School's own 2009 Business Plan Contest, run by the school's Arthur Rock Center. The club's role was different: it hosted founders Matthew Prince and Michelle Zatlyn at a virtual event in October 2020, and it serves as a regional host for the school's New Venture Competition.

### How big is the club financially?

Its IRS Form 990 for the year ending June 2022 reported gross receipts of $2,097,042, total revenue of $2,020,153, and total expenses of $1,044,091, with $155,000 in grants and $140,000 contributed to Harvard Business School. It listed 39 board members, 12 employees, and about 300 volunteers.

## What this profile does not claim

- It does not resolve the founding-date conflict: the club says 1920, its IRS Form 990 says year of formation 1962, and no source read in this pass explains the difference. The 1920 date is the club's own claim, not an independently verified fact.
- It does not assert the club's current membership, revenue, or leadership beyond the figures cited — the most recent fully-read filing covers the year ending June 2022, and the club's president named on its site (Leo Corbett, MBA '75) is first-party information that may have changed.
- It does not claim the club ran the 2009 Business Plan Contest Cloudflare won; that contest was the school's own, and the profile corrects that attribution explicitly.
- "Harvard Business School Club of New York" here means the HBS alumni association (legal name The Harvard Business School Club of Greater New York), not the Harvard Club of New York City.
