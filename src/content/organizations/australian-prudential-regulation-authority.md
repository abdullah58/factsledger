## Overview

The Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA) is Australia's prudential regulator, responsible for licensing and overseeing banking, insurance and superannuation entities in order to protect depositors, policyholders and superannuation fund members and to promote the stability of the broader financial system ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=52364e2c-7193-4c16-8e53-ebf230c4ca3d)). Created on 1 July 1998 after the Wallis Inquiry into the financial system, it supervises banks, credit unions, building societies, general and life insurers, private health insurers, friendly societies and most of the superannuation industry ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=52364e2c-7193-4c16-8e53-ebf230c4ca3d)). It is an independent statutory authority headquartered in Sydney, and since 31 October 2022 its chair has been John Lonsdale, a former Australian Treasury official who joined APRA in 2018 ([Super Review](https://www.superreview.com.au/marking-25-years-apra)).

## Born from the Wallis Inquiry

APRA was born from a deliberate redesign. Far-reaching changes to Australia's financial regulatory structure came into effect on 1 July 1998 as the Government's response to the recommendations of the Financial System Inquiry, known as the Wallis Committee ([Reserve Bank of Australia](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/1998/jul/1.html)). The inquiry's Final Report had been released in April 1997, and the Government accepted its proposals almost in their entirety that September before legislating by 1 July 1998 ([Council of Financial Regulators](https://cfr.gov.au/publications/annual-reports/1998/aus-new-fin-reg-frmwk.html)). Under the new structure, a single prudential supervisor took over supervision of banks from the Reserve Bank of Australia and of life and general insurance and superannuation from the Insurance and Superannuation Commission, while supervision of building societies, credit unions and friendly societies was expected to transfer from State jurisdictions later in 1998 ([Reserve Bank of Australia](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/1998/jul/1.html)). The Bank's prudential standards and supervision staff transferred to APRA on 1 July, and Graeme Thompson served as its first chair ([Reserve Bank of Australia](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/1998/jul/1.html)) ([Super Review](https://www.superreview.com.au/marking-25-years-apra)).

## What APRA regulates, and the powers behind it

APRA was established under the Australian Prudential Regulation Authority Act 1998, and its functions are conferred by the statutes governing the industries it oversees, including the Banking Act 1959, the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993, the Insurance Act 1973 and the Life Insurance Act 1995 ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=52364e2c-7193-4c16-8e53-ebf230c4ca3d)). It grants authorised deposit-taking institution authorisations, general insurance authorisations, life insurance registrations and registrable superannuation entity licences under those Acts ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=52364e2c-7193-4c16-8e53-ebf230c4ca3d)). It sets prudential standards covering governance, risk management and financial soundness, including minimum capital and liquidity requirements for insurers and regular assessment of outcomes for superannuation members ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=52364e2c-7193-4c16-8e53-ebf230c4ca3d)). Its crisis powers are unusually direct: Banking Act amendments gave it clearer and stronger powers than the Reserve Bank had held, including revoking licences, issuing enforceable directions, appointing an investigator or statutory manager, taking control of a troubled authorised deposit-taking institution, and winding it up if its difficulties prove intractable ([Council of Financial Regulators](https://cfr.gov.au/publications/annual-reports/1998/aus-new-fin-reg-frmwk.html)). Depositors hold first claim on a failed institution's assets in a wind-up, but the arrangements confer no guarantee of depositors' funds, and depositors have no recourse to APRA itself ([Council of Financial Regulators](https://cfr.gov.au/publications/annual-reports/1998/aus-new-fin-reg-frmwk.html)).

## Tested by failure: HIH, the financial crisis, and Hayne

APRA's first great test arrived within three years of its creation. HIH Insurance, which had grown from 1968 origins into Australia's second-largest general insurer through rapid acquisition, collapsed after sacrificing prudence in risk management, with under-pricing, under-reserving, governance failures and mismanagement ([Australian Treasury](http://www.tspace.gov.au/sites/default/files/2019-03/Roundup_01-2015_article3_HIH.pdf)). Placed into liquidation in March 2001 with losses totalling 5.3 billion dollars, it became the subject of a royal commission that reported in 2003 ([Super Review](https://www.superreview.com.au/marking-25-years-apra)). Although the Royal Commission found that APRA did not cause or contribute to the collapse, it identified shortcomings in APRA's supervisory practices, after which APRA abandoned "light touch" supervision and significantly strengthened its practices ([Australian Treasury](http://www.tspace.gov.au/sites/default/files/2019-03/Roundup_01-2015_article3_HIH.pdf)). John Laker succeeded Thompson as chair in 2003, and the following year APRA investigated [National Australia Bank](/organizations/national-australia-bank/) over unauthorised foreign-exchange options trades costing 360 million dollars, an episode that led to the departure of chairman Charles Allen and chief executive Frank Cicutto ([Super Review](https://www.superreview.com.au/marking-25-years-apra)). During the 2008 global financial crisis, APRA stress-tested and monitored authorised deposit-taking institutions alongside ASIC and the Reserve Bank, with Australia faring better than the United States and the United Kingdom ([Super Review](https://www.superreview.com.au/marking-25-years-apra)). Wayne Byres succeeded Laker as chair in 2014, and the 2018-19 [Hayne Royal Commission](/events/hayne-royal-commission/) identified shortcomings in APRA's risk assessment and enforcement, recommending changes to its supervisory practices and governance and additional enforcement powers ([Super Review](https://www.superreview.com.au/marking-25-years-apra)). In superannuation, APRA's annual performance test, introduced in 2021, requires a fund that fails once to plan whether to close the product, transfer members or exit, and a second failure closes the product to new members ([Super Review](https://www.superreview.com.au/marking-25-years-apra)).

## The boundary most explainers blur

The most useful thing to understand about APRA is what it is not. Australia runs a "twin peaks" model: APRA is the prudential peak, while the Australian Securities and Investments Commission handles market integrity and consumer protection, and the Reserve Bank retains monetary policy and overall financial stability, including the payments system ([Reserve Bank of Australia](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/1998/jul/1.html)). The two regulators' remits overlap, and they operate under a memorandum of understanding covering coordination, cooperation and information-sharing ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=52364e2c-7193-4c16-8e53-ebf230c4ca3d)). A related misconception concerns the Financial Claims Scheme, the government-backed safety net Parliament created at the height of the global financial crisis in 2008 ([APRA](https://www.apra.gov.au/financial-claims-scheme-protecting-depositors-and-policyholders)). It covers deposits of up to 250,000 dollars per account holder per authorised deposit-taking institution and most general insurance claims up to 5,000 dollars, but not life insurers, private health insurers or superannuation funds ([APRA](https://www.apra.gov.au/financial-claims-scheme-protecting-depositors-and-policyholders)). If the Government activates it, APRA administers it — yet it has been activated only once, for Australian Family Assurance Limited in 2009, resulting in 29 payments ([APRA](https://www.apra.gov.au/financial-claims-scheme-protecting-depositors-and-policyholders)). The 250,000-dollar limit applies to deposits under a single banking licence, so brands of the same institution, such as BankWest within [Commonwealth Bank](/organizations/commonwealth-bank/) or St George within [Westpac](/organizations/westpac/), count together ([APRA](https://www.apra.gov.au/financial-claims-scheme-protecting-depositors-and-policyholders)).
