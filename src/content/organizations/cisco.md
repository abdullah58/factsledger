## Overview

Cisco Systems, Inc. is an American multinational technology company headquartered in San Jose, [California](/places/california/), that builds the networking hardware and software — routers, switches, security and collaboration tools — carrying much of the world's internet and corporate traffic. Founded in late 1984 by two [Stanford University](/universities/stanford-university/) computer staff, Leonard Bosack and Sandra Lerner, it reported $56.7 billion in revenue in fiscal 2025, with security its fastest-growing business after its $28 billion purchase of Splunk ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/cisco-systems-inc/); [Cisco](https://investor.cisco.com/files/doc_news/CISCO-REPORTS-FOURTH-QUARTER-AND-FISCAL-YEAR-2025-EARNINGS-2025.pdf)).

The name is short for [San Francisco](/places/san-francisco/), and the company's early routers linked machines from [IBM](/organizations/ibm/) and [Apple](/organizations/apple/) that could not otherwise talk to each other ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/cisco-systems-inc/)). Cisco trades on [Nasdaq](/organizations/nasdaq/) as CSCO, employs more than 85,000 people, and is led by chair and chief executive Chuck Robbins ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/cisco-systems-inc/); [Cisco](https://investor.cisco.com/files/doc_news/CISCO-REPORTS-FOURTH-QUARTER-AND-FISCAL-YEAR-2025-EARNINGS-2025.pdf)). Its business now spans networking, security, observability and collaboration, and its 2024 Splunk purchase pushed software and subscriptions to the centre of the company ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/cisco-systems-inc/)).

## A contested founding at Stanford

Bosack ran the computer science department's computers at Stanford while Lerner ran the Graduate School of Business's machines, and the two wanted their networks to talk. At the request of Stanford's director of computer facilities, Ralph Gorin, they built a device — the multiprotocol router — that linked systems running different networking technologies, using Stanford Medical School software and a circuit board designed by Andy Bechtolsheim. When they asked Stanford to use the technology commercially, the university refused, and the routers built in their Atherton home resembled Stanford's own "Blue Box" ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/cisco-systems-goes-public/)). The dispute ended on 15 April 1987, when Stanford licensed the router software and two circuit boards to Cisco for $19,300 in cash, $150,000 in royalties and product discounts — declining Cisco stock instead ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/cisco-systems-goes-public/)). The founders had mortgaged their house and maxed out their credit cards; 76 investors turned them down before Sequoia Capital's Don Valentine put in $2.5 million for a third of the company ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/cisco-systems-goes-public/); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/cisco-systems-inc/)).

## The router company that bought the internet

Valentine installed John Morgridge as chief executive in 1988, and sales jumped from $1.5 million in 1987 to $28 million in 1989. Cisco went public on 16 February 1990, selling 2.8 million shares at $18 each; the stock closed its first day at $22.25, with [Morgan Stanley](/organizations/morgan-stanley/) among the lead underwriters ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/cisco-systems-goes-public/)). Within months the founders were gone — Lerner was asked to leave in August 1990, Bosack quit, and in December they sold all their stock for $170 million ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/cisco-systems-goes-public/)). The company they left behind became an acquisition machine: it bought 70 companies between 1993 and 2000, passed $1 billion in annual sales in 1994, and on 27 March 2000 was briefly the most valuable company in the world, with a market capitalisation of $555.4 billion ([EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/cisco-systems-goes-public/); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/cisco-systems-inc/)). John Chambers, chief executive from January 1995, oversaw the buying spree, adding Meraki in 2012 and AppDynamics and BroadSoft in 2017, while alliances with [Microsoft](/organizations/microsoft/), [Hewlett-Packard](/organizations/hp/) and [Intel](/organizations/intel/) widened its reach ([Cisco](https://newsroom.cisco.com/c/r/newsroom/en/us/a/y2015/m05/cisco-board-names-chuck-robbins-as-next-ceo-john-chambers-to-become-executive-chairman-effective-july-26.html); [EBSCO](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/computer-science/cisco-systems-inc/)).

## The software turn: Splunk, security and AI

Chuck Robbins, appointed chief executive effective 26 July 2015 after joining Cisco in 1997, inherited a hardware giant trying to become a software company ([Cisco](https://newsroom.cisco.com/c/r/newsroom/en/us/a/y2015/m05/cisco-board-names-chuck-robbins-as-next-ceo-john-chambers-to-become-executive-chairman-effective-july-26.html)). The decisive move came on 18 March 2024, when Cisco completed its $28 billion purchase of Splunk — $157 a share in cash, as filed with the [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](/organizations/us-securities-and-exchange-commission/) — its largest acquisition ever, making it one of the largest software companies in the world ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/858877/000119312524070175/d783088dex991.htm)). The deal's logic showed up in the numbers: in fiscal 2025, security revenue rose 59% to $8.1 billion, while networking, still the largest business at $28.3 billion, slipped 3% ([Cisco](https://investor.cisco.com/files/doc_news/CISCO-REPORTS-FOURTH-QUARTER-AND-FISCAL-YEAR-2025-EARNINGS-2025.pdf)). Cisco also took more than $2 billion of AI-infrastructure orders from webscale customers in the year, over double its original $1 billion target ([Cisco](https://investor.cisco.com/files/doc_news/CISCO-REPORTS-FOURTH-QUARTER-AND-FISCAL-YEAR-2025-EARNINGS-2025.pdf)). Total revenue reached $56.7 billion, up 5%, with $9.3 billion spent on research and development and $14.2 billion of operating cash flow ([Cisco](https://investor.cisco.com/files/doc_news/CISCO-REPORTS-FOURTH-QUARTER-AND-FISCAL-YEAR-2025-EARNINGS-2025.pdf)).

## FAQ

### What does Cisco do?

Cisco designs and sells networking hardware and software — routers, switches, security, observability and collaboration tools — used by businesses and service providers to run their networks. It also sells services and support, and since its 2024 Splunk acquisition it pitches itself as one of the world's largest software companies.

### Who founded Cisco, and why did the founders leave?

Leonard Bosack and Sandra Lerner, Stanford University computer staff, founded Cisco in late 1984 around their multiprotocol router. After Sequoia Capital's Don Valentine installed John Morgridge as chief executive in 1988, Lerner clashed with the new management, was asked to leave in August 1990, and Bosack quit; they sold their stock for $170 million that December.

### What was Cisco's largest acquisition?

Cisco's largest acquisition is Splunk, the data-analytics and security firm it bought for about $28 billion — $157 a share in cash — completing the deal on 18 March 2024. Cisco said the purchase made it one of the largest software companies in the world, and its security revenue rose 59% in the following fiscal year.

### How big is Cisco today?

In fiscal 2025, ended 26 July 2025, Cisco reported revenue of $56.7 billion, up 5%, and GAAP net income of $10.5 billion. It employs more than 85,000 people, trades on Nasdaq as CSCO, and is led by chair and chief executive Chuck Robbins, in the role since July 2015.
