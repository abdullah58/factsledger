## Overview

Estonian World is an English-language online magazine about [Estonia](/places/estonia/) and its global diaspora, founded in London in 2012 by Silver Tambur, Sander Saar and Sten Hankewitz and now headquartered in [Tallinn](/places/tallinn/)
([Open Estonia Foundation](https://oef.org.ee/en/news/article/estonian-world-bestowed-open-estonia-foundations-concord-award)).
Its editor-in-chief is co-founder Silver Tambur, and its stated mission is to inform and unite Estonians, the
descendants of Estonians and the fans of Estonia around the world
([Open Estonia Foundation](https://oef.org.ee/en/news/article/estonian-world-bestowed-open-estonia-foundations-concord-award)).
In 2020 it received the Open Estonia Foundation's Concord Award for global Estonians and citizen-driven journalism
([Open Estonia Foundation](https://oef.org.ee/en/news/article/estonian-world-bestowed-open-estonia-foundations-concord-award)).

## Founding and founders

Estonian World began in 2012 as a citizen-driven medium. As Tambur put it when accepting the Concord Award, the
three founders started it in London "to help other global Estonians share cool stories about Estonia and
Estonians all over the world"
([Open Estonia Foundation](https://oef.org.ee/en/news/article/estonian-world-bestowed-open-estonia-foundations-concord-award)).
Tambur had been the editor of the Estonian Public Broadcasting service's English news output — ERR, the Estonian
national broadcaster — and had contributed to other outlets before deciding, while still living in emigration in
the UK, to launch Estonian World
([The Baltic Times](https://www.baltictimes.com/the_persevering_the_baltic_times_hosts_its_sixth_annual_gala_dinner/)).
He is a graduate of the University of Tartu
([The Baltic Times](https://www.baltictimes.com/the_persevering_the_baltic_times_hosts_its_sixth_annual_gala_dinner/)),
and describes himself as cofounder and editor-in-chief of the magazine
([Muck Rack](https://muckrack.com/silver-tambur)).
By 2020, Tambur said, almost 300 people had contributed to Estonian World over its first eight years
([Open Estonia Foundation](https://oef.org.ee/en/news/article/estonian-world-bestowed-open-estonia-foundations-concord-award)).
The magazine has editorial representations in London, Chicago, Los Angeles and Tallinn, with contributors around
the world
([Muck Rack](https://muckrack.com/media-outlet/estonianworld)).

## What it covers

Estonian World publishes news and analysis about Estonia for readers in Estonia and among the global
Estonian diaspora: by 2020 its editor-in-chief said it had readers "in every country in the world, including
Estonia"
([Open Estonia Foundation](https://oef.org.ee/en/news/article/estonian-world-bestowed-open-estonia-foundations-concord-award)).
Its recent output spans politics, technology, business, security and culture, as the examples below show
([Muck Rack](https://muckrack.com/media-outlet/estonianworld)).

| Coverage area | Recent example (November 2026) |
|---|---|
| Politics and technology | Tallinn Digital Summit 2026: Estonia debates AI agents and the future of government |
| Business | Eesti Energia named Estonia's most valuable company, valued at €3.6 billion |
| Security and infrastructure | Cyber-attack hits an Estonian supermarket's refrigeration system; €700 million drone-shield proposal |
| Culture | Anu Põder's sculptures at Sweden's Moderna Museet; Estonian-American composer Lembit Beecher |
| Diaspora voices | Jaak Treiman, honorary consul for four decades, on how Estonia's presidents built its influence |

The magazine has also covered Estonian cultural institutions directly. Its 25 December 2020 article by Silver
Tambur, "A Museum of Banned Books Opens in Estonia", reported the opening of the
[Banned Books Museum](/organizations/banned-books-museum/) in Tallinn's Old Town, founded by
[Joseph Maximillian Dunnigan](/people/joseph-maximillian-dunnigan/)
([Estonian World](https://estonianworld.com/culture/a-museum-of-banned-books-opens-in-estonia/)).

## Recognition and standing

The Open Estonia Foundation's Concord Award, founded in 1997, honours people and institutions whose activities
have helped form society in Estonia and elsewhere and helped residents understand one another better
([Open Estonia Foundation](https://oef.org.ee/en/news/article/estonian-world-bestowed-open-estonia-foundations-concord-award)).
On 27 May 2020 the Foundation bestowed its 23rd Concord Award on Estonian World, with the keywords of that
year's award being "global Estonians and citizen-driven journalism"
([Open Estonia Foundation](https://oef.org.ee/en/news/article/estonian-world-bestowed-open-estonia-foundations-concord-award)).
The award carried a €1,000 prize and a graphic drawing by artist Marje Üksine, called "The Concord Pillar"
([Open Estonia Foundation](https://oef.org.ee/en/news/article/estonian-world-bestowed-open-estonia-foundations-concord-award)).
Foundation director Mall Hellam said the magazine had "taken Estonia to the world and brought Estonia closer to
global Estonians, Estonian expats elsewhere and to estophiles" since 2012, and that a medium writing about what
is going on in Estonia and sharing its success stories "deserves recognition and financial support"
([Open Estonia Foundation](https://oef.org.ee/en/news/article/estonian-world-bestowed-open-estonia-foundations-concord-award)).
Two years later, Estonia's Foreign Ministry recognised Tambur personally with a letter of appreciation on
Citizen's Day, 26 November, "for the consistent and sensitive management of the English-language publication
Estonian World, bringing together Estonians and friends of Estonia in Estonia and beyond"
([Global Estonian](https://globalestonian.com/en/news/foreign-ministry-recognises-citizen-diplomats-citizens-day)).
The Baltic Times, the region's long-running English-language newspaper, has also treated Tambur as a peer voice
on independent journalism, inviting him to address its gala dinner about the inception and development of the
platform and the challenges of managing an online newspaper
([The Baltic Times](https://www.baltictimes.com/the_persevering_the_baltic_times_hosts_its_sixth_annual_gala_dinner/)).
