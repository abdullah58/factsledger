## Overview

Dick Clark Productions is an American television production company founded by Dick Clark in 1957, best known for producing the Golden Globe Awards, the [American Music Awards](/events/american-music-awards/) and the Billboard Music Awards — and, since June 2023, for owning the Golden Globes outright. It is the company behind the live-awards-show calendar: the ceremonies, the New Year's Eve countdown and the dance competition that have filled American television for nearly seventy years ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/penske-media-eldridge-acquires-dick-clark-productions); [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/penske-media-eldridge-acquires-dick-clark-productions-producer-of-the-golden-globes-and-billboard-music-awards/?ref=pennyfractions.ghost.io)).

The company was founded in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, in the [United States](/places/united-states/), and describes itself as the world's largest producer and proprietor of televised live event entertainment programming — a self-description, not an independent ranking ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/penske-media-eldridge-acquires-dick-clark-productions)). Its portfolio spans the Golden Globe Awards, American Music Awards, Academy of Country Music Awards, Billboard Music Awards, Dick Clark's New Year's Rockin' Eve, So You Think You Can Dance (with 19 Entertainment) and the Streamy Awards ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/penske-media-eldridge-acquires-dick-clark-productions-producer-of-the-golden-globes-and-billboard-music-awards/?ref=pennyfractions.ghost.io)). It also owns one of the entertainment industry's largest archives: more than 60 years of shows, specials, performances and programming ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/penske-media-eldridge-acquires-dick-clark-productions)). Today it sits inside Penske Media Eldridge, the joint venture of [Penske Media Corporation](/organizations/penske-media-corporation/) and Eldridge Industries that acquired it in January 2023 ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/penske-media-eldridge-acquires-dick-clark-productions-producer-of-the-golden-globes-and-billboard-music-awards/?ref=pennyfractions.ghost.io)).

## From American Bandstand to an awards-show empire

Richard Wagstaff Clark was born on November 30, 1929, in Mount Vernon, New York, graduated from Syracuse University in 1951, and moved to Philadelphia the next year to work as a radio disc jockey ([HISTORY.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/april-18/dick-clark-host-of-american-bandstand-and-new-years-rockin-eve-dies)). In 1956 he became host of a local teen dance show called Bandstand; renamed American Bandstand, it began airing nationwide in 1957, the same year he founded his production company, and ran nationally until 1989 ([HISTORY.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/april-18/dick-clark-host-of-american-bandstand-and-new-years-rockin-eve-dies)). The show introduced rock and roll to millions of young viewers and gave national television debuts to Black artists including Chuck Berry and Chubby Checker ([HISTORY.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/april-18/dick-clark-host-of-american-bandstand-and-new-years-rockin-eve-dies)). Clark's empire was not built without controversy: in 1960 he testified before a congressional subcommittee investigating payola, admitting ownership stakes in more than 30 record labels, distributors and manufacturers, and ABC ordered him to divest his music-related businesses ([HISTORY.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/april-18/dick-clark-host-of-american-bandstand-and-new-years-rockin-eve-dies)).

## The American Music Awards and New Year's Rockin' Eve

Clark created the American Music Awards in 1973, after ABC lost the Grammy Awards broadcast to CBS, as a fan-driven alternative to the industry-voted Grammys; the first ceremony aired on February 19, 1974, hosted by Roger Miller, Helen Reddy and Smokey Robinson ([Billboard](https://djfrosty.com/2024/10/06/american-music-awards-50th-anniversary-special-8-takeaways-from-a-2-hour-crash-course-in-pop-music-history/)). Clark told Billboard in December 1973 that if the show were done properly, "we may have a show that will last 20 years" — it lasted more than fifty, with its 50th Anniversary Special airing on CBS in October 2024 ([Billboard](https://djfrosty.com/2024/10/06/american-music-awards-50th-anniversary-special-8-takeaways-from-a-2-hour-crash-course-in-pop-music-history/)). The anniversary special's clips spanned the show's history, from [Aretha Franklin](/people/aretha-franklin/) among its women artists to [Nicki Minaj](/people/nicki-minaj/) among its hip-hop stars, and recalled Whitney Houston's 22 AMA wins ([Billboard](https://djfrosty.com/2024/10/06/american-music-awards-50th-anniversary-special-8-takeaways-from-a-2-hour-crash-course-in-pop-music-history/)). Dick Clark's New Year's Rockin' Eve debuted on NBC in 1972 with live coverage of the ball drop from Times Square in [New York City](/places/new-york-city/); Clark hosted it every year until a stroke in December 2004, then returned with Ryan Seacrest as co-host and appeared annually through 2011 ([HISTORY.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/april-18/dick-clark-host-of-american-bandstand-and-new-years-rockin-eve-dies)).

## The Billboard connection

Dick Clark Productions first produced the [Billboard Music Awards](/events/billboard-music-awards/) in 2014, in its first collaboration with ABC on the show — a ceremony presented by Billboard magazine and built on its chart data ([BroadwayWorld](https://www.broadwayworld.com/bwwtv/article/ABC-Dick-Clark-Productions-Extend-AMAs-NEW-YEARS-ROCKIN-EVE-Agreements-for-10-Years-20140207)). The pairing was more than a booking: Billboard and DCP were already corporate cousins. In September 2020, Penske Media Corporation and [MRC](/organizations/mrc/) announced PMRC, a joint venture to operate Billboard, [The Hollywood Reporter](/organizations/the-hollywood-reporter/) and [Vibe](/organizations/vibe/) alongside Variety, [Rolling Stone](/organizations/rolling-stone/) and [Music Business Worldwide](/organizations/music-business-worldwide/) — and a second joint venture for MRC to use DCP to develop content from PMRC brands' stories and intellectual property ([Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2020/09/23/pmrc-formed-to-operate-billboard-variety-hollywood-reporter-as-penske-mrc-joint-venture/)). When Penske Media Eldridge acquired DCP in January 2023, the deal reunited the awards producer with the magazine whose charts the awards celebrate, under a parent whose brands include [Variety](/organizations/variety/), Rolling Stone, The Hollywood Reporter, Billboard and Deadline ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/penske-media-eldridge-acquires-dick-clark-productions-producer-of-the-golden-globes-and-billboard-music-awards/?ref=pennyfractions.ghost.io)). The awards are the clearest case of DCP's model: it produces the live events of the media group that owns it.

## A decade of ownership churn

Clark sold the company in 2007, five years before his death, to Daniel Snyder's group for $175 million; in September 2012 RedZone agreed to sell it to a Guggenheim Partners-led group including Mandalay Entertainment and Mosaic Media Investment Partners, on undisclosed terms after seeking $350–400 million ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/guggenheim-partners-agrees-buy-dick-clark-productions-54511/)). Guggenheim's stake later passed to former president Todd Boehly, whose Eldridge Industries owned the company by 2017 ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/news/dick-clark-productions-owner-sues-wanda-for-break-up-fee/5115712.article)). That year Eldridge terminated Dalian Wanda Group's agreed $1 billion purchase and sued in Delaware Chancery Court for the $25 million balance of a $50 million break-up fee ([Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/news/dick-clark-productions-owner-sues-wanda-for-break-up-fee/5115712.article)). In February 2018 Eldridge merged its media assets — DCP, Billboard and The Hollywood Reporter — with MRC to form Valence Media, rebranded as MRC in April 2020 ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/adam-stotsky-named-president-of-mrc-live-and-alternative-unit-in-rebrand-of-dick-clark-productions/)). In September 2021 MRC discontinued the Dick Clark Productions name, folding its shows into MRC Live & Alternative under president Adam Stotsky ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/adam-stotsky-named-president-of-mrc-live-and-alternative-unit-in-rebrand-of-dick-clark-productions/)). Less than a year later, in August 2022, MRC and Eldridge split their combined assets and Eldridge regained DCP, restoring its name ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/mrc-eldridge-todd-boehly-pmrc-reorg/)).

## Owning the Golden Globes outright

DCP had produced the Golden Globe Awards telecast for decades, but in June 2023 it became the show's owner: DCP and Eldridge acquired all of the Golden Globes' assets, rights and properties from the Hollywood Foreign Press Association, which wound down its membership ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/dick-clark-productions-and-eldridge-acquire-golden-globes)). The proceeds, plus the association's existing resources, funded a new Golden Globe Foundation, a California nonprofit continuing the association's charitable giving — more than $55 million donated over three decades ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/dick-clark-productions-and-eldridge-acquire-golden-globes)). The first ceremony under the new ownership, the 81st Golden Globe Awards, took place on January 7, 2024, kicking off awards season ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/dick-clark-productions-and-eldridge-acquire-golden-globes)). The deal also completed a transition the new owners described openly: from a member-led organization to a commercial enterprise, in the words of outgoing HFPA president Helen Hoehne ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/dick-clark-productions-and-eldridge-acquire-golden-globes)). A show DCP had long staged for someone else became an asset it controls, free to pursue commercial opportunities for the Globes worldwide ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/dick-clark-productions-and-eldridge-acquire-golden-globes)).

## The portfolio today

The clearest measure of the portfolio's scale is the New Year's Eve countdown: the 2026 edition drew 30 million viewers at midnight and 34 million overall, a four-year high and the night's No. 1 entertainment broadcast special, headlined by Diana Ross ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/media-platforms/tv/dick-clarks-new-years-rockin-eve-with-ryan-seacrest-2026-ratings/)). That audience — up from 29 million at midnight the year before — shows six decades of brand equity at work: a show Clark started in 1972 still outdraws everything else on television on its night ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/media-platforms/tv/dick-clarks-new-years-rockin-eve-with-ryan-seacrest-2026-ratings/); [HISTORY.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/april-18/dick-clark-host-of-american-bandstand-and-new-years-rockin-eve-dies)). In August 2026 DCP added DCP Studios, a new in-house division staffed by the former Vox Creative team after Penske Media's acquisition of Vox Media and led by Yosef Johnson, to produce branded content across social, digital, podcasts and live experiences ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/dick-clark-productions-launches-dcp-studios-adding-award-winning-branded-content-studio-to-its-business)). The same release lists newer properties "Golden Eve" and "Rising Stars of Country Music" alongside the Emmy-winning So You Think You Can Dance ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/dick-clark-productions-launches-dcp-studios-adding-award-winning-branded-content-studio-to-its-business)). The company Clark founded as a one-man production outfit is now a live-events arm of a media group that also owns the magazines covering its shows.

## Frequently asked questions

**Who founded Dick Clark Productions?**
Radio and television host Dick Clark founded the company in Philadelphia in 1957, the same year his American Bandstand began airing nationwide. Clark hosted Bandstand from 1956 and New Year's Rockin' Eve until a stroke in 2004, and died in 2012 — five years after selling the company. ([HISTORY.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/april-18/dick-clark-host-of-american-bandstand-and-new-years-rockin-eve-dies))

**Who owns Dick Clark Productions now?**
Penske Media Eldridge, a joint venture of Penske Media Corporation and Eldridge Industries, acquired it in January 2023. Penske Media's brands include Variety, Billboard, Rolling Stone and The Hollywood Reporter. A 2026 PMC release describes DCP as owned by Penske Media Corporation, with no mention of Eldridge — a wording difference this profile flags rather than resolves. ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/penske-media-eldridge-acquires-dick-clark-productions-producer-of-the-golden-globes-and-billboard-music-awards/?ref=pennyfractions.ghost.io))

**Does Dick Clark Productions own the Golden Globes?**
Yes. In June 2023 DCP and Eldridge acquired all Golden Globes assets, rights and properties from the Hollywood Foreign Press Association, which wound down. The proceeds funded a new Golden Globe Foundation continuing the association's charitable giving. ([Penske Media Corporation](https://www.pmc.com/news/dick-clark-productions-and-eldridge-acquire-golden-globes))

**What shows does Dick Clark Productions produce?**
The Golden Globe Awards, American Music Awards, Academy of Country Music Awards, Billboard Music Awards, Dick Clark's New Year's Rockin' Eve, So You Think You Can Dance (with 19 Entertainment) and the Streamy Awards — plus newer properties including "Golden Eve" and "Rising Stars of Country Music". ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/penske-media-eldridge-acquires-dick-clark-productions-producer-of-the-golden-globes-and-billboard-music-awards/?ref=pennyfractions.ghost.io))

**Why did the Dick Clark Productions name disappear in 2021?**
After the 2018 merger that formed Valence Media (rebranded MRC in 2020), MRC folded DCP into its MRC Live & Alternative division in September 2021 and dropped the name. When MRC and Eldridge split in August 2022, Eldridge regained the division and restored the Dick Clark Productions name. ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/adam-stotsky-named-president-of-mrc-live-and-alternative-unit-in-rebrand-of-dick-clark-productions/); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/mrc-eldridge-todd-boehly-pmrc-reorg/))
