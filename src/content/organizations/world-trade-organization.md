## The crypto billionaire at the Geneva table

Of all the diplomats posted to the World Trade Organization's Geneva headquarters, few arrived with a backstory like Justin Sun's. In December 2021, Bloomberg reported that the 31-year-old Chinese tech entrepreneur — founder of the Tron cryptocurrency platform — had been appointed [Grenada](/places/grenada/)'s representative to the WTO, citing Grenada's foreign minister Oliver Joseph ([Caribbean Today](https://mail.caribbeantoday.com/sections/politics/this-chinese-cryptocurrency-entrepreneur-is-now-a-caribbean-diplomat)). Sun arrived at WTO headquarters on December 21, where, according to the Grenada government, he was confirmed to his post as Ambassador and Permanent Representative — a role Grenada said would help digitize the island's trade, investment, and governance ([Caribbean Today](https://mail.caribbeantoday.com/sections/politics/this-chinese-cryptocurrency-entrepreneur-is-now-a-caribbean-diplomat)).

Sun said he had been resident on the 135-square-mile island since 2019 and was phasing out his participation in crypto projects, including Tron itself ([Caribbean Today](https://mail.caribbeantoday.com/sections/politics/this-chinese-cryptocurrency-entrepreneur-is-now-a-caribbean-diplomat)). "I will focus lots of my energy, to try and push blockchain technology and cryptocurrency — the importance of all of this in developing countries and developed states as well," he told Bloomberg, adding that he would also promote technology development in Grenada ([Caribbean Today](https://mail.caribbeantoday.com/sections/politics/this-chinese-cryptocurrency-entrepreneur-is-now-a-caribbean-diplomat)). It remains one of the strangest diplomatic postings in the WTO's recent history, and it connects directly to the [Justin Sun](/people/justin-sun/) and [TRON Foundation](/organizations/tron-foundation/) profiles here.

## A court that stopped hearing cases

The WTO's most consequential recent story is the slow death of its Appellate Body — the seven-member panel that served as the final word in trade disputes. The body needs a minimum of three judges to function, and on December 9, 2019, Reuters reported that the terms of two of the three remaining members would expire the next day, with no replacements in sight ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/business/us-seals-demise-of-wto-appeals-bench-trade-officials-idUSKBN1YD1RV/)). The Trump administration had been blocking new appointments for more than two years, arguing the court had gone beyond its remit.

The U.S. ambassador to the WTO, Dennis Shea, said other members had not addressed Washington's concerns about what he called the court's "overreach" and "disregard" of WTO rules, and that the United States would not support filling the vacancies ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/business/us-seals-demise-of-wto-appeals-bench-trade-officials-idUSKBN1YD1RV/)). Much of the American displeasure, Reuters noted, stemmed from rulings that tied Washington's hands in dealing with China — WTO judges had given Beijing the benefit of the doubt on subsidies and rejected the U.S. treatment of dumping ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/business/us-seals-demise-of-wto-appeals-bench-trade-officials-idUSKBN1YD1RV/)).

## The workaround: nineteen members, one arbitration pact

With the appeals court frozen, nineteen members built their own. On April 30, 2020, China, the European Union, Canada, Australia, Singapore, Hong Kong, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Costa Rica, Guatemala, Iceland, Mexico, New Zealand, Norway, Pakistan, Switzerland, Ukraine, and Uruguay launched the Multi-Party Interim Appeal Arbitration Arrangement (MPIA), based on Article 25 of the WTO's Dispute Settlement Understanding ([South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/economy/global-economy/article/3082748/china-eu-join-19-member-temporary-global-trade-dispute)). The arrangement took effect that same Thursday and was designed to mirror the main features of the WTO appeal system, preserving the principle that governments have the right to appeal in a dispute ([South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/economy/global-economy/article/3082748/china-eu-join-19-member-temporary-global-trade-dispute)).

Notably absent: the United States, India, Britain, Japan, and South Korea — a gap that showed the limits of the workaround's reach over the biggest trade fights ([South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/economy/global-economy/article/3082748/china-eu-join-19-member-temporary-global-trade-dispute)). The MPIA was assembled in just over three months, after the members announced at the World Economic Forum in January that they would seek a way around the demise of the regular panel ([South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/economy/global-economy/article/3082748/china-eu-join-19-member-temporary-global-trade-dispute)).

## The first green deal

If the dispute-settlement story is about paralysis, the fisheries story is about the WTO proving it can still close a deal. The Agreement on Fisheries Subsidies was adopted by consensus at the 12th Ministerial Conference in Geneva in June 2022, and it is the first multilateral WTO agreement with an environmental sustainability objective ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/economy/foreign-trade/india-joins-wtos-agreement-on-fisheries-subsidies/articleshow/132517766.cms?from=mdr)). The pact prohibits subsidies linked to illegal, unreported, and unregulated (IUU) fishing and to fishing of overfished stocks, aiming to discipline the harmful subsidies that prop up large, heavily subsidized industrial fleets run by distant-water fishing nations ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/economy/foreign-trade/india-joins-wtos-agreement-on-fisheries-subsidies/articleshow/132517766.cms?from=mdr)).

It entered into force on September 15, 2025, once two-thirds of the WTO's 166 members had deposited their instruments of acceptance ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/economy/foreign-trade/india-joins-wtos-agreement-on-fisheries-subsidies/articleshow/132517766.cms?from=mdr)). India became a party on July 20, 2026, when Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agrawal handed the instrument of acceptance to Director-General Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala in Geneva ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/economy/foreign-trade/india-joins-wtos-agreement-on-fisheries-subsidies/articleshow/132517766.cms?from=mdr)). New Delhi said the deal protects the livelihoods of traditional and small-scale fishers, and noted that aquaculture and inland fisheries — the source of much of India's shrimp exports — fall outside the agreement's scope ([The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/economy/foreign-trade/india-joins-wtos-agreement-on-fisheries-subsidies/articleshow/132517766.cms?from=mdr)).

## Second term, harder job

Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala, the first woman and first African to run the WTO, was reappointed for a second four-year term on November 29, 2024, with the new mandate beginning September 1, 2025 ([Agence France-Presse](http://www.daily-sun.com/post/779691)). The next ministerial conference, MC14, was scheduled for Cameroon on March 26-29, 2026, with Cameroon's ambassador Salomon Eheth hoping the event would show the world Africa's potential for investment and sustainable development ([Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/wto-fixes-date-for-major-meeting-in-cameroon-in-early-2026/)).

The road looked rough: analysts expected trade wars as Donald Trump returned to the White House, and the organization's February 2024 meeting in Abu Dhabi had achieved only minimal results in negotiations where all 166 members must agree by consensus ([Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/wto-fixes-date-for-major-meeting-in-cameroon-in-early-2026/)). Okonjo-Iweala told members that "negotiating in the 2020s requires a more dynamic approach, one where each area is considered on its own merits, allowing us to make progress across the board" ([Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/wto-fixes-date-for-major-meeting-in-cameroon-in-early-2026/)).

## From a 1947 tariff pact to a 166-member institution

The WTO began as a conference table in Geneva. The General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade was negotiated there in 1947 with 23 participating countries — a provisional framework to cut tariffs after the trade conflicts that had deepened the Depression ([World History Encyclopedia](https://ww2.jacksonms.gov/libweb/iOldju/5OK103/history_of-the-world-trade__organization.pdf)). Successive rounds of negotiations — Dillon, Kennedy, Tokyo, and finally the Uruguay Round of 1986-1994 — widened the agenda to services and intellectual property, and the Uruguay Round's conclusion in Marrakesh, Morocco, in April 1994 paved the way for the WTO's formal establishment ([World History Encyclopedia](https://ww2.jacksonms.gov/libweb/iOldju/5OK103/history_of-the-world-trade__organization.pdf)).

The WTO commenced operations on January 1, 1995, as a permanent institution with a broader mandate than GATT: administering trade agreements, providing a forum for negotiations, resolving disputes, monitoring members' trade policies, and building capacity in developing countries ([World History Encyclopedia](https://ww2.jacksonms.gov/libweb/iOldju/5OK103/history_of-the-world-trade__organization.pdf)). It opened with 128 members and counted 164 as of October 2023 ([World History Encyclopedia](https://ww2.jacksonms.gov/libweb/iOldju/5OK103/history_of-the-world-trade__organization.pdf)); two further accessions brought it to 166, the figure cited in 2024-2026 reporting ([Reuters](https://www.globalbankingandfinance.com/wto-fixes-date-for-major-meeting-in-cameroon-in-early-2026/), [The Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/economy/foreign-trade/india-joins-wtos-agreement-on-fisheries-subsidies/articleshow/132517766.cms?from=mdr)).

The Doha Development Round, launched in 2001 to address developing-country needs, was never concluded — a failure that fed lasting doubts about the WTO's ability to adapt ([World History Encyclopedia](https://ww2.jacksonms.gov/libweb/iOldju/5OK103/history_of-the-world-trade__organization.pdf)). Those doubts are the backdrop for everything in this profile: an appeals court that stopped working, a workaround built by nineteen members, and a fisheries deal that showed the institution could still act.
