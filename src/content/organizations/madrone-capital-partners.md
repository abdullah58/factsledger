## Overview

Madrone Capital Partners is a Menlo Park, California private investment firm that manages capital for the Walton family, the family behind Walmart. Founded in 2005 by Greg Penner — Walmart's chairman since 2015 and a co-owner of the Denver Broncos — it invests the family's money directly in companies and in other funds, across venture capital, private equity, and public equities, with a concentrated, long-term approach ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/alumni/contact/alumni-association/about-alumni/gregory-b-penner)).

Madrone is not a conventional venture capital firm: it raises no outside funds and answers to one family, not to limited partners. Its best-known bets show both sides of that concentrated style. It was the largest shareholder in StubHub when the ticket reseller went public in September 2025, holding 24.5% before the offering ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/17/eric-bakers-long-winding-road-to-taking-stubhub-public/)), and it joined [Brex](/organizations/brex/)'s $425 million Series D in 2021 at a valuation above $7.4 billion ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/briefing/briefing-4-26-21/?ref=ctvc.co)). It also backed solar-panel maker Solyndra, which collapsed into bankruptcy in 2011 ([Reuters](https://www.investsmart.com.au/investment-news/singed-by-solyndra/89016)).

## Founding and the Walton family connection

Greg Penner founded Madrone after a career inside Walmart and in venture capital. Before Madrone he was senior vice president and chief financial officer of Wal-Mart Japan and senior vice president of finance and strategy for Walmart.com, and before that a general partner at Peninsula Capital, an early-stage venture fund ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/alumni/contact/alumni-association/about-alumni/gregory-b-penner)). He holds an MBA from [Stanford University](/universities/stanford-university/) (1997) and a BS in international economics from Georgetown (1992) ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/alumni/contact/alumni-association/about-alumni/gregory-b-penner)). Reuters records that he has been a partner at the Walton family-backed firm since 2005 ([Reuters](https://www.vcpost.com/articles/72199/20150605/waltons-cement-grip-wal-mart-new-chairman.htm)).

The firm is, in Stanford's words, "a private investment firm affiliated with S. Robson Walton and other family members" ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/alumni/contact/alumni-association/about-alumni/gregory-b-penner)). That family tie is also Penner's other career: he joined Walmart's board in 2008, became vice chairman in 2014, and succeeded Rob Walton as chairman at the June 5, 2015 shareholders' meeting ([Reuters](https://www.vcpost.com/articles/72199/20150605/waltons-cement-grip-wal-mart-new-chairman.htm)). Jamie McJunkin is the firm's co-founder and general partner, based with Penner in Menlo Park ([Private Equity International](https://www.privateequityinternational.com/institution-profiles/madrone-capital-partners.html)). The same family capital later bought the Denver Broncos: NFL owners voted 32–0 in August 2022 to approve the Walton-Penner group's $4.65 billion purchase, a record for a pro sports team at the time ([Sports Business Journal](https://www.sportsbusinessjournal.com:443/Daily/Closing-Bell/2022/08/09/Broncos-Sale-Official-NFL-Meetings/)).

## Investment approach and portfolio

Madrone invests directly in companies and in other funds, spanning venture capital, private equity, and public equities, with a stated focus on alternative energy technologies and China — and it does so "with a concentrated, long-term approach" ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/alumni/contact/alumni-association/about-alumni/gregory-b-penner)). Its own description, in the 2007 Hyatt announcement, is the same: a private investment firm "affiliated with Rob Walton and his family" that "invests with a concentrated, long-term philosophy" ([Hyatt](https://newsroom.hyatt.com/2007-08-29-GLOBAL-HYATT-CORPORATION-EXPANDS-SHAREHOLDER-BASE)). Because it invests family money rather than raising funds, it discloses no fund sizes or assets under management, and none is stated here.

## The StubHub windfall — and the bets that went wrong

Madrone's biggest public win came from StubHub. When eBay decided to spin off the ticket reseller in 2019, chief executive Eric Baker secured backing from Madrone, WestCap, and Bessemer Venture Partners and bought the company for $4.05 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/17/eric-bakers-long-winding-road-to-taking-stubhub-public/)). The pandemic then nearly destroyed the business as live events stopped, but revenue recovered on the return of concerts and sport, and StubHub went public on September 17, 2025, pricing at $23.50 a share ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/17/eric-bakers-long-winding-road-to-taking-stubhub-public/)). According to the S-1, Madrone held 24.5% of the company before the offering — ahead of WestCap at 12.3% and Bessemer at 8.8% — making it StubHub's largest shareholder ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/17/eric-bakers-long-winding-road-to-taking-stubhub-public/)). The shares closed the first day 6% below the IPO price, valuing the company at over $7 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/17/eric-bakers-long-winding-road-to-taking-stubhub-public/)).

The same concentrated style produced the firm's most visible loss. Madrone put money into Solyndra in 2007, when the solar company's tube-shaped panels were meant to undercut Chinese manufacturers on cost; by 2011 the company had filed for bankruptcy and laid off 1,100 employees ([Reuters](https://www.investsmart.com.au/investment-news/singed-by-solyndra/89016)). The Brex bet sits between the two outcomes: Capital One agreed in January 2026 to buy Brex for $5.15 billion, well below its $12.3 billion peak valuation, so Madrone and the other investors who entered at $7.4 billion or higher exited liquid but at a markdown ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)). Brex's early backers — [Y Combinator](/organizations/y-combinator/), Kleiner Perkins, DST Global, and individuals including [Peter Thiel](/people/peter-thiel/) and [Max Levchin](/people/max-levchin/) — did far better, while rival [Ramp](/organizations/ramp/) overtook Brex during the same period ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/22/capital-one-acquires-brex-for-steep-discount-to-its-peak-valuation-but-early-believers-are-laughing-all-the-way-to-the-bank/)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Madrone Capital Partners the same as Madrona Venture Group?**
No. Madrone Capital Partners is the Menlo Park firm founded in 2005 that invests Walton family capital, while Madrona Venture Group is a Seattle venture firm founded in 1995 and an early Amazon backer. The names differ by one letter and search results mix them up, but they are separate firms in different cities with different founders and capital.

**What is Madrone Capital Partners' connection to Walmart?**
It manages capital for the Walton family, the family behind Walmart, and is affiliated with S. Robson Walton ([Stanford Graduate School of Business](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/alumni/contact/alumni-association/about-alumni/gregory-b-penner)). Its founder, Greg Penner, is Rob Walton's son-in-law and has chaired Walmart's board since June 2015 ([Reuters](https://www.vcpost.com/articles/72199/20150605/waltons-cement-grip-wal-mart-new-chairman.htm)).

**What are Madrone's biggest investments?**
StubHub, where it held 24.5% before the September 2025 IPO ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/17/eric-bakers-long-winding-road-to-taking-stubhub-public/)); a $1 billion minority stake in Global Hyatt with Goldman Sachs in 2007 ([Hyatt](https://newsroom.hyatt.com/2007-08-29-GLOBAL-HYATT-CORPORATION-EXPANDS-SHAREHOLDER-BASE)); Brex's $425 million Series D in 2021 ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/briefing/briefing-4-26-21/?ref=ctvc.co)); and a $100 million investment in Wasserman in 2014 ([SportsBusinessDaily](http://www.sportsbusinessdaily.com/Daily/Issues/2014/09/15/Finance/Wasserman.aspx)).

**Did Madrone Capital Partners invest in Theranos?**
Not as the sources read establish it. Court documents showed the Walton family invested $150 million in Theranos, making the family its largest investor ([FierceBiotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/who-lost-cash-theranos-documents-show-how-much-devos-murdoch-and-walmart-heirs-invested)), but no fully-read tier1 or tier2 source says that investment ran through Madrone, so this profile does not claim it did.

**How big is Madrone Capital Partners?**
Unknown from public sources. It invests family money rather than raising outside funds, so it publishes no fund sizes or assets under management, and no tier1 or tier2 source states a figure. Its scale is visible only through individual stakes, such as its 24.5% of StubHub and its 10 percent owner filings at the SEC ([U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/own-disp?action=getowner&CIK=0001789147&sortid=filings-DESC)).
