## Overview

The **Estate of Michael Jackson** is the legal entity created on the singer's death on June 25, 2009 to administer his property and posthumous income ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/entertainment/explained-paris-jackson-michael-jackson-estate-lawsuit-625000-legal-bonuses-john-branca-john-mcclain-court-ruling-1503593875.html)). Jackson died more than $500 million in debt; under co-executors John Branca and John McClain — the entertainment attorney and the music executive named in his July 7, 2002 will — the estate became one of the most lucrative posthumous businesses in entertainment history, generating an estimated $3.5 billion by 2025 ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/entertainment/explained-paris-jackson-michael-jackson-estate-lawsuit-625000-legal-bonuses-john-branca-john-mcclain-court-ruling-1503593875.html); [Music Ally](https://musically.com/2025/11/04/michael-jackson-is-the-highest-paid-dead-celebrity-of-2025/)). McClain died on May 26, 2026, leaving Branca as the estate's sole executor ([Page Six](https://pagesix.com/2026/05/28/celebrity-news/how-latest-change-in-michael-jacksons-estate-could-impact-paris-fight-to-take-back-control/)). The estate was also a co-producer of the 2026 biopic [*Michael*](/creative-works/michael-2026-film/) — its attorneys discovered the settlement clause that forced the film's third-act rebuild, and it covered the resulting reshoot costs in exchange for an equity stake ([The Playlist](https://theplaylist.net/michael-lionsgates-antoine-fuqua-directed-biopic-underwent-15m-reshoots-after-child-abuse-allegation-scenes-in-third-act-were-cut-report-20260407/)).

## From $500 million in debt to a multibillion-dollar business

When Jackson died, his will — signed July 7, 2002 — named Branca and McClain as co-executors; it was filed at the Los Angeles County courthouse on July 1, 2009 and confirmed by a judge on July 6, after his mother Katherine withdrew her initial objection ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/entertainment/explained-paris-jackson-michael-jackson-estate-lawsuit-625000-legal-bonuses-john-branca-john-mcclain-court-ruling-1503593875.html)). Branca had been Jackson's on-and-off attorney since the early 1980s — he brokered the 1985 purchase of the ATV Music Publishing catalogue — while McClain, a childhood friend, had built a separate career managing Janet Jackson's recording career ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/entertainment/explained-paris-jackson-michael-jackson-estate-lawsuit-625000-legal-bonuses-john-branca-john-mcclain-court-ruling-1503593875.html)). Under their stewardship the estate reversed its fortunes: by June 2024 it was valued at more than $2 billion, with total earnings above $3 billion, driven by the concert documentary *This Is It*, two posthumous albums, a Sony recording deal, two Cirque du Soleil productions and the four-time Tony-winning Broadway musical *MJ* ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/entertainment/explained-paris-jackson-michael-jackson-estate-lawsuit-625000-legal-bonuses-john-branca-john-mcclain-court-ruling-1503593875.html)).

## The catalog sales that funded the turnaround

The estate's biggest paydays came from unwinding assets Jackson had accumulated in life. In March 2016 Sony agreed to buy the estate's 50 percent stake in Sony/ATV Music Publishing — the joint venture of Jackson's ATV catalogue and Sony formed in 1995 — for $750 million, a deal covering more than 2.8 million copyrights ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/03/14/sony-to-buy-the-remaining-half-of-sonyatv-shares-from-michael-jacksons-estate/)). Jackson had bought ATV itself in 1985 for $41.5 million, outbidding Paul McCartney for a trove that included most of the Beatles' songs ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/03/14/sony-to-buy-the-remaining-half-of-sonyatv-shares-from-michael-jacksons-estate/)). Eight years later, Sony acquired a half interest in Jackson's remaining publishing and recorded masters — including the Mijac catalogue — for at least $600 million, reported as the largest single-artist catalog deal ever ([The Business Times](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/lifestyle/sony-buys-michael-jackson-music-us600-million-billboard)). The estate retained Jackson's master recordings, Mijac Music and a stake in EMI Publishing after the first sale ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/03/14/sony-to-buy-the-remaining-half-of-sonyatv-shares-from-michael-jacksons-estate/)).

## The 2026 biopic: producer, clause, and equity stake

The estate did not merely license Jackson's life for the screen — it produced the biopic. As a producer on Antoine Fuqua's *Michael*, the estate's own attorneys discovered the clause in the 1994 Jordan Chandler settlement barring his depiction or mention in any film — after the material had already been shot ([The Playlist](https://theplaylist.net/michael-lionsgates-antoine-fuqua-directed-biopic-underwent-15m-reshoots-after-child-abuse-allegation-scenes-in-third-act-were-cut-report-20260407/)). The fix was a 22-day reshoot in [Los Angeles](/places/los-angeles/) in June 2025, adding $10–15 million to the $155 million budget; because the oversight was the estate's own, it shouldered the added costs and took an equity stake alongside Lionsgate and the producers ([The Playlist](https://theplaylist.net/michael-lionsgates-antoine-fuqua-directed-biopic-underwent-15m-reshoots-after-child-abuse-allegation-scenes-in-third-act-were-cut-report-20260407/)). The episode made the estate the rare rights-holder to both cause and fund a film's third-act rebuild. Branca is portrayed on screen by Miles Teller ([Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/michael-jackson-biopic-fact-fiction-michael-11956448)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Who runs Michael Jackson's estate?**
Entertainment attorney John Branca is now the sole executor, following the death of co-executor John McClain on May 26, 2026, at 71, after a long health battle. Both men were named in Jackson's July 7, 2002 will. No successor co-executor had been appointed; Branca has the authority to name one if he becomes unable to serve.

**How much has the estate earned since 2009?**
Forbes estimates $3.5 billion in total posthumous earnings through 2025 — including $105 million in 2025 alone, when Jackson topped the highest-paid dead celebrities ranking. He has topped that chart in 13 of the 16 full calendar years since his death.

**What did the estate sell to Sony?**
In 2016 it sold its 50 percent stake in Sony/ATV Music Publishing to Sony for $750 million. In 2024 it sold a half interest in Jackson's remaining publishing and recorded masters — including the Mijac catalog — for at least $600 million, reported as the largest single-artist catalog deal ever.

**Why did the estate pay for reshoots on the *Michael* biopic?**
Serving as a producer, the estate's attorneys discovered a clause in the 1994 Jordan Chandler settlement barring his depiction or mention in any film — after the material had been shot. The 22-day June 2025 reshoot cost $10–15 million; the estate covered it as its own error and took an equity stake in the film.

**What is Paris Jackson's dispute with the estate about?**
In July 2025 she formally objected to $625,000 in 2018 bonuses paid to three outside law firms, calling them "irregular" gifts and gratuities. In April 2026 a retired probate judge sustained her objection, ordered the money returned, and directed the estate to withhold 30 percent of attorneys' fees pending court approval.
